# Weil-test fidelity separates scale horizon, test resolution, and admissible-class rigidity

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-020-full-weil-test-family-is-norm-faithful-fixed-support-is-finite-scale|AF-020]] separates the support horizon from information completeness: the full compactly supported Weil-test family determines the weighted prime-power measure, while fixed support sees only the finite arithmetic horizon below `e^A`.

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-021-finite-test-families-have-large-moment-fibers|AF-021]]--[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-023-regular-weil-test-points-have-positive-dimensional-beurling-fibers|AF-023]] isolate the independent finite-dimensional resolution loss. More source degrees of freedom than retained test values give exact generalized-prime collision fibers at regular points.

The newer findings show that merely moving to singular points or imposing familiar finite constraints does not restore intrinsic arithmetic rigidity. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-024-unconstrained-weil-tests-program-local-separable-geometry|AF-024]] proves that unconstrained smooth tests can source-tune arbitrary local separable response germs and can even isolate a chosen finite prime block with a singular scalar test. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-025-finite-linear-admissibility-does-not-block-local-weil-programming|AF-025]] shows finite linear admissibility can be repaired in a response-invisible test subspace. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-026-fourier-positive-compact-tests-retain-full-dimensional-finite-weil-jet-freedom|AF-026]] and [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-027-double-positive-compact-tests-retain-full-dimensional-finite-weil-jet-freedom|AF-027]] show that Fourier positivity, and even simultaneous pointwise/Fourier positivity, still leave full-dimensional finite Weil-jet freedom.

The source-backed filtration is therefore threefold: **support controls visible arithmetic scale; finite test dimension controls resolution inside that scale; and arithmetic specificity requires an admissible test/source class fixed independently strongly enough to remove the remaining local programming freedom.** A source-tuned singular isolator or a finite-order positivity constraint is not by itself evidence of rational-prime specificity.
