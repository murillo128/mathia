# Weil-test fidelity separates scale horizon from test-space resolution

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-020-full-weil-test-family-is-norm-faithful-fixed-support-is-finite-scale|AF-020]] identifies two different information limits in the Weil prime-power functional. The full compactly supported test family determines the weighted prime-power measure and hence the generator-norm multiset, while a fixed support radius sees exactly the finite arithmetic horizon below `e^A`.

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-021-finite-test-families-have-large-moment-fibers|AF-021]] then isolates a second, independent compression: finitely many exact test values cannot identify arbitrary positive measures once the source has more affine degrees of freedom than the test vector. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-022-finite-weil-tests-have-local-beurling-deformation-collisions|AF-022]] realizes that ambiguity inside the exact generalized-prime/prime-power model, producing arbitrarily local collisions for finite Weil-test vectors rather than only ambient moment-cone witnesses.

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-023-regular-weil-test-points-have-positive-dimensional-beurling-fibers|AF-023]] strengthens the local statement at regular source points: whenever more generator coordinates are varied than retained tests and the Jacobian has locally constant rank, the exact same-test fiber through the point is positive-dimensional; full row rank gives dimension `N-d`. Any surviving finite-test rigidity claim at a distinguished rational-prime point must therefore come from a singular or additionally constrained admissible category, not from generic finite-dimensional testing.

The source-backed separation is exact: **support radius controls which arithmetic scales are visible; test-space dimension and source constraints control resolution inside those visible scales**. The full unbounded test family removes the first loss, while finite-dimensional compression introduces the second.
