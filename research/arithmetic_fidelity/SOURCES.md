# Sources

## AF-001 — Fiberwise recoverability and unconstrained lifts

- David Blackwell, **“Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments,”** *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 24(2), 265–272 (1953). DOI: `10.1214/aoms/1177729032`. Role: classical comparison-of-experiments / garbling background for the principle that deterministic or stochastic post-processing cannot increase available decision information.
- William W. Armstrong, **“Dependency Structures of Data Base Relationships,”** *Information Processing 74*, 580–583 (1974). Role: classical functional-dependency language; equality of determinant values forcing equality of dependent values is the relational-data analogue of a discriminator being constant on compression fibers.
- H. S. Witsenhausen, **“The Zero-Error Side Information Problem and Chromatic Numbers,”** *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 22(5), 592–593 (1976). DOI: `10.1109/TIT.1976.1055607`. Role: zero-error side-information prior art; when side information is also known at the transmitter, the alphabet-minimization problem becomes trivial, closely paralleling the unrestricted fiberwise lift construction in AF-001.
- Alon Orlitsky and James R. Roche, **“Coding for Computing,”** *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 47(3), 903–917 (2001). DOI: `10.1109/18.915643`. Role: function-computation with side information; shows that nontrivial compression questions arise once the admissible coding/observation model is constrained rather than allowing an arbitrary target-carrying mark.

The elementary set-theoretic factorization statements in AF-001 are derived directly. These sources locate their closest established languages and prevent treating the basic fiber criterion or unconstrained side-information phenomenon as novel.

## AF-002 — Fixed-observable lifts are decision-relative discernibility reducts

- Zdzisław Pawlak, **“Rough Sets,”** *International Journal of Computer & Information Sciences* 11(5), 341–356 (1982). DOI: `10.1007/BF01001956`. Role: foundational rough-set / information-system framework in which retained attributes induce indiscernibility relations and exact definability depends on the resulting equivalence classes.
- Andrzej Skowron and Cecylia Rauszer, **“The Discernibility Matrices and Functions in Information Systems,”** in *Intelligent Decision Support: Handbook of Applications and Advances of the Rough Sets Theory*, 331–362 (1992). DOI: `10.1007/978-94-015-7975-9_21`. Role: direct prior art for pairwise discernibility sets, discernibility functions, and reducts; the decision-relative form matches AF-002 after conditioning on the already-retained map `T`.

AF-002 derives the conditional hitting-set statement directly from AF-001, but the finite fixed-library mechanism is classical rough-set reduct/discernibility mathematics. The source bridge is used to prevent a false novelty claim and to redirect future Arithmetic Fidelity work toward intrinsically constrained observable families and non-table-like settings.

## AF-003 — Invariant-observable quotients and orbit-closure fidelity

- David Mumford, John Fogarty, and Frances Kirwan, ***Geometric Invariant Theory***, 3rd ed., Ergebnisse der Mathematik und ihrer Grenzgebiete, vol. 34, Springer (1994), ISBN `978-3-540-56963-3`. Role: standard source for reductive-group invariant theory, affine categorical quotients, orbit separation by invariants, and the distinction between quotient points and arbitrary group orbits.
- T. A. Springer, ***Invariant Theory***, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 585, Springer (1977). DOI: `10.1007/BFb0095644`. Role: classical invariant-ring background and finite-generation/separation context for algebraic group actions.
- **“Invariants, theory of,”** *Encyclopedia of Mathematics*. Role: concise authoritative summary of the affine reductive quotient statement used here: the invariant ring defines a quotient whose fibers contain exactly one closed orbit, and invariant functions alone do not generally retain complete information about nonclosed orbits.

AF-003 derives the maximal-observable joint-evaluation criterion and the `\mathbb G_m` weight-`(1,-1)` example directly. The orbit-closure description of the full invariant algebra is classical GIT; the Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is to use that quotient as a no-go test for every lift constrained to the same invariant-observable class.

## AF-004 — Third-order coupling and quadratic phase loss

- Hartmut O. Bartelt, Adolf W. Lohmann, and Bernhard Wirnitzer, **“Phase and Amplitude Recovery from Bispectra,”** *Applied Optics* 23(18), 3121–3129 (1984). DOI: `10.1364/AO.23.003121`. Role: early explicit reconstruction of Fourier phase and amplitude from bispectral data; direct prior art for third-order phase coupling as a recovery mechanism.
- John I. Yellott Jr. and Geoffrey J. Iverson, **“Uniqueness Properties of Higher-Order Autocorrelation Functions,”** *Journal of the Optical Society of America A* 9(3), 388–404 (1992). DOI: `10.1364/JOSAA.9.000388`. Role: systematic uniqueness and non-uniqueness results for higher-order autocorrelations; establishes both the power of triple correlation and important degeneracy/band-limit failures.
- Ramakrishna Kakarala and Geoffrey J. Iverson, **“Uniqueness of Results for Multiple Correlations of Periodic Functions,”** *Journal of the Optical Society of America A* 10(7), 1517–1528 (1993). DOI: `10.1364/JOSAA.10.001517`. Role: periodic/discrete-Fourier setting; shows that triple correlation is not universally complete when spectral support is degenerate and studies recovery from higher moment spectra.
- Ramakrishna Kakarala, **“The Bispectrum as a Source of Phase-Sensitive Invariants for Fourier Descriptors: A Group-Theoretic Approach,”** *Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision* 44(3), 341–353 (2012). DOI: `10.1007/s10851-012-0330-6`. Role: group-theoretic bispectrum and completeness results on compact groups/homogeneous spaces under nondegeneracy conditions; places AF-004's finite-abelian argument inside established invariant theory for correlations.
- Tamir Bendory, Robert Beinert, and Yonina C. Eldar, **“Fourier Phase Retrieval: Uniqueness and Algorithms,”** in *Compressed Sensing and its Applications*, 55–91, Birkhäuser/Springer (2017). DOI: `10.1007/978-3-319-69802-1_2`. Role: modern survey of Fourier phase retrieval and the generic non-uniqueness of one-dimensional Fourier-magnitude data without additional information.

AF-004 derives the finite-abelian power-spectrum counterexample and ratio-character proof directly. The completeness mechanism is classical bispectral mathematics. Its role in Arithmetic Fidelity is to exhibit an exact order-sensitive quotient refinement: quadratic translation invariants leave excess phase freedom, while third-order compatibility can reduce the generic fiber to the intended translation orbit.

## AF-005 — Monomial phase lifts and annihilator lattices

- Sidney A. Morris, ***Pontryagin Duality and the Structure of Locally Compact Abelian Groups***, London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 29, Cambridge University Press (1977), especially Chapter 7. DOI for the digital book: `10.1017/CBO9780511600722`. Role: double-annihilator theorem and the subgroup/quotient correspondence under Pontryagin duality; supplies the exact equivalence `L^\perp=H <=> L=H^\perp` for the compact-torus/discrete-lattice setting.
- Mateusz Michałek and Bernd Sturmfels, ***Invitation to Nonlinear Algebra***, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 211, American Mathematical Society (2021), Chapter 8. Role: standard character-lattice description of algebraic tori and monomial maps; Proposition 8.2 identifies the torus image from the lattice generated by the chosen characters.
- Richard P. Stanley, **“Smith Normal Form in Combinatorics,”** *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* 144, 476–495 (2016). DOI: `10.1016/j.jcta.2016.06.013`. Role: modern account of Smith normal form, invariant factors, and the finitely generated abelian quotient structure used to separate continuous kernel dimension from finite torsion aliases.

AF-005 derives the compact-torus kernel formula, the common-phase counterexample, and the bispectral lattice reinterpretation directly. The mathematics is classical character theory and integer linear algebra; the Arithmetic Fidelity use is to replace coupling-graph or rank heuristics by the exact integer relation lattice and its Smith invariants when auditing phase-type relational lifts.

## AF-006 — Marked Hermitian spectra and matrix spectral measures

- Roger A. Horn and Charles R. Johnson, ***Matrix Analysis***, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press (2012; digital edition 2013). DOI: `10.1017/CBO9781139020411`. Role: standard finite-dimensional matrix-analysis source for unitary equivalence, Hermitian spectral decomposition, and Gram-matrix geometry used in the eigenspace-by-eigenspace classification.
- Marcin Moszyński, **“Spectral Theory of Self-adjoint Finitely Cyclic Operators and Introduction to Matrix Measure L2-spaces,”** arXiv:`2212.13953` (2022). Role: direct infinite-dimensional prior art for representing finitely cyclic self-adjoint operators by multiplication by the identity in a matrix-measure `L^2` space; establishes that matrix-valued spectral measures are the classical multiple-vector analogue of scalar cyclic spectral measures.
- Constanze Liaw and Sergei Treil, **“Matrix Measures and Finite Rank Perturbations of Self-adjoint Operators,”** *Journal of Spectral Theory* 10(4), 1173–1210 (2020). DOI: `10.4171/JST/324`. Role: authoritative modern use of matrix-valued measures as the natural spectral language for finite-rank/multiple-vector self-adjoint theory, including spectral representations.

AF-006 derives the finite-dimensional pointed classification and the two-mark counterexample directly. The theorem is a finite atomic specialization of classical spectral/Gram theory and finitely cyclic matrix-measure representations. Its Arithmetic Fidelity role is to show exactly which relational information a marked spectral lift must retain: diagonal scalar spectral weights can preserve each mark separately while losing cross-mark geometry, whereas the full per-eigenspace Gram matrix closes that quotient fiber up to unitary gauge.

## AF-007 — Vertical differential rank and smooth submersion fidelity

- John M. Lee, ***Introduction to Smooth Manifolds***, 2nd ed., Graduate Texts in Mathematics 218, Springer (2012). DOI: `10.1007/978-1-4419-9982-5`. Role: standard source for the rank/submersion theorem, local normal form of a submersion, tangent spaces to regular fibers, and smooth local sections used in the descent argument and local sharpness proof.
- Ieke Moerdijk and Janez Mrčun, ***Introduction to Foliations and Lie Groupoids***, Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 91, Cambridge University Press (2003), Chapter 1. DOI: `10.1017/CBO9780511615450`. Role: authoritative foliation background; regular foliations are locally modeled by fibers of submersions, supplying the geometric language for the vertical distribution `ker dT`.
- Pierre Molino, ***Riemannian Foliations***, Progress in Mathematics 73, Birkhäuser (1988), especially Chapter 2.1. Role: classical transverse-geometry source for basic functions, i.e. smooth functions constant along leaves, which is the established foliation-theoretic language behind the criterion `dD(ker dT)=0` on connected fibers.

AF-007 derives the vertical-rank lower bound, local sharpness construction, composition monotonicity, and the disconnected-fiber counterexample directly from classical submersion/constant-rank and foliation facts. No novelty is claimed for the descent theorem or the differential-topological ingredients. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is to organize them into a two-layer audit: a computable infinitesimal defect for continuous loss plus a separate global fiber test for discrete or disconnected identifications.

## AF-010 — Full-jet projection fidelity needs quasianalyticity plus cylindrical closure

- Steven G. Krantz and Harold R. Parks, ***A Primer of Real Analytic Functions***, 2nd ed., Birkhäuser (2002; digital edition 2012). DOI: `10.1007/978-0-8176-8134-0`. Role: standard real-analytic reference for convergent multivariable Taylor expansions and analytic inverse/implicit-function machinery; supports the analytic projection proof and analytic-submersion corollary.
- Vincent Thilliez, **“On Quasianalytic Local Rings,”** *Expositiones Mathematicae* 26(1), 1–23 (2008). DOI: `10.1016/j.exmath.2007.04.001`. Role: authoritative survey identifying quasianalyticity with injectivity of the Borel/Taylor map and giving the Denjoy-Carleman criterion; supplies the uniqueness half of the full-jet projection criterion while standard Denjoy-Carleman settings carry additional closure structure.
- André Belotto da Silva, Edward Bierstone, and Michael Chow, **“Composite Quasianalytic Functions,”** *Compositio Mathematica* 154(9), 1960–1973 (2018). DOI: `10.1112/S0010437X18007339`. Role: decisive stronger prior art: formal compositeness at a single point with a generically submersive quasianalytic Denjoy-Carleman map implies local composition, with a possible shift/loss of regularity. This places AF-010's regular projection/submersion mechanism inside established composite-function theory rather than as a novelty claim.
- Edward Bierstone and Pierre D. Milman, **“Composite Differentiable Functions,”** *Annals of Mathematics* 116(3), 541–558 (1982). DOI: `10.2307/2007022`. Role: classical smooth composite-function background; establishes that differentiable factorization/composition is an existing theory and helps delimit AF-010's narrower pointwise-jet obstruction.

AF-010 derives the finite-jet polynomial counterexample, analytic projection criterion, flat-germ smooth counterexample, and the corrected quasianalytic projection theorem directly. The theorem requires not only quasianalytic uniqueness but also dimensional closure under coordinate restriction, cylindrical pullback, and subtraction so that the comparison germ remains admissible. The Arithmetic Fidelity use is a two-gate audit for formal recovery: uniqueness of the representation and closure under the reconstruction operations must both hold before infinite-jet data can certify actual descent.

## AF-009 — Conditional-variance / Bayes-risk fidelity under stochastic compression

- David Blackwell, **“Conditional Expectation and Unbiased Sequential Estimation,”** *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 18(1), 105–110 (1947). DOI: `10.1214/aoms/1177730497`. Role: primary classical source for the conditional-expectation orthogonality identity and variance reduction underlying the Rao–Blackwell mechanism used in AF-009.
- David Blackwell, **“Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments,”** *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 24(2), 265–272 (1953). DOI: `10.1214/aoms/1177729032`. Role: classical garbling/comparison-of-experiments framework; supplies the broader decision-theoretic data-processing context for the stochastic `Y -> Z` no-side-channel condition.
- Olav Kallenberg, ***Foundations of Modern Probability***, 3rd ed., Probability Theory and Stochastic Modelling 99, Springer (2021). DOI: `10.1007/978-3-030-61871-1`. Role: modern authoritative probability reference for conditioning and martingales; the `L^2` conditional-expectation projection/tower framework is the standard setting in which AF-009's residual covariance decomposition lives.

AF-009 derives the matrix Pythagorean identity, exact zero-defect recovery criterion, positive-semidefinite defect increment under deterministic or Markov garbling, surviving-direction kernel criterion, and binary Bayes-error comparison directly. No novelty is claimed for conditional expectation, Rao–Blackwellization, total-variance decomposition, or Blackwell data processing. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is to organize these classical results into a discriminator-relative quantitative defect whose increment localizes the information lost at each compression stage.
