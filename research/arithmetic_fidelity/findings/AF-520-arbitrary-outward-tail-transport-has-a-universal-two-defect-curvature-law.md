# AF-520 — Arbitrary outward tail transport has a universal two-defect curvature law

## Status

`EXACT-DERIVED` · `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY` · `DIRICHLET-SOURCE` · `NONPROPORTIONAL-TRANSPORT` · `TWO-MOMENT-COMPRESSION` · `MESOSCOPIC-CURVATURE` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

Let

\[
P(s)=\sum_{p\in\mathbb P}p^{-s},
\qquad
\kappa_P(s)=\frac{d^2}{ds^2}\log P(s),
\qquad s>1.
\tag{1}
\]

For each cutoff `A`, retain every prime `p\le A` and replace every prime `p>A` by a positive atom

\[
q_{p,A}\ge p.
\tag{2}
\]

The displacement may depend arbitrarily on both `p` and `A`; in particular it need not be proportional, uniformly small, or generated by a finite-dimensional transport rule. Define

\[
Q_A(s)
=
\sum_{p\le A}p^{-s}
+
\sum_{p>A}q_{p,A}^{-s},
\qquad
\kappa_A(s)=\frac{d^2}{ds^2}\log Q_A(s).
\tag{3}
\]

Let `A\to\infty`, let `t_A\downarrow0`, and put

\[
s_A=1+t_A,
\qquad
L_A=\log\frac1{t_A}.
\tag{4}
\]

Assume only the nondegeneracy condition

\[
\rho_{0,A}
:=
\frac{Q_A(s_A)}{P(s_A)}
\ge c>0
\tag{5}
\]

for all sufficiently large `A`. Define also the normalized second-log-moment participation

\[
\rho_{2,A}
:=
\frac{Q_A''(s_A)}{P''(s_A)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then, uniformly over all outward transports satisfying `(2)` and `(5)`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)}{\kappa_P(s_A)}
=
\frac{\rho_{2,A}}{\rho_{0,A}}
+
O_c\!\left(\frac1{L_A}\right).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently, with the zeroth- and second-moment defects

\[
d_{0,A}=1-\rho_{0,A},
\qquad
d_{2,A}=1-\rho_{2,A},
\tag{8}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)}{\kappa_P(s_A)}
=
\frac{1-d_{2,A}}{1-d_{0,A}}
+
O_c\!\left(\frac1{L_A}\right).
}
\tag{9}
\]

No convergence of either defect is required. If

\[
d_{0,A}\to d_0<1,
\qquad
d_{2,A}\to d_2,
\tag{10}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)}{\kappa_P(s_A)}
\longrightarrow
\frac{1-d_2}{1-d_0}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the nonproportional outward-transport frontier left open by AF-519 does **not** require the complete displacement distribution at leading curvature order. At any boundary scale where a positive fraction of the source survives, arbitrary outward transport is compressed to exactly two aggregate quantities: its surviving Dirichlet value and its surviving second logarithmic moment. The first logarithmic moment is uniformly lower order.

A direct indistinguishability corollary is that if two outward transports `Q_A` and `\widetilde Q_A` both satisfy `(5)` and

\[
\frac{Q_A(s_A)}{P(s_A)}
-
\frac{\widetilde Q_A(s_A)}{P(s_A)}
\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{Q_A''(s_A)}{P''(s_A)}
-
\frac{\widetilde Q_A''(s_A)}{P''(s_A)}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{12}
\]

then

\[
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)-\widetilde\kappa_A(s_A)}
{\kappa_P(s_A)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]

Equation `(13)` is a genuine compression no-go statement: boundary log-curvature cannot distinguish two such transports from distributional displacement information that does not change the normalized zeroth or second moment at the probed scale.

## Derivation

### 1. Prime-zeta curvature suppresses the first-derivative square

The classical singular expansion of the prime zeta function at `s=1` gives, for `t\downarrow0`,

\[
P(1+t)
=
\log\frac1t+O(1),
\tag{14}
\]

\[
P'(1+t)
=
-\frac1t+O(1),
\qquad
P''(1+t)
=
\frac1{t^2}+O(1).
\tag{15}
\]

Hence, with

\[
r(t)
:=
\frac{P'(1+t)^2}
{P(1+t)P''(1+t)},
\tag{16}
\]

one has

\[
r(t)
=
O\!\left(\frac1{\log(1/t)}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]

Moreover

\[
\kappa_P(1+t)
=
\frac{P''(1+t)}{P(1+t)}
\bigl(1-r(t)\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]

This is the same derivative-scale separation used in AF-519, but no proportional transport is needed below.

### 2. Outward transport gives uniform first- and second-moment domination

Write the logarithmic support coordinate as `y=\log x`. For fixed `s>1`, the functions

\[
f_{1,s}(y)=y e^{-sy},
\qquad
f_{2,s}(y)=y^2e^{-sy}
\tag{19}
\]

satisfy

\[
f_{1,s}'(y)=e^{-sy}(1-sy),
\qquad
f_{2,s}'(y)=ye^{-sy}(2-sy).
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore `f_{1,s}` is decreasing once `y>1/s`, and `f_{2,s}` is decreasing once `y>2/s`. Since `A\to\infty` and `s_A\downarrow1`, every transported tail atom eventually lies in both decreasing regions. The unchanged finite prefix causes no issue. Consequently, for all sufficiently large `A`,

\[
0<Q_A(s_A)\le P(s_A),
\tag{21}
\]

\[
|Q_A'(s_A)|
\le
|P'(s_A)|,
\tag{22}
\]

and

\[
0\le Q_A''(s_A)\le P''(s_A).
\tag{23}
\]

In particular,

\[
0<\rho_{0,A}\le1,
\qquad
0\le\rho_{2,A}\le1.
\tag{24}
\]

The role of outwardness is now explicit: it gives `(22)` automatically, so no independent first-moment hypothesis is needed despite arbitrary nonuniform displacements.

### 3. Exact normalization leaves only two leading layers

At the observation point `s_A`, write

\[
e_A
:=
\frac{(Q_A'/Q_A)^2}
{P''/P}.
\tag{25}
\]

Using `(5)` and `(22)`,

\[
0\le e_A
\le
\frac1{c^2}
\frac{(P'/P)^2}{P''/P}
=
\frac{r(t_A)}{c^2}.
\tag{26}
\]

On the other hand, definitions `(5)` and `(6)` give the exact identity

\[
\frac{Q_A''/Q_A}{P''/P}
=
\frac{\rho_{2,A}}{\rho_{0,A}}.
\tag{27}
\]

Combining `(18)`, `(25)`, and `(27)`,

\[
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)}{\kappa_P(s_A)}
=
\frac{
\rho_{2,A}/\rho_{0,A}-e_A
}
{1-r(t_A)}.
\tag{28}
\]

Since `(24)` and `(5)` imply

\[
0\le
\frac{\rho_{2,A}}{\rho_{0,A}}
\le\frac1c,
\tag{29}
\]

we obtain the uniform estimate

\[
\left|
\frac{\kappa_A(s_A)}{\kappa_P(s_A)}
-
\frac{\rho_{2,A}}{\rho_{0,A}}
\right|
\le
\frac{c^{-1}+c^{-2}}{1-r(t_A)}\,r(t_A).
\tag{30}
\]

Equations `(17)` and `(30)` prove `(7)`. Substituting `(8)` gives `(9)`, while `(10)` immediately yields `(11)`.

The proof also shows why the full displacement law disappears. Nonproportional transport can change `Q_A`, `Q_A'`, and `Q_A''` in complicated ways, but the prime-zeta boundary singularity demotes the entire normalized first-derivative square by one logarithmic factor. After that suppression, curvature has only the value layer `Q_A/P` and second-moment layer `Q_A''/P''` left at leading order.

### 4. Oscillation changes the two defects, not the dimension of the carrier

AF-519 required limits because it stated a limiting proportional-transport law. Equation `(7)` is stronger in a different direction: it is pointwise along the chosen boundary sequence and therefore remains useful when the transport pattern oscillates with `A`.

If the pair

\[
(\rho_{0,A},\rho_{2,A})
\tag{31}
\]

has several subsequential limits, the relative curvature has the corresponding image under

\[
(x_0,x_2)\mapsto\frac{x_2}{x_0}
\tag{32}
\]

up to an error tending uniformly to zero. Thus an oscillatory displacement field does not by itself force a measure-valued observable. Distributional information may be needed to **compute** the pair `(31)` from a microscopic transport rule, but it does not survive as additional independent information in this curvature carrier at one boundary scale.

## Fidelity and matched-control audit

The theorem makes the compression layer explicit. The microscopic object is an arbitrary map

\[
p\mapsto q_{p,A}\ge p
\tag{33}
\]

on a remote prime tail. The observable is a single scalar, boundary logarithmic curvature. Between them, all leading transport information factors through

\[
Q_A(s_A)/P(s_A)
\quad\text{and}\quad
Q_A''(s_A)/P''(s_A).
\tag{34}
\]

Therefore `(34)` is a sufficient leading-order quotient for this carrier. Matching it is enough to defeat curvature discrimination even if two controls have very different pointwise displacement fields.

For literal arithmetic controls one may additionally require the atoms `q_{p,A}` to be pairwise distinct ordinary integers, or composites when a prime-versus-composite adversary is desired. Those restrictions are not used in the analytic proof. Their role is to ensure that a control is a genuine alternative arithmetic support rather than a weighted multiset produced by collisions.

The theorem does not say that `(34)` is sufficient for every observable. It says precisely that this **particular compression**, logarithmic curvature near the prime-zeta convergence boundary, cannot retain more leading-order information under outward transport.

## Representation audit

The classical identity

\[
(\log Q)''
=
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_s}(\log x)
\tag{35}
\]

for the exponentially tilted weights `d\mu_s\propto x^{-s}` identifies log-curvature as a variance, equivalently as the Hessian of a one-parameter log-partition function. This identity is standard exponential-family/cumulant mathematics and is not claimed as new.

AF-520 adds a line-specific compression statement: at the singular prime-zeta boundary, the variance normalization itself has an asymptotic hierarchy. The squared first moment is suppressed relative to the raw second moment, and outward support transport supplies the uniform domination needed to make that suppression independent of the detailed displacement field.

This is a useful distinction for Arithmetic Fidelity. A measure-valued microscopic perturbation need not require a measure-valued lift at every downstream carrier. Here the carrier itself forces a two-number quotient at leading order.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

No novelty is claimed for the standard ingredients.

- C.-E. Fröberg, **“On the prime zeta function,”** *BIT* 8 (1968), 187--202, DOI `10.1007/BF01933420`, is the classical prime-zeta reference for the singular behavior at `s=1`.
- The log-partition/cumulant identity that first derivatives give means and second derivatives give variances is standard exponential-family theory; equivalently, the Hessian of a log-partition function is the covariance of its sufficient statistic.
- Ordinary monotonicity of `y^j e^{-sy}` on the remote tail is elementary and supplies the support-transport domination in `(22)`--`(23)`.
- AF-518 proves uniform curvature invisibility for uniformly vanishing relative outward displacement. AF-519 proves the two-moment response law for scale-dependent **proportional** transport.

A focused literature search found the classical log-partition/variance framework and the standard prime-zeta boundary expansion, but did not identify a standard result stated as the uniform two-defect law `(7)` for arbitrary nonproportional outward prime-tail transport. The result is therefore recorded as an exact internal classification of this Arithmetic Fidelity carrier with `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`, not as a claim of a new theorem in the external literature.

## Boundaries and failure modes

1. **Outwardness is doing real work.** Mixed-direction or inward transport can increase the first logarithmic moment, so `(22)` may fail. Such transports require an explicit first-moment control or a different argument.
2. **Source survival is required.** If `Q_A(s_A)/P(s_A)\to0`, the normalized first-derivative square need not remain negligible after division by `Q_A`; equation `(7)` is not asserted in that regime.
3. **The theorem is boundary-scale, not full-profile.** Matching `(34)` at one sequence `s_A\downarrow1` does not imply equality of curvature for every `s>1`. A continuum of observation scales may encode more information through the scale dependence of the two moments.
4. **The two defects need not have limits.** Equation `(7)` handles oscillation pointwise; only the limiting corollary `(11)` needs `(10)`.
5. **Arithmetic identification does not follow.** A nonzero curvature defect distinguishes two sources at the chosen scale, while a zero defect exhibits a failure of this carrier. Neither outcome uniquely characterizes the rational primes and neither is evidence for RH.
6. **Lower-order information remains possible.** Matching the leading pair `(34)` removes the leading relative-curvature distinction. Terms of order `1/L_A` or smaller may still depend on first moments or finer displacement structure.

## Research consequence

AF-519 left open whether nonproportional or oscillatory transport requires a scale-dependent measure profile rather than finitely many moments. AF-520 resolves the outward, source-surviving part of that question: **the leading boundary curvature remains two-dimensional regardless of the complexity of the outward displacement field**.

The remaining frontier is now sharper. Genuine distributional information can enter only by leaving at least one of the hypotheses that force `(7)`: allow mixed/inward transport that defeats first-moment domination, approach source extinction `\rho_{0,A}\to0`, inspect lower-order terms, or enrich the observable beyond scalar log-curvature. Those are the natural places to test whether a higher-dimensional or measure-valued fidelity lift is actually necessary.
