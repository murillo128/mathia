# AF-027 — Positive positive-definite compact tests retain full-dimensional finite Weil-jet freedom

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`

## Claim

Fix `A>0` and let

\[
\mathcal D_A
=
\left\{
F\in C_c^\infty((-A,A);\mathbb R):
F(-x)=F(x),\ F(0)=1,\ F(x)\ge0,\ \widehat F(\xi)\ge0
\right\}.
\]

Thus `\mathcal D_A` is the normalized compactly supported cone of functions that are simultaneously pointwise nonnegative and positive definite on `\mathbb R`.

Let

\[
S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_M\}\subset(0,A)
\]

be distinct and let `r\ge0`. For the finite jet map

\[
J_{S,r}(F)
=
\bigl(F^{(k)}(s_j)\bigr)_{1\le j\le M,\ 0\le k\le r}
\in\mathbb R^{M(r+1)},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^{M(r+1)}} J_{S,r}(\mathcal D_A)\neq\varnothing.
}
\]

The same holds for finite-order local prime-power Weil response jets. If

\[
0<c_1<\cdots<c_N<A
\]

is a finite nonresonant generator block in the sense of AF-024,

\[
mc_j\neq c_k
\]

for all `j,k` and all integers `m\ge2` with `mc_j<A`, and

\[
G_F(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}t e^{-mt/2}F(mt),
\]

then for

\[
\mathcal R_r(F)
=
\bigl(G_F^{(k)}(c_j)\bigr)_{1\le j\le N,\ 0\le k\le r}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^{N(r+1)}}\mathcal R_r(\mathcal D_A)\neq\varnothing.
}
\]

In particular, for `c_j=\log p_j` with distinct rational primes, the nonresonance condition is automatic. Therefore adding **pointwise nonnegativity** to the even, compactly supported, normalized, Fourier-positive test class of AF-026 still does not force the finite raw jets or finite Weil-response jets into a lower-dimensional equality locus.

This closes the main cone-intersection boundary left open by AF-026: simultaneous positivity in the test domain and in the Fourier domain is a genuine nonlinear global restriction, but it is still not finite-jet rigid.

## A positive positive-definite modulation family

Choose `R` with

\[
\max S<R<A.
\]

Take a nonnegative real bump

\[
\phi\in C_c^\infty((-R/2,R/2))
\]

that is strictly positive on its interior, and define

\[
K(x)
=
\frac{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(x)}{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(0)},
\qquad
\widetilde\phi(x)=\phi(-x).
\]

Then

\[
K\in C_c^\infty((-A,A)),
\qquad
K(-x)=K(x),
\qquad
K(0)=1.
\]

Because `\phi\ge0`, convolution gives

\[
K(x)\ge0
\]

for every `x`, and because the interiors of the two bumps overlap for every translation of size `<R`,

\[
K(x)>0
\qquad(|x|<R).
\]

Also

\[
\widehat K(\omega)
=
\frac{|\widehat\phi(\omega)|^2}{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(0)}
\ge0.
\]

Thus `K` is already both pointwise nonnegative and positive definite.

Fix once and for all

\[
0<\varepsilon<1
\]

and for each real frequency `\xi` define

\[
F_\xi(x)
=
\frac{K(x)\bigl(1+\varepsilon\cos(\xi x)\bigr)}{1+\varepsilon}.
\]

The factor obeys

\[
1+\varepsilon\cos(\xi x)\ge1-\varepsilon>0,
\]

so

\[
F_\xi(x)\ge0.
\]

The normalization is exact:

\[
F_\xi(0)=1.
\]

Fourier transformation gives

\[
\widehat F_\xi(\omega)
=
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}
\left[
\widehat K(\omega)
+
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\widehat K(\omega-\xi)
+
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\widehat K(\omega+\xi)
\right]
\ge0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{F_\xi\in\mathcal D_A\quad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb R.}
\]

Equivalently, the construction uses only classical closure properties of the positive-positive-definite cone: `1+\varepsilon\cos(\xi x)` is itself nonnegative and positive definite, and products preserve positive definiteness while preserving pointwise nonnegativity.

## Full affine span of finite jets

Put

\[
d=M(r+1).
\]

Let

\[
u(\xi)=J_{S,r}\bigl(K(x)\cos(\xi x)\bigr)\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

AF-026 proves directly, by linear independence of exponential polynomials at the distinct frequencies `\pm s_j`, that

\[
\operatorname{aff}\{u(\xi):\xi\in\mathbb R\}=\mathbb R^d
\]

whenever `K(s_j)>0`. That positivity holds here because every `s_j<R`.

For the present family,

\[
J_{S,r}(F_\xi)
=
\frac{J_{S,r}(K)+\varepsilon u(\xi)}{1+\varepsilon}.
\]

This is an invertible affine rescaling of the modulation curve `u(\xi)`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{aff}\{J_{S,r}(F_\xi):\xi\in\mathbb R\}
=
\mathbb R^d.
\]

Choose `d+1` frequencies

\[
\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_d
\]

whose jet vectors are affinely independent. The class `\mathcal D_A` is convex: pointwise nonnegativity and Fourier nonnegativity are convex cone conditions, while evenness, compact support, smoothness, and the normalization `F(0)=1` are preserved by normalized convex combinations. Hence every

\[
F_w=\sum_{\ell=0}^d w_\ell F_{\xi_\ell},
\qquad
w_\ell\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_\ell w_\ell=1,
\]

lies in `\mathcal D_A`.

Its jet vector runs through the simplex generated by the affinely independent vertices. That simplex has nonempty Euclidean interior in `\mathbb R^d`, proving

\[
\operatorname{int}J_{S,r}(\mathcal D_A)\neq\varnothing.
\]

The argument is stronger than merely exhibiting many admissible tests: it shows that no nonzero affine equality among a finite collection of off-origin derivatives can hold throughout the normalized double-positive class.

## Transfer to finite Weil-response jets

For a finite nonresonant block `c_1,\ldots,c_N`, let

\[
S_c
=
\{mc_j:1\le j\le N,\ m\ge1,\ mc_j<A\}.
\]

This is finite. Apply the raw-jet theorem to all distinct points in `S_c` and all derivative orders up to `r`. The resulting raw test jets attained by `\mathcal D_A` contain an open subset of their full finite-dimensional coordinate space.

As in AF-026, differentiating

\[
G_F(t)=\sum_m a_m(t)F(mt),
\qquad
a_m(t)=t e^{-mt/2},
\]

shows that the response jet is a fixed linear function of those raw jets. The columns corresponding to the first-power samples

\[
F^{(k)}(c_j)
\]

form a block-lower-triangular submatrix whose diagonal entries are

\[
a_1(c_j)=c_j e^{-c_j/2}>0.
\]

Nonresonance ensures that no higher-power sample from another selected generator lands on a first-power center. Therefore the raw-jet-to-response-jet map has full row rank `N(r+1)` and is surjective.

A surjective linear map is open, so the open raw-jet set maps to an open response-jet set. Hence

\[
\operatorname{int}\mathcal R_r(\mathcal D_A)\neq\varnothing.
\]

For distinct rational primes, `m\log p_j=\log p_k` would imply `p_j^m=p_k`, which is impossible for `m\ge2`; therefore every finite rational-prime block satisfies the required nonresonance.

## What this rules out

AF-024 showed that unconstrained smooth tests can program arbitrary separable local germs and can manufacture exact singular point isolation. AF-025 showed that finite linear restrictions are irrelevant whenever they can be repaired inside the exact measurement kernel. AF-026 then showed that Fourier positivity still leaves full-dimensional finite jets, but explicitly left the intersection with pointwise nonnegativity unclassified.

The present result closes that finite-order escape. Even the stronger class

\[
F\ge0,
\qquad
\widehat F\ge0,
\qquad
F\text{ even},
\qquad
F(0)=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}F\Subset(-A,A)
\]

still has full-dimensional finite raw-jet and finite Weil-response-jet images.

Consequently, a finite-test RH mechanism cannot explain a rational-prime-specific finite-order identity or rank drop merely by requiring both ordinary positivity and Fourier positivity. If such a relation is claimed to be intrinsic, some additional independently justified structure must cut this full-dimensional image down to the asserted locus.

This is an obstruction to a **category-level explanation**, not to every individual test. One specially chosen double-positive test may still have an exceptional singularity at the rational-prime point. The result says that the admissible cone itself does not force such a finite-order relation.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The ambient cone is classical.

- Alexandr Borisov, **“Positive positive-definite functions and measures on locally compact abelian groups,”** arXiv:`math/9906126` (1999), studies functions and measures that are simultaneously nonnegative and positive definite and emphasizes their stability under natural constructions.
- Philippe Jaming, Máté Matolcsi, and Szilárd G. Révész, **“On the Extremal Rays of the Cone of Positive, Positive Definite Functions,”** *Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications* 15(4) (2009), 561–582, DOI `10.1007/s00041-008-9057-6`, studies the convex cone of nonnegative positive-definite functions and its extremal structure.
- The autocorrelation, Fourier-shift, and positive-definite-product ingredients are classical harmonic analysis and already sourced in AF-026 through Bochner theory and compactly supported positive-definite kernels.

No novelty is claimed for the positive-positive-definite cone, autocorrelations, products of positive-definite functions, cosine characters, convexity, Fourier shifts, or exponential-polynomial independence.

A targeted search of positive-positive-definite / doubly-positive interpolation and compact-support literature did not identify a standard theorem stated as the exact finite off-origin jet-interior result above, nor its specialization to prime-power Weil responses. That absence is not evidence of novelty. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the category audit obtained by combining classical cone closure with AF-026's modulation-span mechanism: **simultaneous positivity on both sides of the Fourier transform still leaves full-dimensional freedom at every finite Weil-jet level.**

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Full-dimensional image does not imply surjectivity. Both positivity conditions impose genuine inequalities, including `0\le F(x)\le F(0)=1` for this normalized class.
- The result is finite-order. It does not classify full germs, infinite jets, analytic-transform identities, complete test families, or Paley–Wiener-type global constraints.
- The theorem does not reproduce AF-024's exact source-tuned quadratic isolator inside `\mathcal D_A`; it proves only that the admissible finite-jet image has nonempty interior.
- The tests are allowed to vary throughout the full double-positive class. A fixed finite-dimensional operator-generated family, one canonically selected test, or a class satisfying additional differential/integral equations may be much more rigid.
- The argument chooses an autocorrelation seed with support large enough to cover the finite sample set. That is legitimate for class-level non-rigidity but does not provide a single universal seed for all finite windows simultaneously.
- Nonresonance is used in the transparent surjectivity proof for Weil-response jets. Resonant generalized-prime controls require grouping coincident prime-power sample locations and auditing the resulting rank separately.
- The result concerns finite local source information. It does not establish or refute any zero-location, multiplicity, simplicity, or RH statement.

## Decisive audit test

For a finite-test route whose admissibility includes both pointwise and Fourier positivity:

1. identify every additional restriction beyond membership in `\mathcal D_A`;
2. compute the induced finite raw-jet or Weil-response-jet image under those additional restrictions;
3. if that image still has interior, no universal finite-order equality or rank-drop condition is forced by the declared category;
4. if the additional structure collapses the image to a lower-dimensional locus, identify the exact theorem or operator relation causing that collapse;
5. test the same relation at matched generalized-prime controls before interpreting the singular locus as rational-prime-specific;
6. only then ask whether the remaining singularity or equality survives strongly enough to support an RH-relevant downstream operation.

## Consequence for the line

The finite-Weil-test admissibility hierarchy is now sharper:

\[
C_c^\infty
\supset
\text{finite linear constraints}
\supset
\text{Fourier-positive normalized tests}
\supset
\boxed{\text{pointwise-positive + Fourier-positive normalized tests}}.
\]

None of these classes is finite-jet rigid at a finite nonresonant prime block.

The next meaningful escape is therefore no longer another generic positivity adjective. It must use structure that is substantially smaller than the full positive-positive-definite cone: for example a fixed transform family, a canonical operator-generated subspace/cone, a differential or functional equation, an extremal-ray selection principle, or genuinely infinite-order/global data. Any such proposal should first be audited for whether its finite or infinite response image is actually lower-dimensional for a source-independent reason.
