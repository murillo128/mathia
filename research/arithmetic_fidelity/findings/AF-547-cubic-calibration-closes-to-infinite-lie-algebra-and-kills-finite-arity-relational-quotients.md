# AF-547 — Cubic calibration closes to an infinite Lie algebra and kills finite-arity relational quotients

## Status

`EXACT-DERIVED` · `CLASSICAL-INGREDIENTS` · `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` · `CALIBRATION` · `SHARED-NUISANCE` · `EVALUATION-RANK` · `LIE-CLOSURE` · `MINIMAL-RELATIONAL-LIFT` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-546 found the exact finite-branch hierarchy

\[
\text{translations}\to 2\text{-point differences},\qquad
\text{affine}\to 3\text{-point ratios},\qquad
\text{projective/Riccati}\to 4\text{-point cross-ratios}.
\]

A natural extrapolation would be to add one more matched branch for a cubic shared-calibration field and search for a five-point invariant. That extrapolation fails for a structural reason.

For a class of admissible scalar decoder fields `\mathcal V`, the relevant object is not the pointwise polynomial degree of one field but the **Lie closure** generated by all admissible fields. If that closure can prescribe arbitrary velocities at a finite configuration of matched branches, then every differentiable finite-branch relational invariant is locally constant.

For the polynomial nuisance classes

\[
\mathfrak p_d
:=
\operatorname{span}\{Q^k\partial_Q:0\le k\le d\},
\tag{1}
\]

the boundary is exact:

- `d=0,1,2` are Lie algebras and give precisely the translation, affine, and projective rungs of AF-546;
- every `d\ge3` fails Lie closure, and its Lie closure contains **all polynomial vector fields** `Q^k\partial_Q`, `k\ge0`;
- on any finite set of distinct branches, polynomial interpolation then makes the diagonal orbit tangent space full-dimensional;
- therefore an unrestricted cubic-or-higher polynomial calibration family has **no nonconstant universal relational invariant of any finite arity**.

The same no-go holds a fortiori for unrestricted smooth monotone calibration: finitely many point values cannot quotient an arbitrary common one-dimensional diffeomorphism. More matched scalar branches do not continue the `2,3,4,...` hierarchy past the projective case.

This does **not** say that every particular cubic decoder destroys all relational information. A fixed or specially constrained cubic field may generate only a finite-dimensional Lie algebra and can even be straightened locally to translation. The obstruction is the freedom of the admissible nuisance family, measured by its Lie closure and finite-configuration evaluation rank.

## Evaluation-rank criterion

Let `I` be an interval and

\[
\operatorname{Conf}_n^{<}(I)
=
\{(Q_1,\ldots,Q_n)\in I^n:Q_1<\cdots<Q_n\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For a scalar vector field

\[
X_v=v(Q)\partial_Q,
\]

the common action on `n` matched branches is the diagonal prolongation

\[
X_v^{[n]}
=
\sum_{i=1}^n v(Q_i)\partial_{Q_i}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let `\mathfrak g` be the Lie algebra generated by the admissible nuisance fields. A `C^1` relational observable

\[
F:\operatorname{Conf}_n^{<}(I)\to\mathbb R
\]

that is invariant under every admissible common flow must satisfy

\[
X_v^{[n]}F=0
\qquad
\text{for every }X_v\in\mathfrak g.
\tag{4}
\]

It is enough initially to impose `(4)` on generators: if `XF=YF=0`, then

\[
[X,Y]F=X(YF)-Y(XF)=0,
\tag{5}
\]

so every invariant is automatically annihilated by the full Lie closure.

At a configuration `Q=(Q_1,\ldots,Q_n)`, define the evaluation space

\[
E_Q(\mathfrak g)
:=
\operatorname{span}
\{(v(Q_1),\ldots,v(Q_n)):X_v\in\mathfrak g\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^n.
\tag{6}
\]

Equation `(4)` says exactly that `dF_Q` annihilates `E_Q(\mathfrak g)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}E_Q(\mathfrak g)=n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
dF_Q=0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

More generally, at a regular point the number of functionally independent local scalar invariants is at most

\[
n-\operatorname{rank}E_Q(\mathfrak g).
\tag{8}
\]

This is the finite-branch fidelity test: **a relational lift exists only in directions transverse to the nuisance orbit on configuration space**. Branch count by itself is not the controlling quantity.

## AF-546 recovered from Vandermonde rank

For `d\le2`, `\mathfrak p_d` in `(1)` is closed under brackets. On `n` distinct branches its evaluation matrix is the Vandermonde-type matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & Q_1 & \cdots & Q_1^d\\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots\\
1 & Q_n & \cdots & Q_n^d
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{9}
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{rank}E_Q(\mathfrak p_d)=\min(n,d+1).
\tag{10}
\]

Thus no nonconstant local invariant can occur with `n\le d+1`. At `n=d+2` the generic orbit has codimension one, and the familiar invariant appears:

- `d=0`: two branches, one difference;
- `d=1`: three branches, one affine ratio;
- `d=2`: four branches, one cross-ratio.

This gives an algebraic explanation of AF-546's minimal arities. The `2,3,4` sequence works because the corresponding nuisance spaces are finite-dimensional **Lie algebras**, not merely because they contain polynomials of increasing degree.

## Why the hierarchy stops at cubic order

For monomial vector fields

\[
X_m:=Q^m\partial_Q,
\]

the bracket is

\[
\boxed{
[X_m,X_n]=(n-m)X_{m+n-1}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

For `d\le2`, the span of `X_0,\ldots,X_d` is closed. At `d=2`, the only apparent degree-three bracket would be `[X_2,X_2]`, which vanishes.

At cubic order the situation changes immediately:

\[
[X_2,X_3]=X_4,
\tag{12}
\]

and then, for every `k\ge3`,

\[
[X_2,X_k]=(k-2)X_{k+1}.
\tag{13}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathfrak p_d)
\supseteq
\operatorname{span}\{X_k:k\ge0\}
\qquad(d\ge3),
}
\tag{14}
\]

and equality holds with the polynomial vector-field algebra generated by the stated monomials. The closure is infinite-dimensional.

Now fix any finite `n` and any distinct `Q_1,\ldots,Q_n`. The Lie closure contains `X_0,\ldots,X_{n-1}`. Their evaluation matrix is the square Vandermonde matrix

\[
\big(Q_i^{k}\big)_{1\le i\le n,\,0\le k\le n-1},
\]

whose determinant is

\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(Q_j-Q_i)\neq0.
\tag{15}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}E_Q(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathfrak p_d))=n
\qquad(d\ge3),
\tag{16}
\]

and `(7)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{no nonconstant }C^1\text{ universal finite-branch invariant exists.}
}
\tag{17}
\]

This is stronger than AF-546's observation that a cubic term moves the four-point cross-ratio. It rules out the obvious repair of simply adding a fifth, sixth, or any fixed finite number of matched branches when the nuisance class allows arbitrary cubic coefficients.

## Exact five-branch falsification of the naive continuation

The first failed extension is already visible with five branches.

If one ignores Lie closure and looks only at the four instantaneous generators

\[
X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3,
\]

their evaluation matrix on five distinct points has rank `4`. A naive dimension count therefore leaves one transverse direction and suggests that a five-point scalar invariant might exist.

But invariance under `X_2` and `X_3` forces invariance under their commutator `X_4` by `(5)` and `(12)`. The five fields

\[
X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4
\]

have a nonsingular `5\times5` Vandermonde evaluation matrix. The apparent transverse direction disappears.

Thus the five-point candidate is not merely hard to find; **there is no differentiable universal five-point invariant to find** for the unrestricted cubic nuisance class.

This control identifies the exact mistake in the naive `degree + 2` extrapolation: it counts generators but not the transformations generated by composing them.

## Unrestricted smooth calibration

The polynomial result is already decisive for cubic nuisance, but the same criterion gives the full smooth boundary.

Let `\mathfrak X(I)` be the smooth scalar vector fields on `I`. Given distinct points `Q_1,\ldots,Q_n` and arbitrary desired velocities `a_1,\ldots,a_n`, one can choose a smooth function `v` with

\[
v(Q_i)=a_i
\qquad(1\le i\le n),
\tag{18}
\]

for example by local bump interpolation. Therefore

\[
E_Q(\mathfrak X(I))=\mathbb R^n
\tag{19}
\]

for every finite nondegenerate configuration. Equation `(7)` again forces every `C^1` finite-point invariant to be locally constant.

Equivalently, the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism pseudogroup of an interval can move any finite strictly ordered configuration to any other such configuration. Apart from discrete order/collision data, finitely many scalar point values carry no universal invariant of an unrestricted monotone recalibration.

This separates two kinds of compression repair:

1. **finite-dimensional nuisance** can sometimes be quotiented by retaining enough matched branches;
2. **unrestricted calibration freedom** cannot be repaired merely by adding finitely many scalar branches.

The second case requires different retained structure: a restriction or model for the nuisance, calibration anchors, derivative/jet information tied to additional structure, a reconstructible calibration law, or some other marking that changes the action being quotiented. This finding does not assert that any particular such lift is sufficient.

## Important control: a cubic term alone is not the obstruction

Take a fixed nonvanishing cubic field

\[
W(Q)\partial_Q
\]

and allow only

\[
V(Q,u)=a(u)W(Q).
\tag{20}
\]

Its Lie algebra is one-dimensional. On an interval where `W\neq0`, define the straightened coordinate

\[
T(Q)=\int^Q\frac{ds}{W(s)}.
\tag{21}
\]

Then

\[
\partial_u T(Q_i(u))=a(u),
\]

so

\[
T(Q_2)-T(Q_1)
\tag{22}
\]

is an exact two-branch invariant.

Therefore the statement “cubic fields have no finite relational invariant” would be false. The correct discriminator is

\[
\boxed{
\text{nuisance family}\longrightarrow
\text{Lie closure}\longrightarrow
\text{configuration evaluation rank}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Polynomial degree is useful only insofar as it determines that closure for the admissible family.

This also supplies a practical audit rule for future Arithmetic Fidelity candidates: before inventing a higher-arity quotient, compute the Lie closure of the nuisance transformations and its diagonal rank on the proposed retained configuration.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The mathematical ingredients are classical, and no novelty is claimed for them.

- The bracket `(11)` is the standard polynomial/Witt vector-field relation. The projective subalgebra generated by the constant, linear, and quadratic fields is the familiar `\mathfrak{sl}_2` sector of the Witt algebra.
- Lie's classical local classification of finite-dimensional algebras of vector fields on the line gives the same endpoint: one-dimensional/nilpotent, affine solvable, and `\mathfrak{sl}_2` semisimple cases. A modern account explicitly reconstructing that classification is H. Azad, I. Biswas and S. W. Shah, *Lie's classification of finite dimensional algebras of Vector Fields in C^N* (2026), Section 3: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24948 .
- The Lie-Scheffers theorem states that a nonautonomous first-order system admits a finite superposition rule exactly when its time-dependent vector fields lie in a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. A modern statement appears in J. de Lucas et al., *Geometric Models for Lie-Hamilton Systems on R2*, Mathematics 7 (2019), 1053: https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/7/11/1053 . Riccati equations are the standard one-dimensional projective example.
- The infinite-dimensional Witt/Virasoro vector-field framework is classical; a modern open treatment of Witt realizations is Q. Huang and R. Zhdanov, *Realizations of the Witt and Virasoro Algebras and Integrable Equations* (2020): https://doi.org/10.1080/14029251.2020.1683964 .

The durable Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the synthesis and exact placement of these classical facts after AF-545/AF-546:

1. configuration-space **evaluation rank** is the local criterion for whether a finite matched-branch relational lift can retain anything after a shared nuisance;
2. the AF-546 `2,3,4` hierarchy is recovered from Vandermonde rank plus Lie closure;
3. the cubic step is shown to be a phase change rather than the next rung: closure becomes infinite-dimensional and finite arity ceases to help;
4. the correct object to classify in future compression problems is the transformation family's Lie closure, not the apparent complexity of one representative transformation.

No claim is made that this general invariant-theoretic mechanism is new outside Mathia.

## Boundaries

- The no-go concerns **universal finite-point relational invariants** under the stated nuisance family. It does not rule out recovery when the nuisance is known, estimable, anchored, or otherwise restricted.
- A particular time-dependent cubic field can generate a much smaller Lie algebra than the unrestricted cubic family; `(20)` is an explicit control.
- The smooth-diffeomorphism statement is local on the noncollision configuration space. Discrete order type survives orientation-preserving recalibration, but it is not the metric/quantitative relational fidelity sought here.
- The argument does not classify what infinite configurations, jets, marked derivatives, external anchors, or source-coupled structures may retain. Those are possible lifts to study separately.
- The analytic finite-dimensional classification cited above is prior-art orientation, not needed for the direct polynomial no-go: equations `(11)`–`(17)` are self-contained.
- Nothing here uses special arithmetic properties or the rational primes. This is an abstract compression/fidelity result, as required by the line mandate; arithmetic application should come only after the transformation theory is sufficiently understood.
