# AF-221 — prime-tail mesh rigidifies faithful translation coordinates

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `FULL-EULER-GATE`, `COORDINATE-RIGIDITY`, `EXACT-FIDELITY`, `CLASSICAL-BRIDGE`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-220 gives a finite-order Euler sign-regularity certificate whose quantitative resource includes separation in the canonical source coordinate

\[
v_q:=-\log\log q.
\]

On consecutive rational primes that source mesh tends to zero, so the AF-220 certificate necessarily degenerates in the tail. A natural apparent escape is to replace `v_q` by a nonlinear coordinate that spreads the prime tail while preserving a one-dimensional translation/Toeplitz kernel. That escape is impossible under exact faithful reparameterization.

Let `S\subset\mathbb R`, let `f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R` be nonconstant, and suppose the additive subgroup

\[
G:=\langle S-S\rangle
\]

generated by source differences is dense in `\mathbb R`. Let

\[
\Phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
\]

be continuous, let `\Psi:S\to\mathbb R`, and let `F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R` be injective. If

\[
\boxed{
 f(u-v)=F\!\big(\Phi(u)-\Psi(v)\big)
 \qquad(u\in\mathbb R,\ v\in S),
}
\tag{1}
\]

then there exist constants `a\ne0`, `b`, and `c` such that

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(u)=au+b,
\qquad
\Psi(v)=av+c.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus a faithful scalar translation profile has only a common affine coordinate gauge once the source differences generate a dense subgroup. If `\Phi` is order preserving, then necessarily `a>0`.

For the Euler logarithm

\[
f(t):=-\log(1-e^{-e^t}),
\tag{3}
\]

and rational-prime source set

\[
S_{\mathbb P}:=\{-\log\log p:p\text{ prime}\},
\tag{4}
\]

the density hypothesis holds. Hence every exact representation

\[
-\log(1-p^{-s})
=F\!\left(
\Phi(\log s)-\Psi(-\log\log p)
\right)
\tag{5}
\]

with continuous sample coordinate and injective retained translation profile satisfies `(2)`. In particular, for consecutive primes `p<q`,

\[
\boxed{
|\Psi(v_p)-\Psi(v_q)|
=|a|\,|v_p-v_q|
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore the vanishing prime-tail mesh in AF-220 cannot be repaired by a nonlinear exact coordinate warp while remaining inside the same faithful one-dimensional translation-kernel category. Any tail-uniform rescue must change the retained category, retain extra marks/relations, or derive a stronger source restriction; it cannot come merely from stretching coordinates.

## Derivation

### 1. Injectivity of the retained profile produces one common difference coordinate

For `t\in\mathbb R`, choose any `v\in S` and define

\[
H(t):=\Phi(t+v)-\Psi(v).
\tag{7}
\]

This is well defined. If `v,w\in S`, equation `(1)` gives

\[
F(\Phi(t+v)-\Psi(v))=f(t)
=F(\Phi(t+w)-\Psi(w)),
\]

and injectivity of `F` forces equality of the two arguments. Fixing one `v` in `(7)` also shows that `H` is continuous.

Equation `(1)` now factors as

\[
f(t)=F(H(t)),
\tag{8}
\]

while the original coordinates satisfy

\[
H(u-v)=\Phi(u)-\Psi(v).
\tag{9}
\]

### 2. Every source difference acts on `H` by a constant increment

Take `v,w\in S` and put `d=v-w`. From `(7)`, for every `x\in\mathbb R`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(x+d)-H(x)
&=\big(\Phi(x+v)-\Psi(w)\big)
  -\big(\Phi(x+w)-\Psi(w)\big)\\
&=\Phi(x+v)-\Phi(x+w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The well-definedness identity at the same argument `x` gives

\[
\Phi(x+v)-\Psi(v)
=\Phi(x+w)-\Psi(w),
\]

so `(10)` becomes

\[
\boxed{
H(x+d)-H(x)=\Psi(v)-\Psi(w),
}
\tag{11}
\]

independent of `x`.

By composing and reversing such translations, every `g\in G=\langle S-S\rangle` therefore has a constant increment

\[
H(x+g)-H(x)=A(g),
\tag{12}
\]

where `A:G\to\mathbb R` is additive. The definition through `(12)` makes `A` independent of any chosen decomposition of `g` into source differences.

### 3. Density plus continuity forces the Cauchy increment to be linear

For a fixed `x_0`,

\[
A(g)=H(x_0+g)-H(x_0).
\tag{13}
\]

Hence `A(g)\to0` whenever `g\to0` in `G`, by continuity of `H`. Thus `A` is a continuous additive function on the dense subgroup `G`.

The classical continuous Cauchy-equation rigidity now gives a constant `a\in\mathbb R` such that

\[
A(g)=ag
\qquad(g\in G).
\tag{14}
\]

For arbitrary `d\in\mathbb R`, choose `g_n\in G` with `g_n\to d`. Passing to the limit in `(12)` yields

\[
H(x+d)-H(x)=ad
\qquad(x,d\in\mathbb R).
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore

\[
H(t)=at+d_0.
\tag{16}
\]

Substituting `(16)` into `(9)` gives

\[
\Phi(u)-\Psi(v)=a(u-v)+d_0.
\tag{17}
\]

Fixing any source point shows that `\Phi(u)-au` is constant in `u`; comparing source points then shows that `\Psi(v)-av` is constant in `v`. This is `(2)`.

Finally `a\ne0`: if `a=0`, then `H` is constant and `(8)` makes the nonconstant `f` constant, a contradiction.

The functional-equation core is classical Pexider/Cauchy rigidity. The point here is the exact admissibility consequence for a compression category: once all source translations from a dense generated difference group must remain compatible with one injective scalar profile, coordinate freedom collapses to affine gauge.

### 4. Rational-prime log-log coordinates generate a dense difference group

For a prime `p`, write

\[
v_p=-\log\log p.
\tag{18}
\]

If `p<q` are consecutive primes, Bertrand's postulate gives `q<2p`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<v_p-v_q
&=\log\frac{\log q}{\log p}\\
&<\log\!\left(1+\frac{\log2}{\log p}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus `S_{\mathbb P}-S_{\mathbb P}` contains nonzero positive elements arbitrarily close to zero. An additive subgroup of `\mathbb R` with arbitrarily small positive elements is dense: given `x` and `\varepsilon>0`, choose `0<g<2\varepsilon` in the subgroup and an integer `m` nearest to `x/g`; then `|x-mg|\le g/2<\varepsilon`.

Therefore

\[
\overline{\langle S_{\mathbb P}-S_{\mathbb P}\rangle}=\mathbb R,
\tag{20}
\]

and the rigidity theorem applies to `(5)`. Equation `(6)` follows immediately from `(2)` and `(19)`.

### 5. The density hypothesis is load bearing

The theorem is false for a uniformly discrete translation source. Take `S=\mathbb Z` and choose

\[
H(x)=x+\varepsilon\sin(2\pi x),
\qquad
0<|\varepsilon|<\frac1{2\pi}.
\tag{21}
\]

Then `H` is a nonlinear increasing homeomorphism and

\[
H(x-n)=H(x)-n
\qquad(n\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{22}
\]

For the injective Euler profile `(3)`, set

\[
\Phi=H,
\qquad
\Psi(n)=n,
\qquad
F(z)=f(H^{-1}(z)).
\tag{23}
\]

The profile `F` is injective and

\[
F(\Phi(u)-\Psi(n))
=f(H^{-1}(H(u)-n))
=f(u-n).
\tag{24}
\]

Thus an exact nonlinear coordinate gauge exists on the lattice. What removes this periodic freedom for the rational-prime Euler source is not primality as a label but the asymptotically fine log-log difference structure.

## Exact controls and boundaries

### Exact full translation covariance is essential

The hypothesis `(1)` holds for every real sample coordinate `u` and every declared source point. AF-221 does not forbid a nonlinear change of variables tailored to one finite matrix, a finite sample set, or an approximate representation. Such a construction belongs to a different fidelity question and needs its own stability audit.

### Injectivity of `F` is essential

The proof uses injectivity to make the hidden difference coordinate `H` well defined. A noninjective `F` can introduce aliases and therefore more coordinate freedom, but that does not improve exact discriminator fidelity: distinct hidden differences are then deliberately collapsed by the retained scalar profile.

### Continuity is a real regularity gate

Without continuity, additive Cauchy pathologies can survive on dense groups. The theorem concerns continuous geometric/analytic reparameterizations of the Euler translation coordinate, not arbitrary set-theoretic encodings.

### Dense source differences, not primality itself, force rigidity

The positive theorem applies to every source set whose generated difference subgroup is dense. Rational primes enter only through `(19)`. Conversely the lattice construction `(21)`--`(24)` shows that discrete source groups can retain a nonlinear periodic gauge.

### Affine rescaling does not repair tail conditioning

Equation `(6)` shows that every admissible exact coordinate change multiplies consecutive-prime gaps by one fixed factor. It cannot turn a vanishing tail mesh into a positive uniform lower separation.

### Leaving the translation category remains open

A nonstationary kernel, marked source, vector-valued observable, cross-level relation, or other enrichment may preserve additional prime information. AF-221 rules out only the pure coordinate-stretching escape inside the faithful scalar translation category.
