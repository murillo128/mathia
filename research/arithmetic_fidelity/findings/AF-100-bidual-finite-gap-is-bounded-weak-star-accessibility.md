# AF-100 — The bidual/finite gap is bounded pointwise weak-star accessibility

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `STRUCTURAL-CLASSIFICATION`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY-REFINEMENT`, `CLASSICAL-DUALITY-MECHANISM`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

Let `X` and `K` be Banach spaces and let

\[
J_K:K\longrightarrow K^{**}
\tag{1}
\]

be the canonical embedding. Equip `\mathcal L(X,K^{**})` with the topology `\tau_{pw^*}` of pointwise weak-star convergence: a net `U_\alpha` converges to `U` when

\[
\langle U_\alpha x,k^*\rangle
\longrightarrow
\langle Ux,k^*\rangle
\qquad
(x\in X,\ k^*\in K^*).
\tag{2}
\]

For `C\ge0`, define the bounded `K`-valued accessibility envelope

\[
\mathcal A_C(X,K)
:=
\overline{
\{J_KS:S\in\mathcal L(X,K),\ \|S\|\le C\}
}^{\,\tau_{pw^*}}
\subseteq\mathcal L(X,K^{**}),
\tag{3}
\]

and the associated extended gauge

\[
a_K(U)
:=
\inf\{C\ge0:U\in\mathcal A_C(X,K)\},
\qquad
\inf\varnothing:=+\infty.
\tag{4}
\]

Then the following hold.

### 1. Unbudgeted pointwise weak-star observation forgets target range completely

The embedded `K`-valued operator space is dense:

\[
\boxed{
\overline{J_K\mathcal L(X,K)}^{\,\tau_{pw^*}}
=
\mathcal L(X,K^{**}).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus no finite family of exact matrix coefficients

\[
U\longmapsto
\bigl(\langle Ux_i,k_j^*\rangle\bigr)_{i,j}
\tag{6}
\]

can distinguish an arbitrary bidual-valued operator from all `K`-valued operators if no uniform norm budget is imposed. The obstruction, when one exists, is therefore quantitative rather than finite-observational.

### 2. The exact quantitative obstruction is the bounded accessibility gauge

Every `\tau_{pw^*}`-continuous linear functional has finite matrix-coefficient form

\[
\Phi(U)
=
\sum_{j=1}^m
\langle Ux_j,k_j^*\rangle.
\tag{7}
\]

For nonzero `\Phi`, set

\[
h_K(\Phi)
:=
\sup_{\|S\|\le1}
|\Phi(J_KS)|.
\tag{8}
\]

Then `h_K(\Phi)>0`, and

\[
\boxed{
 a_K(U)
 =
 \sup_{0\ne\Phi\in(\mathcal L(X,K^{**}),\tau_{pw^*})'}
 \frac{|\Phi(U)|}{h_K(\Phi)}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Equivalently, writing `\Phi` explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
 a_K(U)
 =
 \sup
 \frac{
 \left|\sum_{j=1}^m\langle Ux_j,k_j^*\rangle\right|
 }{
 \sup_{\|S\|\le1}
 \left|\sum_{j=1}^m k_j^*(Sx_j)\right|
 },
}
\tag{10}
\]

where the supremum ranges over all nonzero finite matrix-coefficient functionals.

Moreover

\[
\boxed{\|U\|\le a_K(U),}
\tag{11}
\]

with equality for every genuinely `K`-valued operator `U=J_KS`.

### 3. AF-098's finite interpolation cost is exactly minimum bidual accessibility cost

Now let `Z\subseteq X` be a closed linear subspace and let

\[
T\in\mathcal L(Z,K).
\tag{12}
\]

Retain AF-098's quantities

\[
e_E(T)
:=
\inf\{\|S\|:S\in\mathcal L(X,K),\ S|_E=T|_E\},
\tag{13}
\]

\[
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
:=
\sup_{\substack{E\subset Z\\\dim E<\infty}}e_E(T),
\tag{14}
\]

and

\[
\lambda_{**}(T)
:=
\inf\{\|U\|:U\in\mathcal L(X,K^{**}),\ U|_Z=J_KT\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
=
\inf_{\substack{U\in\mathcal L(X,K^{**})\\U|_Z=J_KT}}
a_K(U).
}
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (11) and (16) recovers AF-098's inequality

\[
\lambda_{**}(T)\le\lambda_{\rm fin}(T),
\tag{17}
\]

but now identifies the exact missing structure. `\lambda_{**}` asks only for a small-norm bidual-valued recovery. `\lambda_{\rm fin}` asks for a bidual recovery that is also reachable, in every finite matrix coefficient simultaneously in the net sense, by **one uniformly norm-bounded family of genuine `K`-valued operators**.

Hence the possible gap

\[
\lambda_{**}(T)<\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
\tag{18}
\]

is precisely a bounded target-accessibility defect. It is not merely the vague fact that a compactness limit may land in `K^{**}` rather than `K`.

The reusable Arithmetic Fidelity boundary is

\[
\boxed{
\text{unbounded finite weak-star observation loses target range, while uniform norm control can retain it.}
}
\tag{19}
\]

## Derivation

### 1. Finite matrix coefficients are the complete continuous dual

The topology `\tau_{pw^*}` is generated by the scalar seminorms

\[
U\mapsto|\langle Ux,k^*\rangle|,
\qquad x\in X,\ k^*\in K^*.
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore every continuous linear functional depends on only finitely many such coordinates and has the form (7).

Suppose a nonzero `\Phi` of the form (7) vanished on every `J_KS`. Let

\[
E=\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}.
\tag{21}
\]

After choosing a basis `e_1,\ldots,e_n` of `E`, rewrite

\[
\Phi(U)=\sum_{i=1}^n\langle Ue_i,\psi_i\rangle
\tag{22}
\]

for suitable `\psi_i\in K^*`. Since `\Phi\ne0`, at least one `\psi_i` is nonzero. Choose vectors `y_i\in K` so that

\[
\sum_i\psi_i(y_i)\ne0
\tag{23}
\]

and define `A:E\to K` by `Ae_i=y_i`.

Because `E` is finite-dimensional, choose a basis with coordinate functionals, extend those scalar coordinate functionals to `X` by Hahn--Banach, and thereby extend `A` to some bounded finite-rank

\[
S:X\to K.
\tag{24}
\]

Then

\[
\Phi(J_KS)
=\sum_i\psi_i(y_i)\ne0,
\tag{25}
\]

contradicting the assumed annihilation. Thus the annihilator of `J_K\mathcal L(X,K)` in the continuous dual is zero. Hahn--Banach separation for locally convex spaces gives (5), and the same argument proves `h_K(\Phi)>0` for every nonzero `\Phi`.

This density has no norm control: the finite-rank extension in (24) may require a norm that grows with the finite source slice being matched.

### 2. Bipolar separation gives the accessibility gauge

The set `\mathcal A_1(X,K)` is closed, convex, balanced, and contains zero. Since

\[
\mathcal A_C=C\mathcal A_1
\qquad(C>0),
\tag{26}
\]

`a_K` is its extended Minkowski gauge.

For every continuous `\Phi`, continuity implies that closure does not change its support function:

\[
\sup_{V\in\mathcal A_1}|\Phi(V)|
=
\sup_{\|S\|\le1}|\Phi(J_KS)|
=h_K(\Phi).
\tag{27}
\]

The bipolar/Hahn--Banach gauge formula for a closed absolutely convex set therefore yields (9), hence (10).

If `U\in\mathcal A_C`, every approximating `S` satisfies

\[
|\langle J_KSx,k^*\rangle|
\le
C\|x\|\|k^*\|.
\tag{28}
\]

Passing to the pointwise weak-star limit gives the same inequality for `U`, so `\|U\|\le C`. Taking the infimum proves (11). If `U=J_KS`, then `U\in\mathcal A_{\|S\|}`, while `\|U\|=\|S\|`; hence equality holds.

### 3. Any accessible bidual recovery bounds finite interpolation

Let `U:X\to K^{**}` satisfy

\[
U|_Z=J_KT.
\tag{29}
\]

Every finite matrix coefficient on `\mathcal L(Z,K)`,

\[
\phi(A)=\sum_{j=1}^m k_j^*(Az_j),
\qquad z_j\in Z,
\tag{30}
\]

extends tautologically to a `\tau_{pw^*}`-continuous functional on `\mathcal L(X,K^{**})` by the same formula. For that extension `\widetilde\phi`,

\[
\widetilde\phi(U)=\phi(T)
\tag{31}
\]

and

\[
h_K(\widetilde\phi)
=
\sup_{\|S\|\le1}|\phi(S|_Z)|
=\|\phi\circ R\|,
\tag{32}
\]

where

\[
R:\mathcal L(X,K)\to\mathcal L(Z,K)
\tag{33}
\]

is restriction.

AF-099 proved

\[
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
=
\sup_{0\ne\phi}
\frac{|\phi(T)|}{\|\phi\circ R\|}
\tag{34}
\]

over finite matrix coefficients `\phi`. Equations (9), (31), and (32) therefore imply

\[
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)\le a_K(U).
\tag{35}
\]

Taking the infimum over all bidual extensions gives one inequality in (16).

### 4. Uniform finite interpolation constructs an accessible bidual recovery

Assume

\[
c:=\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)<\infty
\tag{36}
\]

and fix `C>c`. AF-099 also proved that, for

\[
D_C
:=
R\bigl(CB_{\mathcal L(X,K)}\bigr),
\tag{37}
\]

one has

\[
T\in\overline{D_C}^{\,\mathrm{WOT}}.
\tag{38}
\]

Choose a net `S_\alpha:X\to K` with

\[
\|S_\alpha\|\le C
\tag{39}
\]

and

\[
S_\alpha|_Z\longrightarrow T
\quad\text{in WOT}.
\tag{40}
\]

The family `J_KS_\alpha` lies in the product of weak-star compact balls

\[
\prod_{x\in X}C\|x\|B_{K^{**}}.
\tag{41}
\]

Banach--Alaoglu and Tychonoff therefore give a pointwise weak-star convergent subnet. Its limit

\[
U:X\to K^{**}
\tag{42}
\]

is linear, bounded by `C`, and belongs to `\mathcal A_C(X,K)` by construction. Equation (40) forces

\[
U|_Z=J_KT.
\tag{43}
\]

Thus

\[
\inf_{U|_Z=J_KT}a_K(U)\le C.
\tag{44}
\]

Letting `C\downarrow c` gives the reverse inequality in (16). If `\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)=+\infty`, equation (35) already forces every bidual recovery to have infinite accessibility gauge, so (16) still holds with extended values.

## Exact controls

### Genuine target-valued recovery

If `T` has a global `K`-valued extension `S:X\to K`, then `U=J_KS` satisfies

\[
a_K(U)=\|S\|.
\tag{45}
\]

Therefore (16) is compatible with

\[
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)\le\lambda_K(T)
\tag{46}
\]

from AF-098. The accessibility gauge introduces no artificial penalty on operators already living in the intended target category.

### Finite-dimensional source

If `X` is finite-dimensional, then

\[
\boxed{a_K(U)=\|U\|\quad\text{for every }U:X\to K^{**}.}
\tag{47}
\]

Indeed `U(X)` is finite-dimensional. The principle of local reflexivity, applied to `U(X)\subset K^{**}` and to the finite family of target dual functionals defining a basic `\tau_{pw^*}` neighborhood, supplies an almost isometric map from `U(X)` into `K` preserving those pairings. Composing it with `U` gives a genuine `K`-valued operator on all of `X` with norm at most `(1+\varepsilon)\|U\|` and the prescribed matrix coefficients. Hence `a_K(U)\le\|U\|`; combine with (11).

This control identifies where the infinite-dimensional difficulty enters. Local reflexivity repairs the finite-dimensional **range slice** of a bidual operator, but when `X` is infinite-dimensional one must additionally realize those repairs as globally bounded operators on the entire source. That uniform global realization is exactly what `a_K` measures.

### Dual or reflexive target

If `K` is a dual or reflexive Banach space, AF-098 proved

\[
\lambda_{**}(T)
=
\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
=
\lambda_K(T).
\tag{48}
\]

The present theorem is consistent with that collapse: a norm-one retraction `K^{**}\to K` bypasses any optimized accessibility penalty by converting a bidual recovery directly into a target-valued recovery. Equation (48) does **not** imply that `a_K(U)=\|U\|` for every unrelated bidual-valued operator `U`.

### No-budget control

Equation (5) says that if arbitrary growth of the approximating operator norms is permitted, every bidual operator is observationally indistinguishable from target-valued operators in `\tau_{pw^*}`. Therefore any purported target-range discriminator built from finitely many matrix coefficients but ignoring the comparison norm budget is impossible.

### No strict-gap claim

The theorem classifies what a strict inequality

\[
\lambda_{**}(T)<\lambda_{\rm fin}(T)
\tag{49}
\]

would mean; it does not assert that a particular pair `(Z\subset X,K,T)` realizes such a strict gap. Producing or ruling out strict examples belongs to the corresponding Banach-space/operator accessibility problem and must not be inferred from the formal distinction alone.

Role: general local-reflexivity theorem allowing compatibility conditions with subspaces and operators; it marks the prior-art boundary for attempts to interpret finite bidual-to-target repairs as a new principle.

Role: operator-ideal accessibility and extension-property prior art, reinforcing that norm-controlled local reflexivity/accessibility is an established operator-theoretic subject rather than a new Mathia theory.
- Standard Banach--Alaoglu, Tychonoff, Hahn--Banach, and locally convex bipolar/separation theorems supply the compactness and gauge identities used in (5)--(16); AF-097--AF-099 already record the adjacent operator-topology and quotient-duality sources.

The exact formulas (9) and (16) are derived here as the natural continuation of AF-097--AF-099. ## Boundaries and failure modes

- `\tau_{pw^*}` is the pointwise weak-star topology induced by the **declared canonical dual pair** `(K^{**},K^*)`. Changing the admissible target duality or topology changes the accessibility envelope.
- Equation (5) is unbudgeted density only. It does not provide a bounded approximating net, a sequence rather than a net, norm convergence, strong operator convergence in `K^{**}`, or a genuine `K`-valued limit.
- `a_K(U)<\infty` means bounded pointwise weak-star accessibility by global operators on `X`; it is stronger than applying local reflexivity separately to each finite-dimensional subset of `U(X)` because those local range repairs need not extend over the whole source with one uniform norm bound.
- Formula (16) concerns the exact linear restriction problem of AF-097--AF-099. Positivity-preserving, equivariant, order-compatible, arithmetic, completely bounded, or other structured operator categories require their own admissible accessibility envelopes.
- The dual formula uses all finite matrix coefficients. A source-natural application may permit only a much smaller observable family, in which case the corresponding polar/gauge must be recomputed rather than importing (9).
- No rational-prime specificity, zeta-zero statement, or RH consequence follows.

## Consequences for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-097 showed that unbudgeted finite point values cannot see global operator nonextension. AF-098 showed that uniform finite interpolation coheres into a bidual-valued recovery, leaving an unexplained possible gap between `\lambda_{**}` and `\lambda_{\rm fin}`. AF-099 showed that every violated finite interpolation budget has a finite matrix-coefficient certificate.

AF-100 closes that conceptual gap without claiming it always vanishes. The exact object retained by finite bounded interpolation is not merely a bidual extension and not merely a target-valued extension: it is a bidual extension lying in a **bounded pointwise weak-star accessibility envelope of genuine target-valued operators**. Its quantitative cost is the polar gauge (9), and optimizing that cost gives exactly `\lambda_{\rm fin}`.

For later arithmetic applications this supplies a stricter audit rule. If a proposed compression/recovery argument escapes into a completion, bidual, distributional enlargement, or other relaxed destination category, proving that the desired discriminator exists in the enlargement is not enough. One must also identify the source-intrinsic topology and admissible resource bound under which the relaxed object remains accessible from the original category. Without that bounded-accessibility bridge, the apparent recovery may be an artifact of changing the target category after the information has already been lost.
