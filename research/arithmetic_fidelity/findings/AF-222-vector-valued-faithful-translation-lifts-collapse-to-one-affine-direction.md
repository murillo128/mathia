# AF-222 — faithful vector-valued translation lifts collapse to one affine direction

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `COORDINATE-RIGIDITY`, `EXACT-FIDELITY`, `CLASSICAL-BRIDGE`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-221 proves that the vanishing rational-prime mesh in the canonical Euler translation coordinate

\[
v_p=-\log\log p
\]

cannot be repaired by a nonlinear **scalar** coordinate warp while preserving one exact faithful translation profile. Merely replacing the hidden scalar coordinate by a finite- or infinite-dimensional vector space does not create an escape.

Let `S\subset\mathbb R`, let

\[
G:=\langle S-S\rangle
\]

be the additive subgroup generated by source differences, and suppose `G` is dense in `\mathbb R`. Let `V` be a Hausdorff locally convex real topological vector space, let

\[
\Phi:\mathbb R\to V
\]

be continuous, let `\Psi:S\to V`, and let `F:V\to Z` take values in an arbitrary set `Z`. Let `f:\mathbb R\to Z` be nonconstant and suppose

\[
\boxed{
f(u-v)=F\!\big(\Phi(u)-\Psi(v)\big)
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R,\ v\in S).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Write

\[
\mathcal R:=\{\Phi(u)-\Psi(v):u\in\mathbb R,\ v\in S\}\subset V
\tag{2}
\]

for the realized hidden-state set. If `F|_{\mathcal R}` is injective, then there are vectors `a,b,c\in V` with `a\ne0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(u)=ua+b,
\qquad
\Psi(v)=va+c.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every exact vector-valued difference representation whose scalar profile remains faithful on the states it actually realizes lies on **one affine line**. Extra ambient dimension is unused.

In particular, if `V` is normed and

\[
S_{\mathbb P}=\{-\log\log p:p\text{ rational prime}\},
\]

then for consecutive primes `p<q`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\Psi(v_p)-\Psi(v_q)\|
=\|a\|\,|v_p-v_q|
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently the prime-tail separation loss exposed by AF-220 and AF-221 cannot be repaired merely by replacing the scalar translation coordinate by a faithful vector-valued one. A genuine vector escape must retain extra states that the scalar Euler profile aliases, or else abandon exact difference form, continuity, or the dense-source setting. Such an escape is therefore an **additional mark/side-information mechanism**, not a higher-dimensional reparameterization of the same faithful observable.

## Derivation

### 1. Realized-state injectivity makes the hidden difference state unique

For `t\in\mathbb R`, choose any `v\in S` and define

\[
H(t):=\Phi(t+v)-\Psi(v).
\tag{5}
\]

This does not depend on the chosen `v`. Indeed, if `v,w\in S`, then `(1)` gives

\[
F(\Phi(t+v)-\Psi(v))
=f(t)
=F(\Phi(t+w)-\Psi(w)).
\]

Both arguments lie in `\mathcal R`, so injectivity of `F|_{\mathcal R}` makes them equal. Fixing one source point in `(5)` also shows that `H:\mathbb R\to V` is continuous.

Equation `(1)` now factors through one hidden state,

\[
f(t)=F(H(t)),
\tag{6}
\]

and the coordinates satisfy

\[
H(u-v)=\Phi(u)-\Psi(v).
\tag{7}
\]

### 2. Every generated source translation gives a constant vector increment

Take `v,w\in S`, put `d=v-w`, and use `(5)` with source point `w`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(x+d)-H(x)
&=\Phi(x+v)-\Phi(x+w)\\
&=\Psi(v)-\Psi(w),
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

where the second equality is the well-definedness identity for `H(x)` using `v` and `w`.

By composing and reversing source differences, every `g\in G` therefore has a vector increment independent of `x`:

\[
H(x+g)-H(x)=A(g),
\tag{9}
\]

with an additive map `A:G\to V`. For any fixed `x_0`,

\[
A(g)=H(x_0+g)-H(x_0),
\tag{10}
\]

so `A` is continuous at zero in the subspace topology inherited by `G`.

### 3. Local convexity forces the dense-group increment into one vector direction

Let `\lambda\in V'` be any continuous linear functional. Then

\[
B_\lambda:=\lambda\circ A:G\to\mathbb R
\]

is additive and continuous. Because `G` is dense, `B_\lambda` extends uniquely to a continuous additive map on `\mathbb R`: if `g_n\to x`, then continuity at zero makes `B_\lambda(g_n)` Cauchy and the resulting limit is independent of the approximating sequence. Every continuous additive real function is linear, hence

\[
B_\lambda(g)=c_\lambda g
\qquad(g\in G).
\tag{11}
\]

Choose one nonzero `g_0\in G` and set

\[
a:=\frac{A(g_0)}{g_0}\in V.
\tag{12}
\]

Equation `(11)` evaluated at `g_0` gives `c_\lambda=\lambda(a)`. Therefore, for every `g\in G` and every `\lambda\in V'`,

\[
\lambda(A(g)-ga)=0.
\tag{13}
\]

The continuous dual of a Hausdorff locally convex space separates points, so

\[
A(g)=ga
\qquad(g\in G).
\tag{14}
\]

Now take arbitrary `d\in\mathbb R` and choose `g_n\in G` with `g_n\to d`. Continuity of `H` and scalar multiplication gives

\[
H(x+d)-H(x)
=\lim_n A(g_n)
=da.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus

\[
H(t)=ta+d_0
\tag{16}
\]

for some `d_0\in V`. Substituting into `(7)` yields

\[
\Phi(u)-\Psi(v)=(u-v)a+d_0.
\tag{17}
\]

Fixing one source point shows that `\Phi(u)-ua` is constant in `u`; comparison across source points then shows that `\Psi(v)-va` is constant in `v`. This proves `(3)`.

Finally `a\ne0`. If `a=0`, then `(16)` makes `H` constant, and `(6)` would make the assumed nonconstant `f` constant.

The functional-equation content is therefore classical Pexider/Cauchy rigidity. The Arithmetic Fidelity consequence is the category statement: **faithful vector dimension cannot create new translation geometry once the source-difference group is dense.**

### 4. The rational-prime Euler source satisfies the dense-difference hypothesis

For

\[
v_p=-\log\log p,
\]

consecutive primes `p<q` satisfy `q<2p` by Bertrand's postulate, hence

\[
0<v_p-v_q
=\log\frac{\log q}{\log p}
<\log\!\left(1+\frac{\log2}{\log p}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{18}
\]

Thus `S_{\mathbb P}-S_{\mathbb P}` contains nonzero positive elements arbitrarily close to zero, so its generated additive subgroup is dense in `\mathbb R`. Applying `(3)` gives `(4)` immediately.

The same conclusion therefore holds in `\mathbb R^d`, Hilbert spaces, Banach spaces, and more general Hausdorff locally convex spaces. The dimensionality of the hidden coordinate cannot turn the vanishing log-log prime mesh into a uniform one while the representation remains faithful in the sense of `(2)`.

## What the theorem does and does not close

### Ambient vector dimension is not an independent resource

A map into `\mathbb R^d` or an infinite-dimensional function space may look richer than the scalar coordinate of AF-221, but under `(1)` and realized-state injectivity its image is forced into the single affine direction `\mathbb R a`. No finite- or infinite-dimensional linear embedding escapes the tail degeneration merely by spreading coordinates in unused dimensions.

### Global injectivity of `F` is not required

Only `F|_{\mathcal R}` must be injective. Values of `F` outside states actually produced by the representation are irrelevant. This is the exact fidelity condition needed by the proof and is weaker than assuming a globally one-to-one profile.

### Noninjective realized fibers identify the real escape hatch

If two distinct realized vector states can map to the same scalar value `f(t)`, the proof stops at `(5)`: the hidden difference state need not be unique. One may then attach a source mark, orientation, provenance label, transverse coordinate, or other side information while the scalar Euler observable ignores it.

That possibility is not ruled out. But it is no longer a pure reparameterization of the scalar Euler translation kernel. The additional coordinate lives inside a nontrivial fiber of `F` and is therefore genuinely **extra retained information**. It must pass the line's ordinary naturality, minimal-lift, matched-control, and downstream-utility tests rather than being counted as a coordinate repair.

### Exact translation covariance remains load bearing

The theorem assumes `(1)` for every real sample coordinate and every declared source point. A nonstationary kernel, a source-dependent profile, a finite-matrix embedding, an approximate factorization, or a relation involving several source points at once belongs to a different category.

### Local convexity is a regularity gate, not an arithmetic assumption

The proof uses continuous linear functionals to turn the vector increment into scalar Cauchy equations and then separate points. Normed and Hilbert settings automatically satisfy this. More exotic non-locally-convex targets are not covered and should not be treated as excluded without a separate theorem.

### Dense source differences remain essential

As in AF-221, a discrete difference group leaves periodic nonlinear freedom. The vector theorem does not remove that boundary; it shows that increasing target dimension does not alter the dense-group rigidity mechanism.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The Pexider/Cauchy mechanism is classical and no novelty is claimed for affine rigidity of regular solutions of additive functional equations. János Aczél, *Lectures on Functional Equations and Their Applications*, Academic Press (1966), is a standard reference for Cauchy and Pexider equations and the regularity assumptions that eliminate pathological additive solutions. János Aczél and Jean Dhombres, *Functional Equations in Several Variables*, Cambridge University Press (1989), provides broader classical functional-equation background.

Vector-valued and topological-vector-space Pexider equations are also established territory. Abbas Najati, M. R. Abdollahpour, and Gwang Hui Kim, **“On Pexider Differences in Topological Vector Spaces,”** *Abstract and Applied Analysis* 2011, Article ID 370104, DOI `10.1155/2011/370104`, studies Pexiderized Cauchy/Jensen stability for maps with topological-vector-space targets. That paper is neighboring prior art for the target category, not a source for the exact dense-subgroup specialization proved above.

AF-221 already gives the scalar dense-source theorem and its prime specialization. AF-222 is the direct vector-valued extension with the minimal realized-state injectivity condition. A targeted literature search found the general Pexider/topological-vector-space theory but no need to claim a new functional-equation theorem. The durable mathematical delta is instead the **Arithmetic Fidelity category boundary**: vector-valued hidden coordinates provide no extra exact faithful translation capacity unless the extra dimensions survive as genuine side information in nontrivial scalar-profile fibers.

## Consequence for the research line

The prime-tail finite-order sign-regularity frontier cannot be repaired by either of two superficially different coordinate moves: AF-221 rules out nonlinear faithful scalar stretching, and AF-222 rules out faithful vector-valued stretching, even in infinite-dimensional locally convex targets.

This makes the next admissible distinction sharper. A tail-uniform mechanism must obtain new information from the source class or retain new relational/provenance structure that is **not determined by one faithful translation state**. Vector dimension alone is not such a resource. Any proposed marked, transverse, or multi-source lift should therefore state explicitly which nontrivial fiber or non-translation relation carries the additional arithmetic discriminator and why matched non-prime controls cannot carry the same mark.
