# AF-149 — Shtarkov envelope escapes mixture-reference family dilution

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-NML-IDENTITY`, `EXACT-DERIVED`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY`, `POSITIVE-REFERENCE-CONSTRUCTION`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-144--AF-145 obtain a whole-experiment recovery certificate and a compositional Bayes/Petz reverse by measuring Pearson chi-square loss relative to one common **mixture of the experiment members**. AF-146 then exposes a family-size dilution obstruction: in the private-label experiment, every in-family mixture must divide its rare private mass among `m` controls, forcing the optimized Pearson loss to remain order one even when the true recovery deficiency tends to zero.

That obstruction is not a no-go for all common references. In the finite statistical category there is a canonical prior-free reference already known from universal coding: the normalized maximum-likelihood, or **Shtarkov**, envelope. It minimizes the worst source likelihood ratio over *all* dominating probability references, generally lies outside the convex hull of the experiment, propagates coherently through a Markov compression, and removes the specific `m`-fold dilution mechanism of AF-146.

Let

\[
\mathcal E=(P_i)_{i=1}^m
\]

be a finite experiment on a finite sample space `X`. Define its pointwise likelihood envelope and Shtarkov mass by

\[
s(x):=\max_{1\le i\le m}P_i(x),
\qquad
C(\mathcal E):=\sum_{x\in X}s(x),
\tag{1}
\]

and define the normalized envelope

\[
M_*(x):=\frac{s(x)}{C(\mathcal E)}.
\tag{2}
\]

Then:

1. `M_*` is the unique minimizer of worst-case essential likelihood ratio,
   \[
   \boxed{
   \inf_M\max_i\left\|\frac{dP_i}{dM}\right\|_\infty
   =C(\mathcal E),
   }
   \tag{3}
   \]
   where the infimum ranges over probability laws dominating every `P_i`. Equivalently,
   \[
   \boxed{
   \inf_M\max_i D_\infty(P_i\|M)=\log C(\mathcal E).
   }
   \tag{4}
   \]
   This is exactly the classical Shtarkov/NML minimax-regret construction.

2. The Shtarkov mass is itself monotone under every stochastic compression `K`:
   \[
   \boxed{
   C(\mathcal EK)\le C(\mathcal E).
   }
   \tag{5}
   \]

3. AF-144's one-kernel chi-square recovery inequality does not algebraically require the common reference to be an in-family mixture. For **any** probability reference `M` dominating all experiment members, with `N=MK` and reference reverse
   \[
   R_M(x\mid y)=\frac{M(x)K(y\mid x)}{N(y)},
   \tag{6}
   \]
   one has simultaneously for every `i`
   \[
   \boxed{
   4\|P_i-P_iKR_M\|_{\rm TV}^2
   \le
   \chi^2(P_i\|M)-\chi^2(P_iK\|MK).
   }
   \tag{7}
   \]
   The same arbitrary source reference can be propagated through a chain exactly as in AF-145, so its Bayes reverses compose and its chi-square losses telescope. What is lost without the mixture condition is AF-144's automatic converse statement that exact experiment sufficiency forces zero loss for every such reference; `(7)` is the positive direction needed here.

4. Applied to the private-label family from AF-146,
   \[
   P_i=(1-\rho)\delta_0+\rho\delta_i,
   \qquad i=1,\ldots,m,
   \tag{8}
   \]
   the Shtarkov mass and reference are
   \[
   \boxed{
   C_{m,\rho}=1+(m-1)\rho,
   }
   \tag{9}
   \]
   and
   \[
   M_*(0)=\frac{1-\rho}{C_{m,\rho}},
   \qquad
   M_*(j)=\frac{\rho}{C_{m,\rho}}
   \quad(1\le j\le m).
   \tag{10}
   \]
   For `m>1` and `rho>0`, this reference is **not** a convex mixture of the `P_i`, because every such mixture has shared mass exactly `1-rho` at `0`, while `(10)` rescales that shared mass by `1/C_{m,rho}`.

5. Under AF-146's compression `K_m`, which fixes `0` and collapses every private label to `*`, the Bayes reverse generated by `M_*` is nevertheless exactly the minimax-optimal recovery already identified in AF-146:
   \[
   R_*(0\mid0)=1,
   \qquad
   R_*(j\mid*)=\frac1m.
   \tag{11}
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   \sup_i\|P_i-P_iK_mR_*\|_{\rm TV}
   =\delta_{\rm rec}
   =\rho\left(1-\frac1m\right).
   \tag{12}
   \]

6. Relative to this source-selected non-mixture reference, the exact Pearson loss is
   \[
   \boxed{
   \varepsilon_i^{\rm NML}
   =C_{m,\rho}\,\rho\left(1-\frac1m\right)
   =C_{m,\rho}\,\delta_{\rm rec}.
   }
   \tag{13}
   \]
   In particular, for AF-146's separating scale `rho=1/m`,
   \[
   C_{m,1/m}=2-\frac1m,
   \qquad
   \varepsilon_i^{\rm NML}
   =\left(2-\frac1m\right)\frac{m-1}{m^2}
   \longrightarrow0,
   \tag{14}
   \]
   whereas the best in-family-mixture profile from AF-146 satisfies
   \[
   \Gamma_{\chi^2}=\frac{m-1}{m}\longrightarrow1.
   \tag{15}
   \]

Thus AF-146's counterexample identifies a **mixture-reference obstruction**, not a universal common-reference obstruction. The normalized likelihood envelope supplies an exact source-selected escape: it allocates probability according to pointwise model envelope rather than a prior over model labels, so a large number of rare alternatives need not force vanishing reference weight per alternative when their total effective envelope complexity stays bounded.

The result does **not** make NML loss a complete metric for approximate sufficiency, nor does it imply an arithmetic mechanism. It isolates a more precise frontier: a useful compositional recovery certificate may require a reference selected from the source family by a minimax domination principle rather than by convex mixing, and that source reference must then be **propagated**, not recomputed independently after each compression.

## Derivation

### The normalized envelope is the minimax dominating reference

Let `M` be any probability law dominating every `P_i` and set

\[
L(M):=\max_i\left\|\frac{dP_i}{dM}\right\|_\infty.
\tag{16}
\]

On a finite space this is simply

\[
L(M)=\max_{x:M(x)>0}\frac{s(x)}{M(x)}.
\tag{17}
\]

Therefore

\[
s(x)\le L(M)M(x)
\quad\text{for every }x.
\]

Summing gives

\[
C(\mathcal E)=\sum_xs(x)
\le L(M)\sum_xM(x)
=L(M).
\tag{18}
\]

For `M_*` from `(2)`, every member satisfies

\[
\frac{P_i(x)}{M_*(x)}
=C(\mathcal E)\frac{P_i(x)}{s(x)}
\le C(\mathcal E),
\tag{19}
\]

and at every point where member `i` attains the envelope, equality holds for that member. Thus `L(M_*)=C(\mathcal E)`, proving `(3)`.

Equality in `(18)` requires `s(x)=C(\mathcal E)M(x)` at every point carrying envelope mass, and a minimizer cannot place probability outside the union of the experiment supports. Hence `M_*` is the unique minimizer. Taking logarithms gives `(4)` because

\[
D_\infty(P_i\|M)
=\log\left\|\frac{dP_i}{dM}\right\|_\infty.
\]

This is precisely Shtarkov's normalized maximum-likelihood law: `s(x)` is the maximized likelihood at observation `x`, `C(E)` is the Shtarkov sum, and `log C(E)` is the minimax pointwise regret.

The construction is invariant under permutation of the parameter labels and equivariant under bijective relabeling of the sample space. It therefore removes the arbitrary prior used by an in-family mixture. This is a finite source-level naturality statement only; it does not claim functoriality under arbitrary stochastic maps.

### Shtarkov mass contracts under compression

Write

\[
Q_i(y)=(P_iK)(y)=\sum_xP_i(x)K(y\mid x).
\]

Then for each output point

\[
\max_iQ_i(y)
\le
\sum_x\max_iP_i(x)K(y\mid x)
=\sum_xs(x)K(y\mid x).
\tag{20}
\]

Summing over `y` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(\mathcal EK)
&=\sum_y\max_iQ_i(y)\\
&\le
\sum_{x,y}s(x)K(y\mid x)\\
&=\sum_xs(x)
=C(\mathcal E),
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves `(5)`. The inequality is another form of data processing: stochastic compression cannot increase the pointwise-envelope complexity of the declared experiment.

This monotonicity should not itself be confused with a recovery metric. In the private-label family with `rho=1/m`, `C(E)` tends to `2` while the collapsed output family has Shtarkov mass exactly `1`, so the Shtarkov-mass drop stays order one even though recovery deficiency tends to zero. Its role here is to select and calibrate a reference, not to replace Le Cam deficiency.

### The chi-square recovery proof works for any dominating source reference

AF-144 stated its family-wide certificate using a full-support mixture because that choice automatically dominates the experiment and makes the reference itself a member of its convex hull. But the Hilbert-space calculation only requires one dominating probability law `M`.

Put

\[
N=MK
\]

and let `R_M` be `(6)`. Under the reference joint law

\[
J(x,y)=M(x)K(y\mid x),
\]

`R_M` is the conditional law of `X` given `Y`; in particular

\[
NR_M=M.
\tag{21}
\]

For one experiment member define

\[
h_i(x)=\frac{P_i(x)}{M(x)}-1.
\tag{22}
\]

Exactly as in AF-144, if `A` is the Markov operator from output functions to source functions and `A^*` is conditional expectation under `J`, then

\[
B=AA^*,
\qquad
0\preceq B\preceq I.
\tag{23}
\]

The chi-square loss is

\[
\chi^2(P_i\|M)-\chi^2(P_iK\|N)
=
\langle h_i,(I-B)h_i\rangle_{L^2(M)},
\tag{24}
\]

while the density error of `P_iK R_M` relative to `M` is `(I-B)h_i`. Since

\[
(I-B)^2\preceq I-B,
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives exactly `(7)`.

Likewise, AF-145's algebraic composition identity for Bayes reverses uses only a source reference and its successive pushforwards. Therefore an arbitrary dominating `M_0` may be propagated as

\[
M_j=M_{j-1}K_j
\]

and the corresponding reverse kernels compose while the Pearson losses telescope.

The distinction from AF-144's mixture theorem matters at the exact-zero converse. If a channel is sufficient only for the declared family `(P_i)`, an arbitrary external reference `M` need not be recovered by the same reverse channel. Therefore one must not infer that every arbitrary-reference loss vanishes from experiment sufficiency alone. The present use is one-way: a small propagated-reference loss certifies recovery via `(7)`.

### Exact private-label calculation

For `(8)`, the pointwise envelope is

\[
s(0)=1-\rho,
\qquad
s(j)=\rho
\quad(1\le j\le m),
\]

so

\[
C_{m,\rho}
=(1-\rho)+m\rho
=1+(m-1)\rho,
\]

which proves `(9)--(10)`.

Every in-family mixture has the form

\[
M_\lambda
=(1-\rho)\delta_0
+\rho\sum_j\lambda_j\delta_j.
\]

Its shared mass at `0` is fixed at `1-rho`, whereas `M_*(0)=(1-rho)/C_{m,rho}`. Since `C_{m,rho}>1` for `m>1` and `rho>0`, the Shtarkov law lies outside the experiment's convex hull. This is exactly the extra freedom AF-146's optimization did not allow.

Push `(10)` through `K_m`. The output reference is

\[
N_*(0)=\frac{1-\rho}{C_{m,\rho}},
\qquad
N_*(*)=\frac{m\rho}{C_{m,\rho}}.
\tag{25}
\]

Conditioning the reference joint law on the output gives `(11)`: output `0` has only source `0`, while output `*` sees equal source masses `rho/C` on every private label. Thus the recovered law is

\[
P_iK_mR_*
=(1-\rho)\delta_0
+\frac\rho m\sum_{j=1}^m\delta_j,
\tag{26}
\]

which AF-146 already proved is minimax optimal, yielding `(12)`.

For the source Pearson divergence,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2(P_i\|M_*)
&=\sum_x\frac{P_i(x)^2}{M_*(x)}-1\\
&=C_{m,\rho}(1-\rho)+C_{m,\rho}\rho-1\\
&=C_{m,\rho}-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

At the output,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2(P_iK_m\|N_*)
&=
\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{(1-\rho)/C_{m,\rho}}
+
\frac{\rho^2}{m\rho/C_{m,\rho}}
-1\\
&=C_{m,\rho}\left(1-\rho+\frac\rho m\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

Subtracting `(28)` from `(27)` gives

\[
\varepsilon_i^{\rm NML}
=C_{m,\rho}\rho\left(1-\frac1m\right),
\]

which is `(13)`. Equations `(14)--(15)` then follow by setting `rho=1/m` and comparing with AF-146.

The mechanism is now transparent. The uniform in-family mixture puts only `rho/m` reference mass on private label `i` while leaving the full shared mass `1-rho` untouched, creating a likelihood-ratio spike `m`. The NML law instead solves a different minimax problem: it rescales the *entire* envelope so every source likelihood ratio is at most `C_{m,rho}`. When `rho=1/m`, that worst ratio is below `2`, uniformly in `m`.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The normalized-envelope construction and its minimax-regret interpretation are classical. No novelty is claimed for `(1)--(4)`.

- Yuri M. Shtarkov, **“Universal Sequential Coding of Single Messages,”** *Problems of Information Transmission* 23(3), 175–186 (1987). Shtarkov introduced the normalized maximum-likelihood distribution and proved its minimax pointwise-regret property. In the finite experiment notation above, the maximized likelihood is exactly `s(x)` and its normalization is `C(E)`.
- Andrew R. Barron, Jorma Rissanen, and Bin Yu, **“The Minimum Description Length Principle in Coding and Modeling,”** *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 44(6), 2743–2760 (1998), DOI `10.1109/18.720554`. Their review develops normalized maximized likelihood/stochastic complexity and records the Shtarkov minimax-regret solution in modern universal-coding language.
- Li Gao, Haojian Li, Iman Marvian, and Cambyse Rouzé, **“Sufficient Statistic and Recoverability via Quantum Fisher Information,”** *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 405, article 180 (2024), DOI `10.1007/s00220-024-05053-z`, arXiv:`2302.02341`. AF-144 specializes their chi-square/Petz recoverability inequality to the finite classical setting. The extension from an in-family mixture to an arbitrary dominating finite reference in `(7)` follows directly from the same conditional-expectation proof; it is not presented as a new Petz theorem.
- Ziyi Liu, Idan Attias, and Daniel M. Roy, **“Sequential Probability Assignment with Contexts: Minimax Regret, Contextual Shtarkov Sums, and Contextual Normalized Maximum Likelihood,”** *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* 37 (2024), arXiv:`2410.03849`. This is modern evidence that Shtarkov sums remain an active and explicitly recognized minimax-complexity object; it does not supply the Arithmetic Fidelity specialization below.

The line-level derived contribution is the exact interaction with AF-146: **the private-label dilution counterexample disappears when the common reference is allowed to be the source family's Shtarkov envelope rather than an in-family mixture, while the induced reverse remains the same minimax-optimal inverse.** The new information is therefore a sharper diagnosis of the previous obstruction and a concrete source-selected reference architecture for the next recovery tests, not a claim to have discovered NML or minimax regret.

## Boundary conditions and falsification tests

1. **NML is not an in-family prior mixture.** That is its useful feature here, but also a categorical change. Any application that requires the reference to represent a genuine prior predictive law over the declared controls cannot silently replace that object by `M_*`.

2. **The Shtarkov selector is source-natural only in a bounded sense.** It is invariant under parameter permutations and sample-space bijections, but generally
   \[
   M_*^{\mathcal EK}\ne M_*^{\mathcal E}K.
   \]
   Recomputing NML after compression is therefore different from propagating the source NML reference.

3. **Propagation, not recanonicalization, carries provenance.** AF-145's compositional reverse and telescoping loss require the same source reference to be pushed through the actual forward maps. Independently replacing `M_j` at each stage by the NML law of the compressed family can break that compatibility. The mismatch between `M_*^{E}K` and `M_*^{EK}` is potentially part of the information lost by compression, not nuisance normalization to erase.

4. **Bounded Shtarkov mass does not imply small recovery error or small information loss.** It gives a uniform likelihood-ratio domination constant. The actual chi-square data-processing loss must still be proved small. The private-label family happens to satisfy `(13)` because the erased event has mass `rho`.

5. **Shtarkov-mass drop is not a recovery surrogate.** Equation `(5)` is monotone, but the example `rho=1/m` has `C(E)->2`, `C(EK)=1`, and `delta_rec->0`. Treating `C(E)-C(EK)` itself as fidelity defect would recreate another badly calibrated scalarization.

6. **The arbitrary-reference recovery bound is one-way.** Small loss implies a common recovery kernel through `(7)`. Exact sufficiency of the declared family need not make the loss vanish for an external reference that the sufficient reverse does not itself recover. AF-144's exact zero equivalence used the special convex-mixture reference for this reason.

7. **Finite-space scope is deliberate.** Continuous NML can fail to normalize because the Shtarkov integral may diverge. The present finding claims the finite result needed for the current whole-experiment frontier; extending it requires explicit finiteness and measurability hypotheses rather than assuming a universal NML law exists.

8. **The construction does not identify the relevant arithmetic family.** An RH-facing use still has to derive the experiment, matched controls, and compression intrinsically, and must show that the source-selected envelope reference is mathematically meaningful rather than an after-the-fact statistical wrapper.

## Arithmetic Fidelity consequence

AF-146--AF-148 showed that fixed divergence profiles built from common mixture references can suffer severe family-size dilution: a growing control family can force tiny reference weights or endpoint effects even when actual whole-experiment recovery is good. AF-149 separates two issues that those negative results had not yet distinguished.

First, **family cardinality is not the right complexity by itself**. The Shtarkov mass

\[
C(\mathcal E)=\sum_x\max_iP_i(x)
\]

measures effective pointwise envelope complexity. It ranges from `1` for an identical family to `m` for mutually disjoint members, while AF-146's `m` private-label controls with `rho=1/m` have `C<2`. The large family is therefore cheap in the precise domination geometry relevant to likelihood-ratio recovery.

Second, a potentially useful common reference need not be a prior predictive mixture. A promising compositional pattern is now:

\[
\text{source control family}
\longmapsto
\text{source-selected minimax dominating reference}
\longmapsto
\text{propagate that reference through compression}
\longmapsto
\text{measure retained likelihood-ratio geometry and recover with its Bayes/Petz reverse}.
\]

The next falsifiable question is whether this escape survives beyond the symmetric private-label test: characterize when a source-selected domination reference with controlled Shtarkov mass also gives a quantitatively calibrated propagated divergence loss, and determine what structural conditions make that calibration stable as the control family grows. If it fails, the failure should now be attributable to the actual overlap/compression geometry rather than to an avoidable `1/m` prior dilution.
