# AF-130 — Metric-local witnesses induce normalized Wasserstein recovery

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY`, `RESTRICTED-WITNESS`, `WASSERSTEIN-RECOVERY`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-129 shows that a compact convex symmetric class of admissible decision witnesses replaces full total-variation recovery geometry by the support seminorm generated by that class. A natural concrete restriction is **metric locality**: nearby source symbols are not allowed to receive arbitrarily different witness values.

For a finite metric space `(X,d)` with at least two points, write

\[
m:=\min_{x\ne x'} d(x,x')>0,
\qquad
D:=\operatorname{diam}(X)>0.
\tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal E=(P_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}
\]

be a finite statistical experiment, let

\[
K:X\rightsquigarrow Y,
\qquad
Q_\theta=P_\theta K,
\]

and let `R:Y\rightsquigarrow X` range over reverse channels. As in AF-129 put

\[
d_R=(P_\theta-Q_\theta R)_{\theta\in\Theta}
\in
H,
\]

where

\[
H=
\left\{v\in\mathbb R^{\Theta\times X}:
\sum_x v_{\theta x}=0\text{ for every }\theta
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

The unrestricted dual ball for twice the recovery deficiency is

\[
B_*=
\left\{r:
\sum_\theta\|r_\theta\|_\infty\le1
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Define the metric-local admissible body

\[
A_d:=
\left\{
 r\in B_*:
 \frac D2
 \sum_\theta \operatorname{Lip}_d(r_\theta)
 \le1
\right\},
\tag{4}
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)
:=
\max_{x\ne x'}
\frac{|f(x)-f(x')|}{d(x,x')}.
\tag{5}
\]

Let `delta_d` be the AF-129 restricted deficiency generated by `A_d`:

\[
2\delta_d
:=
\max_{r\in A_d}
\min_R\langle d_R,r\rangle.
\tag{6}
\]

Then the support gauge of `A_d` on the effective row-zero space is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{A_d}(v)
=
\frac{2}{D}
\max_\theta
\|v_\theta\|_{KR,d}
\qquad(v\in H),
}
\tag{7}
\]

where `||.||_{KR,d}` is the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm on zero-mass signed measures:

\[
\|\mu\|_{KR,d}
:=
\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)\le1}
\sum_x f(x)\mu(x).
\tag{8}
\]

For a difference of probability measures this norm equals the Wasserstein-1 distance. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\delta_d
=
\frac1D
\min_R
\max_\theta
W_{1,d}\!\left(P_\theta,Q_\theta R\right).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus a Lipschitz/local restriction does not merely produce an arbitrary weaker statistic. It changes finite recovery deficiency from worst-case total variation to **diameter-normalized Wasserstein-1 recovery deficiency**.

Moreover, for every `v in H`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac mD
\max_\theta\|v_\theta\|_1
\le
\sigma_{A_d}(v)
\le
\max_\theta\|v_\theta\|_1.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac mD\,\delta_{\rm rec}
\le
\delta_d
\le
\delta_{\rm rec}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

The constant `m/D` is sharp for the induced norm comparison. In particular:

1. `A_d` has no blind row-zero directions, so metric-local witnesses are **zero-error complete** on every genuine finite metric space;
2. their worst-case quantitative loss relative to full total-variation fidelity is exactly controlled by the metric aspect ratio `m/D`;
3. the metric-local support geometry agrees with full total variation on all of `H` if and only if `m=D`, equivalently every two distinct points of `X` are at the same distance.

Hence finite metric locality gives a complete but generally rescaled fidelity language. It becomes fully quantitatively faithful to total variation only for an equilateral discrete metric.

## Derivation

### Rowwise constants remove the apparent `B_*` obstruction

The Lipschitz seminorm is invariant under adding constants. For each function `g:X->R`, choose the midrange constant

\[
c(g):=\frac{\max_X g+\min_X g}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]

Then

\[
\|g-c(g)\|_\infty
=
\frac12\operatorname{osc}(g)
\le
\frac D2\operatorname{Lip}_d(g).
\tag{13}
\]

For `v in H`, rowwise constants are invisible:

\[
\langle v_\theta,g_\theta-c(g_\theta)\rangle
=
\langle v_\theta,g_\theta\rangle.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose a collection `(g_theta)` satisfies

\[
\frac D2\sum_\theta\operatorname{Lip}_d(g_\theta)\le1.
\tag{15}
\]

Center each row by `(12)` and put `r_theta=g_theta-c(g_theta)`. Equation `(13)` gives

\[
\sum_\theta\|r_\theta\|_\infty
\le
\frac D2
\sum_\theta\operatorname{Lip}_d(g_\theta)
\le1,
\tag{16}
\]

so `r in B_*`, and its Lipschitz budget is unchanged. Thus on `H` the explicit intersection with `B_*` in `(4)` removes no effective Lipschitz witness: every admissible Lipschitz class has a gauge-equivalent representative inside the full AF-129 dual ball.

Therefore

\[
\sigma_{A_d}(v)
=
\sup_{\frac D2\sum_\theta\operatorname{Lip}(g_\theta)\le1}
\sum_\theta\langle v_\theta,g_\theta\rangle.
\tag{17}
\]

This centering step is load-bearing. Anchoring every witness at one arbitrary basepoint would also bound its sup norm, but would introduce a coordinate choice and a weaker normalization. Midrange centering uses only the quotient by constants that AF-129 already identifies as pure gauge on recovery residuals.

### Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality identifies the exact gauge

For a zero-mass signed measure `mu` on a finite metric space, Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality gives

\[
\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le L}
\langle\mu,f\rangle
=
L\,\|\mu\|_{KR,d}.
\tag{18}
\]

The budget in `(17)` is an `l^1` sum of rowwise Lipschitz seminorms. Its dual is therefore the `l^infty` maximum of the rowwise KR norms. Explicitly, if

\[
L_\theta:=\operatorname{Lip}(g_\theta),
\qquad
\sum_\theta L_\theta\le\frac2D,
\]

then

\[
\sum_\theta\langle v_\theta,g_\theta\rangle
\le
\sum_\theta L_\theta\|v_\theta\|_{KR,d}
\le
\frac2D\max_\theta\|v_\theta\|_{KR,d}.
\tag{19}
\]

Equality is obtained by assigning the whole Lipschitz budget to a row attaining the maximum and choosing a finite Kantorovich potential for that row. This proves `(7)`.

AF-129 gives

\[
2\delta_d
=
\min_R\sigma_{A_d}(d_R).
\tag{20}
\]

Since each residual row is the difference of two probability distributions,

\[
\|P_\theta-Q_\theta R\|_{KR,d}
=
W_{1,d}(P_\theta,Q_\theta R).
\tag{21}
\]

Substituting `(7)` into `(20)` proves `(9)`.

### Minimum and diameter give the sharp comparison with total variation

Let `mu` be a zero-mass signed measure and write its Jordan decomposition as

\[
\mu=\mu^+-\mu^-.
\]

The two parts have equal mass

\[
M=\mu^+(X)=\mu^-(X)=\frac12\|\mu\|_1.
\tag{22}
\]

Their supports are disjoint. Any transport plan from `mu^+` to `mu^-` therefore moves every unit of transported mass between distinct points. Every such move costs at least `m` and at most `D`. Hence

\[
\frac m2\|\mu\|_1
\le
\|\mu\|_{KR,d}
\le
\frac D2\|\mu\|_1.
\tag{23}
\]

Applying `(23)` rowwise and using `(7)` gives `(10)`. Minimizing over recovery channels and recalling

\[
2\delta_{\rm rec}
=
\min_R\max_\theta\|d_{R,\theta}\|_1
\tag{24}
\]

proves `(11)`.

The lower constant cannot be improved using only `(X,d)`. Choose points `x,y` with `d(x,y)=m` and the row-zero direction

\[
v=\delta_x-\delta_y.
\tag{25}
\]

Then

\[
\|v\|_1=2,
\qquad
\|v\|_{KR,d}=m,
\]

so

\[
\frac{\sigma_{A_d}(v)}{\|v\|_1}
=
\frac{m}{D}.
\tag{26}
\]

Thus `m/D` is the exact AF-129 norming constant for this metric-local witness body.

### Full TV calibration occurs exactly for equilateral metrics

If the metric-local support gauge equals the full AF-129 norm on every row-zero residual, apply it to `(25)`. Equality requires

\[
\frac{2d(x,y)}D=2
\]

for every distinct `x,y`; hence

\[
d(x,y)=D
\qquad(x\ne y).
\tag{27}
\]

Conversely, suppose `(27)` holds. Every unit of unmatched probability mass costs exactly `D` to move, so for all probability measures `P,Q`,

\[
W_{1,d}(P,Q)
=D\,\|P-Q\|_{TV}.
\tag{28}
\]

More generally for every zero-mass signed `mu`,

\[
\|\mu\|_{KR,d}
=
\frac D2\|\mu\|_1.
\tag{29}
\]

Equations `(7)` and `(29)` give

\[
\sigma_{A_d}(v)
=
\max_\theta\|v_\theta\|_1
\qquad(v\in H),
\tag{30}
\]

which is precisely AF-129 full quantitative completeness.

Therefore metric-local witness geometry has a complete classification in the finite case: no blind quotient, sharp robustness `m/D`, and exact TV calibration exactly for the equilateral metric.

The Arithmetic Fidelity value is therefore not a new theorem of optimal transport or statistical decision theory. ## Boundary conditions and falsification checks

The statement has strict boundaries.

1. **Finite metric source.** The positive minimum spacing `m` is essential for the lower comparison in `(23)`. In an infinite metric space with arbitrarily close distinct points, Lipschitz witnesses can still separate measures while no positive global constant analogous to `m/D` exists.

2. **The metric is part of the declared admissible structure.** Choosing or learning a convenient metric after seeing the discriminator would merely hide information in the witness class. An application must justify `d` independently from the structure that survives the compression.

3. **The rowwise `l^1` Lipschitz budget is substantive.** It is chosen because AF-129's full dual ball uses an `l^1` sum of rowwise `l^infinity` budgets, making the effective dual exactly a rowwise maximum. Other couplings across parameters produce other mixed Wasserstein geometries and require a separate derivation.

4. **Midrange centering uses the row-zero quotient.** Equation `(7)` would be false if rowwise additive constants were observable. Recovery residuals make them pure gauge, exactly as AF-129 establishes.

5. **Zero-error completeness does not mean exact quantitative completeness.** For a non-equilateral metric, every nonzero residual is detected but nearest-neighbor residuals are strictly cheaper in the restricted geometry than in total variation.

6. **No prime-specific fidelity follows.** The theorem does not provide a canonical metric on prime-derived states and does not show that any rational-prime discriminator is Lipschitz, well separated, or robust in such a metric. Those are application-specific obligations.

A concrete application is therefore falsifiable in three steps: derive an intrinsic metric on the retained/reconstructed state, compute or bound its effective `m/D` on the relevant finite approximation, and determine whether the rational-prime/control residual lies on short metric scales. If the proposed metric is arbitrary, if `m/D` collapses along the intended limit, or if prime-specific differences concentrate precisely where transport is cheap, metric-local witness completeness gives no robust arithmetic protection.

## Consequences for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-130 supplies the first direct answer to AF-129's request for an actual constrained witness family. It shows that the correct question is not simply whether locality loses information. On finite state spaces, **locality can preserve exact distinguishability while progressively weakening quantitative fidelity as meaningful scales separate**.

This gives a reusable diagnostic for later geometric and arithmetic compressions. When a proposed retained structure comes with a natural metric, replacing arbitrary decision witnesses by metric-local ones has an exact cost: the relevant recovery problem becomes transport rather than total variation. A finite model can therefore remain formally faithful while its robustness constant tends to zero in a scaling limit.

The next useful frontier is to identify which Mathia constructions supply an independently forced metric and then ask whether their prime/control residuals remain separated at transport scale. That test is stricter and more informative than merely observing that a finite collection of Lipschitz witnesses can still distinguish the two objects.
