# AF-029 — Lattice modulation tests have a sharp cosine-alias fidelity horizon

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`

## Claim

Use the same double-positive modulation family as AF-028. Fix

\[
0<B<A,
\]

choose a nonnegative real bump

\[
\phi\in C_c^\infty((-A/2,A/2))
\]

which is strictly positive on `(-B/2,B/2)` and vanishes outside `[-B/2,B/2]`, and put

\[
K(x)
=
\frac{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(x)}{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(0)},
\qquad
\widetilde\phi(x)=\phi(-x).
\]

Then `K` is even, smooth, supported in `[-B,B]`, strictly positive on `(-B,B)`, pointwise nonnegative, and has nonnegative Fourier transform. For `0<\varepsilon<1`, define

\[
F_\xi(x)
=
\frac{K(x)(1+\varepsilon\cos(\xi x))}{1+\varepsilon}.
\]

Every `F_\xi` belongs to the double-positive compact test cone of AF-027.

Fix a lattice spacing `h>0` and retain only the countable family

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_h=\{F_{nh}:n=0,1,2,\ldots\}.
}
\]

This sampling set has no finite accumulation point. Nevertheless its exact information content on finite signed Radon measures on `(0,B)` is completely classifiable.

Let

\[
c_h:(0,B)\to[-1,1],
\qquad
c_h(x)=\cos(hx),
\]

and, for a finite signed measure `\mu`, let `K\mu` denote the weighted measure with density `K` relative to `\mu`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\left(\int F_{nh}\,d\mu\right)_{n\ge0}
=
\left(\int F_{nh}\,d\nu\right)_{n\ge0}
\iff
(c_h)_*(K\mu)=(c_h)_*(K\nu).
}
\]

Thus the lattice family does not merely provide an incomplete list of scalar moments. Its exact destination is the **cosine quotient** of the visible weighted measure.

Consequently:

1. If
   \[
   \boxed{hB\le\pi,}
   \]
   then `c_h` is injective on `(0,B)`, and `\mathcal F_h` is faithful:
   \[
   \boxed{
   \int F_{nh}\,d\mu=\int F_{nh}\,d\nu\ \forall n\ge0
   \Longrightarrow
   \mu=\nu.
   }
   \]

2. If
   \[
   \boxed{hB>\pi,}
   \]
   then `c_h` already has nontrivial fibers inside `(0,B)`, and `\mathcal F_h` is not faithful even on positive finite atomic measures. Hence the threshold `hB=\pi` is sharp at the measure level.

3. For generalized-prime systems `Q,R` satisfying AF-020's local-finiteness hypotheses, equality
   \[
   W_Q(F_{nh})=W_R(F_{nh})
   \qquad(n\ge0)
   \]
   together with `hB\le\pi` forces
   \[
   \boxed{
   \omega_Q|_{(0,B)}=\omega_R|_{(0,B)}
   }
   \]
   and therefore, by AF-020's dilation-Möbius inversion,
   \[
   \boxed{
   Q_{<e^B}=R_{<e^B}
   }
   \]
   as generator-norm multisets.

For a fixed lattice spacing `h`, one may take the limiting visible horizon `B=\pi/h`. Thus the same countable double-positive template determines every generalized-prime generator norm strictly below

\[
\boxed{e^{\pi/h}.}
\]

The key point is that AF-028's finite-accumulation hypothesis is **sufficient but not necessary** for infinite-family fidelity. A discrete family escaping to infinity can still be exactly faithful when its observable algebra separates the source support. What matters is the quotient generated by the retained observables, not accumulation of the parameter set by itself.

## The sequence of test values is exactly a Chebyshev moment sequence

For a finite signed measure `\mu` on `(0,B)`, write

\[
a_n(\mu)=\int F_{nh}(x)\,d\mu(x)
\]

and

\[
m_n(\mu)=\int K(x)\cos(nhx)\,d\mu(x).
\]

Because `F_0=K`,

\[
a_0(\mu)=m_0(\mu).
\]

For every `n\ge1`,

\[
a_n(\mu)
=
\frac{m_0(\mu)+\varepsilon m_n(\mu)}{1+\varepsilon},
\]

so the transformation between the retained test values and the cosine moments is invertible:

\[
\boxed{
m_0=a_0,
\qquad
m_n=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)a_n-a_0}{\varepsilon}
\quad(n\ge1).
}
\]

Now use the Chebyshev identity

\[
T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta).
\]

If

\[
\rho_\mu=(c_h)_*(K\mu),
\]

then

\[
m_n(\mu)
=
\int_{[-1,1]}T_n(t)\,d\rho_\mu(t).
\]

The polynomials `T_n` have degree `n` with nonzero leading coefficient, so their linear span is the full polynomial algebra. Equality of all retained lattice tests is therefore equivalent to equality of all polynomial moments of `\rho_\mu` and `\rho_\nu`.

Since both pushforward measures live on the compact interval `[-1,1]`, polynomial moments determine a finite signed measure uniquely. One can see this directly from Stone-Weierstrass: polynomials are uniformly dense in `C([-1,1])`, so a finite signed measure annihilating every polynomial annihilates every continuous function and is zero.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(a_n(\mu))_{n\ge0}=(a_n(\nu))_{n\ge0}
\iff
\rho_\mu=\rho_\nu.
}
\]

This equivalence is stronger than a one-way recovery statement. It identifies the exact quotient implemented by the entire lattice family.

## The sharp injectivity threshold is geometric, not analytic continuation

On the interval `[0,B]`, the map

\[
x\mapsto\cos(hx)
\]

is strictly decreasing whenever

\[
hB\le\pi.
\]

It is therefore one-to-one on the visible positive interval. If the lattice test sequences of `\mu` and `\nu` agree, the previous section gives

\[
(c_h)_*(K\mu)=(c_h)_*(K\nu).
\]

Injectivity of `c_h` then gives

\[
K\mu=K\nu.
\]

Because `K(x)>0` on every `x\in(0,B)`, multiplication by `K` is itself injective on finite signed measures there. Hence

\[
\mu=\nu.
\]

No holomorphic identity theorem is used. The proof is instead a compact moment problem plus separation of points by the observable algebra

\[
\operatorname{span}\{1,\cos(hx),\cos(2hx),\ldots\}.
\]

Indeed, the product identity

\[
\cos(mhx)\cos(nhx)
=
\tfrac12\cos((m+n)hx)
+
\tfrac12\cos(|m-n|hx)
\]

shows that this span is an algebra. Below the threshold it contains constants and separates points of `[0,B]`, so Stone-Weierstrass gives the same conclusion directly.

This exhibits a second mechanism for infinite-family fidelity beside AF-028's analytic continuation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{observable-algebra separation on the source support}
\Longrightarrow
\text{exact recovery}.
}
\]

## Above the threshold the retained quotient has exact aliases

Assume now

\[
hB>\pi.
\]

Choose

\[
a\in\left(\max\left\{0,\frac{2\pi}{h}-B\right\},\frac{\pi}{h}\right)
\]

and set

\[
b=\frac{2\pi}{h}-a.
\]

Then

\[
0<a<\frac\pi h<b<B
\]

and for every integer `n\ge0`,

\[
\cos(nhb)
=
\cos(2\pi n-nha)
=
\cos(nha).
\]

Thus `a` and `b` lie in the same exact `c_h` fiber.

Because `K(a),K(b)>0`, define two positive finite measures

\[
\mu=\frac1{K(a)}\delta_a,
\qquad
\nu=\frac1{K(b)}\delta_b.
\]

They are distinct, but for every `n\ge0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int F_{nh}\,d\mu
&=
\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos(nha)}{1+\varepsilon},\\
\int F_{nh}\,d\nu
&=
\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos(nhb)}{1+\varepsilon},
\end{aligned}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\int F_{nh}\,d\mu
=
\int F_{nh}\,d\nu
\qquad\forall n\ge0.
}
\]

The failure is therefore not caused by sign cancellation, instability, finite truncation, or lack of enough moments. Infinitely many exact positive test values are retained. The loss is entirely the geometric identification

\[
\boxed{
x\sim_h y
\iff
\cos(hx)=\cos(hy)
\iff
hx\equiv\pm hy\pmod{2\pi}.}
\]

The complete lattice destination remembers the weighted mass only after summing it over these cosine fibers.

This also gives the exact reason the threshold is `\pi/h` rather than merely an artifact of the proof: `\pi/h` is the first turning point of the cosine map on the positive axis. Once the visible interval crosses it, a reflection alias enters the same support window.

## Generalized-prime consequence

For a generalized-prime system `Q`, AF-020 defines

\[
\omega_Q
=
\sum_j\sum_{m\ge1}
\ell_j e^{-m\ell_j/2}\delta_{m\ell_j},
\qquad
\ell_j=\log q_j,
\]

and

\[
W_Q(F)=\int_0^\infty F(x)\,d\omega_Q(x).
\]

The window `K` is supported in `[-B,B]`, so every `F_{nh}` sees only

\[
\omega_Q|_{(0,B)}.
\]

When `hB\le\pi`, equality of the complete lattice sequences for `Q` and `R` gives equality of these restricted prime-power measures. AF-020 then reconstructs the generator multiplicity function by

\[
n_Q(x)
=
\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}m
\frac{e^{x/(2m)}\omega_Q(\{x/m\})}{x/m},
\]

which uses only atoms below `x`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
W_Q(F_{nh})=W_R(F_{nh})\ \forall n\ge0,
\quad hB\le\pi
\Longrightarrow
Q_{<e^B}=R_{<e^B}.
}
\]

In particular, for the rational primes,

\[
W_{\mathbb P}(F_{nh})=W_R(F_{nh})
\qquad\forall n\ge0
\]

forces every matched generalized-prime system `R` to contain exactly the same generator norms, with multiplicity, below `e^B` whenever `hB\le\pi`.

For fixed `h`, choosing `B=\pi/h` gives the explicit norm-fidelity horizon

\[
\boxed{q<e^{\pi/h}.}
\]

The measure-level counterexample above proves that the lattice observable itself cannot distinguish arbitrary visible measures once `B>\pi/h`. It does **not** by itself prove that two genuine generalized-prime systems can always realize the alias fiber beyond that threshold; prime-power weights and multiplicative repetition impose extra category-specific constraints. Any stronger Beurling collision claim must be proved separately rather than inferred from the measure quotient.

## Relation to AF-028: accumulation is one recovery mechanism, not the criterion

AF-028 used a set of frequencies with a finite accumulation point. Compact support made the cosine transform entire, and the identity theorem promoted equality on that frequency set to equality for all complex frequencies. This was a clean sufficient route to recovery.

The lattice

\[
\{0,h,2h,3h,\ldots\}
\]

has no finite accumulation point, so that proof cannot apply. Yet below `hB=\pi` the family is still fully faithful. The reason is that all lattice observables jointly generate a dense algebra on the visible interval.

Conversely, above `hB=\pi`, infinitely many lattice values are insufficient because the generated algebra fails to separate the first cosine-reflection pair.

The corrected hierarchy is therefore not

\[
\text{finite accumulation}
\iff
\text{infinite-family fidelity}.
\]

Instead:

\[
\boxed{
\text{retained family}
\longrightarrow
\text{generated observable quotient}
\longrightarrow
\text{support-level separation or aliasing}.
}
\]

Analytic continuation, dense observable algebras, Fourier uniqueness, rigidity, or another theorem may each certify that the quotient is faithful. The parameter-set topology alone does not.

This is exactly the kind of distinction the line is meant to expose: two countably infinite compressions can have the same cardinality and superficially similar test functions while one is faithful and the other loses a concrete relational identification.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The mathematical ingredients are classical, and this result should be read as an exact Arithmetic Fidelity classification rather than as a new sampling theorem.

- Marshall H. Stone, **“The Generalized Weierstrass Approximation Theorem,”** *Mathematics Magazine* 21(4) (1948), 167–184, DOI `10.2307/3029750`, supplies the classical density principle used here: a real subalgebra containing constants and separating points of a compact space is uniformly dense in the continuous functions.
- Felix Hausdorff, **“Summationsmethoden und Momentfolgen. I, II,”** *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 9 (1921), 74–109 and 280–299, and **“Momentprobleme für ein endliches Intervall,”** *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 16 (1923), 220–248, are foundational compact-interval moment-problem prior art. Compactly supported measures are determined by their full polynomial moment sequence.
- The identity `T_n(cos theta)=cos(n theta)` and the fact that the Chebyshev polynomials span the polynomial algebra are classical approximation/orthogonal-polynomial facts.
- Claude E. Shannon, **“Communication in the Presence of Noise,”** *Proceedings of the IRE* 37(1) (1949), 10–21, DOI `10.1109/JRPROC.1949.232969`, is neighboring sampling-theorem prior art for the reciprocal relation between sample spacing and a bounded information scale. The present theorem is not an application of Shannon interpolation: it concerns exact cosine moments of compactly supported measures and is proved by the quotient map `x -> cos(hx)` plus compact moment determinacy.
- AF-020 supplies the separate arithmetic step from the visible prime-power measure to the generalized-prime generator norms; AF-028 supplies the same admissible double-positive modulation family and the contrasting accumulation-point recovery mechanism.

No novelty is claimed for Stone-Weierstrass, the Hausdorff moment problem, Chebyshev identities, trigonometric aliasing, or classical sampling theory. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the **complete destination classification** for this constrained test family: the infinite lattice sequence is exactly the pushforward of `K\mu` through the cosine quotient, and on a positive support interval its faithfulness changes sharply at the first noninjective cosine fold `hB=\pi`.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- The theorem concerns exact infinite sequences. Any finite truncation falls back under AF-022/AF-023-style finite-dimensional collision mechanisms.
- The threshold uses a one-sided positive support interval. On a symmetric interval around zero, cosine is already even and therefore noninjective before any `\pi/h` fold unless reflection is declared gauge.
- The known window `K` is harmless only because it is strictly positive on the entire visible interval. Zeros of the window create additional fibers independent of cosine aliasing.
- The classification is for finite signed Radon measures on `(0,B)`. More singular distributions require a separate determinacy argument.
- Exact equality is essential. Stability of reconstruction from noisy lattice values is a different inverse problem and is not established here.
- The measure-level alias construction is not automatically a generalized-prime collision. Arithmetic realizability of a quotient fiber must be checked inside the generalized-prime category.
- Nothing here identifies zero locations, proves positivity of a Weil form, or gives evidence for RH.

## Consequence for the line

AF-028 showed that a countably infinite, analytically coupled positive test family can recover an entire visible prime-power measure. AF-029 sharpens the structural lesson: **infinite-family fidelity is governed by the quotient generated by the observables, not by the cardinality or accumulation pattern of the parameter set.**

For the lattice family the quotient is explicit:

\[
\boxed{
\mu
\longmapsto
(c_h)_*(K\mu).
}
\]

Its first failure mode is equally explicit:

\[
\boxed{
B>\frac\pi h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{reflection aliases enter the visible support.}
}
\]

This supplies a reusable audit pattern for other compressed test families: identify the algebra generated by all retained observables, compute the equivalence relation it induces on the source, and only then ask whether the intended arithmetic discriminator factors through that quotient. A large or infinite test family is not evidence of fidelity unless its generated observables actually separate the relevant source class.
