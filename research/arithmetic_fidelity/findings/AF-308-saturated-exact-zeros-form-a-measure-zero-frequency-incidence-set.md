# AF-308 — Saturated exact zeros form a measure-zero frequency incidence set

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `DIRICHLET-SERIES`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-306 and AF-307 separate two questions for saturated equal-weight finite Dirichlet sums on the imaginary axis: whether zero can be approached arbitrarily closely, and whether zero is attained exactly. The second question is much thinner in frequency space.

For `k>=2`, fix the common-phase gauge and write

\[
F_\nu(t)
=\frac1k\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}e^{-it\nu_j}\right),
\qquad
\nu\in\mathbb R^{k-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
E_k
=\left\{\nu\in\mathbb R^{k-1}:
F_\nu(t)=0\text{ for some }t\ne0\right\}
\tag{2}
\]

be the frequency-difference vectors whose one-parameter orbit hits the exact zero locus.

Then:

1. for every `k>=3`,
   \[
   \dim_H(E_k)\le k-2<k-1,
   \tag{3}
   \]
   hence `E_k` has `(k-1)`-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero;
2. the threshold is sharp at `k=2`: every nonzero one-dimensional frequency difference belongs to `E_2`;
3. rationally independent frequency differences have dense torus orbit, so for every such `nu` and every `k>=2`,
   \[
   \inf_{t\in\mathbb R}|F_\nu(t)|=0;
   \tag{4}
   \]
4. consequently, for Lebesgue-almost every `nu` when `k>=3`, the exact zero set is empty while the stable zero margin is zero:
   \[
   F_\nu(t)\ne0\ \forall t,
   \qquad
   \inf_t|F_\nu(t)|=0.
   \tag{5}
   \]

For distinct rational primes `p_1,...,p_k`, the prime frequency differences

\[
\nu_j=\log(p_j/p_k),\qquad 1\le j<k,
\tag{6}
\]

are rationally independent by unique factorization. Therefore every fixed prime support lies on the zero-margin side of (4), but exact attainment asks whether the special arithmetic vector (6) lies in the exceptional incidence set `E_k`.

This sharpens the unresolved `k>=5` frontier from AF-307: generic torus density can prove arbitrarily close approach, but it cannot decide exact zero membership for the rational-prime vector. Exact attainment is an arithmetic incidence/resonance problem inside a measure-zero exceptional set.

## The exact phase-zero locus is an equilateral polygon space

Define

\[
M_k
=\left\{(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1})\in(S^1)^{k-1}:
1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}z_j=0\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

This is the standard normalized moduli space of equilateral plane `k`-gons: the final unit edge is fixed to `1`, while the remaining unit edge vectors close the polygon.

Classical polygon-space theory gives

\[
\dim M_k=k-3.
\tag{8}
\]

Klaus and Kojima recall the standard result that `M_k` is a compact real affine variety of dimension `k-3`; it is smooth for odd `k`, while for even `k` its singular points are precisely the collinear `+/-1` configurations after the chosen normalization. Thus the singular even-cardinality case does not create a hidden full-dimensional component.

The set is nonempty for every `k>=2`: after a common rotation, the `k`th roots of unity give `k` unit phases with zero sum.

Equation (7) is exactly the target hit required by (1). If `F_\nu(t)=0`, then

\[
(e^{-it\nu_1},\ldots,e^{-it\nu_{k-1}})\in M_k.
\tag{9}
\]

## Exact hits are a cone over lifted polygon configurations

Let

\[
\exp_T:\mathbb R^{k-1}\to(S^1)^{k-1},
\qquad
x\mapsto(e^{-ix_1},\ldots,e^{-ix_{k-1}}),
\tag{10}
\]

and set

\[
\widetilde M_k=\exp_T^{-1}(M_k).
\tag{11}
\]

Because `exp_T` is a covering map, `\widetilde M_k` is a countable union of local lifts of `M_k`. Hence it has the same local real dimension `k-3`. More explicitly, choosing angle representatives on local torus charts gives countably many translates by `(2\pi\mathbb Z)^{k-1}` of lifted `(k-3)`-dimensional strata.

Now (9) is equivalent to

\[
t\nu\in\widetilde M_k.
\tag{12}
\]

Since `t\ne0`, this gives the exact representation

\[
E_k
=\left\{r x:
x\in\widetilde M_k,\ r\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus `E_k` is the radial cone generated by the lifted polygon-zero configurations.

To make the dimension statement measure-theoretically explicit, stratify the real algebraic variety `M_k` into finitely many smooth semialgebraic strata of dimension at most `k-3`, lift those strata through the covering map, and restrict both the lift coordinate and `r` in (13) to bounded boxes. Each resulting piece is the locally Lipschitz image of a set of dimension at most

\[
(k-3)+1=k-2.
\tag{14}
\]

A countable union over lifted charts, lattice translates, and bounded radial boxes therefore satisfies

\[
\dim_H(E_k)\le k-2.
\tag{15}
\]

For `k>=3`, the ambient frequency-difference space has dimension `k-1`, so (15) implies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Leb}_{k-1}(E_k)=0.}
\tag{16}
\]

The two real equations encoded by zero phase give codimension two in the phase torus, while the freely chosen hit time restores one dimension. After quotienting the common phase, exact zero frequencies therefore occupy at most a codimension-one exceptional set.

## The k=2 threshold agrees with the exact formula

For `k=2`,

\[
F_\nu(t)=\frac12(1+e^{-it\nu}).
\]

If `nu\ne0`, choose

\[
t=\frac{(2m+1)\pi}{\nu}
\]

for any integer `m`; then `F_\nu(t)=0`. Hence

\[
E_2=\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{17}
\]

This is exactly the exceptional dimensional threshold predicted by the cone count: `M_2` is zero-dimensional and adjoining the hit-time scale already fills the one-dimensional ambient frequency space. It also recovers AF-307's exact two-prime zero formula after taking `nu=log(p/q)` up to orientation.

For `k>=3`, one extra ambient frequency degree of freedom remains beyond the phase-zero configuration plus hit time, and exact incidence becomes measure-zero.

## Stable approach survives outside the exact-hit set

The measure-zero statement concerns exact attainment only. It does not produce a positive distance from zero.

Suppose

\[
\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_{k-1}
\]

are linearly independent over `Q`. Kronecker-Weyl then makes

\[
t\longmapsto
(e^{-it\nu_1},\ldots,e^{-it\nu_{k-1}})
\tag{18}
\]

dense in `(S^1)^{k-1}`. Since `M_k` is nonempty and the map

\[
(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1})
\longmapsto
\frac1k\left|1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}z_j\right|
\tag{19}
\]

is continuous and vanishes on `M_k`, density yields (4). Standard recurrence of the dense torus flow gives the same arbitrarily close approach at arbitrarily large positive heights.

The complement of rationally independent vectors is a countable union of rational hyperplanes and therefore also has Lebesgue measure zero. Combining this with (16) proves (5): for almost every frequency vector, the orbit comes arbitrarily close to the exact zero locus but never intersects it.

This is a strict exact/stable fidelity split. Exact zero membership is generically false, while its conditioning margin is generically zero.

## Rational-prime frequencies are stable-generic but exact-exceptional is undecided

For distinct rational primes `p_1,...,p_k`, suppose

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}m_j\log(p_j/p_k)=0,
\qquad m_j\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{20}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}m_j\log p_j
-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}m_j\right)\log p_k=0.
\tag{21}
\]

Exponentiating gives a multiplicative relation among distinct rational primes. Unique factorization forces every exponent to vanish, hence every `m_j=0`. The frequency differences (6) are therefore rationally independent.

So the rational-prime vector always satisfies the dense-orbit hypothesis and has zero stable margin. But measure-zero genericity says nothing about whether this specially structured vector belongs to `E_k`: arithmetic families can lie entirely inside measure-zero sets.

For `k=3,4`, AF-306--AF-307 settle the special arithmetic incidence negatively by stronger exact arguments. For `k>=5`, (16) neither proves nor disproves a prime-support exact zero. Instead it identifies the missing theorem correctly: one must use arithmetic structure of logarithms of prime ratios to prove membership or non-membership in the cone (13).

## Prior art and novelty assessment

No novelty claim is made for any general ingredient.

- Stephan Klaus and Sadayoshi Kojima, *On the moduli space of equilateral plane pentagons*, **Beiträge zur Algebra und Geometrie** 60 (2019), 487--497, DOI `10.1007/s13366-018-0429-z`, recall the classical normalized equilateral-polygon model (7) and the theorem that `M_k` is a compact real affine variety of dimension `k-3`, smooth for odd `k` and singular for even `k`. They cite the broader polygon-space literature including Kapovich--Millson.
- Jacek Bochnak, Michel Coste, and Marie-Françoise Roy, *Real Algebraic Geometry*, Springer (1998), DOI `10.1007/978-3-662-03718-8`, is standard prior art for semialgebraic dimension, decomposition, and stratification used to pass from the real-algebraic dimension of `M_k` to the measure estimate for its radial images.
- Alexandre Bailleul, *Explicit Kronecker-Weyl theorems and applications to prime number races*, **Research in Number Theory** 8 (2022), Article 43, DOI `10.1007/s40993-022-00349-2`, supplies modern formulations of the classical continuous Kronecker-Weyl theorem used for dense prime-log torus flow.
- J. M. Sepulcre, *On the Result of Invariance of the Closure Set of the Real Projections of the Zeros of an Important Class of Exponential Polynomials*, **Journal of Function Spaces** 2016, Article 3605690, DOI `10.1155/2016/3605690`, and Janne Heittokangas, Katsuya Ishizaki, Kazuya Tohge, and Zhi-Tao Wen, *Value distribution of exponential polynomials and their role in the theories of complex differential equations and oscillation theory*, **Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society** 55(1) (2023), 1--77, DOI `10.1112/blms.12719`, place rationally independent exponential sums, zero-location closure, and finite Dirichlet sums inside established exponential-polynomial theory.

A targeted literature search found extensive classical theory for equilateral polygon spaces, Kronecker-Weyl density, almost-periodic/exponential-sum zero closure, and exact complex zeros. It did not justify treating the elementary frequency-incidence formulation (16) as a new theorem. Its durable value here is instead the Arithmetic Fidelity distinction between **topological access to the failure locus** and **exact incidence with that locus**.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Equation (16) is a generic statement in continuous frequency-difference space. It cannot certify any specific arithmetic vector, including `(log(p_j/p_k))`.
- `E_k` being measure zero does not imply that it is finite, nowhere dense, or separated from the prime-log vector. No such stronger topological claim is made here.
- The zero-margin statement requires rational independence. Exact-hit measure zero and dense-orbit approximation are logically separate claims.
- The common-frequency shift has already been quotiented by fixing the final phase to `1`. Without this gauge, raw frequency space contains a redundant direction because multiplying all phases by the same unit scalar does not affect whether their sum is zero.
- The result concerns equal weights, finite supports, and the imaginary axis. Unequal coefficients, `Re(s) != 0`, repeated frequencies, or nonlinear/vector observations have different phase geometry.
- The result does not classify exact zeros for saturated rational-prime supports with `k>=5`; it shows why generic density or dimension arguments cannot do so.
- Near-zero numerical samples cannot establish exact membership in `E_k`. A prime-support exact-zero claim needs a rigorous analytic/arithmetic certificate, while zero-margin already follows from torus density.
- Empty exact zero sets can still make an exact binary discriminator vacuously faithful while remaining arbitrarily ill-conditioned. This finding therefore does not rehabilitate exact-only compression claims.
- Nothing here supplies a zeta zero count, analytic continuation, a Riemann-zero divisor, or an RH implication.

## Consequence for the research line

AF-307 left `k>=5` as the first unresolved saturated exact-zero cardinalities. The present result changes the form of that frontier.

The generic geometry is now settled: after quotienting common phase, exact hits live in the measure-zero cone `E_k`, whereas rationally independent flows have zero stable margin because their closures meet the polygon-zero locus. Therefore another Kronecker-Weyl, equidistribution, or near-zero argument cannot settle exact prime-support zeros; those tools see closure, not incidence.

The remaining `k>=5` question is specifically arithmetic: determine whether prime-log difference vectors can satisfy the exact polygon-phase resonance encoded by (13), or prove an arithmetic obstruction to such membership. Any attempted discriminator based only on exact zero membership must also pass the AF-307 non-vacuity gate and a separate stable-margin audit.

More generally, this supplies a reusable Arithmetic Fidelity warning: a compressed orbit may be topologically complete relative to a failure set while exact failures occur only on an exceptional incidence set. **Reachability in closure and exact recoverable membership are different resources**, and neither should be substituted for the other in an RH-facing compression argument.
