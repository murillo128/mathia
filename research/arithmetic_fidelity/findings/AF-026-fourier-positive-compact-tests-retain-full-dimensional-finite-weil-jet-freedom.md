# AF-026 — Fourier-positive compact tests retain full-dimensional finite Weil-jet freedom

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`

## Claim

Fix `A>0` and let

\[
\mathcal P_A
=
\left\{
F\in C_c^\infty((-A,A);\mathbb R):
F(-x)=F(x),\ F(0)=1,\ \widehat F(\xi)\ge 0\ \text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb R
\right\}.
\]

For smooth compactly supported functions on the line, the Fourier-positivity condition is the standard positive-definite-function condition under Fourier inversion/Bochner theory. Thus `\mathcal P_A` is a normalized, even, compactly supported positive-definite test class.

Let

\[
S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_M\}\subset(0,A)
\]

be distinct, let `r\ge0`, and define the finite jet map

\[
J_{S,r}(F)
=
\bigl(F^{(k)}(s_j)\bigr)_{1\le j\le M,\ 0\le k\le r}
\in\mathbb R^{M(r+1)}.
\]

Then:

1. **Fourier positivity does not force the finite jets into a lower-dimensional locus.** One has
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^{M(r+1)}}
   J_{S,r}(\mathcal P_A)\neq\varnothing.
   }
   \]
   In fact, `J_{S,r}(\mathcal P_A)` contains a full-dimensional simplex generated by finitely many modulations of one fixed compactly supported positive-definite kernel.

2. **The same remains true for finite-order local Weil response jets at every finite nonresonant generator block.** Fix distinct
   \[
   0<c_1<\cdots<c_N<A
   \]
   satisfying the AF-024 nonresonance condition
   \[
   m c_j\neq c_k
   \]
   for all `j,k` and integers `m\ge2` with `m c_j<A`. For
   \[
   G_F(t)
   =
   \sum_{m\ge1} t e^{-mt/2}F(mt),
   \]
   define
   \[
   \mathcal R_r(F)
   =
   \bigl(G_F^{(k)}(c_j)\bigr)_{1\le j\le N,\ 0\le k\le r}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^{N(r+1)}}
   \mathcal R_r(\mathcal P_A)\neq\varnothing.
   }
   \]

3. For rational-prime centers `c_j=\log p_j`, AF-024's nonresonance condition is automatic. Therefore **evenness, compact support, normalization at the origin, and Fourier positivity/positive definiteness do not by themselves impose any universal finite-order equality constraint on the local prime-power Weil response jets.**

The conclusion is deliberately weaker than AF-024's unconstrained smooth-germ programming. It does **not** say that every prescribed jet is attainable inside the positive-definite cone, nor that one can reproduce AF-024's exact quadratic isolator while preserving Fourier positivity. It says that the cone still has full local dimension at every finite jet level, so Fourier positivity alone cannot explain a prime-specific finite-order differential identity merely by shrinking the admissible test space.

## A compactly supported positive-definite modulation family

Choose a number `R` with

\[
\max S<R<A.
\]

Take a real bump

\[
\phi\in C_c^\infty((-R/2,R/2))
\]

that is strictly positive on `(-R/2,R/2)`, and write

\[
\widetilde\phi(x)=\phi(-x).
\]

Define the normalized autocorrelation

\[
K(x)
=
\frac{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(x)}{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(0)}.
\]

Then

\[
K\in C_c^\infty((-A,A)),
\qquad
K(-x)=K(x),
\qquad
K(0)=1.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\widehat K(\omega)
=
\frac{|\widehat\phi(\omega)|^2}{(\phi*\widetilde\phi)(0)}
\ge0,
\]

so `K` is positive definite. Because the two positive bump interiors overlap after every translation of size `<R`,

\[
\boxed{K(x)>0\qquad(|x|<R).}
\]

In particular `K(s_j)>0` for every sampling point.

For any real frequency `\xi`, set

\[
F_\xi(x)=K(x)\cos(\xi x).
\]

This retains all required constraints:

\[
F_\xi\in C_c^\infty((-A,A)),
\qquad
F_\xi(-x)=F_\xi(x),
\qquad
F_\xi(0)=1,
\]

and

\[
\widehat F_\xi(\omega)
=
\frac12\left(
\widehat K(\omega-\xi)+\widehat K(\omega+\xi)
\right)
\ge0.
\]

Hence

\[
F_\xi\in\mathcal P_A
\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb R.
\]

This construction is source-independent: the same kernel family exists before any arithmetic point is selected. The finite sample set enters only in choosing a support radius large enough that `K` is nonzero at the finitely many points being audited.

## The modulation curve has full affine jet span

Put

\[
d=M(r+1)
\]

and consider the curve

\[
v(\xi)=J_{S,r}(F_\xi)\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

Suppose its image were contained in a proper affine hyperplane. Then there would be real coefficients `a_{jk}`, not all zero, and `b\in\mathbb R` such that

\[
\sum_{j=1}^M\sum_{k=0}^r
 a_{jk}
 \left.\frac{d^k}{dx^k}\bigl(K(x)\cos(\xi x)\bigr)\right|_{x=s_j}
=b
\]

for every real `\xi`.

After writing

\[
\cos(\xi x)=\frac12(e^{i\xi x}+e^{-i\xi x}),
\]

the left side minus `b` is an exponential polynomial

\[
-b+
\sum_{j=1}^M
\left(
P_{j,+}(\xi)e^{i s_j\xi}
+
P_{j,-}(\xi)e^{-i s_j\xi}
\right),
\]

where each `P_{j,\pm}` has degree at most `r`. The frequencies

\[
0,\ \pm s_1,\ldots,\pm s_M
\]

are distinct. Classical linear independence of exponential polynomials therefore forces every polynomial coefficient and the constant term to vanish.

The highest-degree coefficient at frequency `+s_j` is

\[
\frac12 a_{j,r}K(s_j)i^r.
\]

Since `K(s_j)>0`, it follows that `a_{j,r}=0` for all `j`. Descending inductively through powers of `\xi` gives

\[
a_{j,k}=0
\]

for every `j,k`, and finally `b=0`, contradicting the assumed affine dependence.

Thus

\[
\operatorname{aff}\{v(\xi):\xi\in\mathbb R\}
=
\mathbb R^d.
\]

Choose `d+1` frequencies

\[
\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_d
\]

whose jet vectors are affinely independent. Because `\mathcal P_A` is convex, every normalized convex mixture

\[
F_w
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^d w_\ell F_{\xi_\ell},
\qquad
w_\ell\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=0}^d w_\ell=1,
\]

still lies in `\mathcal P_A`. Its jet vector is the corresponding convex mixture of the `v(\xi_\ell)`. Therefore the image contains the full `d`-dimensional simplex generated by those vertices, whose interior is nonempty in `\mathbb R^d`.

This proves the first claim.

## Transfer to finite Weil response jets

Now fix the nonresonant source block `c_1,\ldots,c_N`. The finite set of positive sample locations relevant to order `r` is already contained in

\[
S_c
=
\{m c_j:\ 1\le j\le N,\ m\ge1,\ m c_j<A\}.
\]

It is finite. Apply the preceding theorem to the distinct points of `S_c`, so the raw test jets

\[
\bigl(F^{(h)}(s)\bigr)_{s\in S_c,\ 0\le h\le r}
\]

attained by `\mathcal P_A` contain an open set in their full Euclidean coordinate space.

The response jet `\mathcal R_r(F)` is a fixed linear function of those raw jets. Indeed, differentiating

\[
G_F(t)
=
\sum_m a_m(t)F(mt),
\qquad
a_m(t)=t e^{-mt/2},
\]

shows that `G_F^{(k)}(c_j)` depends linearly only on derivatives `F^{(h)}(m c_j)` with `h\le k`.

The crucial submatrix is supplied by the first-power terms `m=1`. For a fixed `j`,

\[
\left.\frac{d^k}{dt^k}\bigl(a_1(t)F(t)\bigr)\right|_{t=c_j}
=
 a_1(c_j)F^{(k)}(c_j)
+
\text{lower-order derivatives at }c_j,
\]

with

\[
a_1(c_j)=c_j e^{-c_j/2}>0.
\]

By nonresonance, no higher-power sample from another selected generator lands at any first-power center `c_j`. Hence the columns corresponding to

\[
\bigl(F^{(k)}(c_j)\bigr)_{j,k}
\]

form block-lower-triangular response matrices with nonzero diagonal `a_1(c_j)`. The raw-jet-to-response-jet linear map therefore has full row rank

\[
N(r+1)
\]

and is surjective.

A surjective linear map between finite-dimensional real vector spaces is open. The open raw-jet set supplied above consequently maps to an open subset of

\[
\mathbb R^{N(r+1)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{int}\mathcal R_r(\mathcal P_A)\neq\varnothing.
\]

For `c_j=\log p_j`, the nonresonance condition follows from

\[
m\log p_j=\log p_k
\Longrightarrow
p_j^m=p_k,
\]

which is impossible for distinct rational primes and `m\ge2`.

## What Fourier positivity does and does not buy

AF-025 left Fourier positivity / positive definiteness as the first genuinely nonlinear, globally coupled admissibility class not covered by its response-kernel correction argument. The present result shows that this constraint is real but **not finite-jet rigid**.

Positive definiteness couples all translations of a test and rules out arbitrary bump corrections. Nevertheless, the modulation family above leaves enough freedom that every finite collection of sample derivatives, and therefore every finite collection of local Weil response derivatives at a nonresonant block, varies through a set with nonempty interior.

Consequently a proposed finite-test mechanism cannot argue that a rational-prime finite-order relation is intrinsic merely because its tests are even, compactly supported, normalized, and Fourier-positive. If a finite-order equality or singularity is supposed to be forced by that test category, it must arise from **additional structure** that cuts the full-dimensional jet image down to the claimed locus.

This is an interior statement, not an arbitrary-programming statement. In particular:

- positive definiteness implies nontrivial inequalities such as `|F(x)|\le F(0)=1`, so the jet image is not all of Euclidean space;
- the theorem does not produce every prescribed smooth germ;
- it does not prove that a chosen prime point can be made an exact isolated quadratic minimum by a Fourier-positive test;
- it does not show that a canonical source-independent positive-definite test selected by an operator or geometry has no arithmetic content.

The distinction matters. AF-024 killed **unconstrained source-tuned singularity as evidence**. AF-026 kills only the weaker hope that Fourier positivity itself forces a lower-dimensional finite-order response law.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The ingredients are classical and are not claimed as new.

- Bochner theory identifies continuous positive-definite functions with Fourier transforms of finite positive measures; Walter Rudin, ***Fourier Analysis on Groups***, Interscience (1962), and Christian Berg, Jens Peter Reus Christensen, and Paul Ressel, ***Harmonic Analysis on Semigroups***, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 100, Springer (1984), are standard references.
- Compactly supported positive-definite radial functions are classical approximation-theory objects. Holger Wendland, **“Piecewise polynomial, positive definite and compactly supported radial functions of minimal degree,”** *Advances in Computational Mathematics* 4 (1995), 389–396, DOI `10.1007/BF02123482`, and Z. M. Wu, **“Compactly supported positive definite radial functions,”** *Advances in Computational Mathematics* 4 (1995), 283–292, DOI `10.1007/BF03177517`, are foundational constructions.
- Rodrigo B. Platte, **“C-infinity Compactly Supported and Positive Definite Radial Kernels,”** *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* 37(4) (2015), A1934–A1956, DOI `10.1137/14M1000683`, explicitly constructs infinitely smooth compactly supported positive-definite kernels using convolution ideas closely neighboring the autocorrelation seed used here.
- Generalized Hermite–Birkhoff interpolation with positive-definite kernels is established in kernel approximation. Stefano De Marchi, Armin Iske, and Gabriele Santin, **“Image reconstruction from scattered Radon data by weighted positive definite kernel functions,”** *Calcolo* 55 (2018), article 1, DOI `10.1007/s10092-018-0247-6`, is neighboring prior art illustrating derivative/generalized-functional interpolation and also the importance of structural restrictions on a positive-definite kernel class.

No novelty is claimed for autocorrelation kernels, modulation, Fourier shifts, convexity of the positive-definite cone, exponential-polynomial independence, or Hermite-type interpolation ideas.

A targeted literature audit did not identify a standard theorem phrased as the exact normalized-cone statement proved here, nor its specialization to the prime-power Weil response. That absence is not treated as proof of novelty. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the **category audit**: compact support plus evenness plus normalization plus Fourier positivity still leave full-dimensional freedom at every finite raw-jet and nonresonant Weil-response-jet level.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- `\widehat F\ge0` is Fourier positivity / positive definiteness, not pointwise nonnegativity of `F`. The intersection of both cones is smaller and is not classified here.
- The result is finite-order. An infinite jet, an analytic transform identity, Paley–Wiener-type global relation, or a complete test family can be rigid even when every finite projection has interior.
- The result permits the test to vary inside the declared admissible class. It does not rule out arithmetic rigidity of one independently selected canonical test or one finite-dimensional operator-generated family.
- Full-dimensional image does not imply surjectivity. Inequalities imposed by positive definiteness remain genuine and may matter to a proposed mechanism.
- Nonresonance is used only to make the first-power source jets provide an explicit surjective block in the Weil-response map. Resonant generalized-prime controls can have additional coupling and need a separate rank audit.
- The support horizon is fixed and finite. No claim is made that one test family simultaneously realizes independent jets at all prime-power logs on an unbounded scale.
- The theorem concerns local test/source geometry only. It proves nothing about zero locations, multiplicities, simplicity, Weil positivity, or RH.

## Decisive audit test

For a finite-order explicit-formula or positivity route that invokes Fourier-positive tests as the source of arithmetic rigidity:

1. state the exact admissible test class, including whether positivity means `F\ge0`, `\widehat F\ge0`, positive definiteness, both, or an operator positivity condition;
2. write the finite raw-jet and response-jet maps actually used by the claimed mechanism;
3. if the class contains the normalized modulation simplex constructed above, do not attribute a lower-dimensional finite-order identity to Fourier positivity alone;
4. identify the additional constraint that removes the open response-jet freedom and prove that it is source-independent;
5. compare the resulting restricted jet image at the rational-prime block with matched nonresonant generalized-prime blocks;
6. only if the prime block satisfies a relation forced by the stricter category and unavailable to matched controls should the finite-order singular geometry count as a rational-prime discriminator.

The next gate is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Fourier-positive test cone}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{still full-dimensional at finite Weil-jet level};
\qquad
\text{what additional intrinsic constraint actually removes that freedom?}
}
\]

## Consequence for the line

AF-024 showed that unconstrained smooth tests can program arbitrary local separable germs. AF-025 showed that finite linear conditions, ordinary finite moments, evenness, pointwise nonnegativity, and a simple origin normalization do not by themselves cure that source-tuning problem.

AF-026 now audits the first global nonlinear class left explicitly open there. **Fourier positivity / positive definiteness does constrain the test globally, but it still leaves nonempty interior in every finite local response-jet space at a nonresonant source block.**

This rules out another broad class of superficial escapes: no universal finite-order prime identity can be credited to Fourier positivity alone. The remaining frontier is narrower and more structural—simultaneous time- and Fourier-side positivity, fixed low-dimensional or operator-generated test families, analytic/Paley–Wiener relations that couple infinitely many jets, or another independently forced category whose local response image can be proved genuinely smaller and then tested against matched generalized-prime controls.
