# AF-546 — Low-degree calibration flows have an affine-projective invariant hierarchy

## Status

`EXACT-DERIVED` · `CLASSICAL-INGREDIENTS` · `NONLINEAR-RECOVERY` · `CALIBRATION` · `SHARED-NUISANCE` · `AFFINE-INVARIANCE` · `PROJECTIVE-INVARIANCE` · `MINIMAL-RELATIONAL-LIFT` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-545 identified the exact decoder field controlling a shared calibration nuisance. The next structural question is whether one must reconstruct that nuisance accurately, or whether enough matched source branches can quotient it out relationally.

The answer has a sharp low-degree hierarchy.

Let

\[
y=G(z,u),
\qquad G_z\neq0,
\]

and let `u_0` be the true calibration. As in AF-545, write

\[
\Phi_u(z):=\Psi_u(G(z,u_0))
\]

for the local decoder transport and let `q` be a `C^1` readout with `q'\neq0` on the interval of interest. Define

\[
K_q(z,u)
:=-q'(z)\frac{G_u(z,u)}{G_z(z,u)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Use the readout itself as local coordinate,

\[
Q=q(z),
\]

and define the induced shared-calibration vector field

\[
\boxed{
V(Q,u):=K_q(q^{-1}(Q),u).
}
\tag{2}
\]

For every source branch `z_i`, the decoded readout trajectory

\[
Q_i(u):=q(\Phi_u(z_i))
\]

then satisfies the exact scalar flow equation

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u Q_i=V(Q_i,u).
}
\tag{3}
\]

At each fixed `u`, the degree of `V` in the readout coordinate classifies which low-arity relational observable removes the common calibration exactly:

1. if `V(Q,u)=b(u)`, the flow is translational and every two-point difference `Q_2-Q_1` is invariant;
2. if `V(Q,u)=a(u)Q+b(u)`, the flow is affine and every three-point ratio of differences
   \[
   A_{123}:=\frac{Q_2-Q_1}{Q_3-Q_1}
   \tag{4}
   \]
   is invariant;
3. if
   \[
   V(Q,u)=a(u)+b(u)Q+c(u)Q^2,
   \tag{5}
   \]
   the flow is Riccati/projective and every four-point cross-ratio
   \[
   R_{1234}
   :=
   \frac{(Q_1-Q_3)(Q_2-Q_4)}{(Q_1-Q_4)(Q_2-Q_3)}
   \tag{6}
   \]
   is invariant wherever the four points remain distinct.

Conversely, on a connected local interval, invariance of **all** such two-, three-, or four-point relational observables forces `V` respectively to be constant, affine, or quadratic in `Q` (with `C^3` regularity for the cross-ratio converse).

Thus the AF-545 common-mode quotient is only the first member of a hierarchy:

\[
\text{translation nuisance}
\subset
\text{affine nuisance}
\subset
\text{projective/Riccati nuisance}.
\]

Each enlargement of the nuisance class costs one extra matched branch but preserves an exact relational observable. In this sense, matched-source arity is a **minimal relational lift**: the nuisance can be removed without first recovering the calibration parameter itself.

The same hierarchy has a quantitative defect form. Deviations from translation, affine, and projective nuisance are controlled respectively by the first, second, and third `Q`-derivatives of `V`.

## Decoder-flow reduction

AF-545 proved

\[
\frac{d}{du}q(\Phi_u(z))
=K_q(\Phi_u(z),u).
\tag{7}
\]

Since `q` is a local coordinate, `Q_i=q(\Phi_u(z_i))` implies

\[
K_q(\Phi_u(z_i),u)
=V(Q_i,u),
\]

which gives `(3)`.

This step is conceptually important. After the decoder is reduced to the readout coordinate, the original nonlinear inverse problem enters the common-calibration question only through the induced scalar field `V`. Exact nuisance removal can therefore be studied as an invariant problem for the common flow generated by `V`.

The field `V` is not an arbitrary new wrapper: by `(1)` and `(2)` it is fixed by the original observation map, the chosen target/readout, and the actual inverse sensitivity. A change of latent coordinate leaves AF-545's `K_q` unchanged when `G` and `q` are transported together, so this reduction inherits the coordinate audit already established there.

## Exact invariant hierarchy

### 1. Two branches: translations

For two decoded branches,

\[
D_{12}:=Q_2-Q_1,
\]

and `(3)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u D_{12}=V(Q_2,u)-V(Q_1,u).
}
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore all pair differences are calibration invariant if `V` is independent of `Q`. Conversely, if `(8)` vanishes for every pair in a connected interval, `V(\cdot,u)` takes the same value at every point of that interval. This is precisely AF-545's translation criterion in the readout coordinate.

### 2. Three branches: affine shape

For three distinct branches define `(4)`. Differentiating its logarithm gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u\log|A_{123}|
=
\frac{V(Q_2,u)-V(Q_1,u)}{Q_2-Q_1}
-
\frac{V(Q_3,u)-V(Q_1,u)}{Q_3-Q_1}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

If

\[
V(Q,u)=a(u)Q+b(u),
\]

both secant slopes in `(9)` equal `a(u)`, so `A_{123}` is exactly invariant.

Conversely, suppose every nondegenerate three-point ratio is invariant. Then `(9)` vanishes for every triple, so for every base point `Q_1`, all secant slopes

\[
\frac{V(Q,u)-V(Q_1,u)}{Q-Q_1}
\]

are equal. Hence `V(\cdot,u)` is affine on the connected interval. Equivalently, the common decoder flow acts by

\[
Q\longmapsto \alpha(u)Q+\beta(u)
\]

relative to the reference calibration, wherever the flow is defined.

### 3. Four branches: projective shape

For four distinct branches define `(6)`. Its logarithmic derivative is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_u\log|R_{1234}|
={}&
\frac{V_1-V_3}{Q_1-Q_3}
+
\frac{V_2-V_4}{Q_2-Q_4}\\
&-
\frac{V_1-V_4}{Q_1-Q_4}
-
\frac{V_2-V_3}{Q_2-Q_3},
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{10}
\]

where `V_i:=V(Q_i,u)`.

The right-hand side annihilates every quadratic polynomial in `Q`. Constants cancel termwise; a linear term contributes the same constant to each secant; for `V(Q)=Q^2`, each secant equals the sum of its endpoints and the four sums cancel. Therefore every field `(5)` preserves the cross-ratio exactly.

This is the Riccati case. The nonautonomous scalar equation

\[
\partial_u Q=a(u)+b(u)Q+c(u)Q^2
\tag{11}
\]

is the projectivization of a two-dimensional linear system, so its local flow maps are fractional-linear/Möbius transformations. Cross-ratio preservation is therefore also the standard projective invariant of the Riccati flow.

The converse has an equally local differential form. Fix `u` and write `v(Q)=V(Q,u)`. Evaluate `(10)` on four equally spaced points

\[
Q,\quad Q+h,\quad Q+2h,\quad Q+3h.
\]

For `v\in C^3`, Taylor expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u\log|R|
=
\frac{h^2}{6}v'''(Q)+o(h^2).
}
\tag{12}
\]

(With one additional derivative the remainder is `O(h^3)`.) Hence if every sufficiently small nondegenerate cross-ratio is invariant, `(12)` forces

\[
v'''(Q)=0
\]

throughout the interval, so `V(\cdot,u)` is quadratic. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{all four-point cross-ratios are invariant}
\iff
V(\cdot,u)\text{ is quadratic in }Q
}
\tag{13}
\]

locally, under the stated regularity and nondegeneracy assumptions.

## Quantitative defect hierarchy

The exact classifications have stable approximate versions. They show that each successive relational quotient suppresses one additional order of smooth common-mode variation.

### Pair contrast defect

Equation `(8)` and the mean-value theorem give

\[
|\partial_u D_{12}|
\le
\|V_Q\|_{\infty}|Q_2-Q_1|.
\tag{14}
\]

This is the AF-545 inverse-sensitivity variation bound in `Q` coordinates.

### Affine-shape defect

Assume `|V_{QQ}|\le M_2` on the interval containing `Q_1,Q_2,Q_3`. The two terms in `(9)` are secant slopes from the same base point. Writing each as an average of `V_Q` along its segment gives

\[
\boxed{
|\partial_u\log|A_{123}||
\le
\frac{M_2}{2}|Q_2-Q_3|.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus a common field may have large absolute sensitivity and substantial linear variation without harming affine shape. Only curvature of the sensitivity field survives this quotient.

### Projective-shape defect

Assume `|V_{QQQ}|\le M_3` on an interval containing the four points, and let `d` be their diameter. Subtract the quadratic Taylor polynomial of `V` about any point in that interval. Expression `(10)` annihilates that polynomial. For the remainder `E`, Taylor's theorem gives

\[
\|E_Q\|_{\infty}\le \frac{M_3}{2}d^2.
\]

Each of the four secant terms in `(10)` is therefore bounded by `M_3d^2/2`, yielding the safe non-sharp estimate

\[
\boxed{
|\partial_u\log|R_{1234}||
\le
2M_3d^2.
}
\tag{16}
\]

If these derivative bounds and point-diameter bounds hold uniformly for `u` between two calibrations, integration gives finite-calibration-error bounds. For example, over an interval of calibration length `|\varepsilon|`, `(15)` and `(16)` become respectively

\[
|\Delta\log|A_{123}||
\le
\frac{|\varepsilon|M_2D}{2},
\tag{17}
\]

and

\[
|\Delta\log|R_{1234}||
\le
2|\varepsilon|M_3D^2,
\tag{18}
\]

when `D` uniformly bounds the relevant branch separation/diameter along the decoder tube.

So the hierarchy is not merely exact group theory. It supplies target-relative stability scales:

- first variation `V_Q` controls two-point differencing;
- second variation `V_{QQ}` controls three-point affine shape;
- third variation `V_{QQQ}` controls four-point projective shape.

## Minimal relational lift

The branch counts `2,3,4` are generically minimal for these scalar transformation groups, modulo discrete ordering/orientation information and after restricting to a nondegenerate connected component.

A translation acts transitively on single points, so one scalar branch has no nonconstant translation invariant; two branches supply a difference. The one-dimensional affine group acts transitively on ordered pairs of distinct points, so a nonconstant affine shape first appears at three points, as a ratio of differences. The projective/Möbius group acts transitively on ordered triples of distinct projective-line points, so a nonconstant projective shape first appears at four points, as the cross-ratio.

This gives a concrete Arithmetic Fidelity interpretation. If the compressed decoder retains several matched source branches under the **same** nuisance realization, one need not necessarily reconstruct the nuisance coordinate. Instead one may lift from individual decoded values to the smallest relational configuration on which the nuisance group has a nontrivial invariant.

The lift is therefore not arbitrary extra metadata. Its required arity is dictated by the transformation class induced by the actual inverse-sensitivity field.

## Exact falsification controls

The hierarchy separates cleanly under three controls.

### Affine but not translational

Take

\[
V(Q)=Q.
\]

Then pair differences satisfy

\[
\partial_u(Q_2-Q_1)=Q_2-Q_1
\]

and are not invariant, but every three-point ratio `(4)` is invariant.

### Projective but not affine

Take

\[
V(Q)=Q^2.
\]

For `Q_1=0`, `Q_2=1`, `Q_3=2`, equation `(9)` gives

\[
\partial_u\log|A_{123}|=1-2=-1,
\]

so affine shape drifts. Equation `(10)` nevertheless vanishes for every quadruple, so cross-ratio is invariant.

### Cubic escape from projective fidelity

Take

\[
V(Q)=\delta Q^3,
\qquad \delta\neq0,
\]

and the equally spaced quadruple

\[
Q,\ Q+h,\ Q+2h,\ Q+3h.
\]

Substitution into `(10)` gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u\log|R|=\delta h^2.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus a genuine cubic component is visible to the four-point projective invariant. This is the sharp local control behind the third-derivative defect in `(12)` and `(16)`.

These controls rule out the weaker interpretation that “more branches automatically cancel more nuisance.” Cancellation occurs only when the shared decoder field lies in, or is close to, the transformation algebra quotiented by the chosen relational observable.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

None of the geometric ingredients is new.

The simple ratio of three collinear points is a classical affine invariant. The cross-ratio of four projective-line points is a classical projective invariant. Riccati equations are classically related to two-dimensional linear systems and linear-fractional transformations; the preservation of cross-ratios for four Riccati solution curves is also standard in geometric/integrable-system settings.

The durable contribution here is therefore narrower and Mathia-specific:

1. AF-545's exact inverse-calibration sensitivity field `K_q=-q'G_u/G_z` is promoted to the induced readout flow `V` without changing the underlying inverse problem;
2. the degree of that **actual decoder nuisance field** gives an if-and-only-if classification of whether two-, three-, or four-branch relational observables remove the shared calibration;
3. departures from the exact nuisance groups are quantified by the successive derivatives `V_Q`, `V_{QQ}`, and `V_{QQQ}`;
4. the classical translation/affine/projective invariants become a concrete minimal-lift rule for Arithmetic Fidelity: retain enough matched branches to quotient the nuisance class rather than demanding branchwise recovery of the calibration.

No novelty is claimed for affine ratios, cross-ratios, Riccati/Möbius correspondence, or their classical transformation-group interpretation.

## Boundaries

- The result is local in a region where the decoder exists, `G_z\neq0`, and `q` is a valid local coordinate (`q'\neq0`).
- The relational observables require nondegenerate branches: denominators in `(4)` and `(6)` must stay nonzero.
- Projective Riccati flows may encounter poles in an affine chart. The invariant statement is naturally projective; the scalar formulas are valid on intervals avoiding such singularities.
- The `2,3,4` arity statement is generic for continuous scalar invariants on nondegenerate components. Discrete order/orientation data can survive at lower arity under restricted transformation subgroups.
- Equations `(15)`–`(18)` are stability bounds, not claims of optimal constants. Their role is to identify the derivative order that survives each quotient.
- An arbitrary four-point statistic does not inherit projective cancellation. The cross-ratio is load bearing, just as the choice of readout `q` is load bearing in AF-545.
- This finding does not yet show that a prime-derived decoder has an affine or projective shared-calibration field. Such a specialization must compute its actual `V` and compare the surviving relational signal against matched non-prime controls.
- The result concerns **shared** nuisance. If branches use independent calibrations, the common transformation-group action is broken and the invariant need not cancel the differential errors.
