# AF-005 — Monomial phase lifts are controlled exactly by an annihilator lattice

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`

## Claim

Let

\[
K=\mathbb T^n=(S^1)^n
\]

be a residual phase space and let `H <= K` be the **closed subgroup that is intentionally allowed to remain invisible**. For an exponent vector

\[
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]

write

\[
\chi_a(z)=z^a=\prod_{i=1}^n z_i^{a_i}
\]

for the corresponding character of `K`. Given finitely many monomial phase observables with exponent rows

\[
a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(m)}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]

let

\[
L=\langle a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(m)}\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\subseteq\mathbb Z^n
\]

be their integer row lattice and define

\[
Q_L(z)=\bigl(\chi_{a^{(1)}}(z),\ldots,\chi_{a^{(m)}}(z)\bigr).
\]

Then:

1. the residual ambiguity of the complete monomial family is exactly the annihilator
   \[
   L^\perp=\{r\in K:r^a=1\text{ for every }a\in L\};
   \]
   equivalently,
   \[
   Q_L(z)=Q_L(w)\iff wz^{-1}\in L^\perp;
   \]
2. all of the chosen observables are `H`-invariant if and only if
   \[
   L\subseteq H^\perp,
   \qquad
   H^\perp=\{a\in\mathbb Z^n:h^a=1\text{ for every }h\in H\};
   \]
3. under that invariance condition, the observables are **complete modulo exactly `H`** if and only if
   \[
   L^\perp=H,
   \]
   which by Pontryagin double-annihilator duality is equivalent to the integer-lattice condition
   \[
   \boxed{L=H^\perp};
   \]
4. if `A\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n}` has the exponent vectors as rows, has rank `r`, and Smith normal form with nonzero invariant factors
   \[
   d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r,
   \]
   then
   \[
   \ker Q_L\cong
   \mathbb T^{\,n-r}\times
   \mu_{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mu_{d_r},
   \]
   where `\mu_d` is the group of `d`-th roots of unity;
5. therefore graph connectivity, real rank, or rational span of the coupling relations can certify at most the **continuous** part of the residual ambiguity. Non-unit Smith factors detect finite phase aliases that those coarser tests miss.

Consequently, for phase-type residual fibers, the right exact object is not merely a coupling graph but the **integer relation lattice** generated by the admissible observables. Faithfulness modulo a prescribed gauge is a lattice-generation problem.

This is classical character-lattice / Pontryagin-duality / Smith-normal-form mathematics. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is to turn it into a concrete audit for relational lifts: before interpreting a web of couplings as having restored provenance, compute the subgroup of phase freedom that the entire integer relation lattice actually kills.

## Derivation

For `z,w\in K`, put

\[
r=wz^{-1}\in K.
\]

Because every `\chi_a` is a character,

\[
\chi_a(w)=\chi_a(z)\chi_a(r).
\]

Hence all selected monomial observables agree on `z` and `w` exactly when

\[
\chi_{a^{(j)}}(r)=1
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,m).
\]

The same then holds for every integer combination of the exponent rows, so this is equivalent to

\[
r\in L^\perp.
\]

That proves the fiber statement.

Now let `H<=K` be closed. A character `\chi_a` is constant on `H`-cosets exactly when

\[
\chi_a(h)=1
\qquad\text{for every }h\in H,
\]

which is exactly `a\in H^\perp`. Thus the whole observable family is `H`-invariant if and only if

\[
L\subseteq H^\perp.
\]

If this holds, `H\subseteq L^\perp`, so the selected observables can never distinguish points intentionally identified by the gauge. They are complete modulo `H` precisely when they introduce **no additional identifications**, i.e.

\[
L^\perp=H.
\]

Pontryagin duality for locally compact abelian groups gives the double-annihilator identities

\[
(H^\perp)^\perp=H
\]

for closed `H<=K` and

\[
(L^\perp)^\perp=L
\]

for `L<=\widehat K\cong\mathbb Z^n` (all subgroups of the discrete dual are closed). Therefore

\[
L^\perp=H
\iff
L=(L^\perp)^\perp=H^\perp.
\]

So exact recovery modulo the intended phase gauge is equivalent to generating its full annihilator lattice.

## Smith-normal-form certificate

Let the exponent rows form an integer matrix

\[
A\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n}.
\]

The associated compact-torus homomorphism is

\[
Q_A:\mathbb T^n\to\mathbb T^m,
\qquad
(Q_A(z))_j=\prod_i z_i^{a_{ji}}.
\]

Choose unimodular integer matrices `U,V` putting `A` in Smith normal form,

\[
UAV=D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0).
\]

Unimodular changes of rows and columns induce automorphisms of the target and source tori, so they do not change the isomorphism type of the kernel. For `D`, the kernel equations are simply

\[
z_i^{d_i}=1\quad (1\le i\le r),
\]

with the remaining `n-r` coordinates free. Therefore

\[
\ker Q_A
\cong
\mu_{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mu_{d_r}\times\mathbb T^{n-r}.
\]

This separates two different defects in a coupling family:

- `n-r>0` gives a continuous residual phase freedom;
- a factor `d_i>1` gives a finite residual ambiguity even after the continuous rank obstruction has vanished as far as that coordinate is concerned.

Thus ordinary rank tests see only part of the fidelity problem.

## Exact counterexample: the coupling graph can be right while the lattice is wrong

Take

\[
K=\mathbb T^2
\]

and let the intended invisible symmetry be common phase,

\[
H=\{(u,u):u\in\mathbb T\}.
\]

Its annihilator is

\[
H^\perp=\mathbb Z(1,-1).
\]

The observable

\[
q_1(z)=z_1z_2^{-1}
\]

has exponent row `(1,-1)`, so its row lattice equals `H^\perp` and

\[
\ker q_1=H.
\]

It is complete modulo common phase.

Now replace it by

\[
q_2(z)=z_1^2z_2^{-2}.
\]

The support graph is identical: the same two coordinates are coupled. The real and rational row spans are also identical. But the integer lattice is only

\[
2\mathbb Z(1,-1)
\subsetneq
\mathbb Z(1,-1)=H^\perp.
\]

Its kernel satisfies

\[
(z_1/z_2)^2=1,
\]

so

\[
\ker q_2/H\cong\mu_2.
\]

There is one extra binary phase alias. The Smith invariant is `d_1=2`, which detects exactly the ambiguity missed by graph connectivity and rank.

This example is a decisive warning for proposed relational rescues: knowing **which variables interact** is weaker than knowing the primitive integer relations generated by those interactions.

## Minimal number of unrestricted monomial observables

Because every subgroup of `\mathbb Z^n` is free abelian, write

\[
s=\operatorname{rank} H^\perp.
\]

Any family of scalar monomial characters that is complete modulo `H` must generate `H^\perp`, so it contains at least `s` generators. Conversely, any integer basis of `H^\perp` gives exactly `s` characters whose common kernel is `H`. Hence, if **arbitrary** monomial characters are admissible, the minimum number is exactly

\[
\operatorname{rank}H^\perp=n-\dim H.
\]

This minimality is mathematically correct but, like AF-001's unrestricted lift count, is too weak to be the final theory. A basis vector may have very large coefficients, high correlation order, nonlocal support, or no intrinsic meaning in the originating construction. The nontrivial problem is therefore to generate `H^\perp` subject to the actual admissibility constraints: bounded order, locality, symmetry, positivity, available tensor degree, operator category, or another natural restriction.

## Relation to AF-004

AF-004 considered a signal on a finite abelian group `G` with nonzero Fourier coefficients and showed that power spectrum plus bispectrum is complete modulo translation.

After equality of the power spectra has fixed all Fourier magnitudes, the remaining ratio vector

\[
r(\chi)=\widehat g(\chi)/\widehat f(\chi)
\]

lies in the phase torus indexed by `\widehat G`. Equality of bispectra imposes the monomial relations

\[
r(\chi)r(\psi)r(\chi\psi)^{-1}=1,
\]

whose exponent rows are

\[
e_\chi+e_\psi-e_{\chi\psi}.
\]

AF-004 proved directly that the common kernel of these relations is precisely the finite subgroup

\[
H_G=\{(\overline{\chi(t)})_{\chi\in\widehat G}:t\in G\}
\]

coming from translations. The present theorem therefore implies, equivalently, that the triadic bispectral relation rows generate the full annihilator lattice

\[
H_G^\perp.
\]

This turns the phase-ratio proof into a reusable integer-lattice certificate. For a different higher-order correlation family, one can build its exponent matrix and ask directly whether its row lattice equals the annihilator of the intended gauge.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The character group of `\mathbb T^n` is canonically `\mathbb Z^n`, and the annihilator correspondence between closed subgroups and quotient characters is standard Pontryagin duality. Morris gives the double-annihilator theorem and the duality between subgroups and quotients for locally compact abelian groups.

The description of torus morphisms by integer exponent matrices and of monomial maps through the lattice generated by their characters is standard toric/nonlinear algebra. Michałek and Sturmfels develop this character-lattice description explicitly in their chapter on toric varieties.

Smith normal form and its interpretation as the invariant-factor decomposition of a finitely generated abelian quotient are classical; Stanley's survey gives a modern account of the algebraic meaning of the invariant factors.

No novelty is claimed for any of these ingredients or for the theorem above. The line-specific synthesis is the **fidelity certificate**:

\[
\text{admissible coupling exponents}
\longrightarrow
\text{integer row lattice }L
\longrightarrow
L^\perp
\longrightarrow
\text{actual residual gauge}.
\]

It sharpens AF-004's qualitative lesson that higher-order coupling can restore phase into an exact criterion that also detects hidden finite aliases.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- This finding concerns compact abelian phase fibers and character/monomial observables. Nonabelian residual symmetries require representation-theoretic rather than scalar-character data.
- The theorem assumes the relevant residual ambiguity can honestly be modeled as a torus action. It should not be imposed on a concrete compression merely because phases are present somewhere in its notation.
- Completeness is modulo a **closed subgroup** `H`. If the intended equivalence is not a subgroup action, the annihilator formalism is not the right quotient model.
- The Smith decomposition classifies the kernel of the integer-character map; it does not by itself justify why those character observables are natural, measurable, local, positive, or available to a downstream operator.
- Minimal scalar count is not minimal mathematical complexity. Large exponent vectors can smuggle in highly nonlocal or high-order information.
- A support/coupling graph is still useful as a first combinatorial diagnostic, but connectedness does not imply primitive lattice generation and cannot detect finite torsion aliases.
- Nothing here identifies the rational-prime discriminator or establishes that any existing Mathia RH line has a residual torus with the required admissible character family.

## Decisive audit test for phase-coupling lifts

When a proposed lift restores information by products or higher-order couplings among phase-like coordinates:

1. identify the exact residual compact phase group `K` after the lower-order compression;
2. identify the intended invisible gauge `H<=K`;
3. express every admissible coupling as a character and form its integer exponent lattice `L`;
4. compute whether
   \[
   L=H^\perp;
   \]
5. for a finite presentation, compute Smith normal form to expose both continuous kernel dimension and finite torsion aliases;
6. only after exact quotient fidelity passes, ask whether the required generators obey the construction's order, locality, naturality, or operator constraints.

A proper inclusion

\[
L\subsetneq H^\perp
\]

is a complete no-go certificate for that monomial observable family: every downstream function of those couplings remains blind to the nontrivial quotient `L^\perp/H`.

## Consequence for the line

Replace **coupling connectivity** by **primitive integer-lattice generation** as the exact invariant for abelian phase-coupling recovery.

This creates a tractable next layer beyond AF-004. For any proposed bounded-order or local correlation family, the mathematical question is no longer merely whether its interaction hypergraph connects the coordinates; it is whether the allowed exponent vectors generate the full annihilator lattice of the intended gauge, and which Smith factors remain when they do not. That formulation can produce exact no-go results before any numerical reconstruction experiment and can later be tested against arithmetic representations only when such a phase/gauge model is genuinely present.
