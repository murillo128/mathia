# AF-259 — Congruence cancellation is odd-multiplier continued-fraction leakage

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `DIOPHANTINE-FIDELITY`, `PROFINITE-COVERAGE`, `LI-DOMINANT-MODE-CONTROL`, `NO-BROAD-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-258 reduces periodic-complete two-mode fidelity to the congruence-cylinder exponents

\[
K_{M,r}(\alpha)
:=
\limsup_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)}}
\frac{-\log d_\alpha(n)}{n},
\qquad
 d_\alpha(n)=\operatorname{dist}\!\left(n\alpha,\mathbb Z+\frac12\right),
\tag{1}
\]

but leaves those cylinder exponents as raw subsequence quantities. They admit an exact continued-fraction classification.

Let

\[
\alpha=[a_0;a_1,a_2,\ldots],
\qquad \frac{p_j}{q_j}
\tag{2}
\]

be the simple continued-fraction convergents of an irrational `alpha`, and write

\[
\varepsilon_j:=|q_j\alpha-p_j|.
\tag{3}
\]

For every modulus `M>=1` and residue `r mod M`, define the odd-multiplier congruence family

\[
\mathcal C_{M,r}
:=
\left\{
(j,g):
q_j\ \text{even},\ g\ge1\ \text{odd},\
\frac{gq_j}{2}\equiv r\pmod M
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
K_{M,r}(\alpha)
=
\limsup_{\substack{(j,g)\in\mathcal C_{M,r}\\gq_j\to\infty\\g\varepsilon_j<1}}
\frac{2}{gq_j}
\log\frac{2}{g\varepsilon_j},
}
\tag{5}
\]

with value `0` when the displayed family has no unbounded subsequence. Equivalently, using the standard convergent estimates,

\[
\boxed{
K_{M,r}(\alpha)
=
2\limsup_{\substack{(j,g)\in\mathcal C_{M,r}\\gq_j\to\infty}}
\frac{[\log(q_{j+1}/g)]_+}{gq_j}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus a congruence cylinder does not require a new approximation theory independent of AF-257. Every exponentially dangerous sample is an **odd multiple of half an even principal-convergent denominator**, and the odd multiplier both decides which residue classes are reached and dilutes the exponential cancellation rate by essentially a factor `1/g`.

For the trivial modulus, `g=1` is asymptotically optimal in `(6)`, recovering AF-257:

\[
K_{1,0}(\alpha)
=
2\limsup_{\substack{j\to\infty\\q_j\ \mathrm{even}}}
\frac{\log q_{j+1}}{q_j}
=
\kappa_{1/2}(\alpha).
\tag{7}
\]

Combining `(6)` with AF-258 gives an explicit continued-fraction representation of the profinite cancellation exponent

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{pc}}(\alpha)
=
\inf_{M\ge1}\min_{r\bmod M}
2\limsup_{\substack{q_j\ \mathrm{even},\ g\ \mathrm{odd}\\
gq_j/2\equiv r\ (M)}}
\frac{[\log(q_{j+1}/g)]_+}{gq_j}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This converts the source-specific question left by AF-258 from an arbitrary-subsequence problem into a concrete arithmetic one: how the exponentially large continued-fraction jumps of the actual Keiper phase are distributed after taking bounded-cost odd multiples of the corresponding half-denominators.

## Why it matters

AF-258 shows that one safe congruence cylinder is enough to force full two-mode root-rate fidelity for every periodically complete retained set, but it does not say how to prove that a cylinder is safe from the continued fraction of the source phase. Equation `(6)` supplies that missing bridge.

There are two independent resources. A principal convergent with even denominator `q_j` creates a base cancellation event at `q_j/2`; the jump `log q_{j+1}/q_j` measures its exponential strength. An odd multiplier `g` transports that event to the sample index `gq_j/2`, hence potentially into a different residue class, but the same multiplication also weakens the exponent. Congruence reachability and cancellation strength are therefore coupled rather than separate.

This yields a useful positive-threshold principle. Put

\[
\lambda_j:=\frac{2\log q_{j+1}}{q_j}
\qquad(q_j\ \mathrm{even}).
\tag{9}
\]

If one term in `(6)` is at least `c>0`, then necessarily

\[
g\le \frac{\lambda_j}{c}.
\tag{10}
\]

So a fixed positive detectability margin only needs to audit **small odd multipliers relative to the dangerous jump size**. When the parity-filtered jump rates are bounded by `L`, every `c`-level congruence collapse is generated by one of finitely many odd multipliers `g<=L/c`. Periodic-complete fidelity is therefore a finite-multiplier congruence problem at each fixed positive margin, even though the sampler itself ranges over infinitely many profinite constraints.

A coarse but source-useful divisor corollary follows immediately. For an integer `d>=2`, define

\[
\Lambda_d^{\mathrm{out}}(\alpha)
:=
\limsup_{\substack{j\to\infty\\q_j\ \mathrm{even}\\d\nmid(q_j/2)}}
\lambda_j,
\tag{11}
\]

with value `0` if the indexing set is finite. If `r` is nonzero modulo `d`, then every pair contributing to the cylinder `n congruent r (mod d)` must have `d not divide q_j/2`; odd multiplication cannot move a multiple of `d` into a nonzero residue. Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,r}(\alpha)
\le
\Lambda_d^{\mathrm{out}}(\alpha)
\qquad(r\not\equiv0\pmod d),
}
\tag{12}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{pc}}(\alpha)
\le
\inf_{d\ge2}\Lambda_d^{\mathrm{out}}(\alpha).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Equation `(13)` generalizes the mechanism used in AF-258's explicit counterexample: catastrophic ambient approximation can coexist with `K_pc=0` when all exponentially dangerous half-denominators become trapped in a fixed divisibility class.

## Exact derivation

### 1. Every positive exponential event reduces to an even principal convergent

Fix a sequence `n -> infinity` in one cylinder `n congruent r (mod M)` on which

\[
A_\alpha(n):=\frac{-\log d_\alpha(n)}{n}
\tag{14}
\]

stays bounded below by some `c>0`. Choose the nearest half-integer as `P_n/2` with `P_n` odd. Then

\[
\left|\alpha-\frac{P_n}{2n}\right|
=
\frac{d_\alpha(n)}{n}
\le
\frac{e^{-cn}}{n}.
\tag{15}
\]

Let

\[
g_n:=\gcd(P_n,2n).
\tag{16}
\]

Because `P_n` is odd, `g_n` is odd. Reduce

\[
\frac{P_n}{2n}=\frac{p}{q},
\qquad
p=\frac{P_n}{g_n},
\qquad
q=\frac{2n}{g_n}.
\tag{17}
\]

The reduced denominator `q` is still even and `p` is odd. For sufficiently large `n`, the exponential bound `(15)` is much smaller than `1/(2q^2)`, so Legendre's theorem forces `p/q` to be a principal convergent `p_j/q_j` of `alpha`. Consequently

\[
q_j=\frac{2n}{g_n},
\qquad
n=\frac{g_nq_j}{2},
\qquad
g_n\ \text{odd},
\tag{18}
\]

and the cylinder condition becomes

\[
\frac{g_nq_j}{2}\equiv r\pmod M.
\tag{19}
\]

Moreover, with `epsilon_j` from `(3)`, reduction is exact:

\[
d_\alpha(n)
=
\left|n\alpha-\frac{g_np_j}{2}\right|
=
\frac{g_n\varepsilon_j}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore every positive-exponent cylinder sequence is eventually represented by a pair in `C_{M,r}` and satisfies

\[
A_\alpha(n)
=
\frac{2}{g_nq_j}
\log\frac{2}{g_n\varepsilon_j}.
\tag{21}
\]

This gives the upper bound in `(5)`.

### 2. Every sufficiently good odd multiple produces a genuine cylinder event

Conversely, take any even-denominator principal convergent `p_j/q_j` and odd `g` satisfying the congruence in `(4)`. Since `p_j` is automatically odd, `gp_j/2` is an admissible half-integer at

\[
n=\frac{gq_j}{2}.
\tag{22}
\]

If

\[
g\varepsilon_j<1,
\tag{23}
\]

then

\[
\left|n\alpha-\frac{gp_j}{2}\right|
=
\frac{g\varepsilon_j}{2}<\frac12.
\tag{24}
\]

Half-integers are spaced by one, so `gp_j/2` is then the unique nearest half-integer. Hence `(20)` and `(21)` hold exactly. Taking the limsup over all unbounded such pairs proves the reverse inequality in `(5)`. Pairs violating `(23)` cannot contribute a positive limiting exponent, since their candidate error is already of order one.

### 3. Denominator growth gives the simpler odd-multiplier formula

For principal convergents one has the standard estimates

\[
\frac{1}{q_j+q_{j+1}}
<
\varepsilon_j
<
\frac{1}{q_{j+1}}.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus, whenever `g<q_{j+1}`,

\[
\frac{2}{gq_j}\log\frac{2q_{j+1}}{g}
<
\frac{2}{gq_j}\log\frac{2}{g\varepsilon_j}
<
\frac{2}{gq_j}\log\frac{2(q_j+q_{j+1})}{g}.
\tag{26}
\]

Because `q_j<q_{j+1}`, the two outer logarithms differ from `log(q_{j+1}/g)` by a uniformly bounded additive amount. After division by `gq_j` that discrepancy tends to zero along every unbounded pair family. If `g>=q_{j+1}`, the positive part `[log(q_{j+1}/g)]_+` vanishes, while such pairs cannot carry a positive limsup in `(5)`. This proves `(6)`.

For `M=1`, every odd `g` is congruence-admissible. The expression

\[
\frac{[\log(q_{j+1}/g)]_+}{g}
\tag{27}
\]

is maximized at `g=1`, giving `(7)` and checking compatibility with AF-257.

### 4. Positive thresholds see only bounded-cost residue transport

From `(6)` and `(9)`, the contribution of `(j,g)` is

\[
\frac{\lambda_j}{g}
-
\frac{2\log g}{gq_j}
\tag{28}
\]

whenever `g<q_{j+1}`, and zero after positive truncation otherwise. If `(28)>=c`, then in particular `lambda_j/g>=c`, proving `(10)`.

For `(12)`, take a nonzero residue `r mod d`. If `d divides q_j/2`, then every odd multiple `gq_j/2` is still divisible by `d`, so it cannot lie in that cylinder. Every pair that can contribute to `K_{d,r}` therefore has `d not divide q_j/2`. Dropping the nonnegative multiplier penalty from `(28)` gives

\[
K_{d,r}(\alpha)
\le
\limsup_{\substack{j\to\infty\\q_j\ \mathrm{even}\\d\nmid(q_j/2)}}
\lambda_j,
\tag{29}
\]

which is `(12)`. Choosing, for each `d`, any nonzero residue and then using AF-258's definition of `K_pc` proves `(13)`.

For a hypothetical off-critical zero from AF-255--AF-258,

\[
a_\rho=1-\frac1\rho=r_\rho e^{i\theta_\rho},
\qquad
q_\rho=r_\rho^{-1}>1,
\qquad
\alpha_\rho=\frac{\theta_\rho}{\pi},
\tag{30}
\]

assume `alpha_rho` is irrational and let `p_j/q_j` be its convergents. AF-258 says that every periodically complete output sampler preserves a uniform strictly superunit pair rate exactly when

\[
K_{\mathrm{pc}}(\alpha_\rho)<\log q_\rho.
\tag{31}
\]

The new formula makes a concrete sufficient source theorem available. It is enough to find one `d>=2` for which

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_d^{\mathrm{out}}(\alpha_\rho)
<
\log q_\rho.
}
\tag{32}
\]

Full pair-rate fidelity follows from the stronger condition `Lambda_d^out(alpha_rho)=0`. At large zero height AF-255 gives

\[
\log q_\rho
\asymp

\tag{33}

so the required source control is now sharply localized: exponentially large even-denominator continued-fraction jumps above the zero-dependent radial margin must be sufficiently concentrated in one fixed half-denominator divisibility class, or more generally fail to reach one congruence cylinder through odd multipliers before the `1/g` dilution destroys their margin.

This is still a demanding source-specific theorem. No known property of actual zeta zeros is asserted here to imply `(32)`.

## Prior-art audit

The continued-fraction and Legendre ingredients are classical. AF-257 already audits the parity-restricted approximation step against Dong Han Kim, Seul Bee Lee, and Lingmin Liao, **“Diophantine approximation by rational numbers of certain parity types,”** *Mathematika* 71(1) (2025), e12285, DOI `10.1112/mtk.12285`. That work studies best rational approximations in prescribed parity classes and their continued-fraction algorithms.

Congruence-restricted Diophantine approximation is also an established subject. R. T. Worley, **“Restricted diophantine approximation,”** *Journal of the Australian Mathematical Society* 24(4) (1977), 425--439, DOI `10.1017/S1446788700020802`, studies rational approximation under congruence restrictions. Faustin Adiceam, **“Rational approximation and arithmetic progressions,”** *International Journal of Number Theory* 11(2) (2015), 451--486, DOI `10.1142/S1793042115500232`, develops approximation with numerators and denominators in prescribed arithmetic progressions. Erez Nesharim, Rene Ruehr, and Ronggang Shi, **“Metric Diophantine approximation with congruence conditions,”** *International Journal of Number Theory* 16(9) (2020), 1923--1933, DOI `10.1142/S1793042120500980`, gives a metric Khintchine--Groshev theory with congruence conditions. These sources prevent treating congruence-restricted approximation itself as new.

Jonathan Sondow, **“Irrationality Measures, Irrationality Bases, and a Theorem of Jarnik,”** arXiv:`math/0406300` (2004), provides the unrestricted exponential irrationality-base language used earlier in the line.

A targeted search did not locate the exact fixed-`alpha`, half-integer, exponential-rate identity `(5)`--`(6)` or its odd-multiplier interpretation. This is not an exhaustive novelty proof. The narrow `CANDIDATE-NEW-STRUCTURE` content is the synthesis specific to the AF-258 observable: reduction of each profinite cylinder exponent to parity-selected principal convergents plus an odd residue-transport multiplier whose cost simultaneously controls congruence reachability and exponential cancellation strength. No broad novelty claim is made for restricted Diophantine approximation or continued fractions.

## Boundaries and falsification

**Two-mode half-integer observable only.** The odd-multiplier law comes from the lattice `Z+1/2`. A different phase target or multimode zero set can have a different reduction arithmetic.

**Pair-level statement, not a proof of RH.** Equations `(31)`--`(33)` describe when one hypothetical off-critical conjugate pair remains detectable under periodically complete output sampling. They do not establish the required continued-fraction bound for any actual zero and do not by themselves control cancellation among several co-dominant zero pairs.

**Congruence restriction is not generic novelty.** The established literature contains extensive rational approximation with arithmetic-progression and congruence constraints. What is derived here is the exact exponential cylinder invariant needed by AF-258, not a new general theory of restricted approximation.

**The divisor bound is sufficient, not necessary.** Equation `(13)` discards residue information and the precise multiplier penalty. `K_pc` can vanish even when every `Lambda_d^out` is positive. The exact invariant remains `(8)`.

**No bounded-jump assumption is known for Keiper phases.** The finite-multiplier statement after `(10)` becomes literally finite only under a bound on `lambda_j`; no such bound is assumed for `alpha_rho`.

A decisive falsification of the claimed exact classification would be an irrational `alpha`, cylinder `(M,r)`, and positive exponential cancellation sequence in that cylinder which cannot, after reduction, be represented by odd multiples of even principal-convergent half-denominators as in `(18)`--`(21)`. Legendre's theorem rules this out once the exponent is bounded away from zero.

## Consequence

The live source theorem after AF-258 can now be stated without quantifying over arbitrary periodically complete samplers. For each hypothetical off-critical Keiper phase `alpha_rho`, one needs to control the congruence leakage of the parity-selected large continued-fraction jumps at the very small radial margin `log q_rho`.

The strongest immediate target is therefore not a global irrationality-base bound. It is a **zero-dependent congruence leakage estimate**: find one finite cylinder in which every reachable even-convergent jump is either absent or diluted below `log q_rho` by the necessary odd multiplier. A weaker but cleaner sufficient route is `(32)`, which asks whether dangerous half-denominators concentrate in a fixed divisibility class.

This is strictly more source-specific than AF-257 and strictly more computable than AF-258's raw cylinder limsups. It also explains exactly why AF-258's super-Liouville counterexample is harmless for periodic-complete sampling: its huge even-denominator jumps occur at half-denominators trapped in the even class, so the odd cylinder has zero leakage.
