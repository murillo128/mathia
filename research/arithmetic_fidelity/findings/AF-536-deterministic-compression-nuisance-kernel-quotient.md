# AF-536 — Deterministic compression is a nuisance-plus-kernel quotient

## Status

`EXACT-DERIVED` · `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `CLASSICAL-INGREDIENTS` · `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY` · `SEMIPARAMETRIC` · `DETERMINISTIC-COMPRESSION` · `CLOSED-SUM` · `COMPOSITION` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-535 splits semiparametric information loss under compression into direct loss of the upstream efficient score and downstream nuisance recontamination. For a **deterministic** compression these two terms admit a sharper source-space description: they are exactly one quotient defect against the closed span generated by nuisance directions and directions made invisible by the statistic.

At a fixed baseline law `P_0`, let

\[
H=L_0^2(P_0),
\]

let `Lambda subset H` be the closed nuisance tangent space, let `s in H` be a scalar target score, and let

\[
e=(I-\Pi_\Lambda)s,
\qquad
I_X^{\rm eff}=\|e\|^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `Y=T(X)` be a deterministic statistic. Identify

\[
V=L_0^2(\sigma(Y))\subset H,
\qquad
P=\Pi_V=\mathbb E_0[\,\cdot\mid Y],
\qquad
K=V^\perp=\ker P.
\tag{2}
\]

The downstream nuisance tangent is

\[
M=\overline{P\Lambda}\subset V.
\tag{3}
\]

Then the downstream efficient information is exactly

\[
\boxed{
I_Y^{\rm eff}
=\operatorname{dist}_V(Pe,M)^2
=\operatorname{dist}_H\!\left(e,\overline{\Lambda+K}\right)^2.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the correct source-space loss subspace is

\[
S_T:=\overline{\Lambda+\ker P},
\tag{5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
I_X^{\rm eff}-I_Y^{\rm eff}=\|\Pi_{S_T}e\|^2.
}
\tag{6}
\]

This gives three exact boundaries.

1. **Full first-order fidelity** holds iff `e` is orthogonal to `K`, equivalently iff `e in V`, so the efficient score is already `Y`-measurable.
2. **Total first-order collapse** holds iff `e in closure(Lambda+K)`.
3. The closure is essential: if `Lambda+K` is not closed, downstream efficient information can vanish even though there is no exact decomposition `e=lambda+k` with `lambda in Lambda` and `k in K`. Arbitrarily accurate nuisance-plus-invisible approximations suffice.

The same representation gives an exact **composition law**. For nested deterministic compressions with visible subspaces

\[
V_2\subset V_1\subset H,
\]

write `K_i=V_i^perp` and

\[
S_i=\overline{\Lambda+K_i}.
\tag{7}
\]

Then `K_1 subset K_2`, hence `S_1 subset S_2`, and the efficient information after each stage is

\[
I_i=\operatorname{dist}(e,S_i)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

Because nested closed subspaces split orthogonally,

\[
\boxed{
I_1-I_2
=\left\|\Pi_{S_2\cap S_1^\perp}e\right\|^2.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore a chain of deterministic compressions has a nonnegative stagewise orthogonal loss budget. Once part of the efficient direction has entered the nuisance-plus-invisible closure, later deterministic coarsening cannot manufacture that component again.

## Representation declaration

This is a local regular statistical representation, not an intrinsic arithmetic model.

- **Source representation:** the tangent Hilbert space `H=L_0^2(P_0)` together with target score `s` and closed nuisance tangent `Lambda`.
- **Compression representation:** a deterministic statistic `Y=T(X)`, represented by the orthogonal conditional-expectation projection `P:H -> V=L_0^2(sigma(Y))`.
- **Invisible directions:** `K=ker P=V^perp`; these are first-order score directions annihilated by the statistic.
- **Target carrier:** the upstream efficient score `e=(I-Pi_Lambda)s`.
- **Downstream nuisance:** `M=closure(P Lambda)`.
- **Fidelity quantity:** nuisance-robust first-order Fisher information, the squared norm of the downstream efficient score.

The quotient language in the title refers to this Hilbert-space representation: the statistic removes `K`, while nuisance makes directions in `Lambda` irrelevant to the target. Equation (4) says that, for the target carrier, these two identifications combine through the closed sum `closure(Lambda+K)`. It does not assert a literal quotient construction for an undeclared arithmetic object.

## Derivation

AF-535 gives

\[
I_Y^{\rm eff}
=\|(I-\Pi_M)Pe\|^2
=\operatorname{dist}_V(Pe,M)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Since `M=closure(P Lambda)`, distance to `M` can be computed on the dense set `P Lambda`:

\[
\operatorname{dist}_V(Pe,M)
=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|P(e-\lambda)\|.
\tag{11}
\]

For any `h in H`, orthogonal projection onto `V=K^perp` satisfies

\[
\|Ph\|=\operatorname{dist}_H(h,K).
\tag{12}
\]

Applying (12) inside (11),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}_V(Pe,M)
&=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\inf_{k\in K}\|e-\lambda-k\|\\
&=\operatorname{dist}_H(e,\Lambda+K)\\
&=\operatorname{dist}_H\!\left(e,\overline{\Lambda+K}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

which proves (4).

Because `S_T=closure(Lambda+K)` is closed,

\[
\operatorname{dist}(e,S_T)^2
=\|e\|^2-\|\Pi_{S_T}e\|^2,
\tag{14}
\]

and (6) follows from (1) and (4).

The full-fidelity criterion can also be read directly from the geometry. Since `e perp Lambda`, equation (6) vanishes exactly when `e perp K` as well. But `K^perp=V`, so this is precisely `e in V`, recovering AF-535's efficient-score measurability condition.

The total-collapse criterion follows from (4): `I_Y^eff=0` iff `e in S_T`. Importantly, this is membership in the **closure** of the algebraic sum, not necessarily in `Lambda+K` itself.

## Nested deterministic compressions

Let `P_i` be the orthogonal projection onto `V_i`, with `V_2 subset V_1`. Then `K_1 subset K_2`, so

\[
S_1=\overline{\Lambda+K_1}
\subset
S_2=\overline{\Lambda+K_2}.
\tag{15}
\]

For two nested closed subspaces,

\[
S_2=S_1\oplus(S_2\cap S_1^\perp).
\tag{16}
\]

Hence

\[
\|\Pi_{S_2}e\|^2
=\|\Pi_{S_1}e\|^2
+\|\Pi_{S_2\cap S_1^\perp}e\|^2.
\tag{17}
\]

Using `I_i=||e||^2-||Pi_{S_i}e||^2` proves (9).

For a longer nested chain, the same argument iterates. Each stage enlarges the closed loss subspace and subtracts the squared norm of the newly admitted orthogonal component of `e`. This is stronger than monotonicity alone: it localizes exactly *where in the compression chain* each piece of nuisance-robust target information becomes unrecoverable from the retained sigma-field.

There is no resurrection theorem hidden here for arbitrary new observations. A later operation supplied with an external side channel can of course add information. The statement is about a genuine deterministic coarsening chain in which every later observation is measurable with respect to the previous one.

## Matched control: zero information without an exact nuisance-plus-kernel decomposition

The closure in (4) cannot be dropped, even when both `Lambda` and `K` are individually closed.

Let `H_0=ell^2` and define the bounded injective diagonal operator

\[
A:H_0\to H_0,
\qquad
(Ax)_n=\frac{x_n}{n}.
\tag{18}
\]

Its range is dense but not closed. Work in

\[
H=H_0\oplus H_0,
\tag{19}
\]

and set

\[
K=H_0\oplus\{0\},
\qquad
V=K^\perp=\{0\}\oplus H_0,
\tag{20}
\]

with nuisance tangent

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{graph}(A)
=\{(x,Ax):x\in H_0\}.
\tag{21}
\]

`Lambda` is closed because `A` is bounded, but

\[
\Lambda+K=H_0\oplus\operatorname{ran}(A)
\tag{22}
\]

is dense and nonclosed in `H`.

Choose

\[
y=(1/n)_{n\ge1}\in\ell^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Then `y notin ran(A)`, because `Ax=y` would require `x=(1,1,...)`, which is not in `ell^2`. Since `A=A^*`, define

\[
e=(-Ay,y)=(-(1/n^2)_n,(1/n)_n).
\tag{24}
\]

For every `x in H_0`,

\[
\langle e,(x,Ax)\rangle
=-\langle Ay,x\rangle+\langle y,Ax\rangle=0,
\tag{25}
\]

so `e in Lambda^perp` and is a legitimate upstream efficient direction. Yet

\[
e\in\overline{\Lambda+K}=H
\quad\text{while}\quad
e\notin\Lambda+K,
\tag{26}
\]

because its second coordinate is `y notin ran(A)`.

Equivalently, the downstream nuisance image is

\[
P\Lambda=\{0\}\oplus\operatorname{ran}(A),
\tag{27}
\]

which is dense in all of `V`. Therefore `M=V` and

\[
I_Y^{\rm eff}=0.
\tag{28}
\]

The target is perfectly approximable by combinations of nuisance and invisible directions, although no exact such representation exists. This is the deterministic-compression analogue of AF-534's distinction between algebraic identifiability and stable nuisance separation.

## Closed-sum conditioning and prior art

The ingredients are classical, and no theorem-level novelty is claimed.

- Janet M. Begun, W. J. Hall, Wei-Min Huang, and Jon A. Wellner, **“Information and Asymptotic Efficiency in Parametric-Nonparametric Models,”** *The Annals of Statistics* 11(2), 432–452 (1983). DOI `10.1214/aos/1176346151`. Role: foundational tangent-space projection and efficient-information framework.
- Abram Kagan and C. Radhakrishna Rao, **“Some properties and applications of the efficient Fisher score,”** *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* 116(2), 343–352 (2003). DOI `10.1016/S0378-3758(02)00351-8`. Role: direct prior art for monotonicity of efficient Fisher information under transformations in the presence of nuisance parameters.
- Rachel M. Fewster and Peter E. Jupp, **“Information on parameters of interest decreases under transformations,”** *Journal of Multivariate Analysis* 120, 34–39 (2013). DOI `10.1016/j.jmva.2013.05.010`. Role: direct transformation/data-processing prior art for information on parameters of interest.
- Bram Mesland and Adam Rennie, **“The Friedrichs angle and alternating projections in Hilbert C*-modules,”** *Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications* 516(1), 126474 (2022). DOI `10.1016/j.jmaa.2022.126474`. Role: modern source explicitly recalling the classical Hilbert-space criterion that, for closed subspaces, the sum is closed exactly when the cosine of their Friedrichs angle is strictly below one.

For two closed Hilbert subspaces `Lambda` and `K`, the classical Friedrichs-angle criterion gives

\[
\Lambda+K\text{ is closed}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c(\Lambda,K)<1,
\tag{29}
\]

with the usual removal of the common intersection in the definition of the angle. Thus the gap between exact aliasing `e in Lambda+K` and closure aliasing `e in closure(Lambda+K)` is geometrically a **degenerating-angle boundary** between nuisance and invisible directions. The matched control above realizes the limiting case `c=1`.

AF-535 already establishes the general Markov-channel efficient-score formula and its two-term loss decomposition. AF-536 is a deterministic specialization that reorganizes that result into one source-space closed-sum geometry and extracts the nested-compression orthogonal composition law. The synthesis is useful for Arithmetic Fidelity, but every underlying Hilbert/projection/information ingredient is classical.

## Boundary conditions and falsification tests

1. **Deterministic compression is essential to the kernel formula.** A general Markov kernel gives a contraction `C`, not an orthogonal projection onto a sub-sigma-field of the source Hilbert space. AF-535's general two-term formula remains the correct statement there; replacing it blindly by `closure(Lambda+ker C)` is not justified.

2. **This is local first-order information.** Positive distance in (4) gives positive efficient Fisher information at the chosen baseline. It does not by itself prove global identifiability, Blackwell sufficiency, finite-sample recoverability, or an arithmetic rigidity theorem.

3. **The downstream nuisance tangent must be `closure(P Lambda)`.** If the reduced model has additional nuisance directions not generated by pushed-forward source paths, the actual efficient information can be smaller.

4. **Closure cannot be replaced by the algebraic sum.** Equations (18)–(28) give an explicit counterexample. Any proposed theorem that uses only exact decompositions must separately prove `Lambda+K` is closed, for example through a positive Friedrichs-angle gap.

5. **Raw-score fidelity is still insufficient.** The relevant target carrier is `e`, not `s`. A statistic can preserve the raw target score while `e` falls into `closure(Lambda+K)`, as AF-535's finite Gaussian control already demonstrates.

6. **Composition requires genuine nesting.** Equation (9) applies when the second statistic is a deterministic function of the first, giving `V_2 subset V_1`. Two unrelated observations need not define nested loss subspaces and do not admit this stagewise formula without a common refinement.

7. **An arithmetic application must justify all four ingredients.** It must identify a source tangent representation, a target direction, a nuisance class representing the allowed matched controls, and the actual retained sigma-field or deterministic observation. Choosing these after seeing the desired prime discriminator would merely encode the answer.

## Consequence for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-534 showed that robust recovery in an infinite-dimensional Gaussian nuisance model is controlled by distance to the **closed** nuisance geometry rather than by exact algebraic non-membership. AF-535 showed that compression adds a second failure mode: surviving target score can be recontaminated by nuisance after the channel.

For deterministic compression, AF-536 combines both observations into a single audit quantity:

\[
\boxed{
\text{retained target margin}
=\operatorname{dist}\!\left(e,\overline{\Lambda+K}\right),
\qquad
K=\text{invisible compression directions}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

This also answers one of the line's abstract composition questions in an exact Hilbert setting: nested compressions enlarge a closed loss subspace, and each stage pays the orthogonal component of the target carrier newly absorbed by that enlargement. A downstream operation that sees only the coarser observation cannot recover a component already absorbed upstream.

The next source-level gate is therefore sharper than asking whether an arithmetic discriminator survives a chosen statistic pointwise. For any proposed relational or differential lift, identify the natural matched-control tangent `Lambda`, the observation kernel `K`, and ask whether the arithmetic efficient direction has a **positive, preferably uniform, angle/distance from `closure(Lambda+K)`** on the relevant observation window. Without such a separation theorem, exact non-aliasing alone remains compatible with arbitrarily ill-conditioned recovery.
