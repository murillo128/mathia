# AF-103 — Preadjoint provenance is SOT-invisible without a uniform budget

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `STRUCTURAL-CLASSIFICATION`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY-REFINEMENT`, `CATEGORY-EXPLICIT-FIDELITY`, `CLASSICAL-OPERATOR-TOPOLOGY-MECHANISM`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

Let `K` and `L` be Banach spaces. Write

\[
\operatorname{Adj}(K,L)
:=\{B^*:L^*\to K^*:B\in\mathcal L(K,L)\}
\tag{1}
\]

for the operators that possess a genuine preadjoint `B:K\to L`, and for `r\ge0` put

\[
\operatorname{Adj}_r(K,L)
:=\{B^*:B\in\mathcal L(K,L),\ \|B\|\le r\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For an arbitrary bounded

\[
C:L^*\to K^*,
\tag{3}
\]

retain AF-102's transposed bidual realization

\[
\widetilde C=C^*J_K:K\to L^{**}
\tag{4}
\]

and second-leg accessibility cost

\[
\beta_{K,L}(C):=a_L(\widetilde C).
\tag{5}
\]

There are two natural finite-observation topologies on the same provenance question.

First, let `\tau_{pw^*}` be the pointwise weak-star operator topology generated by

\[
C\longmapsto (C\ell^*)(k),
\qquad \ell^*\in L^*,\ k\in K.
\tag{6}
\]

Second, let `\mathrm{SOT}` be the strong operator topology on `\mathcal L(L^*,K^*)`, generated by

\[
C\longmapsto \|C\ell^*\|,
\qquad \ell^*\in L^*.
\tag{7}
\]

For each finite-dimensional `E\subset L^*`, define the exact finite preadjoint-interpolation cost

\[
e_E^{\rm adj}(C)
:=
\inf\{\|B\|:B\in\mathcal L(K,L),\ B^*|_E=C|_E\},
\tag{8}
\]

and the uniform strong-recovery cost

\[
\eta_{K,L}(C)
:=
\sup_{\substack{E\subset L^*\\\dim E<\infty}}
e_E^{\rm adj}(C)
\in[0,+\infty].
\tag{9}
\]

Then the following hold.

### 1. Every finite strong observation is reproduced exactly by a genuine preadjoint

For every finite-dimensional `E\subset L^*`, the restriction map

\[
Q_E:\mathcal L(K,L)\to\mathcal L(E,K^*),
\qquad
Q_E(B)=B^*|_E,
\tag{10}
\]

is surjective. Consequently

\[
\boxed{e_E^{\rm adj}(C)<\infty}
\tag{11}
\]

for every `C` and every finite `E`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\operatorname{Adj}(K,L)}^{\,\mathrm{SOT}}
=
\mathcal L(L^*,K^*).
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus even finitely many **norm-valued** observations of `C\ell^*` cannot certify that `C` lacks a preadjoint when the approximating norm is unconstrained. Preadjoint provenance is completely invisible at every finite SOT scale.

### 2. The missing discriminator is exactly the uniform finite interpolation budget

The quantity `\eta` is the bounded SOT-closure gauge:

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{K,L}(C)
=
\inf\Bigl\{
r\ge0:
C\in
\overline{\operatorname{Adj}_r(K,L)}^{\,\mathrm{SOT}}
\Bigr\}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Equivalently, every `r>\eta_{K,L}(C)` permits all finite sets of dual vectors to be matched by one preadjoint of norm `<r`, whereas every `r<\eta_{K,L}(C)` fails on some finite-dimensional `E\subset L^*`.

The strong operator continuous dual consists exactly of finite matrix-coefficient functionals

\[
\Phi(C)
=
\sum_{j=1}^m
k_j^{**}(C\ell_j^*),
\qquad
k_j^{**}\in K^{**},\ \ell_j^*\in L^*.
\tag{14}
\]

Since (12) makes every nonzero such `\Phi` nonzero on some genuine adjoint, the denominator below never vanishes. Hahn--Banach separation gives the exact polar formula

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{K,L}(C)
=
\sup
\frac{
\left|\sum_j k_j^{**}(C\ell_j^*)\right|
}{
\sup_{\|B\|\le1}
\left|\sum_j k_j^{**}(B^*\ell_j^*)\right|
},
}
\tag{15}
\]

where the supremum ranges over all nonzero finite families `(\ell_j^*,k_j^{**})`.

Hence every failed uniform strong budget has a finite scalar certificate, even though the categorical yes/no question "is `C` an adjoint?" has no finite SOT certificate at all.

### 3. AF-102's `beta` is the same closure problem with only predual source witnesses

Under AF-102's isometric bijection

\[
C\longmapsto\widetilde C=C^*J_K,
\tag{16}
\]

one has

\[
\widetilde{B^*}=J_LB.
\tag{17}
\]

Moreover the scalar coordinates agree:

\[
(B^*\ell^*)(k)
=
\ell^*(Bk)
=
\langle J_LBk,\ell^*\rangle.
\tag{18}
\]

Therefore AF-100's bounded pointwise weak-star accessibility envelope transports exactly to

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{K,L}(C)
=
\inf\Bigl\{
r\ge0:
C\in
\overline{\operatorname{Adj}_r(K,L)}^{\,\tau_{pw^*}}
\Bigr\}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Its polar formula is the restriction of (15) to canonical bidual witnesses

\[
k_j^{**}=J_Kk_j,
\qquad k_j\in K.
\tag{20}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{K,L}(C)
=
\sup
\frac{
\left|\sum_j(C\ell_j^*)(k_j)\right|
}{
\sup_{\|B\|\le1}
\left|\sum_j\ell_j^*(Bk_j)\right|
}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|C\|\le\beta_{K,L}(C)\le\eta_{K,L}(C).
}
\tag{22}
\]

The two costs therefore measure different observational strengths. `\beta` asks whether bounded genuine preadjoints can reproduce every finite **predual scalar** observation `(\ell^*,k)`. `\eta` asks whether they can reproduce finite dual vectors `C\ell^*` in **norm**, equivalently whether every bidual witness `k^{**}\in K^{**}` is also controlled.

### 4. Reflexivity closes the scalar-versus-strong fidelity gap

If `K` is reflexive, every element of `K^{**}` is `J_Kk`. The witness families in (15) and (21) then coincide, so

\[
\boxed{
K\text{ reflexive}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\eta_{K,L}(C)=\beta_{K,L}(C)
\quad\text{for every }C.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Topologically this says the same thing in another language. When `K` is reflexive, `\tau_{pw^*}` on `\mathcal L(L^*,K^*)` is ordinary WOT. Each `\operatorname{Adj}_r(K,L)` is convex, and WOT and SOT have the same continuous linear functionals on an operator space, so their closures of this convex set coincide.

Two stronger controls are inherited from AF-102. If `L` is reflexive, every `C:L^*\to K^*` already has a genuine preadjoint and

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{K,L}(C)=\beta_{K,L}(C)=\|C\|.
}
\tag{24}
\]

If `K` is finite-dimensional, AF-102 gives `\beta=\|C\|`, while (23) gives the same equality for `\eta`.

For nonreflexive `K`, (22) is the unconditional statement. A strict gap `\beta<\eta` is **not** asserted here; establishing or excluding such gaps for important Banach-space classes is a separate operator-theoretic question.

## Derivation

### 1. Finite exact adjoint interpolation is an elementary quotient-lifting fact

Fix finite-dimensional `E\subset L^*`. Define the canonical evaluation quotient

\[
q_E:L\to E^*,
\qquad
(q_E\ell)(e)=e(\ell).
\tag{25}
\]

Its range is all of `E^*`. Indeed, if the range were proper, finite-dimensional separation would give a nonzero `e\in E=(E^*)^*` annihilating every `q_E\ell`; that would mean `e(\ell)=0` for every `\ell\in L`, hence `e=0`, a contradiction.

Because `E^*` is finite-dimensional, choose a bounded linear right inverse

\[
R_E:E^*\to L,
\qquad
q_ER_E=I_{E^*}.
\tag{26}
\]

Given arbitrary `A:E\to K^*`, define

\[
A^\#:K\to E^*,
\qquad
(A^\#k)(e)=(Ae)(k).
\tag{27}
\]

Then `\|A^\#\|=\|A\|`. Put

\[
B=R_EA^\#:K\to L.
\tag{28}
\]

For `e\in E` and `k\in K`,

\[
(B^*e)(k)
=e(Bk)
=(q_EBk)(e)
=(A^\#k)(e)
=(Ae)(k).
\tag{29}
\]

Thus `B^*|_E=A`, proving surjectivity of `Q_E`. Taking `A=C|_E` proves (11), and exact matching on the finite set defining any SOT neighborhood proves (12).

### 2. Uniform finite interpolation is exactly bounded SOT closure

The surjective bounded map `Q_E` makes

\[
A\longmapsto
\inf\{\|B\|:Q_EB=A\}
\tag{30}
\]

the quotient norm on `\mathcal L(E,K^*)`.

Suppose first that `r>\eta_{K,L}(C)`. For every basic SOT neighborhood of `C`, let `E` be the finite-dimensional span of the tested vectors. Equation (9) gives `e_E^{\rm adj}(C)<r`, so some `B` with `\|B\|<r` agrees **exactly** with `C` on `E`. Hence

\[
C\in\overline{\operatorname{Adj}_r(K,L)}^{\,\mathrm{SOT}}.
\tag{31}
\]

Conversely, suppose `C` belongs to that SOT closure. On a fixed finite-dimensional `E`, SOT convergence of the restrictions is operator-norm convergence in `\mathcal L(E,K^*)`. The quotient norm (30) is continuous, while every approximating restriction has quotient norm at most `r`. Therefore

\[
e_E^{\rm adj}(C)\le r.
\tag{32}
\]

Taking the supremum over `E` gives `\eta_{K,L}(C)\le r`. These two implications prove the infimum identity (13), including the possible value `+\infty`.

### 3. The strong dual exposes exactly the missing bidual witnesses

The SOT is generated by the seminorms `C\mapsto\|C\ell^*\|`. Hence every SOT-continuous scalar linear functional is dominated by finitely many such seminorms and, by finite-product Hahn--Banach, has the form (14). Conversely every expression (14) is SOT-continuous.

The SOT closure of the unit adjoint ball is closed, convex, balanced, and contains zero. Its Minkowski gauge is `\eta` by (13). Bipolar separation therefore gives (15).

The same argument after transporting AF-100 through (16)--(18) gives (19)--(21). Because the `\beta` witnesses are a subset of the `\eta` witnesses, the second inequality in (22) follows immediately; AF-102 already gives `\|C\|\le\beta(C)`.

## Exact controls and failure modes

### One exact preadjoint

If `C=B^*` for some `B:K\to L`, then

\[
\eta(C)\le\|B\|.
\tag{33}
\]

The adjoint operation is isometric and injective, so the preadjoint is unique and `\|B\|=\|C\|`. Combining with (22) yields

\[
\eta(C)=\beta(C)=\|C\|.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus the gauges add no artificial penalty when the desired provenance is actually present.

### Finite interpolation without a uniform budget is deliberately non-diagnostic

Equation (12) is not evidence that every `C` has a preadjoint. It says the opposite about the observation model: every finite SOT test can be spoofed by some genuine adjoint, with a norm that may depend badly on the tested finite-dimensional subspace.

This is the same finite-versus-uniform phenomenon seen in AF-097--AF-101, now attached directly to the categorical property of possessing a preadjoint.

### Nonreflexive source dual witnesses are the only possible scalar/strong gap

Equation (21) tests only `J_K(K)\subset K^{**}` while (15) tests all of `K^{**}`. Therefore any strict inequality

\[
\beta(C)<\eta(C)
\tag{35}
\]

must be witnessed by genuinely noncanonical bidual directions. This is a necessary location for a gap, not an existence theorem.

### Topology must not be silently upgraded

For nonreflexive `K`, `\tau_{pw^*}` is generally weaker than WOT on `\mathcal L(L^*,K^*)`, because WOT permits all `k^{**}\in K^{**}` while (6) permits only evaluation by `J_Kk`. Therefore AF-100's weak-star closure statement must not be relabeled as WOT or SOT bounded density without an additional hypothesis. The separate gauge `\eta` records exactly that missing strength.

Their Arithmetic Fidelity role is organizational and diagnostic: they exhibit a clean instance in which a **categorical provenance property is erased by every finite observation unless a uniform budget is retained**, and they identify precisely how strengthening the observation class from predual scalar witnesses to full bidual witnesses changes the recovery cost.

## Consequences for Arithmetic Fidelity

This closes a conceptual gap left by AF-102. There, `\beta` appeared as the maximal second-leg functoriality cost for the `\alpha` family. Here the same number is identified as the exact bounded-closure cost for pretending that an arbitrary dual-side map has a genuine preadjoint when observations are limited to the declared predual scalar category.

The new comparison with `\eta` separates two questions that should not be conflated in later compression audits:

1. **Can the object be reproduced on every finite scalar observation with one uniform budget?** This is the `\beta` question.
2. **Can the object be reproduced on every finite retained vector in norm with one uniform budget?** This is the `\eta` question.

Without a budget, both observation regimes lose preadjoint provenance completely. With a budget, their exact difference is whether the destination category can use only canonical source points `K` or the full bidual witness space `K^{**}`. Reflexivity removes that difference.

For later arithmetic applications, the reusable warning is therefore sharper than "finite data can miss global structure": **a provenance discriminator may survive only as a uniform conditioning law, and changing the admissible witness category can change that law even when every finite unbudgeted test remains perfectly reproducible.**
