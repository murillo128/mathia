# AF-369 — Integer-supported Delone zero divisors are universal; pole residue restores prime identity

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `EXACT-DERIVED`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `MATCHED-CONTROL`, `SOURCE-CLASS`, `ZERO-DIVISOR`, `SCALAR-RECOVERY`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

Let `Q` be a Beurling generalized-prime system whose generalized-integer set `N_Q` has the Delone property and is contained in the ordinary positive integers. Lagarias' classification implies that `N_Q` has unique factorization and that the generalized primes have the form

\[
Q=(\mathbb P\setminus R)\cup C,
\tag{1}
\]

where `R` is a finite set of ordinary primes and `C` is a finite set of ordinary composite integers, subject to the unique-factorization constraints of the resulting multiplicative subsemigroup.

Write

\[
\zeta_Q(s)=\prod_{q\in Q}(1-q^{-s})^{-1}
\qquad (\Re s>1).
\]

Then:

1. **Every such system has the same zero/pole divisor as the Riemann zeta function in `Re(s)>0`.** Indeed
   \[
   \boxed{
   \zeta_Q(s)=\zeta(s)h_Q(s),
   \qquad
   h_Q(s)=
   \frac{\prod_{p\in R}(1-p^{-s})}
        {\prod_{c\in C}(1-c^{-s})},
   }
   \tag{2}
   \]
   and every factor `1-a^{-s}` is zero-free for `Re(s)>0`. Hence `h_Q` is holomorphic and nonvanishing there. Thus `\zeta_Q` has exactly the Riemann zeros with the same multiplicities in `Re(s)>0`, together with the same simple pole location and order at `s=1`.

2. **The pole residue is the natural density of the generalized integers.** If
   \[
   \kappa_Q:=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_Q(s),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \kappa_Q=h_Q(1)=\operatorname{dens}(N_Q)\in(0,1].
   }
   \tag{3}
   \]

3. **Within this source class, residue `1` characterizes the ordinary prime system:**
   \[
   \boxed{
   \kappa_Q=1
   \iff N_Q=\mathbb N
   \iff Q=\mathbb P.
   }
   \tag{4}
   \]

Therefore compression to the meromorphic divisor is completely non-faithful to the rational-prime discriminator on this integer-supported Delone class, but adjoining one natural scalar—the residue at the pole—recovers the **binary** discriminator `Q=\mathbb P` exactly.

This is not a claim that the residue reconstructs an arbitrary nonordinary `Q`; it only separates the ordinary rational-prime system from every other member of this class.

## Derivation

### 1. Lagarias reduces the source category to finite arithmetic modifications

Lagarias proves that a Beurling generalized-integer set with the Delone property has unique factorization into irreducibles and is a multiplicative subsemigroup of `\mathbb R_{>0}`. When that semigroup is contained in `\mathbb Z_{>0}`, its generalized primes consist of all but finitely many ordinary primes plus finitely many composite integers.

Thus there are finite sets `R` and `C` as in `(1)`. Absolute convergence in `Re(s)>1` gives the finite Euler-factor comparison `(2)` directly.

For every integer `a>1`,

\[
1-a^{-s}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s=\frac{2\pi i k}{\log a},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,
\]

so its zeros all lie on `Re(s)=0`. Consequently the finite quotient `h_Q` is holomorphic and nonvanishing throughout `Re(s)>0`. Multiplying `\zeta` by `h_Q` therefore changes neither zero locations nor zero multiplicities there and preserves the simple pole at `s=1`.

This gives a stronger same-divisor control than merely allowing arbitrary real Beurling generators: the source may already be an exact multiplicative subsemigroup of the ordinary integers with Delone spacing and unique factorization, yet the divisor still cannot identify the rational primes.

### 2. Membership is controlled by finitely many ordinary prime valuations

Let `F` be the finite set containing `R` and every ordinary prime divisor of every element of `C`. Every ordinary prime outside `F` is itself a generator of `Q` and can therefore occur with arbitrary nonnegative exponent.

For `n\in\mathbb N`, write

\[
v_F(n)=(v_p(n))_{p\in F}\in\mathbb N_0^F.
\]

Let `H\subseteq\mathbb N_0^F` be the additive monoid generated by the valuation vectors at `F` of the generalized primes that meet `F`: the surviving ordinary primes in `F` and the finitely many composites in `C`. Then

\[
\boxed{
n\in N_Q
\iff
v_F(n)\in H.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed the coordinates outside `F` are supplied freely by the ordinary prime generators outside `F`, while every restriction caused by deleting primes or replacing them by composite irreducibles is concentrated in the finite valuation vector `v_F(n)`.

Thus the source distinction is not a small perturbation of counting density. It is a finite-dimensional arithmetic support constraint expressed in exact ordinary-prime valuations.

### 3. The density exists and equals the pole residue

For a fixed vector `a=(a_p)_{p\in F}`, the valuation cylinder

\[
E_a:=\{n\in\mathbb N:v_p(n)=a_p\text{ for every }p\in F\}
\]

has natural density

\[
\delta_a
=
\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-1})p^{-a_p}.
\tag{6}
\]

The cylinders are disjoint and their densities sum to one over all `a\in\mathbb N_0^F`. Equation `(5)` gives

\[
N_Q=\bigsqcup_{a\in H}E_a.
\]

The union has natural density because the tails in any valuation coordinate have geometrically vanishing density; truncating all coordinates at a finite height gives a finite disjoint union of cylinders, and the omitted density tends to zero. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dens}(N_Q)
=
\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-1})
\sum_{a\in H}\prod_{p\in F}p^{-a_p}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

The same decomposition of the Dirichlet series gives, for `Re(s)>1`,

\[
\zeta_Q(s)
=
\prod_{r\notin F}(1-r^{-s})^{-1}
\sum_{a\in H}\prod_{p\in F}p^{-a_p s}.
\tag{8}
\]

Dividing `(8)` by the corresponding factorization of `\zeta(s)` shows

\[
h_Q(s)
=
\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-s})
\sum_{a\in H}\prod_{p\in F}p^{-a_p s}.
\tag{9}
\]

Evaluating at `s=1` and comparing `(7)` with `(9)` proves

\[
h_Q(1)=\operatorname{dens}(N_Q).
\]

Since `\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=1`, equation `(2)` then gives `(3)`.

### 4. Full density forces the ordinary system in this category

Suppose `Q\ne\mathbb P`. Because `N_Q` has unique factorization and its generalized primes are its irreducible elements, `N_Q=\mathbb N` would force those irreducibles to be exactly the ordinary primes. Hence `Q\ne\mathbb P` implies `N_Q\ne\mathbb N`, so the monoid `H` in `(5)` is a proper subset of `\mathbb N_0^F`.

Choose `a\notin H`. Then the entire positive-density cylinder `E_a` is missing from `N_Q`. By `(6)`,

\[
\operatorname{dens}(N_Q)
\le 1-\delta_a
<1.
\tag{10}
\]

Conversely, for the ordinary prime system `Q=\mathbb P`, one has `N_Q=\mathbb N`, `h_Q\equiv1`, and `\kappa_Q=1`. This proves `(4)`.

The strict inequality is important: within this source category there is no nonordinary density-one control hidden behind a zero-density exceptional set. Any nonordinary finite arithmetic modification excludes at least one full ordinary valuation cylinder and therefore pays a positive density defect.

### 5. Explicit matched control: replace `2` by `4`

Take

\[
Q_2=(\mathbb P\setminus\{2\})\cup\{4\}.
\]

Its generalized integers are exactly

\[
N_{Q_2}=\{n\in\mathbb N:v_2(n)\text{ is even}\}.
\]

This is an integer-supported Delone set: all odd integers occur, so consecutive generalized integers are at distance at most `2`, while integrality gives a positive minimum spacing. Factorization is unique using `4` and the odd ordinary primes.

Its zeta function is

\[
\boxed{
\zeta_{Q_2}(s)
=
\zeta(s)\frac{1-2^{-s}}{1-4^{-s}}
=
\frac{\zeta(s)}{1+2^{-s}}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

The factor `1+2^{-s}` is zero-free in `Re(s)>0`, so `\zeta_{Q_2}` has exactly the Riemann divisor there. But

\[
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{Q_2}(s)
=
\frac1{1+2^{-1}}
=
\frac23.
\tag{12}
\]

This equals the elementary density of integers with even `2`-adic valuation. Thus the divisor cannot distinguish `Q_2` from the rational primes, while the pole residue does so immediately.

## Why this matters for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-017 separated exact Euler-product values from zero-divisor data using the much broader Grosswald--Schnitzer class: the full Euler product recovers its generator-norm multiset, whereas a zero-free analytic factor can change those generators without changing the divisor. AF-368 then showed a different source obstruction: even an arbitrarily small fixed `c\log x` envelope around the ordinary integer-counting function does not identify the rational primes inside the general Beurling category.

The present result isolates a sharper **combination law between retained resources**.

- Exact containment of the generalized integers in `\mathbb N`, even with Delone spacing and unique factorization, does **not** make the zero divisor prime-faithful: every system in the Lagarias class has the Riemann divisor in `Re(s)>0`.
- Asymptotic density `1` by itself does **not** identify the rational-prime source in the unrestricted Beurling category; AF-368's `O(\log x)` matched controls already have `N_Q(x)/x\to1`.
- But inside the integer-supported Delone category, **support category plus density `1` is rigid**: it forces `Q=\mathbb P`.

Analytically, the missing density resource is carried by one scalar discarded by the divisor map: the residue at `s=1`. Hence this gives a concrete example where two individually insufficient descriptions become faithful when coupled, and where the required extra information has an exact natural analytic representative rather than being an arbitrary copy of the target label.

This does not make the residue a category-independent minimal lift. It proves only that, on this declared source class, adjoining this one scalar to the divisor is sufficient for the binary rational-prime discriminator.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The source classification is classical:

- Jeffrey C. Lagarias, **“Beurling generalized integers with the Delone Property,”** *Forum Mathematicum* **11** (1999), 295–312, DOI `10.1515/FORM.1999.295`. Lagarias proves unique factorization for Delone generalized-integer sets and classifies those contained in `\mathbb Z_{>0}`; their generalized primes consist of all but finitely many ordinary primes plus finitely many composites.

The finite Euler-factor identity `(2)`, zero-free half-plane observation, valuation-cylinder density formula, and rigidity implication `(4)` are derived directly from that classification. These are elementary consequences of the finite-modification structure; **no novelty is claimed** for the underlying Beurling, Euler-product, natural-density, or valuation facts.

A literature check found extensive work on Beurling zeta functions and asymptotic densities, but no need to posit a new theorem in that literature here. The Arithmetic Fidelity content is the placement of the exact information boundary: in this arithmetic source category the divisor forgets the source completely, whereas its discarded pole residue already contains enough additional information to recover the ordinary-versus-nonordinary discriminator.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- The theorem requires both the **Delone property** and containment `N_Q\subseteq\mathbb N`. It does not apply to arbitrary Beurling systems, including the noninteger generalized-prime controls used in AF-368.
- The residue recovers only the binary statement `Q=\mathbb P` inside this class. Different nonordinary systems can share the same density/residue; no reconstruction of `R`, `C`, `H`, or the full generalized-prime set is claimed.
- The common-divisor statement is for the open half-plane `Re(s)>0`. The finite factors in `(2)` acquire their own zeros and poles on the boundary line `Re(s)=0`.
- Equality of zero/pole locations and multiplicities does not include pole residues. This result depends precisely on keeping that distinction explicit.
- The ordinary Riemann functional equation is not asserted for `\zeta_Q`; the finite factor generally changes it. A destination retaining a rigid functional equation is a richer compression and requires a separate audit.
- The result does not advance RH directly. In fact every member of this class inherits the same Riemann zeros in `Re(s)>0`; the theorem concerns source provenance and the extra datum required to identify the ordinary prime system.
- `\kappa_Q<1` for a nonordinary member is special to this integer-supported finite-valuation category. It must not be generalized to arbitrary Beurling systems merely because they have positive density.

## Decisive audit test

For a proposed prime-specific destination that retains only a zero divisor, first test it on the explicit family obtained by finite integer-supported Delone modifications such as `Q_2`. If the destination cannot see pole residues or equivalent density information, then it cannot distinguish the rational-prime source even after exact ordinary-integer support and unique factorization have been imposed.

If the mechanism retains an additional scalar or normalization, determine whether it controls `h_Q(1)` or another quantity that is injective for the declared discriminator on the actual source class. Do not infer source recovery merely from normalization in a broader category where density-one nonordinary controls remain available.
