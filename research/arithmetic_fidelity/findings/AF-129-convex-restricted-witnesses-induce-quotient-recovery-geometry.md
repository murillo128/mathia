# AF-129 — Convex restricted witness classes induce quotient recovery geometry

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY`, `RESTRICTED-WITNESS`, `QUOTIENT-GEOMETRY`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-126--AF-128 identify finite Le Cam recovery deficiency as the exact whole-experiment defect and show that the unrestricted defect has complete bounded decision witnesses. The next question is what remains visible when only a mathematically admissible subclass of witnesses is allowed. In finite dimensions, once that admissible class is represented by a compact convex body of prior-weighted payoff witnesses, the restriction has an exact geometric meaning: it replaces total variation by the support gauge generated by the allowed witnesses. When the witness body is symmetric, this is a quotient seminorm whose kernel is precisely the residual information the witness class cannot see.

Let

\[
\mathcal E=(P_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}
\]

be a finite statistical experiment on a finite sample space `X`, let

\[
K:X\rightsquigarrow Y,
\qquad
Q_\theta=P_\theta K,
\]

and let `R:Y\rightsquigarrow X` range over recovery channels. Put

\[
d_R=(P_\theta-Q_\theta R)_{\theta\in\Theta}
\in
V:=\mathbb R^{\Theta\times X}.
\tag{1}
\]

Every residual lies in the row-zero subspace

\[
H:=\left\{v\in V:\sum_x v_{\theta x}=0\text{ for every }\theta\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Define

\[
N(v):=\max_\theta\|v_\theta\|_1.
\tag{3}
\]

Then AF-127's finite dual can be written as

\[
2\,\delta_{\rm rec}(K;\mathcal E)
=
\min_R N(d_R)
=
\max_{r\in B_*}\min_R\langle d_R,r\rangle,
\tag{4}
\]

where the dual unit ball is

\[
B_*:=\left\{r\in V:\sum_\theta\|r_\theta\|_\infty\le1\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

The elements of `B_*` are exactly the effective prior-weighted bounded payoff witnesses from AF-127 after writing `r_{theta x}=lambda_theta u(theta,x)`.

Now let

\[
A\subseteq B_*
\tag{6}
\]

be a nonempty compact convex admissible witness body with `0 in A`. Define the **restricted witness deficiency** by

\[
2\,\delta_A
:=
\max_{r\in A}\min_R\langle d_R,r\rangle.
\tag{7}
\]

Let

\[
\sigma_A(v):=\max_{r\in A}\langle v,r\rangle
\tag{8}
\]

be the support gauge of `A`. Finite-dimensional minimax gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
2\,\delta_A
=
\min_R\sigma_A(d_R).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus restricting decision witnesses does not merely throw away some certificates after the fact. It changes the recovery geometry itself from the full norm `N` to the gauge `sigma_A` generated by the admissible witness class.

If `A=-A`, then

\[
\sigma_A(v)=\max_{r\in A}|\langle v,r\rangle|
\tag{10}
\]

is a seminorm. Its kernel is

\[
A^\perp
:=
\{v\in V:\langle v,r\rangle=0\text{ for every }r\in A\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\delta_A=0
\iff
\exists R\text{ with }d_R\in A^\perp.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Writing

\[
p:=(P_\theta)_\theta,
\qquad
\mathcal C_K:=\{(Q_\theta R)_\theta:R:Y\rightsquigarrow X\},
\tag{13}
\]

and letting

\[
\pi_A:V\to V/A^\perp
\tag{14}
\]

be the quotient map, `(12)` is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\delta_A=0
\iff
\pi_A(p)\in\pi_A(\mathcal C_K).
}
\tag{15}
\]

So exact recovery relative to a symmetric witness class means exact recovery only **modulo the quotient invisible to that class**.

The relevant geometry is the restriction to `H`, because every recovery residual is row-zero. Let

\[
\rho:V^*\to H^*
\tag{16}
\]

be restriction of linear functionals. Parameterwise additive constants belong to `H^\perp` and therefore do not affect any recovery witness. The effective witness body is `rho(A)`, not its particular representative in the ambient payoff space.

This produces three distinct completeness levels for a symmetric admissible body.

1. If
   \[
   A^\perp\cap H\ne\{0\},
   \tag{17}
   \]
   then the class is **directionally blind**: a nonzero row-zero residual direction exists that every admissible witness annihilates. A particular compression may still be detected, but the witness class does not separate the full residual space.

2. If
   \[
   A^\perp\cap H=\{0\},
   \tag{18}
   \]
   then `sigma_A` is a norm on `H`. Since `H` is finite-dimensional and `A subseteq B_*`, there is a sharp positive norming constant
   \[
   \alpha_A
   :=
   \min_{\substack{v\in H\\N(v)=1}}\sigma_A(v)
   >0
   \tag{19}
   \]
   such that
   \[
   \alpha_A N(v)\le\sigma_A(v)\le N(v)
   \qquad(v\in H).
   \tag{20}
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
   \boxed{
   \alpha_A\,\delta_{\rm rec}
   \le
   \delta_A
   \le
   \delta_{\rm rec}.
   }
   \tag{21}
   \]
   The restricted class is then **zero-error complete** and quantitatively comparable to full deficiency, although it may distort the calibrated amount of information loss.

3. The restricted gauge agrees exactly with the full total-variation recovery geometry on every row-zero residual if and only if
   \[
   \boxed{
   \rho(A)=\rho(B_*).
   }
   \tag{22}
   \]
   Indeed compact convex bodies are determined by their support functions, and on `H` the support functions of `A` and `B_*` depend only on their restricted images. Under `(22)`,
   \[
   \sigma_A(v)=N(v)
   \qquad(v\in H),
   \tag{23}
   \]
   hence
   \[
   \delta_A=\delta_{\rm rec}
   \tag{24}
   \]
   for every finite compression with residuals in `H`. This is **full quantitative completeness** at the Le Cam scale.

The distinction is therefore structural: separating residual directions is enough to detect whether exact fidelity has failed; a positive norming constant controls robustness; reproducing the entire effective dual ball is required to retain the exact total-variation calibration.

## Derivation

### AF-127 is the full dual support geometry

For `v=(v_theta)_theta in V`, the norm in `(3)` has dual norm

\[
N_*(r)=\sum_\theta\|r_\theta\|_\infty.
\tag{25}
\]

Therefore

\[
N(v)=\max_{r\in B_*}\langle v,r\rangle.
\tag{26}
\]

AF-127 derives the same body by introducing a prior `lambda` and variables satisfying

\[
|r_{\theta x}|\le\lambda_\theta,
\qquad
\sum_\theta\lambda_\theta=1.
\tag{27}
\]

Eliminating `lambda` gives exactly `(5)`: a vector `r` admits such a prior if and only if the sum of its rowwise infinity norms is at most one. Thus `(4)` is not a new dualization of AF-127; it is its norm-theoretic form.

### Convex admissible witnesses commute with the recovery minimization

The set of recovery channels is a compact convex product of simplices. The residual `d_R` depends affinely on `R`. By assumption `A` is compact and convex, and the pairing

\[
(R,r)\mapsto\langle d_R,r\rangle
\tag{28}
\]

is continuous and affine in each variable. Hence the finite-dimensional minimax theorem gives

\[
\max_{r\in A}\min_R\langle d_R,r\rangle
=
\min_R\max_{r\in A}\langle d_R,r\rangle.
\tag{29}
\]

The inner maximum on the right is precisely `sigma_A(d_R)`, proving `(9)`.

Because `0 in A`, `sigma_A` is nonnegative. If `A` is centrally symmetric, its support function is the absolute pairing supremum `(10)`, hence a seminorm. Its zero set is exactly the common annihilator `(11)`, giving `(12)` and the quotient statement `(15)`.

### Effective witnesses live on the row-zero residual space

For every `theta`, both `P_theta` and `Q_theta R` are probability distributions, so

\[
\sum_x d_{R,\theta x}=0.
\tag{30}
\]

If `c_theta` is added to every action payoff in row `theta`, then

\[
\sum_x d_{R,\theta x}c_\theta=0.
\tag{31}
\]

Such parameterwise constants are therefore pure gauge for recovery deficiency. More generally two witness bodies with the same restricted image under `rho` have the same support function on every possible residual and hence induce the same restricted deficiency.

This is why the completeness criterion must be stated in `H^*`: demanding equality of ambient payoff bodies would falsely distinguish witness descriptions that differ only by residual-invisible constants.

### Separation, norming, and exact calibration are different gates

Under symmetry, condition `(18)` says exactly that `sigma_A` has trivial kernel on `H`. The `N`-unit sphere in `H` is compact, `sigma_A` is continuous, and it is strictly positive there. Hence the minimum in `(19)` exists and is positive. The upper bound in `(20)` follows from `A subseteq B_*`. Taking the minimum over recovery channels proves `(21)`.

For the strongest gate, the support function of `rho(A)` at `v in H` is `sigma_A(v)`, while the support function of `rho(B_*)` is `N(v)`. Equality of compact convex sets in finite dimensions is equivalent to equality of their support functions on every direction. This proves `(22)`--`(23)`.

The three gates therefore cannot be collapsed into one vague claim that a witness family is "rich enough." A class may separate all residual directions and still have a very small `alpha_A`; it then detects every positive defect in principle but can be arbitrarily poorly calibrated relative to total variation across a varying family of admissible classes.

## Convexification is a mathematical assumption, not bookkeeping

Equation `(9)` requires convexity of the admissible witness body. If a natural witness class is not closed under mixing, silently replacing it by its convex hull can create a stronger certificate that no raw admissible witness possesses.

A recovery problem itself gives a finite counterexample. Let

\[
\Theta=\{1,2\},
\qquad
X=\{0,1\},
\qquad
Y=\{*\},
\tag{32}
\]

with

\[
P_1=\delta_0,
\qquad
P_2=\delta_1,
\tag{33}
\]

and let `K` erase the observation completely. Every recovery channel is determined by

\[
R_t(0\mid *)=t,
\qquad
R_t(1\mid *)=1-t,
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{34}
\]

The residual rows are

\[
d_{R_t,1}=(1-t,t-1),
\qquad
d_{R_t,2}=(-t,t).
\tag{35}
\]

Define two admissible dual witnesses by

\[
w_1:
\quad
(w_{1,1},w_{1,2})=
\left((0,0),(-1/2,1/2)\right),
\tag{36}
\]

and

\[
w_2:
\quad
(w_{2,1},w_{2,2})=
\left((1/2,-1/2),(0,0)\right).
\tag{37}
\]

Both lie in `B_*`, and

\[
\langle d_{R_t},w_1\rangle=t,
\qquad
\langle d_{R_t},w_2\rangle=1-t.
\tag{38}
\]

For the raw nonconvex class

\[
W=\{0,w_1,w_2\},
\tag{39}
\]

every individual witness can be defeated by an endpoint recovery:

\[
\max_{w\in W}\min_t\langle d_{R_t},w\rangle=0.
\tag{40}
\]

But the mixed witness

\[
\bar w=\frac12(w_1+w_2)
\in\operatorname{conv}(W)
\tag{41}
\]

satisfies

\[
\langle d_{R_t},\bar w\rangle=\frac12
\qquad\forall t,
\tag{42}
\]

so

\[
\max_{w\in\operatorname{conv}(W)}
\min_t\langle d_{R_t},w\rangle
\ge\frac12.
\tag{43}
\]

Thus convex closure is legitimate only when probabilistic or affine mixing of the candidate witnesses is itself part of the declared admissible category. A locality, positivity, spectral, geometric, or arithmetic restriction must be audited for this closure rather than convexified automatically to make minimax available.

## Relationship to AF-126, AF-127, and AF-128

AF-126 identifies the correct finite approximate object: one reverse channel must simultaneously reconstruct the entire experiment, and selected pairwise scalar diagnostics can miss a positive defect.

AF-127 supplies the unrestricted dual body and ends with the precise open question addressed here: which witnesses remain complete after naturality, symmetry, locality, positivity, spectral, geometric, or arithmetic restrictions are imposed?

AF-128 shows that at least one unrestricted optimal witness can be chosen identity-calibrated upstream. AF-129 does not weaken that calibration result. Instead it gives the next audit layer: after translating a concrete admissibility rule into an effective prior-weighted witness body, determine whether that body is convex, what quotient it annihilates, whether it norms the row-zero residual space, and how much of the full TV dual ball it retains.

This also sharpens the warning from AF-126. A convenient restricted statistic may fail for two fundamentally different reasons: it may have a genuine blind kernel on `H`, or it may separate all residuals but with poor quantitative norming. Those cases require different repairs.

The surrounding mathematics is classical, and no novelty claim is made for restricted deficiency, support-function metrics, or minimax duality.

The chapter explicitly treats deficiencies and distances relative to restricted types of decision problems. Role: classical test-function-generated probability metrics and maximal generating classes, placing witness-class-induced gauges inside established integral probability metric geometry.
- Maurice Sion, **“On general minimax theorems,”** *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* 8(1), 171--176 (1958), DOI `10.2140/pjm.1958.8.171`. Role: classical minimax theorem justifying the exchange in `(29)`; the present setting is finite-dimensional and bilinear.

The derived Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is organizational but exact: it places the finite Le Cam residuals of AF-126--AF-128 in the effective quotient `H`, then separates three questions that a concrete restricted witness class must answer—whether it has blind directions, whether it is uniformly norming, and whether it reproduces the full TV dual geometry. These are consequences of classical convex/statistical decision theory, not a new theory of deficiency.

## Boundary conditions and falsification checks

The statement has strict boundaries.

1. **Finite experiments.** Compactness and minimax are immediate here. Infinite measurable experiments require the topological and measurability hypotheses of the general comparison-of-experiments theory.

2. **`A` is an effective prior-weighted witness body.** A syntactic class of losses or decision problems must first be mapped correctly into the dual variables of AF-127. A restriction on `u`, `lambda`, actions, or decision rules does not automatically become the same set `A`.

3. **Convexity is substantive.** If the raw class is not closed under witness mixing, `(9)` applies to its convex closure only if that closure is genuinely admissible. Equations `(32)`--`(43)` show that unjustified convexification can turn a zero raw witness gap into a positive mixed gap.

4. **Symmetry is required for the quotient-seminorm language.** Without `A=-A`, `sigma_A` remains an exact sublinear support gauge through `(9)`, but its zero set need not be a linear subspace and should not be advertised as a quotient norm.

5. **The effective space is `H`.** Ambient payoff distinctions lying in `H^perp`, including parameterwise additive constants, are irrelevant to recovery residuals. Completeness must be tested after restriction to `H^*`.

6. **Directional separation is not exact calibration.** Trivial kernel on `H` implies a positive finite-dimensional norming constant, but it does not imply `alpha_A=1`. Exact recovery detection, stable quantitative comparison, and equality with full Le Cam deficiency are different claims.

7. **No arithmetic completeness is established.** This finding does not show that any natural prime, Weil, spectral, positivity, or geometric witness class satisfies `(18)` or `(22)`. Those are concrete theorem obligations for later applications.

A decisive falsification test for a proposed application is therefore explicit: derive its effective witness body from the actual admissibility rule, verify or refute convexity/symmetry, compute or bound `A^perp cap H` and `alpha_A`, and compare `rho(A)` with `rho(B_*)`. If those objects cannot be derived from the declared mathematical category, a claim of restricted-witness completeness has not yet been justified.

## Consequences for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-129 turns the vague phrase "the allowed witnesses are rich enough" into a sequence of exact gates. For any proposed naturality, symmetry, locality, positivity, spectral, geometric, or arithmetic restriction, the first question is the quotient it necessarily forgets; the second is whether the surviving witnesses norm all residual directions with a nondegenerate constant; only the strongest classes reproduce the exact Le Cam scale.

For later rational-prime applications this gives a reusable stopping rule. A prime-specific discriminator that is visible only to witnesses outside the intrinsically admissible body is not preserved in the declared destination category merely because an unrestricted decision problem can detect it. Conversely, a concrete admissible body with a quantified norming constant would convert qualitative survival into a robust lower bound on the full matched-control recovery defect.

The next line-specific frontier is therefore concrete rather than axiomatic: take an actual constrained witness family arising from a Mathia compression and determine its effective dual body or a mathematically tractable approximation to it. That is the point at which the general classical geometry can begin to constrain prime-specific constructions rather than only classify abstract information loss.
