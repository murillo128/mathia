# AF-030 — Linear-test fidelity is closed-span density, not generated-algebra density

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`

## Claim

Let `X` be a compact Hausdorff space, let `M(X)` denote the finite signed regular Borel measures on `X`, and let

\[
\mathcal F\subset C(X,\mathbb R)
\]

be any family of retained scalar observables. The corresponding exact linear-test compression is

\[
L_{\mathcal F}:M(X)\to\mathbb R^{\mathcal F},
\qquad
L_{\mathcal F}(\mu)
=
\left(\int_X f\,d\mu\right)_{f\in\mathcal F}.
\]

Define the **closed linear information hull**

\[
V_{\mathcal F}
=
\overline{\operatorname{span}(\mathcal F)}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}
\subset C(X,\mathbb R)
\]

and its annihilator

\[
V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}
=
\left\{
\eta\in M(X):
\int_X v\,d\eta=0
\text{ for every }v\in V_{\mathcal F}
\right\}.
\]

Then the information retained by the test family is classified exactly by this closed linear subspace.

1. **Exact fiber theorem.**
   \[
   \boxed{
   L_{\mathcal F}(\mu)=L_{\mathcal F}(\nu)
   \iff
   \mu-\nu\in V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}.
   }
   \]
   Hence the exact destination identifies precisely the cosets of `V_F^perp` in `M(X)`.

2. **Full signed-measure fidelity.**
   \[
   \boxed{
   L_{\mathcal F}\text{ is injective on }M(X)
   \iff
   V_{\mathcal F}=C(X,\mathbb R).
   }
   \]
   Thus an infinite family of exact real-valued measurements is not faithful merely because it is large, point-separating, or generates a dense algebra. What matters for linear testing is density of its **actual linear span** in the continuous functions.

3. **Positive/probability collision when constants are retained.** If
   \[
   1\in V_{\mathcal F}
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad
   V_{\mathcal F}\ne C(X,\mathbb R),
   \]
   then there exist distinct mutually singular probability measures `P,Q` such that
   \[
   \boxed{
   \int f\,dP=\int f\,dQ
   \qquad\forall f\in\mathcal F.
   }
   \]
   The non-fidelity therefore cannot be dismissed as an artifact of signed cancellation or unequal total mass.

4. **Restricted source classes.** For an admissible model class
   \[
   \mathcal S\subset M(X),
   \]
   exact fidelity is equivalent to
   \[
   \boxed{
   (\mathcal S-\mathcal S)\cap V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}=\{0\}.
   }
   \]
   Ambient non-density is therefore a no-go for unrestricted measures but not automatically for a structured nonlinear family such as prime-power measures.

5. **Adding retained lifts/tests.** For another family `G subset C(X)`, the residual ambiguity on `S` becomes
   \[
   \boxed{
   (\mathcal S-\mathcal S)
   \cap V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}
   \cap V_{\mathcal G}^{\perp}.
   }
   \]
   This gives an exact lift audit: extra observables help only insofar as they kill directions that remain in the previous annihilator on the admissible difference set.

6. **Generated algebras are not free information.** The algebra generated by the retained observables may be dense even when the retained linear tests are highly non-faithful. For example, on `X=[0,1]`, retain only
   \[
   \mathcal F=\{1,x\}.
   \]
   Its generated algebra is the polynomial algebra, which is uniformly dense in `C([0,1])`, but
   \[
   P=\delta_{1/2},
   \qquad
   Q=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1
   \]
   satisfy
   \[
   \int 1\,dP=\int 1\,dQ=1,
   \qquad
   \int x\,dP=\int x\,dQ=\tfrac12.
   \]
   They are therefore indistinguishable by the retained data. The values of `x^2,x^3,...` cannot be inferred merely because those functions are algebraically generated by `x`.

This sharpens the observable-algebra lesson of AF-029. Stone-Weierstrass is a fidelity theorem for a linear-test family only when the **linear information actually retained** is already dense—for example because the retained family is itself closed under products up to linear combinations. One may not replace the measured linear span by a larger generated algebra unless the destination really contains those additional product observables or they reduce exactly to retained measurements.

## Exact quotient is the annihilator quotient

Suppose

\[
L_{\mathcal F}(\mu)=L_{\mathcal F}(\nu).
\]

Then for `eta=mu-nu`,

\[
\int f\,d\eta=0
\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal F.
\]

By linearity the same holds for every finite linear combination of functions in `F`. Integration against a finite signed measure is a bounded linear functional on `C(X)` under the uniform norm, so the equality extends by continuity to the uniform closure `V_F`. Thus

\[
\eta\in V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}.
\]

The converse is immediate because `F subset V_F`. Therefore

\[
\ker L_{\mathcal F}=V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}
\]

and two source measures have the same destination exactly when they differ by an annihilator element.

This is the correct linear analogue of the fiber language used throughout Arithmetic Fidelity: the compression does not retain an informal collection of "features" but the restriction of each source measure, viewed as a continuous functional, to the closed test subspace `V_F`.

## Uniform density is necessary and sufficient

If

\[
V_{\mathcal F}=C(X),
\]

then a measure in `V_F^perp` integrates every continuous function to zero. By uniqueness in the Riesz representation theorem, that measure is zero. Hence `L_F` is injective.

Conversely, suppose `V_F` is a proper closed subspace of `C(X)`. Hahn-Banach separation gives a nonzero bounded linear functional

\[
\Lambda:C(X)\to\mathbb R
\]

that vanishes on `V_F`. By the Riesz-Markov representation theorem there is a nonzero finite signed regular Borel measure `eta` with

\[
\Lambda(g)=\int_X g\,d\eta.
\]

Therefore

\[
\eta\in V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\},
\]

so `L_F(eta)=L_F(0)` and the test map is not injective.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\operatorname{span}(\mathcal F)}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}
=C(X)
}
\]

is not merely a convenient sufficient condition. It is the exact criterion for determining every finite signed regular measure by a family of continuous linear tests on a compact source space.

## Proper unital test spaces force probability collisions

The Hahn-Banach witness can be converted into a positive normalized collision when constants are retained.

Assume

\[
1\in V_{\mathcal F}
\]

and choose nonzero

\[
\eta\in V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}.
\]

Then

\[
\eta(X)=\int_X1\,d\eta=0.
\]

Write the Jordan decomposition

\[
\eta=\eta^+-\eta^-.
\]

Since `eta` is nonzero and has total mass zero, both positive measures are nonzero and have the same mass

\[
m=\eta^+(X)=\eta^-(X)>0.
\]

Set

\[
P=\frac{\eta^+}{m},
\qquad
Q=\frac{\eta^-}{m}.
\]

The measures `P` and `Q` are mutually singular probability measures, and for every retained test

\[
\int_X f\,dP-\int_X f\,dQ
=
\frac1m\int_X f\,d\eta
=0.
\]

Hence properness of a unital closed linear test space forces a genuine positive-probability same-destination pair.

This complements AF-021. There finite-dimensional affine dependence gave explicit finite-support positive collisions. Here the obstruction applies equally to **infinite** continuous test families: unless their linear span is uniformly dense, some positive normalized collision remains.

## Restricted source classes and retained lifts

The ambient theorem should not be overextended to structured model classes. Let

\[
\mathcal S\subset M(X).
\]

The restriction of `L_F` to `S` fails to be injective exactly when there are distinct `mu,nu in S` with

\[
\mu-\nu\in V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
L_{\mathcal F}|_{\mathcal S}\text{ is injective}
\iff
(\mathcal S-\mathcal S)\cap V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}=\{0\}.
\]

This is the precise constrained-model boundary already needed in AF-021 and AF-029. An ambient measure collision is not automatically a Beurling/generalized-prime collision because the admissible difference set of prime-power measures can be much smaller than `M(X)`.

Now add a second retained family `G`. Equality of both test families is equivalent to annihilation of

\[
\operatorname{span}(\mathcal F\cup\mathcal G),
\]

so

\[
V_{\mathcal F\cup\mathcal G}^{\perp}
=
V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}\cap V_{\mathcal G}^{\perp}.
\]

Therefore the surviving ambiguity on `S` is

\[
(\mathcal S-\mathcal S)
\cap V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}
\cap V_{\mathcal G}^{\perp}.
\]

This gives a non-tautological way to assess a proposed relational lift: do not ask only whether it adds more numbers; ask which previously invisible admissible difference directions cease to annihilate the enlarged retained subspace.

## Generated algebras are not automatically retained algebras

There is an important distinction between two statements:

\[
\text{the retained functions generate an algebra that separates points}
\]

and

\[
\text{the retained linear measurements determine integrals of that algebra}.
\]

The first does not imply the second.

Take `X=[0,1]` and retain only `1` and the coordinate function `x`. Since the unital algebra generated by `x` contains every polynomial, Stone-Weierstrass makes its uniform closure all of `C([0,1])`. But the actual retained linear subspace is only

\[
V_{\mathcal F}=\operatorname{span}\{1,x\}.
\]

The probability measures

\[
\delta_{1/2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1
\]

have identical retained values. Their second moments already differ:

\[
\int x^2\,d\delta_{1/2}=\frac14,
\qquad
\int x^2\,d\left(\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1\right)=\frac12.
\]

Knowing `int x dmu` does not give `int x^2 dmu`. Multiplication occurs in the function algebra, while the compression stores linear functionals of the source measure. Unless product observables are separately measured or their integrals are exactly reducible to existing retained values, algebra generation creates mathematical functions but not new information at the destination.

This distinction matters whenever a fidelity argument invokes Stone-Weierstrass, invariant algebras, polynomial closure, moments, or nonlinear combinations of observables. The topology and algebra must match the **measurement model**, not merely the source-side function language.

## Why the AF-029 lattice argument remains valid

AF-029 retains the complete lattice family

\[
\{1,\cos(hx),\cos(2hx),\ldots\}
\]

inside its weighted modulation tests. In that special case the actual linear span is already closed under multiplication because

\[
\cos(mhx)\cos(nhx)
=
\tfrac12\cos((m+n)hx)
+
\tfrac12\cos(|m-n|hx).
\]

Thus no unmeasured product observable is introduced when calling the span an algebra. Below the sharp horizon `hB<=pi`, the cosine family separates points on `[0,B]`; its unital linear span is therefore a point-separating algebra, and Stone-Weierstrass legitimately makes that **same retained linear span** dense.

Above the horizon, the failure of point separation is exactly the cosine alias described in AF-029. Hence AF-030 does not weaken AF-029. It identifies why that argument works and prevents its conclusion from being generalized to arbitrary observable families merely because their abstract generated algebra is rich.

The corrected reusable principle is

\[
\boxed{
\text{linear test compression}
\longrightarrow
\text{closed linear information hull}
\longrightarrow
\text{annihilator quotient}.
}
\]

Stone-Weierstrass enters only when one has independently shown that the actual retained linear hull is a unital point-separating algebra.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The functional-analytic ingredients are classical.

- Walter Rudin, ***Functional Analysis***, 2nd ed., McGraw-Hill (1991), especially the Hahn-Banach separation and annihilator results for closed subspaces of normed spaces. Role: a proper closed test subspace has a nonzero continuous functional annihilating it; equivalently, closed subspaces are recovered from their annihilators.
- Walter Rudin, ***Real and Complex Analysis***, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill (1987), Riesz representation theorems for continuous linear functionals on spaces of continuous functions. Role: identifies the separating functional with a finite regular signed/complex measure, converting abstract non-density into an exact same-test measure witness.
- Marshall H. Stone, **“The Generalized Weierstrass Approximation Theorem,”** *Mathematics Magazine* 21(4) (1948), 167–184, DOI `10.2307/3029750`. Role: classical density theorem for unital point-separating subalgebras of continuous functions; supplies the valid special route from algebra structure to measure determination when the retained linear span itself is such an algebra.

No novelty is claimed for Hahn-Banach, Riesz-Markov representation, annihilators, measure-determining classes, or Stone-Weierstrass. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the exact audit distinction among three objects that can otherwise be conflated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{listed observables}
\subset
\text{closed linear information hull}
\subseteq
\text{generated nonlinear/algebraic function class}.
}
\]

For linear integral measurements, the middle object—and only the middle object—determines the compression fiber unless the destination explicitly includes additional nonlinear/product measurements.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Compact Hausdorff `X` is used so `C(X)` has the standard Riesz-Markov dual of finite regular measures. Locally compact spaces admit an analogous formulation with `C_0(X)` and appropriate finite regular measures.
- The theorem concerns real continuous linear tests and finite signed measures. Complex-valued tests/measures have the analogous annihilator statement after using the complex dual formulation.
- The probability-collision conclusion uses `1 in V_F`. Without that assumption a proper test space still has a nonzero signed-measure collision, but the Hahn-Banach witness need not have zero total mass and therefore need not normalize directly to two probability measures.
- Uniform-density is an exact-identifiability criterion, not a stability theorem. A dense family may be catastrophically ill-conditioned for approximate/noisy recovery.
- Point separation by `F` itself is neither necessary nor sufficient as a standalone slogan for measure fidelity. What matters is whether the closed **linear** hull is all of `C(X)`; point separation becomes decisive only when additional algebraic closure hypotheses let Stone-Weierstrass apply to that same linear hull.
- Ambient non-fidelity does not imply non-fidelity on a constrained source class. Any arithmetic application must test the annihilator against the actual difference set of admissible generalized-prime/prime-power measures.
- Nonlinear measurements change the compression model. If the destination stores products, covariances, determinants, spectra, or other nonlinear statistics, encode those as actual retained observables on the relevant source space before applying this theorem rather than pretending they are generated for free from linear test values.

## Consequences for Arithmetic Fidelity

AF-030 supplies a general exact language for one major compression class: families of continuous scalar linear tests on measures.

The main operational consequence is a stricter information accounting rule. When a proposed argument moves from measured observables to their polynomial, multiplicative, or algebraic closure, it must prove that the corresponding destination values are genuinely recoverable from the retained data. Otherwise the proof has silently added a lift.

For constrained arithmetic systems, the decisive object is not ambient density alone but

\[
(\mathcal S-\mathcal S)\cap V_{\mathcal F}^{\perp}.
\]

This turns the search for a minimal retained lift into a precise separation problem on the residual admissible difference set. A useful new observable is one that kills a surviving direction there; an observable that vanishes on exactly the same residual directions adds no fidelity even if it looks structurally different.

The result also clarifies the relationship between AF-021 and AF-029. AF-021 is the finite-dimensional convex obstruction; AF-029 is a special infinite-family case where the retained span is already an algebra and becomes dense exactly before the cosine alias horizon. AF-030 supplies the general ambient theorem containing both as distinct manifestations of the same closed-linear-hull/annihilator mechanism.
