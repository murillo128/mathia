# AF-158 — Shtarkov loss is two-sided calibrated to canonical Bayes recovery

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `EXACT-DERIVED`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY`, `POSITIVE-REFERENCE-CONSTRUCTION`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-144 and AF-149 show that Pearson chi-square loss relative to one propagated source reference controls the error of the corresponding common Bayes/Petz reverse. AF-157 then identifies the worst-member Shtarkov loss as a convex-hull-invariant likelihood-witness norm, while also proving that it is not uniformly equivalent to the **optimal** Le Cam recovery deficiency over growing experiment families.

The missing distinction can be made exact. Pearson data-processing loss is quantitatively calibrated in both directions to the recovery error of **its own reference-selected Bayes reverse**, with the reverse direction controlled by the oscillation of the source likelihood ratio. For the Shtarkov reference this oscillation is bounded by the Shtarkov mass, so the source envelope complexity is an explicit calibration scale rather than merely a coding-theoretic side quantity.

Let

\[
\mathcal E=(P_i)_{i=1}^m
\]

be a finite statistical experiment on a finite sample space `X`, let

\[
K:X\rightsquigarrow Y
\]

be a stochastic compression, and let `M` be any probability reference dominating every `P_i`. Put

\[
q=MK,
\qquad
f_i=\frac{dP_i}{dM},
\tag{1}
\]

and define the Bayes reverse associated with this same propagated source reference by

\[
R_M(x\mid y)
=\frac{M(x)K(y\mid x)}{q(y)}
\qquad(q(y)>0).
\tag{2}
\]

For each member define

\[
\varepsilon_i(M,K)
:=
\chi^2(P_i\|M)-\chi^2(P_iK\|q),
\tag{3}
\]

its actual error under the canonical reverse

\[
\beta_i(M,K)
:=
\|P_i-P_iKR_M\|_{\rm TV},
\tag{4}
\]

and the source likelihood oscillation

\[
\omega_i(M)
:=
\max_{x:M(x)>0} f_i(x)
-
\min_{x:M(x)>0} f_i(x).
\tag{5}
\]

Then, for every member,

\[
\boxed{
4\,\beta_i(M,K)^2
\le
\varepsilon_i(M,K)
\le
\omega_i(M)\,\beta_i(M,K).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
\beta_*(M,K):=\max_i\beta_i(M,K),
\qquad
\Gamma_M(K;\mathcal E):=\max_i\varepsilon_i(M,K),
\qquad
\Omega_M(\mathcal E):=\max_i\omega_i(M),
\tag{7}
\]

one has the whole-experiment calibration

\[
\boxed{
4\,\beta_*^2
\le
\Gamma_M
\le
\Omega_M\,\beta_*.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Now choose the source Shtarkov reference from AF-149,

\[
s(x)=\max_iP_i(x),
\qquad
C=\sum_xs(x),
\qquad
M_*(x)=\frac{s(x)}C.
\tag{9}
\]

Its member likelihood ratios are

\[
f_i(x)=C\frac{P_i(x)}{s(x)},
\qquad
0\le f_i(x)\le C,
\tag{10}
\]

so

\[
\Omega_{M_*}\le C.
\tag{11}
\]

AF-157's convex-hull-invariant defect is exactly

\[
\Gamma_{\rm Sh}=\Gamma_{M_*}.
\]

Therefore, if `R_*` is the Bayes reverse generated by the **propagated source Shtarkov reference** and

\[
\beta_{\rm Sh}:=\max_i\|P_i-P_iKR_*\|_{\rm TV},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
4\,\beta_{\rm Sh}^2
\le
\Gamma_{\rm Sh}
\le
\Omega_{M_*}\,\beta_{\rm Sh}
\le
C\,\beta_{\rm Sh}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus `Gamma_Sh` does have a two-sided approximate recovery interpretation, but the endpoint is not the optimized Le Cam deficiency. It is the error of the **canonical source-selected Shtarkov Bayes reverse**, and the reverse modulus necessarily carries a source complexity scale. In any family for which `Omega_{M_*}` is uniformly bounded, `Gamma_Sh -> 0` if and only if `beta_Sh -> 0`. More generally,

\[
C\,\beta_{\rm Sh}\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma_{\rm Sh}\to0,
\tag{13}
\]

while

\[
\Gamma_{\rm Sh}\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta_{\rm Sh}\le\frac12\sqrt{\Gamma_{\rm Sh}}\to0
\tag{14}
\]

without any complexity assumption.

This resolves one part of the destination-calibration problem exposed by AF-156--AF-157. The source-natural Shtarkov likelihood geometry is not intrinsically miscalibrated; rather, it is calibrated to the particular common reverse channel selected by that source reference. Passing from that canonical reverse to the **best possible** reverse is an additional optimization that can destroy any converse unless effective family complexity is controlled.

## Derivation

### The recovered density is a self-adjoint Markov smoothing of the source likelihood

Work under the reference joint law

\[
J(x,y)=M(x)K(y\mid x).
\tag{15}
\]

Define the conditional-expectation operator

\[
T:L^2(M)\to L^2(q),
\qquad
(Tf)(y)=\mathbb E_M[f(X)\mid Y=y].
\tag{16}
\]

Its adjoint is forward averaging,

\[
(T^*g)(x)=\sum_yK(y\mid x)g(y).
\tag{17}
\]

Hence

\[
B:=T^*T:L^2(M)\to L^2(M)
\tag{18}
\]

is self-adjoint, positive, Markov, and a contraction:

\[
0\preceq B\preceq I,
\qquad
B\mathbf1=\mathbf1.
\tag{19}
\]

Because `P_i=f_iM`, its compressed likelihood relative to `q` is `Tf_i`. Applying the Bayes reverse `(2)` then gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d(P_iKR_M)}{dM}=Bf_i.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus the actual source-versus-recovered density residual is

\[
h_i:=(I-B)f_i,
\tag{21}
\]

with

\[
\mathbb E_M h_i=0
\tag{22}
\]

because both `P_i` and `P_iKR_M` are probability laws. Equation `(4)` becomes

\[
\boxed{
2\beta_i=\|h_i\|_{L^1(M)}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

### Pearson data-processing loss is the residual energy paired with the source likelihood

Since `Tf_i=d(P_iK)/dq`, expanding Pearson divergence and using `E_M f_i=E_q Tf_i=1` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_i
&=\mathbb E_M[f_i^2]-\mathbb E_q[(Tf_i)^2]\\
&=\langle f_i,(I-B)f_i\rangle_{L^2(M)}\\
&=\mathbb E_M[f_i h_i].
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

Because `E_M h_i=0`, any constant may be subtracted from `f_i` without changing the pairing. Let

\[
a_i=\frac12\left(\max f_i+\min f_i\right).
\tag{25}
\]

Then

\[
|f_i-a_i|\le\frac{\omega_i}{2},
\]

and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_i
&=\left|\mathbb E_M[(f_i-a_i)h_i]\right|\\
&\le\frac{\omega_i}{2}\mathbb E_M|h_i|\\
&=\omega_i\beta_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

This is exactly the total-variation/oscillation duality for the zero-mass signed measure `P_i-P_iKR_M`, specialized to the source likelihood witness `f_i`.

### The forward recovery inequality is the `L^2` contraction half

Because `0<=B<=I`, spectral calculus gives

\[
(I-B)^2\preceq I-B.
\tag{27}
\]

Hence

\[
\|h_i\|_2^2
=\langle f_i,(I-B)^2f_i\rangle
\le
\langle f_i,(I-B)f_i\rangle
=\varepsilon_i.
\tag{28}
\]

Since `M` is a probability law,

\[
\|h_i\|_1^2\le\|h_i\|_2^2.
\tag{29}
\]

Combining `(23)`, `(28)`, and `(29)` proves

\[
4\beta_i^2\le\varepsilon_i,
\]

which is the finite commutative recovery inequality already used in AF-144 and AF-149. Together with `(26)` this proves `(6)`, and maximizing over `i` proves `(8)`.

### Why the Shtarkov mass is the natural complexity ceiling

For `M_*` from `(9)`, every member satisfies `(10)`. Therefore each likelihood oscillation obeys

\[
\omega_i(M_*)\le C.
\]

This is stronger conceptually than replacing family size `m` by another arbitrary normalization. The constant is forced by the same source minimax domination problem that selected `M_*`: AF-149 proves

\[
C
=
\inf_M\max_i\left\|\frac{dP_i}{dM}\right\|_\infty.
\tag{30}
\]

The sharper quantity in `(12)` is actually `Omega_{M_*}`, not `C`; `C` is a canonical source-only upper envelope when no finer memberwise oscillation information is used.

## Sharpness: the private-label family attains the Shtarkov upper modulus

Use the AF-146/AF-149/AF-156 private-label experiment

\[
P_i=(1-\rho)\delta_0+\rho\delta_i,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m,
\tag{31}
\]

and the compression that retains `0` while merging all private labels. Its Shtarkov mass is

\[
C_m=1+(m-1)\rho.
\tag{32}
\]

For member `i`, the Shtarkov likelihood ratio is exactly

\[
f_i=C_m\mathbf1_{\{0,i\}},
\tag{33}
\]

so

\[
\omega_i=C_m.
\tag{34}
\]

The Shtarkov Bayes reverse sends the merged private output uniformly across the `m` private labels. AF-149 shows that in this family it is also the minimax-optimal reverse, hence

\[
\beta_i
=\delta_m
=\rho\left(1-\frac1m\right).
\tag{35}
\]

The exact Pearson loss is

\[
\varepsilon_i=C_m\delta_m.
\tag{36}
\]

Therefore the upper inequality in `(6)` is attained exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_i=\omega_i\beta_i=C_m\beta_i.
}
\tag{37}
\]

The complexity factor cannot be removed from a uniform theorem of this form. At the AF-157 boundary scale `rho=m^{-1/2}`,

\[
\beta_{\rm Sh}=\delta_m\sim m^{-1/2}\to0,
\qquad
C_m\sim m^{1/2},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm Sh}\to1.
\tag{38}
\]

So the apparent paradox from AF-157 is exactly accounted for by source likelihood complexity: the canonical reverse becomes accurate in total variation while the source witness has increasing oscillation quickly enough that its Pearson loss remains macroscopic.

## Arithmetic/analytic stress test: the local `p=2` Euler-factor family

AF-157 gives the two-member normalized local family

\[
P_1=\left(\frac47,\frac27,\frac17\right),
\qquad
P_2=\left(\frac{16}{21},\frac4{21},\frac1{21}\right),
\tag{39}
\]

with the compression retaining the first coordinate and merging the last two. Its Shtarkov reference is

\[
M_*=\left(\frac{16}{25},\frac6{25},\frac3{25}\right),
\qquad
C=\frac{25}{21}.
\tag{40}
\]

The Bayes reverse is deterministic on the first coordinate and, on the merged fiber, returns the second and third coordinates with probabilities `2/3` and `1/3`. It exactly recovers `P_1`, so

\[
\beta_1=\varepsilon_1=0.
\tag{41}
\]

For `P_2`, the recovered law differs only on the merged fiber:

\[
\beta_2=\frac{2}{63}.
\tag{42}
\]

AF-157 gives

\[
\varepsilon_2=\Gamma_{\rm Sh}=\frac{50}{3969}.
\tag{43}
\]

The likelihood ratio is

\[
f_2=\left(\frac{25}{21},\frac{50}{63},\frac{25}{63}\right),
\]

hence

\[
\omega_2=\frac{50}{63}.
\tag{44}
\]

The exact bounds are therefore

\[
\frac{16}{3969}
=4\beta_2^2
\le
\frac{50}{3969}
=\varepsilon_2
\le
\frac{100}{3969}
=\omega_2\beta_2.
\tag{45}
\]

Thus the calibration is non-vacuous on an arithmetic/analytic source family and does not depend on the private-label symmetry used for sharpness.

## Falsification and boundaries

The theorem does **not** give a converse bound from optimal Le Cam deficiency to `Gamma_Sh`. By definition,

\[
\delta_{\rm rec}(K;\mathcal E)
\le
\beta_{\rm Sh},
\tag{46}
\]

because deficiency optimizes over all reverse channels while `beta_Sh` evaluates one fixed source-selected reverse. AF-157 already proves that replacing `beta_Sh` by `delta_rec` in the right side of `(12)` cannot yield a dimension-free converse over arbitrary growing families.

The theorem also does not say that Shtarkov mass alone measures information loss. `C` is a source complexity ceiling on the calibration constant; the actual compression loss still enters through `beta_Sh`. Recomputing a fresh Shtarkov reference after compression changes the reverse and falls outside this statement: the source reference must be propagated through `K` exactly as in AF-149--AF-153.

Nor is the oscillation constant automatically small in arithmetic applications. A proposed rational-prime discriminator may have a likelihood profile whose oscillation grows with scale, support, or control-family richness. In that case `(12)` diagnoses the missing quantitative gate rather than solving it.

Finally, all statements here are finite and classical. Extending them to infinite experiments, nondominated families, operator-valued/noncommutative settings, or asymptotic limits requires separate tightness/domain arguments.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

No novelty claim is made for the inequalities or their ingredients. The lower recovery inequality is the finite commutative form of the chi-square/Petz recoverability mechanism already audited in AF-144; Li Gao, Haojian Li, Iman Marvian, and Cambyse Rouzé, **“Sufficient Statistic and Recoverability via Quantum Fisher Information,”** *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 405, 180 (2024), DOI `10.1007/s00220-024-05053-z`, proves approximate recovery from chi-square data-processing loss in the broader quantum setting and specializes to the classical commutative case.

The decision-theoretic boundary is classical Blackwell--Le Cam theory. Friedrich Liese, **“phi-divergences, sufficiency, Bayes sufficiency, and deficiency,”** *Kybernetika* 48(4), 690--713 (2012), studies precisely the relations among `phi`-divergences, sufficiency, Bayes sufficiency, and Le Cam deficiency. The upper estimate `(26)` is the elementary duality between total variation of a zero-mass signed measure and the oscillation seminorm of a bounded witness. More broadly, reverse inequalities between `f`-divergences and total variation under bounded relative-information hypotheses are part of the established literature; see Igal Sason and Sergio Verdú, **“f-Divergence Inequalities,”** *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 62(11), 5973--6006 (2016), DOI `10.1109/TIT.2016.2603151`.

Targeted searches did not identify the exact Shtarkov-reference packaging `(12)` as a standard named result. That absence is not evidence of novelty. The durable Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the placement of classical ingredients against AF-156--AF-157's explicit obstruction: it identifies **which recovery target the Shtarkov witness norm actually measures** and isolates the likelihood-oscillation/Shtarkov-mass factor as the sharp complexity gate separating canonical Bayes recovery from a complexity-free converse to optimal deficiency.

## Consequence for the line

The live Shtarkov branch can now distinguish three objects that should not be conflated:

1. `delta_rec`, the best possible whole-experiment reverse error;
2. `beta_Sh`, the error of the canonical reverse selected by the propagated source Shtarkov reference;
3. `Gamma_Sh`, the convex-hull-invariant bounded-likelihood-witness loss from AF-157.

`Gamma_Sh` and `beta_Sh` are quantitatively equivalent whenever the source likelihood oscillation is controlled, while `delta_rec` may be much smaller because it introduces a separate optimization over reverse channels. The next useful step is therefore not another aggregate normalization. It is to identify, for a concrete downstream arithmetic decision class, whether the Shtarkov Bayes reverse is competitive with the best admissible recovery and whether the corresponding likelihood-oscillation complexity remains controlled under the scale at which rational-prime versus matched-control discrimination is required.
