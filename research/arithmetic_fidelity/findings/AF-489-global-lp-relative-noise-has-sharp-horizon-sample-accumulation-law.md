# AF-489 — Global `ell_p` relative noise has a sharp horizon–sample accumulation law

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `DIRICHLET-SAMPLING`, `GLOBAL-NOISE-BUDGET`, `MINIMAX-RESOLUTION`, `SHARP-RATE`, `NO-BROAD-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

The history obstruction in AF-488 is specific to a pointwise (`ell_infinity`) critical-noise geometry. If the same relative perturbations are instead coupled by one fixed global `ell_p` budget, accumulated samples become a genuine fidelity resource, with an exact dual-norm law.

Fix a real one-generator local Dirichlet family

\[
F_q(s)=G(s)+a q^{-s},\qquad a>0,\qquad q\in I\Subset(1,\infty),
\]

where `G` and `a` are known and fixed. Let

\[
s_k=s_*+kh,\qquad k=0,\ldots,N,
\]

with fixed `s_*>0` and `h>0`, and write `H=s_N`. Observe

\[
y_k=F_q(s_k)+\eta_k,
\qquad
\eta_k=a q^{-s_k}\xi_k,
\]

under the global relative-noise constraint

\[
\|\xi\|_{\ell_p}\le \varepsilon,
\qquad 1\le p\le\infty,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0<1.
\]

Let `theta=log q`, and let `R_{N,p}` be the deterministic minimax error for estimating `theta` from the whole sample vector. Then there are constants `0<c<=C<infinity`, independent of `N`, such that for all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
c\,\frac{\varepsilon}{\|s\|_{\ell_p}}
\le
R_{N,p}
\le
C\,\frac{\varepsilon}{\|s\|_{\ell_p}},
\qquad
s=(s_0,\ldots,s_N).
\]

For the fixed-step expanding lattice this gives

\[
R_{N,p}=\Theta\!\left(H^{-1-1/p}\right)
\quad (1\le p<\infty),
\]

while

\[
R_{N,\infty}=\Theta(H^{-1}).
\]

Thus `p=infinity` reproduces AF-488's horizon-only law, whereas a fixed total `ell_2` relative-energy budget gives `Theta(H^{-3/2})` resolution and a fixed total `ell_1` budget gives `Theta(H^{-2})`. The gain is not produced by history alone: it comes from **coupling the admissible perturbation across a growing sample set under a fixed total budget**.

## Exact upper bound by log-linearization and duality

Subtract the known background and divide by the known amplitude:

\[
r_k:=\frac{y_k-G(s_k)}{a}
=q^{-s_k}(1+\xi_k).
\]

Because every coordinate satisfies `|xi_k|<=||xi||_p<=epsilon_0<1`, all `r_k` are positive. Define

\[
z_k:=\log r_k=-\theta s_k+u_k,
\qquad
u_k:=\log(1+\xi_k).
\]

Here `u_k=nu_k`; equivalently, using one symbol throughout,

\[
u_k:=\log(1+\xi_k),
\qquad
z_k=-\theta s_k+u_k.
\]

On `[-epsilon_0,epsilon_0]`,

\[
|\log(1+x)|\le \frac{|x|}{1-\varepsilon_0},
\]

so

\[
\|u\|_{\ell_p}
\le
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon_0}.
\]

Let `p*` be the Hölder dual exponent. Finite-dimensional `ell_p` duality gives a vector `w` satisfying

\[
\langle w,s\rangle=1,
\qquad
\|w\|_{\ell_{p^*}}=\frac1{\|s\|_{\ell_p}}.
\]

For `1<p<infinity`, one may take

\[
w_k=\frac{s_k^{p-1}}{\|s\|_p^p};
\]

for `p=1`, take `w_k=1/||s||_1`; for `p=infinity`, place all weight at the terminal sample, `w_N=1/H`.

The estimator

\[
\widehat\theta=-\langle w,z\rangle
\]

therefore satisfies

\[
|\widehat\theta-\theta|
=|\langle w,u\rangle|
\le
\|w\|_{p^*}\|u\|_p
\le
\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon_0)\|s\|_p}.
\]

Hence

\[
R_{N,p}\le
\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon_0)\|s\|_p}.
\]

This direction is exact enough to expose the resource: the relevant sensitivity is the `ell_p` norm of the sampling-abscissa vector, not merely its maximum coordinate.

## Matching lower bound from one coherent alternative source

Fix `q_0` in the interior of `I`. Set

\[
\delta=
\frac{\varepsilon}{2e\,\|s\|_p},
\qquad
q_1=q_0 e^\delta.
\]

For sufficiently large `N`, `q_1` remains in `I`. Consider the noiseless datum generated by `q_0`:

\[
y_k=F_{q_0}(s_k).
\]

The same datum can be represented under `q_1` using the relative perturbation

\[
\xi_k^{(1)}
=
\frac{F_{q_0}(s_k)-G(s_k)}{F_{q_1}(s_k)-G(s_k)}-1
=e^{\delta s_k}-1.
\]

Since

\[
\|s\|_p\ge \|s\|_\infty=H,
\]

we have

\[
0\le\delta H\le\frac{\varepsilon}{2e}<1.
\]

For `0<=x<=1`, `e^x-1<=e x`; therefore

\[
\|\xi^{(1)}\|_p
\le e\delta\|s\|_p
=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\le\varepsilon.
\]

So the identical observation vector has two admissible explanations separated by

\[
|\log q_1-\log q_0|=\delta.
\]

Every estimator must make error at least half this separation on one of them, giving

\[
R_{N,p}
\ge
\frac{\varepsilon}{4e\,\|s\|_p}.
\]

Together with the upper bound, this proves the sharp `Theta(epsilon/||s||_p)` minimax law.

## Fixed-step asymptotics

For `1<=p<infinity`,

\[
\|s\|_p^p
=
\sum_{k=0}^{N}(s_*+kh)^p
\sim
\frac{H^{p+1}}{(p+1)h},
\]

so

\[
\|s\|_p
\asymp H^{1+1/p}.
\]

For `p=infinity`,

\[
\|s\|_\infty=H.
\]

Hence the global-budget interpolation is

\[
\boxed{
R_{N,p}\asymp
\begin{cases}
H^{-1-1/p},&1\le p<\infty,\\
H^{-1},&p=\infty.
\end{cases}}
\]

The `ell_infinity` endpoint is qualitatively special only because its total admissible perturbation grows freely with sample count. Finite `p` couples the coordinates, so each additional well-placed sample increases `||s||_p` and shrinks the indistinguishability radius.

## What actually creates the accumulation gain

The theorem should not be read as saying that replacing the word `pointwise` by `ell_p` automatically improves identifiability. The gain comes from holding the **total** global budget fixed while the number of observations grows.

If instead the admissible budget scales like

\[
\|\xi\|_p\le \varepsilon N^{1/p},
\]

which corresponds to keeping a constant `ell_p` error allowance per sample on average, then

\[
\frac{\varepsilon N^{1/p}}{\|s\|_p}
=\Theta(H^{-1})
\]

on the fixed-step lattice because `N=Theta(H)`. The AF-488 horizon law then reappears.

Likewise, if the number of samples stays bounded while their locations move to scale `H`, then `||s||_p=Theta(H)` for every `p`, and again the best possible order is `Theta(H^{-1})`.

Therefore the new resource is specifically **sample accumulation under a non-growing coupled noise budget**.

## Falsification and boundary audit

The theorem is deliberately local and channel-specific.

- `G` and the amplitude `a` are known. This isolates one moving generator exactly, as in the local minimax family used for the AF-487/AF-488 lower bounds. It is not a theorem for full unknown-depth Dirichlet inversion.
- The perturbations are real and multiplicative relative to the moving atom. The logarithmic upper bound uses positivity of `1+xi_k`; complex relative noise would require a branch-controlled formulation.
- `epsilon_0<1` is a genuine gate for the uniform logarithmic Lipschitz constant. The lower bound itself only needs sufficiently small local perturbations.
- The sample lattice has fixed positive spacing. The stated powers of `H` use the fact that sample count grows linearly with horizon.
- The result is deterministic minimax. Independent random noise can have a different law, while correlated stochastic noise can interpolate between pointwise and global-budget behavior.
- A discrete admissible set of generators or an external arithmetic mark can still convert quantitative resolution into eventual exact identification once its spacing dominates the minimax radius.
- The theorem concerns parameter recovery, not provenance. Recovering the real number `q` to high precision is still logically distinct from certifying that it is the intended rational prime generator rather than a matched control.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The ingredients are classical and the claim is not presented as a new general theorem of inverse problems.

- David L. Donoho, **“Statistical Estimation and Optimal Recovery,”** *Annals of Statistics* 22(1), 238–270 (1994), DOI `10.1214/aos/1176325367`, develops the modulus-of-continuity / optimal-recovery viewpoint in which indistinguishable nearby objects determine minimax difficulty.
- Amir Beck and Yonina C. Eldar, **“Regularization in Regression with Bounded Noise: A Chebyshev Center Approach,”** *SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications* 29(2), 606–625 (2007), DOI `10.1137/060656784`, studies worst-case estimation in regression with norm-bounded noise and the geometry of feasible parameter sets.
- Dmitry Batenkov and Yosef Yomdin, **“On the Accuracy of Solving Confluent Prony Systems,”** *SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics* 73(1), 134–154 (2013), DOI `10.1137/110836584`, studies maximal local accuracy and stability in Prony-type reconstruction and is the closest established inverse-problem setting to the surrounding AF-486/AF-487 Dirichlet sampling work.

The upper bound here is elementary `ell_p`/`ell_{p^*}` duality after a logarithmic change of variables; the lower bound is the standard two-point indistinguishability construction specialized to the source-relative Dirichlet channel. No broad novelty is claimed for those techniques. The Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is the exact comparison with AF-488: changing only the **geometry of admissible compression error** from independent pointwise bounds to a fixed global norm budget changes accumulated history from asymptotically useless to quantitatively useful, with the full interpolation exponent `1+1/p`.

## Relationship to AF-487 and AF-488

AF-487 established `Theta(1/t)` log-generator resolution for one critical terminal window. AF-488 then showed that arbitrarily dense earlier history cannot improve that order under pointwise source-relative noise, because one coherent perturbation can shadow the entire prefix.

AF-489 identifies the precise escape AF-488 left open. A fixed global `ell_p` budget prevents the adversary from spending the critical allowance independently at every retained sample. The resulting minimax radius is controlled by `||s||_p`; when the retained history grows with the horizon, finite `p` creates a strict accumulation gain. This turns **noise-budget geometry** into an explicit fidelity resource rather than treating noise magnitude alone as the relevant quantity.
