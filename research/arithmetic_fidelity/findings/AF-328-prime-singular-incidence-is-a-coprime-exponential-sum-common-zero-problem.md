# AF-328 — Prime singular incidence is a coprime exponential-sum common-zero problem

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `EXPONENTIAL-SUMS`, `FACTORIZATION`, `COMMON-ZEROS`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

The unresolved exact eight-prime singular incidence from AF-318--AF-327 can be written as a common-zero problem for two finite exponential sums, but **not** as a hidden common-factor problem.

Fix eight distinct rational primes `p_1,...,p_8` and set

\[
\lambda_j=-\log p_j.
\]

Define entire exponential sums

\[
F(s)=\sum_{j=1}^8 e^{\lambda_j s}
     =\sum_{j=1}^8 p_j^{-s},
\tag{1}
\]

and

\[
G(s)=\sum_{|I|=4} e^{(\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i)s}
     =\sum_{|I|=4}\left(\prod_{i\in I}p_i\right)^{-s}.
\tag{2}
\]

Then:

1. a nonzero common time `t` lies in the eight-point singular fiber
   \[
   s_1=0,\qquad e_4=0
   \tag{3}
   \]
   exactly when
   \[
   F(it)=G(it)=0;
   \tag{4}
   \]
2. `F` is irreducible in the classical ring `\mathcal E` of finite complex exponential sums;
3. `F` and `G` have no common nonunit factor in `\mathcal E`;
4. therefore Ritt factorization and the Shapiro common-zero/common-factor program cannot by themselves rule out a **single** exact prime singular hit. Shapiro's conjecture, if available for this pair, would upgrade coprimality only to finiteness of the common-zero set, not emptiness. Under Schanuel's conjecture, the D'Aquino--Macintyre--Terzo theorem supplies that finiteness; unconditionally the general Shapiro conjecture remains open.

Thus the AF-327 frontier is now located more sharply. The missing theorem is not another frequency-lattice or factorization obstruction. It must exclude an isolated point of intersection between the prime-log one-parameter subgroup and the codimension-two algebraic target `V(e_1,e_4)`, or else construct such an intersection.

No RH consequence is claimed. This is a closure result for a natural proof mechanism.

## Exact singular hits are common zeros of `F` and `G`

At a real time `t`, put

\[
z_j=p_j^{-it}=e^{\lambda_j it}\in S^1.
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
F(it)=\sum_{j=1}^8 z_j=s_1(z_1,\ldots,z_8),
\tag{6}
\]

while by definition of the fourth elementary symmetric polynomial,

\[
G(it)
=\sum_{|I|=4}\prod_{i\in I}z_i
=e_4(z_1,\ldots,z_8).
\tag{7}
\]

Hence (3) and (4) are equivalent. This reformulates the exact incidence without changing it: no averaging, limiting process, or additional observable has been introduced.

The point is that the two equations now sit in the classical common-zero theory of exponential sums, so its exact factorization boundary can be audited.

## The prime frequency sum `F` is irreducible in the full exponential-sum ring

Let `\mathcal E` denote the ring of finite sums

\[
\sum_{m=1}^r a_m e^{\mu_m s},
\qquad a_m,\mu_m\in\mathbb C,
\tag{8}
\]

with units `c e^{\mu s}`, `c\ne0`. Ritt's factorization theory, in the modern group-ring formulation of Everest--van der Poorten, includes the support-containment fact that a factor of an exponential sum can, after multiplication by a unit, be taken with frequencies in the rational vector space generated by the original support.

The eight real numbers

\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_8
\tag{9}
\]

are linearly independent over `\mathbb Q`: an integer relation exponentiates to a multiplicative relation among distinct rational primes, and unique factorization makes every coefficient zero.

Suppose `F` had a nontrivial factorization in `\mathcal E`. By support containment, after harmless unit shifts all frequencies in the two factors lie in

\[
\Gamma_{\mathbb Q}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_8\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Only finitely many rational coefficients occur, so choose `N>=1` clearing all their denominators and introduce formal Laurent variables

\[
Y_j=e^{\lambda_j s/N}.
\tag{11}
\]

Because the `\lambda_j` are `\mathbb Q`-independent, distinct Laurent monomials in the `Y_j` give distinct exponential frequencies. The exponential factorization would therefore induce, up to Laurent-monomial units, a nontrivial Laurent-polynomial factorization of

\[
P_N(Y)=Y_1^N+\cdots+Y_8^N.
\tag{12}
\]

But `P_N` is irreducible over `\mathbb C` for every `N`. For `N=1` it is linear. For `N>=2`, the projective Fermat hypersurface

\[
Y_1^N+\cdots+Y_8^N=0
\subset\mathbb P^7
\tag{13}
\]

is smooth, since all partial derivatives `N Y_j^{N-1}` cannot vanish at one projective point. A reducible projective hypersurface in `\mathbb P^7` would have two positive-dimensional hypersurface components meeting somewhere; at an intersection point the product equation would have zero gradient. Smoothness therefore excludes reducibility. Since `P_N` has no monomial factor, irreducibility in the polynomial ring also excludes a nontrivial Laurent factorization.

This contradiction proves

\[
\boxed{F\text{ is irreducible in }\mathcal E.}
\tag{14}
\]

The root-extraction freedom present in the full exponential-sum ring is important here: proving only that `X_1+\cdots+X_8` is irreducible in the integer prime-frequency Laurent ring would not suffice. Equation (12) checks every finite rational support refinement that a factor can require.

## `F` does not divide the fourth-elementary exponential sum

Since `F` is irreducible, a nonunit common factor of `F` and `G` would have to be associated to `F`; equivalently `F` would divide `G` in `\mathcal E`.

Assume for contradiction that

\[
G=F H
\tag{15}
\]

for some exponential sum `H`. Applying the same support-containment result to the factors of `G`, and clearing the finitely many rational denominators, reduces (15), up to Laurent-monomial units, to divisibility in a Laurent polynomial ring:

\[
P_N(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^8Y_j^N
\quad\text{divides}\quad
Q_N(Y)=e_4(Y_1^N,\ldots,Y_8^N)
\tag{16}
\]

for some positive integer `N`.

This is impossible. Choose nonzero complex `Y_j` with

\[
(Y_1^N,\ldots,Y_8^N)
=(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1).
\tag{17}
\]

Such roots exist for every `N`. Then

\[
P_N(Y)=4-4=0.
\tag{18}
\]

On the other hand, `e_4(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1)` is the coefficient of `u^4` in

\[
(1+u)^4(1-u)^4=(1-u^2)^4,
\]

so

\[
Q_N(Y)=\binom42=6\ne0.
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore `P_N` cannot divide `Q_N`, contradicting (16). Hence

\[
\boxed{\gcd_{\mathcal E}(F,G)\text{ is a unit}.}
\tag{20}
\]

This is stronger than merely observing that the formal symmetric polynomials `e_1` and `e_4` are coprime in `\mathbb C[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_8^{\pm1}]`: it survives the rational frequency refinements allowed by Ritt's exponential-sum factorization theory.

## Shapiro theory controls repeated common zeros, not isolated incidence

Shapiro's conjecture for exponential sums states that two exponential sums with infinitely many common zeros must have a common exponential-sum factor. Van der Poorten--Tijdeman proved the case in which one sum is simple. D'Aquino--Macintyre--Terzo proved Shapiro's conjecture assuming Schanuel's conjecture. Modern surveys still record the general unconditional conjecture as open.

The pair `(F,G)` is outside the elementary simple-support case: the prime frequencies span an eight-dimensional `\mathbb Q`-space, and (14) puts `F` in the irreducible, non-simple sector. Combining (20) with Shapiro would therefore imply only

\[
\#\{s\in\mathbb C:F(s)=G(s)=0\}<\infty.
\tag{21}
\]

Under Schanuel's conjecture this finiteness follows from D'Aquino--Macintyre--Terzo. It still permits one, two, or any finite number of common zeros. That distinction is essential here because the Arithmetic Fidelity question asks whether **one particular kind** of common zero exists on the imaginary axis.

This is not a technical loophole in the literature. The exponential-sum common-zero literature contains explicit constructions of pairs with exactly a prescribed finite number of common zeros. Coprimality is therefore intrinsically an infinite-recurrence obstruction, not a zero-incidence obstruction.

Consequently, even a future unconditional proof of Shapiro's conjecture would not settle (4). It would close only the possibility that the same fixed eight-prime support produces infinitely many complex common zeros.

## Relation to AF-308--AF-327

AF-308 and AF-309 show that exact zero incidence is a measure-zero but dense and everywhere frequency-unstable phenomenon. AF-310 and AF-311 then show that integer-character and Baker algebraic-linear relation ranks do not exclude the first compatible target geometries. AF-318 identifies the eight-point singular fiber as still compatible with rank-one character data, while AF-320--AF-327 establish increasingly strong recovery and quantitative conditioning away from its exact boundary.

The present result closes a different escape route. Passing from phase geometry to the entire functions `(F,G)` does expose a classical algebraic structure, but that structure is already maximally favorable in the obvious sense: the two functions are coprime in the full exponential-sum ring. There is no missing common factor whose discovery could explain the singular target, and proving the absence of such a factor does not exclude isolated common zeros.

The surviving exact problem can therefore be stated cleanly as

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the one-parameter prime-log subgroup }
 s\mapsto(p_1^{-s},\ldots,p_8^{-s})
\text{ meet }V(e_1,e_4)\text{ at some }s=it\ne0?
}
\tag{22}
\]

A decisive next mechanism must be **pointwise** rather than factor-theoretic: for example, a transcendence/incidence theorem specialized to one intersection, an arithmetic restriction on the imaginary-axis location, or a new exact identity forcing a forbidden multiplicative relation. Generic torus density, common-factor exclusion, and finiteness of repeated zeros do not cross that boundary.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The factorization ingredients are classical. Ritt initiated factorization of exponential sums in 1927; Everest and van der Poorten gave a modern group-ring treatment and support-control formulation. The common-zero/common-factor question is Shapiro's classical conjecture; van der Poorten and Tijdeman treated the simple case, while D'Aquino, Macintyre, and Terzo proved the conjecture conditionally on Schanuel. Heittokangas et al. survey the current factorization/common-zero landscape and explicitly retain the general conjecture as unresolved.

The irreducibility and coprimality calculations for the specific prime pair `(F,G)` above are derived here from those classical tools. No novelty is claimed for Ritt factorization, Shapiro's conjecture, Fermat-hypersurface irreducibility, or the general common-zero theory. The Mathia-specific contribution is the exact identification of the AF-327 singular frontier with a **coprime** exponential-sum pair and the resulting no-go statement: common-factor theory, even if fully resolved, cannot decide the required one-point prime incidence.

## Audit boundaries

- Coprimality in `\mathcal E` does **not** imply that `F` and `G` have no common zero.
- Conditional finiteness under Schanuel does **not** imply unconditional finiteness, and neither form of finiteness implies absence of an imaginary-axis common zero.
- The argument is for a **fixed set of eight distinct primes**. It does not give a uniform bound on the number or height of common zeros as the support varies.
- The smooth Fermat-hypersurface argument is used only to prove irreducibility of the formal pullbacks `\sum Y_j^N`; it does not assert that arbitrary points of those hypersurfaces lie on the one-parameter prime phase curve.
- Equation (22) remains open. This finding closes common-factor/factorization as a sufficient mechanism; it does not solve exact incidence.
