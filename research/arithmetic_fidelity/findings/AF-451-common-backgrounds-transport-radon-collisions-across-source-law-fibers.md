# AF-451 — Common backgrounds transport Radon collisions across source-law fibers

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `DECISIVE-CORRECTION`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `SOURCE-LAW`, `RADON-COMPLETION`, `SPARSE-UNIQUENESS`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

The fiberwise criterion previously stored as AF-450 is false and is withdrawn. Its proof cancelled common mass from two colliding positive sources and then incorrectly treated the remaining Jordan sides as if they still had the original total mass and prescribed source-law barycenter.

The correct exact object is a **Radon core completed by a common positive background**.

Let

\[
\Psi:X\to\mathbb R^q,
\qquad
\Phi:X\to\mathbb R^m,
\qquad
F=(\Psi,\Phi):X\to\mathbb R^{q+m}.
\]

For fixed total mass `M>0`, atom budget `r`, and source-law value `u`, consider positive atomic measures with at most `r` atoms satisfying

\[
\mu(X)=M,
\qquad
\int\Psi\,d\mu=M u,
\]

and retain only

\[
\mathcal M_\Phi(\mu)=\int\Phi\,d\mu.
\]

Normalize by `M` and work with probabilities. Define the **order-`r` completed Radon collision set** `\mathcal C_r(F)` as the set of points `p\in\mathbb R^{q+m}` for which there exist finitely supported probability measures `\alpha,\beta,\rho` and `\tau\in(0,1]` such that

\[
\alpha\ne\beta,
\qquad
\alpha\perp\beta,
\qquad
q:=\int F\,d\alpha=\int F\,d\beta,
\tag{1}
\]

and, with `w=\int F\,d\rho` when `\tau<1`,

\[
p=\tau q+(1-\tau)w,
\tag{2}
\]

while both completed measures

\[
\tau\alpha+(1-\tau)\rho,
\qquad
\tau\beta+(1-\tau)\rho
\tag{3}
\]

have at most `r` support points. For `\tau=1` the background term is absent.

Then the retained observation is injective on the fixed source-law class exactly when

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_r(F)\cap\bigl(\{u\}\times\mathbb R^m\bigr)=\varnothing.
}
\tag{4}
\]

If the Radon core `q` has source coordinate `v` and the background `w` has source coordinate `w_\Psi`, then the contaminated source-law value is

\[
\boxed{u=\tau v+(1-\tau)w_\Psi.}
\tag{5}
\]

So a residual circuit at `v` is not confined to the `v` fiber. A shared background can transport it into another fiber while consuming part of the original atom budget.

## Exact derivation

Normalize to `M=1`. Suppose distinct admissible probability measures `\mu` and `\nu` have the same observation and the same prescribed source law. Their joint barycenters agree:

\[
\int F\,d\mu
=
\int F\,d\nu
=:
p=(u,y).
\tag{6}
\]

Let `\gamma=\mu\wedge\nu` be their maximal common atomic submeasure. Put

\[
c=\gamma(X),
\qquad
\tau=1-c>0,
\]

and define

\[
\alpha=(\mu-\gamma)/\tau,
\qquad
\beta=(\nu-\gamma)/\tau.
\tag{7}
\]

Then `\alpha` and `\beta` are mutually singular probabilities. Subtracting the same `\gamma` from `(6)` yields

\[
\int F\,d\alpha
=
\int F\,d\beta
=:
q.
\tag{8}
\]

If `c>0`, set `\rho=\gamma/c` and `w=\int F\,d\rho`. Then

\[
\mu=\tau\alpha+c\rho,
\qquad
\nu=\tau\beta+c\rho,
\]

hence

\[
p=\tau q+(1-\tau)w.
\tag{9}
\]

The completed support bounds are exactly the original support bounds on `\mu` and `\nu`. Therefore every fixed-fiber collision gives a point of `\mathcal C_r(F)` on the slice `\{u\}\times\mathbb R^m`.

Conversely, witnesses for a point `p=(u,y)` in that intersection give the two measures in `(3)`. They are distinct, have at most `r` atoms, and have the same joint barycenter `p`; hence they have source law `u` and the same retained observation. Multiplying by `M` restores arbitrary fixed mass. This proves `(4)`.

The key point is that `\alpha,\beta` are the disjoint **Radon core** exposed by maximal cancellation, while `\rho` is the common background that places that core in the actual prescribed fiber.

## Why the withdrawn criterion fails

For `\sigma=\mu-\nu`, maximal cancellation gives

\[
\sigma_+=\tau\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma_-=\tau\beta.
\]

Their common mass is `\tau`, not `1`. If `q=(v,z)` and `w=(w_\Psi,w_\Phi)`, equation `(9)` gives `(5)`. There is no reason for `v=u`.

Thus the barycenter of the Jordan sides labels the residual Radon core, not every source-law fiber in which that core can be embedded. The simpler raw-label criterion is valid only under an extra condition, for example when there is no common background (`\tau=1`) or when the common background itself has source barycenter `u`.

## Decisive finite counterexample

Take four latent points `c,a,b,d` with distinct joint features

\[
F(c)=(0,0),
\qquad
F(a)=(1,0),
\qquad
F(b)=(1,1),
\qquad
F(d)=(1,2),
\]

where the first coordinate is `\Psi` and the second is `\Phi`. The augmented columns `(1,F(x))` have rank three and one-dimensional kernel generated by

\[
\delta_a-2\delta_b+\delta_d.
\tag{10}
\]

Every nonzero affine kernel relation therefore has residual source barycenter `v=1`; no residual relation has source barycenter `1/2`.

For `M=1` and `r=3`, however, define

\[
\mu=0.5\,\delta_c+0.25\,\delta_a+0.25\,\delta_d,
\]

\[
\nu=0.5\,\delta_c+0.5\,\delta_b.
\tag{11}
\]

Both have `\Psi` mean `1/2` and `\Phi` mean `1/2`, so the `u=1/2` fiber is not injective. Cancelling the common background `0.5\,\delta_c` leaves

\[
0.5\,\delta_a+0.5\,\delta_d
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\delta_b,
\]

whose source coordinate is `v=1`. The prescribed value is transported by

\[
1/2=(1/2)\cdot1+(1/2)\cdot0.
\]

This is a direct counterexample to AF-450. It is not a duplicate-feature degeneracy: all four joint feature points are distinct. It also shows that background completion consumes sparsity budget: the residual supports have sizes two and one, whereas the completed supports have sizes three and two.

## Correct localization picture

A bad Radon core need not contaminate every fiber, but it can contaminate more than its own source-law slice. The contaminated region is generated by sparse joins

\[
p=\tau q+(1-\tau)w
\]

between Radon cores `q` and feasible common-background barycenters `w`, with the completed support limits enforced.

The same example shows the boundary. All source coordinates are nonnegative and `c` is the only point with `\Psi=0`, so the `u=0` fiber contains only `\delta_c` and is faithful. The core at `v=1` can reach positive source-law values by mixing with `c`, but cannot create a collision at `u=0`.

Two special cases recover raw circuit localization: mutually singular colliders have `\tau=1`, and a common background already centered at `u` forces the residual source coordinate to be `u` by `(5)`.

## Uniform fidelity still has the global affine criterion

The correction is fiberwise and does not invalidate AF-449. Uniform fidelity over all source-law values is exactly injectivity of the joint map

\[
\mu\longmapsto
\left(\int\Psi\,d\mu,\int\Phi\,d\mu\right)
\tag{12}
\]

on all positive fixed-mass sources with at most `r` atoms. This remains the ordinary sparse convex-representation problem governed by AF-449's global affine balanced-kernel index.

Every fixed-fiber collision is a global joint collision, and every global joint collision belongs to the fiber determined by its common `\Psi` barycenter. Completion changes **which individual fibers** inherit a collision, not whether some fiber does.

## Consequence for the quantitative frontier

A stable margin for one prescribed source law cannot be based on distance from `u` to raw circuit labels: `(5)` can move an exact collision into the fiber even when no residual circuit has label `u`.

The coordinate-invariant quantitative object must instead live on the actual restricted source class with a physical source metric and observation norm. In the language of AF-044 and AF-045, the natural target is the lower Lipschitz/secant modulus of `\mathcal M_\Phi` restricted to the prescribed fiber. The completed collision set `\mathcal C_r(F)` is its exact zero-margin locus.

So the conditioning frontier starts one step earlier than AF-450 claimed: first classify completed fixed-fiber secants correctly, then quantify their distance from zero.

A targeted literature search also checked modern oriented-matroid and sparse-positive-recovery formulations. The cancellation/completion identity is elementary convex geometry rather than a claimed new theorem. Its durable Arithmetic Fidelity role is the correction it imposes on the source-law picture: **a residual Radon circuit is a movable core, and a common background determines which prescribed fibers inherit it**.

## Boundaries and falsification checks

- Fixed positive total mass is essential to the probability normalization. Without it, the natural geometry is conic rather than affine.
- The theorem uses affine source constraints `\int\Psi\,d\mu=Mu`; nonlinear source laws need not admit `(5)`.
- `\mathcal C_r(F)` records exact collisions only. Its absence does not imply a positive conditioning margin; near-completions can still make recovery arbitrarily ill-conditioned.
- A residual circuit is not fatal to every `u` satisfying the formal segment equation `(5)`: the background must be realizable by a positive atomic measure and both completed sources must obey the support budget.
- Testing only residual circuits whose source barycenter equals `u` is insufficient unless an extra hypothesis rules out source-shifting common backgrounds.
- The global augmented affine criterion remains exact for uniform fidelity over all source-law values.

## Bottom line

For fixed-mass sparse positive sources, an exact collision consists of a disjoint Radon core plus a shared positive background. Cancelling the background exposes the core but generally changes the source-law barycenter. Therefore fixed source laws localize **completed Radon joins**, not raw circuits by their own barycentric label.

Any later arithmetic or quantitative use of source-law conditioning must work with completed fixed-fiber secants rather than the withdrawn AF-450 criterion.
