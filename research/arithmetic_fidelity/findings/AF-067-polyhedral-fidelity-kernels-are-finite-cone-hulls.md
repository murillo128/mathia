# AF-067 — Polyhedral fidelity kernels are finite cone hulls

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `STRUCTURAL-CLASSIFICATION`, `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM`, `PRIOR-ART-BOUNDARY`

## Claim

Let `(V,\|\cdot\|)` be a finite-dimensional real normed space whose closed unit ball `B` is a polytope, and let `B_*` be its dual unit ball. Let `S\subset V` be nonempty and compact. For a vertex `v\in\operatorname{Vert}(B)`, put

\[
F_v
=
\{\varphi\in B_*:\varphi(v)=1\}
=
J(v),
\tag{1}
\]

so `F_v` is the dual facet polar to `v`, and define the closed convex cone

\[
K_v
=
\{w\in V:\varphi(w)\ge0\ \text{for every }\varphi\in F_v\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Define the **polyhedral fidelity hull** generated by these facet cones by

\[
H_B(S)
=
\bigcap_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}(B)}(S-K_v),
\qquad
S-K_v=\{s-k:s\in S,\ k\in K_v\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then:

1. **AF-066's continuum of directional coherence tests reduces exactly to finitely many dual facets.** If `\mathcal C_S` is the dual-face coherence kernel of AF-066, then
   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal C_S=H_B(S).}
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Equivalently,
   \[
   m\in H_B(S)
   \iff
   \forall v\in\operatorname{Vert}(B)\ \exists s_v\in S
   \text{ with }s_v-m\in K_v.
   \tag{5}
   \]
   Every other norming face `J(u)` is contained in one of the facet faces `F_v`, so a witness for the containing facet automatically witnesses the smaller face.

2. **The powered safe-lift region is therefore a finite cone hull independent of the exponent.** For every finite `p>1`, with
   \[
   \Delta_{p,S}(m)
   =
   \sup_{x\in V}
   \bigl(d(x,S)^p-\|x-m\|^p\bigr),
   \tag{6}
   \]
   AF-066 gives
   \[
   \boxed{
   \{m\in V:\Delta_{p,S}(m)<\infty\}
   =H_B(S)
   \qquad(p>1).
   }
   \tag{7}
   \]
   Thus in the polyhedral category the entire infinite family of far-field powered-fidelity questions is decided by finitely many one-sided cone-incidence tests.

3. **`H_B` is a genuine closure/hull operator.** On arbitrary subsets of `V` (with `H_B(\varnothing)=\varnothing`) it is extensive, monotone, and idempotent:
   \[
   S\subseteq H_B(S),
   \qquad
   S\subseteq T\Rightarrow H_B(S)\subseteq H_B(T),
   \qquad
   H_B(H_B(S))=H_B(S).
   \tag{8}
   \]
   For compact `S`, AF-066 further gives
   \[
   \boxed{S\subseteq H_B(S)\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(S),}
   \tag{9}
   \]
   so the fidelity hull is an intermediate closure between the actual target and ordinary convexification.

4. **Membership has a finite witness certificate.** If
   \[
   r=|\operatorname{Vert}(B)|,
   \tag{10}
   \]
   then every `m\in H_B(S)` has a subset `S_0\subseteq S` with
   \[
   |S_0|\le r,
   \qquad
   m\in H_B(S_0).
   \tag{11}
   \]
   One target point per primal vertex/facet suffices. This is a certificate bound coming from the finite orientation family, not from ordinary Carathéodory dimension.

5. **For `\ell^\infty_d`, the fidelity hull is exactly the classical empty-orthant rectilinear hull.** Here
   \[
   B=[-1,1]^d,
   \qquad
   \operatorname{Vert}(B)=\{\sigma:\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^d\},
   \tag{12}
   \]
   and
   \[
   F_\sigma
   =
   \operatorname{conv}\{\sigma_i e_i^*:1\le i\le d\}.
   \tag{13}
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   \boxed{
   K_\sigma
   =
   \{w\in\mathbb R^d:\sigma_iw_i\ge0\ \forall i\},
   }
   \tag{14}
   \]
   the closed coordinate orthant of sign `\sigma`. Therefore
   \[
   \boxed{
   m\in H_{\infty}(S)
   \iff
   \text{every closed coordinate orthant with apex }m\text{ meets }S.
   }
   \tag{15}
   \]
   For compact `S`, this equals the complement of the union of all open coordinate orthants disjoint from `S`, the empty-orthant formulation of the rectilinear convex hull used in the classical and modern computational-geometry literature.

6. **The `2^d` witness bound is sharp for `\ell^\infty_d`.** Take
   \[
   S=\{\pm1\}^d,
   \qquad m=0.
   \tag{16}
   \]
   Every closed sign orthant at `0` contains exactly the one cube vertex with that sign, so `0\in H_\infty(S)`. Removing any one of the `2^d` vertices empties its corresponding orthant and removes `0` from the hull. Thus no smaller universal witness bound is possible for this norm.

The reusable Arithmetic Fidelity conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{polyhedral first-order fidelity is not merely a facewise asymptotic condition;}\\
\text{it is an exact finite closure system generated by the dual facets;}\\
\ell^\infty\text{ specializes that closure to the classical empty-orthant hull.}
\end{array}
}
\tag{17}
\]

This turns AF-066's dual-face coherence criterion into a finite geometric object with exact closure laws and finite certificates. It also places a substantial part of the mechanism inside mature restricted-orientation and rectilinear convexity rather than treating the hull structure itself as new mathematics.

## Derivation

### Facets suffice for every norming face

For each unit `u`,

\[
J(u)
=
\{\varphi\in B_*:\varphi(u)=1\}
\tag{18}
\]

is a nonempty proper exposed face of the polytope `B_*`. Every proper face of a polytope is contained in a facet. By polarity between the full-dimensional centrally symmetric polytopes `B` and `B_*`, each facet of `B_*` is `F_v=J(v)` for a unique vertex `v` of `B`.

Suppose first that `m\in H_B(S)`. Fix an arbitrary unit `u` and choose a facet `F_v` containing `J(u)`. By (3), there is `s_v\in S` with

\[
s_v-m\in K_v,
\tag{19}
\]

which means

\[
\varphi(s_v-m)\ge0
\qquad\forall\varphi\in F_v.
\tag{20}
\]

Since `J(u)\subseteq F_v`, the same point is a simultaneous witness for every `\varphi\in J(u)`. This is exactly AF-066's directional coherence condition. Hence

\[
H_B(S)\subseteq\mathcal C_S.
\tag{21}
\]

Conversely, every vertex `v` is itself a unit direction and `J(v)=F_v`. Thus `m\in\mathcal C_S` supplies an `s_v\in S` satisfying (20) for every primal vertex, so

\[
\mathcal C_S\subseteq H_B(S).
\tag{22}
\]

This proves (4). Equation (7) is then exactly AF-066's polyhedral powered-fidelity theorem written in finite facet form.

### The finite cone system is idempotent

Extensivity is immediate because `0\in K_v` for every `v`: choosing `s=m` shows every `m\in S` belongs to every `S-K_v`.

Monotonicity follows from

\[
S\subseteq T
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S-K_v\subseteq T-K_v
\tag{23}
\]

for every vertex `v`.

For idempotence, let

\[
m\in H_B(H_B(S)).
\tag{24}
\]

For each vertex `v`, choose `x_v\in H_B(S)` with

\[
x_v-m\in K_v.
\tag{25}
\]

Because `x_v\in H_B(S)`, applying the same `v`-constraint once more gives an `s_v\in S` with

\[
s_v-x_v\in K_v.
\tag{26}
\]

Each `K_v` is a convex cone and is closed under addition, so

\[
s_v-m
=(s_v-x_v)+(x_v-m)
\in K_v.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus `m\in S-K_v` for every `v`, hence `m\in H_B(S)`. Together with extensivity applied to `H_B(S)`, this proves idempotence.

For compact `S`, each `S-K_v` is closed: if `s_n-k_n\to x`, compactness permits `s_n\to s` along a subsequence, so `k_n\to s-x\in K_v`. Equation (9) then makes `H_B(S)` compact as well.

### One witness per facet gives the finite certificate

If `m\in H_B(S)`, choose for each `v\in\operatorname{Vert}(B)` one point `s_v\in S` satisfying

\[
s_v-m\in K_v.
\tag{28}
\]

Let

\[
S_0=\{s_v:v\in\operatorname{Vert}(B)\}.
\tag{29}
\]

Then `|S_0|\le r` and every cone constraint still has its chosen witness inside `S_0`, proving (11). Repetitions can only lower the certificate size.

This bound has different semantics from the `d+1` bound of ordinary convexity. It counts the finite family of destination-sensitive orientation constraints that must retain a real target witness, not the affine dimension needed to represent a convex mixture.

## Exact controls

### `\ell^\infty_d`: orthants and the empty-orthant hull

The dual of `\ell^\infty_d` is `\ell^1_d`. At a cube vertex `\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^d`, equality in the dual pairing forces the signs to agree with `\sigma`, so

\[
F_\sigma
=
\left\{\varphi:\|\varphi\|_1=1,\ \varphi(\sigma)=1\right\}
=
\operatorname{conv}\{\sigma_i e_i^*:1\le i\le d\}.
\tag{30}
\]

Requiring every element of this facet to be nonnegative on `w` is equivalent to requiring every generating signed coordinate functional to be nonnegative, giving (14).

Define the empty-orthant hull by

\[
\operatorname{EOH}(S)
=
\mathbb R^d
\setminus
\bigcup\{a+\operatorname{int}K_\sigma:
(a+\operatorname{int}K_\sigma)\cap S=\varnothing\}.
\tag{31}
\]

For compact `S`,

\[
\boxed{H_\infty(S)=\operatorname{EOH}(S).}
\tag{32}
\]

Indeed, if an `S`-free open orthant `a+\operatorname{int}K_\sigma` contains `m`, then `m+K_\sigma` lies inside that same open orthant, so the closed `\sigma`-orthant at `m` is `S`-free and (15) fails.

Conversely, if `(m+K_\sigma)\cap S=\varnothing`, compactness of `S` and closedness of `m+K_\sigma` give positive separation. Move the apex a sufficiently small distance from `m` in the `-\sigma` direction. The resulting open orthant contains `m`, remains within an arbitrarily small neighborhood of `m+K_\sigma`, and is still disjoint from `S`. Hence `m` belongs to the union removed in (31).

Ottmann--Soisalon-Soininen--Wood explicitly documented that several inequivalent notions have been called a rectilinear convex hull. Equation (31) therefore fixes the intended variant rather than relying on ambiguous terminology. It is the same empty-open-quadrant/octant formulation used in modern rectilinear-hull work.

The sharpness example (16) is then immediate: the `2^d` sign orthants at `0` require `2^d` distinct cube vertices.

### `\ell^1_d`: a different finite cone geometry

For comparison, in `\ell^1_d` the primal vertices are `\pm e_i` and the dual ball is the cube. At `e_i`,

\[
F_{+i}
=
\{\varphi:\varphi_i=1,\ |\varphi_j|\le1\ (j\ne i)\}.
\tag{33}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{+i}
&=
\left\{w:w_i\ge\sum_{j\ne i}|w_j|\right\},\\
K_{-i}
&=-K_{+i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]

The same powered-fidelity region is now decided by only `2d` pointed dominance cones:

\[
H_{\ell^1}(S)
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^d
\bigl[(S-K_{+i})\cap(S-K_{-i})\bigr].
\tag{35}
\]

Thus the finite closure is controlled by the face combinatorics and geometry of the chosen norm representation, not merely by ambient dimension and not merely by ordinary convex hull membership.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The geometric ingredients and the existence of finite-orientation convexity theories are classical.

- R. Tyrrell Rockafellar, ***Convex Analysis***, Princeton University Press (1970), together with the polyhedral polarity sources already audited in AF-066. Role: standard face/facet and supporting-functional machinery underlying the passage from norming faces to facets.
- Thomas Ottmann, Eljas Soisalon-Soininen, and Derick Wood, **“On the definition and computation of rectilinear convex hulls,”** *Information Sciences* 33(3), 157–171 (1984), DOI `10.1016/0020-0255(84)90025-2`. Role: direct classical prior art for point-set rectilinear hulls and, importantly, for the fact that several inequivalent rectilinear-hull definitions coexist.
- Gregory J. E. Rawlins and Derick Wood, **“Optimal computation of finitely oriented convex hulls,”** *Information and Computation* 72(2), 150–166 (1987), DOI `10.1016/0890-5401(87)90045-9`. Role: finite-orientation hull prior art showing that orthogonal hulls sit inside a broader established family of orientation-restricted hull constructions.
- Eugene Fink and Derick Wood, **“Fundamentals of Restricted-Orientation Convexity,”** *Information Sciences* 92(1–4), 175–196 (1996). Role: mature general framework for `O`-convexity in two and higher dimensions; in particular, restricted-orientation convexity is already an established generalization of ordinary and orthogonal convexity and is closed under intersections.
- Pablo Pérez-Lantero, Carlos Seara, and Jorge Urrutia, **“Rectilinear convex hull of points in 3D and applications,”** *Journal of Global Optimization* 90, 551–571 (2024), DOI `10.1007/s10898-024-01402-3`. Role: modern open-access use of the complement-of-empty-open-octants definition in three dimensions and discussion of its finite-orientation extensions.

No novelty is claimed for polytope polarity, closure operators, rectilinear/orthogonal convexity, restricted-orientation convexity, or finitely oriented hulls. In particular, the `\ell^\infty` specialization (31)–(32) is a classical hull once its exact variant is fixed.

A targeted literature audit also makes it unsafe to present the general finite cone construction (3) as a new theory of convexity merely because the exact notation was not found. It lies in a mature neighborhood of restricted-orientation and generalized convexity. The durable Arithmetic Fidelity contribution is narrower: AF-066's analytic safe-lift finiteness criterion is **exactly** this finite facet-generated closure, so geometric hull membership becomes a complete falsification test for every finite powered lift in the declared polyhedral norm category. The closure law and finite witness certificate are then exact consequences useful for auditing compression, not evidence of a new foundational convexity theory.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- The identification with powered safe-lift finiteness inherits AF-066's finite-dimensionality and compact-target hypotheses. The abstract closure law (8) itself needs neither compactness nor finite-dimensionality once a fixed finite cone family has been declared.
- Facet reduction uses polyhedrality essentially. In a nonpolyhedral norm there can be infinitely many genuinely different supporting faces/directions, and AF-065 shows that higher-order contact can remain decisive after first-order gaps vanish.
- The hull depends on the chosen norm representation. Renorming can change the primal vertices, dual facets, cones `K_v`, witness count, and therefore the fidelity hull even when the underlying point set is unchanged.
- `H_B(S)` is not asserted to coincide with the `O`-convex hull or any one named restricted-orientation hull for every polyhedral norm. The exact classical identification proved here is the `\ell^\infty` empty-orthant case; the broader literature is a prior-art boundary and comparison class.
- The finite certificate bound `|\operatorname{Vert}(B)|` is universal for this cone representation but need not be minimal for a particular norm or target. Redundant cone constraints or one point satisfying several facets can reduce it.
- Compactness is used in the converse direction of the empty-open-orthant characterization (32). For arbitrary nonclosed target sets, closure/boundary conventions can distinguish closed-apex cone incidence from complement-of-open-empty-cone definitions.
- Nothing here singles out rational primes or implies RH. The result is an abstract theorem about what one polyhedral compression/fidelity mechanism retains.

## Consequence for the Arithmetic Fidelity frontier

AF-065 and AF-066 separated two ways provenance can survive a far-field compression. Smooth curved norms can erase one-point provenance at first order and retain only a higher-order contact profile. Nonsmooth polyhedral norms can retain provenance immediately because an entire active dual face must be witnessed by one actual target point.

The present result sharpens the second branch: **for a polyhedral norm, that provenance is not hidden in a continuum of directions at all. It is encoded by a finite closure system indexed by the primal vertices / dual facets.** This gives an exact finite audit surface before any downstream power is considered.

It also establishes a useful prior-art discipline for the line. Whenever a proposed fidelity mechanism reduces to finite directional cones, orthants, or orientation-restricted hulls, the first question should be whether the construction is already an instance of rectilinear, finitely oriented, or restricted-orientation convexity. The remaining research value is then not renaming the hull, but determining what mathematically forced compression makes membership in that hull the exact survival criterion and whether the same finite-certificate principle persists in less classical destination categories.
