# AF-452 — Difference-norm conditioning descends exactly to completed source-law secants

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE`, `ARITHMETIC-FIDELITY`, `SOURCE-LAW`, `QUANTITATIVE-FIDELITY`, `COMPLETED-SECANT`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`

## Claim

AF-451 identifies the correct zero-error object for one prescribed affine source-law fiber: a residual Radon core may be moved into that fiber by a common positive background. The same completion mechanism has an exact quantitative form whenever the physical source metric is induced by a homogeneous norm on signed measure differences.

Let

\[
\Psi:X\to\mathbb R^q,
\qquad
\Phi:X\to\mathbb R^m,
\]

fix an atom budget `r>=1`, and work with probability measures; a fixed positive total mass `M` only rescales both numerator and denominator and does not change the ratios below. For a source-law value `u`, write

\[
\mathcal P_{r,u}^{\Psi}
=
\left\{
\mu:\mu\text{ is a finitely supported probability measure},\
|\operatorname{supp}\mu|\le r,\
\int\Psi\,d\mu=u
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `||.||_S` be a norm on the linear span of zero-mass signed atomic differences generated by this source class, and let `||.||_Y` be a norm on `R^m`. Define

\[
d_S(\mu,\nu)=\|\mu-\nu\|_S
\]

and the fixed-fiber lower Lipschitz modulus

\[
\kappa_{r,u}(\Phi;S,Y)
=
\inf_{\substack{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_{r,u}^{\Psi}\\\mu\ne\nu}}
\frac{
\left\|\int\Phi\,d(\mu-\nu)\right\|_Y
}{
\|\mu-\nu\|_S
}.
\tag{2}
\]

Call mutually singular finitely supported probability measures `alpha,beta` an **`(r,u)`-completable source-law secant core** when

\[
\int\Psi\,d\alpha
=
\int\Psi\,d\beta
=:v,
\tag{3}
\]

and there exist a probability measure `rho` and `tau in (0,1]` such that

\[
\mu=\tau\alpha+(1-\tau)\rho,
\qquad
\nu=\tau\beta+(1-\tau)\rho
\tag{4}
\]

both belong to `\mathcal P_{r,u}^{\Psi}`. For `tau=1` the background is absent. Equivalently, if

\[
w_\Psi=\int\Psi\,d\rho,
\]

then completion requires

\[
u=\tau v+(1-\tau)w_\Psi
\tag{5}
\]

plus the actual support-budget conditions on the two measures in `(4)`.

Let `\mathfrak C_{r,u}^{\Psi}` be the normalized carrier of all such cores:

\[
\mathfrak C_{r,u}^{\Psi}
=
\left\{
\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\|\alpha-\beta\|_S}:
(\alpha,\beta)\text{ is an }(r,u)\text{-completable source-law secant core}
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then:

1. **The completed-core carrier is exactly the normalized secant carrier of the prescribed source-law fiber.**
   \[
   \boxed{
   \left\{
   \frac{\mu-\nu}{\|\mu-\nu\|_S}:
   \mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_{r,u}^{\Psi},\ \mu\ne\nu
   \right\}
   =
   \mathfrak C_{r,u}^{\Psi}.
   }
   \tag{7}
   \]

2. **The fixed-fiber conditioning modulus is therefore the restricted minimum gain on completed source-law cores.**
   \[
   \boxed{
   \kappa_{r,u}(\Phi;S,Y)
   =
   \inf_{\eta\in\mathfrak C_{r,u}^{\Psi}}
   \left\|\int\Phi\,d\eta\right\|_Y.
   }
   \tag{8}
   \]

3. **A common background changes reachability but not the normalized condition ratio.** For every completion `(4)`,
   \[
   \boxed{
   \frac{
   \left\|\int\Phi\,d(\mu-\nu)\right\|_Y
   }{
   \|\mu-\nu\|_S
   }
   =
   \frac{
   \left\|\int\Phi\,d(\alpha-\beta)\right\|_Y
   }{
   \|\alpha-\beta\|_S
   }.
   }
   \tag{9}
   \]
   The mixing weight `tau` and the common background `rho` cancel exactly from the ratio.

4. **AF-451 is the zero level of this quantitative carrier.** Exact collision in the `u` fiber occurs exactly when an `(r,u)`-completable core satisfies
   \[
   \int\Phi\,d(\alpha-\beta)=0.
   \tag{10}
   \]
   Stable conditioning is stronger: even with no exact core collision,
   \[
   \kappa_{r,u}=0
   \]
   whenever there is a sequence of completable cores whose normalized observation gain tends to zero.

5. **Background completion cannot repair a bad normalized margin in any difference-norm metric.** If a family of source-law cores has condition ratios tending to zero and each core can be completed into the prescribed fiber, then that fiber has zero lower Lipschitz modulus regardless of how small the completion weights are. The background may move the core to a new source-law value and consume sparsity budget, but it cannot improve its normalized gain.

The reusable Arithmetic Fidelity conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
\text{for difference-homogeneous source metrics, fixed-fiber quantitative fidelity is completely controlled by the normalized carrier of source-law secant cores that can actually be completed into that fiber.}
}
\]

This is the quantitative correction suggested by AF-451. Raw circuit labels are insufficient, but once completion feasibility is imposed, the common background disappears from the normalized conditioning ratio.

## Exact derivation

### Every fixed-fiber secant reduces to a completable disjoint core

Take distinct

\[
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_{r,u}^{\Psi}.
\]

Let

\[
\gamma=\mu\wedge\nu
\]

be their maximal common atomic submeasure, put

\[
c=\gamma(X),
\qquad
\tau=1-c>0,
\]

and define

\[
\alpha=\frac{\mu-\gamma}{\tau},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{\nu-\gamma}{\tau}.
\tag{11}
\]

Then `alpha,beta` are mutually singular probability measures. If `c>0`, let

\[
\rho=\gamma/c.
\]

The source-law equality gives

\[
0
=
\int\Psi\,d(\mu-\nu)
=
\tau\int\Psi\,d(\alpha-\beta),
\]

so `(3)` holds. The original measures themselves provide a valid completion `(4)` and already obey the support budget. Thus every fixed-fiber pair produces an `(r,u)`-completable core.

Moreover,

\[
\mu-\nu
=
\tau(\alpha-\beta).
\tag{12}
\]

Absolute homogeneity of the source norm gives

\[
\|\mu-\nu\|_S
=
\tau\|\alpha-\beta\|_S,
\tag{13}
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{\mu-\nu}{\|\mu-\nu\|_S}
=
\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\|\alpha-\beta\|_S}.
\tag{14}
\]

So every normalized fiber secant belongs to the completed-core carrier.

### Every completable core is an actual fixed-fiber secant direction

Conversely, take any `(r,u)`-completable core `alpha,beta` with witnesses `rho,tau`. The measures in `(4)` are distinct elements of the declared fiber and

\[
\mu-\nu=\tau(\alpha-\beta).
\]

Equations `(13)`--`(14)` hold again. Hence every normalized completed-core direction is realized by an actual fixed-fiber secant. This proves the set equality `(7)`.

Linearity of the observation map now gives

\[
\int\Phi\,d(\mu-\nu)
=
\tau\int\Phi\,d(\alpha-\beta).
\tag{15}
\]

Combining `(13)` and `(15)` proves `(9)`. Taking the infimum over the identical normalized carriers in `(7)` proves `(8)`.

This separates two roles that AF-451 had to keep coupled at zero error:

- **completion feasibility** decides whether a residual source-law core is present in the prescribed fiber at all;
- **normalized observation gain** decides how well that admitted direction survives the retained representation.

The common background changes the first quantity but not the second.

## Relation to exact collisions and limiting collisions

For a completable core, zero numerator in `(9)` means

\[
\int\Psi\,d\alpha=\int\Psi\,d\beta,
\qquad
\int\Phi\,d\alpha=\int\Phi\,d\beta.
\]

Thus `alpha,beta` have the same joint `F=(Psi,Phi)` barycenter. Completing them produces exactly the AF-451 collision pair. Conversely every AF-451 collision gives such a zero-gain core after maximal cancellation. So AF-451's completed Radon collision locus is precisely the zero-gain part of the carrier used in `(8)`.

But the stable problem sees its closure behavior as well. It is possible that every actual completable core has positive gain while there exists a sequence `eta_n in mathfrak C_{r,u}^{Psi}` with

\[
\left\|\int\Phi\,d\eta_n\right\|_Y\to0.
\tag{16}
\]

Then the retained observation is injective on the whole fiber but has

\[
\kappa_{r,u}=0.
\]

This is the fixed-source-law measure-space form of the actual-secant versus limiting-secant distinction in AF-043 and the generic nonlinear lower-Lipschitz distinction in AF-045. The extra content here is the exact characterization of which source-law measure secants survive the sparsity and completion constraints after common mass is removed.

## Difference-norm metrics covered by the theorem

The theorem is not tied to total variation. It applies whenever the declared physical source metric is the norm of the signed difference.

### Integral probability metrics

For a balanced separating test class `G`, an integral probability metric has the form

\[
d_G(\mu,\nu)
=
\sup_{g\in G}
\left|\int g\,d(\mu-\nu)\right|.
\tag{17}
\]

Hence

\[
d_G(\tau\alpha+(1-\tau)\rho,
\tau\beta+(1-\tau)\rho)
=
\tau d_G(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{18}
\]

Total variation, bounded-Lipschitz metrics, and characteristic-kernel MMD fit this difference-homogeneous pattern under their usual separating hypotheses.

### Wasserstein-1

When `X` carries a metric and the measures have finite first moment, Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality identifies

\[
W_1(\mu,\nu)
=
\|\mu-\nu\|_{KR}
\tag{19}
\]

on equal-mass measures. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
W_1(
\tau\alpha+(1-\tau)\rho,
\tau\beta+(1-\tau)\rho)
=
\tau W_1(\alpha,\beta).
}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus a common source-law background cannot improve or worsen the normalized linear-observation condition ratio when the physical source geometry is `W_1`.

This is particularly useful for the present line because the source metric is not being chosen after the fact to rescue a discriminator. Once an independently justified `W_1` or IPM geometry is fixed, completion feasibility and observation gain separate exactly as `(7)`--`(9)` state.

## Category boundary: `W_p` for `p>1` does not obey the same invariance

The difference-norm hypothesis is substantive. Higher Wasserstein metrics provide a two-point falsification control.

Take

\[
X=\{0,2\}\subset\mathbb R,
\qquad
\Phi(x)=x,
\]

with no nontrivial source-law coordinate beyond fixed mass. Let

\[
\alpha=\delta_0,
\qquad
\beta=\delta_2,
\qquad
\rho=\delta_0,
\]

and for `0<tau<1` set

\[
\mu=\tau\alpha+(1-\tau)\rho=\delta_0,
\]

\[
\nu=\tau\beta+(1-\tau)\rho
=(1-\tau)\delta_0+\tau\delta_2.
\tag{21}
\]

The linear observation difference is

\[
\left|\int x\,d(\mu-\nu)\right|
=2\tau.
\tag{22}
\]

For every `p>=1`, all of the unmatched mass `tau` must move distance `2`, so

\[
W_p(\mu,\nu)
=2\tau^{1/p}.
\tag{23}
\]

The normalized ratio is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{2\tau}{2\tau^{1/p}}
=
\tau^{1-1/p}.
}
\tag{24}
\]

For `p=1`, `(24)` is identically one, exactly as `(20)` predicts. For every `p>1`, however,

\[
\tau^{1-1/p}\to0
\qquad(\tau\downarrow0).
\]

The mean observation is injective on the entire probability simplex over `{0,2}`, but its lower Lipschitz modulus relative to `W_p` is zero for every `p>1`.

So common contamination can create arbitrarily poor normalized conditioning in a metric that is not linear in the signed difference, even though it cannot do so in `W_1`, total variation, or another difference-norm geometry. Quantitative fidelity is genuinely category-relative; one cannot carry the cancellation rule from `W_1` into `W_2` merely because both are transport metrics.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The mathematical ingredients are classical and this finding makes **no novelty claim** for them.

- Alfred Müller, **“Integral Probability Metrics and Their Generating Classes of Functions,”** *Advances in Applied Probability* 29(2), 429--443 (1997), DOI `10.1017/S000186780002807X`. Integral probability metrics are defined directly by linear test-function differences, so the common-background scaling `(18)` is immediate from the classical framework.
- D. A. Edwards, **“On the Kantorovich--Rubinstein theorem,”** *Expositiones Mathematicae* 29(4), 387--398 (2011), DOI `10.1016/j.exmath.2011.06.005`. Edwards treats `W_1` as the norm of an equal-mass signed difference and explicitly develops the cancellation of common positive mass through the Jordan parts. This is direct prior art for `(19)`--`(20)`.
- AF-043 and AF-045 already identify normalized secants / lower Lipschitz moduli as the correct stable-fidelity objects for linear and general metric representations. AF-130 specializes metric-local decision witnesses to normalized `W_1` recovery and records the same Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm geometry.

A targeted search for common-contamination scaling in IPMs, Kantorovich--Rubinstein cancellation, and restricted minimum-gain/secant formulations found the constituent mechanisms in these established theories rather than a distinct new theorem. The durable Arithmetic Fidelity contribution here is therefore organizational and line-specific: combining AF-451's **completion feasibility under a prescribed affine source law and atom budget** with the classical difference-norm geometry yields an exact carrier for fixed-fiber conditioning. It shows that background completion transports not only exact Radon collisions but every normalized near-collision direction without changing its gain.

## Boundaries and falsification checks

- The source metric must be induced by an absolutely homogeneous norm on signed differences, or at least have exactly that homogeneity on the admitted secants. The `W_p`, `p>1`, control shows that this cannot be assumed for an arbitrary metric on measures.
- The source norm must separate the admitted differences if `(2)` is to be a genuine metric lower-Lipschitz modulus. A non-characteristic IPM may have nonzero source differences of zero seminorm and requires quotienting before this formulation is used.
- The theorem uses affine source constraints `int Psi dmu=u`. Nonlinear source laws need not survive maximal cancellation as the equal-core relation `(3)`.
- Completion feasibility is still essential. A source-equal core with small observation gain says nothing about the `u` fiber unless a positive common background exists that reaches `u` while respecting the actual atom budget.
- A small completion weight does not improve the normalized ratio, but it does shrink the absolute source distance and absolute observation difference. The theorem concerns a lower Lipschitz condition ratio, not a fixed-resolution hypothesis test with an external noise floor.
- Exact injectivity and positive conditioning remain different properties. Absence of AF-451 collisions excludes zero-gain actual cores; it does not exclude a sequence of normalized completable cores approaching zero gain.
- The observation must be linear in the measure. Nonlinear downstream statistics can respond to common backgrounds in additional ways and require their own secant analysis.
- Nothing here yet singles out rational primes. A prime-specific application must show that the rational-prime source class has a different completable-core carrier or a different restricted minimum gain from matched generalized-prime/non-prime controls under an independently justified source metric.
