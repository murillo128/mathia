# Arithmetic-fidelity research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Arithmetic-Fidelity evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Which source relations remain observable after matched controls, nuisance closure and the actual representation map?

AF-523--AF-528 show that leading scalar boundary-curvature data can be matched while an exact relational carrier still retains hidden source shape. For power-separated anchors, the projective logarithmic derivative of the exact carrier recovers the anchor exponent, but the signal is infinitesimal and therefore does not by itself give a stable finite-resolution observable.

AF-533--AF-538 identify the correct nuisance-relative geometry. In Gaussian and regular semiparametric models the retained target information is the component of the efficient score transverse to the **closed** downstream nuisance-plus-invisible space. For finite-dimensional targets this becomes a positive-semidefinite efficient-information matrix: the fidelity subspace is the kernel of the stagewise information loss, and the end-to-end fidelity spectrum records which target combinations survive compression. Algebraic injectivity, raw sample count or an unprojected carrier norm is not the relevant stability notion.

AF-539--AF-541 then isolate a genuine exact-carrier shape coordinate for the prime-prefix model with scalar tail-multiplier nuisance. The first projective log-slope can be cancelled only when the multiplier reaches its explicit logarithmic threshold, while every fixed higher log-jet annihilates the unbounded affine multiplier direction. More importantly, the two-point contrast

`Q_(A,h)=log R_A(h)-(1+h)log R_A(0)`

removes that unbounded multiplier exactly and, at `h=A^(-1)`, recovers `1-theta` after the normalization `log log A/(h log A)`.

AF-542 shows why this exact-carrier theorem cannot simply be transported through raw normalized curvature: the first nonlinear carrier-to-curvature correction is small at value level but becomes dominant after the shrinking two-point discriminator is applied. The correct transfer criterion is therefore error control in the **seminorm induced by the downstream discriminator**, not a pointwise `1+o(1)` approximation.

AF-543 supplies the first successful curvature-native repair for this model. Writing the normalized curvature defect in terms of the source carrier `D` and the canonical prime reference coordinate

`r(t)=M_1(t)^2/[M_0(t)M_2(t)]`,

the exact map `D -> g_r(D)` is locally invertible in the physical small-`D` regime. Inverting it before taking the relational contrast reconstructs a calibrated carrier `D^sharp` and restores the AF-541 anchor-scale limit. This is a genuine representation-level result: the nonlinear curvature defect can be removed without access to the hidden cutoff carrier itself, but only after retaining the additional reference coordinate `r(t)`.

AF-544 makes the calibration burden quantitative. At the physical endpoint, the leading approximation `r(t)~1/log(1/t)` is not accurate enough after the transverse discriminator; the finite-part constant in the prime-zeta expansion becomes load-bearing. Using

`r(t)=1/[log(1/t)+beta_P]+O(t/log(1/t))`

crosses the common-calibration accuracy threshold in the AF-543 regime, whereas omitting `beta_P` produces a downstream error larger than the target signal. The finding also separates common-mode calibration error from differential error: common errors enjoy cancellation in the relational ratio, while independently varying errors must be controlled at a much finer scale.

AF-558 corrects the withdrawn AF-545 while retaining the inverse-flow sensitivity identity. For a shared decoder calibration, the two-branch contrast endpoint error is the **signed** integral `E=int_(u0)^(u1) Delta K(v)dv` with inverse sensitivity `K_q=-q'(z)G_u/G_z`. The absolute path variation `V=int_I|Delta K(v)|dv` bounds `|E|`, but is not generally a necessary accuracy condition because the sensitivity difference can change sign and cancel. In the physical AF-543/544 curvature decoder with `D>0`, `0<u<1/2` and `partial_D K_q<0`, source ordering forces a constant sensitivity-difference sign and hence `|E|=V`. A uniform quadratic remainder requires Lipschitz sensitivity along the decoder path; `q in C^1` alone gives only first-order little-o. The all-branch translation-quotient classification survives.

AF-546 extends this from translation nuisance to an exact low-degree hierarchy. In the readout coordinate `Q=q(z)`, every decoded branch obeys the common scalar flow `partial_u Q=V(Q,u)`. Two-point differences remove the nuisance exactly iff `V` is constant in `Q`; three-point ratios of differences are invariant iff `V` is affine; four-point cross-ratios are invariant iff `V` is quadratic, equivalently the common flow is locally Riccati/projective. The converse statements hold on connected local intervals under the stated regularity. Thus one extra matched branch can enlarge the exactly quotiented nuisance class without first reconstructing `u`, while departures from these classes are measured by the corresponding `Q`-derivatives of `V`. This is a structural inverse-problem theorem, not evidence that the prime-prefix nuisance actually lies in one of the larger classes.

AF-547 identifies the exact boundary of that finite-arity hierarchy. For a nuisance family of scalar vector fields, every finite-branch invariant is annihilated by the **Lie closure** of the admissible fields, so the relevant local quantity is the evaluation rank of that closure on the retained branch configuration. The constant, affine and quadratic polynomial families are finite-dimensional Lie algebras and recover the `2,3,4` hierarchy. From unrestricted cubic degree onward, however, Lie brackets generate every polynomial vector field; Vandermonde interpolation then gives full evaluation rank on every finite configuration of distinct branches, so no nonconstant `C^1` universal finite-branch invariant exists. The obstruction is the freedom of the nuisance family, not the degree of one field: a fixed nonvanishing cubic field is one-dimensional and can be straightened to translation.

AF-548 closes the ordinary finite-jet escape at the unrestricted-calibration boundary. For finitely many regular `C^k` branches with pairwise distinct base values, orientation-preserving smooth postcomposition is transitive on every fixed finite-jet order/sign chamber: labelled order of the base values and the sign of each first derivative are the only surviving discrete data. The Faà di Bruno action is triangular with nonzero highest-jet coefficient, and distinct base values let the calibration jets be prescribed independently. Thus adding any fixed finite number of derivatives to finitely many separated regular branches still creates no nonconstant universal calibration invariant. The theorem deliberately does not classify singular contact strata or coincident branch values, where the independent-jet interpolation step fails.

The live question is therefore no longer whether simply adding more matched branches or more regular finite jets can continue the low-degree hierarchy. It is to identify the **actual source-induced nuisance Lie algebra and jet coupling** after the physical representation map, then retain only source-natural structure that leaves a genuine transverse direction. If finite-point evaluation rank is full and calibration jets remain independently programmable at the observed values, neither branch count nor regular jet depth repairs the quotient. A viable escape must instead restrict or anchor the nuisance, retain a reconstructible calibration law, use source-forced singular/contact or repeated-value structure, impose a metric/operator/dynamical coupling across observed values, or change the representation. Where a rank deficit or shared-jet constraint survives, the resulting relational lift must still be available from the arithmetic source and stable in the destination-induced seminorm. AF-543--AF-548 provide the calibrated normal form, precision threshold and exact finite-arity/finite-regular-jet no-go boundary; they do not yet produce an arithmetic discriminator or an RH mechanism.

## Classify quotient-visible contact through admission and no-contact accessibility rates

AF-495--AF-508 isolate a separate fidelity mechanism in critical translated-distance problems. Closed quotient geometry determines the asymptotic threshold, membership of the nearest transverse point in the actual quotient image determines whether finite-time contact occurs, and the admission height determines when a plateau begins. In the no-contact branch the exact carrier is the accessibility profile: with `rho(r)=dist(0,C_r)^2-lambda^2`, the distance excess is the one-sided quadratic envelope `inf_r [rho(r)+(r-t)_+^2]`.

The unresolved step is arithmetic realization rather than abstract convex geometry. Identify a natural arithmetic nuisance quotient and admission rule, determine whether its critical transverse point is admitted, and in the no-contact case prove a source-derived law for `rho` (or its inverse admission profile) strong enough to survive the quadratic envelope. Only after actual contact exists do pointwise source attainment and compactness of the retained contact fibre become relevant.

## Distinguish prime-edit peak coverage, nonadaptive identification and adaptive fidelity

AF-554--AF-557 give the critical prime-edit response profile and the smaller adjacent-edit separation `sup_a(Phi_q-Phi_p)~2(q-p)/(e p^3 log p)` for nearby `p<q`. These source curves are generalized-prime *matched controls* indexed by rational-prime edit locations, not different ordinary-prime systems. Peak detection, finite-grid coverage and noise-robust multiway identification therefore have different numerical budgets.

For a finite known set `S={p_1<...<p_m}`, let `d(S)=min_i sup_a(Phi_(p_(i+1))(a)-Phi_(p_i)(a))`. AF-559/AF-562 establish the complete-curve deterministic-noise radius `epsilon_full(S)=d(S)/2`. AF-563 finds one universal first coordinate with **every** adjacent gap `>d(S)/2` once `min S` is sufficiently large; its asymptotic one-sample ratio is `rho=(8e/9)log(3/2)=0.979705...>1/2`. AF-565 establishes a sharp worst-case *nonadaptive* two-probe relative margin `0.994878528675...` on six-prime edit-location families; AF-566 improves fixed multi-probe ratios. These fractions do not yield a uniform positive *absolute* noise margin.

AF-567 shows why adaptivity is a different resource: without output nuisance, a locator leaves at most two adjacent compatible labels and one pair-specific follow-up attains the full radius `d(S)/2`. [MI-080](intuition/MI-080-escaping-anchors-cancel-known-degree-at-constant-noise-cost.md) retains AF-581's extension to every known polynomial degree `r`. One shared `b in Pi_r` reduces the full-ray radius to `d(S)/4`; `r+1` anchors, a locator and a pair-specific follow-up attain every strict noise allowance below it for sufficiently remote finite prime-edit sets. The distant-anchor Lagrange weights reproduce the polynomial exactly while their absolute sum tends to one, so each informative contrast has noise amplification tending to two. This extends AF-568/569's constant and affine cases without merely counting nuisance coefficients.

With only `r+2` values, adaptivity equals fixed design, and the sharp asymptotic worst-family fraction of the full margin remains `rho=(8e/9)log(3/2)`. A zero-mass, total-variation-one signed-measure certificate controls every polynomial annihilator, including coalescing or escaping nodes. The analytic non-arithmetic family in MI-080 attains the same strict gap exactly for every fixed degree. Thus the general budget is `r+3`, under the locator and full-ray hypotheses, while the absolute source margin can still vanish.

[MI-081](intuition/MI-081-quadratic-tail-contact-makes-near-full-recovery-range-algebraic.md) supplies AF-582's first sharp coordinate cost. For the binary profile `f(x)=x exp(1-x)` on `0<x<=B`, the radius is half `dist_infinity(f,Pi_r)`. Retaining fraction `1-delta` of its full-ray radius requires range `R_1(delta)=1+log(1/delta)+o(1)` for affine calibration but `R_2(delta)~sqrt(log(1/delta)/delta)` for quadratic calibration. The extra quadratic alternation contact lies at `t_B=1+2 log B+O(log B/B)`; balancing it against the final contact at `B` creates the algebraic loss. Three or four fixed values approach the respective binary whole-window radii, so more samples inside that window cannot remove this obstruction.

AF-583, retained in the same MI-081, resolves the fixed-degree order without locating further alternation contacts. Markov curvature control gives `Delta_2(B)<=Delta_r(B)<=exp(1-t)+A_r(t-1)/B^2`, where `Delta_r=1-2E_r`, `A_r=2r^2(r^2-1)/3` and `1<t<=B`. Choosing `t=2 log B` proves `Delta_r(B)=Theta_r(log B/B^2)` for every fixed `r>=2`. Its gap-relative source estimate gives `alpha_p=O(1/log p)` uniformly over `q>p`, so `delta log p -> infinity` suffices to transfer the normalized range order `sqrt(log(1/delta)/delta)` to prime-edit curves; the quadratic leading constant stays one. The physical endpoint is the normalized range divided by `exp(B_1)log p`.

AF-584 closes the growing-degree order in MI-081. With `nu=r^2/B<=1/16`, its optimized Markov bound and degree-`2 floor(r/2)` Chebyshev witness give `Delta_r(B)=Theta(nu^2 log(1/nu))` with absolute constants. A positive deficit floor for `nu>=1/16` proves `Delta_(r(B))(B)->0` exactly when `r(B)^2/B->0`. Thus `R_r(delta)=Theta(r^2 sqrt(log(1/delta)/delta))` uniformly over growing supplied degrees `r>=2`. The prime-gap approximation incurs no degree-dependent amplification, so the same range law transfers under `delta log p -> infinity`; the qualitative threshold requires only the absolute profile error to vanish.

AF-585 identifies the cubic constant in MI-081: `Delta_3(B)~(27/2)log B/B^2` and `R_3(delta)~(3 sqrt(3)/2)sqrt(log(1/delta)/delta)`. The source peak forces every rescaled limiting approximant into `|P|<=1/2`, `P(0)=1/2`, `P'(0)=0`; the sharp endpoint curvature `P''(0)>=-27/2` selects `P_*(y)=1/2-(27/4)y^2(1-y)`. A whole-window cubic witness attains that bound. The same range constant transfers to prime-edit curves under `delta log p -> infinity`.

For fixed `r>=4`, does `B^2 Delta_r(B)/log B` converge, and can the saturated-endpoint curvature extremum characterize its value with a matching whole-window witness? When `r^2/B` tends to a positive constant, is there a limiting deficit curve? The sufficient prime-transfer condition need not be necessary: sharper gap-relative or quotient-specific error control could admit smaller `delta log p`. A common design for a multiway source set must still control its minimum pair contrast and absolute coefficient norm. Relative errors, output precision and independently recalibrated probes remain separate questions. No ordinary-prime discriminator follows from these generalized-prime matched controls.

## Which calibration restrictions turn exact identification into a finite certificate?

[MI-079](intuition/MI-079-unknown-polynomial-calibration-separates-random-identification-from-certification.md) retains AF-577's distinction: analytic sources with nonpolynomial pairwise gaps are full-curve identifiable modulo unknown finite-degree polynomial calibration, but no finite deterministic transcript certifies a label. One ideal continuously random validation coordinate, independent of all fitted interpolants, yields almost-sure identification on each fixed input within its actual degree plus two queries. Finite-bit policies instead satisfy `e >= (1-1/m) P_y(return)` on every input, so almost-sure return has blind-guess minimax error `1-1/m`, even without a uniform or expected resource cap. The decisive step is simultaneous interpolation of a finite collection of returning coin transcripts, not interpolation after observing the realized seed. On a bounded window, uniform polynomial approximation also makes the full-curve positive-noise radius zero.

AF-578 also closes the continuous-random fixed-cap boundary: on a bounded window with sources in `C^(N-1)` up to its endpoints, cap `N` has minimax error `c_m=1-1/m`, already for unrestricted-coefficient nuisances of degree at most `N-1`. The smooth source gap has a bounded interpolation coefficient lift at arbitrarily close nodes; broad Gaussian calibration coefficients then drive every pair of adaptive transcript laws together. For smooth sources the stopping law is `e+c_m sup_x P_x(T>N)>=c_m`. Continuous validation attains the bound with per-input finite stopping but no uniform expected query budget. A supplied degree bound `d` gives an exact threshold: `d+1` queries have blind-guess minimax error, and `d+2` give zero error almost surely for analytic nonpolynomial gaps.

AF-579 shows why enlarging the window is insufficient: on the whole ray the prime-edit full-curve noise radius is `d(S)/4>0`, but cap `N` still has error `c_m`. The exact map `x=a/(1+a)`, `z=(1-x)^(N-1)(y-F_1)` preserves `Pi_(N-1)`; exponential decay of every gap derivative validates the compactified endpoint. The full-ray margin comes separately from the fact that only constant polynomials stay uniformly close to a bounded gap. No finite query at an arbitrarily large coordinate captures that full-curve separation uniformly.

AF-580 closes the positive-noise stopping gap: for every `epsilon>0`, exact polynomial-plus-error matches on exhausting compact windows give `e_epsilon >= c_m P_x(return)` at every strict-slack or continuous-error input, even for ideal continuous randomness and unrestricted input-dependent finite query counts. This includes noiseless inputs and, for smooth sources, competitors with smooth compactly supported errors. Below `d(S)/4`, full-curve separation survives but reliable finite return does not. No bound at arbitrary discontinuous noise-saturated inputs or under analytic errors is asserted.

AF-581 answers the known-degree question on the full ray: the construction in [MI-080](intuition/MI-080-escaping-anchors-cancel-known-degree-at-constant-noise-cost.md) guarantees recovery below `d(S)/4` with `r+3` adaptive values for every known finite degree `r` and sufficiently remote finite prime-edit set. It does not assert endpoint recovery. AF-582--AF-584's binary range laws in [MI-081](intuition/MI-081-quadratic-tail-contact-makes-near-full-recovery-range-algebraic.md) classify fixed and growing degree range order without supplying a multiway range or absolute-precision bound. The general class separation remains `min_(i<j) inf_(p in Pi_r) ||F_i-F_j-p||_infinity`, and the finite bank must also control its contrast coefficient norm. AF-575/576's derivative-bounded smooth class instead admits deterministic finite stopping with unknown strict slack in its exact-value experiment; that gluing argument, AF-580's whole-window matches and AF-581's finite-degree annihilation require different admissible classes.
