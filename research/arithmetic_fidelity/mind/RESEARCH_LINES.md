## Match source sign complexity to a quantitative finite-order Euler modulus

**Linked intuition:** `MI-018-signed-complexity-only-transfers-through-sign-regular-observations`.

AF-215--AF-217 show that genuine prime support can sustain arbitrary Prouhet cancellation, exact `m`-moment cancellation forces at least `m` ordered sign changes, and the full Euler-log kernel is not totally nonnegative of all orders. AF-218--AF-220 now supply the positive finite-order side: the kernel is globally strictly sign-regular through order three, and curvature plus coordinate separation certifies any prescribed finite order on compact interaction windows.

The live theorem is quantitative and scale-sensitive. Relate the source-forced sign budget to an observation window whose separation/curvature modulus remains strong enough on the prime tail. A finite-order theorem is useful only if its constants survive the vanishing tail mesh and the inverse problem at the scale required by the target.

## Treat faithful translation reparameterization as affine rigidity

**Linked intuition:** `MI-019-faithful-translation-lifts-are-affine`.

AF-221--AF-223 close a broad coordinate-repair route. Exact faithful scalar translation coordinates on the dense prime-difference source are affine; vector-valued faithful lifts still occupy one affine direction; and stable approximate lifts are Hyers-affine up to the declared representation error.

A useful escape must therefore pay somewhere explicit: lose faithful difference representation, introduce source-specific side information, accept an observation/inverse-conditioning error large enough to matter, or leave the translation category. Merely embedding the prime tail in a higher-dimensional or nonlinear coordinate system does not create new translational separation.
