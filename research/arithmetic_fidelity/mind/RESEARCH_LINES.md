## Resolve the saturated discrete source boundary rather than tightening continuous caps

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-023-local-completeness-can-preserve-a-root-rate-discriminator-at-zero-density`, `MI-024-periodic-completeness-turns-li-cancellation-into-profinite-leakage`, `MI-025-regular-sampling-separates-aliasing-from-finite-window-stability`, `MI-026-zero-count-fidelity-is-distance-to-the-boundary-zero-discriminant`, `MI-027-one-sided-source-constraints-replace-nullspace-rank-by-reachable-cone-geometry`.

AF-282--AF-302 separate acquisition stability from zero-sensitive fidelity and replace deleted linear directions by the exact positive cone or normalized convex body reachable inside each compression fiber. AF-303 then closes a fixed source-independent coefficient cap, and AF-304 closes fixed support cardinality by itself.

AF-305 now closes their entire strict-slack combination. For normalized nonnegative sources with support at most `k` and coefficient cap `alpha`, the class is empty when `k alpha<1`; whenever `k alpha>1`, recurrent rational-prime zero/safe collisions survive at arbitrarily large height. The only nonempty boundary not covered by those continuous mechanisms is exact saturation `k alpha=1`, where every source is the uniform measure on a `k`-subset and all barycentric coefficient freedom disappears.

The live source-restriction question is therefore genuinely discrete. Determine whether equal-weight or otherwise prescribed support families can retain a zero-sensitive rational-prime discriminator after deletion, and identify which support combinatorics or arithmetic coupling actually shrinks the target-reachable failure set. The saturated `k=2` family already shows that the answer depends on the target line: equal-weight two-term sums are zero-free for fixed `Re(s)!=0` but have periodic zeros on the imaginary axis. For `k>=3`, same-fiber collisions between different uniform support patterns remain open.

Geometry-adaptive constraints, discrete coefficient alphabets, prescribed support systems, multiplicative/nonlinear coupling, vector observations, and compact vertical windows remain legitimate categories. They must be tested against the actual destination failure set rather than inferred from nominal source simplicity.

Finite local degree, prime breadth, mixed-channel coupling, sample count, frame conditioning, nuisance gauge, residual jitter, projective distance to failure, linear condition number, nonlinear relative distortion, radial metric singularity, observation leverage, admissible-source dimension, quotient geometry, source-derived anisotropy, fiberwise reachability, and **continuous versus saturated-discrete source geometry** are distinct resources. AF-305 shows that cap and support budgets can remain jointly useless throughout an open parameter region and become qualitatively different only at exact saturation.
