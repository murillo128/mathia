AF-466--AF-468 classify the bounded-distortion scalar escape. The full TV simplex is Hilbertian after `d_TV -> sqrt(d_TV)`, `alpha=1/2` is the sharp power threshold, and every complexity-independent scalar Hilbert repair must amplify small TV distances at least at square-root scale.

AF-469--AF-470 now close the apparent local exactness loophole. If `g(d_TV)` is a squared Hilbert distance on every finite subset of the unrestricted simplex, then `g` obeys complete-alternation finite-difference inequalities on the entire diameter `0<=d_TV<=2`; AF-470 obtains this exactly through replicated private atoms, with no continuity assumption. The remaining radial questions are narrower: whether bounded-interval complete alternation is also sufficient, and whether the physical arithmetic source actually realizes enough unrestricted mixture geometry to inherit the full obstruction.

The live arithmetic question is therefore source-structural and destination-relative. If the physical source excludes the private-atom/weighted-hyperrectangle tests, identify the exact restriction that does so. If not, a useful escape must leave scalar radial repair, or show that the final theorem can genuinely consume the singularly changed metric. Any non-radial/state-dependent structure must remain source-forced and preserve the rational-prime discriminator through later quotient, spectral or compression maps.

## Keep identifiability, anchored provenance, pairwise stability and metric repair separate

Exact injectivity, atom separation, anchored discrimination, bounded-distortion metric repair and exact radial Hilbertization are different claims. AF-462--AF-465 separate anchored boundary geometry from pairwise source cubes; AF-466--AF-468 show that changing the metric repairs Hilbert geometry only by paying singular small-scale sensitivity; AF-469--AF-470 show that exact radial repair additionally inherits complete alternation across the full unrestricted TV diameter.

A fidelity theorem must therefore state the source metric, admissible comparison class, anchored versus pairwise quantifiers, target/operator category, upper sensitivity budget, exact versus bounded-distortion requirement, and the positive lower gain actually consumed downstream. The absence of a negative witness is not a positive theorem, and a successful metric deformation is not fidelity in the original TV scale.
