# Arithmetic-fidelity research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable arithmetic-fidelity intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Match source-mixture complexity to target geometry before tracing downstream gain

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-042-finite-prefix-pole-fidelity-needs-a-tail-class`, `MI-056-bounded-hilbert-marks-have-sharp-sqrt-k-sparse-mixture-tv-fidelity`, `MI-057-paired-sign-cubes-transfer-banach-type-to-source-relative-mixture-fidelity`, `MI-058-boundary-geometry-can-protect-anchored-fidelity-without-pairwise-fidelity`, `MI-059-source-point-cubes-block-stable-nonlinear-hilbert-recovery-unless-the-source-geometry-changes`, `MI-060-cube-poorness-does-not-certify-hilbert-fidelity`, `MI-061-square-root-tv-is-the-sharp-hilbert-snowflake-boundary`.

AF-451--AF-457 identify the completed source-law secant carrier and the target-category obstruction: full-simplex TV fidelity under bounded linear/barycentric observation requires an `ell^1` copy in the target, while Hilbert sparse-mixture gain falls at the sharp `Theta(k^(-1/2))` scale. AF-458--AF-461 transfer that obstruction to restricted sources through paired-cube completion and show that bounded finite-rank laws and regular finite-dimensional `C^1` equality fibers remain cube-complete.

AF-462 identifies a real but one-sided boundary escape. At a finite-support simplex law `mu_0`, anchored directions may be negative only on `supp(mu_0)`, giving `D_k >= (1-s/k)_+` for `s=|supp(mu_0)|`; the canonical one-hot Hilbert embedding has exact anchored TV gain `1/sqrt(s)`. AF-463 classifies why that escape cannot be promoted to arbitrary nearby pairs inside a convex source class under bounded linear observation: the moving-secant paratingent cone is the full affine direction space.

AF-464 closes a broader operator-category escape when the source itself contains large metric cubes. Every nonlinear encoder with finite upper TV-Lipschitz modulus into Hilbert obeys the same `k^(-1/2)` lower-gain ceiling on the paired-mixture cubes. AF-465 then closes the tempting converse: cube-poorness or bounded doubling does not certify Hilbert fidelity.

AF-466--AF-467 now classify the natural metric-change escape. The full TV simplex becomes exactly Hilbertian after `d_TV -> sqrt(d_TV)`, and more generally every `d_TV^alpha` with `alpha<=1/2` is Hilbertian. The exponent `1/2` is sharp: for every `alpha>1/2`, Hilbert distortion on `k`-sparse paired-mixture cubes is at least `k^(alpha-1/2)`. The square-root repair therefore succeeds only by changing the source metric; relative to the original TV scale its explicit embedding has infinite forward Lipschitz sensitivity and retains the complete labelled law rather than compressing it.

The live arithmetic question is consequently a positive source-and-destination geometry question. If the downstream theorem needs pairwise stable recovery in the original TV metric, what property of the physical arithmetic source yields a uniform recovery bound? If it can instead consume a snowflaked metric, why is that deformation compatible with the final discriminator and with every later compression or quotient? A different target, changed source metric, complexity-growing upper sensitivity, or narrower comparison task is meaningful only after that compatibility is proved.

## Keep identifiability, anchored provenance, pairwise stability and metric repair separate

Exact injectivity, atom separation and anchored discrimination can each coexist with zero pairwise inverse margin. AF-462 shows that a convex boundary can stay macroscopically far from large paired cubes for anchored directions; AF-463 shows that moving two comparison points symmetrizes the same boundary back to its full affine direction space; AF-464 shows that arbitrary nonlinear Lipschitz destinations remain constrained once the source contains actual Hamming cubes; AF-465 shows that absence of those cubes is not positive evidence.

AF-466--AF-467 add a different category boundary: changing TV to `d_TV^alpha` crosses an exact Hilbert threshold at `alpha=1/2`. That repair must not be confused with fidelity in the original metric or with information compression. A fidelity theorem must therefore state the source metric, admissible comparison class, anchored versus pairwise quantifiers, target/operator category, upper sensitivity budget, and the positive lower gain actually consumed downstream. A negative obstruction, the absence of its witness, and a successful theorem after metric deformation are three different claims.
