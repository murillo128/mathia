# MI-042 — Finite-prefix pole fidelity needs a structural source class before conditioning can even be priced

**Evidence level:** exact-derived boundary synthesis from [AF-366](../../findings/AF-366-finite-prefix-observations-need-tail-class-for-dirichlet-pole-fidelity.md), [AF-367](../../findings/AF-367-finite-prime-prefix-plus-square-root-beurling-regularity-does-not-determine-zero-geometry.md), [AF-368](../../findings/AF-368-logarithmic-integer-counting-proximity-does-not-identify-rational-primes.md), the category-specific recovery theorems [AF-369](../../findings/AF-369-integer-supported-delone-zero-divisors-universal-residue-restores-prime-identity.md) and [AF-370](../../findings/AF-370-hamburger-source-class-functional-equation-rigidity.md), and the finite Stieltjes-moment obstruction [AF-439](../../findings/AF-439-finite-euler-moment-prefixes-have-local-pole-moving-fibers.md).

AF-366--AF-368 isolate completion freedom. Arbitrarily long exact finite observations, positivity and multiplicativity, near-square-root generalized-integer counting, and even arbitrarily small fixed `O(log x)` counting discrepancy can leave the source or zero geometry undetermined. The missing resource is not simply another digit of scalar accuracy: the admissible completion fibre remains too large.

AF-369 and AF-370 show how that ambiguity can disappear after the source category is narrowed. Inside integer-supported Delone Beurling systems, a residue normalization identifies the rational-prime source. Inside Hamburger's ordinary-Dirichlet finite-order class, the exact completed Riemann functional equation identifies `c zeta`, and `a(1)=1` fixes `c=1`. The same observable or symmetry is therefore weak on a broad class and rigid on a sufficiently structured one.

AF-439 gives the corresponding finite-moment version directly in the exact Euler/Stieltjes language. The complete sequence `zeta(2k)` has the shell-resolved carrier `sum_m 1/(m^2-z)`, but for every fixed `N` the first `N` moments are locally noninjective even among nearby positive finite Stieltjes measures with unit residues. Perturbing only the constant elementary-symmetric coefficient of `N+1` positive nodes preserves the first `N` power sums by Newton identities while moving all poles. Thus positivity, ordered positive support and residue normalization do not by themselves make finite-prefix pole recovery identifiable.

This separates two inverse questions that must not be collapsed. **Identifiability asks whether the admissible finite-data fibre contains one target; conditioning asks how rapidly the inverse amplifies perturbations after that fibre has already been made target-pure.** AF-438 removes the sequential peeling artifact, but AF-439 shows that one cannot immediately replace it by a condition-number question on an unspecified generalized-shell class.

The reusable audit is therefore two-dimensional. Before inferring a pole, zero or source discriminator, specify the admissible source category and the exact analytic/scalar relation retained by the destination, then prove that their joint finite-data fibre is target-pure. Only then price Padé/Hankel/Prony/Stieltjes recovery or another inverse. Narrowing the category can be as information-bearing as adding an observable, and exact infinite-data reconstruction does not waive the finite-prefix identifiability gate.

**Boundary.** AF-439 perturbs to nearby positive nodes that need not remain reciprocal integer squares, so it does not prove nonidentifiability inside the exact Riemann shell class. AF-369 and AF-370 are category-specific rigidity theorems, not universal finite-data recovery. None of these results selects nontrivial zeros or implies RH; they identify which source-class assumptions are load-bearing before a stability claim can even be formulated.
