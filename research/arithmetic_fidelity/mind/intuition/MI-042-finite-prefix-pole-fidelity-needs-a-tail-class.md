# MI-042 — Finite-prefix pole fidelity is source-class dependent before conditioning can even be priced

**Evidence level:** exact-derived boundary synthesis from [AF-366](../../findings/AF-366-finite-prefix-observations-need-tail-class-for-dirichlet-pole-fidelity.md), [AF-367](../../findings/AF-367-finite-prime-prefix-plus-square-root-beurling-regularity-does-not-determine-zero-geometry.md), [AF-368](../../findings/AF-368-logarithmic-integer-counting-proximity-does-not-identify-rational-primes.md), the category-specific recovery theorems [AF-369](../../findings/AF-369-integer-supported-delone-zero-divisors-universal-residue-restores-prime-identity.md) and [AF-370](../../findings/AF-370-hamburger-source-class-functional-equation-rigidity.md), the finite Stieltjes-moment obstruction [AF-439](../../findings/AF-439-finite-euler-moment-prefixes-have-local-pole-moving-fibers.md), and the fixed-support finite-alphabet recovery theorem [AF-440](../../findings/AF-440-reciprocal-square-occupancy-has-linear-depth-finite-moment-fidelity.md).

AF-366--AF-368 isolate completion freedom. Arbitrarily long exact finite observations, positivity and multiplicativity, near-square-root generalized-integer counting, and even arbitrarily small fixed `O(log x)` counting discrepancy can leave the source or zero geometry undetermined. The missing resource is not simply another digit of scalar accuracy: the admissible completion fibre remains too large.

AF-369 and AF-370 show how that ambiguity can disappear after the source category is narrowed. Inside integer-supported Delone Beurling systems, a residue normalization identifies the rational-prime source. Inside Hamburger's ordinary-Dirichlet finite-order class, the exact completed Riemann functional equation identifies `c zeta`, and `a(1)=1` fixes `c=1`. The same observable or symmetry is therefore weak on a broad class and rigid on a sufficiently structured one.

AF-439 gives the negative finite-moment side directly in the exact Euler/Stieltjes language. The complete sequence `zeta(2k)` has the shell-resolved carrier `sum_m 1/(m^2-z)`, but for every fixed `N` the first `N` moments are locally noninjective even among nearby positive finite Stieltjes measures with unit residues. Perturbing only the constant elementary-symmetric coefficient of `N+1` positive nodes preserves the first `N` power sums by Newton identities while moving all poles. Thus positivity, ordered positive support and residue normalization do not by themselves make finite-prefix pole recovery identifiable.

AF-440 supplies the complementary positive theorem once the source class retains the exact reciprocal-square support and a separated finite mark alphabet. If `a_m` and `b_m` take values in a finite alphabet of separation `delta` and diameter `D`, equality of the depth-`N` moment forces `a_m=b_m` whenever `D m <= delta(2N-1)`. For binary occupancy the first `2N-1` shell marks are therefore determined by one sufficiently deep moment. The proof also gives an explicit dominance margin

`delta m^(-2N) - D sum_(j>m) j^(-2N)`,

so identifiability and numerical separation can be discussed separately inside this rigid class.

The reusable lesson is sharper than “choose a tail class.” **Finite-prefix fidelity can change from locally set-valued to linearly deep exact recovery solely by changing the admissible source geometry while keeping the same moment observable.** Identifiability asks whether the finite-data fibre contains one target; conditioning asks how rapidly the inverse amplifies perturbations after that fibre has already been made target-pure. AF-438 removes the sequential peeling artifact, AF-439 shows that a broad movable-support class is nonidentified, and AF-440 shows that source-rigid discrete support can restore exact finite-depth recovery.

The next useful boundary theorem should interpolate between these regimes rather than assume either one is universal. Controlled support jitter, nonbinary separated residues, interval-valued marks, or a source-derived tail law are natural tests because they reveal which part of AF-440's dominance argument is structural and which collapses as soon as the support fibre can move.

**Boundary.** AF-439 perturbs to nearby positive nodes that need not remain reciprocal integer squares, while AF-440 fixes the reciprocal-square support and a finite separated alphabet. Neither theorem settles recovery under support uncertainty, continuous residue amplitudes, or noisy moments. The explicit AF-440 margin may be very small even when exact identifiability holds. None of these results selects nontrivial zeros or implies RH; they identify which source assumptions are load-bearing before a stability claim can be formulated.
