# MI-039 — Exceptional time does not make the prime-phase point algebraic

**Evidence level:** exact pointwise consequence of [AF-332](../../findings/AF-332-six-exponentials-forces-prime-phase-algebraic-sparsity.md), combined only for interpretation with AF-330--AF-331. No exact eight-prime incidence or non-incidence theorem is claimed.

For distinct rational primes and any nonzero real `t`, Six Exponentials applied to any three prime logarithms and the two exponents `1,-it` gives

`#{j : p_j^(-it) in Qbar} <= 2`.

So an eight-prime exact hit of `e_1=e_4=0`, if one exists, has at least six individually transcendental phase coordinates. This is source-specific: it uses multiplicative independence of the primes and the common one-parameter exponent.

Combined with AF-330, a hypothetical hit lives in a mixed regime: `-it` is exponentially algebraic, yet the phase tuple is far outside the algebraic or torsion part of the ambient torus. Parameter exceptionalness and image algebraicity are therefore different notions.

The practical exclusion is sharp enough to kill a tempting continuation: classifying algebraic points, torsion points, or target strata requiring three algebraic coordinates cannot decide the remaining incidence. Any successful pointwise argument must see relations among mostly transcendental coordinates **through their common prime-log provenance**, not replace the target by its algebraic-point locus.

**Boundary.** Coordinatewise transcendence is not algebraic independence. The target equations themselves impose polynomial dependence, so AF-332 does not contradict a hit and gives no positive lower bound for `|e_1|+|e_4|`. It narrows the allowed target stratum but does not solve the exact-incidence problem.
