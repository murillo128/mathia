# MI-027 — One-sided and normalized source restrictions are governed by fiberwise reachable bodies

**Evidence level:** exact for positive coordinate deletion, simplex normalization, and fixed source-independent coordinate caps through AF-303. Extensions to geometry-adaptive caps, discrete or integral sources, bounded support, multiplicatively coupled/nonlinear source classes, or fixed observation windows remain open.

AF-300 shows that a complex-affine source family of intrinsic dimension `r` lowers the leverage bill from the ambient `N^(-1/2)` scale to `r^(-1/2)`, but every complex direction that remains admissible is still target-visible. AF-301 changes category: with nonnegative coefficients, forgotten coordinates expose the positive cone generated by their Dirichlet response rays rather than a deleted linear space. A retained state can reach failure exactly when its negative response lies in that cone.

AF-302 identifies the normalized version. On the coefficient simplex, for retained state `y`, remaining mass `M(y)=1-||y||_1`, retained response `r_S(y;z)`, and discarded response hull `K_D(z)=conv{d^{-z}:d in D}`, the whole fiber is

`r_S(y;z) + M(y) K_D(z)`.

Normalization is therefore a real resource bound: it cuts an unbounded cone to a bounded mass-scaled convex body. It still does not restore exact fidelity when recurrent multiplicatively independent discarded phases place zero inside that hull.

AF-303 shows that a fixed coordinate cap does not change the principle. On the normalized fiber it only replaces `K_D` by a capped feasible polytope. For discarded primes `p_j`, an exact zero polygon is feasible whenever the required inverse-radius barycentric profile obeys

`beta_D(sigma)=max_j p_j^sigma / sum_k p_k^sigma < alpha`.

Strict feasibility persists on an open phase set, and Kronecker--Weyl recurrence revisits that set at arbitrarily large heights. Because sufficiently many comparable primes make `beta_D(sigma)` smaller than any fixed `alpha>0`, no source-independent positive cap gives a uniform repair over enlarging prime source families.

The reusable invariant is the **target-relative reachable set of each compressed fiber together with the source budget defining that set**. Signed linear spaces, positive cones, normalized convex bodies, and capped polytopes are different geometries, but the fidelity test is the same: does the feasible fiber intersect the downstream failure set? A source restriction becomes materially stronger only when its intrinsic constraints prevent that intersection at the observation scale actually consumed.

**Boundary.** AF-303 is a family-level, unbounded-height obstruction. A sufficiently small cap can protect a particular fixed discarded block, and the theorem does not rule out caps adapted to response geometry, fixed support cardinality, integrality, multiplicative coupling, nonlinear source varieties, or compact vertical windows. Those cases require their own reachable body rather than extrapolation from positivity, normalization, or cap size alone.
