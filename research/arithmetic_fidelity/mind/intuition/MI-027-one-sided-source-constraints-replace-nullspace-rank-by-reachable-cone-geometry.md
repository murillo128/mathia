# MI-027 — One-sided, normalized, capped and sparse source restrictions are governed by fiberwise reachable bodies

**Evidence level:** exact for positive coordinate deletion, simplex normalization, fixed source-independent coordinate caps, and fixed support cardinality through AF-304. Extensions to geometry-adaptive constraints, discrete or integral sources, prescribed support patterns, multiplicatively coupled/nonlinear source classes, joint source laws, or fixed observation windows remain open.

AF-300 shows that a complex-affine source family of intrinsic dimension `r` lowers the leverage bill from the ambient `N^(-1/2)` scale to `r^(-1/2)`, but every complex direction that remains admissible is still target-visible. AF-301 changes category: with nonnegative coefficients, forgotten coordinates expose the positive cone generated by their Dirichlet response rays rather than a deleted linear space. A retained state can reach failure exactly when its negative response lies in that cone.

AF-302 identifies the normalized version. On the coefficient simplex, for retained state `y`, remaining mass `M(y)=1-||y||_1`, retained response `r_S(y;z)`, and discarded response hull `K_D(z)=conv{d^{-z}:d in D}`, the whole fiber is `r_S(y;z)+M(y)K_D(z)`. Normalization therefore cuts an unbounded cone to a bounded mass-scaled convex body, but does not restore exact fidelity when recurrent discarded phases place zero inside that body.

AF-303 shows that a fixed coordinate cap only replaces the full hull by a capped feasible polytope. Enough comparable discarded primes make the barycentric weights small enough to fit any fixed positive cap, and Kronecker--Weyl recurrence revisits the corresponding zero configurations at arbitrarily large heights. A source-independent cap is therefore not a uniform repair.

AF-304 shows that fixed support cardinality obeys the same target-relative principle. In a `k`-sparse normalized fiber with `s(y)` retained active coordinates, the forgotten response is exactly

`r_S(y;z) + M(y) conv_(<=k-s(y)){d^(-z): d in D}`.

For one complex scalar observation the target has two real dimensions, so Carathéodory makes three available forgotten support slots equivalent to the full convex hull. More strongly, support cardinality never becomes a uniform zero-fidelity repair once two source terms are allowed: one retained prime and one forgotten prime can cancel exactly at an unbounded arithmetic sequence of heights, while replacing that forgotten prime by another produces a safe source in the same compressed fiber. Thus every fixed `k>=2` still admits recurrent same-fiber zero/safe collisions. The `k=1` case avoids them only vacuously because monomials have no finite zeros.

The reusable invariant is the **target-relative reachable set of each compressed fiber together with the source budget that shapes it**. Signed linear spaces, positive cones, normalized convex bodies, capped polytopes and sparse convex skeleta are different geometries, but the fidelity test is unchanged: does the feasible fiber intersect the downstream failure set? Nominal source simplicity is useful only when it actually removes that intersection at the observation scale consumed.

**Boundary.** AF-303--AF-304 are family-level, unbounded-height obstructions for coordinate deletion and a single complex Dirichlet observation. They do not rule out geometry-adaptive caps, a discrete coefficient alphabet, fixed support patterns, multiplicative coupling, nonlinear source varieties, joint support/cap laws, or compact vertical windows. A vector of several observations also has a larger real response dimension and therefore a different sparse Carathéodory threshold. Those cases require their own reachable-body analysis rather than extrapolation from positivity, normalization, cap size or support count alone.
