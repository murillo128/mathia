# MI-027 — One-sided source constraints replace nullspace rank by reachable-cone geometry

**Evidence level:** exact for positive coordinate deletion in finite Dirichlet-polynomial boundary observations through AF-301; extension to bounded, normalized, discrete, multiplicative, or nonlinear arithmetic source classes remains open.

AF-300 shows that a complex-affine source cannot hide any remaining complex direction: deleting one target-visible complex direction gives enough signed freedom to hit a complex boundary equation exactly. Positivity breaks that proof because the correction coefficient cannot take an arbitrary complex sign.

AF-301 identifies the replacement object. For `c>=0`, forgetting coordinates `D` does not expose a linear correction space at `z`; it exposes the positive cone generated by the discarded response rays `n^{-z}`. A retained state can be moved to failure exactly when its negative retained response lies in that cone. Thus the relevant fidelity question is no longer only `ker(q)`: it is the **reachable correction set inside each fiber**.

This protection is weaker than it first appears. In a two-real-dimensional complex response, three suitably placed positive rays already generate the whole plane. If the discarded indices contain three numbers with Q-independent logarithms—three distinct primes suffice—Kronecker-Weyl phase recurrence produces arbitrarily large heights where those forgotten Dirichlet rays positively span `C`. At such heights positivity recreates the full signed correction freedom and every retained coordinate state has a boundary-zero completion.

So one-sidedness changes the geometry but does not by itself provide durable arithmetic fidelity. A source restriction becomes materially stronger only when it also bounds the reachable cone: normalization, coefficient budgets, discreteness, multiplicative coupling, sparsity, or another intrinsic constraint must prevent recurrent phase mixing from converting forgotten positive coordinates into arbitrary complex corrections.

**Boundary.** AF-301 treats unbounded positive orthants and coordinate deletion. A simplex, integral source, multiplicative variety, nonlinear representation, or fixed finite contour can behave differently. The next useful invariant is the fiberwise reachable correction set together with its target-scale resource bounds, not positivity as a binary label.
