# MI-027 — One-sided source constraints are governed by reachable bodies; discrete saturation separates exact failure membership from stable distance

**Evidence level:** exact for positive coordinate deletion, simplex normalization, fixed source-independent coordinate caps, fixed support cardinality and their joint cap/support law through AF-305; exact for the saturated imaginary-axis stability result AF-306 and the small-cardinality exact classification AF-307. Exact saturated zero attainment remains open for `k>=5` and outside the stated imaginary-axis/equal-weight regime.

AF-300 shows that a complex-affine source family lowers the observation bill to its intrinsic dimension but leaves every surviving complex direction target-visible. AF-301--AF-302 change category: forgotten positive coordinates generate a cone, and normalized fibers generate a convex body. Fidelity is therefore determined by whether the **reachable fiber body** meets the downstream failure set, not by ambient nullspace rank alone.

AF-303--AF-305 make the source-law boundary sharp. For normalized nonnegative sources with support at most `k` and coefficient cap `alpha`, every strict-slack regime `k alpha>1` retains continuous barycentric freedom and recurrent same-fiber zero/safe collisions. Exact saturation `k alpha=1` removes that freedom: every source is the uniform measure on a `k`-subset.

AF-306 shows that this categorical simplification does not buy stable conditioning. For every fixed saturated support with `k>=2`, Kronecker--Weyl recurrence drives the equal-weight Dirichlet polynomial arbitrarily close to zero at arbitrarily large imaginary heights. The normalized failure margin can therefore vanish even after coefficient freedom disappears.

AF-307 shows that exact membership behaves differently again. Two-prime saturated supports have exact imaginary-axis zeros and can collide with safe supports inside one deletion fiber. Three- and four-prime saturated supports have no exact imaginary-axis zeros at all, although their values approach zero arbitrarily closely. For `k=4`, a zero would force the four unit phases into two antipodal pairs and hence a forbidden rational relation between disjoint prime ratios.

The reusable lesson is sharper than “exact and stable fidelity differ.” **A source restriction can make an exact discriminator appear perfectly recoverable simply by making the failure event impossible.** Such vacuous exact fidelity has no conditioning content: the source class may lie outside the failure set while having distance zero from it. Any RH bridge using exact membership must therefore establish both non-vacuity of the discriminator on the admissible class and whatever quantitative margin the downstream theorem actually consumes.

**Boundary.** The small-cardinality exact theorem concerns equal weights on distinct rational primes and `Re(s)=0`. The first unresolved saturated cardinalities there are `k>=5`; nonzero real part, structured support systems, discrete coefficient alphabets, multiplicative/nonlinear coupling, vector observations and compact-height variants remain separate. None of the exact zero-free statements supplies a positive stable margin.
