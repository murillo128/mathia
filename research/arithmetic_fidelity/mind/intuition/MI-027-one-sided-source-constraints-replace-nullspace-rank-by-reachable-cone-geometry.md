# MI-027 — One-sided source constraints are governed by fiberwise reachable bodies, with a sharp discrete saturation boundary

**Evidence level:** exact for positive coordinate deletion, simplex normalization, fixed source-independent coordinate caps, fixed support cardinality, and their joint cap/support law through AF-305. Geometry-adaptive constraints, prescribed support families, discrete alphabets beyond the saturated family, multiplicative/nonlinear source classes, vector observations, and fixed observation windows remain open.

AF-300 shows that a complex-affine source family of intrinsic dimension `r` lowers the leverage bill from the ambient `N^(-1/2)` scale to `r^(-1/2)`, but every complex direction that remains admissible is still target-visible. AF-301 changes category: with nonnegative coefficients, forgotten coordinates expose the positive cone generated by their Dirichlet response rays rather than a deleted linear space. AF-302 identifies the normalized version: on the coefficient simplex, each compression fiber maps to a mass-scaled convex body, and fidelity is exactly the question whether that reachable body meets the downstream failure set.

AF-303 and AF-304 show separately that a fixed source-independent coefficient cap and a fixed nontrivial support cap do not repair zero-sensitive coordinate deletion. Comparable rational-prime responses make any fixed positive cap compatible with recurrent barycentric zero polygons, while even two source terms suffice for recurrent same-fiber zero/safe collisions at arbitrarily large height.

AF-305 gives the exact joint phase boundary. For

`Delta_N^(<=k,alpha)={c>=0: sum c_n=1, |supp c|<=k, c_n<=alpha}`,

one always has `1<=k alpha`. If `k alpha<1` the class is empty. If `k alpha>1`, strict mass slack remains and one of the existing prime-cancellation mechanisms re-enters: for every fixed vertical line there are arbitrarily high same-fiber sources with zero/safe Dirichlet responses. Tightening cap and support together therefore gives no cumulative protection anywhere in the strict-slack regime.

The equality case `k alpha=1` is categorically different. Every inequality in `1=sum c_n<=|supp c| alpha<=k alpha` is then saturated, so every admissible source is exactly

`c=(1/k) 1_A`, with `|A|=k`.

Continuous barycentric freedom disappears and the source class becomes the discrete set of uniform `k`-subset incidence vectors. This is a genuine change of reachable-fiber geometry, not merely a better constant. It is not a fidelity theorem: distinct uniform supports may still have different zero behavior inside one compressed fiber. For `k=2`, equal-weight two-term sums are zero-free on every fixed line `Re(s)=sigma!=0` and have the usual periodic antipodal zeros on `sigma=0`; for `k>=3`, the equal-weight support-collision problem remains open.

The reusable invariant is therefore the **target-relative reachable set of each compression fiber together with the exact source law that shapes it**. Signed linear spaces, positive cones, normalized convex bodies, capped polytopes, sparse skeleta and saturated discrete vertex sets are different categories. A nominal restriction matters only when it actually removes intersection with the destination failure set. AF-305 sharpens this further: several continuous restrictions can remain jointly ineffective until their inequalities force a discrete equality class.

**Boundary.** AF-305 is a family-level, unbounded-height obstruction for coordinate deletion and one complex Dirichlet observation. It does not settle the saturated equal-weight support family, prescribed support systems, arithmetic/discrete coefficient alphabets, multiplicative or nonlinear coupling, geometry-adaptive caps, compact vertical windows, or several simultaneous observations. Those cases need their own reachable-set analysis rather than extrapolation from cap size, sparsity, or saturation alone.
