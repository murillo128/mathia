# MI-036 — Varying prime supports fill finite phase space at fixed time

**Evidence level:** proved by [AF-323](../../findings/AF-323-varying-prime-supports-are-tail-dense-in-fixed-time-phase-space.md) as a support-union closure statement. No equidistribution theorem or exact middle-singular incidence is claimed.

Fix `tau != 0`, an integer `m >= 1`, and a prime cutoff `X`. AF-323 proves

`closure{(p_1^{-i tau},...,p_m^{-i tau}) : p_j>X distinct primes} = (S^1)^m`.

The mechanism is elementary but structurally strong. The prime number theorem gives `log p_{n+1}-log p_n -> 0`; any increasing unbounded sequence with vanishing gaps is tail-dense modulo a fixed period. With period `2 pi/|tau|`, the individual prime phases are therefore tail-dense on `S^1`. Choosing the coordinates one after another beyond the primes already used gives full product density while keeping the support genuinely rational-prime and pairwise distinct.

Hence every continuous finite-phase observable `C:(S^1)^m -> Y` has

`closure C(prime-support union) = C((S^1)^m)`.

This is a sharp warning about what “prime-specific” can mean after finite phase projection. Arithmetic discreteness of each support survives before projection, but the unrestricted union over supports has no open phase-space separation from the ambient torus. A support-uniform stability theorem cannot therefore come from continuity plus the mere fact that the coordinates were generated by rational primes.

For the eight-point singular geometry the obstruction is explicit. A regular octagon has `s_1=...=s_7=0`, while its product phase ranges over all of `S^1` after rotation. AF-323 shows that actual eight-prime supports, at one fixed nonzero time and beyond any prime cutoff, can approach any of these octagons. Thus one can make the retained low-harmonic data arbitrarily close while keeping the missing product phases macroscopically separated. There is no uniformly continuous exact decoder from the first seven power sums on the unrestricted support union.

This does not weaken [AF-322](../../findings/AF-322-prime-middle-singular-conditioning-has-a-finite-height-polynomial-modulus.md): AF-322 fixes the support first and obtains a support-dependent polynomial exclusion modulus in the time height. AF-323 changes the quantifier order by fixing time and varying the support. The two statements together identify the missing resource: cross-support stability must retain some support identity/height restriction, restrict the admissible support family, or exploit an exact incidence condition not visible in ambient continuous phase geometry.

**Boundary.** Density gives approximation, not exact incidence; it gives no limiting distribution; and it does not rule out discontinuous or explicitly support-aware recovery procedures. Its force is topological: unrestricted prime-support unions are already dense at the finite phase layer.
