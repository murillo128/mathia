# MI-030 — Five-prime harmonic rigidity constrains recurrence, not first incidence

**Evidence level:** exact for the five-prime consequences proved in AF-312, conditional on the existence of a hypothetical common-time exact hit. The low-harmonic exclusion is derived from unit-circle power-sum rigidity plus prime-log independence; the integer-lattice recurrence bound uses the classical Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt finite-rank unit-equation theorem. Existence or nonexistence of a first five-prime hit remains open.

At the first unresolved saturated support, the nonlinear information is already stronger than the linear relation tests of AF-310--AF-311, but it appears **after** incidence. If five distinct prime phases satisfy `F_P(t)=0`, the self-inversive root polynomial and Newton identities collapse the higher power sums to one complex parameter. A second zero at harmonic orders `2`, `3`, or `4` forces a regular pentagon, while a fifth-harmonic zero forces an antipodal pair plus an equilateral triple. Both geometries require rational-prime torsion relations that cannot occur at one common nonzero time. Hence `F_P(2t),...,F_P(5t)` are all nonzero.

A different recurrence restriction holds on every fixed integer orbit. After normalizing by one prime, the sequence `F_P(n t_0)` is a unit equation in an infinite cyclic subgroup of `(C^*)^4`. Proper vanishing subsums would create the already forbidden three-prime zero geometry, so the solutions are nondegenerate and finite-rank unit-equation theory gives only finitely many exact zeros on `{n t_0}`.

The important distinction is **first incidence versus recurrence amplification**. AF-312 rules out immediate low-harmonic repetition and unlimited exact recurrence once a base time is fixed, but the unknown first real time is not discretized into a finite-rank multiplicative group. The continuous prime phase curve remains outside that machinery. A proof of zero-freeness at `k=5` therefore needs a source-specific obstruction acting at the first intersection with the polygon-zero manifold, not merely a theorem saying that intersections cannot recur often afterward.

**Boundary.** No five-prime hit is constructed or excluded. The harmonic theorem currently covers `m=2,3,4,5`, not all higher harmonics, and the finite-recurrence theorem concerns one fixed integer time lattice rather than arbitrary real zeros. Neither statement supplies a zeta-zero count or an RH implication.
