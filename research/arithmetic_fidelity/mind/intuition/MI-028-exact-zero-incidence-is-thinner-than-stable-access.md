# MI-028 — Exact-zero incidence is null, dense, and nowhere stably classifiable

**Evidence level:** exact for saturated equal-weight finite Dirichlet sums on the imaginary axis through AF-309, with the prime-support exact classification still open for `k>=5`.

For a saturated `k`-term equal-weight Dirichlet sum, quotient the common phase and write the frequency differences as `nu in R^(k-1)`. AF-308 identifies the exact-zero condition with incidence of the one-parameter orbit `t nu` with the lifted equilateral-polygon zero locus and proves that the resulting cone `E_k` has Hausdorff dimension at most `k-2`. Hence `E_k` is Lebesgue-null for every `k>=3`.

AF-309 adds the complementary topology. One lifted zero configuration generates the affine lattice `theta+2 pi Z^(k-1)`; its directions are dense on the sphere, and the free hit-time scale supplies arbitrary radial rescaling. Therefore `E_k` itself is dense in the whole frequency-difference space. Since it has measure zero, its complement is dense too, so `partial E_k=R^(k-1)` and the exact-hit predicate is discontinuous at every frequency vector.

This creates two different zero margins. Rational independence already gives zero **value** margin, `inf_t |F_nu(t)|=0`, because the torus orbit approaches the phase-zero locus. AF-309 shows that the exact-hit classification also has zero **frequency** margin: every frequency vector is arbitrarily close to systems that hit zero exactly and to systems that do not. Exact zero-freeness can therefore coexist with both kinds of maximal instability.

Prime-log differences are rationally independent by unique factorization, so every fixed prime support has zero value margin. AF-307 settles exact incidence negatively for `k=3,4`, yet AF-309 shows those zero-free prime vectors still have exact-hit systems arbitrarily close in ambient frequency space. For `k>=5`, generic measure, topology, perturbation and near-zero numerics are all incapable of deciding membership. The missing theorem must use the exact arithmetic placement of `(log(p_j/p_k))` inside the dense null cone.

**Boundary.** Density and nullity are statements in the ambient continuous frequency space. The nearby hit/safe vectors need not themselves be prime-log vectors. Nothing here classifies unequal weights, nonzero real part, nonlinear/vector observations, infinite supports, or exact prime incidence for `k>=5`.
