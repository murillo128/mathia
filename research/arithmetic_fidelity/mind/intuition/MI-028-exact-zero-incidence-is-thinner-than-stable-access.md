# MI-028 — Exact-zero incidence is thinner than stable access, algebraic separation and algebraic time admissibility

**Evidence level:** exact for the ambient saturated equal-weight incidence geometry through [AF-308](../../findings/AF-308-exact-zero-frequency-incidence-is-a-null-cone.md)--[AF-309](../../findings/AF-309-exact-zero-frequency-incidence-is-dense-and-nowhere-stable.md), and for the eight-prime two-observable boundary through [AF-328](../../findings/AF-328-prime-singular-incidence-is-a-coprime-exponential-sum-common-zero-problem.md)--[AF-330](../../findings/AF-330-exact-prime-algebraic-incidence-forces-exponentially-algebraic-time.md). Exact eight-prime incidence itself remains open.

For saturated equal-weight Dirichlet sums, AF-308--AF-309 separate exact incidence from stable access. The exact-hit frequency set is dense but Lebesgue-null, while rational independence makes the value orbit approach the ambient zero set arbitrarily closely. Exact zero-freeness can therefore coexist with zero value margin and zero frequency margin.

The eight-prime middle-singular target sharpens this distinction. AF-328 rewrites `s_1=e_4=0` as `F(it)=G(it)=0` for two finite exponential sums and proves that the pair is coprime. AF-329 shows that the dense prime-log orbit approaches an ambient zero with arbitrarily small joint defect and that `(F,G)` is nevertheless a proper ideal, so neither a positive joint margin nor a finite exponential-sum Bézout certificate can decide exact incidence.

AF-330 adds a different kind of necessary condition. If the exact prime configuration occurs, the common phase `z=e^{it}` is forced to be algebraic. Thus the surviving incidence problem lives on the thin exponentially algebraic time locus rather than on arbitrary real time. This restriction does not follow from conditioning: the orbit can still approach the singular set arbitrarily closely at generic times while an exact hit, if one exists, must satisfy the extra algebraic constraint.

The reusable hierarchy is therefore strict: common-factor exclusion, unit-ideal separation, positive joint margin, algebraic admissibility of the time parameter and exact pointwise incidence are different currencies. In the present prime problem the first three fail to decide the target, while the fourth narrows but does not settle the fifth.

A successful next theorem must combine the algebraic-time restriction with the multiplicative independence structure of the prime phases strongly enough to force or forbid simultaneous `e_1=e_4=0`, or construct an actual hit. Conditioning, perturbation stability, near-zero numerics, ambient topology and finite exponential-sum factor/ideal theory cannot decide the remaining predicate by themselves.

**Boundary.** Dense near-incidence is not exact incidence, and algebraicity of `e^{it}` is only a necessary condition for an exact hit. AF-330 does not prove that such a nonzero time exists or is impossible. Nothing here settles unequal weights, nonzero real part, infinite supports, or downstream sufficiency of any finite-height margin.
