# MI-028 — Exact-zero incidence is thinner than stable access and common-factor control

**Evidence level:** exact for the ambient saturated equal-weight incidence geometry through [AF-308](../../findings/AF-308-exact-zero-frequency-incidence-is-a-null-cone.md)--[AF-309](../../findings/AF-309-exact-zero-frequency-incidence-is-dense-and-nowhere-stable.md), and for the eight-prime factorization boundary through [AF-328](../../findings/AF-328-prime-singular-incidence-is-a-coprime-exponential-sum-common-zero-problem.md). Exact eight-prime incidence itself remains open.

For a saturated `k`-term equal-weight Dirichlet sum, quotient the common phase and write the frequency differences as `nu in R^(k-1)`. AF-308 identifies the exact-zero condition with incidence of the one-parameter orbit `t nu` with the lifted equilateral-polygon zero locus and proves that the resulting cone `E_k` has Hausdorff dimension at most `k-2`. Hence `E_k` is Lebesgue-null for every `k>=3`.

AF-309 adds the complementary topology. One lifted zero configuration generates the affine lattice `theta+2 pi Z^(k-1)`; its directions are dense on the sphere, and the free hit-time scale supplies arbitrary radial rescaling. Therefore `E_k` itself is dense in the whole frequency-difference space. Since it has measure zero, its complement is dense too, so `partial E_k=R^(k-1)` and the exact-hit predicate is discontinuous at every frequency vector.

This creates two different zero margins. Rational independence already gives zero **value** margin, `inf_t |F_nu(t)|=0`, because the torus orbit approaches the phase-zero locus. AF-309 shows that the exact-hit classification also has zero **frequency** margin: every frequency vector is arbitrarily close to systems that hit zero exactly and to systems that do not. Exact zero-freeness can therefore coexist with both kinds of maximal instability.

AF-328 shows that the eight-prime middle-singular target stays thin even after passing to the classical algebra of exponential sums. For fixed distinct primes, `s_1=e_4=0` at time `t` is exactly `F(it)=G(it)=0`. The prime-frequency sum `F` is irreducible and `(F,G)` is coprime in the full ring of finite exponential sums. Thus there is no hidden common factor whose removal would decide the target. Even a full Shapiro-type common-zero theorem would convert coprimality only into **finiteness** of common zeros, not absence of one isolated imaginary-axis hit.

Prime-log differences are rationally independent by unique factorization, so every fixed prime support has zero value margin. AF-307 settles exact incidence negatively for `k=3,4`; for the eight-prime target, AF-328 now also eliminates repeated-zero/common-factor structure as a sufficient mechanism. Generic measure, topology, perturbation, near-zero numerics, conditioning away from the boundary and common-factor exclusion are therefore all incapable of deciding the remaining predicate. The missing theorem must use the exact pointwise arithmetic placement of the prime-log one-parameter subgroup inside `V(e_1,e_4)`, or derive an exact identity that forbids the intersection.

**Boundary.** Density and nullity are statements in the ambient continuous frequency space; nearby hit/safe vectors need not themselves be prime-log vectors. Coprimality of `F` and `G` does not imply zero-freeness, and conditional or unconditional finiteness of their common-zero set would not imply emptiness. Nothing here settles unequal weights, nonzero real part, infinite supports, or the exact eight-prime incidence itself.
