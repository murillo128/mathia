# MI-018 — Finite sign complexity transfers once harmonic tail cost beats collision loss

**Evidence level:** supported by AF-217--AF-220 and sharpened quantitatively by [AF-338](../../findings/AF-338-harmonic-collision-count-reduces-euler-sign-depth-to-linear-order.md), with the source-side boundary localized through [AF-341](../../findings/AF-341-prime-layer-retains-rh-pole-discriminator-and-leading-sign-complexity.md), [AF-342](../../findings/AF-342-variable-background-prime-exception-budget.md), and [AF-343](../../findings/AF-343-periodic-backgrounds-cannot-reduce-prime-sign-order.md). The observation theorem is fixed-finite-order in the absolutely convergent half-plane; no uniform growing-order total positivity, critical-line transport or stable inverse bound is claimed.

The useful observation currency is not positivity of every minor. It is a **finite sign-variation budget matched to a finite observation order**. AF-217 shows that the Euler-log family is not globally totally positive, while AF-218--AF-220 recover strict sign regularity at fixed order under quantitative sampling geometry.

AF-338 keeps the collision structure discarded by the earlier tail estimate. For a `k x k` minor, let `m_j` be the column harmonic labels, `S(m)=sum_j(m_j-1)` their total excess order, and `X(m)` the number of column pairs carrying unequal labels. Then `X(m)<=(k-1)S(m)`. Equal harmonic labels preserve the first-harmonic Vandermonde zero; only unequal labels can spend an inverse source-gap factor. Consequently, for fixed order `r`, row spacing `h`, and `q_j in [P,AP]`, the full Euler-log sampling matrix is eventually strictly sign-regular through order `r` whenever `sigma>r-1`.

AF-339--AF-340 show that the pole-cancelled zero-sensitive discrepancy `Lambda-1` has ordered sign variation `Theta(P/log P)`, while uncentered `Lambda` is zero-sensitive and sign-simple. Within the constant-background family `Lambda-c`, exact cancellation of the pole at `1` occurs only at `c=1`, so constant centering cannot simultaneously isolate the zero fluctuation and keep a bounded sign certificate.

AF-341 removes the higher-prime-power loophole. The centered prime layer alone still has exactly the zeta-zero poles in `Re(s)>1/2` after its pole at `1` is cancelled, while its ordered sign variation remains `Theta(P/log P)`. The discarded higher-power tail is one-signed and holomorphic in that half-plane, so it carries neither the relevant zero poles nor the leading sign burden.

AF-342 removes the next loophole: allowing an arbitrary variable baseline does not make that burden disappear unless the baseline itself acquires prime-scale exceptional structure. If `a(p)=log p` on primes and `a(n)=0` on composites, then for `h_b=a-b` the sign variation on `[L,U]` obeys

`V(h_b) >= 2 #primes - 2|E_b| - 4|C_b| - O(1)`,

where `E_b` consists of primes with `b(p)>=log p` and `C_b` consists of prime-adjacent composites with `b<=0`. Hence sub-prime-scale residual variation forces `|E_b|+2|C_b|` to be prime-scale. Every eventually positive baseline that stays below `log p` at prime sites retains the full leading variation. If its Dirichlet transform cancels only the pole at `1` and remains holomorphic at zeta zeros in `Re(s)>1/2`, the residual still carries the RH pole discriminator.

AF-343 shows that prime-scale exceptional cardinality cannot be made cheap merely by replacing individual exceptions with a fixed finite-description periodic mask. More generally, if positive composite residual witnesses are syndetic—every sufficiently long fixed block contains a composite `m` with `b(m)>0`—then every positive run of the zero-deleted residual contains only boundedly many primes and

`V(h_b(P),...,h_b(BP)) = Theta_b(P/log P)`.

Every fixed nonnegative periodic background of positive mean has this property. After mean-one normalization its Dirichlet transform cancels the pole at `1` while remaining holomorphic at zeta zeros, so it preserves the RH right-half-plane pole discriminator **and still cannot lower the sign order**. The real escape is therefore not “structured exceptions” in general but a baseline whose negative composite witnesses develop unbounded gaps, whose structural resolution grows with scale, or which neutralizes prime coefficients on a prime-scale set.

So the source obstruction is now an information-placement statement in the currency actually consumed by the sign theorem: **after the main pole is removed, either the residual keeps prime-scale ordered variation or the baseline must carry scale-growing/non-syndetic structure tied to the prime source**. AF-343 still does not price bit, entropy, circuit or description complexity: a short aperiodic rule might generate unbounded witness gaps. The next bridge must price that surviving representation class, prove a growing-order observation theorem, or replace sign variation by another finite source certificate with an audited recovery theorem.

The reusable principle has four independent currencies. On the observation side, price how much leading collision geometry each tail component actually destroys. On the source side, do not infer a bounded certificate from zero sensitivity. At the centering/interface, do not infer that removing a dominant analytic background is free. At the representation level, do not call complexity reduced merely because prime-scale structure has been moved into a baseline; fixed periodic/syndetic structure is now known to leave the same sign-order bill.

**Boundary.** AF-338 is an exact fixed-order sign theorem, not a prime discriminator or uniform inverse theorem. AF-342 prices exceptional sites only for ordered sign variation, and AF-343 excludes fixed-syndetic/periodic repairs without establishing an information-theoretic lower bound on every non-syndetic generator. Their analytic specialization assumes the baseline is zero-pole-blind in `Re(s)>1/2`; a baseline that absorbs the zeta-zero divisor can simplify the residual precisely by losing the discriminator.
