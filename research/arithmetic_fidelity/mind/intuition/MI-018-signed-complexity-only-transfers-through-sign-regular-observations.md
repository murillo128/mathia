# MI-018 — Finite sign complexity transfers only through a source certificate that survives recoding

**Evidence level:** supported by AF-217--AF-220 and sharpened quantitatively by [AF-338](../../findings/AF-338-harmonic-collision-count-reduces-euler-sign-depth-to-linear-order.md), with the source-side boundary localized through [AF-341](../../findings/AF-341-prime-layer-retains-rh-pole-discriminator-and-leading-sign-complexity.md)--[AF-344](../../findings/AF-344-power-supported-backgrounds-trade-sign-density-for-amplitude.md). The observation theorem is fixed-finite-order in the absolutely convergent half-plane; no uniform growing-order total positivity, critical-line transport or stable inverse bound is claimed.

The useful observation currency is not positivity of every minor. It is a **finite sign-variation budget matched to a finite observation order**. AF-217 shows that the Euler-log family is not globally totally positive, while AF-218--AF-220 recover strict sign regularity at fixed order under quantitative sampling geometry.

AF-338 keeps the collision structure discarded by the earlier tail estimate. For a `k x k` minor, let `m_j` be the column harmonic labels, `S(m)=sum_j(m_j-1)` their total excess order, and `X(m)` the number of column pairs carrying unequal labels. Then `X(m)<=(k-1)S(m)`. Equal harmonic labels preserve the first-harmonic Vandermonde zero; only unequal labels can spend an inverse source-gap factor. Consequently, for fixed order `r`, row spacing `h`, and `q_j in [P,AP]`, the full Euler-log sampling matrix is eventually strictly sign-regular through order `r` whenever `sigma>r-1`.

AF-339--AF-341 show that the pole-cancelled zero-sensitive source is not sign-simple. The centered prime layer alone still has exactly the zeta-zero poles in `Re(s)>1/2` after its pole at `1` is cancelled, while its ordered sign variation is `Theta(P/log P)`. Higher prime powers are one-signed and holomorphic in that half-plane, so they carry neither the relevant zero poles nor the leading sign burden.

AF-342 proves that an arbitrary baseline cannot lower that prime-layer variation without reacting on a prime-scale set of prime or prime-adjacent sites. AF-343 then rules out fixed periodic and, more generally, syndetic positive-composite repairs: after mean-one normalization they can remain zero-pole-blind and cancel the main pole, but they still leave `Theta(P/log P)` residual sign variation.

AF-344 changes the interpretation of that obstruction. For any fixed `k>=2`, the prime-independent perfect-power background

`b_k(m^k)=k m^(k-1)`

has residue one at `s=1`, remains holomorphic at the zeta zeros in `Re(s)>1/2`, and reduces the residual zero-deleted sign variation on `[P,BP]` to `O(P^(1/k))`. It does this with only a fixed algebraic rule and without encoding the prime set. The bill has moved instead into support geometry and amplitude: only `asymp P^(1/k)` background sites occur, each with size `asymp P^(1-1/k)`, while their total mass remains `asymp P`.

Therefore **ordered sign variation is not by itself a representation-independent measure of source complexity**. AF-342--AF-343 remain exact lower bounds for their baseline classes, but AF-344 shows that prime-scale exceptional cardinality cannot be promoted to description complexity and that a short rule can trade sign density for dynamic range. Any observation-to-source theorem that uses sign variation must state what prevents this transfer of cost.

The next bridge is consequently joint rather than scalar. One option is a source certificate combining sign variation with amplitude, local concentration or support-gap control and an observation theorem that consumes that joint certificate. Another is a growing-order sign theorem whose constants themselves price the high-amplitude sparse background. A third is a different finite source statistic with an audited recovery theorem. Merely replacing a periodic background by a non-syndetic one is no longer a research direction: AF-344 gives an explicit fixed-rule non-syndetic escape.

**Boundary.** AF-338 is an exact fixed-order sign theorem, not a prime discriminator or uniform inverse theorem. AF-344 does not show that its sparse high-amplitude background is compatible with every downstream observation norm, nor that amplitude concentration is the unique missing resource. It shows only that zero-pole fidelity and low ordered sign variation can coexist under a fixed prime-independent recoding, so any durable source-complexity claim must price the resource into which the sign burden moved.
