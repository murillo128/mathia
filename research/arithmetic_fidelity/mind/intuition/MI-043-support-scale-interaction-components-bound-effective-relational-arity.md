# MI-043 — Support-scale interaction components bound effective relational arity

**Evidence level:** exact synthesis from [AF-372](../../findings/AF-372-finite-order-total-positivity-does-not-determine-laguerre-polya.md), [AF-376](../../findings/AF-376-uniform-separation-defeats-e-polya-frequency-determination.md) through [AF-379](../../findings/AF-379-vanishing-row-mesh-determines-continuous-translation-total-positivity.md), the generator refinements [AF-381](../../findings/AF-381-prime-ratio-minors-determine-translation-total-nonnegativity.md) through [AF-387](../../findings/AF-387-connected-support-does-not-repair-nonstrict-solid-pf3-recognition.md), and the multiscale closure [AF-388](../../findings/AF-388-shrinking-solid-pf3-excludes-quadratic-boundary-contact.md) through [AF-396](../../findings/AF-396-prime-pf3-topology-has-sharp-linear-successor-depth-scale.md).

Nominal access to determinants of every order does not imply genuinely unbounded relational information. For a compact/local translation kernel with support width `w`, the intrinsic graph `G_w(E)` joins sampled rows only when their translates can interact. If its connected components have size at most `r`, every sampled Toeplitz determinant factors into blocks of order at most `r`; AF-377 makes this obstruction survive even when the minimum spacing of `E` vanishes.

The converse resource is finite-pattern closure. AF-379 shows that density of normalized sampled row patterns determines every continuous one-sided translation minor. AF-381 strengthens this to joint normalized row/column patterns: if arbitrary finite row and column configurations can be approximated after one common translation, fully sampled `E x E` minors determine the entire real translation target. Vanishing tail mesh is a sufficient condition, and `E={log p}` has it.

Placement complexity is a different axis from determinant order. AF-382 gives the strongest order-two collapse for strictly positive profiles: logarithms convert adjacent `2x2` inequalities into mixed differences whose rectangle sums generate every sampled order-two inequality, so successor-prime rectangles already determine global `TN_2` on a vanishing mesh. AF-383 gives the unrestricted higher-order baseline: at fixed order `k`, every lattice Toeplitz minor is a nonnegative integer combination of minors with one consecutive row stencil and arbitrary columns. AF-384--AF-385 show that strict lower-order total positivity collapses the remaining placement axis further to solid generators on shrinking meshes.

AF-386 identifies an exact topological repair at order two. For continuous nonnegative `f`, shrinking solid `2x2` generators determine continuum `TN_2` exactly when `{f>0}` is an interval. Local inequalities do not recover the information that two positive pieces belong to one component.

AF-387 shows why that statement cannot simply be copied to order three on one lattice. A connected positive `PF_2` sequence can have every solid order-three minor nonnegative while a gapped order-three minor is negative because a lower-order zero-minor stratum blocks propagation. AF-388--AF-393 then show how much of this alias disappears when the *same smooth profile* is sampled on meshes tending to zero: finite-order boundary contact is excluded, every surviving zero of `q=-(log f)''` is infinitely flat, and on `{q>0}` all finite-window order-three constraints collapse to the single intrinsic differential bound `(log q)''<=2q`.

AF-394 identifies the exact resource that multiscale local analysis still does not recover. For positive `C^4` `f`, `K_f(x,y)=f(x-y)` is `TN_3` iff `q>=0`, the positive-curvature set `{q>0}` is an interval, and `(log q)''<=2q` on that interval. Under the AF-388 shrinking solid hierarchy the first and third conditions are already forced, so **connectedness of positive curvature is the exact residual global datum**. Boundary zeros, including infinitely-flat ones, are harmless when they terminate one positive component; a zero separating two positive components produces a nonlocal negative minor.

AF-395 quantifies the missing relation. There are smooth positive profiles with two separated curvature components for which every order-three sampled minor of any fixed index radius is eventually nonnegative on a vanishing mesh, even though a negative nonlocal minor persists. Thus **continuum resolution and relational reach are different resources**: as the mesh gets finer, a fixed combinatorial stencil probes a smaller physical neighborhood and becomes less capable of seeing component membership.

AF-396 identifies the exact prime-index scale of that missing reach for the AF-395 control profile. The prime number theorem converts a successor radius `R_N` around the `N`th prime into physical log-prime reach `log(1+R_N/N)+o(1)`. Negative continuum witnesses can be chosen with each axis span arbitrarily close to one, while AF-395 guarantees positivity when the combined local spans stay below the component-gap threshold. Consequently every `R_N=o(N)` stencil is eventually blind; any `R_N` with `liminf R_N/N>e-1` detects a negative minor in every sufficiently large tail block; and `limsup R_N/N<e-1` is eventually blind. The equality regime `R_N/N->e-1` remains a conditioning question, not a change of information scale.

The resource ledger is therefore more precise:

- **relational arity:** unrestricted `PF_infinity` still needs unbounded determinant order by AF-372;
- **placement generators:** one-axis placement suffices without strictness by AF-383, while strict lower-order interiority can collapse the full fixed-order placement family to solid generators;
- **support/component topology:** at order two connected positivity support is the exact nonstrict repair, and at smooth multiscale order three connected *positive logarithmic curvature* is the exact residual repair after the local analytic cone has been recovered;
- **lower-order degeneracy:** it is a real fixed-lattice obstruction, but compatible shrinking-mesh data eliminate every finite-jet version and leave only the component topology described by AF-394;
- **continuum resolution:** shrinking mesh recovers the differential cone but does not recover component membership by itself;
- **nonlocal reach:** on prime logs the AF-395 obstruction requires `Theta(N)` successor depth, with critical coefficient `e-1` away from the unresolved equality boundary, unless a separate source theorem supplies the topology directly.

This prevents two opposite mistakes. Raw inequality count is not information: recognition theorems can make most placements redundant. But a small local generator family is not complete merely because arbitrarily fine scales are present. Even after every relevant local differential inequality has been recovered, one global topological discriminator can remain, and refining the grid does not reveal it if permitted relational reach grows too slowly in the intrinsic source geometry.

For prime logs, arithmetic specificity is therefore pushed into provenance of the generator signs and the mechanism supplying component topology or enough linear-in-index reach. A target-complete grid and a local recognition theorem do not make the final positivity arithmetic. A source-selective route must explain why the RH-facing source forces both the sampled hierarchy and the surviving global component relation, rather than assume the target certificate itself.

**Boundary.** AF-383--AF-396 are source-independent recognition and sampling statements. They do not provide any solid-minor sign for a Riemann-associated kernel, do not weaken AF-372's all-order requirement for unrestricted `PF_infinity`, and do not say every RH-relevant observable needs linear prime-index reach. AF-396 proves the sharp reach scale only for the explicit disconnected-curvature control profile and leaves the exact `e-1` boundary unresolved.
