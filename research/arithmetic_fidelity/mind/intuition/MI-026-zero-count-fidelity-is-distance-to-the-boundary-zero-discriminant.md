# MI-026 — Zero-count fidelity is projective distance to failure; source restriction changes the dimension bill only by changing admissible directions

**Evidence level:** proved for finite Dirichlet-polynomial zero-count geometry through AF-300; transfer from acquisition remains conditional on the actual coefficient-orbit error, and no nonlinear arithmetic source model is established.

AF-290 identifies the exact robustness radius for the zero count inside a fixed zero-free Jordan contour `Gamma`: the coefficient-space distance from `b` to the boundary-zero discriminant. AF-295 shows that, because this discriminant is a complex cone,

`dist_gap([b], P Delta) = dist(b,Delta)/||b||`.

Thus the normalized margin is intrinsic projective separation from failure after quotienting global complex amplitude and phase. AF-291 shows that this margin can still collapse with breadth even while eta truncations converge correctly on the contour.

AF-292--AF-294 close positive diagonal Hilbert reweighting, bounded-condition dense Hilbert mixing, and uniformly quasisymmetric nonlinear transport as free repairs. AF-296--AF-297 classify target-radial conformal repair: a finite order-one repair at raw scale `r_N` must explicitly pay reciprocal raw-margin scale or move failure to an infinite-distance boundary.

AF-298 optimizes the natural full-rank anisotropic geometry generated by scalar boundary observations. The leverage identity `int_Gamma K_(mu,N) dmu=N` forces an irreducible normalized `N^(-1/2)` effective-dimension bill even under `D`-optimal weighting.

AF-299 shows why one cannot pay less merely by making that metric singular or quotienting the unrestricted coefficient space. The boundary-evaluation hyperplanes have trivial common kernel, so every nontrivial additive complex-linear quotient sends the failure set onto the whole quotient and every rank-deficient PSD metric has zero failure distance.

AF-300 resolves the remaining complex-affine source-restriction case. If admissible coefficients really lie in `A=a+U`, with `dim_C U=r`, the exact distance to failure is computed using the restricted evaluations `phi_z=L_z|_U`, and the optimal source-relative leverage law becomes `int K_(mu,U)dmu=r`. The best scalar boundary-observation geometry therefore pays `r^(-1/2)`, not `N^(-1/2)`: removing inadmissible directions before observation is a genuine conditioning gain.

But every remaining complex direction in `U` is still target-relevant. For Dirichlet boundary evaluations, `intersection_z ker(phi_z)={0}`. Hence any complex-linear compression of `U` with nonzero kernel again maps the restricted failure set onto the whole quotient; equivalently every rank-`<r` PSD metric on the admissible tangent space has zero failure margin. **Source restriction lowers the dimension bill exactly to intrinsic source dimension and no further.**

The surviving escape is consequently categorical rather than merely dimensional. A better effective dimension must come from an arithmetic admissible set that is not just another complex-affine subspace—real/positive/discrete/multiplicative/nonlinear geometry, a genuine symmetry whose fibers are saturated by the discriminator, or a richer observable that changes the failure arrangement itself. The proof obligation is to show that the admissible fibers cannot hit the boundary-zero discriminant while preserving enough source breadth for the final analytic carrier.

**Boundary.** AF-300 classifies complex-affine source families and complex-linear compressions only. It does not rule out nonlinear or non-complex-linear admissibility, projective symmetries beyond global complex scale, derivative/vector/nonlocal observations, or source-derived coupled information. The `r^(-1/2)` law is a source-relative observation bill, not evidence that an RH-relevant arithmetic source actually has small `r`.
