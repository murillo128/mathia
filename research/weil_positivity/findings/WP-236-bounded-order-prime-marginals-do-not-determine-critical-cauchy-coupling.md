# WP-236 — Bounded-order prime marginals do not determine the critical Cauchy coupling

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + POSITIVE-GRAM-MATCHED-CONTROLS + HIGHER-ORDER-DEPENDENCE-OBSTRUCTION + FREE-MONOID-CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + DECISIVE-NARROWING + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-234` shows that the critical Bost–Connes Gram and the one-dimensional Gamma/log Gram have the same Cauchy law on every one-prime ray but different cross-prime dependence. The underdetermination is stronger than one-prime locality: for every finite number `d` of prime coordinates there are normalized positive-definite kernels that agree **exactly** with the critical Bost–Connes Gram on every valuation difference involving at most `d` primes, while differing on a `(d+1)`-prime state.

Thus no bounded-order collection of prime-marginal restrictions, even together with positivity and the exact critical `p^{-k/2}` ray law, can select the global coupling. A surviving source-selection mechanism must impose a genuinely global/all-orders relation — for example a factorization identity quantified over arbitrary disjoint prime sets, or a nonseparable global incidence/cohomological law — before the Weil sign is read out. This is a matched-control obstruction, not a candidate Weil positivity proof.

## 1. Critical Bost–Connes as the product Cauchy characteristic kernel

Let `P` be a finite set of `r >= 2` primes and put

\[
w_p:=\log p.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `mu` be the centered Cauchy probability measure of scale `1/2`, so

\[
\widehat\mu(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}\,d\mu(x)=e^{-|t|/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

For integers supported on `P`, write

\[
\Delta_p(m,n):=(v_p(m)-v_p(n))w_p.
\tag{3}
\]

By `WP-234`, the critical Bost–Connes Gram is the characteristic kernel of independent copies of `mu`:

\[
G_{\rm BC}(m,n)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac12\sum_{p\in P}|\Delta_p(m,n)|\right)
=\frac{\gcd(m,n)}{\sqrt{mn}}.
\tag{4}
\]

The live question after `WP-234` is whether progressively richer finite-prime restrictions can force that product dependence law, or at least force a unique alternative global coupling.

## 2. An exact pure `r`-prime perturbation preserving every proper marginal

For each `p in P`, set

\[
a_p:=\widehat\mu(w_p)=e^{-w_p/2}=p^{-1/2},
\qquad
h_p(x):=\cos(w_p x)-a_p.
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
\int h_p\,d\mu=0,
\qquad
\|h_p\|_\infty\le 1+a_p.
\tag{6}
\]

Choose a real `epsilon != 0` satisfying

\[
|\epsilon|
\le
\prod_{p\in P}(1+a_p)^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]

Define a probability measure on `R^P` by

\[
\boxed{
 d\pi_\epsilon(\xi)
 =\left(1+\epsilon\prod_{p\in P}h_p(\xi_p)\right)
   \prod_{p\in P}d\mu(\xi_p).
}
\tag{8}
\]

Equation (7) makes the density in (8) nonnegative. Its integral is one because every `h_p` has zero `mu`-mean.

More importantly, **every proper marginal of `pi_epsilon` is exactly the independent product Cauchy marginal**. If `S` is a proper subset of `P`, choose `q in P\S`. Integrating (8) in coordinate `q` kills the perturbation because

\[
\int h_q\,d\mu=0.
\tag{9}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(\pi_\epsilon)_S=\mu^{\otimes S}
\quad\text{for every proper }S\subsetneq P.
}
\tag{10}
\]

This is not merely pairwise independence: for an `r`-prime block, **all `(r-1)`-prime and lower marginals are exactly independent** while the full `r`-way law can differ.

The construction is also globally consistent. Tensor `pi_epsilon` with independent Cauchy coordinates on every prime outside `P`. This gives a probability measure on the full product coordinate space whose finite restrictions satisfy the same statements. No inconsistent family of finite-dimensional copulas is being used.

## 3. The induced positive kernel is invisible to every proper-prime restriction

Define the translation-invariant kernel

\[
K_\epsilon(m,n)
:=
\int
\exp\!\left(i\sum_p \xi_p\Delta_p(m,n)\right)
\,d\pi_\epsilon(\xi),
\tag{11}
\]

with the independent product measure on coordinates outside `P` understood in the global extension above.

Because `pi_epsilon` is a probability measure, `K_epsilon` is normalized and positive definite:

\[
\sum_{j,k}\overline{c_j}c_kK_\epsilon(n_j,n_k)
=
\int
\left|\sum_j c_j e^{i\langle \xi,x(n_j)\rangle}\right|^2
 d\pi_\epsilon(\xi)
\ge0.
\tag{12}
\]

The density in (8) is even in each coordinate, so the kernel is real symmetric as well.

If the valuation difference between `m` and `n` fails to involve at least one prime of `P`, the perturbation term in (11) contains the factor `int h_q dmu=0` for that missing coordinate. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_\epsilon(m,n)=G_{\rm BC}(m,n)
}
\tag{13}
\]

whenever the support of `v(m)-v(n)` does not contain all of `P`. In particular, on the submonoid generated by **any proper subset of `P`**, the whole Gram kernel is exactly the Bost–Connes kernel.

Thus an observer supplied every one-prime, two-prime, ..., `(r-1)`-prime restriction sees no difference at all.

## 4. A closed-form `r`-prime discrepancy

The full interaction is nevertheless nonzero. Let

\[
N_P:=\prod_{p\in P}p.
\tag{14}
\]

At the pair `(N_P,1)`, every selected coordinate has frequency `w_p`. For one coordinate,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int e^{iw_px}h_p(x)\,d\mu(x)
&=
\frac12\bigl(1+\widehat\mu(2w_p)\bigr)
-a_p\widehat\mu(w_p)\\
&=
\frac12(1+p^{-1})-p^{-1}\\
&=
\frac12(1-p^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
K_\epsilon(N_P,1)
=
N_P^{-1/2}
+
\epsilon\,2^{-r}\prod_{p\in P}(1-p^{-1}).
}
\tag{16}
\]

For every admissible `epsilon != 0`, (16) differs from the Bost–Connes value `N_P^{-1/2}`. We therefore have two normalized positive Gram geometries that agree exactly on every proper prime face of the `P`-coordinate lattice and disagree only when the full `r`-prime interaction becomes visible.

This is a finite exact certificate; no limiting, regularization, zero data, or RH assumption enters it.

## 5. No fixed prime-interaction order can identify the global coupling

Fix any finite `d >= 1` and choose `r=d+1` primes. The globally extended `K_epsilon` above satisfies

\[
K_\epsilon(m,n)=G_{\rm BC}(m,n)
\tag{17}
\]

for every pair whose valuation difference involves at most `d` prime coordinates, while (16) supplies a `(d+1)`-prime pair on which the kernels differ.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{positivity + exact agreement on every at-most-`d`-prime restriction}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{the global Bost--Connes coupling}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Since `d` was arbitrary, increasing a local audit from prime rays to pairs, triples, or any other **fixed** prime-interaction order does not remove the dependence ambiguity identified in `WP-234`.

The quantifier matters. Equation (18) is an identifiability obstruction for bounded-order marginal/restriction data. It does **not** say that every local algebraic rule is powerless: a single rule quantified over arbitrary prime sets can carry all-orders content.

## 6. What would be strong enough: arbitrary-partition factorization

There is a useful control on the other side. Suppose a normalized translation-invariant characteristic kernel has the exact critical one-prime factors and also satisfies, for every pair of disjointly supported valuation vectors `u,v`,

\[
\Phi(u+v)=\Phi(u)\Phi(v).
\tag{19}
\]

Repeated use of (19) gives

\[
\Phi((t_p)_p)
=
\prod_p e^{-|t_p|/2},
\tag{20}
\]

so the coupling is exactly the independent Cauchy/Bost–Connes one. Thus **full coprime/disjoint-support factorization really can source-select the product coupling**.

But that does not solve the research mandate. `WP-216` and `WP-232` show that the exact finite Weil ray remains an indefinite defect of this positive Bost–Connes/Poisson Gram. Therefore the current fork is sharper:

- if the source geometry preserves arbitrary-partition Euler/tensor factorization, it is driven back to the Bost–Connes product coupling, whose ordinary positive Gram has the wrong Weil orientation;
- if a viable geometry uses a different mixed-prime coupling, that departure must be forced by a genuinely global source relation rather than inferred from any bounded hierarchy of matching prime marginals.

This does not prove that such a global relation cannot exist. It specifies what information it must add.

## 7. Generalized-prime / free-monoid control

The obstruction is not specific to rational primes. Let a free commutative monoid have finitely many selected generators `g_j` with arbitrary weights `w_j>0`. Put

\[
a_j=e^{-w_j/2},
\qquad
h_j(x)=\cos(w_jx)-a_j.
\tag{21}
\]

The same density

\[
1+\epsilon\prod_{j=1}^r h_j(x_j)
\tag{22}
\]

preserves every proper product-Cauchy marginal, while at the full generator product the characteristic kernel changes by

\[
\epsilon\,2^{-r}\prod_{j=1}^r(1-e^{-w_j}).
\tag{23}
\]

Thus the higher-order ambiguity is universal to weighted free-monoid Cauchy geometry. The arithmetic values `w_p=log p` merely specialize the matched control; they are not what creates the obstruction.

## 8. Prior art and novelty boundary

No historical novelty is claimed for pairwise or `k`-wise independence failing to imply mutual independence, for constructing dependent distributions with prescribed marginals, for Sarmanov/Lancaster-type product perturbations, or for characteristic-function positivity. These are classical probability/statistics ideas.

Relevant audit anchors include H. O. Lancaster, *On statistical independence and zero correlation in several dimensions*, Journal of the Australian Mathematical Society 1 (1960), 492–496, DOI `10.1017/S1446788700026288`; H. O. Lancaster, *The Multiplicative Definition of Interaction*, Australian Journal of Statistics 13 (1971), 36–44, DOI `10.1111/j.1467-842X.1971.tb01240.x`; and D. Sejdinovic, A. Gretton, W. Bergsma, *A Kernel Test for Three-Variable Interactions*, NeurIPS 26 (2013), 1124–1132, arXiv `1306.2281`, which treats Lancaster higher-order interaction and explicitly separates such interaction from lower-order independence information. Modern Sarmanov literature likewise treats product-density perturbations as a standard way to vary dependence while retaining prescribed marginals.

The Mathia-specific delta is the specialization of this classical higher-order interaction mechanism to the **exact critical Cauchy representation of the Bost–Connes Gram from `WP-234`**, together with the closed-form arithmetic discrepancy (16). It proves that not merely prime rays but every bounded order of exact prime-face Gram data can be held fixed while a positive global mixed-prime kernel changes.

A bounded literature search combining Bost–Connes/GCD kernels, Cauchy characteristic representations, higher-order Lancaster interaction, and `k`-wise independence did not surface this exact arithmetic specialization. Absence from that search is not a historical novelty proof.

## 9. Aggressive falsification and scope

**Is `pi_epsilon` a candidate intrinsic Mathia geometry?** No. The functions `h_p` were chosen deliberately to expose a hidden full-support interaction. That makes `pi_epsilon` a matched falsifier, not evidence for a preferred coupling.

**Does the result show that a finite-arity presentation or local algebra can never determine a global law?** No. A recursively applied identity such as arbitrary-partition factorization (19) can have global force despite a short syntactic presentation. The theorem rules out determination from bounded-order **marginal/restriction information**, not every locally stated axiom.

**Does it preserve Euler multiplicativity?** Generally not; that is exactly why the perturbation can differ from the independent coupling. If Euler/tensor factorization is independently forced at all orders, the perturbation is excluded and (20) returns the Bost–Connes kernel.

**Does it provide the finite Weil sign, Gamma term, or polar counterterm?** No. It leaves those unsolved. Its role is to sharpen the gate before those questions: a mixed-prime dependence law must first be source-forced rather than inferred from positivity plus any finite amount of prime-face agreement.

**Could all finite-dimensional marginals determine the process?** Yes. If the full finite-dimensional distribution is specified for every finite prime set consistently, standard product/process uniqueness applies. The obstruction is that for every fixed observation order `d`, there are global controls invisible to all tests at that order.

## Consequence for the live frontier

`WP-234` moved the problem from finding another positive kernel to deriving the cross-prime dependence law. `WP-236` shows that this derivation cannot be certified by checking progressively richer but bounded-order prime faces: pure higher-order dependence can hide beyond every such fixed cutoff while preserving positivity and every tested critical Bost–Connes restriction exactly.

Accordingly, a plausible next construction must expose an **all-orders source law** before positivity is invoked. Arbitrary coprime factorization is one such law but selects the already-insufficient Bost–Connes product geometry. A genuinely new route therefore needs a canonical global relation — nonseparable finite–archimedean incidence, correspondence/cohomology, boundary condition, or another source identity — that determines its mixed-prime dependence without target-fitting and then independently produces the Weil finite, Gamma, and polar pieces with the required sign.
