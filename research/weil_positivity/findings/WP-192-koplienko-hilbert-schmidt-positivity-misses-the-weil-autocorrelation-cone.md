# WP-192 — Koplienko Hilbert–Schmidt positivity is a convexity cone, not the Weil autocorrelation cone

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + KOPLIENKO-SPECTRAL-SHIFT + HILBERT-SCHMIDT-POSITIVITY + CONVEXITY-CONE + WEIL-AUTOCORRELATION-MISMATCH + UNIVERSAL-WIDE-SUPPORT-NEGATIVITY + GENERALIZED-SCATTERING-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + NOT-WEIL-POSITIVITY`.

`WP-188` rules out the most direct trace-class Birman–Krein escape: the exact archimedean Gamma phase is too large at infinity to be an ordinary `L^1` Krein spectral-shift scattering determinant, even though a positive trace-class perturbation would have supplied a genuine independent sign theorem. The next canonical perturbative category is Hilbert–Schmidt rather than trace class. There the Koplienko spectral shift function is itself nonnegative, so at first sight it is an unusually attractive candidate for the missing source-forced positivity.

That positivity is real but it is positivity for the wrong test-function cone. In the standard bounded self-adjoint Koplienko setup, let

\[
A=A^*,\qquad B=B^*,\qquad X:=A-B\in\mathcal S_2,
\tag{1}
\]

and let `eta=eta_{A,B}` be the Koplienko spectral shift function. The classical second-order trace formula is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{A,B}(f)
:=\operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(f(A)-f(B)-Df_B[X]\bigr)
=\int_{\mathbb R}f''(\lambda)\eta(\lambda)\,d\lambda,
}
\tag{2}
\]

with

\[
\eta\ge0,
\qquad
\eta\in L^1(\mathbb R).
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every convex admissible `f` has `K_{A,B}(f)>=0`. But a Weil test is not selected by convexity: in logarithmic coordinates the relevant positive test cone is generated by autocorrelations

\[
h=g*\widetilde g,
\qquad
\widetilde g(x)=\overline{g(-x)}.
\tag{4}
\]

For every nonzero `g in C_c^infty(R)`, sufficiently wide dilations of this autocorrelation make the Koplienko remainder **strictly negative for every nontrivial Hilbert–Schmidt pair**. More precisely, if

\[
g_L(x)=L^{-1/2}g(x/L),
\qquad
h_L=g_L*\widetilde g_L,
\tag{5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}
L^2\mathcal K_{A,B}(h_L)
=-\frac12\,\|g'\|_2^2\,\|A-B\|_2^2.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently, if `A!=B`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{A,B}(h_L)<0
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }L.
}
\tag{7}
\]

This closes a precise route left open after `WP-188`: a bare Koplienko second-order trace remainder cannot be the global Weil quadratic functional on the full autocorrelation test class, no matter how the nontrivial Hilbert–Schmidt pair is chosen. The obstruction is arithmetic-free and precedes any attempt to fit prime or Gamma data.

## 1. The positive Koplienko density has a fixed nonzero mass

For the bounded self-adjoint pair (1), the Koplienko trace formula (2) is classical. Its positivity is not a formal convention: the KoSSF can be chosen nonnegative and integrable. The total mass is forced exactly by the perturbation norm.

Take

\[
f(\lambda)=\frac12\lambda^2.
\tag{8}
\]

The Fréchet derivative at `B` in the self-adjoint direction `X` is

\[
Df_B[X]=\frac12(BX+XB),
\tag{9}
\]

while `A=B+X`. Hence

\[
\frac12A^2-\frac12B^2-Df_B[X]
=\frac12X^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Taking the trace and applying (2) gives

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R}\eta(\lambda)\,d\lambda
=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(X^2)
=\frac12\|X\|_2^2.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Therefore a nontrivial pair has strictly positive KoSSF mass. Conversely, `eta=0` forces `X=0`; the only way to evade the asymptotic obstruction below by removing the mass is to remove the perturbation itself.

Equation (11) also gives a useful matched control. If `f''>=0`, then (2)--(3) really do imply

\[
\mathcal K_{A,B}(f)\ge0.
\tag{12}
\]

The sign theorem is genuine. The question is whether the Weil autocorrelation cone lies inside this convexity cone. It does not.

## 2. Every nontrivial compactly supported autocorrelation is concave at the origin

Let `0!=g in C_c^infty(R)` and define `h` by (4). Then

\[
h(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\overline{g(x-t)}\,dx.
\tag{13}
\]

Differentiating twice and integrating by parts,

\[
\begin{aligned}
h''(0)
&=\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\overline{g''(x)}\,dx\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb R}|g'(x)|^2\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

A nonzero compactly supported smooth function cannot have `g'=0` identically, so

\[
\boxed{
h''(0)=-\|g'\|_2^2<0.}
\tag{15}
\]

This is not a pathological choice of Weil test. It is the universal local shape of every nontrivial compactly supported autocorrelation: the correlation has its maximum at zero and is strictly concave there.

The `L^2`-normalized dilation in (5) preserves this structure while widening the support. A direct change of variables gives

\[
h_L(t)=h(t/L),
\qquad
h_L''(t)=L^{-2}h''(t/L).
\tag{16}
\]

Each `h_L` is again a smooth compactly supported autocorrelation and hence remains in the same Weil test cone.

## 3. Wide autocorrelations force the Koplienko remainder negative

Substitute (16) into (2):

\[
L^2\mathcal K_{A,B}(h_L)
=\int_{\mathbb R}h''(\lambda/L)\eta(\lambda)\,d\lambda.
\tag{17}
\]

Since `h''` is bounded and continuous and `eta in L^1`, dominated convergence applies. Using (15) and (11),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{L\to\infty}L^2\mathcal K_{A,B}(h_L)
&=h''(0)\int_{\mathbb R}\eta(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&=-\|g'\|_2^2\,\frac12\|X\|_2^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

which is exactly (6). If `X!=0`, the limit is strictly negative, proving (7).

The quantifiers are important. This is not an example of one badly chosen Hilbert–Schmidt pair. For **every** nonzero bounded self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt perturbation and for **every** nonzero compactly supported smooth seed `g`, the corresponding family of dilated Weil autocorrelations eventually gives a negative Koplienko remainder.

Thus no source-specific spectral engineering of the pair can rescue the bare functional (2) on the full global Weil cone. Before arithmetic enters, second-order spectral-shift positivity and Weil autocorrelation positivity already point in different directions.

## 4. Why the positive KoSSF is not a hidden arithmetic selector

The classical theory makes the mismatch sharper. Gesztesy, Pushnitski, and Simon prove that every nonnegative Riemann-integrable compactly supported function occurs as the KoSSF of some Hilbert–Schmidt pair. Hence the condition

\[
\eta\ge0
\tag{19}
\]

is extremely flexible: it does not by itself select the finite-prime measure, the Gamma term, the pole contribution, or any arithmetic normalization. A proposed Mathia pair would still have to derive those data from its intrinsic geometry.

The same paper relates the KoSSF to the modified determinant through a second-order Cauchy transform and also exhibits Hilbert–Schmidt pairs for which the corresponding `det_2` quotient lacks nontangential boundary values. This is a useful control against treating the passage from trace class to Hilbert–Schmidt as a harmless enlargement of `WP-188`: the category change supplies a positive second-order density, but it does not automatically supply an ordinary scattering phase with good boundary behavior.

This result is distinct from `WP-178`--`WP-179`. Those findings classify the **boundary phase** inherited by modified Fredholm determinants of passive unitary paths. The present obstruction concerns the **self-adjoint Koplienko second-order trace remainder** itself. The common lesson is narrower than a general rejection of regularization: the regularized object must inherit the right sign on the right test cone, not merely carry some positive density somewhere in its construction.

## 5. Aggressive falsification and exact scope

**A special nonzero pair cannot rescue the bare remainder.** Equation (18) depends on the pair only through the positive mass `||A-B||_2^2/2`. Spectral support, multiplicity, sign of `A-B`, and detailed shape of `eta` do not affect the eventual negative sign.

**The trivial pair does not help.** If `A=B`, then `eta=0` and `K_{A,B}=0`; there is no finite-prime, archimedean, pole, or global arithmetic content to identify.

**Compact-window positivity is not contradicted.** The dilation `L->infinity` expands the support of the Weil test. A theorem restricted to one fixed small support window can therefore coexist with this obstruction. The global Weil criterion, however, requires the full admissible test class, so a global identification with (2) fails.

**A source-forced counterterm can change the conclusion, but it is new mechanism.** One may consider

\[
\mathcal K_{A,B}(h)+\mathcal C(h),
\tag{20}
\]

or a finite--archimedean coupled functional in which the KoSSF remainder is only one block. Such an additional term could overwhelm (18). It must then be generated intrinsically, reproduce the exact finite-prime/Gamma/polar structure, and carry an independent global sign theorem. Positivity of `eta` alone no longer proves positivity of the assembled form.

**A source-forced transform of the test function is also new mechanism.** Replacing `h` in (2) by a nonlinear or nonlocal `F_h` chosen so that `F_h''>=0` can restore the Koplienko sign, but the map `h -> F_h` must be canonical and arithmetic-compatible. Choosing it because it converts the Weil cone into the convex cone is a repackaging, not an independent explanation.

**Unbounded/domain-changing and more singular pairs remain outside the claim.** The proof is stated in the standard bounded self-adjoint KoSSF setup. Generalized spectral-shift theories for unbounded or resolvent-comparable pairs, renormalized traces, changing domains, indefinite spaces, and genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean geometries can alter the analytic category. Any such route must re-establish its own sign theorem rather than inheriting (3) by analogy.

## 6. Prior art and novelty boundary

The operator theory used here is classical, not a Mathia discovery.

- L. S. Koplienko introduced the second-order trace formula for Hilbert–Schmidt perturbations in *Trace formula for perturbations of nonnuclear type*, Siberian Mathematical Journal 25 (1984), 735--743.
- Fritz Gesztesy, Alexander Pushnitski, and Barry Simon, *On the Koplienko spectral shift function. I. Basics*, Journal of Mathematical Physics, Analysis and Geometry 4 (2008), no. 1, 63--107; arXiv: `0705.3629`, reviews the self-adjoint theory, proves nonnegativity and integrability of the KoSSF, proves that every nonnegative compactly supported Riemann-integrable profile occurs, and studies the associated modified Fredholm determinant.

A targeted audit of the KoSSF, higher-order trace-formula, and spectral-shift literature found the classical convexity theorem and the flexibility results above, but not the specific branch consequence (6)--(7): **every nontrivial KoSSF pair is negative on sufficiently dilated Weil autocorrelations when the KoSSF is used through its canonical second-order trace remainder**. The durable delta is therefore this exact cone-mismatch/no-go at the `weil_positivity` frontier, not a new spectral-shift theorem.

`WP-188` establishes that the exact Gamma carrier does not belong to the ordinary trace-class `L^1` Birman–Krein determinant category. `WP-192` now shows that the first canonical nontrace-class positivity replacement does not solve the problem either: Hilbert–Schmidt KoSSF positivity is **convexity positivity**, whereas Weil asks for positivity on an autocorrelation/positive-definite cone. These are mathematically different order structures.

## Consequence for `weil_positivity`

The Hilbert–Schmidt spectral-shift route has a genuine independent positivity theorem, but its canonical scalar functional fails the required global test cone for every nontrivial pair. Therefore the next viable generalized/renormalized scattering mechanism cannot merely say “the spectral-shift density is positive.” It must explain why the **assembled** Mathia functional is positive on Weil autocorrelations after all finite-prime, archimedean, and polar terms are produced.

This narrows the surviving frontier toward a source-forced counterterm/quotient/compression, a more singular or domain-changing geometry, or a genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean construction in which positivity is proved only after the global coupling. No global Weil positivity statement and no RH consequence is obtained here.
