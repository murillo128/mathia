# WP-024 — modular scattering on the Prime-Circle base has completed zeta but not Weil positivity

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` + `PRIOR-ART-REDIRECTION` for the direct route

```text
Prime-Circle universal base B
  -> canonical full-symmetry modular scattering channel
  -> completed-zeta scattering coefficient
  -> Maass-Selberg / self-adjoint geometric positivity
  -> global Weil positivity.
```

This route is unusually informative because, unlike most earlier candidates, one and the same canonical scattering response really does contain both the finite-prime Euler data and the archimedean Gamma factor. The failure is therefore not “the geometry only sees primes” or “the Gamma factor was inserted separately.” The failure occurs at the sign and parameter-matching step: scattering unitarity is unconditional, the Riemann zeros appear as off-physical-line resonances, and extracting the zeta-bearing logarithmic derivative from the positive Maass-Selberg norm requires subtracting boundary terms. In fact the renormalized scattering density is already strictly negative at the symmetry center.

No novelty is claimed for modular uniformization, Eisenstein scattering, the Maass-Selberg relation, or the realization of zeta zeros as modular-surface resonances. Those are classical. The durable Mathia consequence is that the fixed thrice-punctured base isolated by `PC-040` supplies a remarkably close matched control for the desired mechanism, and that control fails for an exact reason.

## 1. The universal Prime-Circle base has a canonical modular channel

`PC-040` and `PC-042` reduce the full-root Prime-Circle covers to the fixed hyperbolic base

\[
B=\widehat{\mathbf C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}.
\]

Classically,

\[
\boxed{
B\simeq \Gamma(2)\backslash\mathbb H,
}
\]

with the modular lambda function as uniformizing map. Moreover

\[
\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)/\operatorname{P}\Gamma(2)
\simeq \operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_2)
\simeq S_3,
\]

which is exactly the full permutation group of the three punctures of `B`.

Therefore the `S_3`-invariant scalar channel of `L^2(B)` is canonically the lift of the scalar automorphic channel on the modular orbifold

\[
X(1)=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)\backslash\mathbb H.
\]

This is the most favorable possible scalar scattering reduction of the Prime-Circle base: it adds no arbitrary potential and uses only the complete hyperbolic metric plus the full finite automorphism symmetry of the three-punctured sphere.

There is an important boundary to this statement. The Prime-Circle cyclic-cover local systems distinguish the punctures through their monodromy, so the `S_3` projection is not claimed to preserve all marked Prime-Circle information. The claim is only that `B` itself intrinsically contains this canonical full-symmetry channel, making it a legitimate best-case test of whether ordinary hyperbolic scattering positivity can produce the required global sign.

## 2. One geometric response contains both Gamma and every prime power

For the standard modular Eisenstein series, write the constant term as

\[
E(z,s)=y^s+\phi(s)y^{1-s}+\cdots.
\]

Let

\[
\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(u)
:=
\pi^{-u/2}\Gamma(u/2)\zeta(u).
\]

Then the scalar scattering coefficient is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\phi(s)
=
\frac{\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(2s-1)}{\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(2s)}
=
\sqrt\pi\,
\frac{\Gamma(s-1/2)}{\Gamma(s)}
\frac{\zeta(2s-1)}{\zeta(2s)}.
}
\]

This passes a test that many Mathia candidates fail: **finite-prime and archimedean information are generated by the same geometric intertwining/scattering coefficient.** The Gamma factor is not appended after the fact.

In the absolutely convergent region `Re(s)>1`, differentiating gives

\[
\frac{\phi'}{\phi}(s)
=
\psi(s-1/2)-\psi(s)
+2\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2s-1)
-2\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2s),
\]

so that

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{\phi'}{\phi}(s)
=
\psi(s)-\psi(s-1/2)
+2\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)
\bigl(n^{-(2s-1)}-n^{-2s}\bigr).
}
\]

Equivalently, the finite term is

\[
2\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)(n-1)n^{-2s}.
\]

Thus a single hyperbolic boundary response produces an archimedean digamma difference and a full von-Mangoldt prime-power series. This is substantially closer to the requested local-to-global structure than a bare spectral zeta or a spectrum defined from zeros.

But it is **not** the Weil finite distribution. For example, forcing the numerator to carry the Riemann-critical exponent by taking

\[
s=\frac34+\frac{it}{2}
\]

gives

\[
n^{-(2s-1)}=n^{-1/2-it},
\qquad
n^{-2s}=n^{-3/2-it},
\]

so every desired critical-weight term comes with an additional shifted tail. The scattering geometry naturally produces a *ratio of two shifted completed zetas*, not one completed zeta evaluated on the Weil axis.

## 3. The physical scattering line misses both Riemann-critical loci

Self-adjoint hyperbolic scattering is unitary on

\[
\boxed{\Re s=\frac12.}
\]

The two zeta arguments in `phi` are `2s-1` and `2s`. If a Riemann-critical value

\[
u=\frac12+i\tau
\]

is put into the denominator argument `u=2s`, then

\[
\Re s=\frac14.
\]

If it is put into the numerator argument `u=2s-1`, then

\[
\Re s=\frac34.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{modular scattering unitarity line }\Re s=1/2
\text{ lies between the two shifted Riemann-critical lines }1/4,3/4.
}
\]

This is not a cosmetic reparameterization. If `rho` is any nontrivial zero of `zeta`, then

\[
\zeta(\rho)=0
\]

produces, modulo cancellations not present generically,

\[
\boxed{
\text{a pole of }\phi\text{ at }s=\rho/2,
\qquad
\text{a zero of }\phi\text{ at }s=(\rho+1)/2.
}
\]

Under RH these lie on `Re(s)=1/4` and `Re(s)=3/4`, respectively. If RH were false they would move horizontally, but they would still remain on opposite sides of the physical line because every nontrivial zero satisfies `0<Re(rho)<1`.

The completed-zeta functional equation gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
\phi(s)\phi(1-s)=1.
}
\]

Together with the reality symmetry, this implies

\[
\boxed{
|\phi(1/2+it)|=1
}
\]

where the scattering coefficient is regular. This unitarity is unconditional. It is therefore fully compatible with hypothetical zeros of `zeta` away from `Re(rho)=1/2`.

This is the decisive matched control against a Hilbert-Polya-style inference from scattering unitarity: **self-adjointness constrains the physical scattering axis, while the zeta zeros live as resonances away from that axis.** Classical modular-surface scattering already exhibits exactly this separation.

## 4. Maass-Selberg gives a genuine positive norm, but the arithmetic term is obtained by subtraction

There is nevertheless a real positive geometric theorem here. For the truncated modular Eisenstein series at

\[
s=\frac12+it,
\qquad t\ne0,
\]

the Maass-Selberg relation gives, with truncation height `C`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\Lambda^C E(\cdot,1/2+it)\right\|_2^2
={}&2\log C
-\frac{\phi'}{\phi}(1/2+it)\\
&+
\frac{\overline{\phi(1/2+it)}C^{2it}
-\phi(1/2+it)C^{-2it}}{2it}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The left-hand side is an honest nonnegative Hilbert-space norm. This is therefore an exact test of the desired pattern “geometry first, positivity independently, arithmetic in a local-to-global response.”

But solving for the zeta-bearing term gives

\[
-\frac{\phi'}{\phi}
=
\|\Lambda^C E\|_2^2
-2\log C
-\text{oscillatory boundary term}.
\]

The positive bulk norm does **not** transfer its sign to `-phi'/phi`: one must subtract the divergent positive boundary contribution and a boundary interference term with no fixed sign. This is not an arbitrary regularization invented for Mathia; it is the canonical Maass-Selberg decomposition of the classical self-adjoint scattering problem.

So the route reproduces the same structural obstruction encountered elsewhere in this research line in a much stronger matched setting:

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive geometric quantity}
\;=\;
\text{arithmetic response}
+\text{counterterms},
}
\]

but the counterterms are exactly what prevent the arithmetic response itself from inheriting positivity.

## 5. Exact center test: the renormalized scattering density is negative

The failure of sign can be seen without numerical spectral data.

Near `u=1`, the completed zeta has Laurent expansion

\[
\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(1+\varepsilon)
=
\frac1\varepsilon+c_{\mathbb Q}+O(\varepsilon),
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
c_{\mathbb Q}=\frac{\gamma-\log(4\pi)}2.}
\]

Using the functional equation,

\[
\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(\varepsilon)
=
\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(1-\varepsilon)
=
-\frac1\varepsilon+c_{\mathbb Q}+O(\varepsilon).
\]

Putting `s=1/2+h` in the scattering ratio gives

\[
\phi(1/2+h)
=
\frac{-1/(2h)+c_{\mathbb Q}+O(h)}
     { 1/(2h)+c_{\mathbb Q}+O(h)}
=
-1+4c_{\mathbb Q}h+O(h^2).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{\phi'}{\phi}(1/2)
=4c_{\mathbb Q}
=2\bigl(\gamma-\log(4\pi)\bigr)
<0.
}
\]

So even the most obvious renormalized scalar “time-delay / continuous spectral density” candidate is **strictly negative at the exact scattering symmetry center**. Positivity of the truncated Eisenstein norm cannot be reinterpreted as pointwise positivity of this arithmetic response.

This center computation is a particularly useful falsification check because it depends only on the classical Laurent expansion and functional equation, not on any numerical knowledge of Riemann zeros.

## 6. Prior-art audit: this is classical scattering, not a new Mathia RH mechanism

The route has very strong prior art.

- The uniformization `Gamma(2)\H ~= P^1\{0,1,infinity}` and its `S_3` quotient to the modular orbifold are classical modular-lambda geometry; a standard reference is Tom M. Apostol, *Modular Functions and Dirichlet Series in Number Theory*, 2nd ed., Springer GTM 41 (1990), DOI `10.1007/978-1-4612-0999-7`.
- Henryk Iwaniec, *Spectral Methods of Automorphic Forms*, 2nd ed., AMS/RMI Graduate Studies in Mathematics 53 (2002), is a standard source for modular Eisenstein series, the scattering coefficient, functional equation, and Maass-Selberg relations.
- Xiaoqing Li, *Lower bounds for zeta(1+it) and GL(3)*, The Ramanujan Journal 68 (2025), Article 43, DOI `10.1007/s11139-025-01202-7`, equations (2.1)–(2.2), explicitly records
  `phi(s)=Lambda(2s-1)/Lambda(2s)` and the truncated GL(2) Maass-Selberg norm used above.
- Maciej Zworski, *Mathematical study of scattering resonances*, Bulletin of Mathematical Sciences 7 (2017), 1–85, DOI `10.1007/s13373-017-0099-4`, explicitly uses the modular surface as a basic example where Riemann-zeta zeros are scattering resonances and RH becomes a statement about their decay rates.
- Yoichi Uetake, *The Lax-Phillips infinitesimal generator and the scattering matrix for automorphic functions*, Annales Polonici Mathematici 92 (2007), 99–122, DOI `10.4064/ap92-2-1`, constructs an operator whose eigenvalues are the nontrivial zeta zeros from modular automorphic scattering and gives an RH-equivalent condition. This directly blocks any novelty claim based merely on obtaining a scattering operator whose poles encode the zeros.

Thus the striking appearance of the completed Riemann zeta function from the Prime-Circle base is a **prior-art redirection**, not evidence that Mathia has discovered a new spectral proof. The useful result is the exact comparison between that classical mechanism and the stronger positivity requirement imposed by this research line.

## 7. What is ruled out, and what remains open

This finding rules out the direct claim

\[
\boxed{
\text{canonical scalar modular scattering positivity on }B
\Longrightarrow
\text{global Weil positivity}.
}
\]

It also rules out treating either of the following as the missing theorem:

- unitarity of the scattering coefficient on `Re(s)=1/2`;
- positivity of the ordinary truncated Eisenstein `L^2` norm;
- the bare fact that the same scattering coefficient contains `Gamma` and `zeta`;
- the realization of zeta zeros as scattering resonances.

The obstruction is precise: the completed zeta enters as the **shifted quotient**

\[
\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(2s-1)/\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(2s),
\]

its Riemann-critical zeros are resonances on `Re(s)=1/4` and `3/4` under RH rather than on the unitary line, and the zeta-bearing logarithmic derivative inherits no sign from Maass-Selberg positivity.

This does **not** rule out:

- nontrivial `Gamma(2)` cusp channels or unitary local-system twists that are not `S_3` invariant;
- a marking-sensitive combination forced by the actual Prime-Circle cyclic covers rather than by the full-symmetry quotient;
- a relative/compressed/cohomological scattering construction with an additional independent sign theorem;
- a nonlinear operation that couples the two shifted completed-zeta factors for a geometrically forced reason;
- coupling the Prime-Circle scattering base to the exact finite-prime selector of `WP-018`;
- a test-function-level trace or relative-scattering construction that matches the Weil explicit formula exactly rather than only the scalar logarithmic derivative.

Any surviving scattering route must therefore do substantially more than “find zeta in the scattering matrix.” It must explain **why a particular relative/compressed pairing has a sign**, and why its local-to-global decomposition matches the Weil functional rather than the standard shifted Eisenstein quotient.

## 8. Audit and falsification tests

The decisive statements are independently checkable:

1. verify the classical uniformization `B ~= Gamma(2)\H` and the quotient `PSL_2(Z)/PGamma(2) ~= S_3`;
2. compute the modular Eisenstein constant term and recover exactly `phi(s)=Lambda_Q(2s-1)/Lambda_Q(2s)`;
3. differentiate in `Re(s)>1` and verify the digamma plus von-Mangoldt difference displayed above;
4. map a nontrivial zeta zero `rho` to the scattering pole `rho/2` and zero `(rho+1)/2`;
5. use `Lambda_Q(u)=Lambda_Q(1-u)` to check `phi(s)phi(1-s)=1` and hence unconditional unitary modulus on `Re(s)=1/2`;
6. check the Maass-Selberg identity and observe that isolating `-phi'/phi` requires subtracting `2 log C` and the oscillatory boundary term;
7. expand `Lambda_Q` at `0` and `1` to obtain exactly `-phi'/phi(1/2)=2(gamma-log(4pi))<0`.

Failure of the modular uniformization, the scattering ratio, or the Maass-Selberg formula would invalidate the route classification. No claim is made that every relative scattering construction on `B`, every nontrivial cusp channel, or every coupling of Prime Circle to Prime Lattice has been ruled out.
