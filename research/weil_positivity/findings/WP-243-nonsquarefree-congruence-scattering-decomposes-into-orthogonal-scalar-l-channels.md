# WP-243 — Nonsquarefree congruence scattering decomposes into orthogonal scalar L-channels rather than coupled Riemann arithmetic

**Status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NONSQUAREFREE-EISENSTEIN-NEWFORM-DECOMPOSITION + ORTHOGONAL-SCALAR-L-CHANNELS + RIEMANN-BEARING-QUADRATIC-CHANNEL-CENTRALITY + P-SQUARED-MATCHED-CONTROL + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-242` closes the canonical squarefree many-cusp escape from the scalar-normalizer obstruction, but deliberately leaves nonsquarefree level open: square factors can support genuinely new Eisenstein character sectors, so perhaps the resulting scattering system contains several global analytic normalizers whose matrix interaction separates the Riemann zero divisor from the finite-prime and archimedean pieces before positivity is taken.

The standard congruence Eisenstein theory does introduce several global normalizers at nonsquarefree level, but in the wrong way. Young's arbitrary-level newform decomposition splits the continuous spectrum orthogonally into oldclasses indexed by primitive character pairs. Each primitive pair carries one scalar Dirichlet-`L` completion, and the completed functional equation only permutes/conjugates those pair labels. For trivial nebentypus, every Riemann-zeta-bearing nontrivial new sector is forced to come from a quadratic character and is an orthogonal self-channel whose global factor is the same completed zeta package, modified only by conductor and finitely many deleted Euler factors. The extra nonsquarefree channels therefore supply **parallel scalar `L`-functions**, not a source-defined noncommutative coupling of the Riemann arithmetic.

Consequently ordinary `Gamma_0(N)` cusp multiplicity, even at arbitrary nonsquarefree level, does not by itself realize the escape left by `WP-242`. The natural positive spectral/Parseval structure respects the orthogonal character-newform decomposition. To obtain the required Riemann-Weil sign from this setting one would need an additional geometrically canonical operator that couples distinct Eisenstein oldclasses **before** positivity/scalarization. Without such an operator, changing back to the cusp basis merely hides a reducible direct sum, while signed combinations that cancel unwanted common terms abandon the inherited positivity.

## 1. Arbitrary-level Eisenstein space has a canonical character-newform decomposition

Let `Gamma_0(N)` have weight `k` and nebentypus `psi`. For primitive Dirichlet characters `chi_1` modulo `q_1` and `chi_2` modulo `q_2`, Young's Eisenstein newform

\[
E_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(z,s)
\tag{1}
\]

has level `q_1q_2` and nebentypus

\[
\chi_1\overline{\chi_2}.
\tag{2}
\]

The arbitrary-level Eisenstein space is generated from such newforms by degeneracies `E_{chi_1,chi_2}(Bz,s)` with `Bq_1q_2\mid N`. On the unitary axis `s=1/2+it`, Young's Section 8 makes this decomposition orthogonal. In his notation,

\[
\mathcal E_{t,\psi}(N)
=
\bigoplus_{q_1q_2L=N}
\ \bigoplus_{\substack{\chi_1,\chi_2\ \mathrm{primitive}\\
\chi_1\overline{\chi_2}\sim\psi}}^{*}
\mathcal E_{t,\psi}(L;E_{\chi_1,\chi_2}),
\tag{3}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal E_{t,\psi}(L;E_{\chi_1,\chi_2})
=
\operatorname{span}\{E_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(Bz,1/2+it):B\mid L\}
\tag{4}
\]

is the oldclass generated by one primitive pair. The raw degeneracy vectors in (4) are not generally orthogonal, but Young constructs an orthogonal `D_{chi_1,chi_2,f}` basis inside each oldclass and proves that different primitive character pairs are orthogonal. Explicitly, his Theorem 8.1 gives

\[
\frac1{4\pi}
\langle D_{\chi_1,\chi_2,f_1},D_{\eta_1,\eta_2,f_2}\rangle_{\rm Eis}
=
\delta_{f_1=f_2}\delta_{\chi_1=\eta_1}\delta_{\chi_2=\eta_2}
\,\varphi((f,N/f)).
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the positive Hilbert-space structure that enters the continuous spectral decomposition already comes with a canonical orthogonal separation of distinct global character data. Nonsquarefree cusp multiplicity is not, by itself, irreducible matrix arithmetic.

## 2. Each primitive pair carries one scalar Dirichlet-L completion

Young defines the completed Eisenstein newform by

\[
E^*_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(z,s)
=
A_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(s)E_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(z,s),
\tag{6}
\]

where, up to the displayed constant phase convention,

\[
A_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(s)
=
\frac{(q_2/\pi)^s}{i^{-k}\tau(\chi_2)}
\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{k}{2}\right)
L(2s,\chi_1\chi_2).
\tag{7}
\]

His Proposition 4.2 gives a completed functional equation of the form

\[
E^*_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(z,s)
=
E^*_{\overline{\chi_2},\overline{\chi_1}}(z,1-s).
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently the uncompleted functional equation is scalar on each label, followed only by the fixed label involution

\[
(\chi_1,\chi_2)
\longleftrightarrow
(\overline{\chi_2},\overline{\chi_1}).
\tag{9}
\]

More explicitly,

\[
E_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(z,s)
=
r_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(s)
E_{\overline{\chi_2},\overline{\chi_1}}(z,1-s),
\qquad
r_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(s)
=
\frac{A_{\overline{\chi_2},\overline{\chi_1}}(1-s)}
{A_{\chi_1,\chi_2}(s)}.
\tag{10}
\]

The analytic divisor in a primitive sector therefore comes from a scalar ratio of Dirichlet `L`-functions together with Gamma, conductor and Gauss-sum factors. For a non-real pair, (9) can make a two-dimensional conjugate-pair block, but it is a monomial label exchange rather than a coupling among unrelated `L`-channels. Oldform mixing inside (4) may be `s`-dependent and need not be simultaneously diagonalizable by a constant basis; this finding does **not** claim otherwise. What remains invariant is that the global `L`-normalizer is attached to one primitive oldclass and different primitive pairs are orthogonal in (3).

This is the relevant distinction from the escape sought after `WP-242`: several scalar global functions exist, but standard congruence scattering does not supply an intrinsic operator that makes their arithmetic divisors interfere inside one positive irreducible channel.

## 3. Trivial nebentypus forces the new global sectors onto square conductors

Now specialize to the Riemann target: weight zero and principal nebentypus. Equation (2) requires

\[
\chi_1\overline{\chi_2}=1.
\tag{11}
\]

Because both characters are primitive, (11) means that they are the same primitive character. Hence

\[
q_1=q_2=q,
\qquad
\chi_1=\chi_2=\chi,
\qquad
q^2\mid N.
\tag{12}
\]

This gives a structural explanation of the squarefree result `WP-242`. If `N` is squarefree, (12) forces `q=1`, so all cusp multiplicity comes from oldlevel/local degeneracies of the level-one Eisenstein family. When square factors enter, new character sectors appear, but each one is still an independent oldclass generated by

\[
E_{\chi,\chi}(z,s)
\tag{13}
\]

with scalar completion

\[
A_\chi(s)
=
\frac{(q/\pi)^s}{\tau(\chi)}
\Gamma(s)L(2s,\chi^2)
\tag{14}
\]

up to the fixed weight-zero phase convention.

There are now two qualitatively different cases.

If `chi^2` is principal, then `chi` is quadratic and

\[
L(2s,\chi^2)
=L(2s,\chi_{0,q})
=
\zeta(2s)\prod_{\ell\mid q}(1-\ell^{-2s}).
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the Riemann zeta factor survives only as a **scalar** in an orthogonal quadratic-character channel, accompanied by finitely many conductor-prime Euler deletions. If `chi^2` is nonprincipal, the sector carries a different Dirichlet `L`-function (possibly induced from a lower conductor, with only the corresponding finite Euler modifications), not the Riemann zeta divisor.

The completed functional equation (8) sends `chi` to `\overline\chi`. Quadratic Riemann-bearing sectors are therefore self-channels. Non-real sectors occur in conjugate pairs and carry non-Riemann Dirichlet `L`-data. The nonsquarefree transition creates more global scalar channels, but it does not create cross-character Riemann arithmetic.

## 4. Exact matched control at N=p^2

Let `p` be an odd prime. The principal-nebentypus continuous spectrum at level `p^2` has the orthogonal newform decomposition

\[
\mathcal E_{t,1}(p^2)
=
\mathcal E_t(p^2;E_{1,1})
\ \oplus\!
\bigoplus_{\substack{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ p)\\
\chi\ {\rm primitive}}}
\mathbb C\,E_{\chi,\chi}(\cdot,1/2+it).
\tag{16}
\]

The level-one oldclass is

\[
\mathcal E_t(p^2;E_{1,1})
=
\operatorname{span}\{E(z,s),E(pz,s),E(p^2z,s)\},
\tag{17}
\]

so it has dimension three. Modulo a prime `p`, every nonprincipal character is primitive, giving `p-2` one-dimensional new sectors. Therefore

\[
\dim\mathcal E_{t,1}(p^2)=3+(p-2)=p+1,
\tag{18}
\]

exactly the number of cusps of `Gamma_0(p^2)`, since

\[
\sum_{d\mid p^2}\varphi((d,p^2/d))=1+(p-1)+1=p+1.
\tag{19}
\]

This dimension check leaves no hidden cusp sector outside the oldclass-plus-character decomposition.

For `p=3`, the sole nontrivial primitive character is quadratic. Hence the first nonsquarefree example already contains two orthogonal zeta-bearing sectors: the level-one oldclass and the quadratic newform. But the latter merely replaces `zeta(2s)` by

\[
\zeta(2s)(1-3^{-2s})
\tag{20}
\]

inside its scalar completion. For general odd `p`, the unique quadratic character gives the corresponding zeta-bearing new channel, while higher-order characters contribute other Dirichlet `L`-functions.

This is the decisive matched control. Nonsquarefree level really does add new global analytic data, so the negative result is not caused by pretending that `WP-242`'s squarefree factorization remains literally true. The added data are simply organized as orthogonal scalar `L`-channels.

## 5. The Riemann-bearing channels still cannot split the global zeta/Gamma package

For every quadratic character channel, the logarithmic derivative of the scalar functional-equation coefficient can be written schematically, with all conventions fixed, as

\[
\frac{r_\chi'(s)}{r_\chi(s)}
=
G_\zeta(s)+R_\chi(s),
\tag{21}
\]

where `G_zeta(s)` is the same level-one completed-zeta/Gamma contribution and `R_chi(s)` contains only conductor constants and logarithmic derivatives of the finitely many Euler factors at primes dividing the conductor. The exact finite terms depend on normalization; the structural split follows directly from (14)--(15).

Hence for fixed coefficients `c_chi` on quadratic channels,

\[
\sum_\chi c_\chi\frac{r_\chi'}{r_\chi}
=
\left(\sum_\chi c_\chi\right)G_\zeta
+
\sum_\chi c_\chi R_\chi.
\tag{22}
\]

As in `WP-242`, channel differences can change only the finite ramified corrections. They either retain the common Riemann zeta/Gamma package with one total coefficient or cancel that package wholesale. They do not assign one coefficient to the nontrivial-zero divisor and another to the Gamma/polar constituents already joined in the same completed scalar.

Could nonprincipal character channels cancel the unwanted common Gamma term while leaving zeta? Algebraically one can form signed linear combinations in which a quadratic `zeta'/zeta` contribution is compared with `L'/L` for another character. But then the zero divisor of the other Dirichlet `L`-function remains. Eliminating it would require another exact identity or subtraction, and the natural positive spectral form (5) supplies neither: distinct oldclasses are orthogonal and enter Parseval with positive norm weights. Thus this operation is not an inherited positive projection onto the Riemann zero functional; it is a signed arithmetic recombination imposed after decomposing the spectrum.

## 6. Why changing back to the cusp basis does not restore a hidden positive coupling

The cusp-attached Eisenstein basis is useful for the spectral decomposition, while the character basis exposes the `L`-functions. Young's Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 give explicit changes of basis between them, and Proposition 8.2 states that the continuous-spectrum Parseval contribution is independent of which orthogonal Eisenstein basis is used.

Therefore a dense-looking nonsquarefree scattering matrix in cusp coordinates is not evidence of an invariant cross-character interaction. The basis-invariant positive Hilbert structure can be evaluated in the orthogonal decomposition (3), where distinct primitive character sectors have no cross terms. Returning to cusp coordinates merely conjugates/reexpresses that same positive object.

This leaves a precise possible escape rather than an absolute impossibility statement. A Mathia construction could still supply an **additional** canonical operator `K` that does not preserve the oldclass decomposition,

\[
P_F K P_G\neq0
\qquad(F\neq G),
\tag{23}
\]

and whose positivity or Schur complement has an independent geometric theorem. But `K` would have to be justified before inspecting the desired Riemann sign. The standard Eisenstein scattering, Hecke/newform decomposition, and Parseval norm do not provide such cross-oldclass matrix elements. Choosing an off-diagonal kernel specifically to combine `zeta` with other Dirichlet `L`-channels would be exactly the hand-picked-kernel move excluded by the line mandate.

A moving orthogonalization inside one oldclass is not enough either. It may generate `s`-dependent finite-level matrices, but the global scalar `L(2s,chi_1chi_2)` belongs to that primitive pair as a whole. Such local oldform geometry can rearrange ramified factors; it does not turn unrelated global `L`-divisors into one irreducible positive channel.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty boundary

The Eisenstein theory used here is classical. The explicit source is Matthew P. Young, *Explicit calculations with Eisenstein series*, Journal of Number Theory 199 (2019), 1--48, DOI `10.1016/j.jnt.2018.11.007`, arXiv `1710.03624`. Equation (3.8) and the completed definition before Proposition 4.1 display the scalar `L(2s,chi_1chi_2)` factor; Proposition 4.2 gives the completed functional equation; Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 give the cusp/newform changes of basis; Theorem 8.1 and equations (8.3)--(8.4) give the orthogonal oldclass decomposition. Young explicitly places these formulas in the lineage of Huxley's scattering matrices for congruence subgroups.

No novelty is claimed for Eisenstein newform theory, the principal-character identity (15), or the cusp count (19). The Mathia-specific contribution is the **falsification consequence** for the live Weil-positivity route. `WP-242` left nonsquarefree/multiple-`L` scattering as a possible way to escape a common central scalar. The classical arbitrary-level decomposition shows that the first natural enlargement does not produce the required kind of matrix arithmetic: it replaces one scalar zeta channel by an orthogonal family of scalar Dirichlet-`L` oldclasses, and the Riemann-bearing subfamily still shares the same completed-zeta package up to finite conductor corrections.

This is therefore a prior-art redirect plus an exact structural no-go for **standard congruence Eisenstein/Parseval positivity**, not a new theorem about scattering matrices and not a proof that every conceivable operator on the continuous spectrum must preserve the character decomposition.

## 8. Aggressive falsification and exact scope

**Does nonsquarefree level introduce genuinely new global functions?** Yes. Equation (14) shows that it introduces `L(2s,chi^2)` for primitive character sectors. The finding survives this strongest objection because those functions occur in orthogonal scalar channels rather than as noncommuting entries of one positive arithmetic operator.

**Could the extra quadratic channels provide several differently dressed copies of zeta and therefore separate its constituents?** They provide several copies only when square conductors are available, but (15) shows that the differences are finite Euler deletions and conductor factors. The zeta/Gamma package remains common. Fixed channel differences cancel that common package rather than split its zero and archimedean pieces.

**Could nonquadratic channels be used as counterterms?** They bring different Dirichlet `L` zero divisors. A signed combination may cancel a common Gamma term but does not preserve the natural positive direct-sum structure and leaves the additional zero divisors. No standard source identity turns those extra divisors into the archimedean/global counterterms of the Riemann Weil form.

**Could the cusp basis itself create positive interference?** Parseval positivity is basis-independent and Young gives an orthogonal basis respecting the primitive sectors. Cross terms produced by writing a diagonal positive form in a nonorthogonal coordinate system are coordinate artifacts unless an additional operator makes them invariant.

**Could an `s`-dependent oldform basis evade the argument?** This finding does not assume constant diagonalizability at arbitrary level. The allowed `s`-dependence lies inside one oldclass and can affect finite-level data. The global Dirichlet-`L` label remains attached to the primitive pair, while different primitive pairs remain orthogonal on the unitary axis.

**Could vector-valued or higher-rank scattering evade it?** Yes; that category is not classified here. Genuinely matrix-valued normalizing factors with noncommuting global arithmetic, a canonical cross-oldclass correspondence, or a higher-rank residual/intertwining geometry remain possible escape categories. They must still pass the line's independent-sign and full Weil-normalization gates.

## 9. Consequence for Weil positivity

The standard nonsquarefree congruence enlargement now has a precise classification relative to the search:

\[
\boxed{
\text{square factors}
\Longrightarrow
\text{new primitive character oldclasses}
\Longrightarrow
\text{orthogonal scalar Dirichlet-}L\text{ channels},
}
\tag{24}
\]

not

\[
\boxed{
\text{square factors}
\Longrightarrow
\text{irreducible operator-valued Riemann arithmetic}.
}
\tag{25}
\]

Within the Riemann-bearing quadratic subfamily, the scalar channel has the more restrictive form

\[
\boxed{
\text{completed zeta package}
\times
\text{finite conductor/Euler dressing}.
}
\tag{26}
\]

Thus `WP-242`'s squarefree negative was not an accident of squarefree tensor factorization: the obvious nonsquarefree extension also fails, for a deeper representation-theoretic reason. The continuous spectrum resolves into orthogonal character-newform sectors before the natural positive Hilbert form is evaluated.

The live boundary is correspondingly narrower. A successful Maaß--Selberg/scattering route must now exhibit a source-defined **pre-positivity coupling across primitive oldclasses**, or move to a genuinely vector-valued/higher-rank system whose global normalizing operator is not reducible to independent scalar completed `L`-channels. Merely increasing the number of cusps, adding square factors to the level, or changing between cusp and character coordinates does not supply the missing Weil sign.
