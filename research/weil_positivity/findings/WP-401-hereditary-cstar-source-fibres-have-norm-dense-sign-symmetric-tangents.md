# WP-401 — Hereditary C-star source fibres have norm-dense sign-symmetric tangents

## Claim

`WP-400` leaves a technically important source-algebra loophole. Its canonical localization uses the von Neumann spectral projections

\[
e_n=\mathbf 1_{[1/n,\infty)}(d),
\]

but a concrete source C-star algebra containing `d` need not contain those discontinuous spectral projections. Therefore a source relation defined at the C-star level can fail the exact cutoff-stability hypothesis of `WP-400` for a purely category-theoretic reason, even when no genuinely nonlocal arithmetic structure is present.

Continuous functional calculus closes that loophole on the hereditary source corner, and it gives a stronger topology.

Let `A` be a C-star algebra, let `B subseteq A` be a C-star subalgebra, and let

\[
E:A\to B
\]

be a conditional expectation, so in particular `E` is `B`-bimodular. Fix `d in B_+` and let

\[
H_d=\operatorname{Her}_A(d)=\overline{dAd}
\tag{1}
\]

be the hereditary C-star subalgebra generated by `d`. Define the centered hereditary direction space

\[
V_d=\{k\in (H_d)_{\rm sa}:E(k)=0\}
\tag{2}
\]

and its fixed-shadow positive fibre

\[
S_d=\{d+k:k\in V_d,\ d+k\ge0\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Call `k in V_d` a two-sided admissible tangent if there exists `epsilon>0` such that both

\[
d+\varepsilon k\in S_d,
\qquad
d-\varepsilon k\in S_d.
\tag{4}
\]

Then the two-sided admissible tangents are **norm dense** in `V_d`.

More explicitly, with

\[
f_n(t)=\min(1,nt),
\qquad
c_n=f_n(d)\in B\cap H_d,
\qquad
k_n=c_nkc_n,
\tag{5}
\]

one has for every `k in V_d`

\[
E(k_n)=0,
\qquad
k_n\longrightarrow k\quad\text{in norm},
\tag{6}
\]

and

\[
-n\|k\|d\le k_n\le n\|k\|d.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence, for nonzero `k`, every

\[
0<\varepsilon\le (n\|k\|)^{-1}
\]

gives

\[
d\pm\varepsilon k_n\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently, if `L:V_d->R` is any **bounded** real linear functional satisfying

\[
L(a-d)\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }a\in S_d,
\tag{9}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{L=0\text{ on }V_d.}
\tag{10}
\]

This is stronger than the normal-functional conclusion of `WP-400`: norm density kills every norm-continuous linear readout on the hereditary C-star source space, including bounded functionals that need not be normal in a chosen von Neumann representation.

The same conclusion holds for any real linear subspace `V subseteq V_d` that is stable under the continuous source cutoffs `k -> c_nkc_n`. Thus merely replacing the von Neumann algebra by a C-star source algebra, and thereby losing the discontinuous projections `e_n`, does **not** create an orientation mechanism. A surviving linear source relation must fail even these continuous `C^*(d)` localizations, or leave the hereditary source category altogether.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + RH-INDEPENDENT + CSTAR-HEREDITARY-SOURCE-CORNER + CONDITIONAL-EXPECTATION + CONTINUOUS-FUNCTIONAL-CALCULUS + FIXED-SHADOW-POSITIVE-FIBRE + ORDER-DOMINATED-TANGENTS + NORM-DENSE-TWO-SIDED-TANGENTS + BOUNDED-LINEAR-ORIENTATION-ZERO + SOURCE-LOCALITY-OBSTRUCTION + MATCHED-CONTROL-UNIVERSAL + CATEGORY-BOUNDARY + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Continuous source cutoffs stay inside the C-star algebra

The obstruction in `WP-400` used the characteristic functions of the intervals `[1/n,infinity)`. Those are natural in the von Neumann completion but need not belong to the original C-star algebra. Here the cutoff is instead the continuous function

\[
f_n(t)=\min(1,nt),
\qquad f_n(0)=0.
\tag{11}
\]

Continuous functional calculus therefore gives

\[
c_n=f_n(d)\in C^*(d)\subseteq B,
\tag{12}
\]

and because `f_n(0)=0`, `c_n` lies in the hereditary algebra generated by `d`.

These cutoffs form an approximate identity for `H_d`. For completeness this can be checked directly on the dense set `dAd`. Since

\[
\|c_nd-d\|
=
\sup_{t\in\sigma(d)} t|f_n(t)-1|
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{13}
\]

for `x=dad` one has

\[
c_nxc_n=(c_nd)a(dc_n)\longrightarrow dad=x
\tag{14}
\]

in norm. The `c_n` are contractions, so density extends (14) to every `x in H_d`. In particular,

\[
c_nkc_n\longrightarrow k
\tag{15}
\]

in norm for every `k in V_d`.

This is the exact categorical improvement over the spectral-projection argument: the approximants remain in the **source C-star algebra itself**, not merely in its von Neumann closure.

## 2. The continuous cutoffs are automatically order-bounded by the shadow

For `t>=0`,

\[
f_n(t)^2\le nt.
\tag{16}
\]

Indeed, for `t<=1/n` this is `n^2t^2<=nt`, while for `t>=1/n` it is `1<=nt`. Functional calculus therefore gives

\[
c_n^2\le nd.
\tag{17}
\]

For self-adjoint `k`, the elementary order bound

\[
-\|k\|1\le k\le\|k\|1
\]

in the unitization remains valid after compression by `c_n`, hence

\[
-\|k\|c_n^2
\le c_nkc_n
\le \|k\|c_n^2.
\tag{18}
\]

Combining (17) and (18) yields exactly (7):

\[
-n\|k\|d\le k_n\le n\|k\|d.
\tag{19}
\]

The centered condition is source-preserving as well. Since `c_n in B` and `E` is `B`-bimodular,

\[
E(k_n)
=E(c_nkc_n)
=c_nE(k)c_n
=0.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus every `k_n` lies in `V_d`. Equations (19)--(20) give

\[
d\pm\varepsilon k_n
\ge (1-\varepsilon n\|k\|)d
\ge0
\tag{21}
\]

whenever `epsilon n||k||<=1`, and their conditional-expectation shadow remains exactly `d`.

So the canonical norm approximation of every centered hereditary source direction is made entirely from directions for which positivity admits **both signs**.

## 3. Norm density kills bounded linear orientation, not only normal orientation

Let `L` satisfy (9). For each `k_n` choose `epsilon>0` so that both signs in (8) are positive. Applying (9) to both perturbations gives

\[
\varepsilon L(k_n)\ge0,
\qquad
-\varepsilon L(k_n)\ge0,
\]

and therefore

\[
L(k_n)=0.
\tag{22}
\]

But `k_n -> k` in norm. Boundedness of `L` now gives

\[
L(k)=\lim_nL(k_n)=0.
\tag{23}
\]

No normality or chosen Hilbert-space representation is used. The obstruction is intrinsic to the C-star norm geometry of the hereditary fixed-shadow fibre.

This also explains exactly what would be needed to evade the result with a linear source restriction. If `V subseteq V_d` is preserved by every compression `k -> c_nkc_n`, the same proof works verbatim inside `V`. Hence a surviving linear relation must couple different spectral scales of `d` strongly enough that these continuous source-algebra cutoffs destroy the relation. Failure only under the discontinuous spectral projections of `WP-400` is no longer enough.

## 4. Relevance to the Bost--Connes completion fork

`WP-392` and `WP-393` already show that the full bounded Bost--Connes C-star algebra and Woronowicz affiliation with that same unital algebra do not create the desired exact positive prime-power selector. `WP-395`--`WP-400` then move to the critical von Neumann/KMS category and show that a diffuse centralizer shadow cannot by itself orient bounded noncentral directions.

A natural apparent escape between those categories is to insist that the actual arithmetic source is still a C-star algebra and object that the spectral projections used by `WP-399/400` are absent from it. The theorem above shows that this objection does not help on a hereditary source corner. Whenever a gauge-stable source C-star algebra carries its fixed-point conditional expectation and the candidate directions lie in `Her_A(d)`, the continuous elements `f_n(d)` already generate norm-dense both-sign positive perturbations inside the source category.

The result is deliberately conditional on that hereditary/source-local hypothesis. It does **not** prove that every positive element with conditional-expectation shadow `d` belongs to `Her_A(d)` for every conditional expectation, and it does not classify source relations that are destroyed by all the continuous compressions (5). Those are separate questions. The point is narrower and exact: lack of von Neumann spectral projections is not, by itself, a mechanism for preserving a linear positivity orientation.

Because the argument uses only C-star order, conditional-expectation bimodularity and functional calculus, it is also a matched-control obstruction. Nothing in the proof knows the rational primes, the Bost--Connes semigroup, the critical KMS state, or the Weil coefficients. Any claimed arithmetic sign theorem using only this geometry would therefore survive generic C-star controls and cannot supply the missing Riemann-specific orientation by itself.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The operator-algebra ingredients are classical. For a positive element `d`, the hereditary algebra `Her_A(d)=closure(dAd)` and its continuous functional-calculus approximate units are standard C-star theory; Blackadar is a convenient monograph reference. Bimodularity of conditional expectations is standard, with Tomiyama's norm-one projection theorem providing the classical structural source.

The present claim is **not** that hereditary C-star approximate units or Tomiyama bimodularity are new. The research delta is their application to the exact fixed-shadow sign question left open by `WP-400`: continuous cutoffs internal to the source C-star algebra upgrade the strong/ultraweak tangent approximation to norm approximation and thereby eliminate every bounded linear orientation on the hereditary fibre. A structure-based literature search found the standard ingredients and nearby work on hereditary subalgebras/conditional expectations, but no reason to treat this Mathia-specific fixed-shadow consequence as an external novelty theorem. It is recorded as an exact derived obstruction, not as claimed literature novelty.

## 6. Boundaries and remaining escape routes

The theorem closes only a specific category and should not be read as a global Bost--Connes no-go.

It does not rule out a source-selected linear space that fails the continuous compression test `k -> f_n(d)kf_n(d)` for a canonical arithmetic reason. Such failure would now be substantive evidence of cross-scale coupling rather than an artifact of missing spectral projections. It also does not rule out nonlinear/global sign readouts, nonlinear admissibility constraints, discontinuous or genuinely unbounded linear observables, domains not stable under these cutoffs, one-sided correspondences, nonunital boundary categories not modeled by this hereditary corner, or finite--archimedean couplings.

Nor does (10) produce a Weil sign. It is an obstruction: inside this broad source-local C-star class, positivity remains infinitesimally sign-symmetric after every continuous cutoff and therefore cannot orient a nonzero bounded linear Weil functional.

The next useful linear candidate must therefore identify a **specific source-forced relation that continuous shadow localization destroys** and show that the destroyed cross-scale quantity is exactly what carries a destination-relevant orientation. Without that extra structure, moving from the von Neumann cutoff of `WP-400` back to the source C-star algebra changes topology but not the sign obstruction.
