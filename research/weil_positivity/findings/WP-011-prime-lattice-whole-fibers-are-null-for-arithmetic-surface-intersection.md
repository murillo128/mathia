# WP-011 — Prime-Lattice whole fibers are null directions for arithmetic-surface intersection

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE`.

WP-006 left open a natural higher-dimensional escape: instead of sending a Prime-Lattice exponent vector directly to an Arakelov divisor class on `Spec Z`, place the prime axes on a genuine regular arithmetic surface and try to obtain Weil-type positivity from an intersection/Hodge theorem there. That route has a sharp obstruction. If the axis for a prime `p` is lifted in the canonical way to the **whole vertical fiber** `F_p`, then `F_p` lies in the radical of the vertical intersection pairing. The negative-definite part of the fiber intersection matrix appears only after quotienting by the whole fiber, i.e. on differences between irreducible components of a reducible bad fiber. For a fixed arithmetic surface such nontrivial component directions occur at only finitely many primes, while the Weil finite term has nonzero atoms at `p^k` for every prime. Thus a direct

```text
Prime-Lattice axes -> vertical fibers -> arithmetic-surface Hodge/intersection positivity
```

cannot supply the global finite Weil form. A successful intersection route must add genuinely new horizontal/correspondence/cohomological data rather than merely raise the Prime-Lattice divisor picture by one geometric dimension.

## 1. Exact lift of a prime axis to a vertical fiber

Let

\[
\pi:X\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z
\]

be a proper flat regular arithmetic surface with smooth geometrically connected generic fiber. For a rational prime `p`, let

\[
F_p=X\times_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_p
=\sum_i m_{p,i}\Gamma_{p,i}
\]

be the scheme-theoretic special fiber, written as a sum of its irreducible components.

Prime Lattice represents an integer

\[
n=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)}
\]

by the exponent vector `(v_p(n))_p`. The most literal surface-level lift of the prime-axis basis vector `e_p` is therefore

\[
e_p\longmapsto F_p,
\qquad
k e_p\longmapsto kF_p.
\tag{1}
\]

This is canonical at the level of the base: `F_p` is the pullback of the closed point `[p]` on `Spec Z`. Equivalently, as a Cartier divisor on `X`,

\[
F_p=\operatorname{div}_X(p),
\tag{2}
\]

because the constant rational function `p` vanishes exactly on the fiber over `p`, with the fiber multiplicities.

Equation (2) is the surface analogue of the divisor identification used in WP-006, but now there is a genuine two-dimensional intersection theory available.

## 2. The whole fiber is exactly the null direction

For a fixed prime `p`, the standard intersection matrix of the irreducible components of `F_p` is negative semidefinite. More precisely, if

\[
F_p=\sum_i m_i\Gamma_i,
\]

then

\[
F_p\cdot \Gamma_j=0
\quad\text{for every component }\Gamma_j.
\tag{3}
\]

There are two equivalent ways to see (3).

First, `F_p=div_X(p)` is principal and vertical intersection with a principal divisor is zero. Second, in the component matrix the multiplicity vector `(m_i)` spans the defect direction; the row-sum relation is exactly

\[
\sum_i m_i(\Gamma_i\cdot\Gamma_j)=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
F_p^2=0.
\tag{4}
\]

For `p != q`, the fibers `F_p` and `F_q` are disjoint, so

\[
F_p\cdot F_q=0.
\tag{5}
\]

Therefore the entire Prime-Lattice whole-fiber span is totally isotropic:

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_p a_pF_p\right)\cdot
\left(\sum_q b_qF_q\right)=0
}
\tag{6}
\]

for all finitely supported coefficient families `(a_p)` and `(b_p)`.

Multiplying the local intersection contribution at `p` by the usual arithmetic factor `log p` does not alter this conclusion: zero remains zero. Hence the exact `log p` scale needed by the finite explicit formula is not enough; the canonical axis direction itself has vanished from the intersection form.

## 3. Where genuine positivity does live

The classical theorem is not that vertical intersection is trivial. It is that, for a connected closed fiber, the component intersection matrix is negative semidefinite with one-dimensional kernel generated by the whole fiber. Thus

\[
-(\cdot,\cdot)_p
\]

becomes positive definite on the quotient

\[
\left(\bigoplus_i \mathbb Q\Gamma_{p,i}\right)/\mathbb QF_p.
\tag{7}
\]

This is a genuine geometric sign theorem and therefore initially looks close to the target of this research line. But (7) also identifies the obstruction precisely: **the Hodge/Zariski sign is carried by component differences, while the Prime-Lattice axis is the null vector that is removed.**

If `F_p` is irreducible, then the quotient (7) is zero. On the smooth model `P^1_Z`, for example, every fiber is a single irreducible `P^1_{F_p}`, so the entire vertical intersection geometry generated by the whole fibers contributes nothing beyond the null directions.

For a general fixed arithmetic surface associated to a smooth projective curve over `Q`, good reduction holds at all but finitely many primes. At every good prime the fiber is smooth and geometrically connected, hence irreducible, so (7) is again zero. Nontrivial component-difference lattices can therefore occur only at finitely many bad primes.

That support mismatch is fatal for the direct Weil application. The finite Weil coefficient measure contains

\[
\frac{\Lambda(p^k)}{\sqrt{p^k}}
=\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}>0
\tag{8}
\]

for every prime `p` and every `k>=1`. A component-intersection energy from one fixed arithmetic surface is instead nontrivial only at finitely many bad primes, and the canonical whole-fiber vector at every prime is null.

Thus neither the whole-fiber radical nor the bad-fiber quotient can reproduce the infinite finite-place support required by Weil.

## 4. This closes a specific escape left by WP-006

WP-006 proved that the direct Arakelov completion of the Prime-Lattice divisor

\[
\sum_p v_p(n)[p]
\]

becomes a principal zero class after the product-formula archimedean term is added. One possible reaction was to move to a genuine arithmetic surface, where nontrivial intersection forms and Hodge-index phenomena exist.

WP-011 shows that the naive higher-dimensional lift still fails before any subtle archimedean issue arises:

```text
Prime-Lattice axis e_p
    -> whole vertical fiber F_p
    -> null vector of vertical intersection.
```

Passing to component differences does create a signed/positive quotient geometry, but it changes the object. Those components encode reduction data of an additional arithmetic curve/model; they are not present in the Prime-Lattice exponent vector itself. Moreover they disappear at almost every prime.

So the higher-dimensional escape is not “take any arithmetic surface and apply Hodge index.” A successful construction must explain why extra cycles are present at **all** primes and why their interaction carries the prime-power hierarchy (8).

## 5. Why the function-field Weil proof does not contradict the obstruction

Weil's surface proof for a curve `C/F_q` does use exactly the kind of independent geometric sign theorem sought here: on

\[
S=C\times C
\]

the Hodge index theorem controls the intersection pairing. But the arithmetic information is not encoded by the fibers of a map to the base field. It is encoded by **global correspondences**: the diagonal `Delta` and the graph `Gamma_{Frob}` of Frobenius. Their intersections satisfy

\[
\Delta\cdot \Gamma_{Frob^n}
=\#C(\mathbb F_{q^n}),
\]

and Hodge index is applied to suitable combinations of these correspondence classes.

This comparison matters because it identifies the missing structural role. A number-field analogue cannot be obtained by replacing `q` with the collection of primes and using the vertical fibers themselves. The function-field proof needs a single global correspondence whose iterates simultaneously produce all extension-level data, plus an intersection theorem on the surrounding surface.

Prime Lattice supplies the valuation/fiber labels very naturally, but the direct vertical lift supplies no analogue of the Frobenius graph. The null-fiber calculation is therefore not merely a technical failure of normalization; it shows that the **wrong type of cycle** is being fed to the intersection theorem.

## 6. Archimedean completion does not repair the null finite block

One might try to attach Green data at infinity to `F_p`. There are two cases.

1. Use the canonical product-formula completion. Then one returns to the principal arithmetic divisor mechanism of WP-006: the completed divisor is class-trivial.
2. Choose a non-principal Green function or metric. Then the finite whole-fiber block remains zero, while the new archimedean datum is extra structure whose canonical origin still has to be proved. Selecting it to reproduce the gamma/digamma term would be an inserted completion, not an independent geometric derivation.

The standard vertical component pairing itself has no mechanism that can generate the archimedean gamma contribution. Its nontrivial directions are local bad-reduction component differences, and its whole-fiber direction is null.

Therefore the sought global form cannot arise from “vertical intersection plus an ordinary archimedean decoration” unless the decoration also introduces a new coupling that changes the cycle space.

## 7. Adversarial controls and escape routes

### 7.1 Weight the fiber by `log p`

Arithmetic intersection formulas naturally attach `log p` factors to finite local intersections. This is encouraging because `log p` is part of the Weil coefficient. But equations (3)--(6) show that the whole-fiber coefficient multiplying `log p` is zero. Correct scale cannot rescue a null direction.

### 7.2 Use `kF_p` for the prime power `p^k`

By bilinearity,

\[
(kF_p)\cdot(\ell F_p)=k\ell F_p^2=0.
\]

So the prime-power hierarchy remains invisible. The desired attenuation `p^{-k/2}` cannot emerge from the intersection of whole-fiber multiples.

### 7.3 Blow up the fiber to create components

Blowing up can create exceptional vertical components with a nontrivial negative intersection lattice. But then the result depends on the chosen blowups unless another structure forces them. Worse, the whole transformed fiber remains the null direction. Arbitrary blowups therefore manufacture geometry rather than derive it from Prime Lattice.

### 7.4 Choose an arithmetic surface with bad reduction at many primes

A fixed finite-type arithmetic surface has bad reduction at only finitely many primes. Producing nontrivial component geometry at every prime would require an infinite/non-finite-type object or a varying family. That is a genuine change of category and must come with its own canonicality and positivity theorem.

### 7.5 Add horizontal sections or correspondences

This is the main genuine escape. Horizontal cycles can intersect vertical fibers nontrivially, and global correspondences can have a Hodge-index form analogous in role to the function-field proof. But this adds exactly the structure absent from the Prime-Lattice axis lift. Such a route remains open only if Mathia itself forces the horizontal/correspondence object and if the resulting local-to-global intersection formula produces both (8) and the archimedean/polar terms without importing Weil's functional by hand.

## 8. Prior art and novelty assessment

The intersection-theoretic facts are standard. Liu's *Algebraic Geometry and Arithmetic Curves*, Chapter 9, develops intersection theory on regular arithmetic surfaces and proves that the intersection matrix of a closed fiber is negative semidefinite; the defect is the whole-fiber direction. The same structure is commonly used in local height and regular-model calculations.

Kedlaya's exposition of Weil's function-field RH proof is the closest conceptual control for this research question: the Hodge index theorem is applied on `C x C` to the diagonal and Frobenius graph, not to base fibers. Hence “surface intersection positivity” as a phrase is classical prior art, and the relevant novelty test is whether Mathia forces a new correspondence/cycle whose intersection numbers match the completed explicit formula.

No novelty is claimed for the surface theorem. The Mathia-specific contribution is the bridge-and-obstruction calculation:

- Prime-Lattice axis `e_p` has a canonical higher-dimensional image `F_p`;
- that exact image is the null vector of the vertical intersection form;
- the positive quotient uses only extra bad-fiber component data and is supported at finitely many primes;
- therefore the simplest arithmetic-surface/Hodge escape from WP-006 cannot retain the WP-004 finite Weil weights.

This is materially stronger than saying that ordinary Arakelov classes are trivial: it shows that even **before** passing to the global arithmetic divisor class, the natural finite intersection form annihilates the whole-fiber axis data.

## 9. Consequence for the research line

Combining WP-004, WP-006, and WP-011 gives a sharper boundary:

```text
Prime-Lattice axes
    -> exact positive Mangoldt weights                    (WP-004)

axes -> completed principal divisor class
    -> class zero                                        (WP-006)

axes -> whole fibers on an arithmetic surface
    -> intersection radical                              (WP-011)

fiber-component quotient
    -> genuine geometric sign
    -> only extra bad-reduction directions
    -> finite-prime support, not all prime powers.
```

The next viable intersection-style target is therefore much narrower: **a Mathia-forced global correspondence or relative/cohomological cycle space in which the Prime-Lattice finite weights appear as intersection/fixed-point data while positivity comes from a Hodge/intersection theorem on the larger object.** Merely adding dimension, taking vertical fibers, or using bad-fiber component lattices is ruled out.

## 10. Falsification checklist

The exact core is auditable without RH or zeta zeros:

1. verify `F_p=div_X(p)` as a Cartier divisor on an arithmetic surface;
2. verify `F_p . Gamma_{p,i}=0` for every irreducible component;
3. verify the component intersection matrix is negative semidefinite with kernel generated by the whole fiber;
4. verify distinct fibers are disjoint, hence the span of all whole fibers has zero intersection pairing;
5. verify that good fibers are irreducible and that only finitely many primes are bad for a fixed arithmetic surface;
6. compare this finite/nonexistent support with the nonzero Weil weights `log p / p^{k/2}` for every `p^k`;
7. compare with the function-field proof, where the nontrivial cycles are Frobenius correspondences rather than fibers.

A counterexample must therefore provide a canonical arithmetic-surface cycle attached to the existing Mathia data that is **not** merely the whole fiber or an arbitrarily chosen bad-fiber component, is available at all primes/prime powers, and comes with an independently positive global intersection theorem. That would be a genuinely different mechanism rather than a failure of WP-011.
