# WP-207 — Koszul cohomology recovers Euler Gamma only by collapsing Barnes roughness

**Status:** `DERIVED + EXACT + KOSZUL-COHOMOLOGY + ARCHIMEDEAN-GAMMA + BARNES-CANCELLATION + POSITIVE-COHOMOLOGY + ROUGHNESS-COLLAPSE + SUPERTRACE-FORK + MATCHED-CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + DECISIVE-NARROWING + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-206` leaves one important escape hatch deliberately open. The growing Barnes multiplicity

\[
g_d(m)=\binom{m+d-1}{d-1}
\]

can be reduced to the simple Euler-Gamma multiplicity by an alternating finite difference, and a genuinely cohomological geometry might make that alternation intrinsic rather than hand inserted.

That escape is algebraically real. The standard Koszul complex on `d-1` independent linear directions realizes the Barnes finite difference exactly. Its degree-`m` Euler characteristic is one, and because the sequence is regular its actual homology is concentrated in degree zero with one positive state at every spectral level. Thus the signed Barnes cancellation can indeed be promoted from a virtual determinant to an ordinary positive cohomology space.

But the same exactness shows why this does **not** preserve the mechanism that made the higher-dimensional bath useful in `WP-206`. The surviving cohomology is literally a one-variable polynomial/Fock space. Its number-operator inverse has simple multiplicity, belongs to every Schatten class `S^r` for `r>1`, and its centered order-`n` multiplier is `O(|xi|^{-2})` for every allowed `n=4k>=4`. The reciprocal `1/|xi|` floor generated by the `d=n-1` Barnes multiplicity is gone.

So the standard cohomological repair produces a sharp three-way fork:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ccl}
\text{ordinary chain trace} &:& \text{positive + Barnes roughness, but wrong Gamma divisor},\\[2mm]
\text{graded supertrace} &:& \text{Euler divisor, but cancellation is signed and removes the rough floor},\\[2mm]
\text{Koszul cohomology} &:& \text{Euler divisor + positive Hilbert space, but it is the one-mode quotient}.
\end{array}
}
\]

This closes the most direct cohomological interpretation of the `WP-206` finite-difference escape. It does **not** rule out a genuinely global finite--archimedean cohomology whose sign theorem acts on a different assembled pairing. It does show that a standard Koszul/Hodge resolution cannot simultaneously retain dimension-forced critical roughness and turn the same spectrum into the exact Euler-Gamma positive source.

## 1. The Barnes multiplicity is the Hilbert function of a polynomial/Fock space

Let

\[
S_d=\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_d]
=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}(S_d)_m
\tag{1}
\]

with the usual total-degree grading. Then

\[
\dim (S_d)_m
=\binom{m+d-1}{d-1}
=g_d(m).
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the `d`-mode oscillator used in `WP-206` is the Hilbert-space completion of the same graded object: monomials of total degree `m` form the eigenspace of the additive number operator `N^{(d)}` at eigenvalue `m`.

The Hilbert series is

\[
H_{S_d}(t)
=\sum_{m\ge0}g_d(m)t^m
=\frac1{(1-t)^d}.
\tag{3}
\]

The polynomial growth of `g_d(m)` is exactly what gave the positive inverse in `WP-206` the critical Schatten roughness when `d=n-1`.

## 2. A Koszul complex realizes the alternating Barnes difference exactly

Choose `d-1` independent linear forms. After a linear change of variables we may write them as

\[
z_2,\ldots,z_d.
\tag{4}
\]

They form a regular sequence in `S_d`. Consider their graded Koszul complex

\[
K_\bullet
=K_\bullet(z_2,\ldots,z_d;S_d).
\tag{5}
\]

With the grading shift that makes the Koszul differential preserve total degree,

\[
K_j
=S_d(-j)\otimes \Lambda^j\mathbb C^{d-1}.
\tag{6}
\]

Writing `g_d(r)=0` for `r<0`, the degree-`m` chain multiplicity is

\[
\dim (K_j)_m
=\binom{d-1}{j}g_d(m-j).
\tag{7}
\]

Therefore its degreewise Euler characteristic is

\[
\chi_m
=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
(-1)^j\binom{d-1}{j}g_d(m-j).
\tag{8}
\]

The generating function is immediate:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m\ge0}\chi_m t^m
&=(1-t)^{d-1}H_{S_d}(t)\\
&=\frac{(1-t)^{d-1}}{(1-t)^d}\\
&=\frac1{1-t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\chi_m=1\qquad(m\ge0).}
\tag{10}
\]

Equation (10) is exactly the `(d-1)`st Barnes finite difference from `WP-206`, now realized as the Euler characteristic of a canonical algebraic complex once the transverse regular sequence has been chosen.

This already falsifies one overly strong reading of `WP-206`: the alternating coefficients need not remain merely formal negative determinant weights. Standard homological algebra can package them intrinsically as chain degrees.

## 3. Exactness promotes the virtual cancellation to positive cohomology

Because (4) is a regular sequence, the Koszul complex is exact in positive homological degree. Thus

\[
H_j(K_\bullet)=0
\qquad(j>0)
\tag{11}
\]

and

\[
H_0(K_\bullet)
\cong
S_d/(z_2,\ldots,z_d)
\cong
\mathbb C[z_1].
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\dim H_0(K_\bullet)_m=1
\qquad(m\ge0).
}
\tag{13}
\]

This is stronger than an Euler-characteristic identity. With the standard Fock inner product, the degreewise Koszul complex is finite dimensional and has the ordinary Hodge decomposition associated with the differential and its adjoint. Harmonic representatives of (12) therefore carry a positive Hilbert norm.

So a cohomological model can genuinely turn the signed Barnes cancellation into an ordinary positive space. The price is visible directly in (12): the cohomology is **literally the one-mode quotient**.

The construction also exposes a naturalness issue. Choosing `d-1` independent linear forms is equivalent to choosing the surviving one-dimensional direction. No such line is invariant under the full `GL_d` or `U(d)` symmetry of an undistinguished `d`-mode oscillator. If an ambient Mathia geometry supplies a canonical radial or longitudinal line, the quotient may become intrinsic; but even then its surviving source is still one dimensional.

## 4. The chain-level heat traces show the sign/source fork without regularization

Let `F` denote exterior degree on `Lambda^* C^{d-1}` and define the total-degree Hamiltonian on the chain complex by

\[
H_{d,a}=N^{(d)}+F+a,
\qquad a>0.
\tag{14}
\]

The Koszul differential preserves `N^{(d)}+F`, so (14) descends to cohomology.

The **ordinary** heat trace on the total chain space is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{d,a}})
&=e^{-at}
\frac{(1+e^{-t})^{d-1}}
     {(1-e^{-t})^d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Its level multiplicities still grow like a positive constant times `m^{d-1}`. Hence the ordinary positive chain space retains the higher-dimensional roughness of `WP-206`.

The **supertrace** is instead

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Str}(e^{-tH_{d,a}})
&=e^{-at}
\frac{(1-e^{-t})^{d-1}}
     {(1-e^{-t})^d}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{e^{-at}}{1-e^{-t}}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

That is exactly the heat trace of a one-mode number operator with shift `a`. Mellin transformation therefore gives, initially in its half-plane of convergence,

\[
\operatorname{Str}(H_{d,a}^{-w})
=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+a)^{-w}
=\zeta(w,a),
\tag{17}
\]

up to the harmless `pi^w` scaling used in `WP-203`--`WP-206`.

Thus the chain complex realizes the desired Euler/Hurwitz source exactly, before any determinant-normalization ambiguity. But the mechanism that changes Barnes into Euler is precisely the graded cancellation in (16). The ordinary positive trace (15) does not have the Euler divisor.

Passing to cohomology turns (16) into an ordinary trace because (11)--(13) remove all acyclic pairs. That restores ordinary positivity, but simultaneously makes the operator exactly one-mode.

## 5. Positive cohomology loses the `WP-202` reciprocal Fourier floor

On the cohomology (12), the positive inverse corresponding to the `WP-206` normalization is

\[
V_{\mathrm{coh},a}
=\frac{\pi}{N^{(1)}+a}.
\tag{18}
\]

Its singular values are

\[
\frac{\pi}{m+a}
\qquad(m=0,1,2,\ldots)
\tag{19}
\]

with multiplicity one. Hence

\[
V_{\mathrm{coh},a}\in\mathcal S^r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r>1.
\tag{20}
\]

For every allowed spectral-shift order `n=4k>=4`, this implies

\[
\boxed{
V_{\mathrm{coh},a}\in\mathcal S^{n-1}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

So the critical boundary

\[
\mathcal S^n\setminus\mathcal S^{n-1}
\tag{22}
\]

that drove the `WP-206` reciprocal floor has disappeared.

The loss can also be seen directly at the Fourier-multiplier level. Recall the centered scalar order-`n` multiplier

\[
F_b(\xi)
=
\frac1{(n-3)!\,\xi^2}
\int_0^b
(1-\cos(t\xi))(b-t)^{n-3}\,dt.
\tag{23}
\]

Two elementary bounds give

\[
F_b(\xi)
\le \frac{b^n}{n!}
\tag{24}
\]

from `1-cos u <= u^2/2`, and

\[
F_b(\xi)
\le
\frac{2b^{n-2}}
{(n-2)!\,\xi^2}
\tag{25}
\]

from `1-cos u <= 2`.

For the cohomological bath,

\[
F_{\mathrm{coh},a}(\xi)
=
\sum_{m\ge0}
F_{\pi/(m+a)}(\xi).
\tag{26}
\]

Split the sum at `m+a=pi|xi|`. On the large-`m` tail use (24):

\[
\sum_{m+a\ge\pi|\xi|}
F_{\pi/(m+a)}(\xi)
=O(|\xi|^{1-n}).
\tag{27}
\]

On the complementary range use (25). Since `n-2>=2`,

\[
\sum_{m+a<\pi|\xi|}
\frac1{(m+a)^{n-2}}
=O(1),
\tag{28}
\]

and therefore

\[
\sum_{m+a<\pi|\xi|}
F_{\pi/(m+a)}(\xi)
=O(|\xi|^{-2}).
\tag{29}
\]

Combining (27)--(29),

\[
\boxed{
F_{\mathrm{coh},a}(\xi)
=O(|\xi|^{-2}),
\qquad
|\xi|F_{\mathrm{coh},a}(\xi)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{30}
\]

This is the exact opposite of the `WP-206` critical bath, where `d=n-1` gave

\[
\liminf_{|\xi|\to\infty}
|\xi|F_{d,a}(\xi)>0.
\tag{31}
\]

The Koszul cancellation that repairs the Gamma divisor therefore cancels the very spectral multiplicity responsible for the reciprocal floor.

## 6. Aggressive falsification and exact scope

**Could one keep the rough chain space but use its superdeterminant only for Gamma?** Algebraically yes, but that gives two different readouts of the same complex. The ordinary trace sees (15) and retains the Barnes multiplicity; the supertrace sees (16) and cancels it. Positivity of the ordinary Hilbert norm or of a positive chain Hamiltonian does not by itself prove positivity of a quadratic form assembled through the graded readout. A separate theorem on that graded/global pairing would still be required.

**Could the supertrace itself retain the reciprocal floor even though its divisor is Euler?** No for this Koszul construction. Equations (16)--(17) show that the effective graded spectral multiplicity is exactly one at every level. The same cancellation that produces the simple divisor reduces the centered multiplier to (26), and (30) rules out the reciprocal floor.

**Could Hodge theory rescue both features after taking cohomology?** Not in this complex. Hodge theory identifies cohomology with harmonic representatives, but the harmonic spectrum is precisely (19). Positivity is recovered only after the transverse Barnes multiplicities have become exact/acyclic and disappeared.

**Could a different choice of `d-1` linear forms preserve higher-dimensional information?** Any independent linear regular sequence of length `d-1` has quotient isomorphic to a polynomial ring in one variable after a linear change of coordinates. The choice can change the surviving line, not the Hilbert function or Schatten threshold.

**Could a canonical Mathia line make this genuinely geometric?** It could remove the arbitrariness of (4), but not the roughness collapse. A distinguished radial direction plus `d-1` transverse exact directions still has one-dimensional cohomology and therefore the multiplier bound (30).

**Does this rule out all cohomological completions?** No. It rules out the standard Koszul/exterior mechanism that realizes the Barnes finite difference by making `d-1` oscillator directions exact. A more global complex could have nontrivial cohomology in several degrees, a nonlocal intersection form, or a finite--archimedean differential whose sign theorem does not reduce to ordinary chain positivity. Such a construction would be genuinely outside this no-go and would need to produce the prime-power and polar terms from the same object.

**Does any arithmetic enter here?** No. The construction works for every `a>0`, every `d>=1`, and every choice of independent transverse linear forms. It is therefore a matched control, not evidence that Mathia already contains the Riemann global geometry.

## 7. Prior art and novelty boundary

The algebraic ingredients are classical and are **not** claimed as new mathematics.

The Barnes multiple-zeta/gamma hierarchy and its difference relations are standard; see S. N. M. Ruijsenaars, *On Barnes' multiple zeta and gamma functions*, Advances in Mathematics **156** (2000), 107--132. `WP-206` already used this as the prior-art anchor for the finite-difference cancellation.

Koszul exactness for regular sequences is standard homological algebra. The Stacks Project, Section 15.31, *Koszul regular sequences*, records that a regular sequence is Koszul-regular, so its Koszul complex is acyclic in positive degree. Applied to the independent linear forms (4), this gives (11)--(12). The Hilbert-series identity (9) is the graded Euler-characteristic form of the same classical resolution.

The use of a graded trace `Tr((-1)^F e^{-tH})` to cancel bosonic/fermionic pairs is the classical supersymmetric-index mechanism; see E. Witten, *Constraints on supersymmetry breaking*, Nuclear Physics B **202** (1982), 253--316. Equation (16) is an elementary Fock/Koszul instance of that general principle.

Cohomological and regularized-determinant interpretations of archimedean local factors are also established prior art rather than a Mathia novelty. C. Deninger, *Local L-factors of motives and regularized determinants*, Inventiones Mathematicae **107** (1992), 135--150, gives regularized-determinant descriptions of local factors; C. Consani and M. Marcolli, *Archimedean cohomology revisited*, arXiv:`math/0407480`, develops a cohomological interpretation of archimedean local factors and a log-of-Frobenius operator.

The line-specific result here is the **exact compatibility test with the `WP-202`/`WP-206` positivity resource**: the standard cohomological mechanism that repairs Barnes multiplicity to the Euler divisor also eliminates the multiplicity-driven Schatten boundary and reciprocal Fourier floor. The prior-art audit found no theorem that turns this Koszul cancellation into the zeta Weil quadratic positivity while simultaneously retaining the finite-prime sector. Search absence is not a novelty claim.

## 8. Consequence for the research line

`WP-206` correctly left an intrinsic grading/cohomology route open. `WP-207` resolves its most direct realization.

The favorable fact is stronger than the previous discussion suggested: **cohomology can make the Barnes finite difference intrinsic and can turn the virtual alternating multiplicity into an ordinary positive Hilbert space.** The unfavorable fact is equally exact: in the standard Koszul realization that positive space is just the one-mode quotient, so the dimension-forced roughness disappears.

The live target is therefore not merely “find a grading that cancels Barnes multiplicities.” That part is easy and classical. A successful construction must instead supply a genuinely global graded/intersection structure in which

\[
\boxed{
\text{archimedean source cancellation}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{the independent sign theorem}
}
\]

are compatible **without** reducing the sign-carrying object to the one-dimensional Gamma source, and in which the same geometry also produces the finite Mangoldt and pole/global terms.

For the current Barnes oscillator route, standard Koszul/Hodge cancellation is therefore a decisive matched control, not the missing Weil bridge.
