# WP-095 — The finite-band cover-positive cone cannot regularize the Toeplitz endpoint anchor

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + HYBRID-CONE + POSITION-DEPENDENT-INFINITE-RANGE + NONCLOSABLE + MATCHED-CONTROL + CLASSICAL-FORM-MECHANISM`.

`WP-093` and `WP-094` leave two sharply different positive survivors of the exact Mathia cover law

\[
q(W_nx)=nq(x),
\qquad
W_ne_k=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}e_{nk+r}.
\tag{1}
\]

The first is the complete fixed-finite-band positive cone

\[
q_{a,b}(x)
=
a\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)^2|x_{j+1}-x_j|^2
+
(b-2a)\sum_{j\ge0}\left(j+\frac12\right)|x_j|^2,
\qquad
a\ge0,\ b\ge2a,
\tag{2}
\]

whose critical ray is the closable weighted Dirichlet form of `WP-093`. The second is the complete positive block-Toeplitz cone of `WP-094`; in the scalar case it is the singular endpoint form

\[
q_{\partial,c}(x)
=
c\left|\sum_{j\ge0}x_j\right|^2,
\qquad
c\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

A natural escape from the two separate classifications is to add them. This produces a positive, position-dependent, infinite-range form and therefore lies outside the hypotheses of both `WP-093` and `WP-094` taken separately.

That escape fails exactly.

Put

\[
\ell(x):=\sum_{j\ge0}x_j
\qquad (x\in c_{00}(\mathbb N_0)).
\tag{4}
\]

For every nonzero form in the cone (2),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x\ne0}
\frac{|\ell(x)|^2}{q_{a,b}(x)}
=
+\infty.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently, for every `c>0` the positive exact-cover-covariant hybrid

\[
\boxed{
Q_{a,b,c}(x)
=
q_{a,b}(x)+c|\ell(x)|^2
}
\tag{6}
\]

is **not closable** on the ambient Hilbert space `ell^2(N_0)`. Conversely `c<0` cannot give a positive form because of (5). Thus inside the additive cone generated by the two canonical survivors,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{a,b,c}\ge0\ \text{and closable}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c=0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

The stationary infinite-range component cannot be regularized by adding any member of the entire finite-band cover-positive cone. The only possible closable positive hybrids discard the endpoint component altogether.

This closes the most direct position-dependent infinite-range combination left by `WP-093`--`WP-094`. It does **not** classify arbitrary position-dependent infinite-range kernels or genuinely nonseparable prime/global couplings.

## 1. The endpoint functional is exactly cover covariant

For a finitely supported sequence `x`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell(W_nx)
&=
\sum_{k\ge0}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\frac{x_k}{\sqrt n}\\
&=
\sqrt n\,\ell(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Hence

\[
q_{\partial,c}(W_nx)
=
nq_{\partial,c}(x).
\tag{9}
\]

Together with the exact covariance of every `q_{a,b}` from `WP-093`, equation (9) gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_{a,b,c}(W_nx)=nQ_{a,b,c}(x)
}
\tag{10}
\]

for every integer `n>=1`.

So the hybrid does not fail because the two pieces transform incompatibly. They obey precisely the same degree-one law.

## 2. A harmonic test sequence simultaneously probes both survivors

For `N>=1`, define

\[
x^{(N)}_j
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac1{j+1},&0\le j<N,\\[4pt]
0,&j\ge N.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]

Its endpoint value is the harmonic number

\[
\boxed{
\ell(x^{(N)})=H_N:=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1k.
}
\tag{12}
\]

The two pieces of the finite-band energy can be evaluated directly.

For the weighted difference term, if `0<=j<=N-2` then

\[
x^{(N)}_{j+1}-x^{(N)}_j
=
-\frac1{(j+1)(j+2)},
\]

while the final jump at `j=N-1` is `-1/N`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_N
&:=
\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)^2
|x^{(N)}_{j+1}-x^{(N)}_j|^2\\
&=
1+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac1{k^2}\\
&\le
\boxed{\zeta(2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

For the weighted mass term,

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_N
&:=
\sum_{j\ge0}\left(j+\frac12\right)|x^{(N)}_j|^2\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{N}
\left(\frac1k-\frac1{2k^2}\right)\\
&=
\boxed{
H_N-\frac12 H_N^{(2)}
},
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

where

\[
H_N^{(2)}=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1{k^2}.
\]

In particular,

\[
0\le M_N\le H_N,
\qquad
H_N\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{15}
\]

Substituting (13)--(14) into (2) gives the uniform bound

\[
\boxed{
q_{a,b}(x^{(N)})
\le
a\zeta(2)+(b-2a)H_N.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore, for every nonzero pair `a>=0,b>=2a`,

\[
\frac{|\ell(x^{(N)})|^2}
{q_{a,b}(x^{(N)})}
\ge
\frac{H_N^2}
{a\zeta(2)+(b-2a)H_N}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{17}
\]

This proves (5).

Two regimes are worth separating. On the critical Dirichlet ray `b=2a>0`, the finite-band energy of `x^(N)` stays bounded while the endpoint value grows like `log N`. In the interior `b>2a`, the energy grows only like `log N`, while the squared endpoint value grows like `(log N)^2`. The same endpoint singularity therefore survives throughout the whole positive cone, not only on its critical boundary.

## 3. Normalization gives an exact nonclosability sequence

Normalize the harmonic vectors by their endpoint value:

\[
y^{(N)}
:=
\frac{x^{(N)}}{H_N}.
\tag{18}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\ell(y^{(N)})=1
}
\tag{19}
\]

for every `N`, while (16) implies

\[
\boxed{
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)})
\le
\frac{a\zeta(2)}{H_N^2}
+
\frac{b-2a}{H_N}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{20}
\]

At the same time,

\[
\|y^{(N)}\|_{\ell^2}^2
=
\frac{H_N^{(2)}}{H_N^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]

Because `q_{a,b}` is positive semidefinite, its square root is a seminorm. Hence

\[
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)}-y^{(M)})^{1/2}
\le
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)})^{1/2}
+
q_{a,b}(y^{(M)})^{1/2},
\]

so

\[
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)}-y^{(M)})
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{22}
\]

For `c>0`, equations (19) and (22) now give

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{a,b,c}(y^{(N)}-y^{(M)})
&=
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)}-y^{(M)})\\
&\longrightarrow0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

because the endpoint terms cancel exactly. But

\[
\boxed{
Q_{a,b,c}(y^{(N)})
=
q_{a,b}(y^{(N)})+c
\longrightarrow c>0.
}
\tag{24}
\]

Thus `y^(N)` tends to zero in `ell^2`, is Cauchy in the form seminorm of `Q_{a,b,c}`, but its form energy does not tend to zero. This is exactly the standard closability obstruction for a positive quadratic form. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
c>0
\Longrightarrow
Q_{a,b,c}
\text{ is not closable on }\ell^2(\mathbb N_0).
}
\tag{25}
\]

No asymptotic spectral argument, regularization, or zero data is used.

## 4. Negative endpoint coefficient cannot preserve positivity

Suppose `c<0`. By (5), choose `x` with

\[
\frac{|\ell(x)|^2}{q_{a,b}(x)}
>
\frac1{|c|}
\]

when `q_{a,b}` is nonzero. Then

\[
Q_{a,b,c}(x)
=
q_{a,b}(x)-|c||\ell(x)|^2
<0.
\tag{26}
\]

If `q_{a,b}=0`, any `x` with `\ell(x)\ne0` already gives negativity. Hence positivity of the hybrid forces

\[
\boxed{c\ge0.}
\tag{27}
\]

Combining (25) and (27) shows that the additive two-survivor cone has no nonzero endpoint coefficient compatible with both positivity and closability.

## 5. This is the exact collision of `WP-093` and `WP-094`

The result is not contained in either parent classification separately.

`WP-093` assumes fixed finite bandwidth. The endpoint kernel has matrix entries

\[
(A_\partial)_{jk}=c,
\tag{28}
\]

so it is infinite range. Once (3) is added, the hybrid is outside the finite-band theorem.

`WP-094` assumes Toeplitz stationarity. The finite-band terms in (2) contain the absolute-position weights `(j+1)^2` and `j+1/2`, so the hybrid is not Toeplitz unless the finite-band component vanishes.

Thus (6) is exactly the simplest positive position-dependent infinite-range form left in the gap between the two theorems. Equations (11)--(25) show that this gap is nonempty algebraically but contains no new closable form in the additive cone generated by the two canonical survivors.

This also gives a useful interpretation of why adding the local energy does not cure the singular endpoint anchor. The harmonic profile `1/(j+1)` is precisely slow enough that its endpoint sum diverges, while its weighted derivative has finite energy and its weighted mass grows only logarithmically. The local cover-positive geometry therefore cannot make boundary evaluation continuous.

## 6. Relation to the earlier Hardy-anchor obstruction

The functional-analytic mechanism is classical and parallels `WP-068`--`WP-069`, but the mathematical object is different.

`WP-068` proves that the exact **Mangoldt functional on the Prime-Circle Hardy shell algebra** is unbounded in the base-shell Hardy Gram norm. `WP-069` then uses that null sequence to rule out finite-energy positive extensions preserving that Hardy norm.

Here the functional is the coefficient-space endpoint evaluation `ell(x)=sum_j x_j`, and the positive norm is the newly classified **entire finite-band exact-cover cone** of `WP-093`. The explicit sequence (11) proves the new relative-unboundedness statement (5). It then shows directly that the stationary survivor of `WP-094` remains nonclosable after adding any finite-band survivor.

So the abstract lesson is not new: an unbounded boundary functional cannot be turned into a regular positive square merely by adding an energy that still sends an endpoint-normalized sequence to zero. The durable Mathia-specific result is that the latest two independently derived cover-positive classifications collide in exactly this way.

## 7. Matched controls and arithmetic failure

Nothing in the proof uses primes, prime powers, `Lambda`, zeta, the functional equation, or zero data. Equations (8)--(10) hold for every integer cover degree.

The hybrid therefore remains an all-integer universal cover geometry. Even before nonclosability, it has no intrinsic mechanism selecting prime powers or generating the completed-zeta Gamma and polar terms. The nonclosability result is stronger in a different direction: the obvious attempt to combine the nonstationary closable supplier with the stationary infinite-range endpoint supplier does not even produce a regular global positive form on the ambient Hilbert space.

A generalized-prime or non-arithmetic refinement system with the same cover isometries obeys the identical argument.

## 8. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract ingredients are classical quadratic-form theory: the sequential closability criterion for positive forms, Cauchy--Schwarz for a positive form, and the fact that a continuous Hilbert-space functional must be bounded in the relevant norm. The harmonic test sequence and its estimates are elementary Hardy/Cesaro-scale calculations. `WP-093` already identifies its critical Dirichlet generator with the classical Cesaro operator through `G^{-1}=C^*C`, while `WP-094` uses classical Herglotz theory for the stationary cone.

A targeted literature check over nonclosable positive quadratic forms, unbounded boundary/evaluation functionals, weighted discrete Dirichlet energies, Hardy inequalities, and Cesaro operators found the expected classical functional-analytic machinery but no distinct arithmetic or Weil-positive mechanism hidden in this additive hybrid. No theorem-level novelty is claimed for the closability criterion or harmonic estimates.

The Mathia-specific content is the exact combination of the two current cover classifications:

\[
\boxed{
\text{complete finite-band positive cone}
+
\text{complete stationary positive cone}
\Longrightarrow
\text{endpoint coefficient either absent or nonclosable}.
}
\tag{29}
\]

This is a structural narrowing of the current research frontier, not a reformulation of Weil positivity and not evidence for RH.

## 9. Sharp boundary of the no-go

The theorem rules out only the additive hybrid

\[
q_{a,b}+c|\ell|^2.
\]

It does **not** classify:

1. arbitrary position-dependent infinite-range kernels not decomposable into these two pieces;
2. kernels whose absolute-position dependence and long-range interaction are coupled nonlinearly before the form is taken;
3. genuinely prime-sensitive incidence or arithmetic labels acting before reduction to the one-dimensional cover coordinate;
4. a global/archimedean sector that transforms nontrivially under refinement rather than remaining a fixed added form;
5. domain-changing or singular couplings whose closed form is not the algebraic sum (6);
6. quotients, cohomological/intersection constructions, or graded mechanisms whose final sign theorem is not ordinary Hilbert-form positivity.

A counterexample to the present finding would require either an error in the exact estimates (13)--(16), or a `c>0` hybrid (6) that is closable despite the explicit sequence (18)--(24).

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{finite range + position dependence}
&\Longrightarrow&
