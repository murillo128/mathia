# WP-350 — Unitary pre-differentiation compressions are reducing restrictions, and Berry terms cannot erase same-sign ramification in trace

## Claim

`WP-349` left one precise escape open: choose an `s`- or `t`-dependent channel subspace **before** taking the scattering logarithmic derivative. Differentiating the moving frame then introduces connection/Berry terms, so the post-differentiation positivity argument of `WP-349` does not by itself apply.

For the actual squarefree congruence scattering matrix, that escape closes whenever the compressed object is required to remain a genuine unitary scattering matrix on the critical line.

Let

\[
U(t)=\Phi_N\!\left(\frac12+it\right)
\tag{1}
\]

be the finite cusp scattering matrix of squarefree `Gamma_0(N)`, and let

\[
V(t):K\longrightarrow H_N,
\qquad V(t)^*V(t)=I_K,
\tag{2}
\]

be any `C^1` moving isometry of fixed positive rank `k=dim K`. Put

\[
B(t)=V(t)^*U(t)V(t).
\tag{3}
\]

If `B(t)` is unitary, then `Ran V(t)` is automatically a reducing subspace of `U(t)`. Thus a unitary compression is not a genuinely lossy/adaptive compression at all: it is a moving representation of an exact scattering restriction.

More importantly, although differentiating the moving frame does create connection terms at matrix level, **their trace cancels exactly**. With the Hermitian critical-line phase generators

\[
Q_U(t):=-i\dot U(t)U(t)^{-1},
\qquad
Q_B(t):=-i\dot B(t)B(t)^{-1},
\tag{4}
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}Q_B(t)
=
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V(t)^*Q_U(t)V(t)\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

For squarefree congruence scattering, `WP-349` gives

\[
Q_U(t)
=
a(t)I+
\sum_{p\mid N}F_p(t),
\qquad
a(t)=\frac{g'}{g}\!\left(\frac12+it\right),
\tag{6}
\]

and, for every bad prime,

\[
F_p(t)\preceq-c_p I,
\qquad
c_p=
\frac{2\log p\sqrt p}{\sqrt p+1}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently every nonzero unitary moving compression satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}Q_B(t)
\le
k\,a(t)-k\sum_{p\mid N}c_p
<k\,a(t)
\qquad(N>1).
}
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore no source-forced moving channel compression which preserves critical-line unitarity can strip the ramified local factors and retain only the common unramified zeta phase, even if the subspace is chosen **before** differentiation and even if one hoped to use Berry/connection terms generated by that motion.

The only surviving pre-differentiation compression escape is to allow `B(t)` to become a proper contraction/nonunitary object. But then the ordinary unitary scattering logarithmic derivative and its inherited Maaß--Selberg/Wigner--Smith Hermitian structure are lost. Any positivity recovered from such a defect object would require a genuinely new theorem rather than inherited scattering unitarity.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + UNITARY-COMPRESSION-RIGIDITY + MOVING-FRAME-TRACE-CANCELLATION + SAME-SIGN-RAMIFICATION-NO-GO + MATCHED-CONTROL + DECISIVE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Why a unitary compression must be a reducing restriction

The relevant operator statement is elementary and more general than the present arithmetic setting.

Let `U` be unitary on a Hilbert space `H`, let `V:K -> H` be an isometry, and set

\[
B=V^*UV.
\tag{9}
\]

Assume `B` is unitary. For every `x in K`,

\[
\|x\|
=\|Bx\|
=\|V^*UVx\|
\le\|UVx\|
=\|x\|.
\tag{10}
\]

Hence equality holds in the orthogonal-compression inequality. Writing `P=VV^*`, equality

\[
\|V^*y\|=\|y\|
\tag{11}
\]

holds exactly when `y` lies in `Ran V`. Therefore

\[
UVx\in\operatorname{Ran}V
\quad\text{for every }x,
\tag{12}
\]

so

\[
UV=VB.
\tag{13}
\]

Because `B` is onto, (13) also gives

\[
U(\operatorname{Ran}V)=\operatorname{Ran}V.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus `Ran V` is invariant under both `U` and `U^{-1}=U^*`; it is reducing. No finite-dimensional assumption is required for this conclusion.

This is standard unitary-part/contraction theory, not a novel operator theorem. Its role here is to expose what the phrase “moving unitary compression” actually permits: at each parameter value it can only choose an exact reducing scattering sector.

## 2. The moving-frame terms cancel from the trace phase

Now let `U(t)`, `V(t)`, and `B(t)` be `C^1`, with `B(t)` unitary for every `t` in an interval. Define

\[
A(t)=V(t)^*\dot V(t).
\tag{15}
\]

Differentiating `V^*V=I` gives

\[
A(t)^*=-A(t).
\tag{16}
\]

Because the compression is unitary, Section 1 gives `UV=VB`. Reduction also gives

\[
V^*U=BV^*.
\tag{17}
\]

Differentiating (3), and using (15)--(17), yields

\[
\dot B
=-AB+V^*\dot U V+BA.
\tag{18}
\]

The first and last terms are precisely the moving-frame connection terms. Multiplying by `B^{-1}` and taking the trace,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(\dot B B^{-1})
&=-\operatorname{tr}A
+\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*\dot U V B^{-1}\right)
+\operatorname{tr}(BAB^{-1})\\
&=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*\dot U V B^{-1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Since the restriction is reducing,

\[
B^{-1}=V^*U^{-1}V,
\qquad
P U^{-1}V=U^{-1}V,
\tag{20}
\]

and therefore

\[
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*\dot U V B^{-1}\right)
=
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*\dot U U^{-1}V\right).
\tag{21}
\]

Multiplying by `-i` proves (5).

This is the exact place where the Berry escape collapses. The connection can alter the **matrix representative** of the compressed phase generator, but it enters as a commutator/gauge term and carries zero trace. Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{tr}Q_B(t)
=-i\frac{d}{dt}\log\det B(t)
\tag{22}
\]

is basis-invariant, so a moving orthonormal frame cannot manufacture an additional determinant phase.

For rank one the statement is even stronger: there is no nontrivial matrix commutator at all. A unitary scalar compression must be an actual scattering eigenvalue, so the moving phase of the chosen unit vector cancels before differentiation.

## 3. Squarefree congruence scattering leaves a strict ramified trace defect

For the squarefree cusp space, `WP-242` and `WP-348` give the fixed Walsh--Hadamard channel factorization

\[
\lambda_\varepsilon(s)
=g(s)\prod_{p\mid N}\nu_{p,\varepsilon_p}(s),
\qquad
g(s)=\frac{\zeta^\star(2s-1)}{\zeta^\star(2s)}.
\tag{23}
\]

`WP-349` differentiates these factors on `s=1/2+it` and proves that each bad-prime contribution has one strict sign in **every** channel. In operator form this is exactly (6)--(7).

Insert (6) into the trace identity (5):

\[
\operatorname{tr}Q_B(t)
=k a(t)
+
\sum_{p\mid N}
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*F_p(t)V\right).
\tag{24}
\]

For an isometry of rank `k`, (7) implies

\[
V^*F_p(t)V\preceq-c_p I_K,
\tag{25}
\]

hence

\[
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(V^*F_p(t)V\right)
\le-kc_p.
\tag{26}
\]

Summing (26) gives (8).

Thus the obstruction does not depend on which reducing subspace is selected, on eigenvalue crossings, or on whether the reducing subspace moves with `t`. One does not need to prove that a moving subspace follows a fixed set of Walsh channels. The strict operator bound from `WP-349`, together with the gauge-invariant trace identity, handles all unitary moving compressions at once.

In particular, a hoped-for compressed phase generator

\[
Q_B(t)=a(t)I_K
\tag{27}
\]

is impossible for every `N>1`. More weakly, any construction whose only requirement is that its determinant phase reproduce the unramified block would need

\[
\operatorname{tr}Q_B(t)=k a(t),
\tag{28}
\]

and is already contradicted by (8).

## 4. What this does and does not close

**It closes the precise pre-differentiation unitary-compression escape left by `WP-349`.** Choosing the subspace first does produce connection terms after differentiation, but preservation of unitarity forces the subspace to reduce the scattering matrix, and the connection terms have zero trace. The same-sign ramified phase survives with a quantitative gap.

**It does not claim that every nonunitary compression is useless.** If `B=V^*UV` is not unitary, it is merely a contraction. Its polar factor, defect operators

\[
D_B=(I-B^*B)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_{B^*}=(I-BB^*)^{1/2},
\tag{29}
\]

or a unitary dilation could in principle carry new positive data. But that would be a different mechanism: `-i\dot B B^{-1}` is no longer forced to be Hermitian, and the standard lossless-scattering/Maaß--Selberg phase interpretation is not inherited automatically. A successful route must prove a new source-defined positivity theorem for the contraction/defect construction and then show that it yields the full Weil finite-plus-archimedean form.

**It does not strip the ramified factors algebraically.** Dividing (23) by the known `nu_{p,±}` still recovers `g`; that remains the normalization-after-scalarization route already rejected in `WP-348`. The present result says that unitary moving channel geometry cannot perform that division intrinsically.

**It does not prove Weil positivity or RH.** It is a no-go theorem for one increasingly specific candidate architecture.

## 5. Matched controls and edge cases

The operator part is deliberately universal. For any differentiable unitary family

\[
U(t)=u_0(t)\,U_{\mathrm{nuis}}(t)
\tag{30}
\]

whose nuisance phase generator satisfies a strict one-sign bound, every unitary moving compression inherits a same-sign trace contribution. Hence (5) is classical structural geometry; the arithmetic content enters through the source-derived inequality (7).

This matched control is useful rather than damaging to the no-go: the result does **not** smuggle zeta zeros or RH into the argument. It depends only on unitarity, isometric compression, and the explicit local congruence factors already derived independently.

For `N=1` there are no bad primes and (8) becomes equality with the common scalar, exactly as it should. For `k=0` the statement is vacuous. For `k>0` and `N>1`, the strict gap survives uniformly at every regular critical-line point.

Poles and zeros off the critical line are irrelevant to the trace inequality itself; the critical-line local factors are regular and unit modulus. The off-critical divisor remains important for the broader matched-control argument of `WP-348`, but the present obstruction needs only the one-sign phase theorem of `WP-349`.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract statement that a contraction has a unitary part, and that a unitary compression of a unitary operator corresponds to an invariant/reducing unitary sector, belongs to classical Sz.-Nagy--Foias operator theory. Likewise logarithmic derivatives of unitary scattering matrices and Wigner--Smith phase/time-delay matrices are standard. Targeted searches under `unitary compression`, `reducing subspace`, `unitary part of a contraction`, `Wigner-Smith`, `energy-dependent basis`, `Berry connection`, and moving scattering channels returned this classical operator/scattering framework, including literature where Wigner--Smith matrices encode derivatives of unitary scattering matrices and where Berry connections enter scattering descriptions.

A separate search around congruence scattering and moving channel/eigenchannel projections did not identify the exact arithmetic synthesis proved here: combine the unitary-compression equality case with the strict one-sign bad-prime phase operators of `WP-349`, then use trace gauge cancellation to rule out ramification stripping **before** logarithmic differentiation. This is not a historical novelty proof. The durable contribution is the line-specific closure of the exact escape explicitly left open by `WP-349`.

The congruence formulas themselves remain classical, with the squarefree tensor form represented in the repository by the Angelantonj--Florakis--Pioline/Huxley references used in `WP-242`, `WP-348`, and `WP-349`.

## Consequence for `weil_positivity`

The Maaß--Selberg/scattering branch has now crossed a sharper boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{post-differentiation positive moving compression}
\\[-1mm]
\text{fails by the one-sign local operator bound (`WP-349`),}
\\[1mm]
\text{pre-differentiation moving compression + preserved unitarity}
\\[-1mm]
\text{fails by reduction rigidity + trace gauge cancellation (`WP-350`).}
\end{array}
}
\tag{31}
\]

A surviving construction must therefore change category before the final positive readout. The concrete remaining possibilities are narrower: a nonunitary contraction/defect geometry with its own independent positivity theorem; a genuinely noncommuting adelic/global coupling that separates unramified and ramified data before cusp-channel compression; or a different source-defined cohomological/intersection structure that produces the completed finite and archimedean terms together.

Merely allowing the cusp-channel projector to depend on the spectral parameter is no longer an escape if the compressed object is required to remain unitary scattering.
