# WP-055 — minimal cyclotomic double-cover Hodge transfer is degree-flat

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CANDIDATE-LOCAL-BRIDGE + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT` for the most economical higher-genus/Hodge escape left open by `WP-053` and `WP-054`. If one grants the natural hyperelliptic enrichment obtained by double-covering `P^1` over the primitive Prime-Circle shell,

\[
C_n:\qquad y^2=\Phi_n(x),
\]

then repeated-prime refinement really does produce nontrivial `H^1`, a positive Hodge polarization, and a canonical degree-`p` transfer. The same cover tower even contains the critical half-density `p^{-k/2}` and the one-step logarithmic scale `\log p`. However, these quantities arise from universal finite-cover degree calculus: after the canonical `p^{-1/2}` normalization, Hodge pullback is exactly isometric, pull-push is merely the orthogonal projector onto deck invariants, every nontrivial deck element has the same Lefschetz trace `-2`, and the new Prym sector carries only degree/totient multiplicity. Thus adding genuine middle cohomology does **not** by itself create the Frobenius-like arithmetic dynamics or global sign mechanism missing on `P^1`.

The construction below is deliberately treated as a **granted minimal enrichment**, not as something already forced by Prime Circle. `WP-048` intrinsically selects order two in the anchored circle, so a double cover is a natural first Hodge test, but that selector does not logically force the equation `y^2=Phi_n(x)`. The negative conclusion is therefore conditional and sharply scoped: even this favorable higher-genus lift collapses to cover degree unless Mathia supplies additional non-cover correspondence data.

## 1. The minimal shell-branched double cover supplies genuine `H^1`

For `n>2`, the cyclotomic polynomial `Phi_n` is squarefree of even degree `phi(n)`. Let `C_n` denote the smooth projective completion of

\[
\boxed{y^2=\Phi_n(x).}
\tag{1}
\]

Its finite branch locus is exactly the primitive shell

\[
\mu_n^*=\{\zeta:\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=n\},
\tag{2}
\]

and, because `deg Phi_n` is even, the two points at infinity are unbranched. Hence

\[
\boxed{
g_n=\frac{\varphi(n)-2}{2}.}
\tag{3}
\]

For `phi(n)>2` this finally creates the ingredient absent in `WP-053`:

\[
H^1(C_n,\mathbf C)\ne0,
\]

with its ordinary positive Hodge metric on `H^{1,0}` and polarized intersection form on real cohomology.

This is the strongest possible version of the obvious rescue of the genus-zero obstruction: instead of merely decorating the existing `P^1`, grant the primitive shell a compact curve whose middle cohomology is generated by a canonical algebraic cover of that same shell.

## 2. Repeated-prime refinement lifts to an exact cyclic cover

Let `p` be prime and suppose `p\mid n`. The classical cyclotomic identity is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{pn}(x)=\Phi_n(x^p).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Therefore the Prime-Circle old-prime refinement `x\mapsto x^p` lifts without any auxiliary factor to

\[
\boxed{
f_{n,p}:C_{pn}\longrightarrow C_n,
\qquad
f_{n,p}(x,y)=(x^p,y).}
\tag{5}
\]

It is a degree-`p` cyclic map with deck group

\[
\rho_\zeta(x,y)=(\zeta x,y),
\qquad \zeta^p=1.
\tag{6}
\]

For `n>2`, its ramification is completely explicit. The map `x\mapsto x^p` is ramified at `0` and `infinity`; each has two lifts to the even-degree hyperelliptic curve. Thus there are four totally ramified points and total ramification

\[
4(p-1).
\tag{7}
\]

Riemann--Hurwitz checks this exactly. Since `p\mid n`,

\[
\varphi(pn)=p\varphi(n),
\]

so

\[
2g_{pn}-2
=p\varphi(n)-4
=p(2g_n-2)+4(p-1).
\tag{8}
\]

This mirrors the old-prime cover dichotomy of `PC-016`: exponent growth gives a genuine cover. If instead `p\nmid n`, then

\[
\Phi_n(x^p)=\Phi_n(x)\Phi_{pn}(x),
\tag{9}
\]

so the same formula does **not** define a map `C_{pn}->C_n`. New-prime insertion already breaks the simple cover tower.

## 3. Hodge pullback is a pure degree homothety

Let `alpha,beta in H^{1,0}(C_n)`. Because a finite holomorphic map has topological degree `p` and its branch set has measure zero,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle f_{n,p}^*\alpha,f_{n,p}^*\beta\rangle_{Hodge,C_{pn}}
&=i\int_{C_{pn}}f^*\alpha\wedge\overline{f^*\beta}\\
&=p\,i\int_{C_n}\alpha\wedge\overline\beta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\langle f^*\alpha,f^*\beta\rangle_{pn}
=p\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_n.}
\tag{10}
\]

Define the degree-normalized pullback

\[
\boxed{J_{n,p}=p^{-1/2}f_{n,p}^*.}
\tag{11}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{J_{n,p}^*J_{n,p}=I.}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus the inherited Hodge space is transported **isometrically** along the prime-power tower. The presence of genuine `H^1` has not created a nontrivial metric weight; it has reproduced the same degree-normalized flatness found for Weil--Petersson divisor refinement in `PC-041`, now inside honest middle cohomology.

Let `f_*` be the cohomological trace/pushforward. It is adjoint to `f^*`, and the standard push-pull identity gives

\[
\boxed{f_*f^*=pI.}
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
J^*=p^{-1/2}f_*,
\qquad
JJ^*=\frac1p f^*f_*=:P_{inv}.}
\tag{14}
\]

The last operator is exactly the orthogonal projector onto the deck-invariant cohomology. Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
P_{inv}=\frac1p\sum_{\zeta^p=1}\rho_\zeta^*.}
\tag{15}
\]

The complementary Prym/new sector is

\[
\ker f_*=\operatorname{Ran}(I-P_{inv}),
\tag{16}
\]

and is Hodge-orthogonal to the inherited sector. This is genuine geometric positivity, but its canonical correspondence algebra has spectrum only `{0,1}`.

## 4. The critical half-density appears, but only as universal fiber averaging

There is nevertheless an exact numerical bridge worth retaining. The normalized fiber average is

\[
\boxed{E_{n,p}:=\frac1p f_{n,p*}.}
\tag{17}
\]

Using (13)--(14),

\[
E_{n,p}=p^{-1/2}J_{n,p}^*,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{E_{n,p}J_{n,p}=p^{-1/2}I.}
\tag{18}
\]

For a `k`-step repeated-prime refinement of total degree `p^k`, the same calculation gives

\[
\boxed{E^{(k)}J^{(k)}=p^{-k/2}I.}
\tag{19}
\]

The other local scalar is also intrinsic to the cover step:

\[
\boxed{\log\deg(f_{n,p})=\log p.}
\tag{20}
\]

Whenever the target has nonzero `H^1`, the same number is the logarithmic Hodge dilation,

\[
\log\frac{\|f^*\alpha\|^2}{\|\alpha\|^2}=\log p.
\tag{21}
\]

Thus the cover tower contains, without zeros or analytic continuation, the two scalar ingredients

\[
\boxed{
\log p
\quad\text{and}\quad
p^{-k/2},}
\tag{22}
\]

whose product is the local Riemann-Weil coefficient

\[
\boxed{(\log p)p^{-k/2}.}
\tag{23}
\]

This is a real Mathia-side structural bridge, but it must not be overstated. Equation (23) is obtained by multiplying the one-step log-degree label by a `k`-step **averaging transfer coefficient**. It is **not** the Hodge Gram of inherited classes. The actual normalized Hodge Gram is flat by (12): if a class is transported by `J`, its norm and mutual Hodge pairings remain unchanged.

Therefore the positive Poisson/Toeplitz kernel `p^{-|a-b|/2}` does not emerge here as a new Hodge intersection theorem. To turn the contraction scalar in (18) into that positive kernel one must perform the standard positive-definite completion/dilation of a scalar contraction, returning to the local structure already audited in `PC-005`/`PC-006`.

## 5. The deck Lefschetz data are universal, not Mangoldt-like

The higher-genus carrier also fails the most literal Frobenius test. Let `zeta!=1` be any `p`-th root of unity. The nontrivial deck transformation `rho_zeta` fixes exactly four points of `C_{pn}`: the two points above `x=0` and the two points at infinity. Each isolated fixed point has positive topological Lefschetz index, so

\[
L(\rho_\zeta)=4.
\tag{24}
\]

Since the traces on `H^0` and `H^2` are both `1`,

\[
4
=1-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\zeta^*|H^1)+1,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\zeta^*|H^1(C_{pn}))=-2
\qquad(\zeta\ne1).}
\tag{25}
\]

There is also a direct Hodge-basis check. Write

\[
a=\frac{\varphi(n)}2,
\qquad
g_{pn}=pa-1.
\]

A basis of holomorphic forms is

\[
\omega_j=\frac{x^jdx}{y},
\qquad 0\le j\le pa-2,
\tag{26}
\]

and

\[
\rho_\zeta^*\omega_j=\zeta^{j+1}\omega_j.
\tag{27}
\]

For nontrivial `zeta`,

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{pa-2}\zeta^{j+1}
=\sum_{r=1}^{pa-1}\zeta^r
=-1,
\tag{28}
\]

and conjugation supplies the second `-1` on `H^{0,1}`, reproducing (25).

Averaging these traces gives exactly the old-space dimension:

\[
\frac1p\left[
\dim H^1(C_{pn})+(p-1)(-2)
\right]
=\varphi(n)-2
=\dim H^1(C_n).
\tag{29}
\]

Thus the new cohomology has dimension

\[
\boxed{
\dim\ker f_*=(p-1)\varphi(n),}
\tag{30}
\]

but its canonical deck-character statistics reduce to degree and totient data. There is no analogue of the prime-power fixed-point sequence needed in the function-field Frobenius argument.

## 6. Adversarial controls

### 6.1 Replace the cyclotomic curve by an arbitrary finite cover

Equations (10)--(19) are not special to cyclotomy. For any finite holomorphic map of degree `d` between compact curves, degree-normalized pullback is isometric and normalized trace contributes the scalar `d^{-1/2}` relative to that isometry. Thus the apparently suggestive `p^{-1/2}` is a **universal cover-degree half-density**.

The Mathia geometry selects the degree `p` along an old-prime refinement, but the sign theorem attached to the Hodge metric does not know why that degree was called a prime. A matched degree-`p` cover with unrelated branch geometry gives the same transfer coefficient.

### 6.2 Use the positive Prym sector

The Prym complement is a legitimate polarized Hodge subspace, so positivity is not the problem. But its dimension is the elementary value `(p-1)phi(n)` and the projector onto it is `I-P_inv`. No canonical trace or intersection of this projector gives `log p`; inserting the logarithm of the cover degree is again a separate scalar readout.

### 6.3 Iterate deck correspondences instead of transfer

For every nonidentity element of the prime deck group, the Lefschetz trace is the same `-2`. Iteration cycles through the finite group rather than generating weights indexed by all `p^k`. The only growth with `k` comes from composing **different cover levels**, where the transfer has already collapsed to the scalar degree law (19).

### 6.4 Introduce a new prime

Equation (9) prevents the clean cover map. Therefore this construction naturally organizes **individual repeated-prime rays**, exactly where the local coefficient is easiest, but does not provide a canonical cross-prime cohomological correspondence. Taking an orthogonal direct sum over primes would return to separable local positivity and the divergent/global-completion obstruction of `WP-001`, `PC-005`, and `WP-013`.

### 6.5 Identify the double cover with the `q=2` Gamma selector

`WP-048` shows that anchored reflection independently selects order two and that the radial Mellin `q=2` channel contains the Riemann `Gamma_R` logarithmic derivative. That does not imply that the **hyperelliptic sheet involution** in (1) carries the same archimedean response. The present compact Hodge transfer has only discrete cover-degree data and supplies no digamma/polar counterterm. Equating the two appearances of `2` would be target matching unless a new coupling theorem is derived.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

No historical novelty is claimed for the algebraic or Hodge-theoretic ingredients.

- Hyperelliptic/superelliptic curves branched over prescribed divisors, cyclic covers, their deck decompositions, and Prym polarizations are classical. The specialization of the branch divisor to the primitive cyclotomic shell is a Mathia test object, not a claimed new class of curves.
- The cyclotomic identity (4), genus formula, Riemann--Hurwitz calculation, Hodge degree homothety, push-pull identities, invariant projector (15), and Lefschetz trace calculation are standard once the tower is defined.
- `PC-041` already gives a closely matched internal control: divisor-cover pullback in Prime-Circle Weil--Petersson cotangent geometry becomes exactly isometric after square-root degree normalization and is path-flat. The present computation shows that replacing that cotangent bundle by genuine `H^1` does not remove the degree-flatness.
- The local explicit-formula literature already contains contraction/scattering and graded-trace mechanisms; `SOURCES.md` anchors Burnol's nonarchimedean scattering formula. Therefore the appearance of a local contraction coefficient is not itself evidence for a new Weil-positivity mechanism.
- The successful function-field comparison remains the one in `WP-011`/`WP-053`: Frobenius correspondences act nontrivially on global cohomology and their intersections encode arithmetic counts before Hodge index supplies the sign. The cover transfer above instead reduces to isometry plus a projector.

Directed searches for cyclotomic hyperelliptic Hodge towers and cyclic-cover Hodge transfer found broad classical cyclic-cover/Hodge/Prym theory but no reason to elevate the scalar bridge (22)--(23) to historical novelty. Its durable role here is diagnostic: the most economical way to add middle cohomology to the primitive shells still factorizes through universal finite-cover degree calculus.

## 8. Consequence for the Weil-positivity search

The higher-genus escape from `WP-053` is therefore narrowed substantially. The chain

```text
primitive cyclotomic shell
    -> minimal shell-branched double cover C_n
    -> repeated-prime cyclic covers C_{pn} -> C_n
    -> ordinary Hodge polarization / pull-push
    -> global Weil positivity
```

fails at the third arrow as a global mechanism. What survives is only the local numerical bridge

\[
\text{one-step log degree}\times\text{k-step normalized average}
=(\log p)p^{-k/2},
\]

while the actual positive Hodge geometry is transport-flat and the canonical correspondence is an invariant projector.

A viable cohomological continuation must therefore add something qualitatively stronger than genus:

1. a Mathia-forced correspondence whose action is **not** determined solely by finite-cover degree and deck invariants;
2. nonseparable coupling between distinct prime directions before taking a positive quotient or trace;
3. an intrinsic archimedean/infinite-dimensional component producing the `Gamma_R` and polar counterterms in the same object;
4. a sign theorem for the assembled pairing that is independent of RH and of inserted zero data.

In particular, simply replacing the Prime-Circle `P^1` carrier by a shell-branched higher-genus curve does not reproduce the function-field Frobenius/Hodge mechanism. The missing ingredient is not merely `H^1`; it is **arithmetically nontrivial global correspondence dynamics inside a positive cohomological structure**.
