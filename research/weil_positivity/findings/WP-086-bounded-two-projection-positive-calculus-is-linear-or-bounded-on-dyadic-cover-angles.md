# WP-086 — Bounded two-projection positive calculus is linear-or-bounded on dyadic cover angles

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + MATCHED-CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION`.

`WP-085` leaves open an operator-valued escape: retain the noncommuting cover projections before taking the reduced determinant. On the least nontrivial reference cover this escape can be classified sharply. For odd `n`, the pair of finite-cell range projections

\[
P:=P_2|_{E_{2n}},\qquad Q:=P_n|_{E_{2n}}
\]

has exactly one noncommuting Halmos two-projection block. Its principal-angle defect is

\[
\delta_n=\sin^2\theta_n=\frac1n.
\]

All remaining blocks are commuting multiplicity background. Consequently, for **any fixed bounded norm-continuous positive construction from the two projections** — equivalently, any positive element of the universal `C*`-algebra generated by two projections evaluated at `(P,Q)` — the finite-cell trace has only two possible scales:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}A(P,Q)=\alpha_A n+O_A(1),\qquad \alpha_A\ge0.}
\]

If the construction vanishes on commuting projection pairs, then `alpha_A=0` and in fact its trace on this family tends to zero. It therefore cannot produce the intrinsic logarithmic degree `log n` exposed by `WP-085`.

The logarithm appears only after a **singular endpoint operation**. Indeed, the reduced pseudolog of the positive angle defect has the one nonzero interaction eigenvalue

\[
-\log\delta_n=\log n,
\]

but `-log x` is unbounded as `x->0`, and assigning zero to the exact kernel is discontinuous there. This is precisely the operator-level version of the reduced-logdet route already classified in `WP-085`; it inherits its reference relativity, even-degree blindness, signed Möbius primitive, and failure to assemble into a positive degree kernel.

Thus bounded noncommuting operator geometry does not rescue the canonical cover-angle mechanism. Any surviving cross-cover route must be singular/unbounded, explicitly degree-dependent, nonperiodic, or genuinely coupled to an additional global/archimedean sector before scalarization.

## 1. Exact Halmos block for the dyadic reference

`WP-085` proves that for odd `n` the positive defect

\[
\Delta_{2,n}=P(I-Q)P|_{\operatorname{Ran}P}
\]

has eigenvalues

\[
0,\quad \frac1n,\quad \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n-2}.
\tag{1}
\]

The zero mode is the common constant vector. The `n-2` unit modes are vectors in `Ran P` orthogonal to `Ran Q`. The remaining principal angle is therefore characterized by

\[
\sin^2\theta_n=\frac1n,
\qquad
\cos^2\theta_n=1-\frac1n.
\tag{2}
\]

The standard two-projection decomposition gives, up to unitary equivalence,

\[
E_{2n}
\cong
H_{11}
\oplus H_{10}^{\oplus(n-2)}
\oplus H_{\theta_n}
\oplus H_{00}^{\oplus(n-1)},
\tag{3}
\]

where on the one-dimensional commuting blocks `(P,Q)` equals respectively `(1,1)`, `(1,0)`, and `(0,0)`, while on the unique two-dimensional noncommuting block

\[
P_{\theta_n}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q_{\theta_n}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-\frac1n & \frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{n}\\[3pt]
\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{n} & \frac1n
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]

The multiplicities in (3) sum to `2n`, and the ranks are

\[
\operatorname{rank}P=n,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}Q=2,
\]

as required. As `n->infinity` through odd integers, the generic block converges in norm to the commuting endpoint

\[
(P_{\theta_n},Q_{\theta_n})
\longrightarrow
\left(
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\right).
\tag{5}
\]

So the entire genuinely noncommuting part of the cover pair becomes asymptotically commuting while its multiplicity remains exactly one.

## 2. Fixed bounded positive two-projection calculus cannot have logarithmic trace

Let `A` be any fixed positive element of the norm-closed algebra generated universally by two projections. Concretely, it may be obtained as a norm limit of noncommutative `*`-polynomials in `P` and `Q`; no degree-dependent coefficient is allowed.

On the one-dimensional commuting representations write

\[
a_{11}:=A(1,1),\qquad
a_{10}:=A(1,0),\qquad a_{00}:=A(0,0).
\tag{6}
\]

Positivity gives

\[
a_{11},a_{10},a_{00}\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]

Let `A_{theta_n}` be the `2 x 2` value of the same element on (4). Norm continuity and (5) imply

\[
\sup_n\|A_{\theta_n}\|\le\|A\|,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}A_{\theta_n}=O_A(1).
\tag{8}
\]

Using (3),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}_{E_{2n}}A(P,Q)
=
a_{11}+(n-2)a_{10}+(n-1)a_{00}
+\operatorname{tr}A_{\theta_n}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}_{E_{2n}}A(P,Q)
=(a_{10}+a_{00})n+O_A(1).
}
\tag{10}
\]

Because the coefficient is nonnegative, there is no cancellation inside the positive construction that could leave a `log n` term:

- if `a_{10}+a_{00}>0`, the trace is linear in the cover degree;
- if `a_{10}=a_{00}=0`, the trace is bounded.

In particular, if `A` is an **interaction defect** that vanishes whenever its two projection arguments commute, then all scalar commuting evaluations vanish and continuity at (5) gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}A(P,Q)
=\operatorname{tr}A_{\theta_n}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the bounded operator-valued noncommutativity itself has the wrong scale for the desired logarithmic arithmetic response.

The same obstruction applies to every fixed bounded spectral or `C*`-algebraic expression built from `P,Q` before scalarization. Individual eigenvalues are uniformly bounded by `||A||`; the only growing resource available to a regular positive trace is the multiplicity of the commuting blocks, which is linear in `n` rather than logarithmic.

## 3. Canonical positive controls exhibit both sides of the dichotomy

The abstract classification is visible in the simplest positive operators.

### Commutator energy: pure interaction, but it decays

Let

\[
C=[P,Q].
\]

On every commuting block `C=0`. On (4), with `delta_n=1/n`,

\[
C^*C
=\delta_n(1-\delta_n)I_2
=\frac{n-1}{n^2}I_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|[P,Q]\|_{HS}^2
=\operatorname{Tr}(C^*C)
=\frac{2(n-1)}{n^2}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This is a canonical positive noncommutative energy, but it loses rather than gains logarithmic degree.

### Raw angle/gap energies: positive, but degree-dominated

From (1),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\Delta_{2,n}
=n-2+\frac1n.
\tag{13}
\]

Likewise the squared gap has one unit contribution on each `H_{10}` block and two `delta_n` eigenvalues on the generic block, so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(P-Q)^2
=n-2+\frac2n.
}
\tag{14}
\]

These are positive and boundedly constructed, but their growth is the trivial rank mismatch of the two cover ranges. Removing that linear background by subtraction is not an inherited positive operation.

Equations (12)--(14) are matched controls for (10): bounded interaction-sensitive energy decays, while bounded positive energy that charges commuting mismatch grows linearly.

## 4. Exact logarithmic degree forces a singular endpoint functional

For odd `n`, all the nontrivial principal-angle arithmetic of `WP-085` is concentrated in

\[
\delta_n=\frac1n.
\]

The operator-level transform that reproduces its logarithm is the reduced pseudolog

\[
L_{2,n}:=(-\log)'\Delta_{2,n},
\]

defined to be `-log x` on positive eigenvalues and zero on the exact kernel. Its spectrum is

\[
0,\quad\log n,\quad\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-2},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{L_{2,n}\succeq0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}L_{2,n}=\log n.}
\tag{15}
\]

This does **not** contradict (10): the scalar function

\[
f(0)=0,\qquad f(x)=-\log x\quad(x>0)
\tag{16}
\]

is neither continuous nor bounded at the commuting endpoint `x=0`. The logarithmic degree is exactly the price of crossing out of the bounded continuous two-projection calculus.

Moreover (15) is not a new escape from `WP-085`; it is the same reduced-logdet invariant before the final trace, since

\[
\operatorname{Tr}L_{2,n}
=-\log\det{}'\Delta_{2,n}.
\tag{17}
\]

All decisive limitations of that route remain:

1. for even `n`, the ranges are nested and the interaction defect is entirely commuting, so the dyadic reference remains blind;
2. the construction is reference-cover relative;
3. Möbius primitivization yields the signed parity-twisted Mangoldt shadow of `WP-085`, not `Lambda(n)` positively;
4. the scalar pairwise logdet fails PSD and conditional-sign tests;
5. there is still no intrinsic Gamma/polar sector or global Weil autocorrelation form.

So the operator-valued lift identifies **where** the logarithm lives — a spectral singularity at an asymptotically commuting angle — but does not restore a global sign theorem.

## 5. Matched controls and novelty audit

Nothing in the linear-or-bounded dichotomy is specific to arithmetic. Any family of pairs of finite-dimensional subspaces with one fixed-multiplicity principal-angle block approaching a commuting endpoint and only growing commuting multiplicities has the same behavior. The Mathia-specific input is solely the exact cover identity

\[
\sin^2\theta_n=\frac1n
\]

from `WP-085`.

The operator theory is classical. Halmos' two-subspaces theorem gives the canonical decomposition of a pair of orthogonal projections into commuting sectors plus generic `2 x 2` principal-angle blocks; the modern survey by Böttcher and Spitkovsky develops the corresponding algebra generated by two projections and its functional calculus. The present finding does not claim novelty for that theory. Its contribution is the arithmetic specialization and the resulting no-go: the unique Mathia dyadic cover angle approaches the commuting boundary at exactly the rate that makes every bounded continuous positive readout linear-or-bounded, while `log n` requires the singular reduced logarithm already known to fail global assembly.

This also blocks a common false promotion. The fact that a positive operator such as `(-log)' Delta` carries `log n` does not mean that a bounded geometric pairing has discovered the von Mangoldt weight. The logarithm has been inserted through the unique singular spectral function that turns the vanishing angle `1/n` into `log n`.

## 6. Prior-art boundary

The durable classical anchors are:

- P. R. Halmos, *Two subspaces*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society **144** (1969), 381--389, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-1969-0251519-5`;
- A. Böttcher and I. M. Spitkovsky, *A gentle guide to the basics of two projections theory*, Linear Algebra and its Applications **432** (2010), no. 6, 1412--1459, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2009.11.002`.

These references classicalize the two-projection decomposition and continuous algebraic calculus. They do not contain the Mathia cover specialization `(2,n)`, its exact angle `1/n`, or the arithmetic comparison with `log n`.

No conflict was found with Weil positivity, Frobenius/cohomology, Sonin/localization, Hilbert--Pólya, Connes/trace, intersection-form, or related prior art: the result is a local negative classification of one Mathia-native operator escape, not a new global positivity mechanism.

## Research consequence

`WP-085` left open the possibility that retaining the full noncommuting operator before determinant might provide the missing positive logarithmic carrier. For the canonical dyadic cross-cover family, that possibility now has a sharp boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed bounded positive two-projection calculus}
\Longrightarrow
\text{linear rank background or bounded/vanishing interaction},
}
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{
\log n
\Longrightarrow
\text{singular calculus at the collapsing principal angle}.
}
\]

A viable continuation must therefore leave this regular two-projection setting. The remaining live possibilities are a singular/unbounded construction with its own independent domain/sign theorem, a nonperiodic boundary coupling, an operator that mixes more than one cover/reference before the principal-angle limit, or a genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean structure. Merely replacing the reduced determinant by a bounded positive function of the same two range projections cannot work.
