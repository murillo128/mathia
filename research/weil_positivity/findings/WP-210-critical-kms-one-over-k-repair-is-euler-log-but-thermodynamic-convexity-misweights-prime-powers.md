## 1. The exact `1/k` primitive on one prime ray is the local Euler logarithm

Fix a prime `p` and write

\[
r_p=p^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

The critical KMS midpoint identified in `WP-209` supplies the factor `r_p^k=p^{-k/2}` on the orbit `p^k`. If one energy derivative is to produce the Mangoldt coefficient, then the pre-derivative coefficient must be `1/k`. There is a canonical analytic primitive with exactly those coefficients:

\[
G_p(t)
:=-\log\!\left(1-r_pe^{it\log p}\right)
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{r_p^k}{k}e^{ikt\log p}.
\tag{2}
\]

Because `r_p<1`, (2) converges absolutely for each fixed prime. Differentiating termwise gives

\[
\frac1i\,\partial_tG_p(t)
=(\log p)\sum_{k\ge1}r_p^ke^{ikt\log p}.
\tag{3}
\]

Hence the coefficient of the `p^k` mode is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}
=\frac{\Lambda(p^k)}{\sqrt{p^k}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

So the `1/k` normalization demanded by `WP-209` is not merely possible locally; it is canonically generated by the logarithm.

But equation (2) identifies the construction completely. For

\[
s=\frac12-it,
\]

we have

\[
G_p(t)=\log(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the classical local logarithm of the Euler factor. The repair therefore lands exactly on the prior-art boundary already exposed in `WP-012`: `1/k` is the orbit/repetition factor in the Euler logarithm, and the Hamiltonian derivative converts it into Mangoldt weight. No new Mathia-native positivity theorem has appeared.

## 2. The global critical primitive is outside the ordinary thermodynamic domain

The local convergence in (2) can obscure the global obstruction. Formally summing over primes gives

\[
G(t)
=\sum_pG_p(t)
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{p^{-k/2}}{k}e^{ikt\log p}.
\tag{6}
\]

At `t=0`, every term is positive and the `k=1` subseries is

\[
\sum_pp^{-1/2},
\tag{7}
\]

which diverges. Thus (6) is not an ordinary globally summable log-partition/free-energy object at the critical midpoint. The same obstruction applies after differentiation: at `t=0`, (3) summed over primes contains

\[
\sum_p\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p},
\tag{8}
\]

and diverges as well.

In the ordinary Euler-product half-plane `Re(s)>1`, one instead has the classical identities

\[
\log\zeta(s)
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p^{-ks}}{k},
\qquad
-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}(\log p)p^{-ks}.
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore the direct way to interpret (6)--(8) on `Re(s)=1/2` is analytic continuation of `log zeta` or `-zeta'/zeta`. That is precisely a classical zeta representation of known arithmetic/zero data, not an independent geometric positivity theorem. It also inherits the pole/zero singularities of the continued zeta object rather than producing a source-level positive quadratic form.

This distinction is essential. The local Euler logarithm is a legitimate exact **coefficient generator**; the research mandate asks for a global structure whose own positivity proves the Weil-type sign. Equation (9) supplies the former but not the latter.

## 3. The local Euler logarithm itself is not positive on the KMS phase direction

One might hope that the connected/logarithmic primitive (2), rather than its coefficient measure, already has a useful sign. Its real part is

\[
\Re G_p(t)
=-\log\left|1-r_pe^{it\log p}\right|.
\tag{10}
\]

At phase zero,

\[
\Re G_p(0)=-\log(1-r_p)>0,
\tag{11}
\]

whereas at the opposite phase `t=pi/log p`,

\[
\Re G_p\!\left(\frac\pi{\log p}\right)
=-\log(1+r_p)<0.
\tag{12}
\]

So the exact critical-phase primitive is sign-indefinite even before attempting global assembly. Its time derivative in (3) reproduces the desired arithmetic ray, but that identity is a Fourier/Euler identity, not a positivity statement.

The positive object naturally associated with the same thermodynamics instead lives in the inverse-temperature direction, where log-partition functions are convex. That route can be tested exactly.

## 4. Thermodynamic convexity has the wrong prime-power multiplicity

For a real parameter `sigma>0`, define the local log-partition contribution

\[
L_p(\sigma)
:=-\log(1-p^{-\sigma})
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p^{-k\sigma}}{k}.
\tag{13}
\]

The first derivative is

\[
-L_p'(\sigma)
=(\log p)\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus (14) again produces the exact repetition-independent Mangoldt ray. Numerically it is positive. But it is a **first thermodynamic variation / mean-energy scalar**, not a Hessian or Gram theorem that makes the global Weil quadratic form nonnegative. `WP-005` already shows why positive finite coefficients are insufficient: the exact Weil autocorrelation lift turns the finite prime comb into an indefinite translation form.

The source-level sign theorem supplied by ordinary statistical mechanics is convexity of `log Z`. Here

\[
\boxed{
L_p''(\sigma)
=(\log p)^2\sum_{k\ge1}k\,p^{-k\sigma}>0.
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is the local energy variance/susceptibility. The problem is visible coefficient by coefficient: the `p^k` orbit is weighted by `k(log p)^2`, not by `log p`. The same derivative that upgrades a first variation into a canonical positive curvature destroys the required prime-power normalization.

The pattern is exact at every order. For `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
L_p^{(m)}(\sigma)
=(-1)^m(\log p)^m
\sum_{k\ge1}k^{m-1}p^{-k\sigma}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore the first derivative is uniquely the derivative order for which the repetition factor is `k^0`. Every ordinary higher cumulant/thermodynamic derivative inserts a nonconstant power of the orbit multiplicity.

## 5. No finite source-uniform differential combination repairs the curvature

The mismatch in (16) is not specific to choosing exactly the second derivative. Let

\[
P(D)=\sum_{m=1}^M a_mD^m,
\qquad D=\frac d{d\sigma},
\tag{17}
\]

with coefficients `a_m` independent of the prime and repetition index. Applying (17) to (13), the coefficient of `p^{-k\sigma}` is

\[
\sum_{m=1}^M
a_m(-1)^m(\log p)^m k^{m-1}.
\tag{18}
\]

To equal `log p` for every `k>=1` and every prime, (18) must be constant as a polynomial in `k`. Hence all terms of degree at least one vanish:

\[
a_m=0\qquad(m\ge2),
\tag{19}
\]

and then necessarily `a_1=-1`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
P(D)L_p\text{ has exact Mangoldt multiplicity for all }p^k
\Longrightarrow
P(D)=-D.
}
\tag{20}
\]

The only finite source-uniform derivative construction that preserves the prime-power coefficient is the first variation itself. No nontrivial inclusion of the positive Hessian or higher thermodynamic cumulants survives the exact arithmetic matching.

An affine reparametrization `sigma -> a sigma+b` changes the `m`-th derivative only by the common factor `a^m`, so it does not alter this conclusion. More generally, a nondegenerate smooth reparametrization cannot turn the second derivative into the target for all repetitions: the chain rule gives a combination of `L_p'` and `L_p''`, and the coefficient of the `k` term can vanish for every `k` only by eliminating the second-derivative contribution. A mode-dependent division by `k` would work, but that manually reintroduces exactly the orbit normalization that the Euler logarithm supplied in the first place.

## 6. Aggressive falsification and exact scope

**Could one simply call `-L_p'` positive and stop?** No. Equation (14) is indeed a positive scalar for real `sigma`, but this is coefficient/expectation positivity. `WP-005` proves that the Weil autocorrelation lift of the same positive Mangoldt measure is indefinite. The missing theorem is positivity of the assembled quadratic form, not positivity of each coefficient.

**Could free-energy convexity be the missing independent theorem?** It is independent and canonical, but equation (15) falsifies the arithmetic match: variance weights repetitions by `k` and adds another factor `log p`. Equation (20) shows that no finite source-uniform admixture of higher thermodynamic derivatives fixes this while retaining exact prime-power coefficients.

**Could the critical KMS midpoint make the global Euler logarithm converge?** No. The midpoint fixes the desired half-density but places the Euler logarithm far outside its ordinary global convergence region, as (7) shows. The same source property that selects the exponent does not regularize the all-prime mass.

**Could analytic continuation repair global divergence?** It assigns the classical zeta/log-derivative continuation, but then the construction has reverted to a zeta representation whose singular structure is controlled by the pole and zeros. That is explicitly not the independent Mathia-native positivity requested by the mandate.

**Could a different bounded correlation avoid `1/k`?** `WP-209` already shows that exact all-prime Mangoldt mass cannot live in one bounded positive KMS correlation. The present finding tests the canonical connected/unbounded-looking repair of that exact defect, not every conceivable affiliated observable.

**Could an unbounded affiliated observable or closable form still work?** Yes. It would have to obtain its domain and positivity from an operator/geometric theorem not equivalent to log-Euler analytic continuation, and its all-prime limit would have to be proved rather than inherited from finite truncations. This remains a live route.

**Could a boundary/cohomological assembly use the Euler first variation only as one local input?** Yes. The no-go is specifically against identifying Euler-log/free-energy convexity itself with the missing global sign theorem. A genuinely nonlocal assembly may use the exact finite coefficients while deriving sign from a different source, provided the same structure also produces the archimedean and polar sectors canonically.
