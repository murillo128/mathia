# WP-452: Finite phase selection cannot orient infinitely many rays at bounded energy cost

## Claim and evidence boundary

**EXACT-DERIVED; CLASSICAL HARMONIC-ANALYSIS INGREDIENTS;
CATEGORY OBSTRUCTION.** Phase-modulation invariance is not needed
to obstruct simultaneous positivity of infinitely many separate
prime rays, provided the energy is bounded in the original
Maharam norm.

Use the fixed source
\[
 \Omega=\prod_p\mathbb N_0,\quad
 \nu=\bigotimes_p\nu_p,\quad \nu_p(j)=(1-p^{-1})p^{-j},\quad
 \mathcal K=L^2(\Omega\times\mathbb R,d\nu\,e^sds),
\]
\[
 (V_pf)(J)=\sqrt p\,1_{J_p\ge1}f(J-e_p),\qquad
 (U_ch)(s)=e^{-c/2}h(s-c),\qquad
 W_p=V_p\otimes U_{\log p},\quad W_m=\prod_pW_p^{v_p(m)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(Q\) be a positive semidefinite sesquilinear form on all of
\(\mathcal K\), conjugate-linear in its first variable, such that
\[
 0\le Q[f]\le C\|f\|^2,\qquad
 Q[W_m f]=Q[f]\quad(m\ge1),\qquad C<\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]
Let \(\mathcal H_Q\) be its quotient energy completion and
\(S_p[f]=[W_pf]\) the induced commuting isometries. Define
\[
 D_{p,Q}=-(\log p)\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k/2}
                     (S_p^k+S_p^{*k}).
 \tag{3}
\]
The series converges in operator norm; these are energy-space
adjoints, as in WP-451.

Then
\[
 \boxed{\{p:D_{p,Q}\ge0\}\text{ infinite}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q=0.}
 \tag{4}
\]
No diagonal assumption, phase-modulation invariance, coercivity,
faithfulness, or factorization of \(Q\) is required. In fact,
covariance in (2) is needed only for the semigroup generated by
the infinitely many primes whose defects are required to be
nonnegative.

The finite boundary is different. For every finite nonempty prime
set \(F\), there is a nonzero bounded form \(Q_F\) satisfying
all-prime dilation covariance such that every \(D_{p,Q_F}\),
\(p\in F\), is strictly positive. Its construction explicitly
selects favorable phase arcs and therefore supplies a matched
control, not an arithmetic sign law.

This is a statement about **separate ray positivity**. It does not
assert that positivity of a completed Weil form implies (3) is
nonnegative for each prime. Mixed-prime, archimedean and polar
terms may change that comparison. No RH consequence, historical
novelty, formal verification or independent adversarial approval
is claimed.

## Covariant orbit measures and domination

Fix a finite prime set \(F\), write \(W^a=\prod_{p\in F}W_p^{a_p}\),
and fix \(f\in\mathcal K\). Polarization of (2) gives
\[
 Q(W^a f,W^b f)=c_Q(b-a),\qquad
 c_Q(h)=Q(W^{h_-}f,W^{h_+}f),\quad h\in\mathbb Z^F,
 \tag{5}
\]
where \(h_+,h_-\) are its coordinatewise positive and negative
parts. Cancelling the componentwise minimum of \(a,b\) proves
(5). For any finite set of integer indices, translating all of
them into \(\mathbb N_0^F\) shows that \(c_Q\) is positive
definite. The same holds for the original-norm coefficients
\(c_I\), and for \(C c_I-c_Q\), by (2).

Finite-dimensional Herglotz representation therefore gives
unique positive measures on \(\mathbb T^F\) with
\[
 c_Q(h)=\int z^h\,d\mu_{Q,f,F}(z),\qquad
 \mu_{Q,f,F}(\mathbb T^F)=Q[f],\qquad
 0\le\mu_{Q,f,F}\le C\mu_{I,f,F}.
 \tag{6}
\]
No infinite-product measure for \(Q\) is needed. To see directly
the representation being used, for a positive-definite \(c\)
take the nonnegative densities
\[
 N^{-|F|}\sum_{a,b\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^F}c(b-a)z^{a-b}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Their masses equal \(c(0)\); their \(h\)-moments tend to \(c(h)\).
A weakly convergent subsequence on the compact torus supplies the
measure, and density of trigonometric polynomials proves
uniqueness. Applying this to \(C c_I-c_Q\) proves the domination
in (6). This also avoids any multivariable Fejer--Riesz
factorization assumption.

Put \(r_p=p^{-1/2}\), and use normalized Haar measure \(dm\).
The scalar ray symbol is
\[
 w_p(z)=(\log p)\left(1-\frac{1-r_p^2}{|1-r_pz|^2}\right),
 \qquad
 A_p=\{z\in\mathbb T:\operatorname{Re}z\le r_p\}
       =\{w_p\ge0\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
For every analytic polynomial \(P\), (3), (5), and absolute
convergence give
\[
 \langle[P(W)f],D_{p,Q}[P(W)f]\rangle_Q
       =\int w_p(z_p)|P(z)|^2\,d\mu_{Q,f,F}(z).
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(D_{p,Q}\ge0\), the right side is nonnegative. Multiplication
by a monomial turns any Laurent polynomial into an analytic one
without changing its boundary modulus. Uniform approximation of
continuous square roots then shows that \(w_p\mu_{Q,f,F}\) is a
positive measure. Thus it has no mass where \(w_p<0\). If every
prime in \(F\) is oriented, (6) implies
\[
 Q[f]\le C\,\mu_{I,f,F}\left(\prod_{p\in F}A_p\right).
 \tag{10}
\]
The measure for \(Q\) can have arbitrary correlations. Only the
reference measure on the right will be evaluated.

## Fresh primes make every fixed cylinder packet vanish

Take \(f\) depending on a finite set \(E\) of valuation coordinates
and on the real variable, and choose \(F\cap E=\varnothing\).
Such packets span a dense subspace of \(\mathcal K\).
Flatten the real measure by \(f\mapsto e^{s/2}f\) and take the
unitary Fourier transform with convention \(e^{-i\xi s}\).
Write \(\widehat f_E(\xi)\in L^2(\Omega_E,\nu_E)\) for the
result. Then \(U_c\) is multiplication by \(e^{-i\xi c}\).
Directly from the geometric valuation law,
\[
 \langle V_p^a1,V_p^b1\rangle=r_p^{|a-b|}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Consequently the reference measure in (6) is exactly
\[
 d\mu_{I,f,F}(z)
 =\left[\int_{\mathbb R}\|\widehat f_E(\xi)\|^2
       \prod_{p\in F}P_{r_p}(z_p e^{i\xi\log p})\,d\xi\right]
       dm_F(z),
 \qquad
 P_r(z)=\frac{1-r^2}{|1-rz|^2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed its \(h\)-moment is
\(\prod_pr_p^{|h_p|}\int\|\widehat f_E(\xi)\|^2
e^{-i\xi\sum_ph_p\log p}\,d\xi\), the moment calculated from
(1). Plancherel makes its total mass \(\|f\|^2\).
The real frequency may correlate the prime phases after mixing;
the estimate below is uniform before that mixing.

For \(p\ge101\), \(r_p<1/10\). Independently of a rotation
\(\eta\in\mathbb T\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_{A_p}P_{r_p}(z\eta)\,dm(z)
 &\le\frac{1+r_p}{1-r_p}
       \left(\frac12+\frac{\arcsin r_p}{\pi}\right)\\
 &\le\frac{11}{9}\frac{11}{20}
   =\frac{121}{180}<\frac34.
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{13}
\]
Here \(P_r\le(1+r)/(1-r)\) and
\(\arcsin r\le\pi r/2\) on \([0,1]\); the latter follows from
convexity and the endpoint chord. Hence (10)--(12) give
\[
 \boxed{Q[f]\le C(3/4)^{|F|}\|f\|^2}
 \tag{14}
\]
for every finite set of oriented primes outside \(E\) with
\(p\ge101\).

If infinitely many primes are oriented, arbitrarily large such
sets exist for every fixed cylinder packet. Equation (14) forces
\(Q[f]=0\) there. Boundedness and density then force \(Q=0\).
This proves (4), including for an arbitrarily sparse infinite
prime set. There is no prime number theorem, divergence of
\(\sum_p1/p\), rational-independence argument, or all-prime
exchange of integrals in the proof.

Equation (14) also prices finite selection: if a nonzero cylinder
packet is normalized by \(Q[f]=\|f\|^2\), orienting \(N\) fresh
large prime rays forces any bound in (2) to satisfy
\(C\ge(4/3)^N\). This is a sufficient exponential lower bound,
not an optimal constant.

## Finite panels really can be selected

For a fixed finite \(F\), the isometries \(W_p\), \(p\in F\),
are pure and doubly commuting on the original space. Their common
wandering space is
\[
 \mathcal E_F=\{f:f(J,s)=0\text{ unless }J_p=0
                       \text{ for every }p\in F\}.
 \tag{15}
\]
The spaces \(W^a\mathcal E_F\) consist exactly of functions
supported on \(J_F=a\); the real translation in \(W^a\) is
invertible and creates no restriction inside that shell. Thus
\[
 \mathcal K=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb N_0^F}W^a\mathcal E_F
       \simeq H^2(\mathbb T^F;\mathcal E_F),\qquad W_p\simeq M_{z_p}.
 \tag{16}
\]
This proves the required Wold model directly. For \(q\notin F\),
double commutation and preservation of \(\mathcal E_F\) give
\(W_q\simeq I\otimes R_q\), with \(R_q\) an isometry.

Let \(\mathcal U_F\) denote the unitary in (16), and set
\[
 B_F=\prod_{p\in F}\{z:\operatorname{Re}z\le0\},\qquad
 Q_F[f]=\int_{B_F}\|(\mathcal U_Ff)(z)\|_{\mathcal E_F}^2\,dm_F(z).
 \tag{17}
\]
It is a positive contraction form, is invariant under every
\(W_m\), and is nonzero: for \(0\ne f\in\mathcal E_F\),
\(Q_F[f]=2^{-|F|}\|f\|^2\).
For \(p\in F\), the correlation formula gives
\[
 \langle[f],D_{p,Q_F}[f]\rangle_{Q_F}
 =\int_{B_F}w_p(z_p)\|(\mathcal U_Ff)(z)\|^2\,dm_F(z)
 \ge\frac{2(\log p)r_p^2}{1+r_p^2}\,Q_F[f].
 \tag{18}
\]
It follows by density that \(D_{p,Q_F}\) is strictly positive
on the energy completion. The selected arcs break the phase
symmetry of WP-451. They were chosen using the desired sign and
therefore do not solve the source-selection problem.

This also identifies why WP-230's congruence obstruction is not
the proof here: (3) uses the new isometries and their energy-space
adjoints. It is not the fixed original defect inserted between
two copies of a metric square root. No such congruence is asserted.

## Prior art, controls and consequence

Positive invariant metrics and their isometric intertwiners are
classical; Panja's Theorem 1.2 is the relevant bounded
Toeplitz-factorization result. The Herglotz/Fejer argument is
classical (compare McMullen's Theorem 8.6); (7) supplies its
finite-torus version here. Sarkar's Corollary 3.2 and Theorem 3.3
give the general doubly commuting Wold model behind (16).
Nikolski--Pushnitski, Section 3, place multiplicative Toeplitz
operators on the infinite torus. None of these general tools is
claimed as new.

WP-096 concerns prescribing sparse prime-power coefficients of a
positive measure, while WP-100 concerns singularity of a particular
product completion. Here no moments of \(Q\) are prescribed:
bounded domination and separate ray signs force support onto
arcs whose reference mass tends to zero. The argument allows a
correlated \(Q\), off-diagonal excitation couplings and nonlocal
real-coordinate dependence. WP-450 only classified bounded
excitation-diagonal covariant forms; WP-451 allowed unbounded
forms but required phase invariance. The present finite/infinite
boundary therefore addresses a different escape from those results.

The mechanism survives nonarithmetic product controls with local
vacuum moments \(r_j^{|a-b|}\), \(r_j\to0\), arbitrary translation
frequencies and positive ray scales. No zero data or arithmetic
orientation enters it. Boundedness in one fixed original norm
is material: it supplies the single constant in (6) and the final
density passage. No assertion is made here about unbounded
closable energies, singular representations, changed source
relations, or an energy depending on a cutoff without a uniform
bound.

A numerical diagnostic used midpoint quadrature with 16,384
nodes, seven primes from 101 through 10007, seven rotations,
and moments of orders zero through five. All 294 rotated Poisson
moment checks and 49 arc-mass checks passed; 245 evaluations also
checked the positive half-circle floor in (18). These tests check
Fourier direction and local constants only. The measure domination,
infinite-prime obstruction and finite-panel construction are proved
above, not inferred from numerical sampling.

The resulting boundary is precise: breaking phase modulation
permits finite sign selection, but no nonzero bounded
dilation-covariant energy can make an infinite set of unchanged
critical prime rays individually nonnegative. A global construction
may instead couple sectors before sign is read, and must still
derive the exact Gamma/polar terms and an independent global sign.
