# WP-268 — Critical Fock boundary row lies beyond every finite Hamiltonian Hilbert scale but is continuous on a canonical exponential rigging

## Claim

`WP-267` shows that closing the critical common-vacuum row in the ambient first-layer Hilbert space turns it into the maximally multivalued relation and deletes its regular operator part. One of the explicit surviving categories was therefore a source-derived non-Hilbert test topology.

For the actual Fock first layer this escape has a sharp classification. Let

\[
\mathcal K=\ell^2(\mathbb P),\qquad He_p=(\log p)e_p,
\]

and retain the source-forced boundary row from `WP-261`--`WP-267`,

\[
L_\sigma x
=\sum_p u_p^{(\sigma)}x_p,
\qquad
u_p^{(\sigma)}:=\sqrt{\log p}\,p^{-\sigma/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

At the Weil value `sigma=1/2`, `L_{1/2}` is **more singular than every finite-order distribution in the Hilbert scale generated polynomially by the logarithmic Hamiltonian**. For every finite `s>=0`, if

\[
\mathcal K_s=\operatorname{Dom}(1+H)^{s/2},
\qquad
\|x\|_s=\|(1+H)^{s/2}x\|_2,
\tag{2}
\]

then `L_{1/2}` is not continuous on `K_s`. Consequently it is not continuous even on the Hamiltonian-smooth Fréchet space

\[
\Phi_\infty:=\bigcap_{m\ge0}\operatorname{Dom}(1+H)^{m/2}
\tag{3}
\]

with its natural increasing graph seminorms. Equivalently, no finite power of the resolvent of `H` regularizes the critical coefficient vector into `ell^2`.

There is nevertheless a canonical source-derived category in which the row survives. Define the scale-invariant rapid-energy test space

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{\exp}
:=\bigcap_{a>0}\operatorname{Dom}e^{aH}
=\bigcap_{n\ge1}\operatorname{Dom}e^{nH}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

This Fréchet space is determined by `H` alone, is unchanged as a locally convex space by replacing `H` with `cH+dI` for any `c>0`, and is a nuclear countably-Hilbert space. The exact critical functional `L_{1/2}` is continuous on `Phi_exp`. Thus

\[
\Phi_{\exp}\subset\ell^2(\mathbb P)\subset\Phi_{\exp}'
\tag{5}
\]

is a canonical rigging that retains the algebraic critical common-boundary coefficient vector as a genuine continuous distribution even though the same row is nonclosable as an ordinary Hilbert-space operator.

This is a real category escape from the specific closure failure of `WP-267`, but it is **not** a Weil-positivity bridge. The resulting rank-one positive functional

\[
q_{\rm bd}(x)=|L_{1/2}x|^2
\tag{6}
\]

still has only the common-vacuum rank-one geometry, supplies neither the Weil translation/autocorrelation structure nor the Gamma/polar sector, and has no demonstrated source-forced embedding of the full Weil test class. Moreover, on any single exponential Hilbert level that makes the row bounded, normalized prime-event amplitudes are shifted into the ordinary Euler half-plane exactly as in `WP-033`.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CANDIDATE-NEW-ANALYTIC-CATEGORY + DECISIVE-NARROWING + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Polynomial Hamiltonian scales never regularize the critical row

The dual norm of (1) on the Hilbert level (2), when finite, is

\[
\|L_\sigma\|_{\mathcal K_s'}^2
=
\sum_p
\frac{\log p}{p^\sigma(1+\log p)^s}.
\tag{7}
\]

At `sigma=1/2`, for every fixed finite `s`, compare the prime summand with `1/p`:

\[
\frac{\displaystyle \frac{\log p}{\sqrt p(1+\log p)^s}}
     {\displaystyle \frac1p}
=
\frac{\sqrt p\,\log p}{(1+\log p)^s}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{8}
\]

Euler's divergence of `sum_p 1/p` therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p(1+\log p)^s}=\infty
\quad\text{for every finite }s.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Hence `L_{1/2}` is not a continuous element of any finite negative Hamiltonian scale `K_{-s}`.

The same statement can be written in resolvent language. For any fixed `z` outside the spectrum and any integer `m>=1`,

\[
\|(H-z)^{-m}u^{(1/2)}\|_2^2
=
\sum_p
\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p\,|\log p-z|^{2m}}
=\infty,
\tag{10}
\]

because the denominator is only a fixed power of `log p`. Thus no finite chain of ordinary Hamiltonian resolvents produces a Hilbert-space defect vector.

This is stronger than ambient nonclosability. It says that the critical row is not merely one or two Sobolev orders too singular: relative to the source Hamiltonian `H=log p`, it lies beyond **every finite polynomial order**.

## 2. The usual smooth-vector Fréchet topology also fails

The graph norms in (2) are increasing. A linear functional on the projective-limit space (3) is continuous only if it is bounded by finitely many defining seminorms, hence by one sufficiently high graph norm:

\[
|L(x)|\le C\|x\|_s
\tag{11}
\]

for some finite `s`.

Equation (9) rules this out for every `s`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{L_{1/2}\notin\Phi_\infty'.}
\tag{12}
\]

So merely replacing the Hilbert space by the standard `H`-smooth-vector Gelfand-triple candidate does **not** evade `WP-267`.

The mechanism is the spectral multiplicity of arithmetic energy. Although `u_p^{(1/2)}` decays like `p^{-1/4}=e^{-(\log p)/4}`, there are exponentially many prime energies below an energy cutoff in the variable `E=log p`. Multiplying by any fixed polynomial in `E` cannot compensate that density.

## 3. Exponential Hamiltonian regularity has an exact threshold

For `a>=0`, let

\[
\mathcal K_a^{\exp}=\operatorname{Dom}e^{aH},
\qquad
\|x\|_{a,\exp}=\|e^{aH}x\|_2.
\tag{13}
\]

Then

\[
\|L_\sigma\|_{(\mathcal K_a^{\exp})'}^2
=
\sum_p\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma+2a}}.
\tag{14}
\]

At the target value `sigma=1/2`, the series converges exactly when

\[
\boxed{a>\frac14.}
\tag{15}
\]

Indeed, for `a>1/4` it is dominated by the corresponding integer series `sum_n (log n)n^{-1/2-2a}`. At `a=1/4` it contains `sum_p (log p)/p`, which diverges, and for `a<1/4` its terms eventually dominate a constant multiple of `1/p`.

Thus the source Hamiltonian gives a sharp regularity boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite-order }H\text{-smoothness: never enough};
\qquad
\text{exponential }H\text{-regularity: enough iff }a>1/4.
}
\tag{16}
\]

The threshold is consistent with `WP-033`. On the normalized basis vector `p^{-a}e_p` of the single Hilbert level (13), the squared boundary amplitude is

\[
|L_{1/2}(p^{-a}e_p)|^2
=
\frac{\log p}{p^{1/2+2a}}.
\tag{17}
\]

Boundedness begins only when `1/2+2a>1`. So a **single weighted Hilbert norm** repairs the row precisely by shifting normalized event masses into the Euler half-plane. The new point here is that the projective-limit topology (4) can retain the original algebraic critical coefficients without selecting one such Hilbert normalization.

## 4. The rapid-energy space is canonically nuclear

Take the cofinal integer scale

\[
\mathcal K_n^{\exp}=\operatorname{Dom}e^{nH},
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
\]

For `m>n`, the inclusion

\[
J_{m,n}:\mathcal K_m^{\exp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal K_n^{\exp}
\]

is diagonal in the prime basis with singular values

\[
s_p(J_{m,n})=p^{-(m-n)}.
\tag{18}
\]

Hence

\[
\|J_{m,n}\|_{\rm HS}^2
=
\sum_p p^{-2(m-n)}<\infty
\qquad(m-n>1/2).
\tag{19}
\]

In particular every level has a stronger Hilbert level whose inclusion is Hilbert--Schmidt. By the standard countably-Hilbert criterion, `Phi_exp` is nuclear.

The construction is also insensitive to the irrelevant affine choice of energy units. If `H'=cH+dI` with `c>0`, then

\[
\bigcap_{a>0}\operatorname{Dom}e^{aH'}
=
\bigcap_{a>0}\operatorname{Dom}e^{aH}
\tag{20}
\]

with equivalent projective-limit topologies. Thus the rigging is not selected by the desired critical exponent.

Since `a=1` is already above the threshold (15), equation (14) proves that `L_{1/2}` is continuous on `Phi_exp`. Therefore the positive form (6) is continuous there and the common-vacuum mixed-prime coherence survives as a distributional boundary value.

## 5. What this does and does not repair

This result changes the interpretation of the Fock nonclosability obstruction in a precise way.

It **does** rule out the most standard finite-order distributional rescue. Ordinary Hilbert scales, finite resolvent powers, and the smooth-vector space generated polynomially by `H` cannot even host the critical first-layer row continuously. A boundary-triple or singular-rank-one construction whose boundary functional is assumed to live in some finite negative `H`-scale therefore does not apply to this row without a genuine category change.

It also shows that such a category change exists canonically: the full rapid-energy space `Phi_exp` is determined by the source Hamiltonian and supports the exact algebraic row. This is not the programmable single-valued section rejected in `WP-267`; no value of the missing boundary coordinate is chosen after graph closure. Instead the domain topology is strengthened before closure so the original algebraic functional is continuous from the start.

But this does **not** solve the research mandate. In particular:

- `q_bd` is rank one and does not reproduce the full finite-prime Weil autocorrelation/translation form;
- no archimedean Gamma or polar counterterm is generated;
- continuity on `Phi_exp` is not yet a theorem that the full admissible Weil positive-type test class embeds into this topology in the required source-forced way;
- positivity of `|Lx|^2` is generic functional analysis and does not distinguish the Riemann primes from matched coefficient sequences;
- a later map from the nuclear boundary space to the Weil test space could still insert the target kernel by hand.

The result therefore moves the live question from "can a non-Hilbert topology retain the critical row?" to the stricter question: **can the source derive a global finite--archimedean boundary correspondence on this or another equally canonical rigging whose positive form has the Weil orientation before the destination is recognized?**

## 6. Matched controls and aggressive falsification

Several controls prevent overreading the positive category escape.

First, the finite-order obstruction is not a theorem about all possible arithmetic spectra. It comes from combining the critical coefficient with the density of the prime-energy spectrum. A sufficiently sparse label set can make the analogue of (7) summable. The claim is specific to the actual prime first layer and nearby systems with comparable density.

Second, one can always invent a locally convex topology strong enough to make an algebraic functional continuous. That fact is not evidence. `Phi_exp` is retained only because it is generated functorially by the already-existing source Hamiltonian and is invariant under positive changes of energy units. Any weaker or differently weighted topology proposed later must pass the same source-selection test.

Third, using one level `K_a^exp` with `a>1/4` is not a new critical Hilbert realization: equation (17) reduces it to the off-critical normalized-mass repair already classified by `WP-033`.

Fourth, continuity of `L_{1/2}` on a nuclear test space does not create a sign theorem for a completed operator. If the finite--archimedean construction ultimately needs an ordinary closed quadratic form on `ell^2`, `WP-261` and `WP-267` remain decisive.

## 7. Prior art and novelty boundary

No general functional-analysis theorem is claimed as new.

Hilbert scales associated with a positive self-adjoint operator and finite-rank singular perturbations on such scales are classical. Albeverio, Kuzhel, and Nizhnik, *Singularly Perturbed Self-Adjoint Operators in Scales of Hilbert Spaces* (2007, arXiv:`math-ph/0702057`), treats finite-rank perturbations using the scale generated by the underlying semibounded operator. Dijksma, Kurasov, and Shondin, *High order singular rank one perturbations of a positive operator*, Integral Equations and Operator Theory 53 (2005), 209--245, DOI `10.1007/s00020-005-1357-5`, studies perturbing vectors in finite negative levels `H_{-n}`. Later "supersingular" peak-model work likewise develops increasingly high but finite negative-scale classes.

The countably-Hilbert nuclearity criterion used in (19) is standard rigged-Hilbert-space theory. The series estimates are elementary once the Mathia coefficient vector and Hamiltonian are fixed.

The durable Mathia-specific content is the compatibility statement: **the exact critical Fock boundary vector selected by `WP-261` lies outside every finite polynomial Hilbert scale of the source logarithmic Hamiltonian, yet the same Hamiltonian canonically generates a nuclear rapid-energy rigging on which that vector is continuous.** This precisely separates the finite-order singular-perturbation route from a genuine distributional category that survives `WP-267`.

No literature located in the bounded audit supplies the missing Riemann finite--archimedean positive correspondence on this rigging, and none is claimed.

## 8. Audit tests and research consequence

The exact classification can be falsified by any failure of the following checks:

1. the first-layer coefficient is not `sqrt(log p) p^{-1/4}` at `sigma=1/2`;
2. the dual norm formula (7) is incorrect for the `H`-generated polynomial scale;
3. the comparison (8) fails for some finite `s`;
4. the exponential dual norm (14) has a threshold different from `a=1/4`;
5. the inclusion singular values in (18) are not `p^{-(m-n)}` or fail the Hilbert--Schmidt test;
6. `Phi_exp` depends on an arbitrary target-derived normalization rather than only on the source Hamiltonian up to positive affine rescaling.

All six are direct spectral/series calculations.

For the research line, ordinary finite-order boundary triples, finite negative Hamiltonian scales, and polynomial smooth-vector riggings should no longer be treated as unexplored escapes for the critical common-vacuum row. The non-Hilbert branch should instead test whether a **source-derived nuclear boundary correspondence** can couple `Phi_exp` to an archimedean/global sector before scalarization, while preserving the exact finite critical amplitudes and deriving the final sign independently. If that coupling is absent, the nuclear rigging is only a mathematically legitimate home for the same incomplete rank-one carrier, not the missing Weil geometry.
