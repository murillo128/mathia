# WP-109 — Exact critical prime-torus data force infinite Kronecker spectral cost through Sobolev order minus one

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + CORRELATION-ROBUST + SHARP-AXIS-THRESHOLD + MATCHED-CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION`.

`WP-108` rules out the ordinary positive `H^1`/Dirichlet energy of an exact critical prime-torus completion along the intrinsic multiplicative/Kronecker flow, but explicitly leaves negative-order, nonlocal, and distributional spectral norms as a possible escape. The exact one-prime Weil rays already force a much stronger obstruction.

Let

\[
\mathbb T^{\mathcal P}=\prod_p\mathbb T
\]

and let `mu_sigma` be a finite positive measure of mass `C>0` whose one-prime Fourier rays are exactly

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\mu_\sigma(k e_p)
=-(\log p)p^{-|k|\sigma}
\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}).
}
\tag{1}
\]

For a finite prime set `P`, normalize

\[
\eta_P:=C^{-1}(\pi_P)_*\mu_\sigma.
\tag{2}
\]

The Mathia/Prime-Lattice energy gives the Kronecker generator

\[
X_P=\sum_{p\in P}(\log p)\partial_{\theta_p},
\qquad
X_P z^\alpha=iE(\alpha)z^\alpha,
\qquad
E(\alpha)=\sum_{p\in P}\alpha_p\log p.
\tag{3}
\]

For every real Sobolev index `s`, define the extended nonnegative cylindrical spectral form

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{s,P}(\eta_P)
:=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb Z^P}
\bigl(1+E(\alpha)^2\bigr)^s
\left|\widehat\eta_P(\alpha)\right|^2
\in[0,+\infty].
}
\tag{4}
\]

This is the inhomogeneous Fourier scale generated by `1+X_P^*X_P`: `s=0` is the `L^2` Fourier cost, `s>0` gives positive Sobolev/derivative cost along the Kronecker flow, and `s<0` gives the corresponding positive negative-order/resolvent form. It is deliberately only the **Kronecker spectral scale**, not a full elliptic Sobolev norm on the whole torus.

Then the exact rays force

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{s,P}(\eta_P)
\ge
1+\frac{2}{C^2}
\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}
\bigl(1+(k\log p)^2\bigr)^s
\frac{(\log p)^2}{p^{2k\sigma}}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

At the Weil exponent `sigma=1/2`, the first harmonics alone imply

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{P\Subset\mathcal P}\mathcal S_{s,P}(\eta_P)=+\infty
\qquad\text{for every }s\ge-1.
}
\tag{6}
\]

The endpoint `s=-1` is already divergent:

\[
\mathcal S_{-1,P}(\eta_P)
\gtrsim_C
\sum_{p\in P}\frac1p.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus even the canonical positive Green/resolvent smoothing

\[
(1+X_P^*X_P)^{-1}
\tag{8}
\]

cannot assign finite global quadratic spectral cost to exact critical prime-torus data. Within the standard power scale, the mandatory one-prime axes become summable only for

\[
\boxed{s<-1,}
\tag{9}
\]

i.e. only after **more than one full inverse power** of the Kronecker Laplacian. This is a necessary axis condition, not a claim that such stronger negative-order forms solve the global problem.

The obstruction is correlation-independent, uses no zero data, and does not assume a global density.

## 1. Exact Fourier lower bound

For a finite marginal, (1)--(2) give

\[
\widehat\eta_P(k e_p)
=-\frac{\log p}{C}p^{-|k|\sigma}.
\tag{10}
\]

Every term in (4) is nonnegative. Keeping only the mutually distinct modes `+-k e_p` therefore gives (5) immediately. Arbitrary mixed-prime Fourier coefficients can only add further nonnegative cost.

This is stronger than the particular Parseval argument in `WP-108`: no absolute continuity and no derivative domain is required. Equation (4) is an extended quadratic Fourier form defined directly on the finite marginal measure. If the series diverges, the spectral cost is simply `+infinity`.

## 2. The critical threshold is exactly `s=-1` on the mandatory axes

Set `sigma=1/2` and retain only `k=1`. For every fixed real `s`, there is a constant `c_s>0` such that for all sufficiently large primes

\[
\bigl(1+(\log p)^2\bigr)^s
\ge c_s(\log p)^{2s}.
\tag{11}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal S_{s,P}(\eta_P)
\ge
1+\frac{2c_s}{C^2}
\sum_{p\in P}
\frac{(\log p)^{2s+2}}p.
\tag{12}
\]

The prime number theorem plus partial summation gives the standard threshold

\[
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^a}{p}
\begin{cases}
=+\infty,&a\ge0,\\
<+\infty,&a<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed the associated comparison integral is

\[
\int^\infty\frac{(\log x)^{a-1}}x\,dx,
\tag{14}
\]

and the endpoint `a=0` is the classical divergence of the reciprocal-prime sum. Taking `a=2s+2` proves (6).

The same threshold is sharp for the **entire mandatory axis contribution**, not merely its first harmonics. If `s<-1`, the `k=1` series converges by (13), while for `k>=2` one has `s<0` and therefore

\[
\bigl(1+(k\log p)^2\bigr)^s\le1,
\]

so

\[
\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}
\bigl(1+(k\log p)^2\bigr)^s
\frac{(\log p)^2}{p^k}
\le
\sum_p(\log p)^2\frac{p^{-2}}{1-p^{-1}}
<\infty.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus exact critical one-prime data pass this necessary spectral-axis test **if and only if** `s<-1`.

## 3. General positive spectral multipliers obey the same obstruction

The power scale is only a canonical benchmark. More generally, let `w:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity)` be any nonnegative spectral symbol and define

\[
\mathcal Q_{w,P}(\eta_P)
:=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb Z^P}
w(|E(\alpha)|)
|\widehat\eta_P(\alpha)|^2.
\tag{16}
\]

At criticality, exact first harmonics force the necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p
w(\log p)\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}<\infty.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently any eventually regular symbol satisfying

\[
w(t)\gtrsim t^{-2}
\tag{18}
\]

along the prime frequencies still diverges. A canonical spectral multiplier must suppress high Kronecker frequencies **strictly faster than quadratic inverse decay** before even the compulsory one-prime data can have finite raw positive cost.

Equation (17), rather than any chosen power law, is the exact obstruction. A highly irregular symbol could of course be made tiny specifically at `{log p}`; unless Mathia independently forces such a symbol, that is precisely the hand-picked-kernel escape excluded by the research mandate.

## 4. Matched controls show the critical singularity is not a generic flaw of the scale

For every `sigma>1/2`, the exact positive product completion constructed in `WP-108` has factors

\[
\rho_{p,C,\sigma}(\theta)
=1+\frac{\log p}{C}\bigl(1-P_{p^{-\sigma}}(\theta)\bigr)
\tag{19}
\]

and finite global `L^2` product norm because

\[
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^2}{p^{2\sigma}}<\infty.
\tag{20}
\]

For `s<=0`, (4) is bounded above by the `L^2` Fourier norm, so the cylindrical `S_s` costs are uniformly finite.

For `s>0`, choose an integer `m>=s`. The squared Fourier coefficients of the product factor over the prime coordinates. Each one-prime factor has geometrically decaying harmonics, and for every fixed integer `j`

\[
\sum_p
\frac{(\log p)^{2j+2}}{p^{2\sigma}}<\infty.
\tag{21}
\]

Expanding the fixed moment `E(alpha)^{2m}` under these product Fourier weights gives only finitely many partition types, each bounded by products of convergent sums of the form (21). Hence

\[
\sup_{P\Subset\mathcal P}\mathcal S_{m,P}(\eta_P)<\infty,
\tag{22}
\]

and therefore the same holds for `s<=m`.

So for every fixed `s>=-1`, `sigma=1/2` is a genuine boundary: exact rays force infinite Kronecker spectral cost at the Weil exponent, while matched exact positive controls above one half have finite cost in the same spectral geometry.

## 7. Aggressive falsification and surviving routes

The theorem survives the obvious controls, but its scope is deliberately narrow.

- **Mixed-prime correlations cannot repair it.** Equation (5) keeps only mandatory axis squares. Mixed coefficients add nonnegative terms.
- **Near resonances cannot repair the divergence.** The lower bound uses frequencies exactly `+-log p`; small mixed values of `E(alpha)` are irrelevant to the no-go. Conversely, near-resonant mixed modes may make an `s<-1` candidate worse, so passing the axis threshold is not sufficient.
- **One Green resolvent is not enough.** The endpoint `(1+X^*X)^{-1}` still costs `sum_p 1/p`.
- **Stronger smoothing remains open.** Symbols with decay faster than `t^{-2}`, including `s<-1`, pass only this necessary axis test. A viable route must still be independently forced rather than selected to absorb the divergence.
- **Renormalized subtraction is outside the theorem.** Removing the divergent axis cost by hand destroys the raw positive-form interpretation unless another intrinsic construction fixes both subtraction and sign.
- **Nonseparable finite--archimedean coupling remains open.** If the global object changes the state or the operator before positivity is taken, the present fixed prime-torus spectral form need not apply.
- **Quotients, selectors, boundary spaces, cohomological forms, and positive forms on external test functions remain open** when they act before this scalar spectral cost is formed.

Most importantly, `S_s>=0` by itself is not Weil positivity. The construction still does not generate the archimedean Gamma contribution, pole/global counterterms, or the Weil autocorrelation pairing from one geometry.
