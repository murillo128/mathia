# WP-391 — Bost--Connes divisibility diagonal cannot isolate prime-power support

## Claim

`WP-216` found a genuinely source-forced finite-place structure in the Bost--Connes system: the canonical range projections

\[
E_n:=\mu_n\mu_n^*,
\qquad
E_mE_n=E_{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)},
\tag{1}
\]

and their critical `KMS_1` conditioning produce the exact GCD/Poisson Gram with half-density `p^{-k/2}`. This makes the projection family a natural place to ask whether the missing prime-power selector could already be present in the source diagonal rather than inserted by a target-chosen function of the Hamiltonian.

In the canonical positive-energy representation on `ell^2(N^x)`,

\[
\mu_n\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_{nk},
\qquad
E_n\varepsilon_k=\mathbf 1_{n\mid k}\,\varepsilon_k.
\tag{2}
\]

Define the concrete divisibility diagonal

\[
D_{\rm div}:=C^*(E_n:n\ge1)\subset B(\ell^2(\mathbf N^\times)).
\tag{3}
\]

For `A in D_div`, write

\[
a_A(k):=\langle A\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 a_A(k)=0\ \text{for every }k\text{ divisible by at least two distinct primes}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 A=0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

No positivity assumption is needed. Therefore there is **no nonzero positive element, projection, or bounded source-diagonal observable in `D_div` whose exact integer support is contained in the prime powers**. In particular, the prime-power support of the finite Weil/Mangoldt selector cannot be obtained merely by taking a positive element of the norm-closed algebra generated by the canonical divisibility projections.

The obstruction is elementary but exact: every finite divisibility observable is blind to multiplication by a sufficiently fresh prime, and norm closure preserves that indistinguishability strongly enough to destroy exact mixed-prime nullity.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + RH-INDEPENDENT + BOST-CONNES + SOURCE-DIVISIBILITY-DIAGONAL + C*-NORM-CLOSURE + FRESH-PRIME-INDISTINGUISHABILITY + PRIME-POWER-SUPPORT-NO-GO + POSITIVITY-NOT-NEEDED + SOURCE-SELECTION-OBSTRUCTION + CATEGORY-SPECIFIC + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Finite-stage divisibility observables cannot see a fresh prime

Let

\[
D_F:=C^*(E_{n_1},\ldots,E_{n_r})
\tag{6}
\]

be a finite-stage subalgebra and put

\[
L:=\operatorname{lcm}(n_1,\ldots,n_r).
\tag{7}
\]

Because all generators are commuting diagonal projections, every `B in D_F` has a diagonal value

\[
b(k):=\langle B\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle
\tag{8}
\]

that depends only on the finite truth vector

\[
\bigl(\mathbf 1_{n_1\mid k},\ldots,\mathbf 1_{n_r\mid k}\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]

Now choose a prime `q` with

\[
q\nmid kL.
\tag{10}
\]

For every `i`, since `q` is coprime to `n_i`,

\[
n_i\mid kq
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n_i\mid k.
\tag{11}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{b(kq)=b(k).}
\tag{12}
\]

If `k=p^a` is a prime power, choose `q` different from `p` and outside the finite prime support of `L`. Then `kq` has two distinct prime factors, but every observable in `D_F` gives it exactly the same value as `k`.

The unit label `k=1` has the same property. Choose two fresh primes `q,r` not dividing `L`. For every generator with `n_i>1`, neither `n_i|1` nor `n_i|qr`; the possible generator `E_1=I` takes value one on both. Therefore

\[
b(qr)=b(1).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus every non-mixed integer label can be copied, at every finite stage, by a mixed-prime label with exactly the same source-divisibility data.

## 2. Norm closure turns finite-stage blindness into an exact no-go

Take `A in D_div` satisfying the mixed-prime nullity assumption in (5). For arbitrary `epsilon>0`, choose a finite-stage `B in D_F` such that

\[
\|A-B\|<\epsilon.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose first that `k=p^a`. Choose the fresh prime `q` above and put `m=kq`. Then `m` is mixed-prime, so `a_A(m)=0`, while (12) gives `b(m)=b(k)`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
|a_A(k)|
&\le |a_A(k)-b(k)|+|b(m)-a_A(m)|\\
&\le 2\|A-B\|\\
&<2\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Since `epsilon` is arbitrary,

\[
a_A(p^a)=0
\qquad\text{for every prime power }p^a.
\tag{16}
\]

For `k=1`, use the two-fresh-prime label `m=qr` from (13) and obtain identically

\[
a_A(1)=0.
\tag{17}
\]

By assumption `a_A(k)=0` already for every mixed-prime `k`. Equations (16)--(17) therefore show

\[
a_A(k)=0\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{18}
\]

Every element of `D_div` is diagonal in the basis `\{\varepsilon_k\}`, so (18) implies `A=0`. This proves (5).

Equivalently, in the topology on integer evaluation points generated by the divisibility cylinders `n|k`, the mixed-prime labels accumulate on every prime-power label and on `1`. A continuous function in the corresponding uniform algebra cannot vanish on all mixed labels while remaining nonzero on a prime-power label.

## 3. The matched controls separate source selection from target coding

The theorem does **not** say that prime powers cannot be selected by a diagonal operator on `ell^2(N^x)`. `WP-215` proves the opposite. The state Hamiltonian

\[
H\varepsilon_k=(\log k)\varepsilon_k
\tag{19}
\]

has a discrete spectrum, so arbitrary prescribed nonnegative values on the integer labels can be interpolated by a smooth nonnegative scalar function `F`, producing `F(H)`. In particular one can encode the prime-power indicator, `Lambda(k)`, or `Lambda(k)/sqrt(k)`.

The present result identifies the exact categorical difference. A target-coded `F(H)` is an arbitrary diagonal spectral function. The source-generated algebra `D_div=C^*(E_n)` is much smaller: its finite approximants can inspect only finitely many divisibility predicates, and a fresh prime outside those predicates is invisible. Thus

\[
\text{diagonal selectability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{source-divisibility selectability}.
\tag{20}
\]

This is also different from `WP-390`. There, the Ramanujan interface admits arbitrary bounded stationary spectral masks, so exact prime-power selection is possible but explicitly programmable. Here the canonical Bost--Connes divisibility diagonal has the opposite failure mode: exact mixed-prime nullity is impossible for every nonzero element of the source-generated norm closure.

For `beta>1`, the standard Gibbs density is strictly positive on every basis label, with diagonal proportional to `k^{-beta}`. Therefore multiplying a diagonal `A in D_div` by that density cannot repair its support: it rescales each `a_A(k)` by a strictly positive factor. This is only a supercritical positive-energy corollary; the obstruction (5) itself is algebraic and does not rely on a Gibbs trace or extend a `beta>1` trace formula to the critical `beta=1` state.

## 4. Consequence for the Bost--Connes completion route

`WP-216` remains intact and important. The range projections still force the divisibility semilattice, the critical GCD Gram, the half-density and the logarithmic prime scale. What fails is a tempting next step: obtaining the missing finite Weil support by choosing a positive observable **inside the same canonical divisibility diagonal**.

The result therefore narrows, rather than kills, the Bost--Connes clue. Any surviving construction must use structure not reducible to a bounded element of `D_div`: for example off-diagonal crossed-product terms, a source-forced modular/GNS operation, a canonical change of operator category, an active mixed-prime or finite--archimedean coupling, or a mechanism that delays exact support selection until the globally assembled sign theorem. Such a construction must still pass the target-programming controls of `WP-215` and `WP-222`.

In particular, the theorem does not infer that the full Bost--Connes algebra lacks relevant arithmetic information. It says only that the most literal source-native diagonal generated by the range projections cannot itself be the exact prime-power selector.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The source ingredients are standard Bost--Connes material: the canonical multiplicative isometries, their range projections, the positive-energy representation `mu_n epsilon_k=epsilon_{nk}`, and the Hamiltonian with eigenvalues `log k`. `WP-216` already records these relations and their KMS consequence, so no novelty is claimed for them.

The functional-analytic ingredient is also elementary: a commutative C-star algebra generated by projections is the norm closure of its finite projection-generated subalgebras, and a finite-stage diagonal observable depends only on the corresponding finite Boolean pattern. The fresh-prime argument above is a line-specific application of that standard fact.

A bounded prior-art audit around Bost--Connes range projections, divisibility algebras, prime-power selectors and KMS conditioning found the standard Bost--Connes/Q-lattice representation and thermodynamic relations, but no independent result asserting this exact mixed-prime-nullity obstruction for the canonical divisibility diagonal as a Weil-selector test. The novelty claim is therefore deliberately narrow: equation (5) is recorded as a new structural closure **inside this research line**, not as a new general theorem in operator algebras or number theory.

## Limits

- `D_div` is the concrete norm-closed diagonal generated by the canonical range projections in the standard positive-energy representation. The theorem is not asserted for every representation, every KMS von Neumann algebra, or the full Bost--Connes crossed product.
- The result concerns bounded C-star-algebra elements. Unbounded affiliated operators or forms with a different domain are outside the theorem.
- Off-diagonal source terms are outside `D_div` and are not constrained by the fresh-prime argument.
- Exact mixed-prime nullity is essential. Approximate suppression of mixed labels is a different quantitative problem.
- The theorem addresses arithmetic support only. It does not produce the finite Weil coefficients, the archimedean term, the polar normalization, a global positive form, or RH.

## Consequence for the research line

The Bost--Connes branch now has a sharper separation between what the source genuinely forces and what must still emerge globally. `WP-216` shows that the canonical divisibility projections contain enough relational structure to force the critical Poisson/GCD geometry. `WP-391` shows that the norm-closed diagonal generated by those same projections cannot be used as a hidden exact prime-power mask: every such mask is zero.

So the next useful Bost--Connes test should not search for another bounded diagonal function of the `E_n`. It should ask whether **off-diagonal or genuinely category-changing source structure** can orient the canonical critical Gram into the finite Weil defect and couple it to the archimedean/polar sectors without target coding. That is a materially smaller and more falsifiable surface than the original affiliation question.
