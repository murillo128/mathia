For the full-root Prime-Circle controls

\[
F_N(z)
:=\log\frac{1-z^N}{1-z},
\qquad N\ge2,
\tag{1}

put

\[
U_N:=N^{-1/2}F_N
\tag{2}
\]

in the pointed local Dirichlet space of `WP-072`, with

\[
D_1(F)=\left\|\frac{F(z)-F(1)}{z-1}\right\|_{H^2}^2.
\tag{3}

The normalization in (2) is not fitted: `WP-073` proves that `N^{-1/2}` is the unique positive normalization making the intrinsic degree-`N` pullback isometric.

Define, for `x>0`,

\[
\boxed{
a(x):=H_{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\log x-\gamma,
}
\tag{4}
\]

with `H_0=0`. Then for every pair of integer sequences `N_j,M_j -> infinity` such that

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}\longrightarrow r\in(0,\infty),
\tag{5}
\]

one has the exact scaling limit

\[
\boxed{
\langle U_{N_j},U_{M_j}\rangle_{D_1}
\longrightarrow
K(r)
:=r^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty a(x)a(x/r)\,dx.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Writing `r=e^t` and

\[
b(y):=e^{y/2}a(e^y),
\tag{7}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(t):=K(e^t)
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}b(y)b(y-t)\,dy.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Thus the limit is a stationary positive-definite autocorrelation kernel on log-degree scale.

The crucial point is its exact spectral density. For `0<Re(s)<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty a(x)x^{s-1}\,dx
=-\frac{\zeta(1-s)}{s}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Hence, with Fourier convention

\[
\widehat b(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}b(y)e^{-i\tau y}\,dy,
\tag{10}
\]

one gets

\[
\boxed{
\widehat b(\tau)
=-\frac{\zeta(\tfrac12+i\tau)}{\tfrac12-i\tau},
}
\tag{11}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(t)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 e^{i\tau t}
 \frac{|\zeta(\tfrac12+i\tau)|^2}{\tfrac14+\tau^2}
\,d\tau.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This looks strikingly close to the target because the critical line and a positive translation kernel have emerged from Mathia-native pointed-cover geometry without inserting zero data. But it is a **decisive redirect, not a Weil sign theorem**:

1. the positivity in (8) is the universal positivity of an autocorrelation and holds unconditionally;

3. off-critical zeros cannot make the density in (12) negative, so this positivity cannot force RH;

5. the zeta factor arises here already for the all-degree full-root controls, before prime-power support or `Lambda` is used, so its appearance is a universal coefficient-lattice effect rather than a finite-prime/global coupling;
6. no Gamma term or polar counterterm is generated by (6)--(12).

For a prime `p`, `F_p=Log Phi_p`, so (6) also applies along prime sequences. The prime specialization does not change the prior-art diagnosis: the limiting positive kernel has already been fixed by the universal degree geometry.

This closes the route

## 1. Exact pointed-shell coefficients

`WP-072` gives

\[
T_1F_N(z)
:=\frac{F_N(z)-F_N(1)}{z-1}
=\sum_{k\ge0}q_N(k)z^k,
\tag{14}
\]

with

\[
F_N(1)=\log N
\tag{15}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
q_N(k)=\log N-H_k+H_{\lfloor k/N\rfloor}
}
\qquad(k\ge0).
\tag{16}
\]

For `k>=1`, put

\[
\varepsilon_k:=H_k-\log k-\gamma.
\tag{17}
\]

Then (4) and (16) give the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
q_N(k)=a(k/N)-\varepsilon_k.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The decomposition is useful because the second term is a fixed square-summable discretization error,

\[
\varepsilon_k=\frac1{2k}+O(k^{-2}),
\qquad
\sum_{k\ge1}|\varepsilon_k|^2<\infty,
\tag{19}
\]

whereas all degree dependence has become a dilation of the single profile `a`.

The profile itself has the endpoint bounds

\[
a(x)=-\log x-\gamma,
\qquad 0<x<1,
\tag{20}
\]

and

\[
a(x)=O(x^{-1}),
\qquad x\to\infty.
\tag{21}
\]

Indeed, for `n=floor(x)>=1`,

\[
H_n=\log n+\gamma+O(n^{-1})
\]

and `log x-log n=O(n^{-1})` on `[n,n+1)`. Consequently

\[
a\in L^2(0,\infty).
\tag{22}
\]

## 2. The critical Gram scaling limit

By Hardy orthogonality,

\[
\langle U_N,U_M\rangle_{D_1}
=\frac1{\sqrt{NM}}
\sum_{k\ge0}q_N(k)q_M(k).
\tag{23}
\]

The `k=0` term is

\[
\frac{\log N\log M}{\sqrt{NM}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{24}
\]

Using (18), the pure `epsilon` contribution is bounded by

\[
\frac1{\sqrt{NM}}\sum_{k\ge1}\varepsilon_k^2
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{25}
\]

The mixed errors vanish as well. Uniformly for large `N`,

\[
|a(k/N)|
\ll
\begin{cases}
1+\log(N/k),&k\le N,\\
N/k,&k>N,
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]

while `|epsilon_k| << 1/k`. Hence

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}|a(k/N)\varepsilon_k|
=O((\log N)^2),
\tag{27}
\]

which becomes `o(1)` after division by `sqrt(NM)` when `M/N` stays in a compact subset of `(0,infinity)`.

It remains to take the Riemann limit of the main term. Put

\[
r_{N,M}:=M/N.
\]

Then

\[
\frac1{\sqrt{NM}}
\sum_{k\ge1}a(k/N)a(k/M)
=
\frac1{N\sqrt{r_{N,M}}}
\sum_{k\ge1}
a(k/N)a\!\left(\frac{k/N}{r_{N,M}}\right).
\tag{28}
\]

The logarithmic singularity (20) is square-integrable at zero, and the `O(1/x)` tail (21) makes the product integrable at infinity. Truncation near zero and infinity followed by ordinary Riemann convergence on compact intervals therefore yields, under (5),

\[
\frac1{N\sqrt{r_{N,M}}}
\sum_{k\ge1}
a(k/N)a\!\left(\frac{k/N}{r_{N,M}}\right)
\longrightarrow
r^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty a(x)a(x/r)\,dx.
\tag{29}
\]

Equations (24)--(29) prove (6).

The critical exponent is structural here. Without the `N^{-1/2}` normalization forced by `WP-073`, the prefactor in (28) would not become the multiplicative half-density needed to turn dilation ratios into a stationary additive kernel in `log N`.

## 3. Logarithmic stationarity and positivity

Set `r=e^t`, `x=e^y`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
K(e^t)
&=e^{-t/2}\int_0^\infty a(x)a(xe^{-t})\,dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 e^{y/2}a(e^y)
 e^{(y-t)/2}a(e^{y-t})\,dy\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}b(y)b(y-t)\,dy.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]

Thus `kappa` is positive definite for the completely geometric reason that, for arbitrary complex coefficients `c_j` and shifts `t_j`,

\[
\sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_j\kappa(t_i-t_j)
=
\left\|\sum_jc_j b(\cdot-t_j)\right\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2
\ge0.
\tag{31}
\]

No arithmetic theorem is required for this sign.

As a normalization check, the diagonal limit has the closed form

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(0)=\int_0^\infty a(x)^2\,dx
=\log(2\pi)-\gamma.
}
\tag{32}
\]

The same value follows from the fractional-part model in Section 5 below. The appearance of `log(2 pi)-gamma` is not the Riemann Gamma contribution of the explicit formula; it is the norm of the Mellin carrier.

## 4. Exact Mellin transform: the positive spectrum is `|zeta|^2`

For `0<Re(s)<1`, the boundary behavior (20)--(21) permits Stieltjes integration by parts. Between integers,

\[
a'(x)=-\frac1x,
\tag{33}
\]

while at `n>=1` the jump is

\[
a(n+)-a(n-)=\frac1n.
\tag{34}
\]

Therefore, truncating at `R` and integrating against `d(x^s)`,

\[
s\int_0^R a(x)x^{s-1}\,dx
=
a(R)R^s
-
\left(
\sum_{n\le R}n^{s-1}-\frac{R^s}{s}
\right).
\tag{35}
\]

The first term tends to zero for `Re(s)<1`. The standard Euler--Maclaurin continuation of the power sum gives

\[
\sum_{n\le R}n^{s-1}-\frac{R^s}{s}
\longrightarrow
\zeta(1-s)
\qquad(0<\Re s<1).
\tag{36}
\]

This proves (9).

Now (7) and the substitution `x=e^y` give

\[
\widehat b(\tau)
=
\int_0^\infty a(x)x^{-1/2-i\tau}\,dx.
\tag{37}
\]

Taking `s=1/2-i tau` in (9) gives (11). Since `b in L^2(R)`, Fourier--Plancherel applied to the autocorrelation (8) yields (12).

This is an exact identification, not a numerical resemblance or an inserted zeta factor.

## 5. Exact Nyman--Müntz classicalization

Let

\[
h(x):=\{1/x\},
\qquad x>0,
\tag{38}
\]

be the classical fractional-part carrier. It belongs to `L^2(0,infinity)`: it is bounded near zero and equals `1/x` for `x>1`.

The standard Mellin identity underlying Nyman--Beurling/Müntz geometry is

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty h(x)x^{s-1}\,dx
=-\frac{\zeta(s)}{s},
\qquad 0<\Re s<1.
}
\tag{39}
\]

Put

\[
c(y):=e^{y/2}h(e^y).
\tag{40}
\]

On the critical Mellin line its Fourier modulus is

\[
|\widehat c(\tau)|^2
=
\frac{|\zeta(\tfrac12+i\tau)|^2}{\tfrac14+\tau^2},
\tag{41}
\]

the same as (11). Hence the two stationary autocorrelations are identical:

\[
\boxed{
\int b(y)b(y-t)\,dy
=
\int c(y)c(y-t)\,dy.
}
\tag{42}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
K(r)
=r^{-1/2}
\int_0^\infty
\{1/x\}\{r/x\}\,dx.
}
\tag{43}
\]

Thus the large-degree critically normalized pointed-shell Gram kernel is not merely reminiscent of Nyman geometry: **as a positive translation/dilation Gram kernel, it is exactly the same Mellin modulus-square carrier**.

This also proves (32), because direct interval summation gives

\[
\int_0^\infty\{1/x\}^2\,dx
=\log(2\pi)-\gamma.
\tag{44}
\]

## 6. Why the zeta modulus-square cannot be the Weil sign mechanism

The distinction from Weil positivity is structural, not cosmetic.

First, (12) is positive for every zeta function value because its density is a modulus square. A zero on the critical line makes the density vanish at that frequency; it never produces a negative direction. A hypothetical zero off the critical line likewise cannot violate the nonnegativity of (12). Therefore the sign theorem has no route to force all nontrivial zeros onto the critical line.

Second, taking a Gram/autocorrelation has discarded phase. In the Nyman--Beurling Hardy-space picture this is exactly where off-critical zeros can hide: the corresponding Blaschke/inner factor has unit boundary modulus. Burnol's control recorded in `WP-010` shows that the missing RH information reappears only when one asks for the correct subspace totality/projection statement, not when one asks whether the boundary modulus-square Gram is positive.

Third, the spectral type is wrong for the target. Equation (12) has an absolutely continuous spectral measure

\[
\frac{|\zeta(1/2+i\tau)|^2}{1/4+\tau^2}\,d\tau.
\tag{45}
\]

By contrast, the zero-side Weil form is a sampling form on the zero divisor, and the finite-side explicit formula contains the Mangoldt translation comb plus the archimedean and polar terms. This is the same absolutely-continuous-versus-atomic mismatch used as a control in `WP-005` against fixed positive same-space multiplier completions.

Fourth, the mechanism does not use `Lambda` to create its zeta factor. The controls `F_N` in (1) are defined for **every degree** `N`, and (9) arises from the universal harmonic-number staircase in the Hardy coefficient index `k`. For prime `N=p`, `F_p` happens to be the individual cyclotomic shell `Log Phi_p`; for composite `N`, it is the canonical full-root divisor sum already used as a matched control in `WP-068`/`WP-072`. The same limit survives. Therefore the appearance of `zeta` here cannot by itself certify a prime-local/global arithmetic bridge.

Finally, neither (9) nor (12) generates the completed factor

\[
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
\]

or the polar terms. The half-density `1/2` is genuinely forced by the pointed cover geometry, but the resulting modulus-square kernel erases rather than creates the archimedean phase/counterterm information needed by the explicit formula.

## 7. Adversarial controls and research consequence

The route survives several superficial tests: the critical exponent is intrinsic (`WP-073`), the form is positive before zeta is identified, the limit is canonical, and no zero data are inserted. Those successes are precisely why the prior-art redirect matters.

The decisive controls are:

1. **All-degree control.** The same zeta kernel is obtained from the full-root controls for arbitrary `N`; primality is unnecessary.
2. **Sign control.** The spectral density is nonnegative identically, irrespective of RH.
3. **Off-critical-zero control.** Boundary modulus-square data do not expose the inner/Blaschke information that Nyman totality uses to detect zeros to the right of the critical line.
4. **Spectral-type control.** The limit is absolutely continuous, not the atomic zero-sampling measure of the zero-side Weil form.
5. **Completion control.** No Gamma or polar contribution is produced by the same geometry.

Therefore this scaling limit should not be promoted as a global positivity candidate. It is instead a useful classification result: **the most canonical large-degree Gram limit of the strongest pointed positive survivor `WP-072`/`WP-073` is already classical Nyman--Müntz modulus-square geometry.**

A viable continuation must avoid scalarizing to this Gram modulus. The remaining branch-specific frontier is consistent with `WP-166`--`WP-167`: retain source-sensitive phase/incidence in a boundary response, zero-order/off-diagonal operator, or genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean structure **before** taking a norm square. Merely passing to the critical log-scale Gram limit has now been exhausted.
