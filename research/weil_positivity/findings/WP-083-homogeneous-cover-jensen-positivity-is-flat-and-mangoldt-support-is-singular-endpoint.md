## 1. The half-integer origin turns every normalized power gap into a centered Jensen gap

For a diagonal operator `X=diag(x_j)`,
\[
\Phi_n(X)e_k
=
\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}x_{nk+s}\,e_k.
\tag{12}
\]

For `r != 0`, constants cancel in (1), so
\[
D_{n,r}
=
\frac1r\left[
L^r-n^{-r}\Phi_n(L^r)
\right].
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently
\[
D_{n,r}e_k=d_{n,r}(k)e_k
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
d_{n,r}(k)
=
\frac1r
\left[
(k+\tfrac12)^r
-
\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
\left(k+\frac{s+1/2}{n}\right)^r
\right].
}
\tag{14}
\]

The rescaled block points
\[
x_s=k+\frac{s+1/2}{n}
\]
have exact arithmetic mean
\[
\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}x_s
=
k+\frac12.
\tag{15}
\]

For every real `r<1`, the scalar function `f_r` is strictly concave, including the logarithmic limit at `r=0`. Therefore (14) is precisely the scalar Jensen gap
\[
f_r\!\left(\frac1n\sum_sx_s\right)
-
\frac1n\sum_sf_r(x_s),
\]
and
\[
\boxed{d_{n,r}(k)>0}
\qquad(n>1).
\tag{16}
\]

In the operator range `-1 <= r < 1`, these are the standard operator-concave power/log functions. The sign here is nevertheless proved directly by (14), so no unbounded-operator extension of Jensen's theorem is being assumed. No zeta function, zero data, analytic continuation, or fitted kernel is needed for the sign.

The centered-block expansion gives
\[
\boxed{
d_{n,r}(k)
=
\frac{(1-r)(n^2-1)}{24n^2}\,
k^{r-2}
+
O_{n,r}(k^{r-3}).
}
\tag{17}
\]
Hence `D_{n,r}` is trace class for every `r<1`; in particular it is positive trace class throughout the operator-concave interval in the claim.

At `r=-1`,
\[
d_{n,-1}(k)
=
\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
\left(k+\frac{s+1/2}{n}\right)^{-1}
-
(k+\tfrac12)^{-1},
\]
which is exactly the diagonal formula for `Q_n` in `WP-074`. At `r=0`, taking the limit of (14) gives
\[
d_{n,0}(k)
=
\log(k+\tfrac12)
-
\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
\log\left(k+\frac{s+1/2}{n}\right),
\tag{18}
\]
which is the `c=0` logarithmic Jensen defect of `WP-082`.

So the two strongest positive cover responses already found are the `r=-1` and `r=0` points of one exact deformation.

## 2. Homogeneity forces a weighted cocycle and zero cross-degree curvature

The generalized logarithms satisfy the affine scale law
\[
\boxed{
f_r(tx)=t^r f_r(x)+f_r(t)
}
\qquad(r\ne0),
\tag{19}
\]
while
\[
f_0(tx)=f_0(x)+f_0(t).
\tag{20}
\]

Using `Phi_m Phi_n=Phi_mn` and `Phi_n(L)=nL`, let
\[
J_{n,r}:=f_r(nL)-\Phi_n(f_r(L)).
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{mn,r}
&=
f_r(mnL)-\Phi_n\Phi_m(f_r(L))\\
&=
\left[f_r(mnL)-\Phi_n(f_r(mL))\right]
+
\Phi_n\left[f_r(mL)-\Phi_m(f_r(L))\right]\\
&=
m^rJ_{n,r}+\Phi_n(J_{m,r}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

Multiplying by `(mn)^(-r)` gives (4). Commutativity of the degree semigroup gives the symmetric factorization and hence (5).

The flatness is not a peculiarity of the diagonal trace calculation. Equation (21) is algebraic and survives for any positive scale-covariant operator for which the same functional-calculus/compression expressions are defined. The half-integer ladder is needed for the exact trace and arithmetic comparison, not for the vanishing of this elementary two-degree curvature.

There is also a scalar completeness statement. Suppose a continuous nonconstant scalar function `f` obeys an affine scale law
\[
f(tx)=a(t)f(x)+b(t)
\qquad(t,x>0).
\tag{22}
\]
After subtracting `f(1)`, commutativity of multiplication gives
\[
(a(t)-1)g(x)=(a(x)-1)g(t).
\tag{23}
\]
If `a=1`, continuity gives `g(x)=c log x`. Otherwise `g` is proportional to `a-1`, and associativity forces `a(tx)=a(t)a(x)`; continuity gives `a(t)=t^r`. Thus, up to affine terms and scalar normalization, the only continuous functional calculi whose Jensen defects close on one fixed scale-covariant representative are precisely the power family and its logarithmic limit.

This does not classify arbitrary non-homogeneous responses. It does show that changing from `1/x` to `log x` or to another homogeneous power cannot create the missing non-coboundary prime interaction: the whole scale-closed class is already flat.

## 3. The trace is an ordinary convergent quantity with an exact Hurwitz-zeta closed form

Let
\[
S_K(r):=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(k+\tfrac12)^r.
\]
Summing (14) and observing that the pairs `(k,s)` enumerate `0,...,nK-1`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}d_{n,r}(k)
=
\frac1r
\left[
S_K(r)-n^{-r-1}S_{nK}(r)
\right].
}
\tag{24}
\]

Because of the trace-class estimate (17), the limit of the left side is an ordinary absolutely convergent trace. Hurwitz-zeta notation merely packages its closed form:
\[
S_K(r)
=
\zeta(-r,\tfrac12)
-
\zeta(-r,K+\tfrac12).
\tag{25}
\]

The Euler--Maclaurin asymptotic of the last term has leading powers `K^(r+1)` and `K^r`. The `K^(r+1)` pieces cancel in (24), while the `K^r` pieces cancel **only because the origin is `1/2`**. The remaining tail is `O(K^(r-1))`, which tends to zero for `r<1`. Therefore, for `-1<r<1`, `r != 0`,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}D_{n,r}
=
\frac{1-n^{-r-1}}{r}
\zeta(-r,\tfrac12),
\]
which is (6).

No zeta regularization has been used: (25) is applied only after (24) has produced a genuinely convergent positive trace.

At `r=0`, the special value
\[
\zeta(0,\tfrac12)=0
\]
and
\[
\zeta'(0,\tfrac12)=-\frac12\log2
\]
give
\[
\operatorname{Tr}D_{n,0}
=
\frac{\log2}{2}\left(1-\frac1n\right),
\tag{26}
\]
recovering `WP-082`.

At the other endpoint, write
\[
r=-1+\varepsilon.
\]
Since the Hurwitz zeta has residue `1` at `s=1`,
\[
\zeta(1-\varepsilon,\tfrac12)
=
-\frac1\varepsilon+O(1),
\]
while
\[
1-n^{-\varepsilon}
=
\varepsilon\log n+O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Hence
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{1-n^{-\varepsilon}}{-1+\varepsilon}
\zeta(1-\varepsilon,\tfrac12)
=
\log n.
\tag{27}
\]

The `log degree` theorem is therefore the pole-cancellation endpoint of the same positive Jensen trace family, not an interior deformation parameter.

## 4. Möbius extraction has full composite support at every interior parameter

For `-1<r<1`, put
\[
a=r+1>0.
\]
Equation (6) is
\[
\tau_r(n):=\operatorname{Tr}D_{n,r}
=
C_r(1-n^{-a}).
\tag{28}
\]

For `n>1`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}M_{n,r}
&=
\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\tau_r(n/d)\\
&=
-C_r n^{-a}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)d^a\\
&=
-C_r n^{-a}\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^a),
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
which proves (9).

Every factor `1-p^a` is nonzero, and positivity of `D_{n,r}` implies `C_r>0` on this interval. Therefore the primitive trace is nonzero for every `n>1`; its sign alternates with the number of distinct prime factors. In particular, no interior parameter has Mangoldt support.

For `n=p^k`, (29) gives (10). The operator itself remains positive on a prime ray, because the cocycle law gives
\[
\boxed{
M_{p^k,r}
=
D_{p^k,r}-D_{p^{k-1},r}
=
\rho_{p^{k-1}}^{(r)}(D_{p,r})
\succeq0.
}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus the failure is not that the positive prime-ray mechanism disappears. It is that its mass becomes a geometric depth law rather than the exponent-independent `log p` required by von Mangoldt.

Now let `r=-1+a` and send `a` to zero from above. From the pole calculation,
\[
C_{-1+a}
=
\frac1a+O(1).
\tag{31}
\]
Also
\[
p^a-1
=
a\log p+O(a^2).
\tag{32}
\]
If `omega(n)` is the number of distinct prime factors, then (29) gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}M_{n,-1+a}
=
(-1)^{\omega(n)+1}
a^{\omega(n)-1}
\prod_{p\mid n}\log p
+
O(a^{\omega(n)}).
\tag{33}
\]

Therefore:

- if `n=p^k`, the limit is `log p`;
- if `n` has at least two distinct prime factors, the limit is `0`.

This is exactly
\[
\lim_{r\to-1^+}\operatorname{Tr}M_{n,r}
=
\Lambda(n).
\]

The exact prime-power selector is thus a **singular endpoint phenomenon**: the simple pole supplies one inverse power of `a`; a one-prime difference survives, while every higher mixed-prime difference vanishes to higher order.

## 5. The endpoint is simultaneously the arithmetic and operator-concavity boundary

The parameter `r=-1` is not selected by fitting `Lambda` after the fact. It already has two independent structural roles.

First, it is the inverse-scale member
\[
f_{-1}(x)=1-x^{-1},
\]
whose normalized Jensen defect is exactly the Mathia-native `Q_n` of `WP-074`.

Second, `r=-1` is the negative endpoint of the standard operator-concave power/log range for this normalization. Moving from `r=-1` toward `r=0` preserves positivity but immediately changes the trace from additive `log n` to the bounded degree statistic (6), and immediately gives nonzero Möbius mass to mixed composites.

At `r=0`, the logarithmic member is exactly `WP-082`, including its dyadic trace. The present calculation shows why that collapse is not an isolated failure of `log`: every interior homogeneous Jensen member has the same qualitative obstruction. Its degree trace is of the form
\[
C_r(1-n^{-r-1}),
\]
not a new archimedean spectral channel, and its primitive has the wrong arithmetic support.

This rules out using the power/log deformation itself as the missing bridge between the finite inverse-scale mechanism and an archimedean positive completion.

This audit also prevents an overclaim: the result is not a new proof of von Mangoldt's identity. It explains **why this particular Mathia positive cover geometry singles out the inverse-scale response** among its natural homogeneous Jensen deformations.
