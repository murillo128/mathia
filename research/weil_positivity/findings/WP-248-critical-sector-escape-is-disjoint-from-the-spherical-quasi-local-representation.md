# WP-248 — Critical sector escape is disjoint from the spherical quasi-local representation

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUASI-LOCAL-PRODUCT-STATE + WEAK-EQUIVALENCE + CRITICAL-HALF-DENSITY-DISJOINTNESS + NONSPATIAL-PRODUCT-AUTOMORPHISM + REPRESENTATION-SAFE + MATCHED-CONTROLS + DECISIVE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-247` leaves one mathematically legitimate escape from its critical restricted-tensor obstruction: declare the critical all-prime local vectors to be a new reference sequence and work in a different incomplete infinite-tensor sector. That avoids the false claim that the product vector itself does not exist. The next question is whether this is merely a harmless change of Hilbert realization from which the ordinary spherical automorphic/scattering positivity can still be inherited by a bounded comparison.

For the direct product-state realization, the answer is no. At the same critical half-density where `WP-247` leaves the spherical incomplete sector, the new product state is **weakly inequivalent** to the spherical product state and the two GNS representations of the quasi-local algebra are **disjoint**. Equivalently, there is no nonzero bounded intertwiner that represents the same quasi-local observables in the two sectors. An abstract product automorphism carrying one product state to the other still exists, but at critical scale it is not spatially implementable in the spherical representation. Thus moving to the new incomplete sector is a genuine superselection/representation change for the local observable algebra, not only a new choice of reference vector.

This does not rule out a genuinely new global construction in the critical sector. It does rule out the easiest survivor of `WP-247`: switch sectors and then inherit the canonical spherical Maaß--Selberg/scattering positivity through an ordinary bounded identity-observable comparison. A surviving route must build the relevant global/scattering comparison natively in the new sector, enlarge the observable category, or derive a non-quasi-local/unbounded bridge with its own domain and sign theorem.

## 1. A two-plane quasi-local algebra already detects the separation

Retain the notation of `WP-247`. At each finite prime choose the spherical unit vector `Omega_p` and a unit nonspherical vector `eta_p` with

\[
\langle \Omega_p,\eta_p\rangle=0.
\tag{1}
\]

It is enough to use the canonical two-plane generated by these vectors,

\[
K_p=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega_p,\eta_p\}\cong\mathbb C^2,
\tag{2}
\]

and the quasi-local UHF algebra

\[
\mathcal A
=\overline{\bigcup_{F\Subset\mathbb P}
\bigotimes_{p\in F}\mathcal B(K_p)}.
\tag{3}
\]

This avoids any issue about which C*-tensor norm to place on larger infinite-dimensional local operator algebras. Every observable in (3) acts at only finitely many primes, and (3) is a subalgebra of the natural finite-prime observable system in any larger automorphic realization containing the chosen two-planes.

For amplitudes `|a_p|<1`, define

\[
\psi_p
=\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}\,\Omega_p+a_p\eta_p.
\tag{4}
\]

The spherical and deformed product states on `A` are

\[
\omega_\Omega=\bigotimes_p\omega_{\Omega_p},
\qquad
\omega_a=\bigotimes_p\omega_{\psi_p}.
\tag{5}
\]

Their local transition amplitudes are gauge-invariantly

\[
|\langle\Omega_p,\psi_p\rangle|
=\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

The classical weak-equivalence criterion for unit product sequences, equivalently the quasi-equivalence criterion for the associated product states on the quasi-local algebra, is

\[
\omega_\Omega\sim_{\mathrm{q.e.}}\omega_a
\iff
\sum_p\left(1-|\langle\Omega_p,\psi_p\rangle|\right)<\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

For (4),

\[
1-\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}
=\frac{|a_p|^2}{1+\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}},
\tag{8}
\]

so termwise

\[
\frac{|a_p|^2}{2}
\le
1-\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}
\le
|a_p|^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore the representation threshold is exactly the same square-summability boundary found at the Hilbert-vector level in `WP-247`:

\[
\boxed{
\omega_\Omega\sim_{\mathrm{q.e.}}\omega_a
\iff
\sum_p|a_p|^2<\infty.
}
\tag{10}
\]

When the sum diverges, the two product-state GNS representations are disjoint. This is stronger than saying that two particular infinite product vectors are orthogonal: disjointness is a statement about the representations of the entire quasi-local observable algebra.

## 2. The Weil critical amplitudes are in different quasi-local sectors

For the scale-only deformation

\[
a_p(\sigma)=p^{-\sigma},
\tag{11}
\]

(10) gives quasi-equivalence precisely when

\[
\sum_p p^{-2\sigma}<\infty,
\tag{12}
\]

hence precisely for `sigma>1/2`. At the critical half-density,

\[
\sum_p |a_p(1/2)|^2
=\sum_p\frac1p
=\infty.
\tag{13}
\]

The exact first Mangoldt amplitude used in `WP-247`,

\[
b_p(\sigma)=(\log p)p^{-\sigma},
\tag{14}
\]

has the same boundary. At `sigma=1/2`, all local amplitudes are still strictly below one, while

\[
\sum_p|b_p(1/2)|^2
=\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}
=\infty.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus both natural critical product states are weakly inequivalent to the spherical product state and therefore disjoint from it as representations of `A`.

Let `(pi_Omega,H_Omega,xi_Omega)` and `(pi_a,H_a,xi_a)` denote the two GNS triples. Disjointness implies

\[
\boxed{
T\pi_\Omega(A)=\pi_a(A)T\ \ \forall A\in\mathcal A,
\quad T\in\mathcal B(H_\Omega,H_a)
\Longrightarrow T=0.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Indeed a nonzero bounded intertwiner has, via its polar decomposition, a nonzero partial isometry intertwining nonzero subrepresentations, contradicting disjointness. Hence the critical sector cannot be connected to the spherical one by a bounded operator that keeps the identity representation of the quasi-local finite-prime observables fixed.

## 3. The abstract product automorphism survives, but is nonspatial

The preceding statement must not be strengthened into the false claim that the two quasi-local systems are abstractly unrelated. For each `p`, choose a unitary `U_p` on `K_p` with

\[
U_p\Omega_p=\psi_p.
\tag{17}
\]

Because a quasi-local observable has finite support, the rule

\[
\alpha\!\left(\bigotimes_{p\in F}A_p\right)
=
\bigotimes_{p\in F}U_pA_pU_p^*
\tag{18}
\]

defines an automorphism of `A` without requiring any infinite operator product to converge. With the corresponding orientation convention,

\[
\omega_a=\omega_\Omega\circ\alpha^{-1}.
\tag{19}
\]

So an abstract algebraic transport always exists once the local rotations have been chosen. At critical non-square-summable scale, however, `alpha` is not spatial in the spherical GNS representation. If a unitary `V` on `H_Omega` implemented it,

\[
V\pi_\Omega(A)V^*=\pi_\Omega(\alpha(A)),
\tag{20}
\]

then `omega_Omega o alpha^{-1}` would be normal in the spherical representation and its GNS representation would be quasi-equivalent to a subrepresentation of `pi_Omega`, contradicting (10)--(16).

This distinction is the relevant one for the surviving automorphic route. The complete infinite tensor product may contain both incomplete sectors, and the abstract quasi-local algebra admits the product automorphism (18), but the critical rotation is **outer/nonspatial relative to the spherical product representation**. Merely conjugating every observable by the hand-chosen `alpha` transports an already existing theory isomorphically; it does not derive a new finite--archimedean coupling, a Gamma/polar counterterm, or a Weil sign theorem. To matter for the mandate, a comparison must be forced by the automorphic/boundary construction itself and must explain how its global scattering object acts across or inside the new sector.

## 4. Matched controls and representation audit

**Subcritical control.** If `sigma>1/2` in (11), or more generally `a in ell^2(P)`, then (10) gives quasi-equivalence. After local phases are normalized, this is the same regime in which `WP-247` proves convergence of the product vector in the spherical incomplete tensor product. The obstruction is therefore not infinite support by itself; it turns on at the exact critical half-density.

**Finite-support control.** If only finitely many `a_p` are nonzero, both states are related by an ordinary finite tensor unitary and are trivially in the same representation class. Every finite-level approximation therefore misses the global disjointness unless the tail criterion is tracked.

**Phase/gauge control.** Equation (7) uses the modulus of the local overlap. Componentwise phase changes cannot repair the divergence. This is the weak-equivalence, rather than merely strong-equivalence, statement and is the correct representation-level invariant.

**Basis control.** The criterion depends only on the transition probability between the distinguished local line and the deformed local line. Simultaneously changing local coordinates by unitaries leaves (6)--(10) unchanged. The result is not a preferred-basis artifact of writing `psi_p` in the pair `(Omega_p,eta_p)`.

**Bare-Hilbert-space control.** Abstractly isomorphic Hilbert spaces do not falsify (16). The invariant is the representation of the same quasi-local algebra. A Hilbert-space unitary with no intertwining property carries no automorphic or observable meaning.

**Larger-algebra control.** Disjointness already occurs on the two-plane quasi-local subalgebra (3). Any bounded comparison intertwining a larger finite-prime observable algebra would restrict to an intertwiner for (3), so it is also excluded. Conversely, a genuinely non-quasi-local global operator can evade this argument; that is an explicit surviving category change, not a counterexample to the claim.

## 5. Aggressive falsification and surviving escapes

The strongest apparent objection is (18): since the product automorphism exists, perhaps spherical Maaß--Selberg positivity can simply be pushed into the critical sector. This is mathematically possible as abstract transport, but it does not solve the line's problem. The transported positive form is isomorphic to the starting one and has exactly the same signed-remainder/Weil gap unless an additional source-defined global operation changes the decomposition. If `U_p` is chosen solely because its rotation angle encodes `p^{-1/2}` or `(log p)/sqrt(p)`, the arithmetic has been inserted into the equivalence rather than forced by an independent geometric theorem.

A second objection is to work in the complete von Neumann infinite tensor product and use a global unitary that maps one incomplete sector onto another. Such product unitaries are legitimate on the complete tensor product. They do not contradict (16), because they are not bounded intertwiners of the **identity** quasi-local representations on the two GNS sectors; they conjugate the local algebra by (18). This remains a viable ambient construction only if the full automorphic/scattering geometry canonically supplies the cross-sector map and a new positive form rather than importing them by declaration.

Unbounded or distributional intertwiners, crossed-product enlargements that make `alpha` inner, type-III completions, continuous fields of sectors, and correlated global states not of product form all remain outside the theorem. Each is a genuine new object with new domain/closability, normalization, and sign obligations. In particular, `WP-248` does not prove that no critical global automorphic geometry exists.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The representation-theoretic ingredients are classical. Von Neumann's *On infinite direct products*, Compositio Mathematica **6** (1939), 1--77, supplies the complete/incomplete infinite tensor-product sector framework. Araki and Nakagami, *A Remark on an Infinite Tensor Product of von Neumann Algebras*, Publ. RIMS **8** (1972), 363--374, DOI `10.2977/PRIMS/1195193114`, study equivalence classes and central support for infinite tensor-product representations. The weak-equivalence/product-state quasi-equivalence criterion used in (7), and the disjointness of weakly inequivalent product states, are standard operator-algebra facts.

A recent terminology check is Andrew Lesniewski, *A complete ultrametric on von Neumann's incomplete tensor products*, arXiv:`2607.09627` (2026). Its gauge-invariant weak-equivalence relation uses `sum_j (1-|<phi_j,psi_j>|)<infinity` and explicitly identifies that relation with quasi-equivalence of product states on the quasi-local algebra; weakly inequivalent product states are disjoint. This source is used here only as a modern statement of the classical criterion, not as novelty evidence.

A bounded search across infinite tensor products, quasi-local product states, disjoint GNS representations, restricted automorphic tensor products, critical `p^{-1/2}` deformations, Riemann/Weil positivity, Maaß--Selberg, and scattering did not locate a source applying this product-state disjointness threshold to the specific all-prime critical escape left by `WP-247`. That absence is not a completeness claim. The theorem itself is not new; the substantive result is the Mathia-specific synthesis that the exact critical prime weights force a **representation-level** separation at the same boundary where the canonical restricted tensor product fails.

The mechanism is related in spirit to implementability thresholds such as Shale--Stinespring, but this finding does not invoke a CCR/CAR Bogoliubov theorem: the exact object here is a product-state representation of a quasi-local tensor algebra, and the invariant is local fidelity/weak equivalence.

## 7. Consequence for Weil Positivity

`WP-247` showed that critical all-prime nonspherical data cannot arise as a norm/strong limit inside the canonical spherical restricted tensor sector. `WP-248` sharpens the surviving sector-switch escape:

\[
\boxed{
\text{critical all-prime product deformation}
\Longrightarrow
\text{weakly inequivalent product state}
\Longrightarrow
\text{disjoint quasi-local GNS representation}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

The new incomplete sector is therefore not a benign Hilbert-coordinate change from which canonical spherical automorphic positivity can be inherited through a bounded identity-observable intertwiner. The abstract product automorphism remains available but is nonspatial at critical scale and, by itself, only transports the old theory isomorphically.

A surviving route must now derive at least one genuinely global ingredient that is absent from the product-state sector change itself: a source-defined automorphic/scattering construction **native to the critical sector**; a canonical non-quasi-local enlargement in which the cross-sector operation is intrinsic and has an independent positivity theorem; or a correlated global object whose critical arithmetic is not encoded as a product-state deformation. It must still produce the finite-prime, Gamma and polar terms with the exact Weil orientation over the full admissible test class. The sector switch alone supplies none of those missing pieces.
