# WP-140 — minimal Kron pseudodeterminant manufactures the missing log only through a normalization anomaly

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + NEAR-MISS + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIME-CIRCLE + KRON-SCHUR + SINGULAR-DETERMINANT + MATCHED-COMPOSITE-CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` for using the most canonical nonlocal positive one-hole response of `PC-162` as an inherited-positive source of the finite-place Weil coefficient.

`WP-138` shows that fixed regular positive spectral responses of the one-hole new-prime puncture are too small to supply the missing `log q` factor, while `WP-139` shows at the minimal base `d=2` that the first singular ambient/hole pseudodeterminant finite part is bounded and prime-blind. The new `PC-162` result leaves a sharper possibility: instead of reading the puncture defect or the punctured operator, harmonically eliminate the old vertex and use the resulting **nonlocal positive Kron correction**.

At `d=2`, that route contains a striking near-miss. A completely canonical singular determinant ratio formed from the Kron correction and its incident-edge diagonal has the exact asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\det' C_{2,m}}{\det \Delta_{2,m}}
=\log m+\log48+\log\frac{m}{m+1}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus under the normalized Prime-Circle inverse-square operator it produces the missing `+log m` with coefficient exactly one. But the same calculation also shows why this is **not** the sought geometric Weil positivity mechanism. The logarithm is created by comparing determinant lines of different rank. Under a common positive rescaling of the geometry its coefficient changes arbitrarily, even though all underlying operators remain positive. With the unnormalized chord Laplacian the same ratio instead has leading term `-log m`.

The effect is therefore a one-zero-mode scale anomaly, not a sign theorem inherited from positivity. It is also exactly prime-blind: every formula below holds for all odd composite `m` in the same one-hole matched control. The canonical normalization makes the near-miss especially sharp, but it does not convert the determinant ratio into the exact finite Weil measure or a global positive quadratic form.

## 1. Minimal one-hole Kron response is an explicit star-mesh Laplacian

Take `d=2` and odd `m>=3`. As in `WP-139`, after cyclic relabeling the semi-primitive ambient fiber is a regular `m`-gon. Let the deleted old vertex be `0` and put, for the surviving vertices `j=1,...,m-1`,

\[
g_j:=\frac1{4\sin^2(\pi j/m)},
\qquad
D_m:=\operatorname{diag}(g_1,\ldots,g_{m-1}),
\qquad
s_m:=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}g_j.
\tag{2}
\]

The Prime-Circle normalization is

\[
\alpha_m:=(2m)^{-2}=\frac1{4m^2}.
\tag{3}
\]

For a single deleted center, the `PC-162` Schur/Kron formula specializes exactly to

\[
\boxed{
C_{2,m}
=\alpha_m\left(D_m-\frac{g g^*}{s_m}\right)\succeq0,
}
\tag{4}
\]

where `g=(g_1,...,g_{m-1})^T`. The corresponding normalized incident-edge diagonal before harmonic relaxation is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{2,m}=\alpha_m D_m\succ0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

The unnormalized matrix

\[
L_m^{\rm mesh}:=D_m-\frac{g g^*}{s_m}
\tag{6}
\]

is the Laplacian of the complete star-mesh generated by eliminating the center. Its off-diagonal effective conductances are `g_i g_j/s_m`; it has the one-dimensional constant nullspace and rank `m-2`. This is not an arbitrary wrapper around a spectrum: it is the minimum-energy boundary response supplied by the native one-hole geometry of `PC-162`.

## 2. The singular determinant ratio is exactly computable

The classical cosecant and sine products give

\[
\boxed{
s_m=\frac{m^2-1}{12},
\qquad
\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}g_j=\frac1{m^2}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, delete any one leaf from (6). The rank-one determinant lemma gives the corresponding cofactor

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det\left(
D_{\widehat i}
-\frac{g_{\widehat i}g_{\widehat i}^*}{s_m}
\right)
&=
\left(\prod_{j\ne i}g_j\right)
\left(1-\frac{s_m-g_i}{s_m}\right)\\
&=\frac{\prod_j g_j}{s_m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Since (6) is a connected `(m-1)`-vertex weighted Laplacian, Kirchhoff's matrix-tree identity converts a cofactor into the product of its nonzero eigenvalues:

\[
\boxed{
\det' L_m^{\rm mesh}
=(m-1)\frac{\prod_jg_j}{s_m}
=\frac{12}{m^2(m+1)}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Scaling by (3) and using `rank C_{2,m}=m-2` yields

\[
\det' C_{2,m}
=\alpha_m^{m-2}\frac{12}{m^2(m+1)},
\tag{10}
\]

while

\[
\det\Delta_{2,m}
=\alpha_m^{m-1}\frac1{m^2}.
\tag{11}
\]

Hence the ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det' C_{2,m}}{\det\Delta_{2,m}}
=\alpha_m^{-1}\frac{m-1}{s_m}
=\frac{48m^2}{m+1}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Taking logarithms proves (1). In particular,

\[
\frac1{\sqrt m}
\log\frac{\det' C_{2,m}}{\det\Delta_{2,m}}
=
\frac{\log m}{\sqrt m}
+\frac{\log48+\log(m/(m+1))}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{13}
\]

Equation (13) is why this route is a genuine near-miss rather than another bounded response: the leading critical finite-place scale is exactly the Mangoldt scale for a newly adjoined prime.

## 3. The coefficient of `log m` is a determinant-line scaling anomaly

The same exact formula gives the decisive falsification. Replace the normalized operator by an arbitrary common positive scalar multiple `c_m` of the same unnormalized geometry:

\[
C_m(c):=c_mL_m^{\rm mesh},
\qquad
\Delta_m(c):=c_mD_m,
\qquad c_m>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Both matrices remain positive for every choice of `c_m`. But their determinant ranks differ by one, so

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\det' C_m(c)}{\det\Delta_m(c)}
=
\log\frac{12}{m+1}-\log c_m.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the singular readout is not invariant under a harmless positive rescaling of the energy. If

\[
c_m=A m^{-\beta},
\tag{16}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\det' C_m(c)}{\det\Delta_m(c)}
=(\beta-1)\log m+\log\frac{12}{A}+o(1).
}
\tag{17}
\]

The unnormalized geometry `c_m=1` has leading coefficient `-1`; the Prime-Circle continuum normalization `c_m=(4m^2)^{-1}` has coefficient `+1`; another positive normalization changes it again. Positivity of the underlying Schur response therefore determines **neither the sign nor the coefficient of the logarithm**.

This is the exact structural source of the near-miss. `C_m(c)` has one fewer nonzero determinant direction than `Delta_m(c)`. Their ratio consequently carries one inverse power of the arbitrary common energy unit. Taking a logarithm converts that missing determinant-line dimension into `-log c_m`. The Prime-Circle `m^{-2}` normalization contributes `+2 log m`; the unnormalized star-mesh ratio already contributes `-log m`; their combination happens to leave `+log m`.

A positivity theorem is invariant under multiplying the entire energy by a positive scalar. The candidate Weil coefficient in (15) is not. Therefore its sign cannot be the shadow of the same geometric positivity that gives (4).

## 4. Exact finite Weil support is still not obtained

Even accepting the Prime-Circle normalization as canonical, (1) is not exactly `log m`. The residual local term is

\[
\boxed{
E_m:=\log48+\log\frac{m}{m+1}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

It tends to `log 48` rather than to zero. Hence the critical finite measure supplied by this readout would carry

\[
\frac{\log m+E_m}{\sqrt m}
\tag{19}
\]

at first birth, not the Weil coefficient `(log m)/sqrt(m)`. Removing (18) by hand would be exactly the kind of local counterterm that the Weil-positivity mandate forbids unless another part of the same geometry forces it independently. In particular, an archimedean term cannot simply be declared to cancel (18): (18) lives at every finite refinement scale and is part of the finite-place readout itself.

The mismatch is not merely the additive constant. The correction `log(m/(m+1))` is refinement-dependent, so even after a universal normalization shift the coefficient remains nonexact.

## 5. Matched composite controls show that the logarithm is geometric scale, not prime birth

Nothing in (2)--(18) uses primality. The only hypothesis is `(m,2)=1`. Therefore the exact same determinant ratio holds for every odd composite `m` in the one-hole matched-control family of `PC-158`, `PC-161`, and `PC-162`:

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\det' C_{2,m}}{\det\Delta_{2,m}}
=\log m+\log48+\log\frac{m}{m+1}
\quad\text{for every odd }m\ge3.
}
\tag{20}
\]

When `m=q` is prime, the survivor is the genuine primitive shell `U(2q)`. For composite `m`, it is not. Yet the logarithmic response is unchanged. The `log m` therefore records the size/normalization of the one-hole fiber, not the arithmetic event that `m` is a new prime.

This matters for the Weil bridge. A local finite-place mechanism must ultimately cooperate with the exact prime-power selector, not merely produce a logarithm whenever a cyclic fiber is refined. Equation (20) can at most provide a universal scale ingredient to a different construction that already knows which refinements are arithmetic. It is not itself that selector.

## 6. Why positivity has not been transferred to a Weil form

There are three separate positive objects in the derivation: the incident diagonal `Delta_{2,m}`, the Kron response `C_{2,m}`, and the full Dirichlet energy whose Schur complement produces `C_{2,m}`. Their positivity is unconditional and geometric.

The scalar in (1), however, is a **difference of logarithmic determinant lines of unequal rank**. It is not a quadratic form, not a norm square, and not order-positive in a scale-independent sense. Equation (15) proves this directly: multiplying both positive energies by the same scalar changes the readout by `-log c_m` and can reverse its asymptotic sign.

Thus the route

\[
\text{positive one-hole Dirichlet energy}
\to
\text{positive Kron response}
\to
\text{singular pseudodeterminant ratio}
\to
\text{Weil finite coefficient}
\tag{21}
\]

breaks at the last arrow. The positive operators are genuine; the logarithmic coefficient is a normalization-sensitive nonlinear readout. No independent theorem has been found that turns the assembled finite plus archimedean explicit-formula functional into a nonnegative quadratic form from this determinant ratio.

This is distinct from `WP-139`. There the singular finite part of the ambient-versus-punctured determinant ratio is bounded and fails even to produce `log m`. Here the nonlocal Schur response **does** manufacture `log m` at the canonical normalization, but only because a zero-mode rank mismatch converts the normalization scale into a logarithm. The obstruction is therefore not insufficient growth but failure of positivity invariance and arithmetic specificity.

Directed prior-art checks found the expected classical Kron and matrix-tree machinery, not a distinct literature mechanism turning this particular unequal-rank determinant ratio into Weil positivity. This absence is not a historical-priority claim.

## 8. Scope boundary and falsification surface

This finding closes only the direct inherited-positivity interpretation of the singular ratio (1). It does **not** prove that determinant lines are irrelevant to a global solution.

An equal-rank relative determinant, a canonically metrized determinant line, or a global finite--archimedean construction could in principle remove the scale ambiguity before taking a logarithm. Such a construction would have to derive its metric/reference from the same geometry, recover the **exact** finite-prime coefficients rather than (19), survive the matched composite control, produce the Gamma/pole terms intrinsically, and carry an independent global sign theorem. Merely choosing the normalization or subtracting (18) to obtain `log m` does not satisfy those requirements.

The exact calculation is directly falsifiable. For odd `m`, a different value of `s_m`, `prod_j g_j`, the weighted-Laplacian cofactor, or the pseudodeterminant ratio would contradict (7)--(12). More conceptually, any claim that the logarithm in (1) is forced by positivity must explain why the equally positive rescaled family (14) is allowed to change its coefficient according to (17).

## Research consequence

The first canonical nonlocal Prime-Circle boundary response after `WP-139` reaches surprisingly close to the required finite-place arithmetic scale: its minimal-base singular determinant ratio contains `+log q` with exactly the right leading coefficient under the native inverse-square normalization.

But that success is diagnostic rather than a bridge. The logarithm is the scale anomaly of one missing determinant direction, is unchanged on matched composite fibers, carries an incorrect finite-place remainder, and has no inherited quadratic-form sign. Future determinant-line routes should therefore be required to eliminate the unequal-rank scale ambiguity **before** claiming arithmetic significance. A viable global construction would need a canonical equal-rank/relative determinant geometry or another genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean pairing whose positivity survives independently of any chosen normalization.
