# WP-357 — Finite Hecke-polynomial cusp-boundary Grams are universal Brownian overlap geometry

## Claim

`WP-356` left open whether several Hecke correspondences, multiplied or correlated **before** taking the positive cusp-boundary square, could evade the one-prime annulus obstruction. On the cusp constant mode, that entire finite Hecke-algebra escape can be classified exactly.

For the full modular group, every Petersson-normalized Hecke operator `T_n` becomes, after the same unitary log-height conjugation used in `WP-356`, a finite symmetric translation polynomial

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U T_n\mathcal U^{-1}
=
\sum_{d\mid n} S_{\log(n/d^2)} .
}
\tag{1}
\]

For a sharp height projection \(P_\tau=\mathbf 1_{(\tau,\infty)}\), the boundary vectors generated by the individual translations have the exact plane-wave Gram kernel

\[
\boxed{
K(a,b)
=
\langle \chi_{a,\tau},\chi_{b,\tau}\rangle
=
\mathbf 1_{ab>0}\min(|a|,|b|),
}
\tag{2}
\]

where \(\chi_{a,\tau}(x)=P_\tau(x)-P_\tau(x+a)\). Thus the positive boundary Gram of **any finite Hecke polynomial or finite PSD correlation of Hecke boundary defects** is built from two copies of the classical Brownian covariance kernel `min(s,t)`, one for positive and one for negative translations.

This positivity is not arithmetic-rigid. Replacing the actual prime logarithms by arbitrary positive lengths \(L_p\) preserves the complete normalized Hecke multiplication law on this constant-mode representation and preserves every boundary Gram inequality. Likewise, for every finite Hecke polynomial the standing wave

\[
e^{itx}+\eta(t)e^{-itx}
\]

is a simultaneous Hecke eigenwave for **any** reciprocal unimodular phase \(\eta\), not only the Riemann scattering phase. The same CDD/divisor-changing phase controls that survive `WP-345` therefore also survive the **positivity test** of every finite Hecke/cusp-boundary Gram in this reduced class; the numerical Gram value itself need not be phase-independent.

Consequently, multiplying finitely many Hecke correspondences or taking finite positive cross-correlations before the cusp-boundary square does not make the inherited positivity constrain the Riemann scattering divisor. The finite Hecke algebra can supply genuine arithmetic translation lengths and nontrivial cross-prime interference, but the **sign theorem itself** is universal half-line translation geometry.

This does not close nonconstant Fourier modes, an infinite all-prime Hecke completion, a genuinely adelic/higher-rank truncation, or a source theorem that couples Euler/Hecke rigidity to a noncentral positive operator before reduction to the cusp constant mode.

## 1. Exact all-\(n\) constant-mode Hecke representation

Use the standard self-adjoint normalization

\[
(T_nF)(z)
=
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\sum_{\substack{ad=n\\ d>0}}
\sum_{b\!\!\!\pmod d}
F\!\left(\frac{az+b}{d}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

If \(F\) is independent of \(x=\Re z\), then each divisor \(d\mid n\) contributes \(d\) identical terms and

\[
(T_nF)(y)
=
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\sum_{d\mid n}
d\,F\!\left(\frac{n}{d^2}y\right).
\tag{4}
\]

As in `WP-356`, put \(y=e^x\) and use the exact unitary map

\[
(\mathcal UF)(x)=e^{-x/2}F(e^x),
\qquad
\mathcal U:L^2(\mathbb R_+,dy/y^2)\to L^2(\mathbb R,dx).
\tag{5}
\]

For a dilation by \(r=n/d^2\),

\[
e^{-x/2}
F(re^x)
=
r^{1/2}
(\mathcal UF)(x+\log r).
\tag{6}
\]

The coefficient in (4) therefore becomes

\[
\frac d{\sqrt n}\sqrt{\frac n{d^2}}=1,
\tag{7}
\]

and (1) follows. In particular,

\[
T_p\longmapsto S_{\log p}+S_{-\log p},
\tag{8}
\]

recovering `WP-356`, while for distinct primes \(p,q\),

\[
T_pT_q=T_{pq}
\longmapsto
S_{\ell_p+\ell_q}
+S_{\ell_p-\ell_q}
+S_{-\ell_p+\ell_q}
+S_{-\ell_p-\ell_q},
\tag{9}
\]

where \(\ell_p=\log p\).

The crucial point is that the self-adjoint Hecke normalization does not leave a coefficient \(p^{-1/2}\) on these translation branches. The arithmetic norm appears as the **translation length**, while the vector amplitudes are exactly one.

## 2. The complete finite boundary Gram is a Brownian overlap kernel

Let

\[
P_\tau=\mathbf 1_{(\tau,\infty)},
\qquad
S_af(x)=f(x+a).
\tag{10}
\]

For the generalized plane wave \(e_t(x)=e^{itx}\),

\[
[P_\tau,S_a]e_t(x)
=
e^{itx}e^{ita}\chi_{a,\tau}(x),
\tag{11}
\]

with

\[
\chi_{a,\tau}(x)
=
P_\tau(x)-P_\tau(x+a).
\tag{12}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
\chi_{a,\tau}
=
-\mathbf 1_{(\tau-a,\tau]}
\quad(a>0),
\qquad
\chi_{a,\tau}
=
\mathbf 1_{(\tau,\tau-a]}
\quad(a<0),
\tag{13}
\]

and \(\chi_{0,\tau}=0\). Hence intervals from opposite translation directions are disjoint, while same-direction intervals are nested. Therefore

\[
\langle\chi_{a,\tau},\chi_{b,\tau}\rangle
=
\begin{cases}
\min(|a|,|b|),&ab>0,\\
0,&ab\le0,
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]

which is (2).

Now let

\[
A=\sum_{j=1}^m c_jS_{a_j}
\tag{15}
\]

be any finite translation polynomial. From (11),

\[
\boxed{
\|[P_\tau,A]e_t\|_2^2
=
\sum_{j,k}
c_j\overline{c_k}
e^{it(a_j-a_k)}
K(a_j,a_k)
\ge0.
}
\tag{16}
\]

The matrix \(K(a_j,a_k)\) is a Gram matrix of interval indicators. On either sign sector it is exactly the Brownian covariance matrix

\[
K_+(s,t)=\min(s,t),
\qquad s,t>0.
\tag{17}
\]

Thus the positive geometry behind every finite constant-mode Hecke boundary square is completely explicit. It is not a hidden zeta kernel: it is the covariance/overlap geometry of nested half-line boundary intervals.

The same classification covers finite positive cross-correlations. Given finitely many Hecke polynomials \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) and any PSD matrix \(M=(M_{\alpha\beta})\),

\[
Q_M(f)
=
\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
M_{\alpha\beta}
\langle[P_\tau,A_\alpha]f,[P_\tau,A_\beta]f\rangle
\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]

Equation (18) is just the norm square after a finite channel factorization of \(M\). On plane waves, every matrix element reduces to finite linear combinations of the same kernel (14). Therefore allowing finite channel mixing before the positive readout does not introduce a new source of sign.

## 3. A genuine two-prime interference survives, but its positivity is still universal

The result is not merely saying that separate one-prime energies add. Take \(p<q\) and the joint Hecke correspondence \(T_{pq}=T_pT_q\). Write

\[
L_p=\log p,\qquad L_q=\log q.
\tag{19}
\]

The positive translation lengths in (9) are

\[
a_1=L_p+L_q,
\qquad
a_2=L_q-L_p,
\tag{20}
\]

with their negatives. Using (16),

\[
\boxed{
\|[P_\tau,T_{pq}]e_t\|_2^2
=
4L_q
+
4(L_q-L_p)\cos(2tL_p).
}
\tag{21}
\]

The cosine is a real cross-prime interference term generated **before** the square. Nevertheless,

\[
\|[P_\tau,T_{pq}]e_t\|_2^2
\ge
4L_p>0.
\tag{22}
\]

So finite Hecke multiplication does create nontrivial mixed-prime structure, but its positivity is still exactly the nested-interval Gram inequality. Nothing in (21)--(22) uses primality beyond the numerical choice of the two positive lengths.

This is a sharper falsifier than the single-prime calculation in `WP-356`: even when cross-prime interference is present inside the positive square, the sign remains a universal property of the half-line boundary representation.

## 4. Arbitrary prime lengths preserve the normalized Hecke algebra

The decisive matched control keeps more than positivity. Assign an arbitrary positive length \(L_p>0\) to every prime label and extend additively,

\[
\ell(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)L_p.
\tag{23}
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal H_n^{(L)}
=
\sum_{d\mid n}
S_{\ell(n)-2\ell(d)}.
\tag{24}
\]

For a prime power,

\[
\mathcal H_{p^k}^{(L)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^k
S_{(k-2j)L_p}.
\tag{25}
\]

The elementary translation identity gives the normalized local Hecke recurrence

\[
\mathcal H_p^{(L)}
\mathcal H_{p^k}^{(L)}
=
\mathcal H_{p^{k+1}}^{(L)}
+
\mathcal H_{p^{k-1}}^{(L)}
\qquad(k\ge1),
\tag{26}
\]

with \(\mathcal H_{p^0}^{(L)}=I\). Different prime sectors commute, so multiplicativity gives the full relation

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_m^{(L)}\mathcal H_n^{(L)}
=
\sum_{d\mid(m,n)}
\mathcal H_{mn/d^2}^{(L)}.
}
\tag{27}
\]

Thus the complete finite normalized Hecke algebra relation survives when the actual arithmetic lengths \(\log p\) are replaced by arbitrary positive numbers.

Equations (14)--(18) then remain unchanged with the new lengths. The matched control therefore preserves simultaneously:

- the finite Hecke multiplication law;
- self-adjoint symmetric translation structure;
- the sharp cusp-boundary commutator construction;
- every finite positive Gram/correlation inequality.

What it does **not** preserve is the actual rational-prime norm map \(p\mapsto\log p\). Hence a sign theorem that only sees this Hecke/half-line representation cannot distinguish the Riemann norm data from a continuous generalized-length deformation.

This does not say that Hecke arithmetic is fake. It says that the inherited **positivity** is too representation-universal to force the arithmetic completion.

## 5. Every reciprocal unitary scattering phase is invisible to this finite positivity theorem

For the representation (24), a plane wave is a simultaneous eigenvector:

\[
\mathcal H_n^{(L)}e_t
=
h_n^{(L)}(t)e_t,
\tag{28}
\]

where

\[
h_n^{(L)}(t)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}
e^{it(\ell(n)-2\ell(d))}.
\tag{29}
\]

The divisor involution \(d\leftrightarrow n/d\) makes the shift multiset symmetric, so

\[
h_n^{(L)}(-t)=h_n^{(L)}(t)\in\mathbb R.
\tag{30}
\]

Consequently, for **any** unit-modulus phase \(\eta(t)\),

\[
f_{t,\eta}(x)
=
e^{itx}+\eta(t)e^{-itx}
\tag{31}
\]

obeys the same simultaneous eigenrelation

\[
\mathcal H_n^{(L)}f_{t,\eta}
=
h_n^{(L)}(t)f_{t,\eta}.
\tag{32}
\]

For the genuine modular Eisenstein constant term, \(\eta(t)=\varphi(t)\) is the arithmetic scattering phase. But (32) remains true if \(\varphi\) is multiplied by any reciprocal unimodular inner/CDD factor of the type used in `WP-345`--`WP-347`, changing the meromorphic scattering divisor while preserving the boundary modulus and functional-equation symmetry.

At the same time,

\[
Q_M(f_{t,\eta})\ge0
\tag{33}
\]

for every \(\eta\), simply because (18) is a positive Gram form. The value of \(Q_M(f_{t,\eta})\) may depend on the phase; the point is that **its nonnegativity does not**. Thus finite Hecke eigenstructure plus finite cusp-boundary Gram positivity does not constrain the scattering phase enough to select its divisor.

This is the central no-go statement. The finite prime correspondences now enter before positivity and may be correlated nontrivially, yet the resulting sign still survives a divisor-changing matched control.

## 6. Critical half-density remains outside the inherited vector geometry

Equation (7) generalizes the normalization observation of `WP-356`: in every canonical \(T_n\), each constant-mode translation branch has unit amplitude after the hyperbolic Jacobian is absorbed. Products do not repair this; they only generate more translation branches and their overlaps.

A scalar coefficient \(c_n\) inserted before the commutator enters a positive self-pairing quadratically,

\[
\|[P_\tau,c_nT_n]f\|^2
=
|c_n|^2
\|[P_\tau,T_n]f\|^2.
\tag{34}
\]

Therefore the finite Hecke algebra does not by itself turn the critical one-power attenuation \(n^{-1/2}\) into a vector amplitude. As in `WP-226` and `WP-356`, such an attenuation would have to be supplied at square-root scale before the positive self-pairing, or arise from a genuinely different non-self-pairing/operator-order mechanism.

The present result should not be overstated: a source-defined global construction could in principle provide such a vector weight from nonconstant Fourier coefficients, an adelic measure, or a different Hilbert module. What is excluded is obtaining it merely by multiplying finitely many standard Hecke correspondences on the cusp constant mode and then taking their sharp-boundary Gram square.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

All ingredients are classical. The Hecke formula (3), its Eisenstein eigenvalues, the Petersson/self-adjoint normalization, half-line translations, sharp projections, and the kernel `min(s,t)` are standard. Classical Wiener-Hopf/half-line translation theory is the natural operator-theoretic neighborhood, while Maaß-Selberg/Arthur truncation and Wong's explicit-formula decomposition remain the relevant global automorphic background.

A bounded literature search for `Hecke + Arthur/Maaß-Selberg truncation + commutator`, `half-line translation + projection commutator`, and the `min(s,t)` overlap kernel did not surface a named result asserting the exact all-\(n\) reduction (1)--(2) as a Weil/RH positivity mechanism. **No historical novelty is claimed.** The Mathia contribution is the exact synthesis needed by the branch: extend the `WP-356` one-prime boundary defect to the whole finite Hecke algebra, identify its positive cross-channel kernel explicitly, and construct a matched deformation that preserves the Hecke relations while freeing the arithmetic lengths and scattering divisor.

This is distinct from `WP-029`, whose Clifford commutator energies radialize on a Boolean prime-support cube, and from `WP-187`, whose Hardy compression splits a unimodular multiplier into positive Hankel leakage defects. Here the source operator is the automorphic Hecke algebra itself, the prime correspondences are inserted before truncation positivity, and the no-go comes from exact half-line boundary geometry plus a Hecke-algebra-preserving generalized-length control.

## 8. Aggressive falsification and surviving scope

The strongest reading of the route survives several easy objections:

- the prime operators are not appended after scalar scattering normalization;
- several primes can interfere inside one positive square, as (21) shows;
- arbitrary finite PSD mixing of Hecke boundary defects is allowed;
- the Hecke multiplication relations are retained by the matched control.

Nevertheless the mechanism still fails the mandate because its positivity theorem survives both arbitrary positive deformations of the prime lengths and arbitrary reciprocal unimodular changes of the scattering phase. It therefore cannot, by itself, orient the Riemann zero divisor or supply the full Weil form.

The following escapes remain open and are not ruled out here:

1. nonconstant Fourier modes, where Hecke action couples arithmetic Fourier coefficients rather than only the cusp radial coordinate;
2. an infinite all-prime completion whose domain/closability supplies new rigidity absent from every finite Hecke polynomial;
3. an adelic or higher-rank truncation in which finite and archimedean local operators mix before reducing to a scalar translation representation;
4. a specifically arithmetic operator-order or cohomological theorem that fails under the generalized-length deformation;
5. a source-defined construction that forces the critical half-density at vector level before the positive square.

Repeating finite products of constant-mode Hecke operators, adding more finite boundary channels, changing the finite PSD channel metric, or using another reciprocal unitary scattering phase does not leave this classified regime.

## Consequence

`WP-356` found that one prime Hecke correspondence crossing the cusp boundary produces a positive annulus energy but leaves the scattering orientation uncontrolled. The finite-algebra extension is now exact:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite normalized Hecke algebra}
+
\text{sharp cusp boundary}
\longrightarrow
\text{two-sided Brownian interval-overlap Gram geometry}.
}
\tag{35}
\]

The arithmetic lengths and their cross-prime interference are real, but the **positivity** is inherited from a universal representation that survives generalized prime lengths and divisor-changing boundary phases. Any viable Maaß-Selberg/Hecke route must therefore use information that does not collapse to this finite constant-mode Hecke/half-line Gram.
