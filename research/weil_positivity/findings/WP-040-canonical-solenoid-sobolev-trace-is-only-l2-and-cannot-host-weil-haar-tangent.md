# WP-040 — canonical solenoid Sobolev trace is only L2 and cannot host the Weil Haar tangent

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-SOBOLEV-FRAMEWORK + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the specific Sobolev/distributional escape left open by `WP-037` when the regularity scale is required to come from the canonical Prime-Circle solenoid geometry of `PC-064`/`PC-065` and `WP-038`. Powers of the canonical leafwise Laplacian do not create any conductor-growing regularity on the profinite anchor fiber. For Sobolev order `s>1/2`, the exact quotient trace space on `\widehat{\mathbb Z}` is merely `L^2(\widehat{\mathbb Z})` up to equivalent norm; for `s\le1/2`, restriction to the fiber has no nondegenerate continuous extension at all. Consequently the singular Haar tangent `eta` of `WP-037`, whose primitive-conductor Fourier coefficients grow like `log q`, is not continuous in the trace/dual of **any** Sobolev order generated solely by the canonical leafwise Laplacian.

This does **not** rule out a genuinely transverse finite-adic operator, a conductor/height energy independently forced by Prime Circle, a nonseparable leaf--fiber operator, or another intrinsic distribution topology. It does rule out treating higher powers of the already-derived leafwise solenoid Laplacian as the missing Mathia-native regularization of the finite Weil birth tangent.

## 1. The open escape from WP-037 meets the canonical Sobolev scale from WP-038

`WP-037` identifies the finite arithmetic birth operator `C` as a first scaled moment variation of the normalized positive Prime-Circle radial law at profinite Haar. If

\[
K:=\widehat{\mathbb Z},
\]

and `eta` denotes the resulting cylindrical functional on locally constant observables, then for every nontrivial finite-order additive character represented by a root of unity `zeta`,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\eta(\chi_\zeta)
=-\Log(1-\zeta).
}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, for

\[
\zeta_q=e^{2\pi i/q},
\]

`WP-037` proves

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}\widehat\eta(\chi_{\zeta_q})
=\log q-\log(2\pi)+o(1).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus `eta` is not a finite measure and not an `L^1` or `L^2` Haar density. `WP-037` correctly left open a weighted Sobolev/distribution completion, subject to the hard requirement that the weight be forced by Mathia rather than chosen simply to absorb the `log q` growth.

There is now an obvious intrinsic weight to test. `PC-064` identifies the compatible ambient-circle refinement as the arithmetic solenoid

\[
\Sigma_{\mathbb Q}
\cong(\mathbb R\times\widehat{\mathbb Z})/\mathbb Z_{\rm diag},
\]

with exact sequence

\[
\boxed{
0\longrightarrow K
\longrightarrow\Sigma_{\mathbb Q}
\longrightarrow S^1
\longrightarrow0,
}
\tag{3}
\]

while `PC-065`/`WP-038` derive the canonical flat leafwise Laplacian

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\rm leaf}\chi_r=(2\pi r)^2\chi_r,
\qquad r\in\mathbb Q.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The question is therefore precise: **does the Sobolev scale generated by `(1+Delta_leaf)^{s/2}` provide the natural distribution topology in which the `WP-037` tangent becomes continuous?**

The answer is no, for every real `s`.

## 2. Restriction to the anchor fiber groups rational frequencies by `Q/Z`

Dualizing (3) gives the exact sequence already recorded in `PC-064`,

\[
\boxed{
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow\mathbb Q
\longrightarrow\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Hence

\[
\widehat K\cong\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.
\]

If `r\in\mathbb Q`, then the solenoid characters `chi_r` and `chi_{r+k}`, `k\in\mathbb Z`, restrict to the **same** character of `K`. Conversely, two rational solenoid characters restrict identically exactly when their frequencies differ by an integer.

Take a finite Fourier polynomial

\[
F=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Q}a_r\chi_r
\]

on the solenoid. Its restriction to `K` has Fourier coefficient at the class `\bar r=r+\mathbb Z` equal to

\[
\boxed{
g_{\bar r}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_{r+k}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

For finite sums this is simply character restriction; there is no analytic continuation or zeta input.

The canonical inhomogeneous leafwise Sobolev norm is

\[
\boxed{
\|F\|_{H^s_{\rm leaf}(\Sigma)}^2
=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Q}
\bigl(1+4\pi^2r^2\bigr)^s|a_r|^2.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Define the induced trace/quotient seminorm on finite Fourier polynomials on `K` by the standard minimal-extension prescription

\[
\boxed{
\|g\|_{{\rm Tr},s}^2
:=\inf\left\{
\|F\|_{H^s_{\rm leaf}(\Sigma)}^2:
F|_K=g
\right\}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This restriction-with-infimum construction is standard in Sobolev theory on subsets of locally compact abelian groups. What is Mathia-specific here is that the exact sequences (3)--(5) and the leaf symbol (4) make (8) explicitly computable.

## 3. The exact one-coset minimization has threshold `s=1/2`

Fix one class `\bar r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z` and abbreviate

\[
w_s(r+k):=\bigl(1+4\pi^2(r+k)^2\bigr)^s.
\]

Among all extension coefficients `a_k:=a_{r+k}` satisfying

\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_k=g_{\bar r},
\tag{9}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
|g_{\bar r}|^2
\le
\left(\sum_k w_s(r+k)|a_k|^2\right)
\left(\sum_k w_s(r+k)^{-1}\right).
\tag{10}
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{
S_s(r):=
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\bigl(1+4\pi^2(r+k)^2\bigr)^{-s}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Because the summand is asymptotic to a constant times `|k|^{-2s}`, one has

\[
\boxed{
S_s(r)<\infty
\iff s>\frac12.
}
\tag{12}
\]

When `s>1/2`, equality in (10) is attained by

\[
a_k=
\frac{g_{\bar r}\,w_s(r+k)^{-1}}{S_s(r)},
\]

so the exact minimal cost of that fiber Fourier coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{(9)}
\sum_k w_s(r+k)|a_k|^2
=
\frac{|g_{\bar r}|^2}{S_s(r)}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Different classes in `Q/Z` use disjoint cosets in `Q`, so the minimizations decouple. Therefore, for `s>1/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\|g\|_{{\rm Tr},s}^2
=
\sum_{\bar r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z}
W_s(\bar r)|g_{\bar r}|^2,
\qquad
W_s(\bar r):=S_s(r)^{-1}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

This is an exact Fourier trace formula for the canonical Prime-Circle leafwise Sobolev scale.

## 4. Above the trace threshold, every Sobolev order induces only `L^2` on the profinite fiber

The crucial point is not merely that (14) exists. The weight `W_s` does **not grow with conductor**.

Choose the unique representative `r\in[-1/2,1/2)` of a class in `Q/Z`. For `s>1/2`, the series (11) converges uniformly in this interval by comparison with

\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}(1+|k|)^{-2s}.
\]

Hence `S_s(r)` is continuous and has a finite uniform upper bound. It also has a uniform positive lower bound: the `k=0` term gives

\[
S_s(r)
\ge
(1+\pi^2)^{-s}.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus there exist constants `0<c_s\le C_s<\infty` such that

\[
\boxed{
c_s\le W_s(\bar r)\le C_s
\qquad
\text{for every }\bar r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (14) and (16),

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Tr}_K H^s_{\rm leaf}(\Sigma_{\mathbb Q})
\cong L^2(K)
\quad\text{with equivalent Hilbert norms, for every }s>\frac12.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Increasing the leafwise Sobolev order arbitrarily far therefore adds **zero transverse Sobolev regularity** on the anchor fiber. A character of enormous finite conductor remains uniformly as cheap in the trace norm as any other fiber character.

The geometry behind this is exact. A fiber character is a coset `r+Z`. No matter how large its conductor is, that coset has a representative in `[-1/2,1/2)`. The leaf Laplacian sees only real frequency `|r|`, not denominator/finite conductor, and the quotient norm is free to choose the cheapest integer lift.

For the primitive classes

\[
\bar r_q=\frac1q+\mathbb Z,
\]

one has even

\[
W_s(\bar r_q)\longrightarrow W_s(0)
\qquad(q\to\infty),
\tag{18}
\]

rather than any conductor penalty.

## 5. At and below `s=1/2`, there is no nondegenerate continuous fiber trace

Suppose `s\le1/2`. Then `S_s(r)=\infty` for every class. For a fixed nonzero fiber character `g=\chi_{\bar r}`, truncate the reciprocal-weight minimizer to `|k|\le N`:

\[
a_k^{(N)}
=
\frac{w_s(r+k)^{-1}}
{\sum_{|j|\le N}w_s(r+j)^{-1}}
\qquad(|k|\le N).
\tag{19}
\]

The corresponding finite Fourier polynomial `F_N` satisfies

\[
F_N|_K=\chi_{\bar r},
\tag{20}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\|F_N\|_{H^s_{\rm leaf}}^2
=
\left(\sum_{|k|\le N}w_s(r+k)^{-1}\right)^{-1}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore restriction of trigonometric polynomials cannot extend continuously from `H^s_{leaf}(Sigma_Q)` to any Hausdorff topological vector space in which the nonzero character `chi_{\bar r}` remains nonzero. Otherwise (21) and continuity would force the fixed trace (20) to converge to zero.

Equivalently, the quotient seminorm (8) is identically zero on every individual fiber character when `s\le1/2`. The canonical leafwise geometry has not supplied enough regularity even to define a nondegenerate trace there.

Thus the entire canonical Sobolev scale has a sharp dichotomy:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
s\le1/2:&\text{no nondegenerate continuous trace to }K,\\[2mm]
s>1/2:&\text{trace exists but is only }L^2(K).
\end{array}
}
\tag{22}
\]

There is no Sobolev order at which a conductor-weighted distribution space appears intrinsically.

## 6. The WP-037 Weil tangent is outside every canonical trace dual

For `s>1/2`, equation (17) says that the trace Hilbert space is equivalent to `L^2(K)`. Its continuous dual is therefore again `L^2(K)` under Haar pairing. In Fourier coordinates a continuous functional must have square-summable coefficients (up to the bounded weights (16)).

But the `WP-037` tangent satisfies (2). Along the primitive sequence `1/q+Z`,

\[
|\widehat\eta(1/q+\mathbb Z)|
\ge
\log q-O(1).
\tag{23}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\bar r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z}
\frac{|\widehat\eta(\bar r)|^2}{W_s(\bar r)}
=\infty
\qquad(s>1/2),
}
\tag{24}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\eta\notin
\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}_K H^s_{\rm leaf}(\Sigma_{\mathbb Q})\bigr)'
\qquad\text{for every }s>1/2.
}
\tag{25}
\]

For `s\le1/2`, Section 5 shows that there is no nondegenerate canonical trace space to dualize in the first place.

Therefore the open chain

```text
WP-037 singular Haar tangent
    -> choose the Sobolev scale already forced by the PC-064/065 solenoid
    -> interpret eta as a continuous negative-order trace distribution
    -> seek a positive global Weil form there
```

fails at the second arrow for **every** Sobolev order generated by the canonical leafwise Laplacian.

## 7. Why choosing a conductor Sobolev weight would be genuinely extra geometry

There are of course many Hilbert topologies on `K` in which coefficients growing like `log q` define continuous distributions. For example, one can assign a positive transverse symbol `A(\bar r)` that grows with the order/denominator of `\bar r\in Q/Z` and use a negative power of that symbol in the dual norm.

But equations (14)--(18) prove that no such growth comes from the canonical leafwise Laplacian. It cannot be obtained merely by taking a higher power of `(1+Delta_leaf)`, because restriction always collapses the rational frequency to its class modulo integers and then selects a bounded real representative.

This sharpens `PC-065` and `WP-038`. Those findings showed that denominator growth is invisible to the *bulk* leaf spectrum, producing arbitrarily soft modes `1/n`. The present finding shows that the problem remains after passing to the exact **fiber trace geometry**: even arbitrarily high leafwise Sobolev regularity induces only an `L^2` transverse norm.

A successful Sobolev rescue of `WP-037` must therefore derive an additional operator which genuinely differentiates in the finite-adic/transverse direction, or couples leaf and fiber before restriction. Candidate classes include a Prime-Circle-forced ultrametric/conductor energy, a cross-level nonlocal form, or a nonseparable matrix-valued boundary response. Merely selecting a Vladimirov/height/conductor weight because it absorbs (2) would be an external regularization and does not pass the branch's intrinsic-geometry gate.

## 8. Matched controls and adversarial tests

### 8.1 Replace `eta` by an actual `L^2` fiber density

Then its Fourier coefficients are square summable, and (17) shows that it is a continuous functional on every canonical trace space with `s>1/2`. The obstruction is therefore not that the trace construction is empty; it detects exactly the absence of transverse regularity needed by the arithmetic tangent.

### 8.2 Increase the leafwise Sobolev order

No improvement occurs. Equation (16) remains a bounded-weight equivalence for every fixed `s>1/2`. In particular, taking `s` arbitrarily large cannot turn `log q` conductor growth into a continuous fiber distribution.

### 8.3 Choose a nonminimal extension of a fiber character

A high-conductor character can be represented by many rational lifts differing by integers. Declaring one expensive lift canonical would change the quotient geometry. The intrinsic trace norm is the infimum over all extensions, and (13) computes it exactly. Any rule that forbids the low-real-frequency lifts requires additional structure not present in the compact-group extension or leaf metric.

### 8.4 Add a transverse conductor symbol

This can evade the result. It is precisely the escape not ruled out. The substantive gate then moves one level deeper: the symbol and its normalization must be derived from Prime Circle itself, must couple correctly to the `WP-037` finite birth term and the `WP-036` archimedean Mellin response, and must carry an independent sign theorem.

### 8.5 Replace the arithmetic tangent by a generic cylindrical tangent with bounded coefficients

Such a tangent may still fail `L^2` if infinitely many coefficients remain of order one, but sufficiently square-summable tangents are accepted by (17). Thus the conclusion is not an RH-equivalent tautology; it is a concrete topological mismatch between one exact Mathia tangent and one exact Mathia Sobolev geometry.

## 10. Exact audit tests and research consequence

The exact core can be falsified directly:

1. dualize the fiber inclusion in `PC-064` and verify that restriction is `Q -> Q/Z`;

The research frontier is consequently narrower.
