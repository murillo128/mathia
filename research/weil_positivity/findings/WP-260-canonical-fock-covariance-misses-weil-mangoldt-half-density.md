# WP-260 — Canonical Gibbs/Fisher positivity on the symmetric prime Fock sector differentiates the Mangoldt response once too far

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + SYMMETRIC-PRIME-FOCK + THERMODYNAMIC-RESPONSE + FISHER-COVARIANCE + PRIME-POWER-COEFFICIENT-MISMATCH + FACTORIZED-PRIME-METRIC + MATCHED-CONTROLS + CLASSICAL-RIEMANN-GAS-PRIOR-ART + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-259` leaves open whether the canonical interface between the ordered free prime cover and its permutation-fixed bosonic sector has a boundary response or positive thermodynamic form that does more than carry the diagonal coefficients `Lambda(n)/sqrt(n)`. The most canonical positive response on the bosonic side is the Hessian of the log partition function: equivalently the covariance/Fisher form of Gibbs observables.

That route fails for an exact coefficient-level reason. For every finite prime truncation, a one-body prime-local source whose **first** response is the Mangoldt prime-power series has a canonical positive **second** response with coefficients

\[
k(\log p)^2p^{-k\sigma},
\]

not

\[
(\log p)p^{-k\sigma}.
\]

More generally, if the source on the `p`-mode has any amplitude `a_p` depending only on the one-particle prime mode, then its positive Hessian has coefficient `k a_p^2 p^{-k\sigma}`. No choice of `a_p` can equal the Weil/Mangoldt coefficient `(\log p)p^{-k\sigma}` simultaneously for `k=1,2,...`. The factor `k` is forced by Bose occupation variance.

Thus the canonical Gibbs/Fisher positivity of the permutation-fixed prime Fock sector is **one derivative too high** for the finite Weil coefficient list. At the target half-density `sigma=1/2`, the global bosonic partition function is additionally outside the normal Gibbs domain, but the obstruction does not rely on that divergence: it is already exact in every finite prime truncation.

The escape is correspondingly narrow. One must either insert occupation-level-dependent weights such as `a_{p,k}` that already know `k`, abandon the canonical covariance/Fisher form, or introduce a genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean/cross-prime geometry. A separable Gamma factor or an affine reparametrization cannot repair the mismatch.

## 1. Exact finite-prime response geometry

Let `P` be a finite set of primes and consider the symmetric bosonic Fock sector generated by the modes in `P`. Write

\[
H_P=\sum_{p\in P}(\log p)N_p,
\tag{1}
\]

where `N_p` is the occupation-number operator of the `p`-mode. For a real one-body source

\[
A_a=\sum_{p\in P}a_pN_p,
\tag{2}
\]

introduce

\[
Z_P(\sigma,t)
:=\operatorname{Tr}\exp(-\sigma H_P+tA_a).
\tag{3}
\]

Because the modes are independent,

\[
Z_P(\sigma,t)
=\prod_{p\in P}
\left(1-e^{t a_p}p^{-\sigma}\right)^{-1}
\tag{4}
\]

whenever `t` is in a neighborhood of zero small enough that every factor converges. Since `P` is finite, all differentiations below are elementary and require no analytic continuation.

Put `x_p=p^{-\sigma}`. Then

\[
\log Z_P(\sigma,t)
=-\sum_{p\in P}\log(1-e^{t a_p}x_p).
\tag{5}
\]

The first response is

\[
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log Z_P(\sigma,t)\right|_{t=0}
=\sum_{p\in P}a_p\frac{x_p}{1-x_p}
=\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}a_p p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{6}
\]

For the arithmetic energy source `a_p=\log p`, this is exactly

\[
\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}(\log p)p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{7}
\]

In the infinite-prime region `sigma>1`, equation (7) is the standard identity

\[
-\frac{\zeta'(\sigma)}{\zeta(\sigma)}
=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma}.
\tag{8}
\]

Coefficientwise, at `sigma=1/2`, equation (7) is precisely the finite Weil half-density list

\[
\frac{\Lambda(p^k)}{\sqrt{p^k}}
=(\log p)p^{-k/2}.
\tag{9}
\]

This is only a **linear response** identity. It carries no sign theorem by itself.

## 2. The canonical positive Hessian forces an extra occupation factor

Differentiate (5) again. One obtains

\[
\left.\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}\log Z_P(\sigma,t)\right|_{t=0}
=\sum_{p\in P}a_p^2\frac{x_p}{(1-x_p)^2}
=\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}k a_p^2p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{10}
\]

For a normal Gibbs state this is exactly the variance

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\sigma}(A_a)\ge0,
\tag{11}
\]

and with several source coordinates it is the covariance/Fisher matrix. Hence (10) is not an arbitrary positive functional added by hand: it is the canonical convexity form of the bosonic partition function.

For the arithmetic source `a_p=\log p`, however, its coefficient at `p^k` is

\[
\boxed{
k(\log p)^2p^{-k\sigma}
}
\tag{12}
\]

rather than

\[
\boxed{
(\log p)p^{-k\sigma}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

The mismatch is structural. The first derivative counts the mean occupation

\[
\mathbb E(N_p)=\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k\sigma},
\tag{14}
\]

whereas the Hessian sees the Bose occupation variance

\[
\operatorname{Var}(N_p)
=\sum_{k\ge1}k p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{15}
\]

The extra factor `k` is therefore the exact price of passing from the linear Mangoldt response to canonical thermodynamic positivity.

## 3. A one-body source cannot be rescaled to remove the mismatch

Suppose one does not insist on the energy source and allows an arbitrary real prime-local one-body amplitude `a_p`. To make the positive Hessian (10) reproduce the target coefficient at every power of the same prime would require

\[
k a_p^2=\log p
\qquad\text{for every }k\ge1.
\tag{16}
\]

Taking `k=1` and `k=2` already gives

\[
a_p^2=\log p,
\qquad
2a_p^2=\log p,
\tag{17}
\]

which is impossible for every prime `p>1`.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{no prime-local one-body source has canonical Gibbs Hessian }
\Lambda(p^k)p^{-k\sigma}
\text{ for all }k.
}
\tag{18}
\]

This is stronger than observing that the specific choice `a_p=\log p` fails. It eliminates the entire canonical class obtained by second quantizing a diagonal one-particle observable on the prime Hilbert space.

One can of course manufacture the coefficient by allowing occupation-level data, for example weights proportional to `\sqrt{(\log p)/k}` on the `k`th occupation contribution. But such a source is no longer the second quantization of a one-particle prime observable: it explicitly inserts the prime-power index `k` that the desired coefficient is supposed to emerge from. Under the research mandate this is a hand-picked kernel/category change, not an intrinsic positivity theorem.

## 4. The multi-source Fisher metric is prime-factorized and has no cross-prime orientation

Give every prime its own source coordinate `u_p`:

\[
Z_P(\sigma,u)
=\prod_{p\in P}
\left(1-e^{a_pu_p}p^{-\sigma}\right)^{-1}.
\tag{19}
\]

Then

\[
\left.
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial u_p\partial u_q}
\log Z_P(\sigma,u)
\right|_{u=0}
=0
\qquad(p\ne q).
\tag{20}
\]

Thus the canonical Fisher/covariance geometry is an orthogonal direct sum of one-prime blocks. It has automatic positivity, but no cross-prime coupling and no oriented translation/autocorrelation structure. This is exactly the kind of local positive geometry that the earlier Weil-positivity obstructions show is insufficient by itself.

The result also survives a generalized-prime control. Replace the rational primes by any multiplicatively independent alphabet `q_j>1`; equations (4)--(20) remain valid with `p` replaced by `q_j`. Hence neither the positivity nor the derivative mismatch is Riemann-specific.

## 5. Criticality does not rescue the Hessian

`WP-259` identifies `beta=1` as the sharp normal Gibbs boundary of the symmetric sector and obtains the desired half-density separately through the operator factor `e^{-H/2}`. A direct Gibbs response has a different exponent bookkeeping:

\[
\left.-\partial_\sigma\log Z(\sigma)\right|_{\sigma}
=\sum_{p,k}(\log p)p^{-k\sigma}.
\tag{21}
\]

At `sigma=1` it carries `Lambda(n)/n`, not `Lambda(n)/sqrt n`. To obtain the half-density coefficient directly from (21), one would evaluate coefficientwise at `sigma=1/2`.

But the global bosonic partition function

\[
Z(\sigma)=\zeta(\sigma)
\tag{22}
\]

is a normal Gibbs partition function only for `sigma>1`; in particular there is no normalized global bosonic Gibbs state at `sigma=1/2`. This already prevents interpreting the target half-density as an ordinary equilibrium Fisher metric.

More importantly, divergence is **not** the decisive obstruction. For every finite `P`, equations (10), (16), and (18) are exact at `sigma=1/2`. Thus finite truncation, a semifinite coefficientwise reading, or later analytic continuation cannot remove the coefficient mismatch without changing the positive form itself.

## 6. Reparametrization and separable archimedean completion do not fix it

An affine temperature reparametrization `sigma=at+b` merely multiplies the Hessian by `a^2`; the forced `k` factor in (10) remains.

A nonlinear reparametrization gives

\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\log Z(\sigma(t))
=(\sigma'(t))^2\,\partial_\sigma^2\log Z
+\sigma''(t)\,\partial_\sigma\log Z.
\tag{23}
\]

The second term can mix the desired first derivative back into the expression, but its sign is controlled by the externally chosen curvature `sigma''(t)`, not by Gibbs covariance. The independent positivity theorem is therefore lost precisely when nonlinear reparametrization is used to cancel the derivative mismatch.

Likewise, let an archimedean sector be separable:

\[
Z_{\rm tot}(\sigma,u)
=Z_{\rm fin}(\sigma,u)Z_\infty(\sigma).
\tag{24}
\]

Then every finite-source derivative satisfies

\[
\partial_{u_p}\partial_{u_q}\log Z_{\rm tot}
=\partial_{u_p}\partial_{u_q}\log Z_{\rm fin}.
\tag{25}
\]

A separate Gamma oscillator/determinant can add an archimedean scalar term, but cannot change the finite prime-power Hessian coefficients or create the missing cross-prime orientation. Any viable repair must therefore couple the finite and archimedean variables **before** the positive form is taken, or use a different geometric sign theorem altogether.

## 7. Prior art and novelty boundary

The bosonic prime gas itself is classical. Bernard Julia's Riemann/primon gas uses one bosonic mode per prime with one-particle energy `log p`, giving `zeta(beta)` as partition function. A modern explicit treatment is J. G. Dueñas and N. F. Svaiter, *Thermodynamics of the Bosonic Randomized Riemann Gas*, Journal of Physics A 48 (2015), 315201, DOI `10.1088/1751-8113/48/31/315201`, arXiv:`1401.8190`. The Bost--Connes system is another classical arithmetic quantum-statistical model with partition function `zeta(beta)`.

The identities `-zeta'/zeta=sum Lambda(n)n^{-s}` and positivity of a log-partition Hessian as a covariance are also standard. No novelty is claimed for the Riemann gas, its mean energy, Bose occupation variance, Fisher information, or the logarithmic derivative of zeta.

The Mathia-specific durable content is the **negative compatibility theorem** obtained by applying those standard facts to the exact `WP-259` escape route: once the permutation-fixed Fock sector is asked to provide its own canonical positive boundary response, the positive Hessian necessarily changes the prime-power coefficient from `log p` to `k(log p)^2` (or, for a general one-body source, from `a_p` to `k a_p^2`). The same calculation simultaneously shows that the multi-source Fisher geometry remains prime-factorized. A bounded prior-art search found the classical primon-gas thermodynamics but no result asserting this specific coefficient-level obstruction to using its canonical covariance geometry as a Weil half-density pairing.

## 8. Consequence for the Weil-positivity mandate

`WP-259` remains a valid improvement at the level of **carrier geometry**: the free cover plus its symmetric sector can canonically align the thermodynamic boundary at `1` while retaining the ambient prime-power selector and the positive diagonal operator `Lambda(n)/sqrt n`.

What `WP-260` rules out is the most immediate attempt to turn that alignment into an independent positive pairing:

\[
\boxed{
\text{symmetric prime-Fock Gibbs/Fisher convexity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{finite Weil Mangoldt half-density pairing}.
}
\tag{26}
\]

The reason is not merely that the completed Gamma term is missing. The finite coefficient list already changes when one passes from the linear arithmetic response to the canonical positive quadratic response.

The remaining Fock-interface question is therefore narrower. A successful construction must use a positive mechanism that is **not equivalent to the separable one-body Gibbs covariance** above and must create nontrivial orientation/cross-prime structure before positivity. A nonseparable finite--archimedean boundary form, relative modular/interface object, or genuine correspondence remains logically open, but it must defeat equations (18), (20), and (25) rather than merely repackage the bosonic partition function or its thermodynamic Hessian.
