## 1. The Gamma Lévy measure canonically defines a shared Hilbert embedding

From `WP-117`, on the Riemann critical line

\[
H_\infty(t)
=
\operatorname{Re}\psi\!\left(\frac14+\frac{it}{2}\right)
-
\psi\!\left(\frac14\right)
\tag{1}
\]

has the symmetric Lévy representation

\[
H_\infty(t)
=
\int_{\mathbb R}(1-\cos ty)\,\nu_\infty(dy),
\qquad
\nu_\infty(dy)
=
\frac{e^{-|y|/2}}{1-e^{-2|y|}}\,dy.
\tag{2}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal H_\infty:=L^2(\mathbb R,\nu_\infty)
\tag{3}
\]

and for real `t` define the canonical increment vector

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(t)(y):=e^{ity}-1.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (2) gives

\[
\|\Phi(t)\|_{\mathcal H_\infty}^2
=
\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{ity}-1|^2\,\nu_\infty(dy)
=
2H_\infty(t).
\tag{5}
\]

Because `nu_infty` is symmetric, the Gram kernel is real and equals

\[
\boxed{
K_\infty(s,t)
:=
\langle\Phi(s),\Phi(t)\rangle
=
H_\infty(s)+H_\infty(t)-H_\infty(s-t).
}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the standard Schoenberg Hilbert realization of a conditionally negative-definite function, here with the exact Prime-Circle-selected Gamma symbol rather than an inserted kernel. Unlike the scalar functional calculus in `WP-117`, (6) already mixes distinct prime frequencies through a single positive Gram form.

## 2. The Riemann Gamma Schoenberg cone is acute on positive frequencies

The crucial extra fact is monotonicity. NIST DLMF §5.7(ii), Eq. 5.7.6 gives, for `a>0`,

\[
\psi(z)
=-\gamma+
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\left(\frac1{n+1}-\frac1{n+z}\right).
\tag{7}
\]

Taking `z=a+iu`, subtracting the value at `a`, and taking real parts yields the exact positive series

\[
\boxed{
h_a(u)
:=
\operatorname{Re}\psi(a+iu)-\psi(a)
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\frac{u^2}{(n+a)((n+a)^2+u^2)}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Every summand is strictly increasing in `u>0`, since with `A=n+a`

\[
\frac{d}{du}
\frac{u^2}{A(A^2+u^2)}
=
\frac{2uA}{(A^2+u^2)^2}>0.
\tag{9}
\]

Hence `h_a(|u|)` is strictly increasing in `|u|`, and in particular

\[
\boxed{
H_\infty(t)\text{ is strictly increasing for }t>0.
}
\tag{10}
\]

For `s,t>0`,

\[
|s-t|\le\max(s,t),
\tag{11}
\]

so (10) and (6) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_\infty(s,t)
&=H_\infty(s)+H_\infty(t)-H_\infty(|s-t|)\\
&\ge
H_\infty(s)+H_\infty(t)-H_\infty(\max(s,t))\\
&=
\boxed{H_\infty(\min(s,t))}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus all nonzero positive-frequency increment vectors lie in an **acute cone** of the Gamma Hilbert space. This is stronger than mere positive semidefiniteness of the Schoenberg kernel: no pair of positive logarithmic frequencies can supply a negative Gram cross term.

The same argument works for every `a>0`; therefore acuteness is a classical special-function consequence rather than a uniquely Riemann arithmetic theorem. The arithmetic content below comes from combining it with the independently selected `a=1/4` channel and the exact same-sign prime moments.

## 3. Exact critical prime moments cannot cancel in the shared Gamma space

Let `mu` be any exact critical positive completion of finite mass `C>0` as in `WP-114`. Its normalized one-prime moments have the fixed common sign

\[
\widehat\eta(e_p)
=-\frac{\log p}{C\sqrt p}.
\tag{13}
\]

A global minus sign is irrelevant to a norm, so put

\[
a_p:=\frac{\log p}{C\sqrt p}>0
\tag{14}
\]

and for a finite prime set `P` form the most direct shared archimedean coupling

\[
\boxed{
V_P
:=
\sum_{p\in P}a_p\,\Phi(\log p)
\in\mathcal H_\infty.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Its positive energy is genuinely nonseparable:

\[
\|V_P\|^2
=
\sum_{p,q\in P}
a_pa_qK_\infty(\log p,\log q).
\tag{16}
\]

By (12), every summand in (16) is nonnegative. In particular the cross-prime terms cannot cancel the diagonal, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|V_P\|^2
&\ge
\sum_{p\in P}a_p^2K_\infty(\log p,\log p)\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{2}{C^2}
\sum_{p\in P}
H_\infty(\log p)\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The right-hand side diverges by `WP-117`/`WP-115`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{P\Subset\mathcal P}\|V_P\|_{\mathcal H_\infty}=+\infty.
}
\tag{18}
\]

This is not just `WP-117` rewritten. There the scalar operator `H_\infty(X)` charges distinct Fourier modes independently and the lower bound is diagonal by construction. Here all prime modes are first embedded into the **same infinite-dimensional archimedean Hilbert space** and only then is a single norm taken; cross-prime interference was available in principle. Equation (12) proves that the canonical Gamma geometry makes that interference constructive rather than destructive.

## 4. The coherent shared coupling has the sharp threshold sigma equals one

The preceding argument can be sharpened with the matched family

\[
a_{p,\sigma}
:=
\frac{\log p}{C p^\sigma},
\qquad \sigma>0,
\tag{19}
\]

and

\[
V_{P,\sigma}
:=
\sum_{p\in P}a_{p,\sigma}\Phi(\log p).
\tag{20}
\]

For a dyadic prime shell

\[
P_X:=\{p:X<p\le2X\},
\tag{21}
\]

(12) gives uniformly for `p,q in P_X`

\[
K_\infty(\log p,\log q)
\ge H_\infty(\log X).
\tag{22}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|V_{P_X,\sigma}\|^2
\ge
H_\infty(\log X)
\left(
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\frac{\log p}{C p^\sigma}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{23}
\]

By the prime number theorem in Chebyshev form,

\[
\vartheta(2X)-\vartheta(X)\asymp X,
\tag{24}
\]

so

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{\log p}{p^\sigma}
\ge
(2X)^{-\sigma}\bigl(\vartheta(2X)-\vartheta(X)\bigr)
\gg X^{1-\sigma}.
\tag{25}
\]

Also `WP-117` gives

\[
H_\infty(\log X)=\log\log X+O(1).
\tag{26}
\]

Therefore the shell norm in (23) tends to infinity for every `sigma<=1`: polynomially times `log log X` for `sigma<1`, and at least like `log log X` when `sigma=1`. Since all coefficients and all cross Gram entries are nonnegative, adding earlier prime shells cannot reduce the norm. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sigma\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_{P,\sigma}\text{ has no finite Hilbert-space limit under prime exhaustion.}
}
\tag{27}
\]

Conversely, for `sigma>1`, the triangle inequality and (5) give

\[
\sum_p
\|a_{p,\sigma}\Phi(\log p)\|
=
\frac1C
\sum_p
\frac{\log p}{p^\sigma}
\sqrt{2H_\infty(\log p)}.
\tag{28}
\]

Using `H_\infty(\log p)=O(1+\log\log p)`, the series in (28) is dominated by the corresponding integer series and converges for every `sigma>1`. Hence the vector series converges absolutely in `mathcal H_infty`:

\[
\boxed{
V_\sigma:=
\sum_p a_{p,\sigma}\Phi(\log p)
\in\mathcal H_\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma>1.
}
\tag{29}
\]

For this canonical shared coupling, `sigma=1` is therefore the sharp summability boundary. This is strictly more restrictive than the scalar Gamma energy in `WP-117`, for which the one-prime diagonal series is already finite for every `sigma>1/2`. Coherent use of a common archimedean Hilbert space does not repair the critical endpoint; it creates positive cross-prime reinforcement that pushes the boundary outward.

## 5. What this rules out — and what it deliberately leaves open

The excluded architecture is precise. Start from the intrinsic Prime-Circle Riemann Gamma Lévy measure of `WP-117`; use its canonical Schoenberg increment vectors `Phi(log p)`; insert the exact one-prime Weil amplitudes linearly; let all primes share the same archimedean Hilbert space; and take the inherited positive Gram norm. This is a natural realization of the surviving phrase “couple finite and infinite places nonseparably before positivity,” and (18)–(29) show that it cannot be the desired global Weil geometry.

The theorem does **not** say that every nonseparable finite–archimedean coupling diverges. In particular, a geometry that canonically creates prime-dependent phases or signs before the Gram pairing can invalidate the termwise positivity in (16). Likewise a quotient/compression may change the vectors before the norm; a matrix- or graded-valued pairing may not reduce to the scalar Schoenberg kernel; and a boundary/cohomological construction may identify a smaller primitive sector. Those remain live only if their sign theorem and normalization arise independently rather than being chosen to cancel the known divergence.

There is a useful adversarial control here. If arbitrary phases are manually attached to the coefficients, cross terms in (16) need not remain positive after multiplication by the coefficient products. Therefore the obstruction is not a tautology about every linear combination in a positive Hilbert space. It uses two exact features simultaneously: the **acute Gamma Schoenberg kernel on positive log frequencies** and the **common arithmetic sign of the one-prime Weil moments**. A successful phase-twisted escape would consequently need Mathia-native geometry that forces those phases before the Weil target is consulted.

`WP-099` is also distinct: it rules out passive Schur/shorting elimination by the order relation `0<=S<=R_C`, while the construction here performs no minimization and allows an infinite-dimensional shared auxiliary space with cross-prime terms before the norm. `WP-115` is distinct because it treats scalar CND spectral costs; the present result tests the canonical shared Hilbert realization left open by that scalar no-go.

Accordingly the durable content here is a **Mathia-specific obstruction**: the most canonical shared Hilbert coupling supplied by the newly exposed Prime-Circle Gamma positivity is too coherent to regularize the exact finite-prime amplitudes.
