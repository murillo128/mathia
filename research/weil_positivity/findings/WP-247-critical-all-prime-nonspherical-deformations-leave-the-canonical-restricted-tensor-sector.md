# WP-247 — Critical all-prime nonspherical deformations leave the canonical restricted-tensor sector

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + AUTOMORPHIC-RESTRICTED-PRODUCT + INFINITE-TENSOR-SECTOR + ORTHOGONAL-ONE-PRIME-CHANNELS + CRITICAL-HALF-DENSITY-BOUNDARY + STRONG-LIMIT-FAILURE + DECISIVE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

`WP-246` leaves an explicit category-level escape from its fixed-level obstruction: abandon a fixed finite exceptional set and let genuinely nonspherical local data occur at unboundedly many primes, hoping that the scalar Riemann Euler tail can then enter a noncentral operator channel before positivity is taken. The most direct realization of that escape is to stay inside the ordinary Hilbert completion of the automorphic restricted tensor product and approximate an all-prime nonspherical state or product unitary by larger and larger finite-level perturbations.

That direct realization fails exactly at the critical half-density scale. Relative to the distinguished spherical tensor, the one-prime nonspherical sectors are mutually orthogonal. Hence every vector in the canonical completed restricted tensor product has square-summable one-prime amplitudes. More sharply, a product deformation with local amplitudes `a_p` is a norm limit of its finite-prime truncations exactly when `sum_p |a_p|^2 < infinity`. Therefore neither the critical half-density amplitudes `a_p=p^{-1/2}` nor the exact first Mangoldt/Weil amplitudes `a_p=(log p)/sqrt(p)` define an all-prime nonspherical deformation in that canonical Hilbert sector. The corresponding partial product unitaries also have no strong limit there.

This does **not** say that infinite tensor products cannot contain such a product vector. Von Neumann's complete infinite tensor product has different incomplete sectors, and one may choose a new reference sequence. The obstruction is that the critical deformation is not in the **same spherical-reference sector** used by the automorphic restricted product. Moving to a different sector is therefore a genuine representation/category change, not the limit of the fixed-level escape left open by `WP-246`; it must independently recover a canonical automorphic/scattering comparison and an independent positive theorem.

## 1. Canonical Hilbert completion and one-prime excitations

Let

\[
\mathcal H_\Omega
=
\widehat{\bigotimes}'_{p}(\mathcal H_p,\Omega_p)
\tag{1}
\]

be the Hilbert completion of a restricted tensor product with distinguished unit spherical vectors `Omega_p`. Algebraically it is generated by pure tensors equal to `Omega_p` at all but finitely many primes; this is the standard Hilbert completion underlying the unitary restricted-product form of Flath's automorphic factorization.

Whenever a local nonspherical direction exists, choose a unit vector

\[
\eta_p\perp\Omega_p.
\tag{2}
\]

Define the normalized one-prime excitation

\[
e_p
=
\eta_p\otimes\bigotimes_{q\ne p}\Omega_q.
\tag{3}
\]

Then for distinct primes `p != q`,

\[
\langle e_p,e_q\rangle=0,
\qquad
\|e_p\|=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus `(e_p)_p` is an orthonormal family in `H_Omega`. By Bessel's inequality, every `Psi in H_Omega` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p |\langle e_p,\Psi\rangle|^2
\le \|\Psi\|^2.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) already blocks a broad direct encoding: no vector of the canonical automorphic restricted-product Hilbert sector can have cofinite one-prime coordinates proportional to either

\[
\frac1{\sqrt p}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p},
\tag{6}
\]

because Euler's `sum_p 1/p=infinity` gives

\[
\sum_p\frac1p=\infty,
\qquad
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}=\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

The second divergence is the same exact critical mass already isolated for a different purpose in `WP-032`. Here it is not used as a nonclosability criterion for a rank-one Gram functional. It says instead that the desired all-prime arithmetic cannot occur as the primal coordinates of a Hilbert vector in the orthogonal one-prime nonspherical channels.

## 2. Product deformations have an exact square-summability criterion

The natural infinite-level limit can be tested without any representation-theoretic approximation. For numbers `a_p` with `|a_p|<1`, set

\[
\psi_p(a_p)
=
\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}\,\Omega_p+a_p\eta_p.
\tag{8}
\]

For a finite set of primes `S`, define the legitimate finite-level vector

\[
\Psi_S(a)
=
\bigotimes_{p\in S}\psi_p(a_p)
\otimes
\bigotimes_{p\notin S}\Omega_p.
\tag{9}
\]

If `S subset T`, then

\[
\langle\Psi_S(a),\Psi_T(a)\rangle
=
\prod_{p\in T\setminus S}\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2},
\tag{10}
\]

and therefore

\[
\|\Psi_T(a)-\Psi_S(a)\|^2
=
2\left(
1-
\prod_{p\in T\setminus S}\sqrt{1-|a_p|^2}
\right).
\tag{11}
\]

For `0<=x<1`,

\[
1-\sqrt{1-x}
=
\frac{x}{1+\sqrt{1-x}},
\qquad
\frac{x}{2}\le 1-\sqrt{1-x}\le x.
\tag{12}
\]

Hence the tail product in (10) tends uniformly to `1` exactly when

\[
\sum_p |a_p|^2<\infty.
\tag{13}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
(\Psi_S(a))_S\text{ converges in }\mathcal H_\Omega
\iff
(a_p)_p\in\ell^2(\mathbb P).
}
\tag{14}
\]

This is the elementary restricted-tensor version of von Neumann's strong-equivalence criterion for incomplete infinite tensor products. It also makes the failure directly auditable from finite levels: if (13) diverges, then after every finite `S` one can enlarge to a finite `T` whose tail product in (10) is bounded away from `1`, so the net is not Cauchy.

## 3. The critical half-density is exactly the sector boundary

First take the scale-only family

\[
a_p(\sigma)=p^{-\sigma}.
\tag{15}
\]

Then (14) becomes

\[
\sum_p p^{-2\sigma}<\infty.
\tag{16}
\]

The prime Dirichlet series converges for `2 sigma>1` and diverges at `2 sigma<=1`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
a_p(\sigma)=p^{-\sigma}
\text{ lies in the spherical restricted-tensor sector exactly for }
\sigma>\frac12.
}
\tag{17}
\]

At the Weil half-density `sigma=1/2`, the obstruction is Euler's harmonic-prime divergence.

The exact first Mangoldt amplitude gives the same boundary. Put

\[
b_p(\sigma)=(\log p)p^{-\sigma}.
\tag{18}
\]

At `sigma=1/2`, these are legitimate local rotation amplitudes because the continuous function `log x/sqrt(x)` has maximum `2/e<1`. But

\[
\sum_p |b_p(1/2)|^2
=
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}
=\infty.
\tag{19}
\]

More generally, exactly as in `WP-032`, `sum_p (log p)^2 p^{-2 sigma}` converges precisely for `sigma>1/2`. Thus both the bare half-density and the Mangoldt-weighted half-density hit the same sharp Hilbert-sector wall.

This is an important matched control. Infinite support itself is not the problem: every `sigma>1/2` product deformation in (15) has a well-defined limit in the same spherical sector. The failure occurs at the exact critical exponent required by the Riemann/Weil normalization.

## 4. The infinitesimal and unitary versions fail for the same reason

One might try to avoid the fixed product vector and instead define the infinite-level object as a differentiable deformation of the spherical vacuum. Let

\[
\psi_p(t)
=
\sqrt{1-t^2|a_p|^2}\,\Omega_p+t a_p\eta_p
\tag{20}
\]

for finite support first. Differentiating the finite product at `t=0` gives

\[
\dot\Psi_S(0)
=
\sum_{p\in S}a_p e_p,
\qquad
\|\dot\Psi_S(0)\|^2
=
\sum_{p\in S}|a_p|^2.
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore a differentiable path in `H_Omega` whose local first-order nonspherical amplitudes are the prescribed `a_p` can exist only if `a in ell^2(P)`. This is also immediate from (5), because its derivative vector would have one-prime coordinates `a_p`.

Likewise choose local two-plane rotations `U_p(t)` with

\[
U_p(t)\Omega_p=\psi_p(t).
\tag{22}
\]

The finite product unitaries `U_S(t)` are perfectly well defined. But if `a notin ell^2(P)`, then for any fixed nonzero sufficiently small `t`,

\[
U_S(t)\Omega=\Psi_S(ta)
\tag{23}
\]

is not Cauchy by (14). Hence the partial product unitaries have no strong limit on the spherical vacuum and therefore no strong operator limit on `H_Omega` implementing that critical all-prime rotation.

So the obvious passage

\[
\text{finite nonspherical levels}
\longrightarrow
\text{all-prime product state/unitary}
\longrightarrow
\text{noncentral critical arithmetic}
\tag{24}
\]

already fails at the first arrow inside the canonical automorphic Hilbert representation.

## 5. Adversarial controls and what the result does not prove

The obstruction is deliberately narrow enough to survive the obvious counterexamples.

**Changing incomplete sector.** A unit sequence `(psi_p(a_p))_p` still defines a product vector in the complete von Neumann infinite tensor-product formalism, but when (13) diverges it is not strongly equivalent to the spherical reference sequence. Equivalently, one can declare the `psi_p` themselves to be new reference vectors and build another incomplete tensor-product sector. That is mathematically legitimate, but it is no longer a vector obtained by completing the standard automorphic restricted tensor product around `(Omega_p)_p`. The candidate must newly explain why this sector is canonical, how ordinary automorphic/scattering operators compare with it, and where an independent positive form comes from.

**Correlated vectors.** Equation (14) concerns product deformations, but (5) is stronger: even a highly entangled/correlated vector in `H_Omega` cannot have a non-square-summable sequence as its direct coefficients on an orthonormal family of one-prime excitation channels. Correlation can evade the finding only if the Riemann arithmetic is stored in a genuinely different collective observable or nonorthogonal/global channel rather than those direct local coordinates.

**Smaller amplitudes with larger readout.** The finding does not rule out encoding an `ell^2` sequence of local perturbations and recovering `log p/sqrt(p)` through a source-forced prime-dependent unbounded observable. Such a mechanism must prove its domain, convergence and sign independently; choosing the gain after observing the desired Weil coefficient is merely a reparameterization.

**Non-Hilbert or renormalized limits.** Distributional, rigged-Hilbert, type-III, continuous-field, renormalized, or other infinite-level constructions are not excluded. They are precisely category changes for which ordinary restricted-product norm convergence and Bessel's inequality are no longer the complete story. They also do not inherit a Weil sign theorem automatically.

**The scalar normalizer may remain scalar anyway.** Even if one constructs another sector, this result does not prove that the global `L`-normalizer becomes noncentral there. `WP-246` shows only the fixed-level scalarization; a new infinite-level theory must derive the desired operator-valued coupling rather than assume that infinitely many local channels create it.

These controls prevent the claim from becoming the false statement that "all infinite nonspherical support is impossible". Square-summable all-prime deformations are present in the ordinary completion; the exact obstruction is the critical non-square-summable scale in the direct local channel.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The ingredients are classical.

- J. von Neumann, *On infinite direct products*, Compositio Mathematica **6** (1939), 1–77, is the foundational source for complete and incomplete infinite tensor products and the equivalence classes of reference sequences. The strong-equivalence condition is the classical home of the convergence test derived directly in (10)--(14).
- D. Flath, *Decomposition of representations into tensor products*, in *Automorphic Forms, Representations and L-functions*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. **33**, Part 1 (1979), 179–183, DOI `10.1090/pspum/033.1/546596`, is the automorphic restricted-product anchor already used by `WP-246`.
- Andrew Lesniewski, *A complete ultrametric on von Neumann's incomplete tensor products*, arXiv:`2607.09627` (2026), is a recent explicit restatement of the sector classification in terms of convergence of `sum_j |<phi_j,psi_j>-1|`; it is useful here only as a modern terminology check, not as evidence for the Mathia-specific critical-weight synthesis.

A bounded search around automorphic restricted tensor products, incomplete infinite tensor products, all-prime nonspherical data, Riemann/Weil positivity, and critical `p^{-1/2}` tensor deformations recovered the classical restricted-product and infinite-tensor machinery but did not locate a source asserting the specific line-level obstruction above. That absence is not a completeness claim. The theorem ingredients -- Bessel, convergence of product vectors, Euler's divergence, and automorphic restricted products -- are all standard. The substantive content of `WP-247` is their application as a **sharp matched control on the precise infinite-level escape left open by `WP-246`**, not a claim of a new infinite-tensor theorem.

## 7. Consequence for Weil Positivity

The direct infinite-level repair of `WP-246` is now sharply narrower. It is not enough to let the exceptional set grow and hope that the canonical finite-level nonspherical vectors converge to an all-prime critical operator state. At the exact half-density needed for Weil arithmetic, they do not converge in the spherical-reference restricted tensor product, and their infinitesimal one-prime amplitudes are not the coordinates of any vector in that Hilbert space.

A surviving route must therefore do at least one genuinely new thing before positivity is read out: construct a **canonical different infinite-tensor sector** together with a source-defined comparison to automorphic scattering; encode the arithmetic in a **collective correlated operator** rather than direct one-prime Hilbert amplitudes; derive a controlled **unbounded local-to-global readout** from smaller square-summable perturbations; or leave ordinary Hilbert restricted-product geometry for a renormalized/boundary category with its own well-posed positive form. In every case it must still solve the harder problem untouched here: producing the finite-prime, Gamma and polar pieces with the exact Weil orientation from one independently positive global structure.
