# WP-059 — Prym theta adjoint defect is a universal cycle-Laplacian log-determinant

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLUE-RESOLUTION + CLASSICALIZED-LOCAL-BRIDGE + DECISIVE-BOUNDARY + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT` for `CLUE-prym-torsion-action-before-positivity`. `WP-056` found `log p` in the integral discriminant of the old-prime Prym polarization, `WP-057` showed that the finite torsion carrier itself cannot support a nonzero ordered real quadratic form, and `WP-058` exported the same scale to a positive determinant-line norm while showing that the canonical real polarization operator has flat spectrum. The strongest canonical action escape suggested by the clue can now be computed exactly.

Let

\[
K=\ker\lambda_{n,p}\cong (\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^{2g_n}
\]

for an old-prime degree-`p` Prym with `g_n>0`, and let `V` be the irreducible weight-one theta/Heisenberg representation. Then `dim V=p^{g_n}`. Although the projective action of an element `x in K` on `V` is defined only up to phase, conjugation on

\[
\mathcal E=\operatorname{End}(V)
\]

is phase-independent. For every nonzero `x`, define the canonical unitary conjugation operator

\[
A_x(T)=W_xTW_x^{-1}
\]

and its positive defect

\[
D_x=(I-A_x)^*(I-A_x)\succeq0.
\]

The adjoint `K`-representation on `End(V)` is exactly the regular representation of `K`. Consequently, for every nonzero `x`, each `p`-th root of unity occurs as an eigenvalue of `A_x` with multiplicity

\[
m=|K|/p=p^{2g_n-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Spec}(D_x)
=\left\{4\sin^2\frac{\pi j}{p}:0\le j<p\right\}
\]

with that same multiplicity, and the nonzero spectral determinant satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\det{}'D_x=p^{2m},
\qquad
\frac{1}{2m}\log\det{}'D_x=\log p.
}
\]

Thus the clue was not vacuous: **the Prym torsion can act on a genuine non-torsion Hilbert space before positivity is formed, and a canonical phase-free positive relative operator then retains `log p` without first inserting `log|K|` by hand.**

But the calculation also classicalizes the mechanism. Restricted to the cyclic subgroup generated by `x`, `D_x` is precisely `m` copies of the ordinary `p`-cycle graph Laplacian. The displayed logarithm is just the classical product

\[
\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}|1-\zeta_p^j|^2=p^2,
\]

the same positive cycle-log-determinant mechanism already isolated in `WP-043`. The quadratic energy itself does not equal `log p`: its normalized trace is exactly `2`, and the fully symmetry-averaged defect is a scalar on the traceless endomorphisms. Moreover a direct degree-`p^k` polarization gives a `p^k`-cycle and normalized log-pseudodeterminant `k log p`, not `Lambda(p^k)=log p`, unless the one-step filtration is retained separately. Arbitrary matched polarized varieties with the same finite symplectic kernel give exactly the same response.

The durable boundary is therefore sharper than `WP-057`/`WP-058`: **theta/Heisenberg action before positivity does escape torsion-annihilation, but its canonical adjoint defect reduces to universal cyclic Laplacian spectral geometry. It exports the prime only through a logarithmic determinant and does not produce a linear Weil quadratic pairing, the prime-power Mangoldt normalization intrinsically, or any archimedean/polar completion.** Any surviving action-based Prym route must use a target or coupling not equivalent to this regular-adjoint/cycle mechanism and must acquire global finite--archimedean sign before a determinant readout.

## 1. Setup: the Prym theta group and its non-torsion representation

Keep the old-prime Prym setup of `WP-056`--`WP-058`. For

Let `P=P_{n,p}` be the Prym, let `L` be an ample line bundle inducing its restricted polarization, and put

\[

By `WP-056`, the polarization type has `p` as elementary divisor exactly `g` times, so over characteristic zero

\[
\boxed{
K\cong(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^{2g},
\qquad |K|=p^{2g}.

\tag{1}

The theta group of `L` is a central extension

\[
1\longrightarrow\mathbf G_m
\longrightarrow\mathcal G(L)
\longrightarrow K
\longrightarrow1,
\tag{2}
\]

whose commutator is the nondegenerate alternating pairing

\[
e_L:K\times K\longrightarrow\mu_p.

The standard weight-one theta/Heisenberg representation is irreducible. Write it as

\[

The classical theta-group dimension formula already anchored for `WP-057` gives

\[
\boxed{

Because the relevant finite Heisenberg quotient is finite, `V` has an invariant Hermitian metric, unique up to a positive scalar by irreducibility. A global rescaling of that metric does not change adjoints of endomorphisms or the Hilbert--Schmidt geometry on `End(V)`, so the constructions below do not require a normalization choice.

## 2. Conjugation removes the theta-lift phase ambiguity exactly

For `x in K`, choose any theta-group lift and denote its action on `V` by `W_x`. A different lift multiplies `W_x` by a nonzero central scalar. Therefore the operator on

\[
\mathcal E:=\operatorname{End}(V)
\]

defined by

\[
\boxed{
A_x(T):=W_xTW_x^{-1}
}
\tag{5}
\]

is independent of the lift.

With the invariant Hermitian structure, `A_x` is unitary for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product on `E`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
D_x:=(I-A_x)^*(I-A_x)
=2I-A_x-A_x^*\succeq0
}
\tag{6}
\]

is a canonical positive operator attached to the torsion action and the chosen `x`, not a positive scalar manufactured from `log p`.

This is precisely the category escape left open by `WP-057`: `D_x` acts on the non-torsion complex vector space `End(V)`, not on the finite group `K` itself. Neron--Tate annihilation of torsion therefore does not apply.

The phase cancellation in (5) is also important for the canonicality audit. The more obvious defect `(I-W_x)^*(I-W_x)` depends on the central phase chosen for a lift of `x`; its spectrum is not an invariant of the projective theta action. Passing to conjugation removes that defect exactly rather than by a convention.

## 3. The adjoint `K`-representation is exactly regular

The spectrum of (6) can be obtained without choosing a Schrödinger model.

For `x ne 0`, nondegeneracy of `e_L` gives a `y in K` with

\[
e_L(y,x)\ne1.
\tag{7}
\]

Theta commutation implies, up to the harmless convention of inverting the pairing,

\[
W_yW_xW_y^{-1}=e_L(y,x)W_x.
\tag{8}
\]

Taking traces and using (7),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}_V(W_x)=0
\qquad(x\ne0).
}
\tag{9}
\]

The character of conjugation on `End(V)` is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal E}(A_x)
=\operatorname{Tr}(W_x)\operatorname{Tr}(W_x^{-1}).
\tag{10}
\]

Hence, by (4) and (9),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal E}(A_x)
=\begin{cases}
|K|,&x=0,\\
0,&x\ne0.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]

But (11) is exactly the character of the regular representation of the finite abelian group `K`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal E,A)\cong\mathbf C[K]
}
\tag{12}
\]

as a `K`-representation.

This identifies the proposed Prym action invariant completely. No zeta function, zero data, analytic continuation, or chosen basis has entered.

## 4. A nonzero torsion direction gives a repeated `p`-cycle Laplacian

Fix `x ne 0`. Since `K` is elementary `p`-torsion, `x` has order `p`. The perfect pairing identifies the characters of `K` with elements of `K`; on the regular decomposition (12), `A_x` therefore has eigenvalues

\[

The linear functional on `K`

\[

\[
\boxed{
m=|K|/p=p^{2g-1}.}
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently each `p`-th root occurs with multiplicity `m`. Equation (6) then gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(D_x)
=\left\{
|1-\zeta_p^j|^2
=4\sin^2\frac{\pi j}{p}
:0\le j<p

\tag{14}

again with multiplicity `m`.

Equivalently, after restricting the regular representation (12) to the cyclic subgroup `<x>`,

\[
\mathcal E\cong
\left(\mathbf C[\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z]\right)^{\oplus m},
\tag{15}
\]

and `D_x` is `m` copies of

\[
(I-U_p)^*(I-U_p),
\tag{16}
\]

where `U_p` is the cyclic shift on `p` vertices. Thus the positive operator produced by theta conjugation is literally a repeated cycle graph Laplacian.

## 5. Its positive pseudodeterminant exports exactly `log p`

The kernel of `D_x` is the `A_x`-fixed subspace, corresponding to `j=0`, and has dimension `m`. On its orthogonal complement, (14) is strictly positive.

Using the elementary cyclotomic product

\[
\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta_p^j)=p,
\tag{17}
\]

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det{}'D_x
&=
\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}|1-\zeta_p^j|^{2m}\\
&=
\left|
\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta_p^j)
\right|^{2m}\\
&=p^{2m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2m}\log\det{}'D_x=\log p.
}
\tag{19}
\]

The formula includes `p=2`: then the only nonzero cycle eigenvalue is `4`, with multiplicity `2^{2g-1}`, and (19) still gives `log 2`.

This is a positive answer to the narrow existence question posed by the clue. Unlike `WP-056`, equation (19) does not begin by counting `|K|` and taking its logarithm. Unlike `WP-058`, it is not the covolume of the original polarization lattice. The torsion first acts on a non-torsion Hilbert space, positivity is formed as the squared norm

\[
\langle T,D_xT\rangle_{HS}
=\|T-A_xT\|_{HS}^2\ge0,
\tag{20}

This is the same category distinction seen in `WP-043` and `WP-058`: **a positive operator may contain the prime in its full spectrum or integral structure while its natural linear quadratic readouts do not equal the Mangoldt coefficient.**

## 8. Full theta symmetry flattens the positive defect

There is a second, independent canonicality test. A polarized Prym does not distinguish one nonzero element of its symplectic kernel. Indeed the symplectic automorphism group acts transitively on nonzero vectors of the elementary symplectic space `K`.

Average the phase-free defects over all torsion directions:

\[
\overline D
:=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{x\in K}D_x.
\tag{23}
\]

Using (6),

\[
\overline D
=2I-rac1{|K|}\sum_xA_x
-rac1{|K|}\sum_xA_x^*.
\tag{24}
\]

For the regular representation, the group average

\[
P_{inv}:=\frac1{|K|}\sum_xA_x
\tag{25}
\]

is the orthogonal projection onto the invariant subspace. By irreducibility of the original theta representation, the `K`-invariant endomorphisms are exactly the scalars, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}P_{inv}=1.
\tag{26}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\overline D=2(I-P_{inv}).
}
\tag{27}
\]

On the traceless endomorphisms, the fully symmetric averaged positive energy is exactly `2I`. It has no `p`-dependent spectral scale at all; `p` remains only in the dimension of the ambient space.

More generally, any linear average of the one-direction defects whose weights are invariant under all symplectic automorphisms has one common weight on `K\setminus\{0\}`. Since `D_0=0`, it is therefore a scalar multiple of (27). So the most obvious way to remove the arbitrary choice of a torsion direction also removes the nontrivial `p`-dependent cycle spectrum.

There is no contradiction with (19): the **common scalar pseudodeterminant response** of any chosen nonzero direction is itself symplectically invariant. What fails is promotion of that scalar to a distinguished global positive quadratic operator without either choosing a direction or collapsing to the symmetry average.

\[
\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}(1-\zeta_q^j)=q.

\boxed{

The desired Mangoldt normalization can be recovered only by retaining the tower one `p`-step at a time, or by taking a primitive/exact-order spectral quotient. That is exactly the filtration/shell distinction already encountered in `WP-043`, `WP-056`, and `WP-058`; it is not forced by positivity of the direct theta defect.

Thus the action escape does not solve the repeated-prime problem. It reproduces the same rule: whole-cycle determinant measures total degree, while Mangoldt needs primitive-step information.
