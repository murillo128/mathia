# WP-265 — Sharp and Gibbs minimal subtractions give opposite critical-incidence inertia

## Claim

The finite-part ambiguity in `WP-264` is not merely a formal freedom in an abstract counterterm. Two standard regulator families generated by the same Mathia source Hamiltonian land on opposite sides of the `C=0` inertia boundary when each subtracts only its leading logarithmic divergence.

For the hard Hamiltonian cutoff `q<=X`, Mertens' second theorem gives

\[
\sum_{q\le X}\frac1q
=\log\log X+B_1+o(1),
\qquad
B_1=0.2614972128\ldots>0.
\]

Consequently the minimally subtracted Gram converges to `R_{B_1}` from `WP-264`, which has **infinite positive index**.

For the Gibbs/Abel regulator obtained by replacing the partner half-density `q^{-1/2}` with `q^{-(1+\varepsilon)/2}`, the divergent scalar is the prime zeta function

\[
P(1+\varepsilon)=\sum_q q^{-1-\varepsilon}
=\log(1/\varepsilon)+(B_1-\gamma)+o(1),
\]

where `gamma` is Euler's constant and

\[
B_1-\gamma=-0.3157184520\ldots<0.
\]

Subtracting only `log(1/epsilon) D_W` therefore gives `R_{B_1-\gamma}`, which has **exactly one positive direction** by `WP-264`.

Thus two natural source-compatible regularizations of the same critical complete incidence produce qualitatively different sign theorems. Making them agree at `R_0` requires inserting regulator-specific finite constants: `B_1` for the sharp cutoff and `B_1-gamma` for the Gibbs regulator. Their difference is exactly `gamma`. The Mathia Hamiltonian and the local positivity construction therefore do not yet determine a regulator-independent Hodge/Weil form.

**Classification:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`.

## Derivation

Retain the critical prime-layer quantities of `WP-264`,

\[
w_p=\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p},
\qquad
D_W=\operatorname{diag}(w_p),
\qquad
d_p=\frac{w_p}{p},
\qquad
a_p=\sqrt{d_p},
\]

and

\[
R=aa^*-2D,
\qquad
R_C=R+CD_W.
\]

### Hard spectral cutoff

Let `P_X` denote projection onto primes `p<=X`. The exact cutoff Gram is

\[
M_X=H_XP_XD_W+a_Xa_X^*-2D_X,
\qquad
H_X=\sum_{q\le X}\frac1q.
\tag{1}
\]

Subtract only the universal leading divergence on the active spectral subspace:

\[
\widehat R_X^{\rm sharp}
:=M_X-(\log\log X)P_XD_W.
\tag{2}
\]

Mertens' second theorem gives `H_X-log log X -> B_1`. Also `P_XD_W -> D_W` in operator norm because `w_p -> 0`, while `a_Xa_X^* -> aa^*` and `D_X -> D` in trace norm. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R_X^{\rm sharp}
\longrightarrow
R+B_1D_W
=R_{B_1}
}
\tag{3}
\]

in operator norm. Since `B_1>0`, equation (7) of `WP-264` implies

\[
\boxed{n_+(R_{B_1})=\infty.}
\tag{4}
\]

This is already enough to show that the attractive index-one finite part of `WP-264` is not preserved by the most literal sharp-cutoff minimal subtraction.

### Gibbs/Abel regulator

Now keep all partner primes but apply an additional positive source-Hamiltonian damping. For `epsilon>0`, define

\[
U_\varepsilon e_p
:=
\sqrt{w_p}
\sum_{q\ne p}q^{-(1+\varepsilon)/2}e_{pq}.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the critical half-density multiplied by `e^{-epsilon H/2}` for `H e_q=(log q)e_q`. Set

\[
P(1+\varepsilon)=\sum_q q^{-1-\varepsilon},
\qquad
d_p^{(\varepsilon)}=w_pp^{-1-\varepsilon},
\qquad
a_p^{(\varepsilon)}=\sqrt{d_p^{(\varepsilon)}}.
\]

The same unique-factorization calculation as in `WP-264` gives the exact full-prime Gram

\[
M_\varepsilon
:=U_\varepsilon^*U_\varepsilon
=P(1+\varepsilon)D_W
+a_\varepsilon a_\varepsilon^*
-2D_\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
\]

As `epsilon downarrow 0`, dominated convergence gives `a_epsilon -> a` in `ell^2` and `D_epsilon -> D` in trace norm. The classical prime-zeta expansion at its logarithmic singularity is

\[
P(1+\varepsilon)
=\log(1/\varepsilon)+\alpha_0+o(1),
\qquad
\alpha_0=B_1-\gamma.
\tag{7}
\]

Therefore the corresponding leading-term subtraction satisfies

\[
\boxed{
M_\varepsilon-\log(1/\varepsilon)D_W
\longrightarrow
R+(B_1-\gamma)D_W
=R_{B_1-\gamma}
}
\tag{8}
\]

in operator norm. Since `B_1-gamma<0`, `WP-264` yields

\[
\boxed{n_+(R_{B_1-\gamma})=1.}
\tag{9}
\]

Equations (4) and (9) are the matched-control result: the regulator changes not merely a numerical finite part but the qualitative inertia class.

## Why this is stronger than WP-264

`WP-264` proved that an abstract finite scalar `C` is unfixed and that the sign changes at `C=0`. The present finding shows that **both sides of that boundary are actually realized by natural regulators already available from the source construction itself**. No hand-picked kernel or zero data is needed to obtain either outcome.

The sharp cutoff is the spectral projection of the logarithmic Hamiltonian; the Gibbs regulator is its heat/Gibbs damping. If one demands that both define the same finite operator, then one must go beyond subtraction of the divergent singularity and specify the scheme-dependent finite constants

\[
c_X^{\rm sharp}=\log\log X+B_1,
\qquad
c_\varepsilon^{\rm Gibbs}=\log(1/\varepsilon)+(B_1-\gamma).
\tag{10}
\]

Both choices then converge to `R_0`, but equation (10) is an additional finite-part prescription, not a consequence of positivity: the two constants differ by `gamma`, while every `C<=0` already has the same index-one theorem. This realizes the canonicity defect of `WP-264` as a regulator comparison rather than a hypothetical counterterm freedom.

## Aggressive falsification and controls

The comparison does **not** say that sharp cutoff and Gibbs damping must be physically or geometrically equivalent. A future global construction may prefer one because of an independently proved boundary theorem, functoriality, cohomological normalization, or an archimedean matching law. That would be a genuine escape. The point is narrower: the current Mathia source Hamiltonian plus local incidence positivity contains no such selector.

Nor does the result prohibit adding the full finite constants in (10). Doing so is mathematically legitimate and makes both regulators converge to `R_0`; it simply makes explicit that the finite normalization has been supplied in addition to the leading divergence. Selecting those constants because they restore the desired index or coefficient would be target-driven regularization and fails the branch canonicity test.

The result is also independent of RH and of zero data. It uses only the prime reciprocal asymptotic, the prime-zeta expansion at `s=1`, and the exact incidence algebra of `WP-264`.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The scalar asymptotics are classical. Mertens' second theorem gives

\[
\sum_{p\le X}p^{-1}=\log\log X+B_1+o(1).
\]

Franz Mertens proved the relevant prime reciprocal theorem in *Ein Beitrag zur analytischen Zahlentheorie*, J. reine angew. Math. 78 (1874), 46--62, DOI `10.1515/crll.1874.78.46`.

The prime-zeta function and its logarithmic singularity are likewise classical; C.-E. Froberg, *On the prime zeta function*, BIT 8 (1968), 187--202, DOI `10.1007/BF01933420`, is a standard reference. A recent explicit coefficient treatment is Artur Kawalec, *On the series expansion of the prime zeta function about s=1 and its coefficients*, arXiv:`2603.21535` (2026), whose zeroth coefficient is exactly `alpha_0=B_1-gamma`.

Abelian/Gibbs versus sharp-cutoff finite-part differences and regulator dependence are standard analytic phenomena. No novelty is claimed for the constants or for renormalization-scheme dependence in general. The Mathia-specific derived statement is the exact placement of these two standard source regulators inside the `WP-264` one-parameter operator family and the resulting **opposite inertia classifications**. The literature search found the classical scalar ingredients and general renormalization phenomenon, but no prior result asserting this particular critical Fock-incidence/Hodge-index comparison.

## Falsification target

The obstruction is defeated by a Mathia-native global theorem that selects a finite subtraction **before** comparison with the Weil target and makes that selection stable under the hard/Gibbs matched control, or explains rigorously why only one regulator is geometrically admissible. The same theorem must preserve an independent sign statement and still generate the finite-prime orientation together with the archimedean Gamma and polar terms.

A weaker but still meaningful escape would be a regulator-covariant boundary object whose transformation law accounts for the `gamma` shift and whose completed finite-plus-archimedean form is invariant even though the finite block alone is not. That would move the missing structure from an arbitrary scalar counterterm into a genuine global local-to-global law.

## Consequence

The index-one remainder of `WP-264` remains a real geometric feature, but it is not yet an intrinsic feature of the **critical completed construction**. At the same critical half-density, a hard spectral cutoff with leading-only subtraction has infinite positive index, while Gibbs damping with leading-only subtraction has exactly one positive direction. Positivity therefore cannot choose the regulator or the finite part.

The next viable route must couple renormalization to global geometry strongly enough that the finite constant, its regulator transformation, and the archimedean/polar completion are one theorem. Without that extra structure, the critical complete-incidence form is regulator-dependent at precisely the level where the proposed Hodge/Weil sign is supposed to become canonical.
