# WP-392 — Full Bost--Connes positive cone cannot isolate prime-power support

## Claim

`WP-391` proves that the norm-closed diagonal generated by the canonical Bost--Connes range projections cannot contain a nonzero exact prime-power selector. The obvious escape is to use the **full crossed product**, hoping that off-diagonal source terms can create a positive operator whose quadratic form vanishes on mixed-prime labels while remaining nonzero on prime powers.

That escape also fails at the bounded C-star-algebra level.

Let `A_BC` be the Bost--Connes C-star algebra and let `pi_alpha` be any of its standard positive-energy representations on

\[
\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbf N^\times),
\]

with basis `epsilon_k`. For `A in pi_alpha(A_BC)`, define its basis diagonal

\[
a_A(k):=\langle A\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 a_A(k)=0\quad\text{for every }k\text{ divisible by at least two distinct primes}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 a_A(k)=0\quad\text{for every }k\ge1.
}
\tag{2}
\]

No positivity is needed for (2). Consequently, if `A>=0`, then

\[
\boxed{
 a_A(k)=0\text{ on every mixed-prime }k
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 A=0.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus **no nonzero positive element of the full bounded Bost--Connes C-star algebra can have exact diagonal support contained in the prime powers**. Off-diagonal crossed-product structure does not rescue the literal source-positive prime-power selector left open by `WP-391`.

The mechanism is stronger than divisibility blindness. Every finite algebraic Bost--Connes observable has a single composite multiplier `Q`, carrying at least two distinct fresh primes, such that its entire diagonal is exactly invariant under `k -> kQ`. Norm closure turns this finite-stage recurrence into the exact diagonal no-go (2), and positivity turns zero diagonal into zero operator.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + RH-INDEPENDENT + BOST-CONNES + FULL-BOUNDED-C*-ALGEBRA + OFF-DIAGONAL-TERMS-ALLOWED + FRESH-COMPOSITE-RECURRENCE + PRIME-POWER-SUPPORT-NO-GO + POSITIVITY-KILLS-OFF-DIAGONAL-ESCAPE + SOURCE-SELECTION-OBSTRUCTION + CATEGORY-SPECIFIC + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Algebraic crossed-product monomials have a finite arithmetic horizon

The standard Bost--Connes generators may be written as multiplicative isometries `mu_n` together with the finite-order diagonal characters `e(r)`, `r in Q/Z`. In the standard representation `pi_alpha`,

\[
\pi_\alpha(\mu_n)\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_{nk},
\qquad
\pi_\alpha(e(r))\varepsilon_k=\zeta_{\alpha,r}^{\,k}\varepsilon_k,
\tag{4}
\]

where `zeta_{alpha,r}` is a root of unity whose order is the order `d(r)` of `r` in `Q/Z`.

A dense algebraic span is formed by finite sums of monomials

\[
\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*.
\tag{5}
\]

For one such monomial,

\[
\pi_\alpha(\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*)\varepsilon_k
=
\mathbf 1_{b\mid k}\,
\zeta_{\alpha,r}^{\,k/b}
\varepsilon_{ak/b}.
\tag{6}
\]

Hence its diagonal matrix coefficient is

\[
\left\langle
\pi_\alpha(\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*)\varepsilon_k,
\varepsilon_k
\right\rangle
=
\mathbf 1_{a=b}\,\mathbf 1_{a\mid k}\,
\zeta_{\alpha,r}^{\,k/a}.
\tag{7}
\]

So although the full algebra contains off-diagonal shifts, the diagonal of a **finite** algebraic element sees only finitely many divisibility conditions and finitely many residue classes coming from roots of unity.

This is the exact extra structure absent from `WP-391`: periodic character data enlarge the source diagonal beyond the projection algebra `D_div`, but they still have only a finite arithmetic horizon at every algebraic stage.

## 2. One fresh composite exactly reproduces the whole finite-stage diagonal

Let `B` be a finite algebraic linear combination of monomials (5). Choose an integer `L>=1` divisible by

- every semigroup index `a` and `b` appearing in `B`; and
- every character order `d(r)` appearing in `B`.

Now set

\[
Q:=(L+1)(2L+1).
\tag{8}
\]

Then

\[
Q\equiv1\pmod L,
\qquad
\gcd(Q,L)=1.
\tag{9}
\]

Moreover

\[
\gcd(L+1,2L+1)=1,
\tag{10}
\]

and both factors exceed one, so `Q` has at least two distinct prime divisors. Therefore `kQ` is mixed-prime for **every** `k>=1`.

Consider a diagonal contribution (7). Since `a|L` and `gcd(Q,L)=1`,

\[
a\mid kQ
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\mid k.
\tag{11}
\]

When `a|k`, the character order `d(r)` divides `L`, while `Q congruent 1 mod L`; hence

\[
\frac{kQ}{a}\equiv\frac{k}{a}\pmod{d(r)}.
\tag{12}
\]

The root-of-unity phase in (7) is therefore unchanged. Monomials with `a != b` have zero diagonal at both labels. Summing all monomials gives the finite-stage recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\langle B\varepsilon_{kQ},\varepsilon_{kQ}\rangle
=
\langle B\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle
\qquad\text{for every }k\ge1.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Unlike the fresh-prime argument in `WP-391`, the same `Q` works simultaneously for the entire diagonal of `B`, and `Q` itself is guaranteed to be mixed-prime. No prime-distribution theorem is required.

## 3. C-star norm closure forces every mixed-null diagonal to vanish everywhere

Take arbitrary `A in pi_alpha(A_BC)` satisfying the mixed-prime nullity assumption in (2), and fix `k>=1`. For any `epsilon>0`, choose a finite algebraic `B` such that

\[
\|A-B\|<\epsilon.
\tag{14}
\]

Construct `Q` from `B` as in (8). The integer `kQ` has at least two distinct prime divisors, so by assumption

\[
a_A(kQ)=0.
\tag{15}
\]

Using (13),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|a_A(k)|
&\le
|a_A(k)-a_B(k)|
+|a_B(k)-a_B(kQ)|
+|a_B(kQ)-a_A(kQ)|\\
&\le 2\|A-B\|\\
&<2\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Since `epsilon` is arbitrary,

\[
a_A(k)=0.
\tag{17}
\]

The choice of `k` was arbitrary, proving (2).

This statement is deliberately stronger than a positive-selector theorem: **every bounded Bost--Connes element with mixed-prime-null diagonal has identically zero diagonal**, even if the element itself is not positive and retains off-diagonal matrix coefficients.

## 4. Positivity removes the remaining off-diagonal freedom

Now assume `A>=0`. Equation (2) gives

\[
\langle A\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle=0
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{18}
\]

But for a positive operator,

\[
\langle A\varepsilon_k,\varepsilon_k\rangle
=
\|A^{1/2}\varepsilon_k\|^2.
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore `A^{1/2} epsilon_k=0` for every basis vector. The basis is total, so `A^{1/2}=0` and hence

\[
A=0.
\tag{20}
\]

This is the point at which off-diagonal terms cease to provide an escape. A nonpositive Bost--Connes element may have zero diagonal and nonzero off-diagonal matrix coefficients. A positive element cannot: exact nullity of the quadratic form on every basis vector forces the whole positive operator to vanish.

Equivalently, a positive Bost--Connes quadratic form cannot annihilate all mixed-prime basis states while retaining a nonzero prime-power sector.

## 5. Relation to `WP-391`, `WP-215`, and the accepted Bost--Connes clue

`WP-391` handled only

\[
D_{\rm div}=C^*(\mu_n\mu_n^*:n\ge1),
\tag{21}
\]

where finite observables see divisibility cylinders. The present theorem allows the full crossed-product span, including finite-order cyclotomic characters and genuinely off-diagonal monomials. The obstruction survives because those extra diagonal phases are still finite-period at every algebraic stage, while positivity forbids hiding the selector purely in off-diagonal matrix entries.

This does not contradict `WP-215`. There the Hamiltonian

\[
H\varepsilon_k=(\log k)\varepsilon_k
\tag{22}
\]

has discrete spectrum, so target-chosen spectral calculus can interpolate prime-power support and even the critical Mangoldt half-density. General `F(H)` selectors need not belong to the Bost--Connes C-star algebra. The contrast is now sharp:

\[
\text{arbitrary Hamiltonian spectral coding}
\quad\text{can select prime powers,}
\]

while

\[
\text{the full bounded source C-star algebra}
\quad\text{cannot do so positively with exact mixed nullity.}
\tag{23}
\]

The accepted clue `CLUE-weil-positivity-bost-connes-affiliation-conditioning-boundary` therefore narrows again. `WP-391` left source-forced off-diagonal structure as a literal support-selection escape. `WP-392` closes that escape for positive elements of the full bounded Bost--Connes C-star algebra. What remains must change category or delay selection: for example a genuinely modular/von-Neumann constraint, an unbounded closable form with a source-derived domain, a one-sided correspondence/range defect, a different boundary algebra, or a global operation in which prime-power support appears only after finite--archimedean assembly.

## 6. The von Neumann boundary is intentionally outside the theorem

The standard `pi_alpha` are classically irreducible. Consequently their bicommutant is

\[
\pi_\alpha(A_{BC})''=B(\ell^2(\mathbf N^\times)).
\tag{24}
\]

That weak closure contains arbitrary diagonal projections, including the projection onto the prime-power basis vectors. Thus (3) is **genuinely a C-star norm-closure obstruction**, not a theorem about every operator generated after passing to weak closure.

This boundary is useful rather than cosmetic. Simply saying "pass to the von Neumann algebra" destroys source selectivity: the category becomes large enough to program the desired diagonal by hand. A surviving von-Neumann/modular route must therefore specify an additional source-forced weight, modular relation, domain, expectation, covariance law, or correspondence before the prime-power target is inspected. Otherwise it recreates the programmability problem already exposed by `WP-215` in a larger operator category.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The ingredients through (7) are standard Bost--Connes structure: the semigroup crossed-product generators, their positive-energy representations on `ell^2(N^x)`, finite-order cyclotomic characters, and the algebraic crossed-product span. The standard irreducibility statement used only to mark the von Neumann boundary in (24) is likewise classical. No novelty is claimed for any of these facts.

The arithmetic step (8)--(13) is elementary: a finite family of divisibility and finite-period character tests is unchanged after multiplication by a number congruent to one modulo their common modulus and coprime to it. The particular choice `(L+1)(2L+1)` makes the reproducing multiplier automatically contain two distinct primes.

A bounded literature search around Bost--Connes semigroup crossed products, canonical `ell^2(N^x)` representations, prime-power projections/selectors, and positive support restrictions found the standard crossed-product and representation theory but no direct statement of the implication (2)--(3). The novelty claim is therefore narrow: this is recorded as a **Mathia-derived support obstruction for the full bounded Bost--Connes positive cone**, not as a new general theorem in C-star algebra theory or number theory.

## Limits

- The theorem is representation-level and applies to every standard positive-energy `pi_alpha` on `ell^2(N^x)`. It is not asserted for arbitrary nonstandard representations or GNS completions.
- The result concerns the norm-closed Bost--Connes C-star algebra. Its von Neumann closure is much larger and explicitly lies outside the no-go.
- Unbounded affiliated operators, quadratic forms with nontrivial domains, and modular weights are outside the theorem.
- Exact mixed-prime nullity is essential. Approximate suppression is a different quantitative problem.
- Positivity is essential only for the final implication `zero diagonal => zero operator`. Without positivity, nonzero off-diagonal elements with zero diagonal may exist.
- The theorem closes a support-selection mechanism, not the whole Bost--Connes completion programme. It does not produce or rule out the archimedean Gamma term, polar normalization, a completed Weil form, a source-forced sign theorem, or RH.

## Consequence for the research line

The bounded Bost--Connes source category is now much more sharply classified. `WP-216` still supplies a nontrivial source-forced critical Gram carrying the half-density and logarithmic prime scale. `WP-391` shows its canonical divisibility diagonal cannot hide the prime-power selector. `WP-392` shows that allowing the **entire bounded crossed product, including off-diagonal terms, does not repair that exact selector inside the positive cone**.

The next useful Bost--Connes test should therefore not search for another positive bounded element of the same C-star algebra with mixed-prime nullity, and it should not pass to the bicommutant without an independent source-selection law. A genuinely new route must derive additional structure before the target is read: a canonical modular/weight constraint, an unbounded source form with a nonprogrammable domain, a one-sided correspondence defect, a new boundary algebra, or a global completion in which arithmetic support is an output rather than a preselected diagonal mask.
