# WP-396 — Single modular-frequency positivity collapses to the critical centralizer

## Claim

`WP-395` leaves open the full critical Bost--Connes type-III factor and, explicitly, its **noncentral modular spectral sectors**. A first natural escape is therefore to take one source-selected nonzero modular frequency and use its positive modulus, square, or graph norm as the positivity carrier.

That escape is exact but empty.

Let

\[
M=\pi_{\phi_1}(A_{BC})''
\]

be the critical `KMS_1` GNS von Neumann algebra. As in `WP-395`, write

\[
\Gamma=\mathbf Q_+^\times,
\qquad
K=\widehat{\Gamma},
\]

and let `theta` be the normal compact gauge action extending the Bost--Connes energy grading. For `r in Gamma`, define the spectral subspace

\[
M_r
=
\{x\in M:\theta_\chi(x)=\chi(r)x\ \text{for every }\chi\in K\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Then:

1. for every `x in M_r`,

\[
x^*x,\ xx^*\in M_1=M_{\phi_1};
\tag{2}
\]

2. the polar decomposition `x=v|x|` satisfies

\[
|x|\in M_{\phi_1},
\qquad
v\in M_r,
\qquad
v^*v,\ vv^*\in M_{\phi_1};
\tag{3}
\]

3. if `r != 1`, the positive cone of `M_r` is trivial:

\[
\boxed{M_r\cap M_+=\{0\};}
\tag{4}
\]

4. consequently the canonical positive quadratic form generated by a single homogeneous mode,

\[
Q_x(\xi)=\|x\xi\|^2
=\langle x^*x\xi,\xi\rangle,
\tag{5}
\]

is represented entirely by the critical modular centralizer.

The same reduction survives for a closed densely defined operator `T` affiliated with `M` whenever the compact gauge action is defined on `T` and

\[
\theta_\chi(T)=\chi(r)T
\qquad(\chi\in K)
\tag{6}
\]

in the usual affiliated-operator sense. Then

\[
T^*T,\ |T|
\quad\text{are affiliated with }M_{\phi_1},
\tag{7}
\]

and a positive such `T` is zero unless `r=1`.

Combining (2)--(7) with `WP-395` gives the line-specific obstruction: **moving from the centralizer to one noncentral modular frequency does not move canonical positivity out of the centralizer.** The phase/partial-isometry component can carry nonzero modular frequency, but its positive amplitude and norm-square cannot recover exact prime-power-versus-mixed-prime support on a standard arithmetic orbit, because that amplitude is a centralizer object and the orbit is Haar-null there.

This does not classify the full critical factor. A sum of two or more distinct modular frequencies has cross terms in its positive square, and a continuous-core or correspondence construction can introduce additional structure. The result closes only the natural **single homogeneous mode + canonical positive square/modulus** completion family.

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + RH-INDEPENDENT + CRITICAL-KMS-GNS + COMPACT-GAUGE-SPECTRAL-SUBSPACE + NONCENTRAL-HOMOGENEOUS-MODE + POLAR-DECOMPOSITION + POSITIVE-SQUARE-IN-CENTRALIZER + NONZERO-FREQUENCY-POSITIVE-CONE-TRIVIAL + AFFILIATED-OPERATOR-EXTENSION + WP-395-REDUCTION + SINGLE-MODE-NO-GO + NOT-A-WEIL-BRIDGE`.

## 1. Spectral-subspace multiplication forces the square to frequency zero

The compact gauge action from `WP-395` satisfies

\[
\theta_\chi(x)=\chi(r)x,
\qquad
\theta_\chi(y)=\chi(s)y
\]

for `x in M_r`, `y in M_s`. Therefore

\[
\theta_\chi(xy)
=\chi(rs)xy,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
\theta_\chi(x^*)
=\overline{\chi(r)}x^*
=\chi(r^{-1})x^*.
\tag{9}
\]

Hence

\[
M_rM_s\subseteq M_{rs},
\qquad
M_r^*=M_{r^{-1}}.
\tag{10}
\]

Taking `y=x` in the adjoint product gives

\[
x^*x\in M_{r^{-1}r}=M_1,
\qquad
xx^*\in M_1.
\tag{11}
\]

`WP-395` identifies the zero-frequency algebra exactly as

\[
M_1=M_{\phi_1}
\cong L^\infty(\widehat{\mathbf Z},m_{\rm Haar}).
\tag{12}
\]

Thus every single noncentral spectral mode has a positive square in the same diffuse abelian centralizer that already forgot the countable standard arithmetic orbit.

No Bost--Connes-specific approximation is being used in (8)--(11). They are the ordinary multiplication laws for spectral subspaces of an abelian group action. The arithmetic content enters through the source-selected grading `Gamma=Q_+^*` and through the identification (12).

## 2. Polar decomposition localizes all positive amplitude in the centralizer

Let

\[
x=v|x|
\tag{13}
\]

be the polar decomposition of a bounded `x in M_r`. Since

\[
|x|=(x^*x)^{1/2},
\]

functional calculus and (11) imply

\[
|x|\in M_1=M_{\phi_1}.
\tag{14}
\]

The support projections of `|x|` and `|x^*|` are therefore centralizer projections:

\[
v^*v=s(x^*x)\in M_{\phi_1},
\qquad
vv^*=s(xx^*)\in M_{\phi_1}.
\tag{15}
\]

Applying `theta_chi` to (13), using (14), gives

\[
\theta_\chi(v)|x|
=\chi(r)v|x|.
\tag{16}
\]

Uniqueness of the polar partial isometry on the support of `|x|` yields

\[
\theta_\chi(v)=\chi(r)v,
\tag{17}
\]

so `v in M_r`.

Therefore the nonzero modular frequency lives entirely in the **orientation** `v`, while the positive amplitude `|x|` and both source/range supports live at frequency zero. This is exactly the wrong direction for a single-mode positivity mechanism: taking the canonical modulus discards the noncentral frequency before any Weil sign theorem can be obtained.

## 3. A nonzero frequency cannot itself be positive

Suppose `x in M_r` and `x>=0`. Then `x=x^*`. By (9),

\[
x\in M_r\cap M_{r^{-1}}.
\tag{18}
\]

If `r != 1`, choose a character `chi in K` with `chi(r) != 1`; Pontryagin duality separates points of the discrete abelian group `Gamma`. From positivity, `theta_chi(x)` is positive, while the spectral relation gives

\[
\theta_\chi(x)=\chi(r)x.
\tag{19}
\]

A nonzero positive operator cannot equal a nontrivial unit-modulus scalar multiple of another positive operator. Hence `x=0`, proving (4).

Equivalently, because `Gamma=Q_+^*` is torsion-free, `r=r^{-1}` already forces `r=1`; distinct characters separate the corresponding spectral subspaces.

So the phrase "use a positive operator from a noncentral modular frequency" is internally inconsistent unless the operator is zero. A noncentral homogeneous object may exist, but its positivity must be obtained only after combining it with its adjoint or with other frequencies.

## 4. Canonical norm-square positivity returns exactly to `WP-395`

For a bounded homogeneous mode `x`, the most direct positive construction is the graph/norm square (5). Its representing operator is `x^*x`, and (11)--(12) put it in

\[
L^\infty(\widehat{\mathbf Z},m_{\rm Haar}).
\tag{20}
\]

Now fix a standard arithmetic unit orbit

\[
\mathcal O_\alpha=\{n\alpha:n\ge1\}
\]

and its prime-power subset `P_alpha`. `WP-395` proves that `O_alpha` and `P_alpha` are Haar-null and that exact prime-power support has only two operator-theoretic readings in the centralizer:

- literal support inside `P_alpha` gives the zero operator;
- prescribed point values on `O_alpha` are representative-dependent and therefore are not operator invariants.

Thus the canonical positive quantity attached to one noncentral mode cannot use its modular frequency to rescue exact arithmetic point selection. The frequency disappears under `x -> x^*x`, while the remaining positive amplitude is measured modulo the diffuse Haar class.

This is stronger than merely saying that `x^*x` is invariant. It identifies the exact destination of the positivity operation with the previously classified centralizer and therefore imports the precise selector obstruction already established there.

## 5. Closed affiliated homogeneous operators obey the same reduction

The unbounded route survives `WP-393`--`WP-395` only when the ambient category is genuinely enlarged. A homogeneous closed operator affiliated with the full critical factor is such an enlargement, so it is worth checking separately rather than assuming the bounded argument.

Let `T` be densely defined, closed, affiliated with `M`, and suppose the normal compact gauge action extends to it with (6). Automorphisms preserve adjoints and the affiliated functional calculus, so

\[
\theta_\chi(T^*T)
=\theta_\chi(T)^*\theta_\chi(T)
=\overline{\chi(r)}\chi(r)T^*T
=T^*T.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence `T^*T` is affiliated with the fixed algebra `M_1=M_phi1`; by functional calculus,

\[
|T|=(T^*T)^{1/2}
\]

is affiliated with the same centralizer. Its closed nonnegative graph form

\[
Q_T(\xi)=\|T\xi\|^2
\tag{22}
\]

therefore has centralizer modulus data.

If `T>=0`, then every `theta_chi(T)` is again positive. Relation (6) can hold for nonzero `T` only when `chi(r)=1` for every character, hence only when `r=1`. Thus a genuinely noncentral homogeneous affiliated operator is necessarily non-self-adjoint; its canonical positive square again drops to frequency zero.

This extension does **not** claim that every affiliated operator of the type-III factor has a discrete gauge frequency. It classifies only the source-constrained family in which the proposed noncentral escape is precisely one homogeneous modular/gauge mode.

## 6. What this closes and what remains open

`WP-395` deliberately left noncentral modular spectral subspaces open. The present result shows that merely stepping into one such subspace is not enough if the desired positivity is obtained in the standard canonical way from one mode.

The obstruction has two layers:

1. there is no nonzero positive object in a nonzero spectral frequency itself;
2. the canonical operation that makes a homogeneous object positive, `x -> x^*x` or `T -> T^*T`, removes the frequency and lands in the diffuse centralizer classified by `WP-395`.

Therefore a surviving critical-factor construction must use structure that is not reducible to a single homogeneous modulus. Concrete possibilities left open include:

- cross-frequency interference from at least two genuinely source-selected modular frequencies;
- a continuous-core/crossed-product construction where the dual variable participates in the positive form;
- a one-sided correspondence or range defect whose positivity is not merely `T^*T` in the original factor;
- a finite--archimedean or global assembly in which arithmetic weights emerge only after coupling several sectors.

Those are only surviving categories, not evidence that they work. In particular, a finite sum

\[
y=\sum_j x_{r_j}
\]

has

\[
y^*y
=\sum_j x_{r_j}^*x_{r_j}
+\sum_{i\ne j}x_{r_i}^*x_{r_j},
\tag{23}
\]

and the cross terms lie in frequencies `r_i^{-1}r_j`; they need not be centralizer elements. Equation (23) is exactly why this finding does not overclaim a classification of all positive elements of `M`.

## 7. Stress tests

### Nonzero spectral sectors really exist

The result is not vacuous because the original Bost--Connes generators already supply homogeneous noncentral elements:

\[
\theta_\chi(\mu_n)=\chi(n)\mu_n.
\tag{24}
\]

Their positive squares satisfy

\[
\mu_n^*\mu_n=1,
\qquad
\mu_n\mu_n^*=E_n,
\tag{25}
\]

which are zero-frequency positive elements. This is the basic concrete example of the theorem: the arithmetic energy frequency sits in the isometry, while its norm-square/support data are fixed by the gauge action.

### The theorem does not prohibit noncentral positive elements

A positive element of the full factor can contain several frequencies simultaneously. For example, if `x in M_r`, then

\[
(1+x)^*(1+x)
=1+x+x^*+x^*x
\tag{26}
\]

is positive and generally noncentral. The theorem says only that no **single nonzero spectral component** is positive and that the one-mode norm square is central.

This is a crucial boundary: replacing a single-mode proposal by a genuinely fixed multi-frequency source relation is a changed hypothesis, not a contradiction of the no-go.

### No target-dependent scalar interpolation enters

The reduction uses only the source gauge action, adjoint, multiplication, polar decomposition, and positive square. There is no chosen scalar `F(H)`, quotient selected by sign, or interpolating diagonal bath. Thus the negative result is not caused by a target-programmability restriction; it is intrinsic to homogeneous positivity.

## 8. Prior-art and novelty audit

The spectral-subspace algebra in (8)--(11) is standard for abelian group actions on operator algebras; Zsido's work is classical background for spectral subspaces, and Tomita--Takesaki theory supplies the modular setting. Bost--Connes supplies the arithmetic energy grading and the critical KMS factor. No novelty is claimed for polar decomposition, spectral-subspace multiplication, or the fact that a homogeneous square has zero frequency.

A bounded literature search for modular eigenoperators/spectral subspaces, centralizers, polar decomposition, and Bost--Connes critical KMS structure found the general operator-algebraic machinery and the classical Bost--Connes factor theory, but no source treating the specific prime-power-support consequence developed here.

The line-specific derived contribution is the composition with `WP-395`: the most immediate noncentral escape left by that finding—**one critical modular frequency made positive by its canonical square or modulus**—returns exactly to the diffuse Haar centralizer at the moment positivity is imposed. Therefore it cannot restore the discrete arithmetic selector that motivated leaving the centralizer.

## Limits

- This is not a theorem about arbitrary positive elements of the full critical type-`III_1` factor.
- It does not rule out finite or infinite sums of distinct modular frequencies whose positive squares retain noncentral cross terms.
- It does not classify the continuous core, crossed product by the modular action, canonical correspondences, or one-sided Hilbert-module defects.
- It does not produce the archimedean Gamma term, polar sector, completed Weil form, or an RH implication.
- The affiliated-operator extension assumes a genuine homogeneous covariance relation under the source compact gauge action; arbitrary affiliated operators need not admit such a discrete frequency.

## Research consequence

The critical Bost--Connes factor remains a legitimate larger category after `WP-395`, but the next test must no longer be phrased as "choose a noncentral modular mode and take its positive square." For one homogeneous mode, positivity removes precisely the modular information that distinguished it from the centralizer.

A materially new candidate must therefore specify a **source-forced interaction between frequencies or a larger source-derived category before positivity is taken**, and then derive rather than choose the resulting sign. The smallest unresolved critical-factor test is a fixed two-frequency or correspondence construction whose cross term is source-selected independently of the Weil target and whose positive form can still be compared exactly with the finite-prime defect of `WP-216`.
