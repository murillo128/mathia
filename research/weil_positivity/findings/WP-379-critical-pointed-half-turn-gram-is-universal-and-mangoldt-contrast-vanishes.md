# WP-379 — Critical pointed half-turn Gram is universal while the Mangoldt contrast vanishes

## Claim

`WP-378` leaves pointed or otherwise non-homogeneous disk geometry as one of the few ways to escape the rotation-invariant Hardy factorization obstruction. The most economical such test is the canonical pointed Dirichlet form already isolated in `WP-072`, but now retaining the half-turn pair that full-circle energies lose.

For every odd integer `N>=3`, define

\[
A_N(z):=\log\frac{1-z^N}{1-z},
\qquad
B_N(z):=A_N(-z)=\log\frac{1+z^N}{1+z},
\tag{1}
\]

with the analytic branches vanishing at `z=0`. For an odd prime `p`, these are exactly the two cyclotomic shells

\[
A_p=\log\Phi_p,
\qquad
B_p=\log\Phi_{2p},
\tag{2}
\]

because `Phi_{2p}(z)=Phi_p(-z)`. Thus the pair is a genuine Prime-Circle `(p,2p)` test, while arbitrary odd composite `N` supplies the matched non-prime control.

Let

\[
D_1(F,G)
:=
\left\langle
\frac{F(z)-F(1)}{z-1},
\frac{G(z)-G(1)}{z-1}
\right\rangle_{H^2},
\tag{3}
\]

with `D_1(F):=D_1(F,F)`. Under the critical cover normalization forced by `WP-073`, the two-channel Gram has the exact scaling limit

\[
\boxed{
\frac1N
\begin{pmatrix}
D_1(A_N) & D_1(A_N,B_N)\\
D_1(B_N,A_N) & D_1(B_N)
\end{pmatrix}
\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}
C & \frac{C-\log2}{2}\\
\frac{C-\log2}{2} & \log2
\end{pmatrix},
}
\tag{4}
\]

where

\[
C:=\int_0^\infty a(x)^2\,dx,
\qquad
a(x):=H_{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\log x-\gamma
\tag{5}
\]

is the one-channel Nyman constant from `WP-168`.

The limiting matrix is positive definite, but it is **independent of whether `N` is prime**. At the same time the boundary values are

\[
A_N(1)=\log N,
\qquad
B_N(1)=0,
\tag{6}
\]

so on prime shells the exact Mangoldt contrast becomes

\[
\frac{\Lambda(p)}{\sqrt p}
=\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p}
\longrightarrow0
\tag{7}
\]

under the same critical normalization for which the positive bulk Gram converges to the nondegenerate matrix (4).

Hence pointed base-point geometry really does evade the rotation-invariant blindness of `WP-167` and `WP-378`, but its most canonical half-turn completion still fails the arithmetic matched-control test: **the surviving positive critical geometry is universal degree geometry, while the arithmetic boundary distinction disappears.**

## 1. Exact pointed coefficients

For

\[
T_1F(z):=\frac{F(z)-F(1)}{z-1},
\tag{8}
\]

`WP-168` gives the coefficients of `T_1A_N` as

\[
q_{A,N}(k)
=\log N-H_k+H_{\lfloor k/N\rfloor}.
\tag{9}
\]

For odd `N`, expanding (1) gives

\[
B_N(z)
=\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{(-1)^m\bigl(1-N\mathbf 1_{N\mid m}\bigr)}{m}
 z^m.
\tag{10}
\]

Because `B_N(1)=0`, the `k`-th coefficient of `T_1B_N` is the tail of (10). Define the alternating harmonic remainder

\[
R_n:=\sum_{m>n}\frac{(-1)^m}{m}
=H_n-H_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}-\log2.
\tag{11}
\]

Since `N` is odd, divisibility preserves parity and therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_{B,N}(k)=R_k-R_{\lfloor k/N\rfloor}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This identity is exact, not asymptotic.

## 2. Critical scaling produces a universal half-turn profile

Put `k=floor(Nx)`. From (9), the standard harmonic asymptotic used in `WP-168` gives

\[
q_{A,N}(\lfloor Nx\rfloor)\longrightarrow
 a(x)
=H_{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\log x-\gamma.
\tag{13}
\]

Since `R_k->0`, (12) gives

\[
q_{B,N}(\lfloor Nx\rfloor)
\longrightarrow
 d(x):=-R_{\lfloor x\rfloor}.
\tag{14}
\]

Using (11),

\[
d(x)
=\log2-H_{\lfloor x\rfloor}+H_{\lfloor x/2\rfloor}
=a(x/2)-a(x).
\tag{15}
\]

The same remainder bounds and Riemann-sum argument as in `WP-168` then yield

\[
\frac{D_1(A_N)}N\to\int_0^\infty a(x)^2dx=C,
\tag{16}
\]

\[
\frac{D_1(B_N)}N\to\int_0^\infty d(x)^2dx,
\qquad
\frac{D_1(A_N,B_N)}N\to\int_0^\infty a(x)d(x)dx.
\tag{17}
\]

Nothing in (13)--(17) detects primality: the limit only sees the odd cover degree and its half-turn.

## 3. The half-turn mass is exactly `log 2`

On every interval `[n,n+1)`, equation (14) gives `d(x)=-R_n`. Hence

\[
\int_0^\infty d(x)^2dx
=\sum_{n\ge0}R_n^2.
\tag{18}
\]

The elementary integral representation

\[
R_n=(-1)^{n+1}\int_0^1\frac{t^n}{1+t}\,dt
\tag{19}
\]

therefore gives, by summing the geometric series,

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}R_n^2
=
\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
\frac{dx\,dy}{(1+x)(1+y)(1-xy)}.
\tag{20}
\]

Integrating first in `y`,

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}R_n^2
=\int_0^1
\frac{\log2-\log(1-x)}{(1+x)^2}\,dx.
\tag{21}
\]

The first term contributes `(log2)/2`. For the second, setting `t=1-x` and expanding

\[
\frac1{(2-t)^2}
=\frac14\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)\left(\frac t2\right)^n
\tag{22}
\]

with

\[
\int_0^1(-\log t)t^n\,dt=\frac1{(n+1)^2}
\tag{23}
\]

gives another `(log2)/2`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty d(x)^2dx=\log2.
}
\tag{24}
\]

The `log 2` is therefore intrinsic to the half-turn channel; it is not inserted as a regularization constant.

## 4. The full two-channel Gram

Let

\[
J:=\int_0^\infty a(x)a(x/2)\,dx.
\tag{25}
\]

Because `d(x)=a(x/2)-a(x)` and

\[
\int_0^\infty a(x/2)^2dx=2C,
\tag{26}
\]

we obtain from (24)

\[
\log2
=2C+C-2J,
\qquad
J=\frac{3C-\log2}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]

Therefore

\[
\int_0^\infty a(x)d(x)dx
=J-C
=\frac{C-\log2}{2},
\tag{28}
\]

which proves (4). Positivity is not a sign inference from zeta data: (4) is the Gram matrix of the two real `L^2` profiles `a` and `d`. It is positive definite because the profiles are not scalar multiples; for example, on `(0,1)`, `d(x)=log2` is constant while `a(x)=-log x-\gamma` is not.

Thus this candidate passes the narrow requirement that nonnegativity be forced independently by geometry. What fails is the arithmetic identification.

## 5. Matched control kills the arithmetic interpretation

For odd prime `p`, the pair `(A_p,B_p)` equals the primitive cyclotomic pair `(F_p,F_{2p})`. But equations (1), (9), and (12) hold unchanged for every odd composite `N`. Consequently the complete limiting matrix (4), including its nonzero off-diagonal term, is reproduced by non-prime full-root controls.

This is stronger than observing that a diagonal norm is universal. The **entire smallest pointed half-turn incidence matrix** that survives critical scaling is cloned by arbitrary odd degree. The prime-shell interpretation therefore cannot be recovered from that critical Gram itself.

At finite scale, pointed geometry does retain the exact boundary distinction (6). For prime `p`, this is precisely

\[
F_p(1)=\log p=\Lambda(p),
\qquad
F_{2p}(1)=0=\Lambda(2p).
\tag{29}
\]

But the cover-isometric normalization `N^{-1/2}` forced in `WP-073` sends the nonzero boundary component to `(log p)/sqrt(p)`, which vanishes, while (4) remains nondegenerate. So the arithmetic signal and the positive critical bulk live at incompatible scales in this canonical two-channel construction.

## 6. What this rules out

This does **not** rule out pointed local Dirichlet geometry in general. It rules out a more specific surviving route after `WP-378`: obtaining the needed arithmetic Weil positivity merely by replacing the rotation-invariant Hardy Gram with the canonical pointed `D_1` Gram and adjoining the half-turn shell before taking the critical cover limit.

A surviving pointed construction must do at least one additional nontrivial thing before positivity is invoked: keep finite-scale boundary data at nonvanishing strength, introduce a source-forced singular or archimedean coupling that is not reproduced by the odd-degree control, retain a genuinely arithmetic multi-place incidence before scalarization, or use a topology in which the boundary trace and the bulk critical norm remain coupled rather than separating as above.

No Gamma/polar term is generated here, and there is no global Weil sign theorem. The result is therefore a narrowing obstruction, not a candidate proof of Weil positivity.

## 7. Adversarial novelty audit

Internally, the nearest findings are distinct. `WP-070` studies a rotation-invariant antipodal Hardy correction and finds blindness to odd full-root controls. `WP-167` shows full-circle radial-angular Dirichlet energy cannot distinguish `Phi_p(z)` from `Phi_p(-z)`. `WP-168` identifies the one-channel pointed critical Nyman kernel, but does not compute the half-turn channel or its two-by-two matched-control Gram. `WP-378` classifies the unpointed cyclotomic Hardy Gram and leaves pointed/non-homogeneous geometry open.

The present result closes exactly that smallest pointed half-turn test: the point breaks the rotational blindness, yet the critical two-channel geometry is still degree-universal and loses the Mangoldt boundary contrast.

The external audit was deliberately bounded around local Dirichlet boundary forms, Nyman-Beurling step-function geometry, and the half-turn/alternating-harmonic profile. Those ingredients are classical; no literature-novelty claim is made for them. The substantive contribution recorded here is the exact branch-specific combination (12)--(29) and its matched-control falsification of this Mathia-native route.

## Conclusion

The canonical pointed Dirichlet topology does something the rotation-invariant candidates could not: it sees the `(p,2p)` half-turn pair and produces a genuine positive mixed Gram. But after the intrinsic critical normalization its full two-channel limit is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
C & (C-\log2)/2\\
(C-\log2)/2 & \log2
\end{pmatrix},
\]

for **every odd degree**, prime or composite, while the exact prime boundary contrast `log p/sqrt(p)` tends to zero. The first pointed escape from `WP-378` is therefore geometric but not arithmetic enough. Any viable continuation must couple the boundary/arithmetic incidence to the positive geometry before the critical limit erases it.
