## Derive the nonlocal sampling object independently before asking it to reproduce Weil positivity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-positivity-needs-a-sign-producing-global-operation`, `MI-010-source-forced-critical-gram-can-have-the-wrong-weil-orientation`, `MI-011-prime-ray-marginals-do-not-determine-mixed-prime-coupling`, `MI-012-automorphic-multiplicity-does-not-prevent-global-scalarization`, `MI-013-critical-sector-transport-needs-new-global-arithmetic-structure`.

WP-267--WP-273 show that hostability, category descent, scalar sign and localizable compression all fail as free routes. A surviving positive compression must be globally nonlocal and reconstruct continuum mass from arithmetic samples.

WP-274 shows that **nonlocal inverse representation is still not enough**. Pole normalization turns the critical Mangoldt carrier into the positive Herglotz measure with masses `Lambda(n)/(n+1)`. Classical Clark inversion can manufacture a meromorphic inner function whose model space makes the pole-completed form identically zero. The strengthened result now shows the same for de Branges inverse spectral theory: the Herglotz function can be turned into a positive trace-normalized canonical-system Hamiltonian, uniquely up to the standard normalization.

Neither construction is a Weil sign mechanism because both begin with the desired target measure. The same inverse pipelines work for generic Poisson-finite singular Jordan data. Replacing a manufactured model space by a manufactured positive Hamiltonian does not restore arithmetic causality.

The surviving route must reverse the order. Derive an inner function, canonical system, de Branges Hamiltonian, boundary correspondence, matrix/noncommutative coupling, or equivalent nonlocal object from **independent source/Mathia geometry first**. Only then ask whether its pre-existing sampling, Weyl or positivity law reproduces the Mangoldt term together with the Gamma and polar sectors in the correct Weil orientation.
