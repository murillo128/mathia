## Treat mass class, curvature orientation, gauge rigidity, transform admissibility and asymptotic neutrality as separate gates

Matching `log y` establishes only the infinite-mass class. Producing a nonuniversal `y^-2` coefficient establishes arithmetic sensitivity but not the correct orientation. WP-307 closes positive affine input recentering; WP-308 closes constant projective output gauge; WP-309 collapses holomorphic scalar automorphism families to the same constant class. WP-310 adds the admissibility gate: a positive Herglotz source can lie outside the domain of a desired transform.

WP-311--WP-312 add a distinct gate: **an admissible positivity-preserving transform can damage the target asymptotic hierarchy even when it is canonical.** Fixed-interior Schur stripping and regular fixed-boundary Julia reduction insert a larger positive `log(y)/y` term; pure pole boundary stripping preserves that order but worsens the wrong-sign curvature. A future transform must prove both admissibility and discriminator-scale neutrality from the source.

## Use matched controls against universal leading scale and post-hoc recoding

Unit-density positive measures control the leading logarithm, while the Mangoldt curvature sign is source-specific. WP-307--WP-309 show that affine or projective scalar gauge cannot flip that sign while preserving the required logarithmic end. WP-310 shows that the standard moment-Schur algorithm cannot start on the critical source without changing its normalization or positivity class. WP-311--WP-312 show that the natural fixed interior and fixed boundary scalar alternatives are defined and positivity-preserving but move larger subleading terms or curvature in the wrong direction.

Future constructions must therefore alter the geometry or realization before final scalar comparison and must prove both **positivity/admissibility** and **asymptotic-scale compatibility** rather than importing either by recoding, target-dependent cancellation or a moving normalization whose source meaning has not been established.
