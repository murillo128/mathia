## Find a non-scalar automorphic sign mechanism before the critical carrier is squared

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-039-enriched-semigroups-inherit-obstructed-scalar-shadows-or-universal-lifts` through `MI-051-nonreversible-positivity-can-carry-orientation-without-forcing-it`.

WP-356--WP-357 classify the finite constant-mode Hecke route: cusp-boundary defects reduce to universal interval-overlap/Brownian geometry, so finite arithmetic operator structure survives before positivity but becomes nonselective after positive closure.

WP-358 shows that nonconstant Fourier modes do retain genuine arithmetic information. For prime `p`, the angular range projection is exactly divisibility by `p`. Yet the sharp boundary square scalarizes that information to density `1/p`; the desired Weil coefficient has critical half-density `p^(-1/2)`. The canonical positive quadratic form therefore **squares the critical amplitude** rather than preserving it.

WP-359 closes the fixed smooth-height repair. Smoothing adds only off-diagonal coherence along `p^2` Fourier orbits. On individual nonzero modes the two Hecke branches remain orthogonal, and the natural Fourier Følner scalarization kills the cross terms exactly, retaining the same `1/p` arithmetic scale. A fixed-width smooth cutoff changes the height profile but not the half-density exponent.

WP-360 tests whether an infinite all-prime packet can restore the missing amplitude before squaring. On the natural finite-Fourier cusp core, different prime boundary outputs are orthogonal and strong convergence is equivalent to `l2` summability of the prime coefficients. Critical amplitudes `p^(-1/2)` and `(log p)p^(-1/2)` fail that threshold. The critical line is precisely where the ordinary Hilbert completion ceases to exist.

WP-361 then moves to the globally correlated Eisenstein eigenline. Finite Hecke packets act there through one scalar multiplier, so the truncated Hecke Gram is rank one and positive Maaß--Selberg averages are exact pullbacks to another admissible test function. The cross-prime coherence is real but maximally scalar; the unrenormalized critical all-prime multiplier still diverges.

WP-362 tests the canonical analytic completion rather than an arbitrary summation rule. The Eisenstein Hecke Dirichlet series is exactly `zeta(s+ir)zeta(s-ir)`, and its completed central value is a modulus square containing both finite and archimedean factors. This positivity is tautological with respect to the zero configuration. Taking the logarithmic derivative recovers the exact Mangoldt half-density on every prime power, but simultaneously imports the zeta divisor as poles and loses inherited positivity. The route therefore bifurcates sharply: **canonical scalar positivity has the wrong orientation; the correct linear carrier already contains the divisor it is supposed to constrain**.

The direct scalar level-one Hecke/Eisenstein escape is now tightly classified. A viable continuation must create a genuinely non-scalar geometric sign theorem before or while the logarithmic/divisor readout is formed. The surviving local possibility from WP-359 is coherent off-diagonal structure along multiplicative Fourier orbits before scalarization; broader possibilities include nonlocal Arthur/Maaß--Selberg coupling, higher-rank/adelic geometry, or another source-forced positive object whose orientation is not a reversible scalar gauge.

## Keep arithmetic content, convergence category and sign coercivity separate

The current audit distinguishes finite Hecke algebra, nonconstant divisibility projections, smooth-orbit interference, strong all-prime completion, Eisenstein eigenline coherence, canonical Euler completion and logarithmic-derivative divisor readout. Each retains genuine arithmetic information, but none by itself supplies Weil positivity.

A proposal should therefore state where the critical `p^(-1/2)` or `(log p)p^(-1/2)` amplitude lives **before** the positive square, what completion/domain makes the all-prime object meaningful, which noncentral degrees of freedom survive scalarization, and what theorem fixes their orientation independently of the target RH sign. If the construction becomes a modulus square, a rank-one test pullback or a `1/p` projection density, it has crossed back into one of the classified nonselective closures.
