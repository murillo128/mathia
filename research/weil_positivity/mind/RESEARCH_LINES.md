## Couple the exact signed finite selector to the completion before taking positivity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-positivity-needs-a-sign-producing-global-operation` and `MI-003-arithmetic-selector-and-positive-bulk-are-separated-by-symmetry-breaking`.

The finite arithmetic side is now much less mysterious than the sign. WP-018 derives `Lambda(n)` intrinsically from the backward Boolean cube, and WP-022 derives the critical finite Weil cosine coefficients as the radial score of the canonical prime-torus Poisson family. In both cases the selector is a signed first-order object. Ordinary Hodge/supersymmetric positivity cancels it to an index (WP-019--WP-020), while Fisher/divergence positivity becomes singular or universal at the critical boundary (WP-022--WP-023).

The live question is to construct one canonical **coupled finite-plus-archimedean/polar object before the signed selector is quotiented away**, and then prove its final Weil-type sign from an independent geometric/order theorem. A decisive mechanism must generate the gamma/polar sector and the sign together; appending those terms to a finite supertrace/score or choosing a positive functional that erases the selector does not qualify.

## Derive a category-changing boundary or relative operation with its own sign theorem

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-002-operator-category-is-a-hard-compatibility-gate` and `MI-003-arithmetic-selector-and-positive-bulk-are-separated-by-symmetry-breaking`.

The exact Prime-Flute Schiffer coupling is too soft in operator-ideal terms to become the finite-Weil operator through bounded positive calculus. WP-015 further closes the ordinary DtN/Feshbach escape: zero-energy DtN positivity does not survive positive spectral continuation, and critical-line outgoing response is non-Hermitian because of universal cusp flux. WP-021 shows that eta/boundary asymmetry can retain information beyond the positive square, but not a sign inherited from that square.

The remaining boundary route must therefore be genuinely relative, singular, noncommuting, cohomological, or asymmetry-sensitive **and** come with a new ordering/intersection theorem at the destination. The decisive test is an operation forced by Mathia geometry that both crosses the Schatten/category mismatch and proves the completed sign without inserting the Weil functional or zero divisor. A boundary invariant that merely carries information but has uncontrolled sign is not enough.
