## Find arithmetic coupling that survives scalar normalization and the critical tensor boundary

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-positivity-needs-a-sign-producing-global-operation`, `MI-010-source-forced-critical-gram-can-have-the-wrong-weil-orientation`, `MI-011-prime-ray-marginals-do-not-determine-mixed-prime-coupling`, `MI-012-automorphic-multiplicity-does-not-prevent-global-scalarization`.

WP-216--WP-244 show that correct critical prime amplitudes, positivity, cusp multiplicity, congruence sectors, and spherical higher rank repeatedly leave Riemann/Dirichlet arithmetic inside scalar global normalizers or orthogonal scalar channels before the available positive/unitary structure acts.

WP-245 closes the bare higher-rank parameter-Hessian escape. On the unitary axis the magnitude Hessian of the Gindikin--Karpelevich scalar is zero, while the phase Hessian is odd under spectral reversal and cannot define a nonzero Weyl-invariant positive energy. WP-246 shows that fixed-level non-spherical data do not solve centrality either: almost every finite place remains spherical, so the cofinite Riemann Euler tail stays in a scalar normalizer; normalized-intertwiner unitarity gives Hermiticity, not positivity.

WP-247 tests the direct infinite-level repair. In the canonical spherical-reference restricted tensor product, one-prime nonspherical channels are orthogonal and an all-prime deformation exists only with square-summable amplitudes. The critical `p^{-1/2}` and `(log p)/sqrt(p)` scales lie exactly beyond that boundary. Moving to a different infinite-tensor sector is therefore a genuine category change, not a limit of fixed-level non-sphericalization.

The live escape must now identify a canonical source-defined coupling that acts before scalar normalization and is not confined to the spherical-reference Hilbert sector, while bringing an independent sign theorem with it. A different infinite-tensor sector, boundary/truncation form, or coupled automorphic family is relevant only if the construction explains why the all-prime Riemann arithmetic becomes genuinely noncentral and why the resulting operator has the required Weil orientation.

## Treat phase curvature, fixed-level non-sphericality, and critical all-prime product limits as controls

A nontrivial Hessian need not be positive, an operator-valued local intertwiner need not carry the cofinite Euler tail, and finite-level nonspherical perturbations need not converge at the critical half-density. Future proposals should locate the exact stage at which global arithmetic and sign become coupled, rather than infer that coupling from matrix size, unitarity, or an infinite collection of local channels.
