## Couple the exact finite selector to the archimedean/polar sector before determinant, class, or absolute-value reduction

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-positivity-needs-a-sign-producing-global-operation` and `MI-003-arithmetic-selector-and-positive-bulk-are-separated-by-symmetry-breaking`.

Finite arithmetic recovery is no longer the main mystery. Canonical positive objects can store `Lambda`, `log p`, and even higher Hardy cross-shell information. But the direct completions repeatedly externalize the desired coefficient into a determinant/index, erase it in a cohomological completion, or replace the signed full-root channel by an absolute-value-type positive object.

The live question is to form the finite-plus-archimedean/polar object **before** those reductions. A decisive mechanism must retain the exact finite coefficient and the canonically selected archimedean channel in one nontrivial class/operator and derive its sign independently. A positive determinant whose logarithm is read afterwards, a universal cycle Laplacian, or a polar absolute value does not qualify.

## Derive a genuinely new symmetry-breaking metric rather than the canonical polar repair

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-002-operator-category-is-a-hard-compatibility-gate` and `MI-003-arithmetic-selector-and-positive-bulk-are-separated-by-symmetry-breaking`.

The strongest current carriers make the obstruction explicit. The Prym polarization determinant line has positive covolume `p^{g}`, but its canonical polarization operator is spectrally flat; theta/Heisenberg action before positivity recovers `log p` only through a universal cycle-Laplacian log determinant; untwisted analytic torsion cancels the same determinant-line scale. On the independently selected full-root `q=2` Hardy channel, every nonzero half-turn-equivariant compression is indefinite, and the natural parity-exchanging Krein repair is uniquely the polar absolute value.

A surviving operation must therefore use additional geometry that forces a non-polar metric/sign, a twisted/family/boundary completion that does not erase the selector, or a genuinely nonseparable finite--archimedean construction. A decisive result must provide its own sign theorem and preserve the exact arithmetic selector rather than merely converting an indefinite operator into `|A|`.
