WP-395 changes category to the critical factor but finds a diffuse centralizer `L^infinity(hat Z,m_Haar)` in which the standard countable arithmetic orbit is Haar-null. WP-396 shows that one nonzero modular/gauge frequency does not fix the problem, and WP-397 closes the immediate multi-frequency interference escape: every useful Fourier coefficient of a bounded positive element remains supported over the same positive centralizer floor.

WP-398 shows that quotienting away a centralizer containing the order unit destroys inherited order: the image of the positive cone fills the entire self-adjoint information quotient. WP-399 then shows that fixing a nonconstant centralizer shadow still leaves strongly and ultraweakly dense two-sided centered tangents on its support.

WP-400 narrows the source-relation escape. Any linear source-selected subspace stable under canonical shadow cutoffs inherits the same dense two-sided tangent geometry, so every normal linear sign functional forced only by positivity vanishes. WP-401 replaces discontinuous von Neumann projections by continuous `C*(d)` cutoffs inside the hereditary source C-star algebra and obtains **norm-dense** two-sided tangents. Missing source spectral projections therefore do not create an orientation mechanism by themselves.

WP-402 closes the apparent “make the readout genuinely unbounded” continuation whenever the map remains closable. Let `D` be a real linear source domain stable under the canonical continuous cutoffs and `T:D->Y` a possibly unbounded linear map into a Banach space with pointed cone. If `d+h>=0` is required to place `T(h)` in that cone, every localized cutoff response is exactly zero. The tails `r_n=k-c_nkc_n` then satisfy `r_n->0` while `T(r_n)=T(k)`, so closability forces `T(k)=0`. Every such closable cutoff-stable orientation is trivial.

WP-403 removes cutoff stability for every **closed finite-codimensional** source slice. The order-bounded tangent core is dense in the full centered hereditary fibre, and its image in a finite-dimensional quotient is automatically surjective. Any cutoff approximation can therefore be corrected by finitely many order-bounded directions so that it satisfies all the global constraints while converging to the original vector. Thus `F intersect P_d` remains dense in `F`; pointed-cone positivity kills the response there, and closability kills it on all of `F`.

The surviving linear route is consequently narrower than either “use an unbounded functional” or “impose a global relation that cutoffs violate.” Finitely many continuous moments, normalization equations, balance laws or matching constraints are still too small: their quotient errors can be repaired inside the two-sided tangent core. A nontrivial linear orientation must exhibit a genuinely infinite-dimensional or singular obstruction to that repair—such as an infinite-codimensional slice with non-dense order-bounded core, a proper singular operator domain, deliberate nonclosability, or a one-sided admissible set—or else change category to nonlinear/quadratic or a finite--archimedean completion whose sectors alter the geometry together.

## Keep selector existence, support, frequency, quotient order, tangent topology and destination sign distinct

Category enlargement can fail by being too programmable, returning through bounded transforms, erasing the arithmetic orbit as null, projecting a single frequency under positivity, forcing every multi-frequency coefficient to live over the same diffuse support, making the projected positive cone improper after the fixed component is forgotten, leaving dense two-sided fixed-shadow tangents, leaving an explicit graph-defect sequence for every nonzero closable linear orientation, or allowing finite global constraints to be repaired inside the order-bounded tangent core. These are different obstructions.

A new proposal must state which arithmetic information remains invariant, how positivity is formed, what its fixed-point component is, where every useful frequency is supported, whether the post-quotient cone is proper, what topology controls the fixed-shadow tangent set, whether the defining source relation changes the **closure** of the order-bounded tangent core rather than merely breaking literal cutoff invariance, and—if the readout is unbounded—whether its graph is closable. A successful linear relation must fail one of those gates for a source-forced reason and carry the final Weil sign through the resulting cross-scale coupling; noncentrality, ambient positivity, gauge-band selection, absence of source spectral projections, unboundedness, cutoff instability and finite-dimensional global memory alone are no longer enough.
