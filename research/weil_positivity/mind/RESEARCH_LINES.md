## Find a sign mechanism that survives scalar reconstruction after normalization

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-039-stationary-negative-type-transport-needs-quadratic-drift-for-weil-tail-control` through `MI-050-arithmetic-cusp-normalization-exhausts-the-order-one-scalar-gauge-but-leaves-divisor-reconstruction`.

The stationary-geometry results show that the Weil log-square cannot be manufactured for free by oscillatory negative-type structure: a scalar or fixed-positive-functional readout obtains the required quadratic arithmetic direction only when that direction is already present in the quadratic sector. Singular-character tangents merely relocate the same target square to a boundary state.

The Maaß--Selberg/congruence branch gives complementary matched-control obstructions. Positive truncated norms and boundary squares can be divisor-blind; arithmetic congruence scattering can carry independent reciprocal inner factors; and bad-prime logarithmic-derivative blocks have one strict sign. WP-350 closes unitary moving compression, while WP-351 shows that canonical proper-contraction defect positivity is exactly blind to the common unimodular scalar zeta phase.

WP-352 closes the most direct cross-parameter Berry/Chern repair. Projective eigenspace geometry cannot see a common scalar factor; the full scalar Maurer--Cartan term is exact and central; complex scalar curvature is flat away from the divisor; and singular Poincare--Lelong curvature is exactly the divisor itself. Its zero-free reciprocal control `exp(a(s-1/2))` preserves reciprocal symmetry, critical-line unitarity, zeros/poles, divisor current and all projective geometry while shifting the critical-line phase generator by `a`.

WP-353 now classifies that residual control in the natural finite-order category. Every entire nowhere-zero order-at-most-one reciprocal/unitary control with central normalization is exactly `exp(a(s-1/2))`. Standard width-one arithmetic cusp normalization can remove this one real dilation parameter, so the scalar gauge is not unavoidable in the normalized arithmetic problem.

That closure narrows rather than solves the sign problem. Once the arithmetic normalization fixes the exponential gauge, an order-one scalar normalizer is determined by its divisor plus normalization. Scalar phase recovery has therefore collapsed to canonical-product/divisor reconstruction, not an independently positive source mechanism. The matched conformally compact category retains exactly the exponential boundary-scale gauge, confirming that its removal comes from extra arithmetic normalization rather than abstract scattering positivity.

The surviving category is consequently sharper than “use a connection,” “fix the phase,” or “allow nonunitarity.” A successful route must force positivity **before scalar reconstruction**: through noncentral finite--archimedean/global coupling, matrix/operator structure whose sign is not scalar-gauge inheritance, or an independently positive larger object whose projection yields the Weil form without encoding the zeta divisor as the source of its sign.

## Keep source dependence distinct from sign coercivity

Arithmetic labels, self-adjoint realization, unitary scattering, moving channel choice, contraction defects, projective curvature and even a canonically normalized scalar phase are all source-dependent structures. Yet each can either admit matched controls or reduce to reconstruction of already-present divisor data. They are not Weil positivity merely because they are canonical, positive or geometric.

The next proposal should state exactly which scalar phase or divisor coordinate survives its quotient, which higher-order/CDD/noncentral controls remain admissible, what source normalization removes, and which independent theorem forces the required sign. Algebraic division by known local factors, a reducing subspace, scalar-gauge-blind defects, projective curvature, Poincare--Lelong recovery of the divisor or divisor-plus-normalization reconstruction are representation mechanisms rather than independently generated positivity.
