## Find a source-forced sign mechanism that survives scalar reconstruction after normalization

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-039-enriched-semigroups-inherit-obstructed-scalar-shadows-or-universal-lifts` through `MI-051-nonreversible-positivity-can-carry-orientation-without-forcing-it`.

The stationary-geometry results show that the Weil log-square cannot be manufactured for free by oscillatory negative-type structure: a scalar or fixed-positive-functional readout obtains the required quadratic arithmetic direction only when that direction is already present in the quadratic sector. Singular-character tangents merely relocate the same target square to a boundary state.

The Maaß--Selberg/congruence branch gives complementary matched-control obstructions. Positive truncated norms and boundary squares can be divisor-blind; arithmetic congruence scattering can carry independent reciprocal inner factors; and bad-prime logarithmic-derivative blocks have one strict sign. WP-350 closes unitary moving compression, while WP-351 shows that canonical proper-contraction defect positivity is exactly blind to the common unimodular scalar zeta phase.

WP-352--WP-355 classify the main finite-dimensional phase-geometry repairs. Projective Berry/Chern geometry cannot see a common scalar factor; the full scalar Maurer--Cartan term is exact and central; normalized order-one scalar gauges reduce to `exp(a(s-1/2))`; every conjugation-invariant positive quadratic metric orthogonally splits scalar center from traceless directions and therefore sees only the **square** of determinant-phase velocity; and non-reversible Finsler positivity can store central orientation only through a freely chosen closed odd term whose sign may be reversed without losing positivity.

WP-356 tests the most literal arithmetic escape left by the earlier Maaß--Selberg matched controls: couple a finite-prime Hecke correspondence to the cusp boundary **before** taking the positive square. On the constant Fourier mode, the standard self-adjoint prime Hecke operator becomes

`T_p = S_(log p)+S_(-log p)`

after the natural unitary change to log height. For the sharp cusp projection `P_tau`, the boundary defect satisfies the exact operator identity

`[P_tau,T_p]^*[P_tau,T_p] = 1_(tau-log p, tau+log p]`.

So the source-native positive form really does recover the prime logarithmic scale, but only as unsigned annulus energy. On the critical Eisenstein wave its value is

`4 log p + 2 Re(phi(t)e^(-2it tau) sin(2t log p)/t)`.

The scattering/divisor information is entirely in the sign-indefinite interference term; averaging in the truncation height erases it and leaves `4 log p`. Continuous nonarithmetic translations have the same positive annulus identity, and arbitrary unit-modulus scattering phases preserve positivity. Subtracting the universal background isolates the phase-sensitive term only by abandoning the inherited sign.

The same computation exposes a separate critical-weight obstruction. The self-adjoint Hecke normalization and cusp Jacobian cancel to unit translations, so the positive square supplies `log p` but no surviving critical `p^(-1/2)` attenuation. A scalar rescaling would have to insert a `p^(-1/4)` factor at vector level because the norm squares amplitudes; the tested cusp geometry does not select it.

This closes the literal **constant-mode Hecke × sharp truncation boundary × positive norm** route without reopening the earlier divisor-blindness issue. The residual automorphic frontier is narrower: nonconstant Fourier modes, several Hecke operators before scalarization, nonlocal operator order, higher-rank/adelic truncation, or a source theorem that installs the critical half-density and orientation at vector level before the positive square. Any such proposal must explain why its source-sensitive component survives the same continuous-translation and unitary-phase controls.

## Keep source dependence distinct from sign coercivity

Arithmetic labels, self-adjoint realization, unitary scattering, moving channel choice, contraction defects, projective curvature, a canonically normalized scalar phase, a bi-invariant positive matrix metric, a non-reversible positive Finsler metric and even a genuine prime Hecke/cusp-boundary commutator are all source-dependent or canonical-looking structures. Yet each can admit matched controls, discard scalar orientation, reduce to divisor reconstruction, or retain source information only in a signed piece not protected by positivity. They are not Weil positivity merely because they are canonical, positive or geometric.

WP-356 sharpens the audit: inserting arithmetic **before** positivity is necessary but still not sufficient. One must track what the positive closure actually retains. Here the Hecke label survives only as the annulus width `log p`, while the scattering phase and desired one-power half-density do not survive with the required sign/weight.

The next proposal should state exactly which scalar phase or divisor coordinate survives its quotient, which higher-order/CDD/noncentral controls remain admissible, what source normalization removes, whether finite-prime and archimedean data couple before positivity, whether the positive closure preserves or squares the desired arithmetic amplitude, and what fixes any surviving orientation independently of the target Weil sign. A source-defined positive object that collapses to universal annulus geometry is still a representation mechanism rather than an independently generated sign theorem.
