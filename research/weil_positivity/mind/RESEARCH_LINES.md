# Weil-positivity research lines

This file holds the current mathematical lines of investigation suggested by the durable Weil-positivity intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Fix the critical prime-layer finite part intrinsically and regulator-covariantly before claiming a Weil geometry

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-positivity-needs-a-sign-producing-global-operation`, `MI-010-source-forced-critical-gram-can-have-the-wrong-weil-orientation`, `MI-011-prime-ray-marginals-do-not-determine-mixed-prime-coupling`, `MI-012-automorphic-multiplicity-does-not-prevent-global-scalarization`, `MI-013-critical-sector-transport-needs-new-global-arithmetic-structure`.

WP-216--WP-255 show that canonical gauge/Toeplitz/Morita/KMS routes either miss the critical sector, scalarize the needed relation, or cannot pack independent prime channels into a normalized `KMS_1` state. WP-256--WP-260 then produce the exact Mangoldt half-density in symmetric Fock space but show that the most obvious convex/Gibbs pairing has the wrong occupation factor.

WP-261--WP-263 separate closability, programmability, and critical normalization. WP-264 shows that the pre-normalization failure hides a real global geometry: after subtracting the divergent diagonal coefficient, the finite part

\[
R=aa^*-2D
\]

has exactly one positive direction and strict negativity on the canonical primitive hyperplane. But the shifted family

\[
R_C=aa^*-2D+CD_W
\]

shows that the sign theorem does not determine the finite constant.

WP-265 makes that ambiguity concrete rather than hypothetical. Two standard regulators generated by the same source Hamiltonian land on opposite sides of the `C=0` inertia boundary under leading-only subtraction. The sharp prime cutoff gives

\[
C=B_1>0
\]

and therefore **infinite positive index**, while Gibbs/Abel damping gives

\[
C=B_1-\gamma<0
\]

and therefore **exactly one positive direction**. Making both regulators converge to `R_0` requires adding different finite constants whose difference is `\gamma`.

The live theorem must consequently supply a source-derived finite-part law **before** comparison with the Weil target and explain its behavior under regulator change. A viable boundary, quotient, cohomological, or archimedean completion should either select one regulator for an independent geometric reason or produce a regulator-covariant completion whose finite-plus-archimedean form is invariant. Choosing the finite term because it restores the desired index or coefficient is target-driven regularization.

## Treat coefficient sourcing, interaction, closability, critical normalization, regulator choice, finite-part canonicity, and Weil orientation separately

The Fock hierarchy solves coefficient sourcing and symmetric-sector criticality. Complete incidence supplies a genuine mixed-prime finite part, but its qualitative signature is not even invariant under the two natural leading-only regularizations tested in WP-265.

A Hodge-like index at one chosen scheme is therefore weaker than a canonical arithmetic metric. Future progress must identify both **why the sign holds** and **why the source fixes this regulator/finite scale or compensates its regulator transformation in the completed form**.
