# Weil-positivity research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Weil-positivity intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Find a genuinely noncentral finite--archimedean sign mechanism that survives critical specialization and scale globalization

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-039-enriched-semigroups-inherit-obstructed-scalar-shadows-or-universal-lifts` through `MI-052-rankin-selberg-positive-grams-die-at-the-weil-half-density`.

WP-356--WP-357 classify the finite constant-mode Hecke route: cusp-boundary defects reduce to universal interval-overlap/Brownian geometry, so finite arithmetic operator structure survives before positivity but becomes nonselective after positive closure.

WP-358 shows that nonconstant Fourier modes retain genuine arithmetic information. For prime `p`, the angular range projection is exactly divisibility by `p`. Yet the sharp boundary square scalarizes that information to density `1/p`; the desired Weil coefficient has critical half-density `p^(-1/2)`. The canonical positive quadratic form therefore squares the critical amplitude rather than preserving it.

WP-359--WP-362 classify scalar/smooth repairs. Fixed smooth-height interference does not preserve the critical carrier; all-prime critical amplitudes fail ordinary `l2` completion; the Eisenstein eigenline is scalar; and the completed logarithmic derivative recovers the desired Mangoldt half-density only by importing the zeta divisor and abandoning inherited positivity.

WP-363--WP-365 test increasingly canonical positive geometry. The finite Rankin--Selberg Gram dies at the critical exponent; projectivization leaves unbounded overlap and negative Fubini--Study geometry near critical-line zeros; and tensoring with an independent positive Bessel/Whittaker Hilbert Gram generates the completed zeta factors but does not preserve Hilbert positivity through critical specialization.

WP-366 retains genuine finite--archimedean incidence at fixed physical height through `lambda_r(n)K_(ir)(2 pi nY)`, but canonical positive scalar dilation-covariant globalization forces a power-Haar measure and Mellin-separates the construction back into Rankin--Selberg/Bessel geometry. At critical half-density the positive diagonal already diverges.

WP-367 closes a much larger version of the same escape. Replace the scalar height measure by an arbitrary positive-semidefinite radial sesquilinear form `b` applied componentwise to every Whittaker coordinate and assume only exact dilation homogeneity

`b(D_a f,D_a g)=a^(-delta)b(f,g)`.

At every finite Fourier cutoff the form factors exactly as

`E_(delta,X)(r,u)=b(F_r,F_u) sum_(n<=X) conj(lambda_r(n)) lambda_u(n) n^(-delta)`.

Thus nonlocal kernels, differential energies and Sobolev-type radial geometries still lose the `nY` incidence once the same exactly homogeneous radial form is used on every component. At `delta=1/2`, positivity gives a sharp null-or-divergent dichotomy: a positive radial norm makes the Rankin--Selberg diagonal diverge, while zero radial norm makes the entire spectral row and column null.

The matched fixed-height point-evaluation control shows that positivity itself is not the culprit. The obstruction is **componentwise common scale globalization together with exact homogeneity**. The remaining frontier is therefore genuinely noncentral: cross-Fourier coupling, source-forced nonhomogeneous scale interaction, operator-valued mixing not reducible to one common radial form, a boundary/counterterm remainder whose sign is fixed independently, or an indefinite/intersection/cohomological sign theorem.

## Keep arithmetic content, local positivity, tensorization, scale globalization and critical sign coercivity separate

The current audit distinguishes finite Hecke structure, nonconstant divisibility projections, finite--archimedean incidence, common versus cross-index radial geometry, exact dilation homogeneity, scalar and operator globalization, critical overlap blowup and sign coercivity.

WP-365 removes the objection that the previous failure came from omitting archimedean Hilbert geometry. WP-366 removes the objection that literal `nY` coupling by itself survives canonical scalar averaging. WP-367 removes the broader objection that one merely needs a more sophisticated positive radial norm: if it is common componentwise and exactly dilation-homogeneous, the finite index factors out before any infinite-limit issue appears.

A proposal should therefore state where the critical `p^(-1/2)` or `(log p)p^(-1/2)` amplitude lives before positive closure, which operation treats the auxiliary scale, whether that operation is common across Fourier indices, what exact symmetry it obeys, which non-product degrees of freedom survive it, and what theorem fixes their orientation independently of the divisor being constrained. More elaborate common radial positivity is now classified rather than a new route.
