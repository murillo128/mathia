# MI-013 — Critical coherence needs a nonlocal arithmetic sampling law, not merely a larger host or scalar sign repair

**Evidence level:** supported by the exact carrier/category/sign/compression results through WP-273; a completed Weil-positive correspondence remains open.

WP-267--WP-269 separate source coherence, critical-amplitude hostability, and positive spectral hostability. A source-generated rapid-energy rigging can host the singular critical amplitude while the exact positive Mangoldt spectrum still lies beyond tempered distributions and Fourier hyperfunctions.

WP-270 tests the canonical route back to ordinary distributions. After zeta-pole subtraction the completed carrier is `T_pole=nu_(1/2)-cosh(x/2) dx`, and its temperedness is equivalent to RH. Thus classical pole cancellation plus polynomial growth is not an unconditional analytic cleanup step.

WP-271--WP-272 show that even a legal carrier does not acquire the right sign through finite-order conditional positivity or continuous scalar spectral filtering. A continuous multiplier that makes `q T_pole` positive must annihilate every Mangoldt atom; a nonnegative continuous square filter therefore vanishes identically. Scalar sign correction and arithmetic retention are incompatible in that class.

WP-273 moves beyond scalar multipliers and asks what an arbitrary positive **compression** of the same signed carrier would mean. In the canonical Krein decomposition `H_+ direct-sum H_-`, every nonnegative subspace is the graph of a contraction over its arithmetic projection. For function-generated ranges this is exactly a weighted prime-power sampling inequality: the continuum norm against `cosh(x/2) dx` must be controlled by the discrete Mangoldt samples.

This gives a sharp localizability obstruction. A nonzero continuous test space closed under smooth compact cutoffs cannot satisfy the inequality, because any nonzero function can be localized to an interval containing no prime-power atom, where the positive sampling term is zero and the continuum term remains negative. On a bounded interval containing only `N` atoms, a supported positive subspace has dimension at most `N`.

The surviving category is therefore not merely “non-scalar.” It must be **globally nonlocal in exactly the way a sampling/interpolation space is nonlocal**: analytic/model/de Branges-type structure, a distributional source-derived range, or another canonical law that reconstructs continuum mass from arithmetic samples and forbids arbitrary localization. Such a law would change the coupling before scalar sign reading without deleting the source atoms.

**Boundary.** WP-273 does not prove that a suitable global sampling space exists or that its contraction follows from the zeta source. It does not rule out matrix/noncommutative completions, singular boundary maps, or a different finite--archimedean carrier. It rules out localizable function-space compression and identifies the exact reconstruction burden any surviving positive compression must carry.
