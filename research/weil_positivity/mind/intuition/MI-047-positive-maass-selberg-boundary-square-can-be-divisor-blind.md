# MI-047 — A positive Maaß--Selberg boundary square can be divisor-blind even inside one-cusp self-adjoint hyperbolic scattering

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed synthesis from [WP-237](../../findings/WP-237-arthur-height-operations-cannot-isolate-the-zeta-zero-block.md), [WP-287](../../findings/WP-287-nonlinear-height-postprocessing-does-not-escape-linear-weil-distribution.md), [WP-345](../../findings/WP-345-maass-selberg-height-derivative-is-a-positive-boundary-square-but-does-not-constrain-the-scattering-divisor.md), [WP-346](../../findings/WP-346-self-adjoint-robin-scattering-realizes-cdd-control-with-positive-full-truncated-norm.md), and [WP-347](../../findings/WP-347-self-adjoint-point-scatterers-show-one-cusp-hyperbolic-realizability-does-not-fix-the-scattering-divisor.md).

The rank-one Maaß--Selberg relation supplies a genuine source-defined positive object. If `N_T(r)` is the truncated Eisenstein norm and `tau=log T`, then on the unitary axis `partial_tau N_(e^tau)(r)=|1+S(r)e^(-ir tau)|^2>=0`. But the same operation deletes the `T`-independent logarithmic-derivative term `m'/m` whose explicit-formula decomposition carries the zero divisor. The sign-visible boundary response and divisor-sensitive carrier live in different pieces of the formula.

WP-345 gives a scalar matched control: inner/CDD factors preserve reciprocal symmetry and unitary-axis modulus while moving the meromorphic divisor, so every direct boundary-square inequality survives. WP-346 then realizes the elementary CDD control with concrete self-adjoint Robin channels whose full truncated Hilbert norms remain positive. Generic self-adjointness therefore does not couple norm positivity to the sign of the divisor-sensitive finite part.

WP-347 moves the control into the geometric category that remained open. A real point interaction on a fixed finite-volume **one-cusp hyperbolic surface** is a genuine self-adjoint extension. Its perturbed Eisenstein coefficient satisfies the exact relative-scattering identity

`phi_(alpha,z0)(s)=phi(s) S_(alpha,z0)(1-s)/S_(alpha,z0)(s)`.

The relative factor is source-defined by the regularized automorphic Green function and coupling constant, obeys the reciprocal functional identity, and changes the continued resonance divisor. Self-adjointness constrains nonreal `L^2` eigenvalues on the physical side, but it does not freeze resonances in `Re s<1/2`. The same one-cusp geometry, positive Hilbert space, Eisenstein channel and relative trace formalism therefore coexist with movable continued scattering data.

This closes the inference that **hyperbolic automorphic realizability plus self-adjoint positivity** is enough to make the positive Maaß--Selberg architecture divisor-selective. The point scatterer preserves much more structure than the Robin toy while still introducing a nontrivial relative divisor. What it does not preserve is precisely the strongest arithmetic structure: generic point interaction breaks Hecke symmetry and replaces the original Euler/local-global data by a Green-function spectral factor and diffractive terms.

The surviving escape is therefore specifically arithmetic. A viable positivity theorem must use a rigidity principle absent from the point scatterer—Hecke equivariance, Euler-product/local-global characterization, a canonical arithmetic correspondence, or an operator-level coupling that cannot be deformed by rank-one self-adjoint extension while preserving its hypotheses. Merely asking that the scattering coefficient arise from a genuine one-cusp hyperbolic self-adjoint system is no longer enough.

The reusable audit principle is now stronger. When a positive scattering identity is proposed as source leverage, test matched deformations **inside the claimed source category**, not only abstract inner functions or free channels. If a source-defined self-adjoint deformation preserves the positivity architecture while changing the continued divisor, then the missing selectivity lies in whatever extra structure the deformation destroys.

**Boundary.** The point scatterer is not Hecke-equivariant and does not preserve the original Euler product, so WP-347 does not rule out a genuinely arithmetic Maaß--Selberg theorem. Nor does it assert that every resonance motion realizes an arbitrary CDD factor. It shows that one-cusp hyperbolic geometry, self-adjointness, positive Hilbert norms and Eisenstein/trace realizability remain too broad; the residual route must use arithmetic rigidity in an essential, sign-producing way.
