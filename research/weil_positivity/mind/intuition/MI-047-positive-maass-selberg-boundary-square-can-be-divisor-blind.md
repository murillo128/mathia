# MI-047 — A positive Maaß--Selberg boundary square can be divisor-blind even inside self-adjoint scattering

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed synthesis from [WP-237](../../findings/WP-237-arthur-height-operations-cannot-isolate-the-zeta-zero-block.md), [WP-287](../../findings/WP-287-nonlinear-height-postprocessing-does-not-escape-linear-weil-distribution.md), [WP-345](../../findings/WP-345-maass-selberg-height-derivative-is-a-positive-boundary-square-but-does-not-constrain-the-scattering-divisor.md), and [WP-346](../../findings/WP-346-self-adjoint-robin-scattering-realizes-cdd-control-with-positive-full-truncated-norm.md).

The rank-one Maaß--Selberg relation supplies a genuine source-defined positive object. If `N_T(r)` is the truncated Eisenstein norm and `tau=log T`, then on the unitary axis

`partial_tau N_(e^tau)(r) = |1+S(r)e^(-ir tau)|^2 >= 0`.

This is stronger than a formal positivity rewrite: it is the actual logarithmic-height derivative of a geometric Hilbert norm. But the same operation deletes the `T`-independent logarithmic-derivative term `m'/m` whose explicit-formula decomposition carries the zero divisor together with prime and archimedean contributions. The sign-visible boundary response and the divisor-sensitive carrier live in different pieces of the Maaß--Selberg formula.

WP-345 gives a decisive matched control. Multiply a scalar meromorphic scattering function by an inner/CDD factor that preserves reciprocal symmetry and unitary-axis modulus while inserting an off-axis zero/pole pair. Every boundary-square inequality remains true because it depends only on the unitary boundary phase, while the logarithmic derivative changes by the added divisor term. Thus **positivity of the direct height boundary square is insensitive to exactly the divisor motion a Weil mechanism must constrain**.

WP-346 removes the simplest objection that this control is merely formal non-Hilbert scattering. On a half-line with a self-adjoint Robin boundary condition, the reflection factor is `(z-a)/(z+a)`; the determinant of two identical channels realizes the squared CDD factor of WP-345 exactly. Each channel has a positive full truncated `L^2` norm and a positive boundary square. Nevertheless the logarithmic-derivative finite term isolated from that norm need not be positive, and the CDD zero/pole pair is still present.

So generic self-adjointness does not couple the positive norm to the sign of the divisor-sensitive finite part. Nor does positivity of the *full* truncated norm imply positivity of each term in its decomposition. The matched control now preserves the natural Hilbert-space architecture, unitary-axis scattering and complete norm positivity, not only scalar inner-function identities.

The surviving place for genuine source structure is consequently narrower and more precise: **automorphic realizability must forbid or correlate the CDD freedom for a reason absent from generic self-adjoint scattering**. A successful Maaß--Selberg route must use the complete finite part together with Euler--Gamma/local-global structure, noncentral operator data, correlations between automorphic sectors, or a higher-rank truncation form whose sign theorem acts directly on the divisor-sensitive coordinate.

This sharpens the scalarization audit. A source-derived positive square is not automatically selective merely because it comes from the correct global geometric object, and a positive self-adjoint Hilbert norm is not enough merely because it contains the logarithmic derivative in its finite part. One must test whether the sign theorem itself survives deformations that preserve the positivity architecture while changing the target divisor.

The lesson is complementary to the fixed-boundary-state obstruction of MI-046. There the desired quadratic form was already encoded in a common scalar boundary readout. Here the positive boundary response is genuinely source-derived, but its sign is **too universal**: divisor-changing inner freedom survives even inside a concrete self-adjoint positive-norm realization.

**Boundary.** The Robin channels of WP-346 are self-adjoint but not automorphic scattering matrices. WP-345--WP-346 therefore do not rule out a source-realizability theorem using the complete Maaß--Selberg finite part or operator-level finite--archimedean coupling. They rule out the weaker inference that unitary-axis symmetry, self-adjoint Hilbert-space realization and positivity of the full/boundary truncated norm are themselves enough to control the scattering divisor.
