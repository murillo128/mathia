# MI-003 — Arithmetic selection can survive at zero order while canonical positive completion and local finite-energy normalization erase its global use

**Evidence level:** supported through WP-150 by exact resultant, inertia, graph-Dirichlet, effective-resistance, and finite-core normalization controls

## Core intuition

The zero-order cyclotomic resultant is unusually faithful to finite Weil arithmetic: it retains prime-power support and the critical half-density amplitudes. Yet the simplest positive completions exhibit a three-way conflict. Keeping the original interaction makes the all-prime energy domain collapse; normalizing locally enough to restore finite fixed-shell energy erases every fixed arithmetic edge; and weaker normalization leaves the divergence.

Thus positivity, a nontrivial global domain, and retention of the sparse prime selector cannot be obtained independently by post-processing the same resultant graph. The interaction or assembly must change before spectator-prime proliferation has already destroyed one of those resources.

## Strongest justified principle

WP-140--WP-145 separate generic positive scale responses from the exact zero-order cyclotomic resultant. The resultant carries the desired sparse prime-power amplitudes, while its sign-flipped Hessian fills in missing support and loses the selector.

WP-146--WP-147 keep the zero-order kernel and test finite repair. Mixed-prime chains already have centered negative directions, and spectator-prime replication makes both primitive inertia indices unbounded. Fixed rank, fixed codimension, and fixed finite-dimensional auxiliary/archimedean Schur sectors cannot repair the sign.

WP-148 gives the canonical positive finite-cutoff completion: conservation plus off-diagonal interaction `-J` forces the weighted graph Laplacian. Every shell nevertheless has infinite fresh-prime degree in the full alphabet. WP-149 strengthens that domain obstruction: fresh spectator primes provide infinitely many exact parallel bypasses around every resultant edge, driving effective resistance to zero and forcing every finite-energy function to be constant, independently of faithful changes of vertex measure.

WP-150 closes the natural local renormalization escape. For any positive vertex-local diagonal congruence of the cutoff Laplacians, bounded energy on every fixed shell forces the local scale at that shell to zero and hence sends every fixed off-diagonal resultant coefficient to zero. Every finite-core cluster limit is diagonal. In particular, the symmetric normalized Laplacian tends to the identity on fixed finite cores; for degree powers `D^{-alpha}`, `alpha<1/2` leaves divergence, `alpha=1/2` gives the identity core, and `alpha>1/2` gives zero.

The theorem deliberately does not assert strong operator convergence: normalized adjacency mass may escape to shell indices tending to infinity. That possible escape is a different assembly topology and would need an independent source interpretation.

## What remains possible

A surviving positive completion must alter the interaction or identification before the all-prime spectator network forms. Live categories include pair-dependent/nonlocal renormalization, cutoff-following test spaces with a source-forced limiting interpretation, provenance-sensitive quotients that remove spectator replication, genuinely mixed-prime interactions, and nonseparable finite--archimedean/cohomological assembly.

Any such route must derive the selector, dense/nontrivial domain, normalization, and sign together. Subtracting spectator conductance or choosing a moving basis merely because it restores the desired coefficients is additional structure, not inherited positivity.

## Status / novelty

Cyclotomic resultants, graph Laplacians, normalized Laplacians, electrical networks, and finite-rank inertia are classical. The persisted synthesis is the selector/sign/domain boundary: **the faithful sparse resultant is indefinite; its canonical positive all-prime energy is short-circuited; and every vertex-local finite-energy normalization erases the fixed sparse interaction**.

## Falsification criterion

Construct a nonconstant finite-energy function for the unchanged WP-149 network, or a vertex-local congruence with bounded fixed-shell energies and a nonvanishing fixed resultant edge, contradicting WP-150. A nonlocal, cutoff-following, or interaction-changing construction would evade rather than falsify the boundary.
