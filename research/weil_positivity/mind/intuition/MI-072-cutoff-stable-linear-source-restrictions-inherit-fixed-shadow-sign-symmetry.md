# MI-072 — Cutoff-stable linear source restrictions inherit fixed-shadow sign symmetry

**Evidence level:** exact operator-algebraic source-restriction synthesis from [WP-400](../../findings/WP-400-centralizer-local-linear-source-relations-inherit-dense-sign-symmetric-tangents.md), extending the fixed-shadow tangent obstruction of MI-071.

MI-071 leaves one natural escape: perhaps the arithmetic source restricts the centered noncentral directions to a proper linear subspace `V`, so the two-sided tangents generated by ambient positivity no longer remain source-admissible. WP-400 identifies a broad class for which that escape still fails.

Let `d` be the fixed positive centralizer shadow and `e_n=1_[1/n,infinity)(d)` its canonical spectral cutoffs. If the real linear source space `V` is stable under these localizations, `k in V => e_n k e_n in V`, then every `k in V` is the strong and ultraweak limit of source-admissible directions that are order-bounded by `d`. Each such cutoff supports both signs `d+/-epsilon e_n k e_n>=0` for small enough `epsilon`.

Therefore every normal real linear functional whose sign is supposed to follow from positivity on the source-restricted fixed-shadow fibre must vanish on `V` and on its ultraweak closure. Selecting a proper linear source subspace does not create an orientation when the relation survives the same spectral localization that drives the WP-399 tangent-density argument.

Centralizer bimodules and fixed nonzero gauge-frequency selections satisfy this locality condition automatically. They may encode real arithmetic organization, but positivity plus the linear admissibility rule still sees a dense both-sign tangent family. A viable linear source relation must instead couple information nonlocally across spectral scales of `d`, so that canonical cutoff destroys a specific source-forced relation; that destroyed cross-scale term is then the actual candidate orientation carrier.

**Boundary.** WP-400 is a bounded, normal, first-order theorem for linear cutoff-stable source restrictions. It does not rule out linear relations that genuinely fail spectral localization, nonlinear admissibility constraints or readouts, singular/non-normal functionals, unbounded/domain-sensitive constructions, continuous-core changes, or finite--archimedean coupling. The theorem narrows the source-relation escape rather than eliminating every possible one.
