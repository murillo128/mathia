# MI-063 — The canonical divisibility diagonal cannot hide a prime-power selector

**Evidence level:** exact C-star-algebraic synthesis from [WP-391](../../findings/WP-391-bost-connes-divisibility-diagonal-cannot-isolate-prime-power-support.md), interpreted against the source-forced Bost--Connes Gram of WP-216 and the programmable stationary masks of WP-390. Finite projection algebras and norm closure are classical; the line-specific content is the exact selector obstruction for the canonical divisibility diagonal.

In the standard positive-energy representation, the canonical range projections satisfy

`E_n epsilon_k = 1_(n|k) epsilon_k`.

Let `D_div=C^*(E_n:n>=1)`. Any finite-stage observable in this algebra sees only finitely many divisibility predicates, so multiplying a label by a sufficiently fresh prime leaves all of those predicates unchanged. Norm closure turns that finite-stage blindness into an exact no-go: if `A in D_div` vanishes on every integer divisible by at least two distinct primes, then `A=0`.

Thus the same source-native diagonal that carries the Bost--Connes divisibility semilattice cannot secretly contain a nonzero exact prime-power mask. This is categorically different from arbitrary Hamiltonian functional calculus `F(H)`, which can interpolate any desired diagonal values, and from the stationary Ramanujan interface of WP-390, where the prime-power selector exists precisely as an explicitly programmable spectral mask.

The Bost--Connes route therefore cannot earn source-selection credit merely by staying diagonal. A surviving mechanism must leave `D_div` through source-forced off-diagonal, modular/GNS, unbounded/affiliated, finite--archimedean or other category-changing structure, and then still explain the Weil orientation and global sign without target coding.

**Boundary.** The theorem is specific to the concrete norm-closed divisibility diagonal in the canonical positive-energy representation and to exact mixed-prime nullity. It does not rule out the full Bost--Connes crossed product, other representations, unbounded affiliated operators, approximate suppression, or globally assembled selection. Those categories need independent source-selection and positivity tests.
