# MI-065 — Woronowicz affiliation does not enlarge the unital Bost--Connes source algebra

**Evidence level:** exact classical-category synthesis from [WP-393](../../findings/WP-393-unitality-collapses-woronowicz-affiliation-back-to-bounded-bost-connes-algebra.md), combined with the bounded positive-cone obstruction in [WP-392](../../findings/WP-392-full-bost-connes-positive-cone-cannot-isolate-prime-power-support.md).

Woronowicz affiliation is not automatically an unbounded enlargement. For a unital C-star algebra `A`, the classical affiliation framework gives

`A^eta = A`.

The standard Bost--Connes algebra `A_BC` is unital, so every Woronowicz-affiliated element of `A_BC` is already a bounded element of the same C-star algebra. The `z`-transform formulation makes the mechanism explicit: in the unital case the affiliation density condition forces `(1-z_T^*z_T)^(1/2)` to be invertible, and hence reconstructs a bounded `T` inside `A`.

Consequently, replacing `A in A_BC` by `T eta A_BC` does not evade WP-392. In a standard positive-energy representation, a positive Woronowicz-affiliated `T` whose basis diagonal vanishes on every mixed-prime label is bounded in the original source algebra, so WP-392 forces its representation to vanish.

A meaningful unbounded Bost--Connes escape must therefore change the ambient category in a load-bearing way. Possibilities still outside this obstruction include affiliation with a representation von Neumann algebra under an additional canonical modular/weight law, a source-derived closable form with a nonprogrammable domain, regular operators on a larger Hilbert C-star module or correspondence, or affiliation with a canonically chosen nonunital ideal/boundary algebra. Merely saying “affiliated operator” without changing the unital source algebra changes notation, not the admissible objects.

**Boundary.** WP-393 does not rule out those genuinely different unbounded categories, approximate mixed-prime suppression, finite--archimedean assembly, or a global Weil construction. Passing to a much larger category is useful only if an additional source law prevents arbitrary target-dependent programming.
