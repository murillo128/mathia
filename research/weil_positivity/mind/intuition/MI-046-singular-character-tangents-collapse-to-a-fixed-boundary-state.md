# MI-046 — Singular character tangents collapse to a fixed boundary state

**Evidence level:** exact synthesis from [WP-341](../../findings/WP-341-prime-exponent-cnd-dominance-forces-arithmetic-quadratic-sector.md), [WP-342](../../findings/WP-342-positive-scalarization-cannot-hide-arithmetic-quadratic-drift.md), [WP-343](../../findings/WP-343-uniformly-dominated-moving-readouts-collapse-to-fixed-scalarization.md), and [WP-344](../../findings/WP-344-singular-character-tangent-readout-is-a-fixed-boundary-state-in-disguise.md).

A tempting way around stationary scalarization is to use a family whose interior points are purely oscillatory and extract the desired quadratic form only from a singular tangent. WP-344 shows that the basic character construction does not actually escape.

For `chi_t(alpha)=exp(it h(alpha))`, the normalized chord

`Psi_h(alpha)(t)=|chi_t(alpha)-1|^2/t^2`

has no quadratic Levy--Khinchin sector at any fixed nonzero `t`, yet tends to `h(alpha)^2` as `t->0`. On the `C*`-algebra generated by these functions, all relevant generators extend to the same boundary point. Evaluation there is one fixed positive character/state `epsilon_0`, and

`epsilon_0(Psi_h(alpha))=h(alpha)^2`.

On this family the same boundary evaluation also realizes the operator norm. The apparent moving/singular readout is therefore a fixed scalarization after compactification; the target quadratic direction was encoded in the chosen one-parameter character through `h`.

For prime exponents, choosing `h(alpha)=sum_p alpha_p log p` gives the Weil-facing log-square, but arbitrary weights give their own squares by the identical construction. The mechanism contains no theorem selecting the arithmetic logarithm.

The general audit is stronger than “check finite-dimensional domination.” A proposed singular escape must prove that its target square is not carried by a **common boundary functional** after the relevant operator algebra is completed. Otherwise singularity only moves the fixed readout from the interior to the boundary.

**Boundary.** WP-344 treats a commutative character-tangent architecture and does not rule out genuinely noncommutative, nonstationary or operator-level positivity without a common scalar boundary state. It also does not prove Weil positivity. The durable result is a no-escape test for singular readouts that compactify to one fixed state.
