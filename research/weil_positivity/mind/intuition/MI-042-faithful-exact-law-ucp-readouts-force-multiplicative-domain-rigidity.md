# MI-042 — Faithful exact-law UCP readouts are rigid, but bounded asymptotic topologies miss moving observable energy

**Evidence level:** exact/classical-mechanism synthesis from [WP-329](../../findings/WP-329-deterministic-amalgamated-lifts-make-operator-valued-free-divisibility-universal-before-scalarization.md) through [WP-335](../../findings/WP-335-max-divergence-and-channel-norm-convergence-still-miss-fixed-ucp-variance.md). Kadison--Schwarz, Stinespring dilation and multiplicative-domain theory are classical; the Mathia content is their source-readout boundary at expanding Mangoldt cutoffs.

WP-329--WP-330 show that enriched variables can be universally positive/divisible while all source specificity sits only in an external scalar state or in a fluctuation sector annihilated by that state. WP-331--WP-332 show the opposite: if a faithful UCP readout preserves the exact second moment of the same self-adjoint source observable, the positive Kadison defect must vanish. The observable lies in the multiplicative domain and the off-diagonal Stinespring block that could have carried hidden coupling is zero.

At a finite atomic cutoff the basic stability estimate divides scalar defect by the least visible atom, so approximate rigidity is nonuniform as that atom moves into a thinner tail. WP-333 realizes this inside the intrinsic dyadic prime-power support: a barycentric Markov/UCP move keeps an order-one Kadison defect on a rare moving state while weak scalar metrics and fixed moments converge.

WP-334 shows that no fixed finite averaged information divergence repairs the problem. Even with mutually absolutely continuous laws and bounded likelihood ratios, every fixed finite `f`-divergence and finite Renyi divergence can vanish around the same order-one tail defect. In that construction max-divergence still detected the rare atom, so worst-case likelihood control remained a plausible asymptotic replacement.

WP-335 closes that loophole. A more asymmetric intrinsic three-point move has source-to-output likelihood ratio converging uniformly to one, hence both max-divergences tend to zero. The UCP channel itself also converges to the identity in operator norm on the bounded algebra. Nevertheless the Kadison defect of the logarithmic coordinate is fixed at `(log 2)^2`.

The mechanism is exact: the exceptional transition has vanishing probability but a jump whose squared size grows at the reciprocal scale, so probability times squared jump remains order one. Uniform control on bounded functions cannot see this because the observable `X_R` is itself unbounded with the cutoff. The failure is therefore not another scalar-divergence pathology; it is a **mismatch between the topology and the moving observable's energy scale**.

This changes the asymptotic rigidity principle. Exact same-observable second-moment preservation is rigid at each fixed cutoff, but neither uniform likelihood-ratio convergence nor ordinary channel norm on bounded observables implies approximate multiplicative-domain rigidity for a growing unbounded observable. A usable stability theorem must control the channel in a weighted/graph/energy norm adapted to `X_R`, control the second-moment defect directly, or impose an equivalent relative estimate that prices large jumps on rare states.

The support-preserving construction remains a matched martingale mechanism, not arithmetic leverage. Arithmetic supplies an intrinsic equally spaced thin ray, but the same variance mechanism is generic. A positive Weil route therefore needs a source-forced coupling whose destination sign cannot be reproduced by the matched control and a topology strong on the actual observable, not merely on the bounded ambient algebra.

**Boundary.** WP-335 is a sharp asymptotic counterexample for this same moving coordinate and class of Markov/UCP readouts. It does not prove that a particular weighted graph norm is sufficient for Weil positivity, trivialize the full dilation algebra, or rule out other observables/interfaces. The lesson is scale matching: convergence must be stated in a norm that sees the energy of the object whose multiplicative-domain defect matters.
