# MI-009 — The Gamma phase cannot survive regular positive completion without changing analytic and orientation category

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed passive, analytic-category, Hardy-compression, and ordinary trace-class scattering controls through WP-188

## Core intuition

The passive Gamma boundary is no longer just a scalar rigidity/flexibility problem. WP-182 shows that vector completion can preserve a finite positive form after scalar dissipation, but WP-183--WP-185 show that the **exact Gamma relative phase itself cannot be carried by finite regular bounded/projective analytic completion**. WP-186 shows that pointwise positive-semidefinite encoding retains the phase only as an unoriented metric object, and WP-187 shows that canonical Hardy compression leaves orientation in an indefinite signed difference or a singular trace boundary.

WP-188 closes a different attractive escape in which positivity would have been supplied by self-adjoint order rather than by the boundary phase. For ordinary trace-class Birman--Krein scattering, the spectral shift lies in `L^1`, so the scattering determinant is `L^1`-close to `1`. The exact Gamma phase is not `L^1`-close to any constant phase on a high-energy tail. Therefore even a positive trace-class perturbation, despite having a genuine nonnegative spectral-shift theorem, cannot carry the exact archimedean phase.

A viable completion must change the analytic/projective/scattering category enough to carry the source phase **and** rebuild its orientation/coercivity after the category change. Positivity inherited from a universal lift, unordered leakage energies, or the ordinary trace-class scattering formalism is not enough.

## Strongest justified principle

WP-179--WP-181 classify scalar passive routes. Ordinary determinant and `det_2` inherit one-sided phase order, while higher regularization or dissipative Schur responses can change sign only after losing the corresponding scalar coercivity. WP-182 gives the canonical two-channel escape: a Pythagorean mate absorbs positive boundary defect, but the construction is universal unless the second channel is source-forced.

WP-183--WP-185 show that bounded analytic mixing, bounded-characteristic repair, and any finite bounded-characteristic projective packet with finite rational decoder fail to realize the exact Gamma ratio. WP-186 shows that the universal rank-one PSD lift forgets signed velocity in its positive metric data. WP-187 shows that Hardy compression produces two positive leakage defects, while orientation is their difference; for smooth finite-window Gamma phases that difference is nonzero, trace zero, and indefinite, and the exact phase is too rough for both canonical leakage traces to stay finite.

WP-188 tests ordinary self-adjoint scattering. If a pair has an `L^1` Krein spectral shift, the Birman--Krein determinant satisfies `|det S-1| <= 2 pi |xi|` and is integrably close to `1`. The exact Gamma phase instead winds with asymptotic velocity of logarithmic size and stays a fixed distance from every constant phase on intervals of infinite total measure. This excludes every ordinary trace-class scattering determinant, including the sign-definite `H_1=H_0+B`, `B>=0`, route where spectral-shift positivity would otherwise be genuine.

## Counterevidence / boundary

WP-188 does not rule out generalized or renormalized scattering with only weighted spectral-shift control, resolvent-comparable/domain-changing pairs, regularized determinants, singular relative indices, or a nonseparable global construction coupling finite primes to the real place before determinant reduction. These are real category changes.

But ordinary positive-perturbation sign cannot simply be carried across that boundary. Any regularization subtracting the extensive Gamma tail becomes part of the mechanism and must be source-forced; the larger category needs its own order/coercivity theorem and must preserve finite-prime incidence and signed orientation.

## Epistemic status

**Proved finite regular projective boundary, pointwise/Hardy orientation-loss controls, and ordinary trace-class scattering no-go; open source-forced singular/global category exit.**

## Falsification criterion

Construct an ordinary trace-class Birman--Krein pair whose determinant equals the exact Gamma phase up to constant phase on a full tail despite WP-188's `L^1` obstruction, or violate the earlier finite-projective/Hardy controls in their hypotheses. A genuinely source-forced generalized scattering or singular global completion carrying both arithmetic incidence and a replacement coercive orientation theorem would instead be the intended escape.
