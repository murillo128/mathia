PC-330 closes the most natural smooth growing-complexity repair. PC-331 isolates the exact source-sensitive excess above the universal logarithmic floor. For comparable conductors `q<=r<=2q`, the band-normalized Hilbert--Schmidt Green defect is `log q` plus a positive Hankel/Hardy-square energy of the Fourier magnitude-square profiles. PC-332 gives its exact multiscale form: if `Gamma_a(H)=[1/(H mu(a))] sum_(1<=i<=H)|a_hat(i)|^2`, then the excess is equivalent up to absolute constants to `sum_m Gamma_a(2^m) Gamma_b(2^m)`. A superlogarithmic excess therefore forces an explicit dyadic band with anomalous magnitude concentration, but it still contains no phase information.

PC-333 identifies the remaining band estimate itself. Cyclic Parseval writes moving-window discrepancy variance as the same Fourier magnitude energy with a Dirichlet-kernel weight. At reciprocal scales `H asymp q/L`, uniform control `E_q(H) lesssim H mu_q^res` and the natural short-window variance bound imply each other up to absolute constants, with Fejer/hard cutoffs interchangeable at neighboring scales. After undoing the source normalization this is the finite-cyclic analogue of the classical short-interval prime-variance/Selberg-integral problem.

The scalar Green-Hardy route has therefore reached a **classical autocorrelation boundary**. Proving its surviving band estimate by ordinary analytic-number-theory means would establish a classical short-interval discrepancy theorem in Fourier coordinates, not a new geometric RH mechanism. A substantive prime-circle advance must force that estimate from an intrinsic geometric identity unavailable to the classical variance problem, improve its range/asymptotic materially, retain phase or cross-level information discarded by the magnitude-square quotient, or add an independent zero-sensitive structure.
