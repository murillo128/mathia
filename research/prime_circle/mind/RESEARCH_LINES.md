## Add source-native zero-selecting structure beyond finite-Fourier closure and classical multiple-`L` recoding

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-012-finite-scalar-cross-shell-fusion-is-closed-under-classical-packet-algebra` through `MI-028-canonical-mixed-packet-fails-finite-fourier-self-duality`.

PC-295--PC-310 progressively separate phase retention, classical Fourier/polyspectral data and stable source separation. At the mesh boundary `1-r=Theta(1/q)`, the sampled Poisson field is already an invertible cyclic decoder; single-level recovery there is source reconstruction rather than a new RH-sensitive observable.

PC-311 closes the direct cross-level escape: bilinearly pairing two centered prime-level fields passes to one periodic coefficient on `Z/(qr)Z`, and the double Mellin transform becomes a finite bundle of ordinary `L(s+t,chi)` functions. PC-312 proves that the canonical coefficient is not a finite-Fourier eigenvector. PC-313 then shows that adjoining its Fourier dual gives only the universal Hurwitz swap relation; diagonalizing produces the manually symmetrized scalar `+1/-1` sectors for every even periodic sequence.

PC-314 makes that even post-compression closure decisively unsuitable as an RH-like repair. For the smallest admissible canonical mixed pair `(q,r)=(5,7)`, both source-generated even Fourier eigensectors fail the exceptional `P(s)L(s,chi)` classification, so Saias--Weingartner forces linearly many zeros in strips strictly inside `Re s>1`.

PC-315 closes the remaining chirality loophole. Keeping the unsymmetrized positive-frequency coefficient adds the odd component; because `F_N^2=-I` there, its forced `+i/-i` Fourier eigensectors complete the order-four orbit. For the same canonical `(5,7)` source, both odd sectors also lie outside the exceptional single-`L` class. Thus **all four scalar sectors `+1,-1,+i,-i` of the complete finite-Fourier orbit have infinitely many zeros in `Re s>1`**.

PC-316 tests the most canonical nonlinear escape before scalar cyclic compression. Pointwise multiplication of the two oriented prime-level Hardy fields retains independent Fourier indices `k,l`; propagating the product by the same Poisson semigroup supplies the additive output mode `k+l`. Triple Mellinization therefore produces `sum_(k,l>=1) a(k)b(l)/(k^s l^t (k+l)^u)`. This is genuinely richer than PC-311's one-index collapse, but character decomposition identifies it exactly as a finite bundle of classical character-weighted Mordell--Tornheim double `L`-functions.

PC-317 now identifies where that two-index information is lost under one-parameter radial scalarization. On the common diagonal the additive-convolution coefficient satisfies the exact affine recurrence `c(n+qr)=c(n)+1` for the canonical prime source, so `c(n)=n/(qr)+d(n)` with `d` periodic. Its Mellin transform is therefore one shifted `zeta(s-1)` term plus a finite rational-shift Hurwitz packet. More generally every **rational** one-parameter radial ray has a rational generating function with root-of-unity poles of order at most two, hence eventually quasipolynomial coefficients and the same finite Hurwitz closure. The Mordell--Tornheim information survives only while enough independent radial geometry is retained to avoid that commensurate one-dimensional collapse.

The live construction must now do more than act “before compression.” It must retain a source-forced object not exhausted by the finite-Fourier orbit, immediate reduction to a standard multiple-Dirichlet family, or rational one-dimensional radial Hurwitz recoding, and it must arrive with an independently justified zero-selection theorem. A source-specific theorem for the exact Mordell--Tornheim bundle would be legitimate. So would a genuinely different scalarization, but only after showing that it is neither classical recoding nor source decoding.

## Demand a zero-sensitive destination theorem before interpreting source distinguishability

Stable sub-mesh observations collapse matched sources; mesh-scale one-level observations decode the source; direct bilinear fusion classicalizes; the canonical scalar packet fails self-duality; universal Fourier closure is source-independent; PC-314--PC-315 show that the complete canonical scalar Fourier orbit can have abundant zeros in the half-plane of absolute convergence; PC-316 shows that the simplest pre-compression nonlinearity lands in classical Mordell--Tornheim theory; and PC-317 shows that every rational one-parameter radial projection of that nonlinear field collapses again to finite Hurwitz data.

A viable observable must therefore state exactly what additional source-native quantity survives these reductions and which theorem converts that quantity into zero confinement. Functional-equation symmetry, orientation/parity completion, additional Mellin variables, rational radial slicing, or the mere label “nonlinear coupling” are not destination criteria. Future mixed-conductor proposals should be reduced completely before their analytic complexity is interpreted as new information.
