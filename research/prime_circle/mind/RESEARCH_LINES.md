## Leave one-shell reciprocal Fourier zeros unless a source principle selects the deformation

**Linked intuition:** `MI-011-scalar-fourier-zero-existence-is-deformation-dependent`.

PC-193--PC-194 already showed that scalar Fourier sign/zero existence in the minimally renormalized reciprocal family depends on the exponent: prime powers are strictly zero-free for `0<alpha<=1`, while large prime shells acquire zeros at `alpha=2`.

PC-195--PC-196 now close the immediate quantitative-location escape. At the quadratic exponent the first prime-shell zero lives on the mesoscopic scale `p^(3/4)` and the next on scale `p`, but the `p^(3/4)` law is a singular even-exponent resonance caused by cancellation of the leading outer Fourier tail. For every fixed `1<alpha<2`, no zero can occur on any `o(p)` scale; near `alpha=2` the surviving zeros instead live on the ordinary boundary-layer scale `Theta(p)`.

Thus neither zero existence nor the first-zero scaling is an intrinsic one-shell spectrum of the Prime-Circle geometry. A useful scalar spectral law now needs a source-forced exponent/operator and a relation that survives the natural deformation controls; choosing `alpha=2` because it creates an attractive mesoscopic zero is not admissible.

## Demand cross-shell or multi-carrier structure before assigning spectral meaning

Prime-power shells inherit the same reciprocal-zero geometry by deterministic cyclotomic dilation. The immediate one-shell location laws therefore add no independent zero-divisor information.

A surviving spectral mechanism should couple different shells, different intrinsic carriers, or another source-native operation before scalar Fourier reduction. The relation must have a matched control that does not follow automatically from boundary/refinement asymptotics or cyclotomic scaling.

## Require quantitative deformation stability of any proposed spectral relation

A candidate cross-shell relation should be tested under the reciprocal exponent and other source-compatible scalarizations. Stability does not mean that individual zero locations remain fixed; it means that the claimed arithmetic discriminator survives after the representation-dependent boundary layer has been factored out.

The interval `1<alpha<2` need not be classified pointwise unless the construction itself selects it. The exact lesson from PC-196 is already enough to reject the quadratic `p^(3/4)` scale as a robust arithmetic invariant.

## Keep topology and realization separate from scalar cyclotomic identities

Potential-theoretic, geometric, or operator enlargements remain live only when the required object exists canonically and retains information beyond the classical cyclotomic/Euler data. A new coordinate system for the same one-shell scalar amplitude is not a new mechanism.
