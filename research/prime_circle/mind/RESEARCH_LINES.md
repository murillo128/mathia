PC-331--PC-333 isolate the exact magnitude-only remainder. Hence prime support and nonprime complement have identical normalized Fourier-power profiles, dyadic Green--Hardy energies, cyclic window variances and source-normalized two-level Green/Hilbert spectral data.

The scalar Green--Hardy route has consequently reached both a **classical autocorrelation boundary** and an exact binary matched-control boundary. A substantive prime-circle advance must retain information lost by the magnitude quotient — for example through genuinely nonseparable phase coupling — or use a source amplitude/normalization that the complemented control cannot match, and then prove an independent zero-sensitive destination theorem. Merely proving the surviving scalar band estimate by ordinary analytic-number-theory means would establish a classical short-interval discrepancy theorem in Fourier coordinates, not a new geometric RH mechanism.

## Treat bounded-characteristic scalar Mellin interpolation on the full Gamma strip as a classified classical channel

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-009-signed-radial-interior-survives-only-in-ordered-structure-before-mellin-or-self-adjoint-boundary-collapse`, `MI-024-exact-cyclotomic-recovery-can-be-algebraically-lossless-and-analytically-useless`, `MI-040-endpoint-mellin-singularities-form-a-classical-von-mangoldt-jet`, `MI-041-compact-mellin-frequency-support-still-collapses-to-the-von-mangoldt-endpoint`, `MI-042-gamma-strip-admissibility-collapses-many-global-mellin-measures-to-the-von-mangoldt-endpoint`.

PC-179 gives the exact signed cyclotomic radial flux with Mellin transform `R_n(s)=-Gamma(s)zeta(s)n^(1-s) prod_(p|n)(1-p^(s-1))`, and the singular endpoint identity `R_n(1)=Lambda(n)`. PC-183--PC-187 show that bounded refinement-equivariant filtering, positive scalarization, exact child-shell differences and the first ordered weighted refinement all collapse back to one universal Mellin carrier or a cyclotomic coboundary.

PC-335--PC-338 successively close bounded sign-changing selectors, endpoint finite-order distributions, finite point support and compactly supported Mellin-frequency distributions. Exact mixed-semiprime nulls force zero response away from `s=1`, while the endpoint response reduces to `c Lambda(n)`.

PC-339 removes compact support for a broad class of finite measures. The native `Gamma` decay supplies the horizontal strip `|Im z|<pi/2`; semiprime nulls turn every prime logarithm into a zero. After conformal transport those zeros violate the Blaschke condition because `sum_p 1/p` diverges, so a bounded strip transform vanishes. PC-340 shows that finite distributional order does not escape: fixed polynomial growth can be divided out by a zero-free strip factor without shrinking the strip, leaving the same classification.

PC-341 identifies the actual analytic boundary. The polynomial-growth hypothesis is not load-bearing: it is enough that the semiprime interpolation functions lie in the **Nevanlinna class / bounded-characteristic class** on the full Gamma strip. Under the conformal map `phi_a(z)=tanh(pi z/(4a))`, prime logarithms satisfy

`1-|phi_a(log p)| = 2/(p^(pi/(2a))+1)`.

Therefore they are a uniqueness set for bounded-characteristic functions exactly when `a>=pi/2`. At the native half-width `pi/2`, the semiprime-zero argument still forces the PC-339 classification: off `s=1` every visible shell response vanishes, while on `s=1` the selector is `c delta_0` and the response is `c Lambda(n)`. Conversely, for every narrower strip `a<pi/2`, the prime-log points satisfy the Blaschke condition and there is a nonzero bounded holomorphic function vanishing on all of them.

The surviving scalar radial escape is therefore sharply stated. Keeping the full Gamma strip requires an interpolation function **outside bounded characteristic**; losing part of the strip requires genuinely new constraints beyond the prime-log zero set, because zeros alone admit a bounded Blaschke survivor. Shell-dependent symbols/order, nonlinear or matrix-valued operations and cross-shell coupling before scalar evaluation remain outside the scalar classification. None of these representation escapes supplies a zero-sensitive destination theorem by itself.

## Demand a zero-sensitive destination theorem before interpreting source distinguishability

Prime Circle now exhibits many forms of retained structure without zero selection: lossless cyclic decoding, multivariable source tensors, analytic continuation, a universal Hilbert `1/2` boundary, trace-class relative data, source autocorrelation, discrete Green bound states, universal conductor singularity, PNT-driven escaping outliers and a positive Hardy-square excess functional. PC-331--PC-334 make the normalized magnitude branch multiscale, identify its scalar theorem target with classical short-interval variance and provide an exact complemented binary control.

Separately, PC-335--PC-341 classify an increasingly broad scalar Mellin selector family. The decisive boundary is no longer compactness, finite order or polynomial strip growth: on the native Gamma strip, bounded characteristic plus the prime-log zero set already collapses exact semiprime-null selectors to the von Mangoldt endpoint, and `pi/2` is the sharp zero-set width threshold.

The next proposal should therefore state the source-sensitive zero/sign statement first and identify what extra information survives the relevant matched-control/no-go boundary. Source distinguishability, spectral richness, sign-indefinite weighting, global Mellin support, very large bounded-characteristic growth or a hard band estimate count only after the surviving invariant is control-resistant and independently coupled to the Riemann zero restriction.
