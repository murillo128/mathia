# MI-015 — Count-matched one-profile spectra cross a universal mesoscopic amplitude threshold before prime specificity appears

**Evidence level:** unconditional classicalization from PC-282--PC-285, conditional RH sharpening through [PC-290](../../findings/PC-290-count-matched-l2-transport-reaches-square-root-boundary.md), and unconditional square-root boundary results [PC-291](../../findings/PC-291-microscopic-resonance-band-is-spectrally-invisible-at-square-root-resolution.md)--[PC-292](../../findings/PC-292-mesoscopic-resonance-threshold-is-operator-norm-sharp.md). The count-matched control is deliberately given the scalar source cardinality `M_q`; the square-root results do not prove full prime-versus-control collapse at `B~sqrt(q)`.

After selector subtraction, the complete-domain remainder is a classical logarithmic/digamma Green symbol. Prime dependence in this branch survives only through source placement inside the finite section. PC-285 reproduces that placement with a matched Li-grid control through the unconditional Vinogradov--Korobov range, while PC-287--PC-290 show under RH that the normalized one-profile singular-spectrum laws collapse for every `B=o(sqrt(q))` once total source count is matched.

PC-291 tests the most tempting explanation for why that theorem stops at square-root scale. PC-271 says `B~sqrt(q)` is the first denominator-microscopic window where an exact or bounded-residue integer relation is forced. But every fixed microscopic residue class is a partial permutation, so bands `T=o(sqrt(q)/log q)` have vanishing normalized operator norm under the established bound. The first exact source relation is therefore too small in the destination normalization to matter.

PC-292 proves that this invisibility scale is not merely a Schur-bound artifact. At `N~sqrt(q)`, the intrinsic same-source configuration `p=r=1` has anti-diagonal residue selectors. For `T_q~alpha N/log q`, the normalized retained-band operator norm tends to `alpha`; the all-ones vector asymptotically saturates the upper bound. Thus `N/log q` is the correct order-one amplitude threshold.

The new catch is provenance. The saturating mechanism is a universal same-source Hankel geometry, not exact prime placement. So the first order-one mesoscopic band is real but **still not an arithmetic discriminator**. Geometric resolution, destination amplitude and source specificity are three separate gates.

The live one-profile question is now whether, after matching or subtracting this universal Hankel contribution, a mesoscopic remainder can retain order-one dependence on the actual prime locations. Otherwise the architecture must preserve richer pre-spectral information: cross-level, multi-source, directional, phase-sensitive, raw-resonance or noncommuting structure.

The reusable lesson is five-layered. Integrated output-path variation is cheaper than global Lipschitz control; source-aware mass is cheaper than worst-atom charging; matching scalar endpoint count exposes the positional transport norm; the first exact relation can be destination-negligible; and the first destination-visible mesoscopic effect can still be universal rather than source-specific.

**Boundary.** PC-290 remains RH-conditional and gives the control `M_q`. PC-291--PC-292 are unconditional matrix-norm statements about residue bands. They neither control the full complementary Green remainder nor prove prime-versus-control collapse at `B` comparable with `sqrt(q)`.
