# MI-015 — Selector subtraction leaves a classical digamma Green symbol

**Evidence level:** proved by PC-282--PC-283 for the radial logarithmic one-profile boundary branch. No theorem is claimed for the source-dependent finite section in the simultaneous critical scaling.

PC-282 separates the divergent boundary anchor from the finite-section operator: after normalization by `a_rho=-log(1-rho)`, the boundary-first regime collapses to the exact modular collision selector. That left open whether subtracting the selector might expose a new arithmetic scalar symbol.

PC-283 classifies that remainder. The unique anchor-local counterterm making the full cyclic profile mean zero has strictly negative Fourier multipliers on every nonconstant mode. As `rho -> 1^-` its spectrum is an explicit digamma function, and the low-mode large-`q` limit is the classical logarithmic Green kernel, equivalently `-|D|^{-1}/2` on the mean-zero circle.

Thus the scalar boundary remainder is not a new arithmetic carrier. Both layers visible on the **complete cyclic domain** are classical: the singular part is the exact collision selector and the renormalized finite part is a harmonic Green kernel. Any prime-specific information in this architecture must survive in what complete-domain diagonalization discards: the source-dependent two-sided finite-section placement, a simultaneous critical scaling in which `q`, `B` and `rho` interact before either limit dominates, or structure beyond one source-independent scalar profile.

The practical test is therefore to subtract both universal pieces before interpreting a residual. A candidate effect that survives only because the selector divergence or the digamma/Green multiplier was left inside the observable has not isolated new arithmetic information.

**Boundary.** PC-283 does not classicalize the finite section `P_(p,B)D_fP_(r,B)` in the critical coupled regime. It classifies the full-domain renormalized scalar symbol that would otherwise be mistaken for the surviving source signal.
