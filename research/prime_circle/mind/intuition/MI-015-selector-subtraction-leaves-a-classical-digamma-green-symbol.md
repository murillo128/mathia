# MI-015 — Count-matched one-profile spectra suppress local square-root residue bands after diffuse source averaging

**Evidence level:** unconditional classicalization from PC-282--PC-285, conditional RH sharpening through [PC-290](../../findings/PC-290-count-matched-l2-transport-reaches-square-root-boundary.md), unconditional square-root boundary results [PC-291](../../findings/PC-291-microscopic-resonance-band-is-spectrally-invisible-at-square-root-resolution.md)--[PC-292](../../findings/PC-292-mesoscopic-resonance-threshold-is-operator-norm-sharp.md), and the unconditional source-averaged suppression theorem [PC-293](../../findings/PC-293-diffuse-source-averaging-suppresses-square-root-residue-bands.md). The count-matched control is deliberately given the scalar source cardinality `M_q`; no full prime-versus-control collapse at `B~sqrt(q)` is claimed.

After selector subtraction, the complete-domain remainder is a classical logarithmic/digamma Green symbol. Prime dependence in this branch survives only through source placement inside the finite section. PC-285 reproduces that placement with a matched Li-grid control through the unconditional Vinogradov--Korobov range, while PC-287--PC-290 show under RH that the normalized one-profile singular-spectrum laws collapse for every `B=o(sqrt(q))` once total source count is matched.

PC-291 tests the most tempting explanation for why that theorem stops at square-root scale. Although `B~sqrt(q)` is the first denominator-microscopic window where an exact or bounded-residue relation is forced, bands `T=o(sqrt(q)/log q)` have vanishing normalized operator norm. PC-292 then proves the invisibility threshold sharp in worst case: for the fixed same-source pair `p=r=1`, anti-diagonal residues of width `T~alpha N/log q` already have order-one normalized norm.

PC-293 separates that worst-case transition from the observable actually averaged over source pairs. For any probability law `mu` on nonzero residues, a fixed nonzero matrix entry hits each prescribed residue through at most one partner after the other source coordinate is fixed. Therefore

`E ||K^(<=T)/N||_F^2 <= ||mu||_infty E_q(T) + (log q)^2/N^2`.

When `N~sqrt(q)` and `||mu||_infty=O(log q/q)`, every local band `T=O(N)` is `o(1)` in the averaged normalized singular-spectrum law. Both the uniform prime source and the PC-290 count-matched Li-grid control satisfy this diffuseness scale. In particular, the exact PC-292 band `T~N/log q` is washed out even though it is order one for an exceptional fixed pair.

The conceptual boundary is now sharper: **microscopic resonance, worst-case mesoscopic amplitude, source-averaged amplitude and source specificity are four different gates**. A local singular band can be operator-norm sharp and still carry no order-one information after the averaging that defines the one-profile law. The remaining source-averaged information, if any, must be assembled from the nonlocal residue complement, a non-averaged/extremal statistic, or information discarded before one-profile singular-spectrum compression.

The live one-profile question is therefore whether `|ph+rk|_q` outside every `O(sqrt(q))` local band retains an order-one dependence on actual prime positions after count matching. If that complement also classicalizes, the architecture must preserve richer pre-spectral structure: cross-level, multi-source, directional, phase-sensitive, raw-resonance or noncommuting information.

The reusable lesson is that a worst-case extremizer must be tested against the **same source aggregation used by the destination**. An order-one fixed-pair Hankel mechanism cannot explain a source-averaged endpoint once diffuse incidence makes its total squared contribution vanish.

**Boundary.** PC-290 remains RH-conditional and gives the control `M_q`. PC-293 is unconditional and uses only maximal source atom plus the exact residue decomposition; it does not control the nonlocal complement or prove prime-versus-control collapse at square-root resolution. Measures with large atoms, fixed exceptional pairs, non-averaged observables and richer multi-level summaries lie outside its suppression theorem.
