# MI-021 — Oriented-frame repair is mode-reversal gauge or classical sine-Fourier data

**Evidence level:** exact finite-frame decomposition and classicalization boundary from [PC-302](../../findings/PC-302-oriented-cross-frame-repair-is-mode-reversal-gauge-or-sine-fourier-covariance.md)

PC-301 shows that centered same-source Gram formation erases coordinatewise source orientation before averaging. PC-302 tests the most direct earlier repair: retain the oriented low-frequency frames themselves and compare distinct source frames before forming a same-source Gram.

At the level of unlabelled frame geometry, reflection is still exact gauge. If `J` reverses the latent Fourier-mode labels, then

`X_-s = X_s J`.

Hence source reflection leaves the frame subspace unchanged and preserves singular values, principal-angle data, Schatten norms and other positive basis-invariant cross-frame geometry. Merely moving the contraction one stage earlier does not recover orientation if the mode labels are then quotiented away.

The labelled reflection-odd sector is genuinely nonzero, but PC-302 identifies it exactly: it is a finite sine cross-Gram. Under independent same-law source averaging, its mean becomes a positive Gram matrix built from the imaginary parts of the ordinary additive Fourier transform of the source law. For the prime source these entries are classical additive exponential sums over primes.

Thus the simplest quadratic oriented-frame repair has a sharp split. **Positive/unlabelled geometry remains in the mode-reversal gauge quotient, while the first labelled orientation-sensitive sector classicalizes to sine-Fourier covariance.** Retaining frames before Gram formation restores a channel that PC-301 erased, but it does not by itself create a new zeta-sensitive spectral variable.

A genuinely different route must therefore justify a canonical chirality or mode label that survives positive compression, retain higher joint information not reducible to quadratic Fourier covariance, use an order-sensitive carrier with independent geometric meaning, or introduce a cross-level observable not determined by the same finite Fourier packet.

**Boundary.** PC-302 does not rule out explicitly order-sensitive antisymmetric statistics, nonnormal labelled operators, higher joint frame tensors, asymmetric windows justified by the source geometry, or cross-level carriers. It says only that the natural basis-invariant positive geometry is reflection gauge and that the first labelled quadratic odd component is classical sine/cosine Fourier data.
