# Is the Mertens-scale top-band rotation asymptotically generated by bounded chord windows?

## Observation

`PC-143` proves that the isolated gap-two top band converges to the gap-two matching space in normalized Frobenius average while retaining a nonzero worst principal angle. `PC-144` sharpens the scalar compressed remainder energy to an exact `1/log x` asymptotic and writes its limiting constant as an absolutely convergent signed-offset sum with terms proportional to

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{h\equiv0,2\pmod6,\ h\ne0,2\}}
\frac{S(h)}{h^2},
\qquad
S(h)=\prod_{\substack{p\ge5\\p\mid h(h-2)}}\frac{p-2}{p-3}.
\]

The retained visualization of this exact series shows strong short-chord concentration: symmetric windows `|h|<=6,20,50,100,200` carry approximately `50.9%`, `81.7%`, `92.3%`, `96.1%`, and `98.0%` of the limiting offset sum. Separately, `VIS-005` already shows that the fixed offsets `4` and `6` suffice to obstruct operator-norm locking. These facts make bounded chord locality a plausible explanation for much of the remaining average rotation, but neither result establishes that conclusion at projector level.

## Research question

Let `R_x^(H)` be the part of the Prime-Circle remainder Laplacian `R_x` retaining only nonmatching primitive edges of cyclic chord distance at most a fixed `H`, let

\[
L_x^{(H)}=\beta_{N_x}P_x+R_x^{(H)},
\]

and let `Q_x^(H)` be its isolated rank-`E_x` top-band projector. Does the bounded-window approximation recover the full projector at the natural Mertens/Frobenius scale, in the sense that

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{H\to\infty}\limsup_{x\to\infty}
\frac{\log x}{E_x}
\|Q_x-Q_x^{(H)}\|_F^2=0
}?
\]

If so, the average gap-two spectral rotation is asymptotically finite-local after the exact matching baseline is removed. If not, long chords have a coherent eigenspace effect that survives at Mertens scale even though their contribution to the exact compressed trace constant can be made arbitrarily small.

## Why it may matter

The current Prime-Circle boundary is unusually sharp: average locking is classical Mertens-scale, while sparse exceptional directions remain macroscopic in operator norm. Distinguishing finite-local from genuinely nonlocal production of the average projector defect would say whether there is any unexplained global organization left inside that `1/log x` layer. A positive result would further classicalize the top-band geometry into bounded CRT constellations; a negative result would expose a concrete nonlocal residual rather than another raw spectral pattern.

## Decisive test

For fixed `H`, exploit the exact chord decomposition of `R_x` and the `PC-142` spectral cliff to compare `Q_x^(H)` with `Q_x`. First derive the fixed-window asymptotic for the compressed energy `tr(P_x R_x^(H) P_x)` from the finite subset of the `PC-144` offset formula. Then bound or compute the off-diagonal block discarded by `R_x-R_x^(H)` strongly enough to decide the boxed double limit, using a Sylvester/Davis--Kahan style estimate only after preserving the exact arithmetic block structure.

A decisive negative should exhibit a family of long-offset couplings whose normalized compressed trace is negligible but whose collective action contributes a nonvanishing amount to `(log x/E_x)||Q_x-Q_x^(H)||_F^2` for every fixed `H`. A decisive positive should give a tail bound tending to zero with `H` uniformly at the scaled asymptotic level.

## Evidence boundary

The visualization establishes no projector-locality theorem. Its percentages describe the exact **scalar limiting offset sum** from `PC-144`, numerically evaluated after the theorem has already supplied absolute convergence. Trace-energy concentration does not control coherent subspace rotation by itself. The proposed double-limit statement is therefore an unproved Prime-Circle question, not a consequence of `PC-143`, `PC-144`, or `VIS-005`.

## Research disposition

Outcome: supported

Resolved by:
- [[research/prime_circle/findings/PC-145-bounded-chord-windows-recover-mertens-scale-top-band-projector]]

PC-145 proves the boxed double limit. For every fixed cutoff, the truncated operator retains the same isolated rank-`E_x` band because its positive remainder is dominated by the PC-142 remainder. The discarded long-chord Laplacian has operator norm `O(N_x^2/H)`, while its matching-compressed energy has the exact `C_gap^{>H}/log x` asymptotic obtained by restricting the absolutely convergent PC-144 offset series. A Sylvester comparison of the full and truncated top-band projectors then gives a scaled Frobenius bound tending to zero as `H->infinity`.

Thus the average Mertens-scale rotation is asymptotically generated by bounded chord windows. This does not imply operator-norm locality or uniform matching-space locking: the PC-143 obstruction already comes from the fixed offsets `4` and `6`, so sparse macroscopic defect directions survive inside finite windows.
