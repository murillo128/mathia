# PC-417 — bounded log-scale functional calculus stays on the Mellin boundary

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-WEYL-STRUCTURE` + `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` for the first genuinely infinite-order continuation of the PC-416 log-scale route. Exponentiating the canonical log-position operator

\[
Qf(x)=(\log x)f(x)
\]

does escape the finite-jet hypothesis of PC-416, but its bounded unitary group acts in Mellin space only by **real translations of the same one-shell Mellin amplitude**. Alternating those translations with the intrinsic descendant multipliers has an exact Weyl-type normal form: every finite word is a universal multiplier followed by one real Mellin translation, and finite-measure/Fourier-Stieltjes superpositions remain integrals of the same shifted one-shell data. For the Prime-Circle source this samples the classical Gamma/zeta/local-prime formula only on the original line `Re(s)=1`; it does not move the zeta argument into the critical strip.

The only exponential of `Q` that shifts the Mellin contour horizontally is the nonunitary multiplication `e^{beta Q}=x^beta`, which is unbounded on the ambient Haar space for every nonzero real `beta`. On the actual source where such a shift is defined, it merely evaluates the already classical PC-179 Mellin formula at `Re(s)=1+beta`; choosing `beta=-1/2` inserts the critical line by hand. Thus the canonical bounded exponentiated-`Q` repair does not supply a new RH mechanism.

This result is deliberately narrower than a no-go for arbitrary nonlocal operators. It does not classify general bounded Borel functions of `Q` whose Fourier kernels are not finite measures, source-dependent kernels, nonlinear source/conductor couplings, or angular/chord/old-new operators acting before the shell-2 radial reduction.

## 1. Exponentiating the intrinsic log-position gives Mellin translation

Use the PC-415/PC-416 Hilbert representation

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx/x),
\qquad
\widehat f(t)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{-it}\frac{dx}{x}.
\tag{1}
\]

The operator `Q` is self-adjoint multiplication by `log x` on its natural domain. Its unitary group is therefore

\[
(U_a f)(x):=e^{iaQ}f(x)=x^{ia}f(x),
\qquad a\in\mathbb R.
\tag{2}
\]

Unlike a finite polynomial in `Q`, `U_a` is an infinite-order function of `Q` but is bounded with norm one. Directly from (1),

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{U_a f}(t)=\widehat f(t-a).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Writing

\[
(T_aF)(t):=F(t-a),
\tag{4}
\]

we have the exact unitary equivalence

\[
\mathcal M U_a\mathcal M^{-1}=T_a.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus exponentiating the phase-sensitive operator of PC-416 does create a genuinely nonlocal operation in Mellin frequency, but the nonlocality is only translation along the existing real `t` axis.

## 2. Descendant multipliers and translations have an exact normal form

Let `C_m` be any source-independent descendant operator from PC-405--PC-416, with Mellin symbol `c_m(t)`:

\[
\widehat{C_m f}(t)=c_m(t)\widehat f(t).
\tag{6}
\]

For each fixed conductor label `m`, `c_m` is a bounded finite exponential polynomial in `t`. If `M_c` denotes multiplication by `c`, equations (3)--(6) give the covariance relation

\[
\boxed{
T_aM_c=M_{c(\,\cdot-a\,)}T_a.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Repeatedly applying (7) normal-orders every finite word generated by the unitary translations `T_a` and descendant multipliers into

\[
\boxed{
W=M_{A_W}T_{\sigma_W},
}
\tag{8}
\]

where `sigma_W` is the sum of the translation parameters and `A_W(t)` is a finite product of descendant symbols evaluated at real translates of `t`. Both are determined entirely by the chosen word and conductor labels, not by the source profile.

Therefore for the one-shell source `h_n`, with

\[
H_n(t):=\widehat h_n(t),
\tag{9}
\]

every such infinite-order word has the exact form

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{Wh_n}(t)=A_W(t)H_n(t-\sigma_W).
}
\tag{10}
\]

Finite linear combinations give only

\[
\sum_{j=1}^N A_j(t)H_n(t-\sigma_j).
\tag{11}
\]

This is a stronger boundary than the finite-jet statement of PC-416: the whole elementary unitary group generated by `Q` is admitted, yet the source channel is still just the same one-shell Mellin function sampled at translated real frequencies.

## 3. Fourier-Stieltjes functional calculus remains a real-frequency superposition

The same conclusion extends from discrete translations to the natural bounded Fourier-Stieltjes calculus. Let `mu` be a finite complex Borel measure on `R` and define

\[
\Phi_\mu(Q)
:=\int_{\mathbb R}U_a\,d\mu(a),
\qquad
\|\Phi_\mu(Q)\|\le \|\mu\|.
\tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
\phi(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{iau}\,d\mu(a),
\tag{13}
\]

then `Phi_mu(Q)=phi(Q)`. Under the Mellin transform,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\Phi_\mu(Q)f}(t)
=
\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat f(t-a)\,d\mu(a).
}
\tag{14}
\]

A finite product alternating operators of the form (12) with descendant multipliers expands, by repeated use of (7) and Fubini for finite measures, into a finite-measure integral of normal forms (8):

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{Wh_n}(t)
=
\int A_W(t;\mathbf a)
H_n\!\left(t-\Sigma(\mathbf a)\right)
\,d\nu_W(\mathbf a).
}
\tag{15}
\]

Here `A_W` is again a product of real-translated descendant symbols and `nu_W` is a finite product/linear combination of the input measures. Absolutely summable discrete superpositions are the special case where the measures are atomic, and their operator-norm limits remain in this same Fourier-Stieltjes/Wiener class.

Consequently even this bounded nonlocal extension can recover Mellin phase correlations that PC-415 lost, but it does not create an independent arithmetic carrier. Cross-readouts have the generic form

\[
H_n(t-a)\overline{H_n(t-b)}
\times
\text{universal translated conductor factors},
\tag{16}
\]

integrated against source-independent measures. Replacing `H_n` by an arbitrary matched source `G` reproduces the identical operator architecture with `G(t-a)\overline{G(t-b)}`. The extra structure is the chosen translation kernel, not a Prime-Circle-specific relation among Mellin frequencies.

## 4. The actual Prime-Circle source never leaves `Re(s)=1`

PC-407/PC-416 give

\[
\boxed{
H_n(t)
=
-\Gamma(1-it)\zeta(1-it)n^{it}
\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-it}),
}
\tag{17}
\]

with the removable zero-frequency presentation understood as before. For real `a`, equation (10) replaces `t` only by `t-a`, so the zeta factor becomes

\[
\zeta\!\left(1-i(t-a)\right).
\tag{18}
\]

Its real part is still exactly one. The same is true pointwise inside every finite-measure superposition (15). Thus bounded exponentiation of `Q` does not generate a horizontal Mellin displacement, a `s\leftrightarrow1-s` symmetry, a Gamma-completion mechanism, or access to the Riemann critical line. Any zeros created by interference between several translated terms depend on the chosen Fourier-Stieltjes kernel; they are not a geometrically forced relocation of the nontrivial zeta zero set.

This also gives a clean matched control. Formulae (7)--(16) hold for every source profile in the same Hilbert space, while only the inserted one-shell amplitude distinguishes the cyclotomic source from an arbitrary control. The bounded translation algebra therefore supplies universal log-scale kinematics, not additional arithmetic dynamics.

## 5. Horizontal contour motion requires an unbounded nonunitary weight

There is a sharp operator-theoretic boundary. For real `beta`, consider

\[
E_\beta:=e^{\beta Q},
\qquad
(E_\beta f)(x)=x^\beta f(x).
\tag{19}
\]

For every `beta!=0`, multiplication by `x^beta` is unbounded on `L^2(R_+,dx/x)`. Wherever a particular source lies in its domain, however,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{E_\beta f}(t)=\widehat f(t+i\beta).
}
\tag{20}
\]

Applying (20) to (17) gives

\[
\widehat{E_\beta h_n}(t)
=
-\Gamma(1+\beta-it)\zeta(1+\beta-it)
 n^{it-\beta}
\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{\beta-it}).
\tag{21}
\]

Thus one can reach the critical line by setting `beta=-1/2`, but this does not derive the critical line: it selects the desired Mellin contour through an unbounded nonunitary weight. More importantly, (21) is exactly the same classical cyclotomic Mellin factorization already classified in PC-179, now evaluated at another real part. PC-179 shows that throughout `0<Re(s)<1` the nontrivial zeros of the scalar shell Mellin transform are precisely the already-present zeros of `zeta(s)`.

So exponentiating `Q` yields a dichotomy:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
e^{iaQ},\ a\in\mathbb R &: \text{bounded/unitary, real Mellin translation, }Re(s)\text{ unchanged},\\[2mm]
e^{\beta Q},\ \beta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\} &: \text{unbounded/nonunitary, horizontal contour shift chosen by }\beta.
\end{array}}
\tag{22}
\]

Neither side supplies a new Prime-Circle reason for the Riemann critical line.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The analytic machinery in this result is classical. After the change of variables `u=log x`, the Mellin transform is an ordinary Fourier transform; multiplication by `e^{iau}` becomes translation of Fourier frequency, and multiplication/translation obey the standard Weyl--Heisenberg covariance relation. Directed checks against standard Mellin-transform literature and Weyl--Heisenberg modulation/translation calculus confirm that (3), (7), and the finite-measure superposition (14) are textbook harmonic analysis rather than new operator algebra.

No historical novelty is claimed for those identities. The line-local delta is the exact scope classification after PC-416: **one natural infinite-order extension really can be admitted without a finite-jet truncation, but the entire bounded Fourier-Stieltjes calculus generated by the intrinsic log-position still moves only along the original Mellin boundary and normal-orders against descendant multipliers.** The only exponential that moves horizontally is the unbounded real weight, whose output is already the classical PC-179 zeta Mellin representation.

The proof is self-contained and the literature check is classificatory rather than load-bearing, so no new `SOURCES.md` anchor is required.

## 7. Boundary, falsification, and research consequence

The exact claim can be falsified in three direct ways: exhibit a finite word in real translations and descendant multipliers that violates the covariance normal form (8); exhibit a finite-measure Fourier-Stieltjes superposition whose Mellin action is not the real-frequency integral (14); or obtain a horizontal change in `Re(s)` from a unitary `e^{iaQ}` with real `a`. Equations (3) and (7) exclude all three.

The scope boundary is equally important. General bounded Borel functions of `Q` need not have a finite-measure Fourier representation, and source-forced singular kernels or other genuinely nonlocal integral operators may couple Mellin frequencies in ways not covered by (15). Nor does this result touch nonlinear source/conductor constructions or angular/chord/old-new geometry before radial scalarization. Those remain legitimate candidates if their kernel or topology is forced by the Prime-Circle construction rather than inserted to target zeta.

For the radial descendant route, however, PC-416 and the present result now close both finite log-scale jets and the canonical bounded exponentiated/Fourier-Stieltjes continuation. A viable next operator must therefore use genuinely additional source-derived structure, not merely a more elaborate bounded function assembled from the same log-scale generator and source-independent descendant multipliers.
