# PC-427 — two prime-log translations generate a universal cyclic Hilbert-commutator sector

**Status:** EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE for obtaining a zeta-specific, unmarked spectral representation by taking the closed translation orbit of the source-generated Hilbert--Schmidt commutators from PC-421--PC-426. For each fixed \(0<\sigma<1\), every nontrivial primitive shell, the regularized Bose seed, and PC-424's zero-free matched-control shells are individually cyclic for **the same closed subspace of Hilbert--Schmidt operators**. Even the group of log-scale conjugations generated by the two prime shifts \(\log 2\) and \(\log 3\) suffices. On this space the translation generator is multiplication by the real spectral variable, with absolutely continuous spectrum \(\mathbb R\) and multiplicity one.

The representation *without a distinguished vector* is universal. The distinguished shell/seed spectral measures are not universal: their densities retain \(|\Gamma(\sigma-it)\zeta(\sigma-it)|^2\), respectively the zero-free matched Mellin density. Thus abstract cyclicity and spectral multiplicity do not select the critical line, but this theorem neither erases the source-specific vector measure nor resolves RH. This result concerns the **closed orbit with inverse translations admitted**, not whether the bare sequence of prime-index Gram vectors is a discrete frame (PC-426).

## 1. A canonical closed Hilbert--Schmidt Fourier sector

Let \(\mathcal K=\mathcal S_2(L^2(\mathbb R))\) over the complex numbers, and let \(H\) denote the real-line Hilbert transform with Fourier multiplier \(-i\operatorname{sgn}\xi\). For a regular real symbol \(h\), PC-421 gives
\[
\widehat{[H,M_h]}(\xi,\eta)
=-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
  (\operatorname{sgn}\xi-\operatorname{sgn}\eta)
  \widehat h(\xi-\eta).
\tag{1}
\]
Here the hat on the operator denotes its integral kernel in Fourier coordinates on both input and output variables.

Define the weighted Hilbert space and kernel map
\[
\mathcal E=L^2\!\left(\mathbb R,\frac{2}{\pi}|t|\,dt\right),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal Jg}(\xi,\eta)
=-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
(\operatorname{sgn}\xi-\operatorname{sgn}\eta)g(\xi-\eta).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(t=\xi-\eta>0\), the opposite-sign frequency pairs occupy
\(\xi\in(0,t)\), of length \(t\); for \(t<0\) they occupy
\(\xi\in(t,0)\), of length \(|t|\). Plancherel therefore yields
\[
\boxed{\|\mathcal Jg\|_{\mathcal K}^2
=\frac2\pi\int_{\mathbb R}|t||g(t)|^2\,dt
=\|g\|_{\mathcal E}^2.}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal J\) is a unitary identification of \(\mathcal E\) with its **closed** image
\[
\mathcal A:=\mathcal J(\mathcal E)\subset\mathcal K.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a closed Hilbert--Schmidt *Fourier-kernel sector*: an arbitrary \(g\in\mathcal E\) need not come from a bounded multiplication operator \(M_h\). Calling every element of \(\mathcal A\) a literal bounded-symbol commutator would be incorrect.

For \((\tau_a f)(u)=f(u+a)\), let
\(\mathcal T_aX=\tau_aX\tau_{-a}\). Conjugation is strongly continuous and unitary on \(\mathcal K\), and the exact Fourier translation law is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J^{-1}\mathcal T_a\mathcal Jg(t)
=e^{iat}g(t).}
\tag{5}
\]
The intrinsic log-scale group thus acts on the sector as ordinary multiplication by characters.

## 2. Sharp cyclicity criterion: only positive-measure Fourier gaps matter

For any nonzero \(g\in\mathcal E\), set
\(\mathcal H_g=\overline{\operatorname{span}}^{\mathcal K}
\{\mathcal T_a\mathcal Jg:a\in\mathbb R\}\), where *complex* linear combinations are taken. Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_g=
\mathcal J\bigl(\{f\in\mathcal E:
f=0\text{ a.e. on }\{t:g(t)=0\}\}\bigr).}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_g=\mathcal A
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 g(t)\ne0\text{ for Lebesgue-a.e. }t.}
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, if \(f\in\mathcal E\) is orthogonal to all \(e^{iat}g(t)\), then
\[
0=\int_{\mathbb R}f(t)\overline{g(t)}e^{-iat}
\,\frac2\pi|t|dt
\qquad(a\in\mathbb R).
\tag{8}
\]
The measure with density \((2/\pi)|t|f(t)\overline{g(t)}\) has finite total variation by Cauchy--Schwarz. Uniqueness of the Fourier transform of a finite complex measure gives \(f\overline g=0\) almost everywhere. The orthogonal complement is therefore precisely the functions supported where \(g=0\), proving (6). Since \(|t|dt\) and Lebesgue measure have identical null sets, (7) follows. This is the relevant \(L^2\) Wiener/cyclic-spectral phenomenon, not a source-specific zero theorem.

The criterion is sharp: a nonzero \(g\in\mathcal E\) vanishing on an interval of positive measure generates only the proper sector supported off that interval. Isolated Fourier zeros, including potential off-critical zeta zeros on a fixed vertical line, cannot shrink the **closed** translation orbit.

## 3. All primitive layers and the zero-free control generate this same embedded sector

For \(0<\sigma<1\), PC-424 defines
\[
r(x)=\frac1{e^x-1}-\frac1x,\quad
b_\sigma(u)=e^{\sigma u}r(e^u),\quad
B_\sigma=[H,M_{b_\sigma}].
\]
Its Fourier symbol is
\[
\widehat b_\sigma(t)
=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\Gamma(\sigma-it)\zeta(\sigma-it).
\tag{9}
\]
The Gamma function has no zeros; \(\zeta\) is analytic and not identically zero on the open critical strip. On each fixed line \(s=\sigma-it\), its zeros are therefore isolated, hence a null set. By PC-421, \(\widehat b_\sigma\in\mathcal E\). Equations (1), (7), and (9) imply
\[
\boxed{\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{\mathcal T_a B_\sigma:a\in\mathbb R\}=\mathcal A.}
\tag{10}
\]

For any \(n>1\), write
\[
A_n(s)=n^{1-s}\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{s-1}),
\qquad
q_{n,\sigma}(t)=-A_n(\sigma-it).
\tag{11}
\]
The actual primitive-shell Fourier multiplier is, by PC-421/PC-424,
\[
\widehat h_{n,\sigma}(t)
=q_{n,\sigma}(t)\widehat b_\sigma(t),
\qquad
C_{n,\sigma}=\mathcal J\widehat h_{n,\sigma}.
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(n>1\), every Euler factor is bounded away from zero throughout the real \(t\)-axis:
\[
\boxed{
n^{1-\sigma}\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{\sigma-1})
\le |q_{n,\sigma}(t)|
\le n^{1-\sigma}\prod_{p\mid n}(1+p^{\sigma-1}).
}
\tag{13}
\]
Both bounds are finite and the lower one is strictly positive. Thus multiplication by \(q_{n,\sigma}\) is bounded and boundedly invertible on \(\mathcal E\), commutes with the translation representation, and preserves the cyclic subspace. Equations (7)--(13) give
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_{C_{n,\sigma}}=\mathcal H_{B_\sigma}=\mathcal A
\quad\text{for every }n>1.}
\tag{14}
\]
The source-native Möbius selectors survive in the *marked vectors* \(q_{n,\sigma}\widehat b_\sigma\); they are invisible to the unmarked closed cyclic space.

For the explicit zero-free control from PC-424,
\[
r_0(x)=-\frac{1-e^{-x}}x+\frac12e^{-x},\qquad
\widehat{\widetilde b}_\sigma(t)
=(2\pi)^{-1/2}Z_0(\sigma-it),\qquad
Z_0(s)=-\frac{1+s}{2(1-s)}\Gamma(s).
\tag{15}
\]
This control has \(Z_0(\sigma-it)\ne0\) at **every** real \(t\) for \(0<\sigma<1\), and its shells have the same Euler multiplier (11). Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_{\widetilde B_\sigma}
=\mathcal H_{\widetilde C_{n,\sigma}}=\mathcal A
\quad(n>1).}
\tag{16}
\]
The equality in (16) is equality of **embedded subspaces of \(\mathcal K\)** in the common Fourier coordinates, not merely abstract unitary equivalence.

## 4. Even the two-prime translation subgroup is cyclic

Let
\[
G_{2,3}=\{m\log2+k\log3:m,k\in\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The ratio \(\log2/\log3\) is irrational: a rational relation would give \(2^m=3^k\) for some nonzero integers, contradicting unique prime factorization. The additive subgroup (17) is therefore dense in \(\mathbb R\). By strong continuity of \(a\mapsto\mathcal T_a X\), the closed orbit span under \(G_{2,3}\) equals its full continuous-translation orbit span. Thus, for every \(n>1\),
\[
\boxed{
\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{\mathcal T_{m\log2+k\log3}C_{n,\sigma}:
m,k\in\mathbb Z\}=\mathcal A,
}
\tag{18}
\]
and likewise for the seed and zero-free matched control.

The crucial distinction is **group generation with inverse shifts**. Equation (18) makes no assertion that the sparsely sampled set \(\{C_{p,\sigma}:p\text{ prime}\}\) is itself a frame, Riesz basis, or complete discrete family. PC-426's no-Riesz/no-Bessel statements remain untouched. Nor does (18) insert a compact operator into the shift-only norm-closed \(C^*\)-algebras excluded by PC-419/PC-420: the Hilbert--Schmidt source commutator is an explicitly supplied vector of \(\mathcal K\), not an element constructed from translations alone.

## 5. Spectrum versus distinguished-vector spectral measure

Under \(\mathcal J^{-1}\), the self-adjoint generator \(D\) of \(\mathcal T_a|_{\mathcal A}=e^{iaD}\) is
\[
(Dg)(t)=tg(t),\qquad
\operatorname{Dom}D=\{g\in\mathcal E:tg(t)\in\mathcal E\}.
\tag{19}
\]
It has spectrum \(\sigma(D)=\mathbb R\), **purely absolutely continuous of spectral multiplicity one**, no eigenvalues, and no isolated discrete spectrum. Its unitary-equivalence type is independent of the source seed and of \(\sigma\in(0,1)\).

But the measure of a **specified vector** \(B_\sigma\) is
\[
\boxed{
d\nu_{B_\sigma}(t)=\frac{|t|}{\pi^2}
|\Gamma(\sigma-it)\zeta(\sigma-it)|^2\,dt,
}
\tag{20}
\]
and for every primitive shell
\[
\boxed{d\nu_{C_{n,\sigma}}(t)
=|q_{n,\sigma}(t)|^2d\nu_{B_\sigma}(t).}
\tag{21}
\]
For the zero-free seed,
\[
\boxed{d\nu_{\widetilde B_\sigma}(t)
=\frac{|t|}{\pi^2}|Z_0(\sigma-it)|^2\,dt,}
\tag{22}
\]
with the analogous universal Euler reweighting for \(\widetilde C_{n,\sigma}\). All these measures have the same Lebesgue measure class and yield the same **unmarked** generator representation, but their exact densities generally differ. In particular a unitary intertwining the translation representations is **not** thereby entitled to carry \(B_\sigma\) to \(\widetilde B_\sigma\): that would require equality of their marked measures, which (20) and (22) do not supply.

An individual zeta zero has zero Lebesgue measure and so does not change spectral multiplicity or support. Nevertheless the exact analytic/continuous representative of the distinguished density (20) records its zeros away from \(t=0\) (which is a universal \(|t|\) zero). Thus the marked vector retains the classical Mellin information; stripping off that marking destroys the arithmetic specificity. No theorem above restricts the real part of a zeta zero.

## 6. Prior art, exact obstruction, and falsification boundary

The weighted Hilbert--Schmidt kernel (1)--(3) is the same classical Hilbert/Hankel commutator calculus already audited in PC-421 (Janson--Wolff, Peller). The density of character translates and Fourier uniqueness in (6)--(8) are a standard Hilbert-space version of Wiener's Tauberian/cyclicity theorem; see N. Wiener, *Tauberian Theorems*, *Annals of Mathematics* 33 (1932), 1--100, DOI 10.2307/1968102. The multiplicity-one cyclic spectral model is standard spectral theory (e.g., M. Moszyński, *Spectral Theory of Self-adjoint Finitely Cyclic Operators and Introduction to Matrix Measure L²-spaces*, arXiv:2212.13953, 2022). The irrationality/density step (17) is elementary. No novelty is claimed for these abstract ingredients or for the Mellin identity (9).

The line-local delta beyond PC-424--PC-426 is the **exact universal closure** (14), (16), and its source-native two-prime subgroup form (18): the whole continuously translated, non-diagonal Hilbert--Schmidt kernel sector is independent of primitive-shell selection and even of the presence of zeta zeros. This closes attempts to read RH solely from cyclicity, the unmarked closed invariant subspace, spectral type, multiplicity, or support of the translation generator. It does not exclude arithmetic carried by distinguished-vector measures, source-fixed cross-shell spectral products, domains depending on the actual seed, or angular/old--new interactions formed before radial scalarization.

Decisive tests: rederive the sign and factor \(2/\pi\) in (1)--(3) by integrating the opposite-frequency wedges; verify the direction \(e^{ia(\xi-\eta)}\) in (5); justify finite-measure Fourier uniqueness in (8), rather than incorrectly demanding the Fourier transform have *no* point zeros; verify bounded invertibility in (13); and use strong continuity plus **both** inverse prime shifts in (18). A profile with a positive-measure Fourier gap is the sharp control that falsifies indiscriminate universality. A representation that preserves the marked density in (20), or a kernel not of the scalar difference form (2), lies beyond the obstruction and must be audited separately.
