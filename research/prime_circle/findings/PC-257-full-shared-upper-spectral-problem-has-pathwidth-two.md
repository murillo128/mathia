## 1. Every collision is one weighted `K_{2,2}` tile

Put row vertices

\[
x_0,\ldots,x_{p-1}
\]

and column vertices

\[
y_0,\ldots,y_{r-1}.
\]

Because

\[
\delta_i=e_{i-1}-e_i,
\]

the `a`th summand in (1) is supported on exactly the four row-column pairs

\[
\{x_{i_a-1},x_{i_a}\}
\times
\{y_{j_a-1},y_{j_a}\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Its four values are

\[
-w_a,\quad +w_a,\quad +w_a,\quad -w_a
\tag{6}
\]

in the natural `2 x 2` order. Hence the support graph of the symmetric dilation (4) contains one weighted `K_{2,2}` four-cycle for every collision.

The strict monotonicity (2) makes the overlap pattern rigid. Nonconsecutive collision tiles are vertex-disjoint. Two consecutive tiles can share a row vertex only when

\[
i_{a+1}=i_a+1,
\tag{7}
\]

and can share a column vertex only when

\[
j_{a+1}=j_a+1.
\tag{8}
\]

If both equalities hold, the two tiles share the single graph edge `(x_{i_a},y_{j_a})`. Its contribution is `-w_a` from tile `a` and `-w_{a+1}` from tile `a+1`, so the shared edge cannot cancel. Therefore every active tile leaves an actual four-cycle in the support graph.

This already identifies the exact information retained by the support topology. Define the two adjacency bits

\[
\varepsilon_a=\mathbf 1_{\{i_{a+1}=i_a+1\}},
\qquad
\eta_a=\mathbf 1_{\{j_{a+1}=j_a+1\}},
\qquad 1\le a<k.
\tag{9}
\]

Apart from the ordered weights `w_a`, no absolute collision position or larger gap can affect the nonzero singular spectrum: PC-254's two Gram matrices are

\[
(G_p)_{aa}=2,
\qquad
(G_p)_{a,a+1}=-(\varepsilon_a),
\tag{10}
\]

\[
(G_r)_{aa}=2,
\qquad
(G_r)_{a,a+1}=-(\eta_a),
\tag{11}
\]

with all more distant entries zero. The complete finite spectral object therefore factors through the weighted binary collision word

\[
\bigl((w_a), (\varepsilon_a),(\eta_a)\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

## 2. An explicit width-two path decomposition

For each collision tile define two bags

\[
A_a=\{x_{i_a-1},y_{j_a-1},y_{j_a}\},
\qquad
B_a=\{x_{i_a},y_{j_a-1},y_{j_a}\}.
\tag{13}
\]

Order the bags as

\[
A_1,B_1,A_2,B_2,\ldots,A_k,B_k.
\tag{14}
\]

Every edge of the `a`th `K_{2,2}` tile is contained in either `A_a` or `B_a`, so the edge-cover condition for a path decomposition is immediate. It remains only to check the running-intersection condition.

A row vertex can pass from tile `a` to tile `a+1` only in case (7). Then the shared row vertex is `x_{i_a}` and appears in exactly the consecutive bags `B_a,A_{a+1}`. A column vertex can pass between those tiles only in case (8). Then `y_{j_a}` appears in

\[
A_a,B_a,A_{a+1},B_{a+1},
\]

again a consecutive interval of bags. Nonconsecutive tiles cannot share a vertex because both index sequences are strictly increasing. Thus (13)--(14) are a valid path decomposition with bags of size three, proving

\[
\operatorname{pw}(\operatorname{supp}\mathscr H_q)\le2.
\tag{15}
\]

If `k\ge1`, one noncancelling tile gives a `K_{2,2}` subgraph. Since `K_{2,2}` has treewidth two,

\[
2\le
\operatorname{tw}(\operatorname{supp}\mathscr H_q)
\le
\operatorname{pw}(\operatorname{supp}\mathscr H_q)
\le2,
\tag{16}
\]

which proves (3). If `D=0`, there is no active collision support and the width is zero under the usual convention for an edgeless support graph.

The conclusion is invariant under all absolute relabelings of the source vertices. It is therefore stronger than an observation that a particular matrix display happens to be banded: the interaction graph of the complete symmetric spectralization has intrinsically bounded pathwidth.

## 3. The entire characteristic polynomial is a fixed-width transfer object

Let the nonzero singular values of `D` be

\[
\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_k.
\]

PC-254 proves that their squares are exactly the eigenvalues of

\[
M_q:=WG_rWG_p.
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently the symmetric dilation has the exact characteristic polynomial

\[
\boxed{
\det(zI-\mathscr H_q)
=z^{p+r-2k}
\det\!\bigl(z^2I_k-WG_rWG_p\bigr).
}
\tag{18}
\]

This retains **all** singular values at once and makes no bounded-moment approximation. In the ordered collision coordinate, both `G_p` and `G_r` are tridiagonal with breaks, so `M_q` is pentadiagonal, as noted in PC-256. Equation (3) explains the same fact invariantly and gives the corresponding elimination theorem: sweeping through the bags (14), Schur elimination of completed vertices leaves at most two frontier vertices. Over the rational-function field in `z`, each step updates only a scalar determinant prefactor and an effective matrix of size at most `2 x 2` on that frontier.

Thus there is an exact fixed-width matrix-valued Schur/Riccati recursion

\[
\mathcal S_{a+1}(z)
=\mathcal F_{w_a,\varepsilon_a,\eta_a}
\bigl(z,\mathcal S_a(z)\bigr),
\tag{19}
\]

where the *type* of the local update depends only on the current collision weight and the two adjacency bits. No state dimension grows with `k`. Pivots that vanish at isolated `z` can be avoided by carrying the identities over `\mathbb C(z)` and then clearing denominators; the resulting polynomial identity is (18).

The important boundary is therefore not computational. Given the ordered collision word (12), the full characteristic polynomial can be generated in one left-to-right sweep using constant-size local linear algebra. Increasing `rank D` increases only the number of transfer steps, not the width of the spectral state.

This is precisely the all-depth extension that the fixed-moment calculation of PC-256 did not supply. It also explains why fixed moments classicalize to finite-shift walks: they are bounded-length observables of the same one-dimensional transfer chain. Letting the moment order grow with `k` probes longer and eventually global products, but it still does not create an additional transverse degree of freedom.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

Nothing about bounded-width determinant recurrences or transfer matrices is new. Block-tridiagonal and banded spectral problems have classical transfer-matrix descriptions. A directly relevant reference is Luca G. Molinari, **Spectral duality and distribution of exponents for transfer matrices of block-tridiagonal Hamiltonians**, *Journal of Physics A* 36 (2003), 4081--4090, DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/36/14/311`, arXiv:`math-ph/0210042`, which treats determinant/transfer duality for block-tridiagonal Hamiltonians. See also Luca G. Molinari, **Identities and exponential bounds for transfer matrices**, *Journal of Physics A* 46 (2013), 254004, arXiv:`1210.8001`.

Bounded-treewidth linear algebra is likewise established. Nikhil Balaji and Samir Datta, **Bounded Treewidth and Space-Efficient Linear Algebra**, in *Theory and Applications of Models of Computation* (TAMC 2015), LNCS, pp. 297--308, DOI `10.1007/978-3-319-17142-5_26`, arXiv:`1412.2470`, treat determinant, characteristic-polynomial and related linear-algebraic computations for matrices whose support graph has bounded treewidth. The graph-theoretic fact that series-parallel/partial-2-tree structures have treewidth at most two is standard. No novelty is claimed for transfer matrices, Schur complements, pathwidth, treewidth, banded determinant recurrences, or bipartite spectral symmetry.

The project-local content is the exact identification (3) for the **source-forced PC-251 shared-upper collision defect** and its consequence (18)--(19): the complete singular-spectrum problem left open after the fixed-depth reductions is already a width-two one-dimensional transfer problem for every finite prime triple. This statement is proved directly from the monotone collision theorem and does not rely on absence of prior literature.

The matched-control test is negative for prime specificity. PC-251's corresponding pairwise-coprime uniform rational-partition controls produce the same monotone weighted collision factorization (1). Once that factorization is present, the pathwidth proof and fixed-width transfer reduction use only incidence geometry and positivity of the collision weights. Hence the width-two spectral carrier itself cannot distinguish the cyclotomic source from the rational-partition model that represents it.

## 6. RH-facing consequence and strict surviving boundary

The complete shared-upper spectrum is more constrained than the phrase “growing full-rank matrix” suggests. Its rank can grow linearly with the collision count, yet its interaction width is frozen at two. Therefore neither growing matrix dimension nor retention of the full characteristic polynomial, by itself, yields a new two-dimensional or high-rank geometric carrier. The spectral variable `z` in (18) is intrinsic, but the symmetry

\[
z\longleftrightarrow -z
\tag{26}
\]

is merely the generic chiral symmetry of the bipartite dilation (4), not a derived analogue of the zeta functional equation and not a mechanism selecting `Re(s)=1/2`.

What is **not** closed is the only genuinely global part that remains after (3): a product of a growing number of local transfer updates (19). Its Lyapunov exponent, discriminant, winding, rotation number, or another source-forced asymptotic could in principle retain long-range arithmetic information even though each local state has fixed dimension. Any such proposal must now pass two additional controls. First, it must distinguish the prime-circle collision word from the matched uniform-partition control rather than merely exploiting generic one-dimensional transfer dynamics. Second, a complex parameter, functional equation, explicit-formula term, zero detector, or critical normalization must be derived from that source-forced long cocycle rather than imported through an arbitrary spectral wrapper.

Accordingly PC-257 is a boundary theorem rather than evidence for a new zeta Hamiltonian. It closes the “growing transverse matrix width” interpretation of the PC-251 carrier and replaces the broad full-spectrum escape of PC-254/PC-256 with a much narrower question about the asymptotics of a **fixed-width, growing-length collision transfer cocycle**.
