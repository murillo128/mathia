# PC-166 — power-homogeneous local metrics are flat log cylinders

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for obtaining a new Prime-Circle/RH spectral mechanism by choosing a regular local Riemannian metric on `C^*` for which the intrinsic power refinements act by homotheties. The rigidity is stronger than the conformal case: already the single refinement `z -> z^2`, together with regularity across the original unit circle, forces the metric to have constant coefficients in logarithmic cylinder coordinates. The compatible all-level leafwise Laplacian therefore has an unavoidable continuous radial channel and no compact resolvent or discrete Riemann-zero divisor.

PC-165 shows that the compatible punctured-plane tower splits as `R x Sigma_Q` and that the canonical Haar half-density gives only the classical Berry--Keating dilation channel. It explicitly leaves open the possibility that an operator-level radial/angular cross-term could break that product triviality. A first intrinsic repair is to ask whether the power maps themselves select a non-product **local two-dimensional metric**, hence a Laplace--Beltrami operator richer than the separated radial and angular operators already classified in PC-065/PC-165. The answer is no within the natural regular homothety class.

## 1. One power-map homothety rigidifies the whole local metric

Let

\[
p_2:\mathbb C^\times\to\mathbb C^\times,
\qquad p_2(z)=z^2,
\]

and let `g` be any continuous positive-definite Riemannian metric on `C^*`. No rotational symmetry or conformal hypothesis is assumed. Suppose only that `p_2` is a Riemannian homothety in the pullback sense,

\[
\boxed{
p_2^*g=\rho^2 g
}
\tag{1}
\]

for one constant `rho>0`.

Lift to logarithmic coordinates

\[
\pi(x,\theta)=e^{x+i\theta},
\qquad (x,\theta)\in\mathbb R^2,
\]

where the lifted metric has the form

\[
\pi^*g
=
\begin{pmatrix}dx&d\theta\end{pmatrix}
G(x,\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}dx\\ d\theta\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
G(x,\theta+2\pi)=G(x,\theta),
\tag{2}
\]

with `G` continuous, symmetric, and positive definite. The power map lifts to

\[
D_2(x,\theta)=(2x,2\theta).
\]

Equation (1) becomes

\[
4G(2x,2\theta)=\rho^2G(x,\theta),
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
G(2x,2\theta)=aG(x,\theta),
\qquad a=\rho^2/4>0.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The original unit circle is exactly `x=0`. Put

\[
d(\theta)=\det G(0,\theta)>0.
\]

Taking determinants in (3) gives

\[
d(2\theta)=a^2d(\theta).
\tag{4}
\]

Because doubling is onto the circle, the maximum of the left side over `theta` equals the maximum of `d`. Hence

\[
\max d=a^2\max d,
\]

so positivity forces

\[
\boxed{a=1,\qquad \rho=2.}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus

\[
G(2x,2\theta)=G(x,\theta).
\tag{6}
\]

Now apply (6) to the preferred square root in the logarithmic cover:

\[
G(x,\theta)
=G\!\left(\frac{x}{2},\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
=G\!\left(\frac{x}{2^j},\frac{\theta}{2^j}\right)
\quad(j\ge1).
\tag{7}
\]

The argument on the right converges to `(0,0)`. Continuity at the unit circle therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
G(x,\theta)=G(0,0)=:G_0
\quad\text{for every }(x,\theta).
}
\tag{8}
\]

So a single regular power homothety already forces the whole metric to be a flat constant-coefficient metric on the logarithmic cylinder. In particular every power map then satisfies automatically

\[
\boxed{
p_n^*g=n^2g
\qquad(n\ge1).
}
\tag{9}
\]

The full Prime-Circle refinement semigroup does not leave a larger regular local metric family to tune.

## 2. Conformal and inversion-symmetric subcases collapse further

Write

\[
G_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&B\\
B&C
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A>0,
\qquad AC-B^2>0.
\tag{10}
\]

In polar coordinates `z=re^{i theta}`, equation (8) is

\[
\boxed{
g
=A\left(\frac{dr}{r}\right)^2
+2B\frac{dr}{r}\,d\theta
+C\,d\theta^2.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus even the most general regular homothety-compatible metric has only a constant radial/angular cross-term. If one also requires the intrinsic Prime-Circle interior/exterior inversion

\[
\iota(z)=1/\overline z,
\qquad (x,\theta)\mapsto(-x,\theta),
\]

to be an isometry, then the cross-term changes sign and hence

\[
\boxed{B=0.}
\tag{12}
\]

If, more naturally for the original Euclidean roots-of-unity geometry, `g` is required to remain in the standard complex conformal class, then `A=C` and `B=0`. Up to scale the unique metric is therefore

\[
\boxed{
g_{\rm cyl}
=\frac{|dz|^2}{|z|^2}
=dx^2+d\theta^2,
}
\tag{13}
\]

the ordinary flat cylindrical metric on the punctured plane.

This shows that an operator-level radial/angular cross-term cannot be generated by choosing a different regular conformal metric compatible with refinement. Even if conformality and inversion symmetry are dropped, the only surviving freedom is the fixed positive matrix `G_0`, which is spectrally just a constant quadratic form.

## 3. The all-level compatible metric is a flat two-dimensional solenoidal leaf metric

PC-165 identifies the compatible punctured-plane tower

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}
=\varprojlim_{m\mid n}
(\mathbb C^\times,z\mapsto z^{n/m})
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}
\cong\mathbb R\times\Sigma_{\mathbb Q},
}
\tag{14}
\]

where the first coordinate is level-one log radius and `Sigma_Q` is the arithmetic solenoid of PC-064.

Equation (9) gives an exact compatible metric normalization. Put

\[
g_n=n^2g
\tag{15}
\]

on the level-`n` copy of `C^*`. If `m|n` and `k=n/m`, then

\[
p_{n,m}^*g_m
=m^2p_k^*g
=m^2k^2g
=n^2g
=g_n.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus the bonding maps become local isometries. Under the product coordinates of PC-165, both the radial leaf coordinate and the angular leaf coordinate divide by `n` at level `n`, while `g_n` contributes the compensating factor `n^2`. Consequently every two-dimensional leaf of the completion carries the same constant metric `G_0`.

This is exactly the standard leafwise-Riemannian situation, not a hidden archimedean/finite-adic curvature. General Riemannian/Hodge theory on measured solenoids is classical; PC-064/PC-065/PC-165 already audit the relevant solenoidal and harmonic background. The new point here is the rigidity statement (8): **Prime-Circle power homogeneity cannot select a more structured regular local leaf metric before that classical theory is applied.**

## 4. Every such local Laplacian has an unavoidable continuous prime-blind channel

Let `G_*` denote the same constant metric after fixing the angular leaf normalization used in PC-065, and write its inverse quadratic form as

\[
Q(\tau,\eta)
=\alpha\tau^2+2\beta\tau\eta+\gamma\eta^2,
\qquad
\alpha>0,
\qquad
\alpha\gamma-\beta^2>0.
\tag{17}
\]

On Haar

\[
L^2(\mathbb R\times\Sigma_{\mathbb Q}),
\]

the constant-coefficient positive leafwise Laplacian diagonalizes by ordinary Fourier transform in the radial coordinate and by the Pontryagin characters `chi_q`, `q in Q`, in the solenoidal coordinate. With the PC-065 convention `chi_q(t)=e^{2 pi i q t}`, its generalized spectral symbol is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda(\tau,q)=Q(\tau,2\pi q),
\qquad
\tau\in\mathbb R,
\quad q\in\mathbb Q.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The decisive control is the solenoid-constant sector `q=0`. There

\[
\lambda(\tau,0)=\alpha\tau^2,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(\Delta_{G_*})=[0,\infty).
}
\tag{19}
\]

The resolvent is not compact, and the heat operator is not trace class: already the invariant `q=0` subspace is an ordinary constant-coefficient Laplacian on `L^2(R)`. Removing the arithmetic solenoid entirely and replacing it by a point leaves that complete obstruction unchanged.

The constant cross-term `beta` does not help. For each fixed `q` it merely shifts the minimum of a one-dimensional quadratic dispersion relation in `tau`; it cannot discretize the radial frequency. Exact-order projectors may still label the rational angular channels, as in PC-066, but no such projector changes the continuous `q=0` radial sector.

Likewise, if one cuts at the original unit circle and treats `x>0` and `x<0` as separate half-cylinders, any ordinary local Dirichlet, Neumann, or Robin condition at `x=0` leaves the radial half-line with essential spectrum. A discrete RH-like divisor would require additional confinement, a second boundary, a growing potential, a nonlocal condition, or some other structure not supplied by the regular power-homogeneous metric itself.

## 5. The apparent functional-equation symmetry is only flat-cylinder parity

When inversion is required to be an isometry, (12) removes the cross-term and `iota` acts spectrally by

\[
(\tau,q)\longmapsto(-\tau,q).
\tag{20}
\]

This is the same ordinary reflection already isolated in the radial analysis of PC-029/PC-165. It supplies neither the completed-zeta gamma factor nor a zero-selective quantization. Writing a complex parameter as `1/2+i tau` would convert (20) into the visual reflection about `Re(s)=1/2`, but the metric has produced only the universal parity `tau <-> -tau`; it has not produced the Riemann functional equation.

## 7. Boundary, stress tests, and consequences

Regularity at the unit circle is load-bearing and gives a sharp falsification boundary. If continuity at `x=0` is removed, equation (3) admits singular or log-periodic solutions on `x>0` and `x<0`; for example, positive matrix profiles periodic in `log_2|x|` satisfy doubling invariance away from the circle. Such metrics do not extend regularly across the original Prime-Circle locus and therefore are additional singular/domain-changing structure rather than counterexamples to PC-166.

The finding also does **not** rule out:

- shell-dependent or anchor-dependent nonlocal operators whose coefficients are not encoded by a local Riemannian metric;
- old/new couplings inserted before the all-level product completion;
- genuinely multi-hole or growing-base operators of the type left open after PC-164;
- singular metrics or geometry-forced domain changes that deliberately fail regularity at the unit circle and survive a matched control;
- nonlocal boundary conditions or confining terms derived independently from the root geometry;
- the global uniformization/monodromy branch of PC-017.

It does rule out a broad and natural local repair of PC-165: **one cannot evade the radial/solenoidal product collapse merely by asking the same power maps to choose a more sophisticated regular two-dimensional metric or Laplace--Beltrami operator.** The only metrics they can choose are flat constant log-cylinder metrics, and their prime-blind radial continuum survives every constant radial/angular mixing.

The exact audit surface is short: verify the pullback equation (3), use the positive determinant on the unit circle to force `a=1`, iterate the inverse doubling relation to obtain (8), check the compatible rescaling (16), and restrict the leafwise Laplacian to the `q=0` sector to obtain (19). Failure of any of these identities would invalidate the claimed no-go; changing the hypotheses by allowing singularity or shell-dependent/nonlocal coefficients lies outside it.
