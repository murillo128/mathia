# PC-026 — prime-level projective cross-ratios are cyclotomic-unit data

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for a single-level prime-circle route that tries to escape the anchored chord profile by passing to finite projective cross-ratios, their logarithms, or log-linear spectralizations.

## 1. Exact cross-ratio reduction

Let `p` be an odd prime and

\[
\zeta=\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}.
\]

The full anchored `p`-gon is exactly the set `mu_p`: the common vertex is `1=\zeta^0`, and every other vertex is primitive. For four distinct vertices

\[
z_a=\zeta^a,\quad z_b=\zeta^b,\quad z_c=\zeta^c,\quad z_d=\zeta^d,
\]

define the complex projective cross-ratio

\[
[z_a,z_b;z_c,z_d]
:=
\frac{(z_a-z_c)(z_b-z_d)}{(z_a-z_d)(z_b-z_c)}.
\]

Using

\[
\zeta^u-\zeta^v=-\zeta^v(1-\zeta^{u-v}),
\]

the root-of-unity prefactors cancel exactly, giving

\[
\boxed{
[z_a,z_b;z_c,z_d]
=
\frac{(1-\zeta^{a-c})(1-\zeta^{b-d})}
{(1-\zeta^{a-d})(1-\zeta^{b-c})}.
}
\]

Thus the entire finite projective geometry of the anchored prime polygon is built from ratios of the same chord factors `1-zeta^r` that appear in PC-001 and PC-025.

Because the four vertices lie on one circle, the cross-ratio is real (up to the usual six anharmonic conventions). Algebraically it lies in the maximal real subfield of `Q(zeta_p)`.

## 2. Every such cross-ratio is a cyclotomic unit

For `r` not congruent to `0 mod p`, all principal ideals `(1-zeta^r)` are the same prime ideal above `p`. Hence

\[
\frac{1-\zeta^r}{1-\zeta^s}
\]

is a unit of `Z[zeta_p]` whenever `r,s` are nonzero modulo `p`. These ratios are standard generators of the cyclotomic/circular-unit group up to roots of unity.

The displayed cross-ratio is a product of two such ratios. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
[z_a,z_b;z_c,z_d]\in C_p\cap \mathbb Q(\zeta_p)^+,
}
\]

where `C_p` denotes the cyclotomic-unit group (allowing the harmless sign/root-of-unity convention before taking the real representative).

So passing from Euclidean chords to Möbius-invariant four-point data does not leave cyclotomic arithmetic: at a prime level it lands directly in the classical circular-unit package.

## 3. Log-cross-ratios are finite differences of the PC-025 chord profile

Let

\[
r_p(k)=\log|1-\zeta^k|,
\qquad k\in(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]

Taking absolute values in the exact cross-ratio formula gives

\[
\boxed{
\log|[z_a,z_b;z_c,z_d]|
=
r_p(a-c)+r_p(b-d)-r_p(a-d)-r_p(b-c).
}
\]

This has two consequences.

First, the complete collection of logarithmic projective invariants is contained in the finite linear span of the anchored chord profile already classified in PC-025. No new linear coordinate space appears after projectivization.

Second, the four coefficients on the right sum to zero. Therefore every log-cross-ratio kills the constant/principal component of `r_p`. PC-025 gives

\[
\sum_{k\in G_p}\overline{\chi(k)}r_p(k)
=
-\tau(\overline\chi)L(1,\chi)
\]

for nonprincipal even characters, while odd characters vanish and the principal coefficient is `log p`. Since the principal component cancels in every projective finite difference,

\[
\boxed{
\text{logarithmic cross-ratio data contain only the nonprincipal even}
\ L(1,\chi)\text{ modes of PC-025, not the }\log p\text{ mode.}
}
\]

Equivalently, projectivization removes precisely the scalar anchor mode that PC-001 identifies with the prime-level value `log p`.

This is stronger than merely saying that individual cross-ratios are algebraic. The whole log-linear projective package is a quotient of the already-classical anchored multiplicative spectrum.

## 4. Consequence for finite projective constructions

Cross-ratios generate the rational Möbius invariants of ordered configurations on `P^1`. Therefore any finite projective observable obtained algebraically from the prime-level vertices remains inside the cyclotomic field generated by these circular units.

If one then takes logarithmic absolute values and applies a fixed finite linear matrix, determinant after linearization, quadratic form, or other log-linear spectral construction, all input coordinates factor through the same classical nonprincipal-even Dirichlet `L(1,chi)` data identified above.

This closes the natural escape route

\[
\boxed{
\text{anchored prime polygon}
\to
\text{projective cross-ratios}
\to
\text{finite/log-linear spectrum}
\to
\text{new RH mechanism}.
}
\]

The obstruction is not that cyclotomic units are trivial; their regulators and class-number relations are deep. The obstruction is that this route has not produced a new geometric package: it has moved from chord logs to the classical cyclotomic-unit/regulator realization of the same Dirichlet-character information.

## 5. Relation to earlier prime-circle obstructions

This result is distinct from PC-013. PC-013 showed that multiplying canonical one-dimensional projective moving-frame transfers telescopes and that a Hill spectrum needs an extra lift gauge. PC-026 addresses the obvious response: discard transfer matrices and use the projective invariants themselves. At prime level those cross-ratios are cyclotomic units, and their logarithms are finite differences of PC-025.

It also sharpens PC-025. PC-025 classified the full multiplicative spectrum of the common-vertex chord fan. PC-026 shows that passing to **all four-point projective invariants of the prime polygon** does not escape that classification; in the logarithmic real geometry it actually removes the principal `log p` mode.

PC-019 remains complementary: forgetting the anchor gives the exact `n <-> 2n` half-turn degeneracy. Here the anchor may be retained, but projective ratios erase its absolute scalar component anyway.

## 6. Prior art and novelty audit

No novelty is claimed for the underlying arithmetic.

- Ratios `(1-zeta_p^a)/(1-zeta_p)` and their variants are the standard cyclotomic/circular units; their structure and finite-index relation to the full unit group are classical. Washington's *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, especially the chapter on cyclotomic units, is a standard reference; Sinnott gives the classical circular-unit/Stickelberger framework.
- Cross-ratios are the classical generators of projective invariants of point configurations on `P^1`.
- The `L(1,chi)` log-sine decomposition is already recorded and sourced in PC-025.

Directed searches did not reveal a need to treat the formula above as a new theorem: it is an immediate specialization of these standard facts. The substantive contribution is the **scope classification for the prime-circle program**: finite prime-level projective geometry does not constitute an unexplored escape from the classical cyclotomic/Dirichlet-L package.

## 7. Boundary of the no-go

This does **not** prove that every nonlinear function of projective moduli is arithmetically useless, nor that cross-level dynamics built from several levels must collapse. In particular it does not touch the genuinely nonlinear Fuchsian uniformization/accessory-parameter defect of PC-017, whose metric and projective connection are solutions of a global analytic problem rather than finite rational functions of cross-ratios.

The surviving region is therefore narrower and clearer:

- nonlinear global uniformization or its second variations;
- nonseparable cross-level interactions;
- infinite-mode or singularly renormalized constructions not reducible to finite cyclotomic-unit algebra;
- genuinely two-dimensional operators whose coefficients are not fixed rational/log-linear functions of a single level's projective moduli.

Any future candidate based only on finite prime-level cross-ratios should first demonstrate what information it contains beyond the circular-unit / `L(1,chi)` package above.
