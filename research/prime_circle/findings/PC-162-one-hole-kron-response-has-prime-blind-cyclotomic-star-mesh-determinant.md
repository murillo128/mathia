# PC-162 — one-hole Kron response has a prime-blind cyclotomic star-mesh determinant

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the most canonical **nonlocal harmonic-elimination repair** left by PC-161. PC-039 classified Kron reduction only onto exact divisor subpolygons, while PC-161 showed that the one-hole new-prime puncture defect itself has a prime-blind trace-class star limit but explicitly left relational uses of that defect open. If the deleted old vertices are not discarded but instead integrated out by the canonical Schur/Kron response, the resulting nonlocal correction still has a fixed-base prime-blind limit. More strongly, every limiting star-mesh block has a Fredholm determinant equal to the normalized derivative of an explicit cyclotomic/hyperbolic product with only negative-real zeros.

Fix `d>=2`, put `r=phi(d)`, and let `m>=2` satisfy `(m,d)=1`. Use the one-hole ambient geometry of PC-158/PC-161,

\[
S_{d,m}=\{x\bmod dm:(x,d)=1\},
\qquad
O_{d,m}=\{x\in S_{d,m}:m\mid x\},
\qquad
X_{d,m}=S_{d,m}\setminus O_{d,m}.
\tag{1}
\]

There is one old point in every coarse fiber, so `|O_{d,m}|=r`. When `m=q` is a prime not dividing `d`, the survivor is exactly the genuine primitive shell,

\[
X_{d,q}=U(dq).
\tag{2}
\]

Let

\[
M_{d,m}:=(dm)^{-2}L_{dm}[S_{d,m}]
\tag{3}
\]

be the normalized inverse-square chord Laplacian on the complete semi-primitive ambient set, and let

\[
A_{d,m}^{\rm hole}:=(dm)^{-2}L_{dm}[X_{d,m}]
\tag{4}
\]

be the induced one-hole survivor operator. The canonical Kron reduction of `M_{d,m}` onto the survivor is

\[
\operatorname{Kron}_{X_{d,m}}M_{d,m}
=A_{d,m}^{\rm hole}+C_{d,m}.
\tag{5}
\]

The main result is that, for fixed `d`, the complete nonlocal correction `C_{d,m}` converges in trace norm along **all** coprime fiber sizes to an explicit direct sum of infinite star-mesh operators. Hence primes are only a subsequence of the same limit.

## 1. The finite Kron correction is exactly the old-point harmonic response

Order the ambient vertices as `X_{d,m} sqcup O_{d,m}`. The block form of (3) is

\[
M_{d,m}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_{d,m}^{\rm hole}+\Delta_{d,m}&-B_{d,m}\\
-B_{d,m}^*&G_{d,m}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]

Here `Delta_{d,m}` is the diagonal old/new conductance seen from survivor vertices, `B_{d,m}` is the old/new conductance block, and `G_{d,m}` is the Dirichlet block on the deleted old section. Since the ambient weighted graph is connected and a nonempty boundary is retained, `G_{d,m}` is positive definite. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_{d,m}
:=\Delta_{d,m}
-B_{d,m}G_{d,m}^{-1}B_{d,m}^*
\succeq0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (7) is not an arbitrary spectral wrapper. It is the unique minimum-energy boundary response obtained by allowing the deleted old values to relax harmonically. In electrical language it is exactly the extra mesh generated by eliminating the old section. For prime `q`, it is therefore a canonical genuinely nonlocal operator on `U(dq)` derived from the same embedded old/new geometry that produced the puncture defect in PC-158/PC-161.

This construction is different from PC-039. There the retained set was an exact divisor subgroup of the full regular polygon and Fourier aliasing solved the entire quotient. Here the retained set is the one-hole primitive shell inside the semi-primitive cyclic ambient geometry, so the old/new block is the essential object rather than a divisor-subpolygon symmetry.

## 2. The PC-161 star limit survives Schur elimination

For `a in U(d)` define the allowed offset set

\[
E_a:=\{h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}:(a+h,d)=1\}
\tag{8}
\]

and weights

\[
w_h:=\frac1{4\pi^2h^2},
\qquad
s_a:=\sum_{h\in E_a}w_h.
\tag{9}
\]

The sum is finite and strictly positive. Splitting the offsets into residue classes modulo `d` gives the exact base-level identity

\[
\boxed{
s_a
=\frac{\deg_d(a)}{d^2}+\frac1{12d^2},
}
\tag{10}
\]

where `deg_d(a)` is the unnormalized inverse-square degree of `a` inside `U(d)`. The extra `1/(12d^2)` is the contribution of nonzero offsets `h=kd`.

Let `W_a` be the diagonal trace-class operator on `ell^2(E_a)` with diagonal `w_h`, and let

\[
v_a=(w_h)_{h\in E_a}\in\ell^2(E_a).
\tag{11}
\]

The PC-161 limiting star on the center plus its allowed leaves has block matrix

\[
\mathcal K_a
=
\begin{pmatrix}
s_a&-v_a^*\\
-v_a&W_a
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]

Eliminating the center gives the limiting mesh

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_a
:=W_a-\frac1{s_a}v_av_a^*\succeq0.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Its off-diagonal entries are

\[
(\mathcal C_a)_{h,k}
=-\frac{w_hw_k}{s_a}
\qquad(h\ne k),
\tag{14}
\]

so harmonic elimination turns the local star into an **all-to-all nonlocal mesh** on its leaves, with effective conductances `w_h w_k/s_a`. This is precisely the kind of nonlocal information-preserving operator that a scalar evaluation does not retain.

Put

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{d,\infty}
:=\bigoplus_{a\in U(d)}\mathcal C_a.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Because `W_a` is trace class and `v_av_a^*` is rank one, every `mathcal C_a` and hence (15) are positive trace class.

The finite Kron corrections converge to this operator in the same fixed-base limit as PC-161:

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\substack{m\to\infty\\(m,d)=1}}
\|C_{d,m}-\mathcal C_{d,\infty}\|_1=0,
}
\tag{16}
\]

under the bounded-window coordinate identifications used for the star limit.

The reason Schur complementation does not spoil the PC-161 trace-class localization is that the eliminated space has the fixed dimension `r`. For a bounded chord window `H` and `m>2H`, the old neighborhoods are disjoint finite stars, so their Schur complements are exactly the finite versions of (13). The omitted long-edge part has the `O_d(H^{-1})` trace-norm bound proved in PC-161. Meanwhile the old Dirichlet block has a uniform positive gap: its old/new diagonal tends to `diag(s_a)`, old/old normalized conductances are `O_d(m^{-2})`, and therefore

\[
G_{d,m}\longrightarrow \operatorname{diag}(s_a)
\tag{17}
\]

in operator norm with uniformly bounded inverses for large `m`. Block compression of the PC-161 trace-class estimates gives trace-norm convergence of the boundary diagonals, Hilbert--Schmidt convergence of the fixed-column old/new blocks, and operator-norm convergence of the old blocks. The standard continuity estimate for

\[
\Delta-BG^{-1}B^*
\tag{18}
\]

then gives (16). Equivalently, one can first pass to the exact finite-star Schur complements at fixed `H`, then let `H->infinity` using the same summable `1/h^2` tail.

Thus not only the defect spectrum but also its canonical harmonic boundary response has a fixed coprime limit.

## 3. Each nonlocal mesh determinant is a derivative of the diagonal edge product

Define the entire Fredholm product

\[
F_a(z)
:=\det(I+zW_a)
=\prod_{h\in E_a}(1+zw_h).
\tag{19}
\]

The product is genus zero because `sum_h w_h=s_a<infinity`, and

\[
F_a'(0)=s_a.
\tag{20}
\]

The rank-one determinant lemma applied to (13) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(I+z\mathcal C_a)
&=F_a(z)
\left[
1-\frac z{s_a}
\sum_{h\in E_a}
\frac{w_h^2}{1+zw_h}
\right]\\
&=\frac{F_a(z)}{s_a}
\sum_{h\in E_a}
\frac{w_h}{1+zw_h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

But the last sum is the logarithmic derivative `F_a'(z)/F_a(z)`. Hence the complete nonlocal Fredholm determinant collapses to

\[
\boxed{
\det(I+z\mathcal C_a)
=\frac{F_a'(z)}{F_a'(0)}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

This is the infinite star-mesh analogue of the derivative/cofactor mechanisms already encountered at finite prime level in PC-032/PC-038: integrating out one distinguished center does not generate an independent spectral entire function; it differentiates the product attached to the uncoupled leaf weights.

There is also an immediate zero-location obstruction. Since `mathcal C_a` is positive trace class,

\[
\det(I+z\mathcal C_a)
=\prod_j(1+z\lambda_j(\mathcal C_a)),
\tag{23}
\]

so every zero of (22) is real and negative. The same conclusion follows from the real-negative zero products approximating `F_a` and their derivatives. Thus the canonical nonlocal limit produces an unconditional real-zero entire function, not a new zero set wandering in a complex critical strip.

## 4. The edge product itself is an explicit cyclotomic/hyperbolic function

The periodic reduced-residue mask in (8) makes (19) completely explicit. Define the finite cosine polynomial

\[
Q_{d,a}(t)
:=
\prod_{b\in U(d)}
\left(
 t-\cos\frac{2\pi(b-a)}d
\right).
\tag{24}
\]

The factor `b=a` is the unique root at `t=1`, so `Q_{d,a}'(1)\ne0`. Euler's sine product, applied separately to each arithmetic progression `h congruent b-a (mod d)`, gives

\[
\boxed{
F_a(z)
=
\frac{2d^2}{z}
\frac{
Q_{d,a}\!\left(\cosh(\sqrt z/d)\right)
}{Q_{d,a}'(1)},
}
\tag{25}
\]

with the removable value `F_a(0)=1`. Indeed the zero residue contributes

\[
\prod_{k\ne0}
\left(1+\frac{z}{4\pi^2d^2k^2}\right)
=
\left[
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt z/(2d))}{\sqrt z/(2d)}
\right]^2,
\tag{26}
\]

while every nonzero residue `r` contributes

\[
\prod_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\left(1+
\frac{z}{4\pi^2(r+kd)^2}
\right)
=
\frac{
\cosh(\sqrt z/d)-\cos(2\pi r/d)
}{1-\cos(2\pi r/d)}.
\tag{27}
\]

The finite polynomial `Q_{d,a}` is itself only a real projection of the cyclotomic shell. With `zeta_d=e^{2\pi i/d}`, `R=phi(d)`, and `t=(Y+Y^{-1})/2`, direct factorization gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_{d,a}(t)
=(2Y)^{-R}
\Phi_d(\zeta_d^aY)
\Phi_d(\zeta_d^{-a}Y).
}
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (22), (25), and (28) therefore classify the limiting Kron determinant entirely in terms of the fixed base cyclotomic polynomial and elementary hyperbolic propagation. No new dependence on the refining integer survives.

As a minimal exact audit, take `d=2`. Then `U(2)={1}`, the allowed offsets are the nonzero even integers, and

\[
F_1(z)
=
\left[
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt z/4)}{\sqrt z/4}
\right]^2,
\qquad
F_1'(0)=\frac1{48}.
\tag{29}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\det(I+z\mathcal C_1)=48F_1'(z),
}
\tag{30}
\]

whose zeros are again confined to the negative real `z` axis. This is already enough to show how a superficially archimedean `sinh/sqrt z` expression can arise from nothing more than the inverse-square star mesh.

## 5. Matched composite controls remove the new-prime interpretation

Nothing in (8)--(30) uses primality of `m`. Equation (16) holds along every sequence of integers coprime to the fixed base. When `m=q` is prime, the boundary is exactly `U(dq)`; when `m` is composite, it is the same one-hole matched control used in PC-158/PC-161. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_{d,q}\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{d,\infty}
}
\tag{31}
\]

along primes and the **same** limit is obtained along composite coprime fiber sizes.

This rules out the canonical route

\[
\boxed{
\text{new-prime old/new puncture}
\to
\text{harmonically eliminate old vertices}
\to
\text{nonlocal Kron/Feshbach correction}
\to
\text{new prime-specific Fredholm zero set}
\to
\text{RH}.
}
\tag{32}
\]

The negative result is stronger than saying that the correction is small or low rank. The limit is an infinite-rank, all-to-all nonlocal trace-class operator. What fails is arithmetic specificity: its complete limiting operator and determinant are already forced by a fixed reduced-residue mask and classical cyclotomic/sine products.

The negative-real zero set deserves one explicit falsification warning. Because (25) naturally contains `sqrt z`, one can of course impose an external substitution such as

\[
z=-(s-\tfrac12)^2
\tag{33}
\]

and thereby place every negative `z`-zero on the vertical line `Re(s)=1/2`. That does **not** supply a critical-line mechanism: the line has been inserted by the coordinate change, the zero ordinates are those of the star-mesh determinant rather than those of `zeta`, and no intrinsic functional equation or prime-specific selector produces (33). Counting such a reparameterization as progress would violate the Prime-Circle falsification control against arbitrary spectral wrappers.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The general mechanisms are classical. Florian Dörfler and Francesco Bullo, **Kron Reduction of Graphs With Applications to Electrical Networks**, *IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I* 60:1 (2013), 150--163, DOI `10.1109/TCSI.2012.2215780`, treat graph-Laplacian Kron reduction as Schur complementation and the resulting electrical-network reduction; this is already the Prime-Circle prior-art anchor for PC-039. The one-center transformation (13)--(14) is the standard star-mesh/Y--Delta mechanism in weighted-network language, and the determinant step (21)--(22) is the standard diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix determinant lemma specialized to that mesh.

The infinite-product identity behind (25)--(27) is Euler's classical sine product; NIST DLMF §4.22.1 records `sin z = z prod_{n>=1}(1-z^2/(n^2 pi^2))`. The real-zero nature of the genus-zero products belongs to the classical Laguerre--Pólya landscape; here no deep real-entire-function theorem is required because positivity of `mathcal C_a` gives the Fredholm zero location directly. Equation (28) is elementary cyclotomic factorization.

Directed searches across roots-of-unity/cosecant-square Kron reduction, cyclotomic Schur complements, weighted-star Fredholm determinants, and Laguerre--Pólya rank-one perturbations did not locate the exact fixed-base Prime-Circle chain (16), (22), (25), and (28). That absence is not evidence of historical priority, and no new general theorem about Kron reduction, star-mesh transforms, Fredholm determinants, or real-entire functions is claimed. The durable contribution is the line-specific classification: the most canonical nonlocal harmonic response to the one-hole new-prime puncture has a prime-blind trace-class limit whose spectral determinant collapses back to fixed-base cyclotomic data.

## 7. Boundary and falsification surface

This finding closes only the **one-hole Kron-response** repair. It does not classify a true multi-hole primitive refinement for a composite added conductor, simultaneous growth of the base `d`, the spectrum of the sum `A_{d,m}^{hole}+C_{d,m}` relative to `A_{d,m}^{hole}` beyond the operator limit of the added correction, growing-support nonlinear cross-level observables, or the global uniformization/monodromy branch. In particular, PC-161's broader relational frontier is narrowed but not eliminated: a surviving mechanism would have to use how the prime-shell background acts jointly with the correction, rather than the Kron correction's own operator or Fredholm determinant.

The result is directly falsifiable. A fixed base `d` and coprime sequence `m->infinity` for which the Schur corrections fail to converge to (15) in trace norm would break (16). A limiting star whose eliminated-center mesh differs from (13) would contradict the Schur formula. Any `z` for which the Fredholm determinant differs from `F_a'(z)/F_a'(0)` would contradict the rank-one determinant lemma. Finally, a residue class for which (25) fails would contradict Euler's sine product on the corresponding arithmetic progression. Under the stated hypotheses, the canonical nonlocal one-hole harmonic-elimination route is therefore exactly classified.
