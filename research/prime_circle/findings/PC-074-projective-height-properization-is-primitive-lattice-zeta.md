\[

\qquad b>0,

Now let `N:\mathbb R^2\to[0,\infty)` be any fixed even proper homogeneous gauge of degree one,

\[
N(tv)=|t|N(v),
\qquad N(v)>0\quad(v\ne0),
\qquad N(-v)=N(v).

Define the projective-height operator on rational characters by

\[
H_N\chi_{a/b}=N(a,b)\chi_{a/b}.
\]

Because `N` is proper and only finitely many primitive lattice vectors lie in every bounded `N`-ball, `H_N` has compact resolvent. So projective height really does repair the compactness failure of PC-065/PC-068.

However its spectral zeta is forced into a completely classical primitive-lattice factorization. Put

\[
Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)
=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}}N(v)^{-s}.

Every nonzero lattice vector has a unique decomposition `v=dv_0` with `d\ge1` and `v_0` primitive. Homogeneity therefore gives, for `\Re s>2`,

\[
Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)
=\sum_{d\ge1}d^{-s}
 \sum_{\substack{v_0\in\mathbb Z^2\\(v_{0,1},v_{0,2})=1}}
 N(v_0)^{-s}
=\zeta(s)Z_N^{\rm prim}(s).
\]

Since `\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)` is the set of primitive integer pairs modulo `v\sim-v`,

\[
\boxed{
Z_{\mathbb P^1,N}(s)
=\frac{Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)}{2\zeta(s)}.
}
\]

The actual solenoid character set is the affine chart `\mathbb Q=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)\setminus\{\infty\}`. Hence, after normalizing `N(1,0)=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(H_N^{-s})
=\frac{Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)}{2\zeta(s)}-1.
}
\]

The reciprocal Riemann-zeta factor is therefore not generated by a new Prime-Circle Hamiltonian mechanism. It is exactly the Möbius/visibility factor that removes nonprimitive integer multiples from a two-dimensional lattice.

## 1. The standard height has exact totient shells

For

\[
H(q)=\max(|a|,b),
\qquad q=a/b\text{ reduced},\ b>0,
\]

the height-one affine rationals are `-1,0,1`, so the multiplicity is `3`.

For every integer `n\ge2`, a primitive pair with `\max(|a|,b)=n` lies in exactly one of two disjoint cases:

- `b=n` and `1\le |a|<n`, giving `2\varphi(n)` choices;
- `|a|=n` and `1\le b<n`, giving another `2\varphi(n)` choices.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
m_H(1)=3,
\qquad
m_H(n)=4\varphi(n)\quad(n\ge2).
}
\]

Adding the single projective point `\infty=[1:0]` changes only the height-one multiplicity from `3` to `4`. Hence

\[
\#\{x\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q):H(x)\le N\}
=4\sum_{n\le N}\varphi(n),
\]

and the spectral zeta is the standard totient series above.

This also gives a matched-composite control. Height multiplicity does not characterize primality: for example

\[
\varphi(15)=\varphi(16)=8,
\]

so the two distinct levels have the same eigenspace multiplicity. Prime selectivity has not been manufactured by properness; the operator merely repackages primitive-pair counts.

## 2. Why every fixed homogeneous projective norm carries the same reciprocal-zeta factor

The key factorization is independent of the shape of the unit ball. Let

\[
\mathcal P
=\{v\in\mathbb Z^2:(v_1,v_2)=1\}
\]

be the visible lattice points. Then

\[
\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}
=\bigsqcup_{d\ge1} d\mathcal P.
\]

Degree-one homogeneity gives

\[
\sum_{v\ne0}N(v)^{-s}
=\sum_{d\ge1}d^{-s}\sum_{v_0\in\mathcal P}N(v_0)^{-s}.
\]

Nothing about the Prime-Circle chord metric, roots of unity, or solenoid dynamics is used in the factor `\zeta(s)`. It appears solely because primitive projective representatives are obtained by dividing a lattice vector by its gcd.

Equivalently, Möbius inversion gives

\[
Z_N^{\rm prim}(s)
=\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)d^{-s}Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)
=\frac{Z_N^{\rm lat}(s)}{\zeta(s)}.
\]

This identifies the exact source of the Riemann denominator: **primitive-ray projection itself**. The mechanism is the two-dimensional analogue of the classical reciprocal-zeta factors already repeatedly encountered in the Prime-Circle divisor/Möbius branches.

## 3. The `L^\infty` height does not cancel nontrivial Riemann zeros

For the standard projective height,

\[
Z_{\mathbb P^1,N_\infty}(s)
=4\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}.
\]

If `\rho` is a nontrivial zero of `\zeta`, then

\[
-1<\Re(\rho-1)<0.
\]

The Riemann zeta function has no zeros in that strip: by its functional equation such a zero would correspond to a zero in `\Re s>1`, apart from the known trivial zeros at negative even integers, which do not lie there. Hence

\[
\zeta(\rho-1)\ne0.
\]

Every nontrivial Riemann zero therefore becomes a pole, with the same order, of the meromorphic continuation of the standard projective-height zeta.

This is an exact zero locator, but it is not a Hilbert--Pólya mechanism. The complex number `\rho` is not an eigenvalue of the positive self-adjoint height operator; it is a pole of a meromorphically continued trace because the classical totient coefficients already have Dirichlet series `\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)`.

The same warning already appears in PC-066 for the transverse conductor. Projective properness changes the compactness problem, not the provenance of the zeta zeros.

## 4. Quadratic metrics return to Epstein/Eisenstein theory

The Euclidean or quadratic alternative does not escape the classical boundary. For positive-definite `T`,

\[
E_T(t)
=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}}(v^TTv)^{-t}
\]

is the Epstein zeta function. Its meromorphic continuation and functional equation are classical. The primitive/projective restriction simply divides this lattice zeta by `2\zeta(2t)`; with the spectral parameter `s=2t`, this is the formula in the claim.

Thus an apparent functional equation obtained by choosing a quadratic projective metric is inherited from classical Epstein/Eisenstein theory plus the primitive-lattice factor. It is not a new functional equation forced by Prime Circle.

The square Euclidean example is especially diagnostic: the numerator introduces the Dirichlet beta function `L(s/2,\chi_{-4})`. That extra `L`-function comes from the chosen square lattice metric, not from the roots-of-unity birth geometry. A different quadratic form changes the Epstein numerator while leaving the primitive `1/\zeta(s)` denominator mechanism intact.

## 5. Adversarial controls

Unlike the regular scalar calculi ruled out by PC-068, `H_N` really has compact resolvent. The negative result is therefore not another compactness obstruction. It shows instead that the most canonical scalar **repair** returns directly to a known height/visible-lattice zeta.

\[

=4\frac{\zeta(\alpha s-1)}{\zeta(\alpha s)}-1.

The apparent location of denominator poles in the spectral-zeta variable scales with `\alpha`. Therefore a critical-line normalization cannot be inferred from the existence of a proper projective height alone.

The affine solenoid basis omits `\infty`. Adding it changes the spectral zeta by exactly `+1` when `N(1,0)=1`. This entire correction cannot create, cancel, or move nontrivial poles. The clean `\mathbb P^1` formula is therefore a harmless projective completion, not an extra arithmetic mechanism.

For every degree-one homogeneous gauge the denominator is generated before any analytic continuation, directly by the gcd decomposition. A more complicated norm can make `Z_N^{\rm lat}` analytically complicated, but that complexity belongs to the chosen lattice norm. It does not alter the origin of `1/\zeta(s)`.
