**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the most direct source-forced refinement-incidence escape left open by PC-369. After passing from the raw radial cyclotomic potentials to the inversion-even centered profiles used in PC-350--PC-355, the exact prime-child recursion remains homogeneous under the global transverse sign involution. The apparent inhomogeneous calibration term is not an external fixed defect: it is exactly the centered doubled-prime shell `W_{2p}`. Therefore the canonical cyclotomic refinement law cannot break the matched reciprocal control of PC-355.

This result is deliberately narrower than a no-go for every shell-label or cross-level operator. It closes the exact linear child relation forced by cyclotomic refinement, including the tempting repair in which centering against shell `2` seems to leave a fixed calibration cocycle. Nonlinear pointwise relations, genuinely new boundary/domain structure, noncanonical truncation defects, and source-forced operators not generated by the cyclotomic child law remain outside the theorem.

## 1. Centered radial profiles and the live parity question

For `0<r<1`, write

\[
X_n(r):=\log \Phi_n(r),
\qquad n>1,
\tag{1}
\]

and use the centered transverse profile of PC-350,

\[
\boxed{
W_n(r):=X_n(r)-\varphi(n)X_2(r).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Since `Phi_n` is reciprocal for `n>1`, PC-350 gives

\[
W_n(r)=W_n(r^{-1}),
\tag{3}
\]

so the universal radial scaling term has been removed. In particular `W_2=0`.

PC-355 identifies the relevant matched reciprocal control on the transverse shell family as the common sign involution

\[
\boxed{
W_m\longmapsto \widetilde W_m:=-W_m,
\qquad m>2.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The later PC-369 result shows that universal tensor/Hopf/Lie constructions are equivariant under this common one-particle unitary and leaves a source-forced divisor/refinement incidence relation as one possible way to break it. The exact cyclotomic child law is the canonical first test of that possibility.

## 2. Raw cyclotomic refinement produces an apparent calibration defect

Let `p` be prime and `n>1`. The standard cyclotomic recursion is

\[
\Phi_{pn}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\Phi_n(z^p)/\Phi_n(z),&p\nmid n,\\[2mm]
\Phi_n(z^p),&p\mid n.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
X_{pn}(r)=
\begin{cases}
X_n(r^p)-X_n(r),&p\nmid n,\\[2mm]
X_n(r^p),&p\mid n.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the radial refinement identity already underlying PC-186. Substituting (2), together with

\[
\varphi(pn)=
\begin{cases}
(p-1)\varphi(n),&p\nmid n,\\
p\varphi(n),&p\mid n,
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
W_{pn}(r)=
\begin{cases}
W_n(r^p)-W_n(r)+\varphi(n)C_p(r),&p\nmid n,\\[2mm]
W_n(r^p)+\varphi(n)C_p(r),&p\mid n,
\end{cases}}
\tag{8}
\]

where the apparent centering defect is

\[
\boxed{
C_p(r):=X_2(r^p)-pX_2(r)
=\log(1+r^p)-p\log(1+r).
}
\tag{9}
\]

If one treats `X_2` as a fixed calibration background while flipping all other centered profiles, (8) appears to supply exactly the extra source tensor sought after PC-369: `C_p` would remain fixed while every `W_n` changed sign. That interpretation is false because `C_p` is already an ordinary transverse shell.

## 3. The centering defect is exactly the doubled-prime shell

The key identity is

\[
\boxed{
C_p(r)=W_{2p}(r)
\qquad\text{for every prime }p.
}
\tag{10}
\]

For odd `p`, the classical relation

\[
\Phi_{2p}(z)=\frac{\Phi_2(z^p)}{\Phi_2(z)}
=\frac{1+z^p}{1+z}
\tag{11}
\]

and `varphi(2p)=p-1` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{2p}(r)
&=X_{2p}(r)-(p-1)X_2(r)\\
&=X_2(r^p)-X_2(r)-(p-1)X_2(r)\\
&=X_2(r^p)-pX_2(r)\\
&=C_p(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

For `p=2`, `Phi_4(z)=Phi_2(z^2)=1+z^2` and `varphi(4)=2`, so

\[
W_4(r)=X_2(r^2)-2X_2(r)=C_2(r).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus no new calibration direction has been generated. The term created by centering the `p`-refinement is exactly the already existing shell indexed by `2p`. It is inversion-even by (3), lies in the same transverse family as every other nontrivial `W_m`, and is therefore acted on by the same matched-control sign involution.

Equation (8) becomes the closed homogeneous shell relation

\[
\boxed{
W_{pn}(r)
=
W_n(r^p)
-\mathbf 1_{p\nmid n}W_n(r)
+\varphi(n)W_{2p}(r).
}
\tag{14}
\]

The cases `n=2` simply reduce to identities because `W_2=0`; they introduce no exceptional calibration component.

## 4. Exact equivariance under the PC-355 matched control

Define the source relation functional

\[
\mathcal R_{p,n}(W)(r)
:=
W_{pn}(r)-W_n(r^p)
+\mathbf 1_{p\nmid n}W_n(r)
-\varphi(n)W_{2p}(r).
\tag{15}
\]

The cyclotomic recursion says exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{p,n}(W)=0.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Under the matched control (4), every profile appearing in (15), including `W_{2p}`, receives the same sign. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{p,n}(\widetilde W)
=-\mathcal R_{p,n}(W)
=0.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Hence the complete family of exact prime-child relations is invariant under the source/control involution. In operator language, once the state space contains the refinement-closed transverse shell family, the incidence constraint is a homogeneous linear subspace preserved by the global parity `J=-I` on that transverse sector. No spectral function, norm, nullity, determinant, or constraint variety formed solely from this exact linear relation can distinguish the source from the matched reciprocal control.

This is the important distinction from artificially freezing the term (9). If `C_p` were declared external and fixed while the other transverse shells were flipped, (8) would indeed fail to be equivariant. But (10) proves that such a rule does not apply the PC-355 control to the full source state: it singles out the genuine transverse shell `2p` and refuses to transform it. The apparent parity breaking is therefore introduced by the representation choice, not forced by Prime-Circle geometry.

## 5. Finite truncations can manufacture a boundary defect, but it is not intrinsic

A finite shell set need not be closed under the maps

\[
n\mapsto pn,
\qquad
p\mapsto2p.
\tag{18}
\]

If one projects (14) to such a truncated set, the missing `W_{2p}` or `W_{pn}` term appears as an inhomogeneous boundary remainder. That remainder can break the projected sign symmetry, but the full exact relation shows where it came from: it is leakage into a shell omitted by the cutoff.

Consequently a finite-section parity defect is not by itself a new source invariant. To become relevant it would need an independently forced truncation, boundary condition, or renormalization rule whose mathematical content survives enlargement of the shell family and matched nonarithmetic controls. Merely discarding the restoring doubled-prime shell would repackage information loss as symmetry breaking.

This leaves room for a genuinely source-derived domain or boundary relation, one of the other possibilities listed after PC-369, but it prevents using ordinary conductor truncation as evidence for such a relation without an additional argument.

## 8. Falsification checks and surviving frontier

The result has direct exact tests.

1. For arbitrary primes `p` and levels `n>1`, verify (8) directly from (5) and the two cases of (7).
2. Verify `C_p=W_{2p}` separately for odd `p` using (11) and for `p=2` using `Phi_4(z)=1+z^2`.
3. Substitute `W_m -> -W_m` simultaneously for every `m>2` and check that (14) changes only by an overall sign.
4. Numerically or symbolically test mixed cases with `p|n` and `p∤n`; the identities are exact polynomial/logarithmic identities and require no asymptotic limit.
5. Any claimed finite-section parity defect must restore to zero when the omitted `2p` and child shells are reinstated, unless an additional source-forced boundary/domain condition has genuinely altered the problem.

A falsifier would be a prime/level pair for which (10) or (14) fails, or a source-native refinement relation not generated by the standard child law whose extra tensor is not transformed by the PC-355 control.

The surviving frontier after PC-369 is therefore narrower. The canonical **linear cyclotomic refinement incidence** is not the missing symmetry breaker. A viable mechanism must use additional geometry that is not swallowed by the global transverse sign: a genuinely nonlinear pointwise relation with source-forced asymmetry, a physical boundary/domain condition, an order/positivity structure that is not transported by the matched control, a fixed background tensor whose fixedness is itself geometrically compelled, or another cross-level operation beyond the homogeneous child recursion. Such a mechanism must still pass the classical cyclotomic/Bost--Connes and matched-control audits before it can count as progress toward an RH-relevant operator.
