# PC-420 — bounded radial diagonal repair still has no compact sector

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-CROSSED-PRODUCT-BOUNDARY` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the most direct source-derived repair left open by PC-419. Let

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb R,du),
\qquad
D:=\log\mathbb Q_{>0},
\qquad
(S_hf)(u)=f(u+h).
\]

Let `C` be any unital C*-subalgebra of `L^\infty(R)` that is invariant under translations by `D`, and let `M_f` denote multiplication by `f`. Define

\[
\mathcal B(\mathcal C,D)
:=C^*\!\left(\{M_f:f\in\mathcal C\}\cup\{S_h:h\in D\}\right)
\subset B(\mathcal H).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B(\mathcal C,D)\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H)=\{0\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

The result remains true for the maximal bounded diagonal choice `C=L^\infty(R)`. In particular, adjoining **any collection of bounded radial Prime-Circle source profiles**, all of their rational-log translates, or even every bounded Borel function of log-position to the discrete scale/refinement shifts cannot create a nonzero compact, trace-class, or finite-rank sector in operator norm.

This strengthens PC-419. PC-419 ruled out compact operators for the Weyl-descendant algebra generated by log modulations and rational-log translations. The theorem below shows that replacing the modulation algebra by an arbitrarily rich bounded radial diagonal algebra does not help. A viable compact/Fredholm repair must therefore introduce something genuinely non-diagonal in log scale — for example a source-forced integrated scale kernel, smoothing/resolvent operator, angular/chord/old-new carrier, or nonlinear cross-shell construction — rather than merely a more informative bounded radial observable.

## 1. Exact source-to-operator specialization

PC-179 starts directly from the cyclotomic radial field

\[
\rho_n(x)
=-\frac{d}{dx}\log\Phi_n(e^{-x})
=-\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\frac{d}{e^{dx}-1}.
\tag{2}
\]

For fixed `n`, PC-179 proves

\[
\lim_{x\to0^+}\rho_n(x)=\frac{\varphi(n)}2,
\qquad
\rho_n(x)=O(e^{-x})\quad(x\to\infty),
\tag{3}
\]

so the log-scale profile

\[
g_n(u):=\rho_n(e^u)
\tag{4}
\]

is bounded. Thus `M_{g_n}` is a bounded, source-native radial observable on the same shell-2 Hilbert space used in PC-405--PC-419. Let `C_n` be the unital C*-algebra generated by all `D`-translates of `g_n`. It is automatically `D`-translation invariant, so the theorem applies to

\[
\mathcal B_n
:=C^*\!\left(\{M_f:f\in\mathcal C_n\}\cup\{S_h:h\in D\}\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Nothing has been spectralized by hand here: the diagonal observable comes from the exact cyclotomic logarithmic field, while the shifts are the exact rational refinements already derived in PC-405--PC-418.

## 2. Normal form for the enlarged algebra

Translation covariance gives

\[
S_hM_fS_h^*=M_{\tau_hf},
\qquad
(\tau_hf)(u)=f(u+h).
\tag{6}
\]

Because `C` is invariant under every `tau_h`, each algebraic word in the generators normal-orders to one coefficient followed by one shift. Hence the finite sums

\[
\mathcal B_0
=
\left\{
\sum_{j=1}^N M_{f_j}S_{h_j}:
N<\infty,\ f_j\in\mathcal C,\ h_j\in D
\right\}
\tag{7}
\]

form a dense `*`-subalgebra of `B(C,D)`.

This normal form is the first important control. The richer diagonal coefficient algebra can carry arbitrarily complicated bounded radial information, but it does not alter the fact that every norm approximation uses only finitely many individual scale shifts.

## 3. Dual modulation produces arbitrarily large norm recurrences

For `a in R`, let

\[
(E_a\xi)(u)=e^{iau}\xi(u)
\tag{8}
\]

and define the external conjugation

\[
\gamma_a(T):=E_{-a}TE_a.
\tag{9}
\]

The unitary `E_a` need not belong to `B(C,D)`. Nevertheless it normalizes the algebra, because it commutes with every multiplier and phases each shift:

\[
\gamma_a(M_f)=M_f,
\qquad
\gamma_a(S_h)=e^{iah}S_h.
\tag{10}
\]

For a finite sum

\[
T=\sum_{j=1}^N M_{f_j}S_{h_j},
\]

we therefore have

\[
\gamma_a(T)-T
=
\sum_{j=1}^N
(e^{iah_j}-1)M_{f_j}S_{h_j}.
\tag{11}
\]

For the finite set `{h_1,...,h_N}`, simultaneous recurrence on the compact torus gives arbitrarily large `a` with

\[
\max_j|e^{iah_j}-1|<\varepsilon.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus every finite sum has arbitrarily large near-periods under `gamma_a`.

Now let `A in B(C,D)`. Choose `T_m in B_0` with

\[
\|A-T_m\|<1/m.
\]

Choose `a_m>m` satisfying the recurrence for the finite shift set occurring in `T_m` strongly enough that

\[
\|\gamma_{a_m}(T_m)-T_m\|<1/m.
\]

Since `gamma_a` is isometric,

\[
\boxed{
\|\gamma_{a_m}(A)-A\|<3/m,
\qquad
a_m\to\infty.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Crucially, the recurrence is completely insensitive to the coefficient functions `f_j`. It survives even when the diagonal algebra is all of `L^\infty(R)`.

## 4. Compactness is incompatible with the recurrence

For fixed `\xi,\eta in L^2(R)`,

\[
\langle E_a\xi,\eta\rangle
=
\int_{\mathbb R}e^{iau}\xi(u)\overline{\eta(u)}\,du.
\tag{14}
\]

The product `xi overline{eta}` lies in `L^1(R)`, so the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma gives

\[
E_a\xi\rightharpoonup0
\qquad(|a|\to\infty).
\tag{15}
\]

Assume that

\[
K\in\mathcal B(\mathcal C,D)\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H).
\]

For any fixed `xi`, compactness and (15) imply

\[
\|KE_{a_m}\xi\|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]

On the other hand, from (9),

\[
KE_a=E_a\gamma_a(K),
\]

and therefore

\[
\|KE_{a_m}\xi\|
=
\|\gamma_{a_m}(K)\xi\|
\longrightarrow
\|K\xi\|
\tag{17}
\]

by (13). Equations (16) and (17) force `Kxi=0` for every `xi`, hence `K=0`. This proves (1).

The argument does not use continuity, decay, smoothness, arithmetic structure, or any special spectral property of the diagonal profiles. Boundedness and multiplication structure are enough.

## 5. Maximal bounded radial repair is therefore source-blind

Take first the actual cyclotomic algebra `C_n` generated by (4) and its rational-log translates. Equation (1) gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_n\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H)=\{0\}.}
\tag{18}
\]

Now enlarge further to the maximal bounded radial diagonal algebra

\[
\mathcal C=L^\infty(\mathbb R).
\]

The same proof gives

\[
\boxed{
C^*\!\left(L^\infty(\mathbb R),\{S_h:h\in D\}\right)
\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H)
=\{0\}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus no special bounded radial signature of the cyclotomic source can rescue the compact sector: the no-go already holds after replacing that source by **all possible bounded radial data**. This is a stronger matched-control failure than merely observing that a particular transform collapses to classical zeta data.

Every trace-class, Hilbert--Schmidt, or finite-rank operator is compact, so

\[
\mathcal B(\mathcal C,D)\cap\mathcal S_p=\{0\},
\qquad 1\le p<\infty.
\tag{20}
\]

Consequently a Fredholm determinant `det(I+T)` with `T` produced inside this norm-closed algebra is necessarily trivial. Likewise, an unbounded self-adjoint operator whose nonreal resolvent lies in the algebra cannot have compact resolvent or an isolated finite-multiplicity spectral sector supplied by such a compact resolvent.

## 6. Relation to PC-418 and PC-419

PC-419 used translation conjugacy to prove that the smaller norm-closed Weyl-descendant algebra has no compact sector. The present proof uses the dual symmetry: **modulation conjugacy** fixes every radial multiplier and only phases the individual scale shifts. This is why arbitrary bounded diagonal enrichment does not change the obstruction.

PC-418 gives the opposite endpoint. After strong/weak completion, the available multiplication and refinement structure generates all of `B(H)`, which is too large to provide a source-selective spectral principle. The three relevant completion regimes are therefore now sharper:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{bounded radial diagonal + individual scale shifts, norm closure}
&\Rightarrow&\text{no nonzero compact sector},\\[1mm]
\text{Weyl-descendant norm closure}
&\Rightarrow&\text{no nonzero compact sector (PC-419)},\\[1mm]
\text{von Neumann / strong-weak closure}
&\Rightarrow&B(\mathcal H)\text{ (PC-418)}.
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]

So adding more bounded radial information does not create an intermediate arithmetic selection mechanism between the two failed closure regimes.

## 7. The continuous crossed-product boundary identifies what is still missing

There is an important classical control that prevents over-reading (19). For the **continuous** translation action of a locally compact group `G` on itself,

\[
C_0(G)\rtimes G\cong\mathcal K(L^2(G))
\tag{22}
\]

in the regular representation. For `G=R`, this is the standard Stone--von Neumann / transformation-group compact-operator model.

This does not contradict (19). In the continuous crossed product, the translation representation is used through **integrated** compactly supported coefficient functions of the group variable; the individual translation unitaries live naturally in the multiplier algebra. By contrast, `B(C,D)` is the operator-norm algebra generated by individual discrete rational-log shifts and bounded multipliers. The map `h -> S_h` is strongly continuous but not norm continuous, so an integral over a continuum of shifts is not obtained as an operator-norm Riemann-sum limit of those individual unitaries.

The distinction is exactly the surviving research boundary. A source-forced operator of the form

\[
T=\int_{\mathbb R} M_{k(h,\cdot)}S_h\,dh
\tag{23}
\]

with enough intrinsic regularity/decay could escape the theorem and can in principle be compact or smoothing. But the measure, kernel `k`, and continuous scale coupling would have to be derived from Prime-Circle geometry. Inserting them merely to obtain compactness would be an arbitrary spectral wrapper.

The same boundary is visible in the resolvent-algebra literature: adjoining resolvents of genuine conjugate position/momentum combinations can recover compact operators, whereas the unitary Weyl algebra alone does not provide that compact ideal. Therefore PC-420 rules out bounded diagonal enrichment, **not** every possible source-forced nonlocal or resolvent enlargement.

## 8. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract operator-algebra ingredients are classical. Standard transformation-group crossed-product theory gives (22); see, for example, Dana P. Williams, *Crossed Products of C*-Algebras*, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 134, AMS, 2007, together with the Stone--von Neumann/Green-imprimitivity description of the translation action.

D. Buchholz and H. Grundling, **The Resolvent Algebra: A New Approach to Canonical Quantum Systems**, *Journal of Functional Analysis* 254 (2008), 2725--2779, DOI `10.1016/j.jfa.2008.02.011`, develops the resolvent algebra precisely to retain analytic and ideal structure absent from the ordinary Weyl algebra. D. Buchholz and T. D. H. van Nuland, **The basic resolvents of position and momentum operators form a total set in the resolvent algebra**, *Letters in Mathematical Physics* 113 (2023), article 119, DOI `10.1007/s11005-023-01741-4`, proves in particular that compact operators are norm approximable by linear combinations of basic resolvents. These provide a direct prior-art control for the claim that a genuinely different, non-diagonal/resolvent generator class can change the compact-ideal story.

No historical novelty is claimed for crossed products, dual/gauge conjugations, compact-torus recurrence, the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma, or the continuous translation crossed product. The persisted contribution is the **Prime-Circle-specific no-go obtained by combining the exact source-derived bounded radial observable of PC-179 with the exact rational refinement shifts of PC-405--PC-419**: even the maximal possible bounded radial diagonal enrichment cannot create the compact spectral sector that PC-419 left as a possible source-derived repair.

The novelty audit therefore classifies PC-420 as a project-specific decisive negative inside classical operator-algebra machinery, not as a new theorem about crossed products.

## 9. Falsification, limitations, and RH relevance

The main theorem is falsified by any explicit nonzero compact operator in the norm closure of finite sums

\[
\sum_{j=1}^N M_{f_j}S_{h_j},
\qquad f_j\in\mathcal C,\ h_j\in D.
\tag{24}
\]

Such an example would have to defeat the modulation recurrence (13) and compact weak-to-norm decay (15)--(17). Because the proof applies already to `C=L^\infty(R)`, changing the bounded radial cyclotomic profile cannot do so.

The theorem does **not** rule out unbounded source operators, integrated continuous-scale kernels such as (23), momentum/resolvent-type observables, angular/chord/old-new operators before radial collapse, nonlinear source-dependent operators, or cross-level constructions that do not reduce to a bounded diagonal coefficient followed by finitely many individual scale shifts. Those are precisely the mechanisms that still need an exact geometry-to-operator derivation before any spectral claim is meaningful.

No new zeta zero, functional equation, critical-line selector, or explicit-formula mechanism is produced here. The substantive progress is negative but structural: **bounded radial source information, no matter how rich, cannot repair the norm-closed shell-2/refinement algebra into a Hilbert--Pólya or Fredholm-determinant carrier.** Any surviving Prime-Circle spectral route must leave that representation class rather than enriching its diagonal coefficients.
