# PC-037 — rotation-invariant linear harmonic forms collapse to universal divisor tails

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for shell-independent rotation-invariant linear/quadratic operators acting on the harmonic cyclotomic fields. The Fourier diagonalization, Ramanujan-sum divisor formula, polylogarithms, and resulting Dirichlet-series factorization are classical ingredients; no theorem-level novelty is claimed for them.

PC-027 closed the canonical all-mode Dirichlet energy, but deliberately left open other genuinely two-dimensional or nonlocal operators. This finding closes a much larger natural class: changing the radial/nonlocal linear operator while keeping it independent of the shell and invariant under rotations cannot create new prime-circle arithmetic. On the harmonic cyclotomic subspace, every such operator reduces to a scalar Fourier multiplier, and the primitive-shell dependence then collapses to a finite Möbius/divisor recombination of one universal tail sequence.

## 1. The operator class

For `n>1`, inside the unit disk choose the analytic logarithm

\[
F_n(z):=\Log\Phi_n(z),\qquad F_n(0)=0.
\]

The classical Ramanujan expansion is

\[
\boxed{
F_n(z)=-\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{c_n(k)}{k}z^k,
\qquad |z|<1,
}
\]

where `c_n(k)` is the Ramanujan sum of the primitive `n`-th roots. The physical logarithmic potential is `U_n=Re F_n`.

Let `B` be any sesquilinear form on the analytic harmonic subspace containing the monomials `z^k`, and assume only that it is independent of `n` and invariant under rotations:

\[
B(R_\theta f,R_\theta g)=B(f,g),
\qquad
(R_\theta f)(z)=f(e^{i\theta}z).
\]

This includes, whenever they are defined on the relevant fields, quadratic forms generated by shell-independent rotation-equivariant linear operators: Dirichlet-to-Neumann and fractional boundary multipliers, radial Sobolev energies, convolution/Green/resolvent kernels, and nonlocal disk operators restricted to harmonic data.

No positivity or locality is assumed. Convergence is the only analytic requirement for the infinite sums below; every identity is already exact for finite Fourier truncations.

## 2. Rotation invariance forces mode-by-mode diagonalization

Since

\[
R_\theta z^k=e^{ik\theta}z^k,
\]

rotation invariance gives

\[
B(z^k,z^\ell)
=e^{i(k-\ell)\theta}B(z^k,z^\ell)
\]

for every `theta`. Hence

\[
\boxed{B(z^k,z^\ell)=0\quad(k\ne\ell).}
\]

Write

\[
\beta_k:=B(z^k,z^k).
\]

Thus even a genuinely two-dimensional or nonlocal linear operator has only one effective scalar `beta_k` on each harmonic mode of the cyclotomic fields. For the real potentials `U_n`, the same statement follows from the real two-dimensional sine/cosine representation of each nonzero angular frequency.

With a radial cutoff `0<r<1`, put `F_{n,r}(z)=F_n(rz)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
B(F_{m,r},F_{n,r})
=
\sum_{k\ge1}
\beta_k\frac{c_m(k)c_n(k)}{k^2}r^{2k}.
}
\]

The entire spatial operator has therefore been compressed to a universal Fourier multiplier before any arithmetic simplification is used.

## 3. Exact finite divisor reduction for an arbitrary multiplier

Use the classical divisor formula

\[
c_m(k)
=\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d\mid k}}
d\,\mu(m/d).
\]

Set

\[
a_k(r):=\beta_k\frac{r^{2k}}{k^2},
\qquad
W_L(r):=\sum_{j\ge1}a_{Lj}(r),
\]

whenever the tail converges. Expanding the two Ramanujan sums and collecting the integers divisible by both `d` and `e` yields

\[
\boxed{
B(F_{m,r},F_{n,r})
=
\sum_{d\mid m}\sum_{e\mid n}
 d e\,\mu(m/d)\mu(n/e)
 W_{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)}(r).
}
\]

This is the decisive structural collapse. For fixed `m,n`, **all shell dependence is a finite divisor-lattice recombination**. Every analytic, radial, nonlocal, or spectral choice made in `B` is contained in the single shell-independent sequence `W_L`.

Consequently, if a proposed operator has interesting poles, zeros, or a functional equation already encoded in `beta_k`, those features came from the chosen operator. Primitive-root geometry only applies the displayed finite Möbius/lcm filter to them.

PC-027 is one special member of this theorem: for the analytic Dirichlet form `beta_k=k`, the tail is logarithmic and the formula reduces to its finite divisor-log expression.

## 4. The homogeneous spectral family produces zeta only as a universal scalar

A particularly tempting attempt is to introduce a complex spectral parameter through the homogeneous multiplier

\[
\beta_k(s)=k^{2-s}.
\]

Then

\[
Q_s^{(r)}(m,n)
:=B_s(F_{m,r},F_{n,r})
=\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{c_m(k)c_n(k)}{k^s}r^{2k}.
\]

At every finite cutoff `0<r<1`, the divisor reduction is

\[
\boxed{
Q_s^{(r)}(m,n)
=
\sum_{d\mid m}\sum_{e\mid n}
 d e\,\mu(m/d)\mu(n/e)
 \ell^{-s}\operatorname{Li}_s(r^{2\ell}),
\qquad
\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e).
}
\]

For fixed `0<r<1`, `Li_s(r^{2\ell})` is entire in `s`. Thus the finite-radius geometry does **not** contain Riemann-zero singularities or a critical line in this family.

Only after removing the geometric cutoff, and initially for `Re(s)>1`, does

\[
\operatorname{Li}_s(r^{2\ell})\longrightarrow\zeta(s).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_s(m,n)
:=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{c_m(k)c_n(k)}{k^s}
=
\zeta(s) H_{m,n}(s),
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
H_{m,n}(s)
=
\sum_{d\mid m}\sum_{e\mid n}
 d e\,\mu(m/d)\mu(n/e)
 \operatorname{lcm}(d,e)^{-s}.
}
\]

For fixed `m,n`, `H_{m,n}` is a finite entire Dirichlet/exponential polynomial.

This makes the status of the apparent zeta spectrum unambiguous: `zeta(s)` is a **common scalar factor produced by the homogeneous Fourier tail**, not an eigenvalue condition generated by the primitive shells.

For any finite collection `S` of `N` shell levels,

\[
\boxed{
Q_S(s)=\zeta(s)H_S(s),
\qquad
\det Q_S(s)=\zeta(s)^N\det H_S(s).
}
\]

After meromorphic continuation, every nontrivial zero of zeta annihilates the entire matrix simultaneously. That maximal common degeneracy is the opposite of an independently generated Hilbert--Pólya-type spectrum: the zero set has been inserted through the universal Fourier multiplier tail.

Moreover, for complex `s` on the critical strip the analytically continued multiplier is not a positive Hilbert-space quadratic form in general. Calling these common zeros a `Gram spectrum` would therefore be a spectral wrapper, not a positivity mechanism for RH.

## 5. Why the critical line and functional equation do not emerge

The homogeneous family has no intrinsic preference for `Re(s)=1/2`. The parameter `s` was introduced by choosing `beta_k(s)=k^{2-s}`. Replacing that multiplier by any other spectral family changes the analytic factor accordingly.

Likewise, the gamma factor and the reflection `s <-> 1-s` of the completed zeta function do not follow from rotation invariance or from the divisor reduction. They would have to be placed in the multiplier, supplied by an additional archimedean/global construction, or produced by a genuinely different nonlinear/cross-level mechanism.

This is stronger than merely observing that one can recover a classical Dirichlet series. It shows that **the whole shell-independent rotation-invariant linear operator class has no place for additional prime-circle arithmetic beyond a finite divisor filter of its universal mode response**.

## 6. Relation to earlier no-go results

- PC-021 ruled out regular linear probes and independently transformed multilinear probes.
- PC-024 ruled out fixed finite same-shell Fourier polynomial nonlinearities followed by a level Dirichlet transform.
- PC-027 ruled out the canonical all-mode quadratic Dirichlet energy and basis-invariant finite subspace data.
- The present result allows an arbitrary shell-independent rotation-invariant linear/nonlocal operator before pairing the full harmonic fields. It therefore closes the natural idea that a more sophisticated two-dimensional Green, fractional, resolvent, or pseudodifferential kernel could escape PC-027 while retaining the circle symmetry.

The distinction matters: the operator may be highly nonlocal in the disk and may use infinitely many modes. What kills the route is not locality but the combination of **linearity + shell independence + rotation symmetry**.

## 7. Prior art and novelty audit

The ingredients are classical.

1. Rotation invariance diagonalizes the `U(1)` characters `z^k`; this is elementary harmonic/representation theory on the circle.
2. Ramanujan's divisor formula for `c_n(k)` is classical.
3. Products/correlations of Ramanujan sums and their divisor/lcm reductions are established arithmetic territory; László Tóth, *Sums of products of Ramanujan sums*, Ann. Univ. Ferrara 58 (2012), 183--197, DOI 10.1007/s11565-011-0143-3, is the existing source anchor in `SOURCES.md`.
4. Dirichlet generating series of Ramanujan sums and the appearance of zeta factors are classical; the standard identities are already recorded in `SOURCES.md`.

A directed search around products of Ramanujan sums, weighted averages, and Ramanujan Dirichlet series found substantial classical prior art rather than a distinct prime-circle RH mechanism. No novelty is claimed for the algebraic identity as an abstract theorem.

The durable contribution is a **scope theorem for this research construction**: a broad class of apparently richer two-dimensional/nonlocal linear operators is provably no richer arithmetically than a universal multiplier followed by the same finite Möbius/divisor machinery already visible in simpler probes.

## 8. Boundary of the no-go

This result does **not** cover operators that explicitly break at least one of its hypotheses. In particular it leaves open:

- shell-dependent metrics or operators whose kernel itself changes with `n` in a way forced by the geometry;
- nonlinear spatial operators that mix Fourier modes before the quadratic evaluation;
- nonseparable cross-level operators that act simultaneously on several shell indices before harmonic diagonalization;
- pointed/rotation-breaking constructions in which the common vertex `1` remains an active degree of freedom rather than merely a coordinate choice;
- nonlinear global uniformization, monodromy, Liouville, or Weil--Petersson data such as the surviving part of PC-017;
- a rigorously defined infinite-dimensional anomaly in which the operator/regularization itself is not represented by a fixed multiplier sequence on the harmonic subspace.

Those are genuine ways to escape the theorem. Merely replacing the Dirichlet energy by a more elaborate shell-independent radial, Green, resolvent, fractional, or pseudodifferential linear kernel is not.

## 9. Audit / falsification test

The claim is exact under its stated hypotheses. A counterexample would require either:

1. a shell-independent rotation-invariant sesquilinear form on the harmonic monomials for which `B(z^k,z^ell)` is nonzero with `k != ell`; or
2. a convergent multiplier sequence for which substituting the classical divisor formula for both Ramanujan sums fails to give the finite `lcm`-tail identity in Section 3.

For the homogeneous family, one may additionally check directly for `Re(s)>1` that numerical partial sums of `sum_k c_m(k)c_n(k) k^{-s}` agree with `zeta(s) H_{m,n}(s)` for arbitrary composite pairs. Any failure there would falsify the specialized factorization.

## Research consequence

A large natural escape route from the earlier Fourier/Dirichlet no-go results is now closed:

\[
\boxed{
\text{harmonic primitive-shell fields}
\to
\text{shell-independent rotation-invariant linear/nonlocal operator}
\to
\text{quadratic/spectral form}
\to
\text{new RH mechanism}
}
\]

cannot work without importing the interesting analytic structure through the operator itself.

The surviving prime-circle frontier is therefore forced toward geometry that is genuinely **shell-dependent, nonlinear, cross-level, or pointed before diagonalization**, rather than merely more sophisticated linear harmonic analysis on the fixed circle.
