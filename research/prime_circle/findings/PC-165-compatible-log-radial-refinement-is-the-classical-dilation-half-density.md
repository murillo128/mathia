# PC-165 — compatible log-radial refinement is the classical dilation half-density

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for treating the most canonical power-map coupling of Prime-Circle radial inversion and all-level refinement as a new critical-line or Hilbert–Pólya mechanism.

PC-029 left a precise escape after showing that Fourier/Mellin spectralization of the inversion-even cyclotomic potential in `x=log r` has only elementary Ramanujan/divisor data: a future mechanism could in principle force a half-density and couple the radial spectral coordinate to arithmetic refinement rather than shift the spectral center to `1/2` by hand. PC-064 subsequently identified the compatible angular refinement tower with the classical arithmetic solenoid `Sigma_Q`. The natural way to combine those two structures is to retain the entire punctured plane `C^*` at every refinement level and use the same power maps that generate the Prime-Circle tower.

That construction does force a `1/2`, but in the strongest possible classical way. The all-level punctured-plane completion splits exactly as a direct product of ordinary log radius and the arithmetic solenoid. The raw power-map pullbacks on Haar `L^2` are the standard contraction representation of dilations, whose generalized eigenfunctions have exponent `-1/2+it`; formally summing the integer pullbacks produces `zeta(1/2-it)`. This is exactly the Berry–Keating dilation mechanism, now acting on **distance in log radius from the unit circle**, and it is already present on the solenoid-constant sector. Its self-adjoint dilation generator has purely continuous spectrum, while the unweighted integer-dilation sum that would expose `zeta(1/2-it)` is not a bounded operator and does not even converge strongly on a simple compactly supported test function.

Thus Prime Circle can derive the critical half-density from canonical power-map/Haar geometry, but that derivation does not quantize the Riemann zeros and does not use primitive-shell arithmetic. The apparent bridge classicalizes before it becomes an RH mechanism.

## 1. The compatible punctured-plane tower splits exactly

For positive integers ordered by divisibility, define

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}
:=\varprojlim_{m\mid n}
\left(\mathbb C^\times,
\ p_{n,m}(z)=z^{n/m}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

This uses no new structure beyond the original Prime-Circle power maps; the unit-circle subsystem of (1) is exactly the inverse limit of PC-064.

Write every `z in C^*` uniquely as

\[
z=e^x u,
\qquad x=\log|z|\in\mathbb R,
\qquad u\in S^1.
\tag{2}
\]

Under the `k`-th power map,

\[
(x,u)\longmapsto(kx,u^k).
\tag{3}
\]

The radial bonding maps `x -> kx` are isomorphisms. If `(x_n)_n` is compatible, then comparison with level one gives

\[
x_1=n x_n,
\qquad
\boxed{x_n=\frac{x_1}{n}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Hence radial refinement contains no finite-adic branching at all. All branching lies in the angular coordinate.

Let

\[
\Sigma_{\mathbb Q}
=\varprojlim_{m\mid n}(S^1,u\mapsto u^{n/m})
\]

be the PC-064 arithmetic solenoid. For `x in R` and `sigma=(u_n)_n in Sigma_Q`, define

\[
\Psi(x,\sigma)_n=e^{x/n}u_n.
\tag{5}
\]

Compatibility is immediate:

\[
\left(e^{x/n}u_n\right)^{n/m}
=e^{x/m}u_m.
\]

Conversely, from `(z_n)_n in C_Q`, put `x=log|z_1|`. Since `z_n^n=z_1`, positivity of the modulus gives `|z_n|=e^{x/n}` uniquely, and

\[
u_n=e^{-x/n}z_n\in S^1
\]

forms a compatible solenoid point. The two constructions are continuous inverse homomorphisms. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}
\cong
\mathbb R\times\Sigma_{\mathbb Q}
}
\tag{6}
\]

as locally compact abelian topological groups.

This is already a strong information boundary. The most canonical completion that simultaneously retains radial position and every angular refinement does **not** create an intrinsic archimedean/finite-adic extension: the log-radius coordinate splits off as an ordinary real factor.

By Pontryagin duality and PC-064,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}}
\cong
\mathbb R\times\mathbb Q.
}
\tag{7}
\]

So linear translation-invariant harmonic analysis has a continuous real frequency and the classical rational solenoid frequency; no new discrete critical-line divisor is generated by the completion itself.

## 2. Prime-Circle interior/exterior inversion acts only on the radial factor

PC-003/PC-029 use the harmonic inversion that preserves the angular ray,

\[
\iota(z)=\frac1{\overline z}.
\tag{8}
\]

In log-polar coordinates this is simply

\[
(x,u)\longmapsto(-x,u).
\]

It commutes with every power bonding map and therefore acts on the inverse limit as

\[
\boxed{
\iota(x,\sigma)=(-x,\sigma).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus even after retaining the full compatible refinement tower, the spatial inversion responsible for PC-029's reflection does not exchange or mix any finite-adic/solenoidal coordinate. Its spectral action remains

\[
(\tau,q)\longmapsto(-\tau,q).
\tag{10}
\]

This sharpens PC-029. There, moving the reflection center from `lambda=0` to `s=1/2` was arbitrary. The power dynamics below does produce a genuine half-density `1/2`, but equation (9) shows that inversion itself still does not couple that half-density to arithmetic refinement or implement a Riemann functional equation.

## 3. Power refinement forces the `1/2` half-density on Haar `L^2`

For every integer `n>=2`, coordinatewise powering is an automorphism of the inverse limit. Under (6),

\[
D_n(x,\sigma)=(nx,D_n\sigma),
\tag{11}
\]

where `D_n` is the PC-064/069 solenoid automorphism. Haar measure on `C_Q` is, up to scale,

\[
dx\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.
\tag{12}
\]

The solenoid automorphism preserves `dmu_Sigma`, while `x -> nx` has Jacobian `n`. Therefore the raw pullback

\[
(C_n f)(x,\sigma)
:=f(nx,D_n\sigma)
\tag{13}
\]

satisfies exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|C_n f\|_2=n^{-1/2}\|f\|_2.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
U_n:=n^{1/2}C_n
}
\tag{15}
\]

is unitary, and the semigroup laws are exact:

\[
C_mC_n=C_{mn},
\qquad
U_mU_n=U_{mn}.
\tag{16}
\]

The exponent `1/2` is therefore not a relabeling here. It is forced by the Haar half-density of the real log-radial factor.

To expose what this means spectrally, restrict to either component `x>0` or `x<0` and put `y=|x|`. On the solenoid-constant sector, the generalized dilation waves are

\[
\psi_t(y)=y^{-1/2+it},
\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\tag{17}
\]

They satisfy

\[
\boxed{
C_n\psi_t
=n^{-1/2+it}\psi_t,
\qquad
U_n\psi_t
=n^{it}\psi_t.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Thus the critical half-density is real and canonically selected. But the variable is

\[
y=|\log r|,
\]

not the original radius `r`, the polygon level, a conductor, or a prime scale. The associated self-adjoint infinitesimal dilation operator on `L^2(R_+,dy)` is

\[
\boxed{
H_{\rm dil}
=-i\left(y\frac d{dy}+\frac12\right).
}
\tag{19}
\]

Under the unitary change of variables

\[
(Wf)(v)=e^{v/2}f(e^v),
\qquad v=\log y,
\tag{20}
\]

one has

\[
WH_{\rm dil}W^{-1}=-i\frac d{dv}.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(H_{\rm dil})=\mathbb R
}
\tag{22}
\]

with the ordinary absolutely continuous translation spectrum. The geometry has forced the Berry–Keating half-density but has not produced any quantization condition.

## 4. The formal zeta multiplier is exactly the classical integer-dilation sum

Equation (18) makes the seductive bridge completely explicit. Formally,

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}C_n\psi_t
=
\left(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-1/2+it}\right)\psi_t
=
\zeta\!\left(\frac12-it\right)\psi_t.
\tag{23}
\]

So the Prime-Circle power semigroup can be packaged to display `zeta` directly on the critical line without manually replacing a spectral coordinate by `s-1/2`.

However (23) is **not** a bounded spectral operator identity on the natural Haar Hilbert space. The unweighted operator series already diverges strongly on a trivial control vector.

Take

\[
f(y,\sigma)=\mathbf 1_{[1,2]}(y)
\]

in the solenoid-constant sector and let

\[
S_N=\sum_{n=1}^N C_n.
\]

For

\[
y\in\left[\frac4{3N},\frac3{2N}\right]
\]

and every integer

\[
\left\lceil\frac{3N}{4}\right\rceil\le n\le N,
\]

one has `1 <= ny <= 2`. Hence, for all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
S_Nf(y)\ge\frac N4
\]

through an interval of length `1/(6N)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|S_Nf\|_2^2
\ge\frac{N}{96}
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{24}
\]

Thus the canonical unweighted sum whose generalized eigenvalue would be `zeta(1/2-it)` is not an operator obtained by converging the intrinsic power pullbacks.

If an external Dirichlet weight is inserted,

\[
T_w:=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-w}C_n,
\tag{25}
\]

then the exact norm formula (14) gives absolute operator-norm convergence at least in the natural half-plane

\[
\operatorname{Re}w>\frac12.
\tag{26}
\]

On the same generalized radial wave,

\[
T_w\psi_t
=
\zeta\!\left(w+\frac12-it\right)\psi_t.
\tag{27}
\]

But the critical-line specialization is precisely `w=0`, outside this norm-convergent construction. Reaching it requires analytic continuation or another regularization. At that point the zeta function has been reintroduced through the standard Dirichlet dilation sum, not obtained as the discrete spectrum of a Prime-Circle operator.

There is also a decisive arithmetic control. Equations (17)--(27) live entirely in the `q=0` solenoid-constant sector. Replacing the whole angular solenoid by a point leaves the half-density, the continuous dilation generator, the formal critical-line zeta multiplier, and the divergence (24) unchanged. **Primitive roots, new/old shells, cyclotomic interactions, and the common anchor contribute nothing to this apparent zeta channel.**

For `q!=0`, the full action moves the rational character by

\[
U_n:\ (t,q)\longmapsto(t,nq)
\]

up to the radial phase `n^{it}`; this is the same classical two-sided rational-character dilation already analyzed in PC-064/069/070, not a quantization of `t`.

## 6. Prior art and novelty audit

The ambient completion is classical solenoidal geometry. Juan Manuel Burgos and Alberto Verjovsky, **Adelic solenoid I: Structure and topology**, arXiv:1603.05676 (2016), construct the adelic Riemann-sphere inverse-limit framework associated with all power coverings; the punctured part is the standard algebraic/adelic solenoidal completion of `C^*`. PC-064 already uses the corresponding arithmetic solenoid as prior art for the unit-circle subsystem. The product splitting (6) is elementary from the log-polar decomposition and is not claimed as a new theorem in inverse-limit theory.

The spectral half-density is even more directly classical. Michael V. Berry and Jonathan P. Keating, **H = xp and the Riemann zeros**, in *Supersymmetry and Trace Formulae: Chaos and Disorder* (1999), pp. 355--367, proposed the dilation Hamiltonian associated with `xp`; its symmetric quantization is precisely

\[
-i\left(y\frac d{dy}+\frac12\right),
\]

and the generalized waves `y^{-1/2+it}` together with integer dilations give the familiar formal critical-line zeta factor. Later analyses of the Berry--Keating program emphasize the same boundary: without an additional regularization/confinement the dilation Hamiltonian has continuous spectrum, while obtaining the Riemann-zero counting law requires extra phase-space restrictions or other structure. A useful review is Germán Sierra, **The Riemann zeros as spectrum and the Riemann hypothesis**, *Symmetry* 11 (2019), 494, DOI `10.3390/sym11040494`.

Therefore no novelty is claimed for the `1/2`, the dilation generator, Mellin diagonalization, or the formal zeta sum. The durable Prime-Circle contribution is the exact placement of that classical mechanism inside the original geometry: extending the same roots-of-unity power maps radially produces it automatically, but the exact splitting (6) and the control sector show that it is **not supplied by primitive-shell arithmetic**.
