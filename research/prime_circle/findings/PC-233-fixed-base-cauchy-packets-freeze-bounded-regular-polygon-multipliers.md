# PC-233 — fixed-base Cauchy packets freeze bounded regular-polygon multipliers to coarse samples

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-BOUNDARY` for a source-forced operator escape left open by PC-227 and only indirectly tested after compression in PC-230. At fixed coarse conductor `N`, let a fresh prime `q -> infinity` in one residue class modulo `N` produce the fully mode-resolved PC-227 Cauchy packets. If one inserts, **before residue or singular-value compression**, any uniformly bounded rotation-invariant operator on the full regular `Nq`-gon whose Fourier symbol has a fixed continuous macroscopic profile `m(k/(Nq))`, then every packet sees only the value of `m` at its coarse packet center.

More precisely, the exact finite-level packet coordinate from PC-231 satisfies

\[
\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}
\equiv
\frac{u-t}{N}-\frac{j+\sigma_{a,t,r}}{q}
\pmod 1.
\]

Hence for every fixed packet coordinate `j`, the multiplier converges to the finite coarse sample `m((u-t)/N)`. PC-227's uniform `1/(1+|j|)` packet bound and vanishing regular-region mass upgrade this pointwise statement to `ell^2`: after the canonical packet identification, the inserted operator converges to diagonal multiplication of each limiting Cauchy packet by that one coarse sample. If `m` is Lipschitz, the finite-level freezing error on the transfer state is `O_N(q^{-1/2})`.

For the canonical normalized inverse-square chord Laplacian of the full regular polygon, whose Fourier symbol is `m_ch(x)=x(1-x)/2`, the coarse sample is exactly the normalized parent-`N` eigenvalue. Thus inserting the complete fine chord operator on the mode-resolved PC-227 state **before** the PC-230 residue/chord compression still does not expose a fresh-prime spectral coordinate at fixed base: to leading operator norm on the transfer state it merely multiplies each packet by a parent-level chord eigenvalue.

This is not a no-go for non-rotation-invariant old/new operators, mode mixing, symbols varying on the fresh `1/q` scale, singular or unbounded functional calculi, renormalized subleading corrections, or simultaneous `N,q -> infinity`. Those are precisely the regimes in which the full operator can resolve structure inside a packet instead of freezing to its coarse center.

## 1. Setup: a second full-polygon operator acts on the uncompressed packet state

Keep the notation of PC-227. Fix `N>1`, let `q \nmid N` be prime, put

\[
M=Nq,
\qquad
r=q\bmod N,
\]

and let `T_{N,q}` denote the normalized exact-order-`N` source-to-new-target transfer generated by the coefficient mask of `Phi_{Nq}`. For a source character `e_u^(N)`, `(u,N)=1`, PC-227 decomposes every nonzero coarse target channel into finitely many local packets around the primitive roots

\[
\alpha_t=\zeta_N^t,
\qquad t\in U(N),
\]

with limiting coordinates

\[
\frac{C_{N,r}(a,t)}{j+\sigma_{a,t,r}},
\qquad j\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\sigma_{a,t,r}\in\left\{\frac1N,\ldots,\frac{N-1}{N}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

PC-227 proves `ell^2` convergence after the canonical local packet identification, together with the uniform estimate

\[
|A_{N,q}(k_{t,j}^{(q)},u)|
\le \frac{C_N}{1+|j|}
\tag{2}
\]

inside fixed disjoint arcs around the primitive `N`-th roots and total squared mass `O_N(q^{-1})` outside those arcs.

Now let `D_M^(m)` be a rotation-invariant operator on the **full** regular `M`-gon, diagonal in its Fourier basis:

\[
D_M^{(m)}e_k^{(M)}
=
 m\!\left(\frac{k}{M}\right)e_k^{(M)},
\tag{3}
\]

where `m:T -> C` is bounded and continuous. No scalar or positive-square compression of `T_{N,q}` is performed before inserting `D_M^(m)`.

The question is whether the growing fine Fourier coordinate inside the Cauchy packet lets this second operator create a new `q`-dependent spectral carrier.

## 2. The exact packet label approaches a fixed coarse point

PC-231 derives the exact finite cyclic relation between the target Fourier label and the packet coordinate:

\[
\boxed{
 k_{t,j}^{(q)}
\equiv
q(u-t)-Nj-s_{a,t,r}
\pmod{Nq},
}
\tag{4}
\]

where

\[
\sigma_{a,t,r}=\frac{s_{a,t,r}}{N}.
\]

Dividing (4) by `Nq` gives an identity on the circle:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}
\equiv
x_{u,t}-\frac{j+\sigma_{a,t,r}}q
\pmod1,
\qquad
x_{u,t}:=\frac{u-t}{N}\pmod1.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the packet is not merely concentrated near a zero of `Phi_N` in the auxiliary `z`-variable. Its actual fine target Fourier labels occupy a shrinking `1/q` neighborhood, in normalized frequency, of the finite coarse point `x_{u,t}`.

For each fixed `j`, continuity immediately yields

\[
 m\!\left(\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}\right)
\longrightarrow
m(x_{u,t}).
\tag{6}
\]

The endpoint case causes no ambiguity: `m` is a continuous function on the circle, so approaching `0` from either representative gives the same value.

## 3. Packet localization upgrades scalar convergence to `ell^2` multiplier freezing

Equation (6) alone would not control packet coordinates whose magnitude grows with `q`. The PC-227 mass estimates do.

Fix disjoint small arcs around the primitive roots used in the packet identification. On each packet arc, split the local coordinate into `|j| <= J` and the tail. For fixed `J`, equation (6) is uniform over the finitely many packet sectors and finitely many `j`, so

\[
\sum_{|j|\le J}
\left|
\left[
 m\!\left(\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}\right)-m(x_{u,t})
\right]
A_{N,q}(k_{t,j}^{(q)},u)
\right|^2
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

On the tail, boundedness of `m` and (2) give a uniform bound

\[
\sum_{|j|>J}
\left|
\left[
 m\!\left(\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}\right)-m(x_{u,t})
\right]
A_{N,q}(k_{t,j}^{(q)},u)
\right|^2
=O_N(J^{-1}).
\tag{8}
\]

The regular region outside all packet arcs contributes `O_N(q^{-1})` squared norm because the multiplier is bounded. Taking first `q -> infinity` and then `J -> infinity` proves the finite-packet freezing theorem:

\[
\boxed{
D_{Nq}^{(m)}T_{N,q}e_u
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{(a,t)}
 m(x_{u,t})
 \left(
 \frac{C_{N,r}(a,t)}{j+\sigma_{a,t,r}}
 \right)_{j\in\mathbb Z}
}
\tag{9}
\]

under the same canonical packet identification as PC-227. The direct sum is only over the finitely many nonzero packet sectors allowed by the PC-227 coarse selection rule.

Since the source exact-order space has fixed dimension `phi(N)`, the columnwise statement is also Hilbert--Schmidt, hence operator-norm, convergence after packet identification.

The important point is structural: **a bounded macroscopic Fourier multiplier creates no packet mixing and no new packet coordinate.** It contributes only one scalar sample per coarse source/root label.

## 4. Lipschitz profiles freeze at rate `O_N(q^{-1/2})` on the transfer state

If `m` is Lipschitz on the circle with constant `L`, equation (5) gives inside a fixed packet arc

\[
\left|
 m\!\left(\frac{k_{t,j}^{(q)}}{Nq}\right)-m(x_{u,t})
\right|
\le
L\,\frac{|j+\sigma_{a,t,r}|}{q}.
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (10) with (2), every local squared error is `O_N(q^{-2})`; a packet arc contains `O(q)` mesh points, so its total squared error is `O_N(q^{-1})`. The regular region has the same `O_N(q^{-1})` squared-mass bound from PC-227. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
\bigl(D_{Nq}^{(m)}-D_{N,q,u}^{\mathrm{coarse}}\bigr)
T_{N,q}e_u
\right\|_2
=O_N(q^{-1/2}),
}
\tag{11}
\]

where `D_{N,q,u}^coarse` denotes the piecewise packet operator that uses the constant sample `m(x_{u,t})` on the arc assigned to packet center `t` (its bounded value on the vanishing regular region is irrelevant).

Equation (11) is a statement about the multiplier acting on the **specific source-forced transfer state**, not an operator-norm approximation on the whole fine polygon Hilbert space. The latter is false: a nonconstant multiplier certainly retains its full fine spectrum on arbitrary states.

## 5. The canonical inverse-square chord operator samples exactly the parent spectrum

For the full regular `M`-gon, the normalized inverse-square chord Laplacian used in PC-230 has the classical Fourier spectrum

\[
\lambda_k^{(M)}
=
\frac{k(M-k)}{2M^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

This is the continuous macroscopic profile

\[
 m_{\mathrm{ch}}(x)=\frac{x(1-x)}2,
\qquad x\in[0,1],
\tag{13}
\]

viewed periodically on the circle; the endpoint values agree. It is Lipschitz. If `d` is the representative in `{0,1,...,N-1}` of `u-t mod N`, then

\[
\boxed{
 m_{\mathrm{ch}}(x_{u,t})
=
\frac{d(N-d)}{2N^2}
=
\lambda_d^{(N)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently the full fine chord operator inserted **directly on the PC-227 mode-resolved transfer state** satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D_{Nq}^{(\mathrm{ch})}T_{N,q}e_u
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{(a,t)}
\lambda_{u-t\bmod N}^{(N)}
\left(
\frac{C_{N,r}(a,t)}{j+\sigma_{a,t,r}}
\right)_{j\in\mathbb Z}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the second canonical geometric operator does act differently on different packet sectors, but at fixed base its action is exhausted by **parent-level regular-polygon eigenvalues**. It does not resolve the fresh-prime coordinate asymptotically.

The same conclusion holds for any bounded continuous functional calculus of the normalized full chord operator: if `F` is continuous on its compact spectral range, then `F(D_M^(ch))` freezes to `F(lambda_d^(N))` on the corresponding packet.

This strengthens PC-230 in a different order of operations. PC-230 first performs a Cauchy-residue compression and then shows that the weighted fine chord form renormalizes to the parent Laplacian. Equation (15) shows that even **before that compression**, while the full `j`-resolved Cauchy tail is still present, the bounded full-polygon chord multiplier cannot extract a new fixed-base fine spectral variable.

## 6. Exact composite-base control

Take the displayed PC-227 control `N=6`, `q congruent 1 (mod 6)`, source `u=1`, and nonzero coarse channel `b=3`. The primitive roots are indexed by `t=1,5`.

For `t=1`, equation (5) has coarse center

\[
x_{1,1}=0,
\]

so the normalized chord multiplier freezes to

\[
m_{\mathrm{ch}}(0)=0.
\]

For `t=5`, the center is

\[
x_{1,5}=\frac{1-5}{6}\equiv\frac13\pmod1,
\]

and therefore

\[
m_{\mathrm{ch}}(1/3)=\frac19
=\lambda_2^{(6)}.
\]

The two packet sectors are not merged: the fine chord operator preserves both, but its asymptotic weights are the finite parent values `0` and `1/9`. This is exactly the behavior predicted by (15), with no `q`-dependent eigenvalue left in the packet limit.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The regular-polygon inverse-square spectrum (12) is classical; the Prime-Circle source ledger already records Calogero--Perelomov, *Some Diophantine relations involving circular functions of rational angles* (1979), as the primary prior-art anchor for the `sin^{-2}` polygon matrix and its Fourier eigenstructure. The new step here does not claim a new Fourier-multiplier theorem: localization of a bounded continuous multiplier on frequency-localized states is standard harmonic-analysis behavior, and equations (5)--(11) are an elementary specialization using the exact source-forced PC-227 packet coordinates and mass estimates.

The project-specific content is the **boundary identification**: the nontrivial cyclotomic transfer state produced by fresh-prime refinement is so sharply localized in normalized target frequency that the canonical full-polygon chord operator, despite being inserted before packet compression, asymptotically sees only the parent coarse frequencies.

A matched nonarithmetic control behaves identically. Any family of `ell^2` states with the same finite packet localization and `1/(1+|j|)` tail bound freezes every bounded continuous macroscopic multiplier in the same way. Therefore multiplier freezing itself is not a prime selector and supplies no novelty claim toward RH.

No new complex spectral parameter, gamma factor, functional-equation involution, zeta zero divisor, or critical-line condition is generated by (9) or (15).

## 8. Surviving Prime-Circle frontier

This finding closes only the route

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed-base PC-227 mode-resolved transfer}
\longrightarrow
\text{bounded rotation-invariant full-polygon multiplier}
\longrightarrow
\text{new fresh-prime spectral coordinate}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

It does **not** cover the operator classes that can actually resolve structure inside the shrinking packet. In particular, the following remain outside the theorem:

- an intrinsically non-rotation-invariant old/new or primitive-restricted operator that mixes target Fourier modes;
- a source-forced symbol whose variation occurs on the fresh `1/q` scale rather than through one fixed equicontinuous macroscopic profile;
- unbounded or singular functional calculus, or a renormalized subtraction that magnifies the `O(1/q)` variation across a packet;
- several noncommuting insertions retained jointly before scalarization;
- simultaneous growth `N,q -> infinity`, where packet centers and separation constants need not remain uniform;
- the global uniformization/accessory sector outside finite regular-polygon multiplier calculus.

The most immediate live distinction is therefore no longer “operator before versus after packet compression.” For fixed base, **bounded diagonal regular-polygon operators commute asymptotically with the packet limit**. A viable cross-prime mechanism must instead carry information in mode mixing, fresh-scale variation, singular/rescaled subleading structure, or genuinely growing multi-prime state.
