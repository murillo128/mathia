- **Research line:** `prime_circle`
- **Result type:** exact self-adjoint Jacobi / two-sided grade-coupling classification
- **Scope:** closes the simplest scalar backward/two-sided repair left explicitly open by PC-361 and PC-364: symmetrizing the canonical linear weighted raising shift by adding its adjoint to a linear proper grade Hamiltonian
- **RH relevance:** high as a boundary result. The first self-adjoint repair that actually breaks one-sided triangularity is exactly a positive-discrete-series `su(1,1)` Jacobi operator. Its spectrum has only three universal regimes—an affine discrete lattice, a Laguerre half-line, or the whole real line—and no Prime-Circle arithmetic or zeta-zero selector survives.

## Claim

PC-361 shows that a proper grade Hamiltonian plus a bounded strictly raising term cannot move the ordinary eigenvalues, and PC-362--PC-364 classify the simplest unbounded one-sided scalar shift. The most immediate way to escape that triangular obstruction is the self-adjoint-looking repair suggested explicitly in PC-361: add the lowering adjoint.

Let

\[
N e_n=n e_n,
\qquad
A e_n=(n+1)e_{n+1}
\]

on the finite sequences in `ell^2(N_0)`. For `a>0` and `c in C`, consider the Jacobi operator

\[
H_{a,c}=aN+cA+\overline c\,A^*.
\tag{1}
\]

The closure of (1) is self-adjoint, and its spectral type depends only on `r=|c|`. More precisely,

\[
\boxed{
0\le r<\frac a2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma(H_{a,c})
=
\left\{
\sqrt{a^2-4r^2}\left(n+\frac12\right)-\frac a2:
 n\in\mathbb N_0
\right\},
}
\tag{2}
\]

with simple pure-point spectrum;

\[
\boxed{
r=\frac a2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma(H_{a,c})=[-a/2,\infty),
}
\tag{3}
\]

with simple absolutely continuous spectrum; and

\[
\boxed{
r>\frac a2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma(H_{a,c})=\mathbb R,
}
\tag{4}
\]

again with simple absolutely continuous spectrum.

For the exact scalar family of PC-362--PC-364, `a=1`. Therefore the canonical two-sided repair

\[
N+Q_c+Q_c^*
\]

has a transition at `|c|=1/2`; at the unscaled symmetric choice `|c|=1` its spectrum is simply all of `R`. The number `1/2` here is a universal Jacobi/`su(1,1)` coupling threshold. It is not the Riemann critical line and does not arise from cyclotomic, von-Mangoldt, or zeta data.

## 1. Phase is a unitary gauge

Write `c=r e^{i theta}` and define

\[
D_\theta e_n=e^{-in\theta}e_n.
\]

Since `D_theta` commutes with `N`, while the phase of the one-step shift is cancelled,

\[
D_\theta H_{a,c}D_\theta^*=H_{a,r}.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the spectrum cannot see the phase of the scalar two-sided coupling. We may take `r>=0`.

For `r>0`, (1) is the Jacobi matrix with diagonal and off-diagonal coefficients

\[
b_n=an,
\qquad
a_n=r(n+1).
\tag{6}
\]

Carleman's criterion applies because

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{a_n}
=
\frac1r\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{n+1}
=\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence the symmetric operator on finite sequences is essentially self-adjoint. The case `r=0` is the diagonal operator `aN`.

## 2. The two-sided repair closes exactly into `su(1,1)`

Introduce

\[
K_0e_n=\left(n+\frac12\right)e_n,
\qquad
K_+e_n=(n+1)e_{n+1},
\qquad
K_-e_n=n e_{n-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

These are the Bargmann-index `k=1/2` positive discrete series of `su(1,1)`, with `K_- = K_+^*`. Equation (1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
H_{a,r}+\frac a2
=
aK_0+r(K_++K_-).
}
\tag{9}
\]

This already exposes the decisive control. The putatively new self-adjoint spectralization is not a new operator class generated by the Prime-Circle source; it is a real linear combination of the three standard `su(1,1)` generators.

To make the spectrum explicit, use the standard normalization

\[
\pi_k(L)e_n
=
R\sqrt{(n+1)(n+2k)}e_{n+1}
+S(2k+2n)e_n
+R\sqrt{n(n+2k-1)}e_{n-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

At `k=1/2`, `R=r` and `S=a/2`, equation (10) is exactly `H_{a,r}+a/2`. The invariant controlling the three Jacobi regimes is therefore

\[
S^2-R^2=\frac{a^2}{4}-r^2.
\tag{11}
\]

No arithmetic datum enters (11).

## 3. Subcritical coupling gives only a rescaled grade lattice

Assume `0<r<a/2` and set

\[
C=\sqrt{\frac{a^2}{4}-r^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

The Jacobi recurrence (10) is the Meixner recurrence for `k=1/2`. Its standard unitary spectral transform sends `H_{a,r}+a/2` to multiplication on a positive discrete measure on `N_0` by

\[
2C\left(x+\frac12\right).
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently

\[
\lambda_n(H_{a,r})
=
2C\left(n+\frac12\right)-\frac a2
=
\sqrt{a^2-4r^2}\left(n+\frac12\right)-\frac a2,
\tag{14}
\]

which proves (2). At `r=0`, (14) reduces continuously to `an`, as required.

So adding a genuine lowering channel does move the eigenvalues, unlike the one-sided theorem PC-361, but the movement is completely universal: one scalar square root rescales the equally spaced grade spectrum. No shell arithmetic, zeta factor, functional equation, or zero set appears.

## 4. The threshold is the classical Laguerre half-line

At

\[
r=\frac a2,
\tag{15}
\]

the invariant (11) vanishes. The same Jacobi recurrence becomes the Laguerre recurrence. For `k=1/2`, the standard Laguerre transform sends

\[
H_{a,a/2}+\frac a2
\]

unitarily to multiplication by `x` on

\[
L^2\!\left([0,\infty),\frac{2}{a}e^{-2x/a}\,dx\right).
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore its spectrum is `[0,infinity)` with multiplicity one and is purely absolutely continuous. Shifting back proves (3).

This is an important adversarial control because a `1/2` threshold has already appeared in several analytic RH-adjacent constructions. Here, for `a=1`, the edge `-1/2` and the coefficient threshold `r=1/2` are forced solely by the degeneration of the elementary quadratic invariant `1/4-r^2`. The spectral measure is an ordinary Laguerre measure. There is no complex spectral parameter whose real part is being selected, and no Riemann zero information is present.

## 5. Supercritical coupling gives the whole real line

For `r>a/2`, set

\[
C=\sqrt{r^2-\frac{a^2}{4}}.
\tag{17}
\]

The Jacobi recurrence is now the Meixner--Pollaczek recurrence. The standard positive-discrete-series transform sends

\[
H_{a,r}+\frac a2
\]

unitarily to multiplication by `-2Cx` on a positive Meixner--Pollaczek weight supported on all of `R`. Hence

\[
\sigma(H_{a,r})=\mathbb R,
\tag{18}
\]

with simple absolutely continuous spectrum.

In particular, the most literal symmetric completion of the PC-364 shift has `a=1`, `r=1`, so it lies deeply in the supercritical class. Symmetrization does not turn the universal one-sided half-plane/full-plane transition into a discrete Hilbert--Polya spectrum. It instead converts the model into a classical self-adjoint Jacobi operator with featureless support `R`.

## 6. Prime-Circle consequence and matched control

PC-361 correctly left `N+Q+Q^*` outside its triangular theorem. The present result shows that the **scalar** version of that escape does not help. Once the raising and lowering amplitudes are a common scalar, the operator closes inside a three-dimensional Lie algebra before any Prime-Circle data can enter. Its entire spectral classification is controlled by the single ratio

\[
\frac{2|c|}{a}.
\tag{19}
\]

A matched non-arithmetic control with the same `a` and `|c|` has exactly the same operator up to the phase gauge (5), hence the same spectrum and spectral type. Even if one derives `c` from a source-dependent scalar statistic, ordinary spectrum can retain at most its modulus through (2)--(4); the phase is invisible and the transition itself is universal.

This closes only the simplest two-sided repair. It does **not** rule out a Jacobi operator whose off-diagonal coefficients `c_n` are themselves forced, grade by grade, by the Prime-Circle geometry; a non-scalar same-grade block; a matrix-valued/multichannel recurrence; a source-forced domain or boundary condition; or a genuinely nonlocal two-sided coupling. Those are mathematically different because they no longer reduce to the fixed `k=1/2` linear `su(1,1)` element (9). Any such continuation must derive its coefficient sequence or domain from the roots-of-unity source and pass the usual matched-control test before a spectral feature can count as RH evidence.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The complete Jacobi classification above is classical. D. R. Masson and J. Repka, **Spectral Theory of Jacobi Matrices in `l^2(Z)` and the `su(1,1)` Lie Algebra**, *SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis* 22:4 (1991), 1131--1146, DOI `10.1137/0522073`, identifies real linear combinations of `su(1,1)` generators with the Meixner, Meixner--Pollaczek, and Laguerre Jacobi families.

A particularly direct modern reference is Wolter Groenevelt and Erik Koelink, **Orthogonal functions related to Lax pairs in Lie algebras**, *The Ramanujan Journal* 61 (2023), 445--474, DOI `10.1007/s11139-021-00424-9`. Their positive-discrete-series Theorems 4.3--4.5 give exactly the three cases used above: discrete Meixner spectrum for `S^2-R^2>0`, Laguerre multiplication on `[0,infinity)` at equality, and Meixner--Pollaczek multiplication on `R` for `S^2-R^2<0`.

No novelty is claimed for `su(1,1)`, Carleman's criterion, Jacobi matrices, or these orthogonal-polynomial transforms. The line-local mathematical delta is the exact closure of the specific two-sided scalar escape left by PC-361/PC-364 and the identification of its apparent `1/2` threshold as a classical representation-theoretic transition rather than a zeta-critical phenomenon. The derivation above reproduces the parameter match explicitly, so these references serve as prior-art/verification anchors rather than as a hidden new mechanism.

## Falsification / disproof audit

- **Phase check:** diagonal conjugation must remove `arg(c)` exactly; otherwise the claimed scalar reduction fails.
- **Self-adjointness check:** for `r>0`, `sum_n 1/[r(n+1)]` diverges, so the Jacobi operator is essentially self-adjoint on finite sequences.
- **Generator check:** `H_{a,r}+a/2` must equal the `k=1/2` positive-discrete-series element `aK_0+r(K_++K_-)` coefficient by coefficient.
- **Subcritical check:** at `r=0`, (2) returns `sigma(aN)={an}`; finite Jacobi truncations for `0<r<a/2` converge at the low end to the explicit lattice (14).
- **Critical check:** at `r=a/2`, the recurrence is the `alpha=0` Laguerre recurrence after the constant shift, giving support `[0,infinity)` before shifting by `-a/2`.
- **Supercritical check:** the Meixner--Pollaczek weight is strictly positive on all of `R`, so no hidden gaps or discrete eigenvalues can supply arithmetic spectral points.
- **Control check:** the proof uses only `a` and `|c|`; it is unchanged under replacement of the Prime-Circle source by any matched control producing the same two scalars.
- **Scope check:** a grade-dependent coefficient sequence, matrix-valued recurrence, source-dependent boundary/domain, or other non-`su(1,1)` two-sided operator is explicitly outside this finding and must not be declared closed by PC-365.

## Boundary assessment

PC-362--PC-364 showed that the canonical scalar **one-sided** weighted shift has only a universal subcritical/critical/supercritical phase diagram. PC-365 shows that adding its adjoint—the first obvious way to break triangularity—does not create an arithmetic spectrum either. It lands exactly in the classical `su(1,1)` Jacobi phase diagram.

The live operator frontier is therefore narrower: a viable two-sided Prime-Circle spectralization must put source information into the **coefficient sequence, channel structure, or domain itself**, not merely into a common scalar amplitude multiplying a universal raising/lowering pair.
