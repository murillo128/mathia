# PC-020 — the entire anchored local jet collapses to classical Jordan-totient data

**Status:** `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for any RH mechanism that uses only finite-order local differential/Taylor data of one anchored primitive shell at the common vertex.

PC-019 showed that forgetting the common vertex is fatal: an unanchored shell cannot distinguish an odd level `n` from `2n`. This finding shows the complementary obstruction. Keeping the common vertex is necessary, but **localizing too aggressively at that vertex is also too weak**: the complete holomorphic jet of the cyclotomic field there is already classical Jordan-totient data.

## 1. Exact logarithmic-coordinate expansion at the common vertex

For `n>1`, let

\[
F_n(t):=\log \Phi_n(e^t),
\]

where the branch is chosen near `t=0`; this is legitimate because \(\Phi_n(1)\neq0\).

Using

\[
\Phi_n(x)=\prod_{d\mid n}(x^d-1)^{\mu(n/d)},
\]

we obtain

\[
F_n(t)=
\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\log(e^{dt}-1).
\]

For `n>1`, \(\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)=0\), and

\[
\log\frac{e^x-1}{x}
=
\frac{x}{2}
+
\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k)!}x^{2k}.
\]

Therefore the singular \(\log t\) terms cancel exactly and the full Taylor series is

\[
\boxed{
\log \Phi_n(e^t)
=
\Lambda(n)
+
\frac{J_1(n)}2 t
+
\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{B_{2k}J_{2k}(n)}{2k(2k)!}
\,t^{2k}.
}
\]

Here

\[
J_r(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)d^r
=n^r\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-r})
\]

is the Jordan totient, with \(J_1=\varphi\).

Thus

\[
\boxed{
F_n(0)=\Lambda(n),
\qquad
F_n'(0)=\frac{\varphi(n)}2,
}
\]

and for `k>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
F_n^{(2k)}(0)=\frac{B_{2k}}{2k}J_{2k}(n),
\qquad
F_n^{(2k+1)}(0)=0.
}
\]

The vanishing of all odd derivatives beyond the linear term is the local logarithmic-coordinate expression of cyclotomic reciprocity.

## 2. Geometric meaning for the original prime-circle potential

The primitive-shell potential is

\[
U_n(z)=\log|\Phi_n(z)|.
\]

Write the pointed logarithmic coordinate

\[
z=e^{x+iy}
\]

so that the common vertex is `(x,y)=(0,0)`. Then

\[
U_n(e^{x+iy})
=
\Re F_n(x+iy).
\]

Consequently **every finite-order local differential invariant of this harmonic field at the common vertex**—radial derivatives, angular derivatives, Hessians, higher harmonic multipoles, and any polynomial/rational expression in a finite jet—is determined by

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(n),\ \varphi(n),\ J_2(n),\ J_4(n),\ldots
}
\]

up to universal Bernoulli constants.

No extra local prime geometry remains hidden in the anchored Taylor expansion.

## 3. The spherical projection does not rescue the local branch

PC-015 gave the exact spherical potential

\[
G_n(\Sigma(z))
=
U_n(z)
-
\frac{\varphi(n)}2\log(1+|z|^2)
+
\frac{\varphi(n)}2\log2.
\]

In logarithmic coordinates, \(|z|^2=e^{2x}\), so the spherical correction is a universal analytic function of `x` multiplied only by \(\varphi(n)\).

Hence the complete pointed spherical jet at the equatorial common vertex is again generated by the same classical data:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(n),\ \varphi(n),\ J_2(n),\ J_4(n),\ldots
}
\]

and universal constants.

Thus passing from the plane to the exact sphere/hemisphere picture preserves the interior/exterior duality but does **not** make local anchored derivatives into a new arithmetic object.

## 4. This subsumes local chord-sum observables

Differentiating

\[
\log\Phi_n(z)=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_n^*}\log(z-\zeta)
\]

at `z=1` produces sums such as

\[
\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_n^*}\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)^r}
\]

and their polynomial combinations. The Lehmer formulas for higher derivatives of \(\Phi_n\) at `1` express precisely these quantities through Euler/Jordan totients, Bernoulli numbers and Stirling/Bell-polynomial combinatorics.

Therefore the no-go statement is broader than the particular coordinate expansion above: any proposed invariant obtained from a **finite local jet of the anchored single-shell cyclotomic field** is already inside a heavily studied classical arithmetic algebra.

## 5. Serious novelty check

This is a negative result, not a novelty claim.

Known literature is directly on top of the mechanism:

- D. H. Lehmer's 1966 formulas express higher derivatives \(\Phi_n^{(k)}(1)/\Phi_n(1)\) in terms of Bernoulli numbers, Stirling numbers and Jordan totients.
- Herrera-Poyatos and Moree, *Coefficients and higher order derivatives of cyclotomic polynomials: Old and new* (Expositiones Mathematicae), survey and reorganize these formulas using logarithmic derivatives and Bell polynomials.
- Moree, Saad Eddin, Sedunova and Suzuki study the resulting Jordan-totient quotients and explicitly use Lehmer's formulas for derivatives at `1`.

The compact identity in logarithmic coordinate

\[
\log\Phi_n(e^t)
=
\Lambda(n)+\frac{\varphi(n)}2t
+
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{B_{2k}J_{2k}(n)}{2k(2k)!}t^{2k}
\]

is an efficient geometric packaging of the same classical information; it should not be advertised as new mathematics.

## 6. Decisive research consequence

After PC-019 and PC-020, the viable single-shell region is sharply constrained:

\[
\boxed{
\text{unanchored global shell geometry} \;\to\; \text{too coarse},
}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\text{anchored but purely local shell geometry} \;\to\; \text{Jordan-totient classical data}.
}
\]

Therefore a genuinely new candidate must use something that is simultaneously

1. **anchored** to the common vertex / absolute phase, and
2. **nonlocal** in the plane or sphere, or genuinely **multi-level**.

Examples that remain legitimate are:

- global anchored uniformization of a punctured sphere;
- interactions between distinct birth shells before evaluating at the anchor;
- loops/caps crossing both hemispheres and several labeled levels;
- nonlinear global functionals of the Liouville/uniformization defect;
- a dynamics on surfaces/fields that retains the full spatial configuration rather than a finite Taylor jet.

Do not pursue more gradients, Hessians, higher derivatives, curvature jets, or finite multipole expansions at the common vertex as potential RH mechanisms. They are now decisively accounted for by classical Jordan-totient theory.
