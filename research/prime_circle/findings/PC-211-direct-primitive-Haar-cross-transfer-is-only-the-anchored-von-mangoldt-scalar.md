## 1. Universal cross-Gram lemma for one covering block

Let

\[
f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_jz^j,
\qquad
g(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g_jz^j
\tag{4}
\]

be arbitrary masks of degree `<N`, and define

\[
T_f:=M_fC_N,
\qquad
T_g:=M_gC_N
\tag{5}
\]

on `H^2(D)`. On the Hardy basis `e_k(z)=z^k`,

\[
T_fe_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_j e_{Nk+j},
\qquad
T_ge_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g_j e_{N\ell+j}.
\tag{6}
\]

The length-`N` coefficient blocks belonging to distinct `k` are orthogonal. Therefore

\[
\langle T_fe_k,T_ge_\ell\rangle
=
\delta_{k\ell}
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\overline{f_j}g_j.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence, exactly,

\[
\boxed{
T_f^*T_g
=
\langle f,g\rangle_{H^2}\,I.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This is stronger than the individual scaled-isometry identity used in PC-209. It computes the *relative* operator geometry of any two masks that fit inside the same covering block.

No asymptotic limit, trace, determinant, boundary regularization, or Fourier contraction is involved.

## 2. Direct primitive versus full refinement gives `Phi_N(1)`

For `N>1` we have `deg Phi_N=phi(N)<N`, so (8) applies to

\[
f=\Phi_N,
\qquad
g=S_N.
\tag{9}
\]

Every coefficient of `S_N` is `1`. Since the coefficients of `\Phi_N` are real integers,

\[
\langle \Phi_N,S_N\rangle
=
\sum_j [z^j]\Phi_N(z)
=
\Phi_N(1).
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
W_N^*F_N=\Phi_N(1)I.
}
\tag{11}
\]

The classical cyclotomic evaluation is

\[
\Phi_N(1)=
\begin{cases}
p,&N=p^r,\ r\ge1,\\[2mm]
1,&N>1\text{ has at least two distinct prime divisors}.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\log\Phi_N(1)=\Lambda(N).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the exact common-vertex von Mangoldt value has an operator interpretation:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(N)=\log\|W_N^*F_N\|.
}
\tag{14}
\]

This is a genuine relation between the two operator channels emphasized by PC-210. It is not, however, new arithmetic: equation (14) is exactly the classical value `Phi_N(1)` expressed as a cross-Gram norm.

## 3. Normalized ranges are isoclinic

Put

\[
h_N:=\|\Phi_N\|_{H^2}^2
\tag{15}
\]

as in PC-209, and normalize

\[
V_N:=h_N^{-1/2}W_N,
\qquad
H_N:=N^{-1/2}F_N.
\tag{16}
\]

Both are isometries. Equation (11) becomes

\[
\boxed{
V_N^*H_N=c_N I,
\qquad
c_N:=
\frac{\Phi_N(1)}{\sqrt{Nh_N}}.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Since `Phi_N(1)>0`, `c_N` is real and positive. Let

\[
P_N:=V_NV_N^*,
\qquad
Q_N:=H_NH_N^*.
\tag{18}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
P_NQ_NP_N=c_N^2P_N,
\qquad
Q_NP_NQ_N=c_N^2Q_N.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Hence the two infinite-dimensional ranges are **isoclinic**: every principal angle is the same,

\[
\boxed{
\theta_N=\arccos c_N.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Even the most immediate noncommutative invariant of the two range projections is only a function of this scalar. On each two-plane generated by the corresponding one-dimensional mask directions,

\[
\boxed{
\|[P_N,Q_N]\|
=
c_N\sqrt{1-c_N^2}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore marking the discrepancy between direct primitive and full refinement by a pair of projections does not create a new spectral packet: the full angle spectrum is a single repeated value.

## 4. The pair is a standard Cuntz mask pair

The block geometry has an exact operator-algebra realization. Define

\[
S_{N,j}e_k:=e_{Nk+j},
\qquad
0\le j<N.
\tag{22}
\]

These operators satisfy the Cuntz relations

\[
S_{N,i}^*S_{N,j}=\delta_{ij}I,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}S_{N,j}S_{N,j}^*=I.
\tag{23}
\]

For every mask `f` in (4),

\[
\boxed{
T_f=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} f_j S_{N,j}.
}
\tag{24}
\]

Thus the normalized primitive and Haar covers are simply the two unit vectors

\[
u_N:=\frac{\operatorname{coeff}(\Phi_N)}{\sqrt{h_N}},
\qquad
v_N:=\frac{(1,\ldots,1)}{\sqrt N}
\tag{25}
\]

inside the standard `N`-dimensional Cuntz mask space, with

\[
\langle u_N,v_N\rangle=c_N.
\tag{26}
\]

For composite `N`, `0<c_N<1`, and Gram--Schmidt gives

\[
X_N:=V_N,
\qquad
Y_N:=
\frac{H_N-c_NV_N}{\sqrt{1-c_N^2}},
\tag{27}
\]

with

\[
X_N^*X_N=Y_N^*Y_N=I,
\qquad
X_N^*Y_N=0.
\tag{28}
\]

So the supposedly special two-channel system becomes two ordinary orthogonal Cuntz-mask isometries after one scalar change of basis.

More invariantly, a unitary `U in U(N)` acting on the mask space sends the Cuntz generators to another Cuntz family and gives the standard Bogoliubov automorphism

\[
S_{N,j}\longmapsto
\sum_k U_{kj}S_{N,k}.
\tag{29}
\]

Any two ordered pairs of unit mask vectors with the same Gram matrix are related by such a `U`. Consequently the ordered normalized pair `(V_N,H_N)`, viewed inside the natural Cuntz mask algebra, is classified up to this intrinsic mask rotation by

\[
\boxed{
G_N=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&c_N\\
c_N&1
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

At this same-level two-mask stage there is no further `U(N)`-invariant operator datum in the detailed cyclotomic coefficient vector.

The unnormalized pair adds only the two already explicit scales `\sqrt{h_N}` and `\sqrt N`; PC-209 already classifies `h_N` as the ordinary coefficient energy of `Phi_N`. The only new cross-channel scalar is `Phi_N(1)=e^{Lambda(N)}`.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

Every abstract ingredient of the reduction is classical.

The evaluation `Phi_N(1)=e^{Lambda(N)}` is a standard cyclotomic identity. It follows immediately from the usual prime-power formula for cyclotomic polynomials together with the divisor factorization of `z^N-1`; modern notes on cyclotomic forms state the identity explicitly.

The relations (23) are the defining relations introduced by Joachim Cuntz in *Simple C*-algebras generated by isometries*, Communications in Mathematical Physics 57 (1977), 173--185, DOI `10.1007/BF01625776`. The connection between finite filter masks, wavelets, and representations of the Cuntz relations is established terrain; a direct neighboring reference is Ola Bratteli, David E. Evans, and Palle E. T. Jorgensen, *Compactly supported wavelets and representations of the Cuntz relations*, Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis 8 (2000), 166--196, DOI `10.1006/acha.2000.0283`. The `U(N)` action on the Cuntz generators is the standard Bogoliubov automorphism family; for modern context see Roberto Conti and Stefano Rossi, *Groups of isometries of the Cuntz algebras*, Documenta Mathematica 26 (2021), 1799--1815, DOI `10.4171/DM/856`.

A directed search did not locate this exact project-specific pairing between the cyclotomic coefficient mask and the uniform refinement mask. That absence is **not** used as evidence of novelty. Equations (8)--(14) show that the pairing is an elementary composition of two classical structures: one-block filter/Cuntz orthogonality and the classical value `Phi_N(1)`.

Accordingly no novelty is claimed for the Cuntz representation, Bogoliubov mask rotations, isoclinic subspaces, `Phi_N(1)`, or the von Mangoldt identity. It does not produce a richer same-level spectral or operator invariant.
