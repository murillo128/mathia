# PC-159 — Bloch half-reflection is universal time reversal, not a zeta functional equation

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for interpreting the exact `t <-> 1-t` symmetry of the PC-156 full-chord Bloch pencil as a zeta functional equation or critical-line mechanism. This finding materially corrects only the boundary statement in PC-156 that the pencil supplied no such involution; the main PC-156 classification of repeated-prime depth as sampling one fixed finite-dimensional pencil remains unchanged.

PC-156 introduced, on the primitive shell `U(d)`,

\[
\mathcal P_d(t)
=\frac1{d^2}\left(L_d^{\rm int}+\frac t2 C_d-\frac{t^2}{2}I\right),
\qquad C_d=H_d+J_d.
\]

The pencil does have an exact reflection about `t=1/2`. More strongly, the same reflection holds for the identical inverse-square/cotangent construction on **every finite subset of roots of unity**, whether arithmetic or not. It is therefore the ordinary opposite-quasimomentum reality symmetry of a real cyclic Bloch problem, not an arithmetic functional equation.

## 1. The half-reflection holds for an arbitrary root subset

Fix `d>=2` and any finite subset

\[
X\subseteq\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
\]

No coprimality, primitiveness, subgroup, or divisor condition is imposed. On `\mathbb C^X` define the induced inverse-square chord Laplacian

\[
(L_Xf)(a)
=\sum_{\substack{b\in X\\b\ne a}}
\kappa_d(a-b)(f(a)-f(b)),
\qquad
\kappa_d(h)=\frac1{4\sin^2(\pi h/d)},
\tag{1}
\]

and the oriented cotangent matrix

\[
H_X(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
i\cot\!\left(\dfrac{\pi(a-b)}d\right),&a\ne b,\\[2mm]0,&a=b.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

Put

\[
C_X:=H_X+J_X,
\qquad
Z_X:=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in X}\!\left(e^{2\pi i a/d}\right),
\tag{3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_X(t)
:=\frac1{d^2}\left(L_X+\frac t2 C_X-\frac{t^2}{2}I\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

For `X=U(d)`, equation (4) is exactly the PC-156 pencil.

Let `a\ne b` and write

\[
\theta=\frac{\pi(a-b)}d,
\qquad
\rho=e^{-2i\theta}=e^{2\pi i(b-a)/d}.
\]

Two elementary circle identities are

\[
\boxed{
\rho(1-i\cot\theta)=-(1+i\cot\theta),
}
\tag{5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_d(a-b)(1-\rho)
=\frac12(1+i\cot\theta).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Applying them entry by entry, including the diagonal entries, gives the exact matrix identities

\[
\boxed{
Z_X^{-1}\overline{C_X}Z_X=2I-C_X,
}
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
Z_X^{-1}L_XZ_X=L_X+\frac12C_X-\frac12I.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Since `L_X` is real, substituting (7)--(8) into (4) yields, for real `t`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_X(1-t)
=Z_X^{-1}\overline{\mathcal P_X(t)}Z_X.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Equivalently, with componentwise conjugation `K`, the antiunitary

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_X:=Z_X^{-1}K
}
\tag{10}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_X\mathcal P_X(t)\Theta_X^{-1}=\mathcal P_X(1-t),
\qquad
\Theta_X^2=I.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the half-reflection is exact before imposing the primitive-shell arithmetic.

## 2. The characteristic polynomial really has a `t <-> 1-t` functional equation

Define

\[
F_X(z,t):=\det\!\left(zI-\mathcal P_X(t)\right).
\tag{12}
\]

For real `t`, `\mathcal P_X(t)` is Hermitian. Equation (9) therefore makes `\mathcal P_X(t)` and `\mathcal P_X(1-t)` isospectral. Hence

\[
F_X(z,t)=F_X(z,1-t)
\]

for every real `t`; since both sides are polynomials in `t`, the identity is algebraic:

\[
\boxed{
F_X(z,t)=F_X(z,1-t)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb C[z,t].
}
\tag{13}
\]

The invariant ring of the reflection `t -> 1-t` is generated by

\[
u=t(1-t)
\]

(or equivalently by `(t-1/2)^2`). Therefore there is a polynomial `G_X` such that

\[
\boxed{
F_X(z,t)=G_X\!\left(z,t(1-t)\right).
}
\tag{14}
\]

For the repeated-prime fiber of PC-156, `t=k/m`; the partner `1-t=(m-k)/m` is precisely the opposite fiber Fourier momentum. Equation (13) is therefore an intrinsic symmetry of the finite Bloch spectral curve, not merely a numerical pairing.

## 3. Why the midpoint `1/2` is not a Riemann critical line

The reflection center is visually tempting because it is exactly `1/2`, but the matched-control test removes the arithmetic interpretation immediately. Equations (5)--(11) hold for an arbitrary finite root subset `X`; they use only the circle embedding and the reality of the original inverse-square chord operator. They do not use `U(d)`, `phi(d)`, `mu`, `Lambda`, cyclotomic resultants, prime-power birth, or any prime discriminator.

Moreover `Theta_X^2=+1`. At the fixed point `t=1/2`, the antiunitary symmetry therefore supplies no Kramers-type forced degeneracy and no reason for the determinant to vanish. The smallest primitive example is already decisive. For `d=3`, `X=U(3)`, exact calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\det\mathcal P_3(t)
=\frac{t(t-1)(3t^2-3t-8)}{972}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

At the reflection point,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal P_3(1/2)
=\left\{\frac5{72},\frac7{216}\right\},
\qquad
\det\mathcal P_3(1/2)=\frac{35}{15552}>0.
}
\tag{16}
\]

The two additional zeros of the analytically continued determinant are

\[
\boxed{
t=\frac12\pm\frac{\sqrt{105}}6,
}
\tag{17}
\]

which are real and lie far away from the midpoint. Thus even after making the **external** step of regarding the finite Bloch coordinate as a complex variable, the exact reflection does not place the determinant zeros on `Re(t)=1/2`.

The intrinsic object also has no archimedean gamma factor and no canonical identification of Bloch quasimomentum with Riemann's complex variable `s`. The symmetry in (13) is consequently insufficient in exactly the way a bare `s <-> 1-s` symmetry is insufficient to prove or encode RH: it pairs spectral data but supplies neither the completed-zeta analytic structure nor zero localization.

## 4. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract mechanism is classical Bloch/Floquet time reversal. For a real periodic Hamiltonian, complex conjugation sends quasimomentum `k` to `-k`; on a unit-period Brillouin circle, `-t` is the same point as `1-t`. Gianluca Panati, **Triviality of Bloch and Bloch-Dirac bundles**, *Annales Henri Poincaré* 8 (2007), 995--1011, DOI `10.1007/s00023-007-0326-8`, uses precisely time-reversal symmetry in the periodic Bloch setting. The general opposite-quasimomentum pairing is therefore not a new spectral principle.

What is derived here is the exact Prime-Circle specialization (7)--(11): the `C_X` affine transformation and the inverse-square/cotangent gauge combine so that the PC-156 quadratic pencil realizes the standard Bloch reality symmetry as the literal algebraic reflection `t <-> 1-t`. No claim of a new general theorem is made. The durable value is the classification boundary and correction: the symmetry exists, but it survives unchanged on non-arithmetic root subsets and so cannot by itself be the zeta functional equation sought by the Prime-Circle program.
