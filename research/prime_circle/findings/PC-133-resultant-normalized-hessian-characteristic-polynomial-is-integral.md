# PC-133 — resultant-normalized Hessian characteristic polynomial is integral

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `STRUCTURAL-COLLAPSE` + `DECISIVE-BOUNDARY` for the finite symmetric spectral invariants of the vertexwise resultant Hessian from PC-128. PC-129 proved integrality after resultant normalization only for the Kirchhoff cofactor, i.e. the product of the nonzero eigenvalues. The same normalization in fact controls **every coefficient of the full characteristic polynomial**: for distinct primitive shells `P_m^*` and `P_n^*`, if `H_{m,n}` is the PC-128 weighted bipartite Laplacian and `R_{m,n}=|Res(Phi_m,Phi_n)|`, then

\[
\boxed{
R_{m,n}^{\,2}\det(tI+H_{m,n})\in\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

Outside the classical prime-power-quotient resultant cases, `R_{m,n}=1`, so the whole finite Hessian characteristic polynomial is already integral even though individual eigenvalues need not be rational. This closes the natural fixed-level route in which the nonlinear eigenvalue data of the PC-128 Hessian was hoped to create a new cyclotomic spectral field after the linear/character information of PC-130--PC-132 had collapsed. It does **not** rule out cross-level or infinite-dimensional organizations of these integer/rational coefficients, and it does not diagonalize the Hessian.

## 1. The PC-128 graph and the resultant denominator

Let

\[
X=P_m^*=\{x_1,\ldots,x_r\},\qquad
Y=P_n^*=\{y_1,\ldots,y_s\},\qquad m\neq n,
\]

so the two exact-order shells are disjoint. PC-128 identifies the Hessian of the vertexwise logarithmic shell interaction with the weighted Laplacian `H=H_{m,n}` of the complete bipartite graph `K_{r,s}` whose edge conductances are

\[
\boxed{
 c_{ij}=\frac1{\delta_{ij}},\qquad
 \delta_{ij}=|x_i-y_j|^2
 =(x_i-y_j)(x_i^{-1}-y_j^{-1}).
}
\]

Every `delta_ij` is a totally real algebraic integer in the cyclotomic field generated by the two shells. The product over **all** bipartite edges is exactly the squared absolute cyclotomic resultant,

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{i=1}^r\prod_{j=1}^s\delta_{ij}
=
|Res(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)|^2
=R_{m,n}^{\,2}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
}
\]

PC-129 used this identity only for spanning trees. The point here is that the same complement-product cancellation works for every spanning forest simultaneously.

## 2. Matrix-forest expansion of the full characteristic polynomial

For an undirected weighted graph with Laplacian `H`, the matrix-forest theorem / all-minors matrix-tree expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI+H)
=
\sum_{F}
 t^{c(F)}
 \left(\prod_{T\in\pi(F)}|V(T)|\right)
 \prod_{e\in F}c_e,
}
\]

where the sum is over spanning forests `F`, `c(F)` is the number of connected components, and `pi(F)` is the set of tree components. The factor `prod_T |V(T)|` counts the possible choice of one root in each component. In particular, every characteristic coefficient is a finite integer-weighted sum of squarefree edge monomials.

This is classical graph theory, not a new matrix identity. Chebotarev--Shamis prove the weighted matrix-forest theorem and the rooted-forest interpretation of `det(I+L)`; Chebotarev--Agaev identify forest matrices as the coefficients in the Laplacian characteristic/adjugate polynomial (P. Chebotarev and R. Agaev, *Forest matrices around the Laplacian matrix*, Linear Algebra Appl. 356 (2002), 253--274, DOI `10.1016/S0024-3795(02)00388-9`; P. Chebotarev and E. Shamis, *Matrix-Forest Theorems*, arXiv:`math/0602575`). The Prime-Circle-specific step is what the cyclotomic resultant does to **all** those forest monomials at once.

## 3. One resultant clears every characteristic coefficient

For any spanning forest `F`, multiply its conductance monomial by the full resultant product:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{m,n}^{\,2}\prod_{e\in F}c_e
&=
\left(\prod_{e\in E}\delta_e\right)
\left(\prod_{e\in F}\delta_e^{-1}\right)\\
&=
\boxed{
\prod_{e\in E\setminus F}\delta_e.
}
\end{aligned}
\]

The right-hand side is an algebraic integer. Therefore, if

\[
\det(tI+H)=\sum_{q=0}^{r+s}a_qt^q,
\]

then every `R^2 a_q` is an algebraic integer.

Now let `M=lcm(m,n)` and work in `K=Q(zeta_M)`. For every `u in (Z/MZ)^times`, the Galois automorphism `zeta_M -> zeta_M^u` preserves exact order, hence permutes `X`, permutes `Y`, and sends each edge weight to the correspondingly relabelled edge weight. Thus

\[
\sigma_u(H)=P_uHP_u^{-1}
\]

for a permutation matrix `P_u`, so every `a_q` is fixed by `Gal(K/Q)` and therefore lies in `Q`. Equivalently, the same Galois action merely permutes the forest summands above.

Consequently `R^2 a_q` is simultaneously rational and an algebraic integer, hence an integer. This proves

\[
\boxed{
R_{m,n}^{\,2}\det(tI+H_{m,n})\in\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

The denominator bound is notably **uniform in the coefficient degree**: one factor `R^2`, not a power `R^{2q}`, clears the entire polynomial. Since `H` has one zero mode, the reduced polynomial also satisfies

\[
\boxed{
R_{m,n}^{\,2}\frac{\det(tI+H_{m,n})}{t}\in\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

By Apostol's classical cyclotomic-resultant theorem, for distinct `m,n` the resultant is `1` except in the prime-power quotient cases (up to ordering). Hence generically

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI+H_{m,n})\in\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

## 4. Exact controls: irrational eigenvalues can coexist with an integral polynomial

For `(m,n)=(3,4)`, PC-129 gives the conductance block

\[
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
2+\sqrt3&2-\sqrt3\\
2-\sqrt3&2+\sqrt3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The four Hessian eigenvalues are

\[
\boxed{0,\quad 8,\quad 4-2\sqrt3,\quad 4+2\sqrt3,}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI+H_{3,4})
=t(t+8)(t^2+8t+4)\in\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

Thus the theorem is not claiming rational eigenvalues. It says that the complete unordered finite spectrum is a Galois-stable algebraic packet whose elementary symmetric invariants are rational, and in the generic resultant-one case integral.

For the exceptional pair `(m,n)=(3,6)`, the conductance block is

\[
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/4\\
1/4&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with Hessian eigenvalues

\[
\boxed{0,\quad \tfrac12,\quad 2,\quad \tfrac52.}
\]

Here `R_{3,6}=4` and

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI+H_{3,6})
=
\frac14\,t(t+2)(2t+1)(2t+5).
}
\]

Multiplication by `R^2=16` gives an integer polynomial, as required. The coefficient of `t` is `5/2`, equal to `(r+s) tau_{3,6}=4(5/8)`, recovering the PC-129 Kirchhoff cofactor as the lowest nonzero characteristic coefficient.

## 5. Relation to PC-091 and what is genuinely stronger here

PC-091 already gives a broad fixed-field warning: a fully contracted finite rational-function network over complete Galois-stable root shells is rational. That principle alone predicts rational characteristic coefficients once they are written as complete contractions, so **no historical novelty is claimed for Galois rationality itself**.

The stronger Prime-Circle-specific statement here comes from combining the PC-128 inverse-square edge structure with the cyclotomic resultant product and the matrix-forest expansion. It supplies a single explicit denominator for the **whole** characteristic polynomial,

\[
\boxed{a_q\in R_{m,n}^{-2}\mathbb Z\quad\text{for every }q,}
\]

and upgrades this to integrality of the entire polynomial whenever `R=1`. PC-129 was the extremal spanning-tree coefficient of this more general forest identity.

A targeted prior-art search found the two ingredients separately -- classical matrix-forest expansions and classical cyclotomic resultants -- but no source for this exact resultant-normalized characteristic-polynomial statement for the Prime-Circle bipartite inverse-square Hessian. That absence is not a proof of novelty; the durable claim is the exact derived classification and the research boundary it creates.

## 6. Consequence for the RH search

PC-130--PC-132 showed that the **linear coefficient alphabet** of the shell Hessian reduces to fixed Dirichlet/Bernoulli data. PC-129 left open whether nonlinear finite spectral invariants could escape that collapse. For the most canonical such invariant -- the full finite characteristic polynomial -- the answer is now sharply constrained:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite PC-128 Hessian}
\longrightarrow
\text{Galois-stable algebraic spectrum}
\longrightarrow
R^{-2}\mathbb Z[t].
}
\]

The spectral variable `t` here is only the ordinary variable of a finite characteristic polynomial. The derivation introduces no analytic continuation in `s`, no gamma factor, no `s <-> 1-s` involution, no distinguished `Re(s)=1/2`, and no zero divisor related to the nontrivial zeros of `zeta`. Therefore **passing from the edge coefficients to the full fixed-level characteristic polynomial does not by itself produce the missing RH mechanism or a new cyclotomic spectral phase**.

This is a scoped boundary, not a blanket no-go for integer/rational data. PC-091's Lewis--Zagier warning remains decisive: cross-scale or asymptotic organizations of rational cotangent matrices can encode GRH criteria. Likewise an arithmetic sequence of integral characteristic polynomials could still carry nontrivial information after a genuinely intrinsic cross-level/infinite construction. What is closed here is the hope that finite Hessian spectral symmetrization alone supplies a new analytic object.

## 7. Exact falsification tests and surviving boundary

The claim has direct finite checks:

1. Construct `H_{m,n}` from `c_{ij}=|x_i-y_j|^{-2}` for any distinct shell pair and compute `R=|Res(Phi_m,Phi_n)|`.
2. Expand `det(tI+H)` exactly in the common cyclotomic field; every coefficient multiplied by `R^2` must be an integer.
3. In a resultant-one pair, every coefficient itself must be an integer even when individual eigenvalues are irrational.
4. For each coefficient, independently enumerate spanning forests with the root multiplicity `prod_T |V(T)|`; after multiplication by `R^2`, each monomial becomes the complement product `prod_{e notin F} delta_e`.
5. Apply every Galois automorphism of `Q(zeta_lcm(m,n))`; it must permute the forest terms and fix each coefficient.

A single coefficient with `R^2 a_q notin Z` would refute the theorem. A counterexample would have to expose either an error in the PC-128 Hessian weights, the forest expansion, or the resultant product identity.

The result leaves open exactly the structures not captured by a single finite symmetric spectrum: individual eigenvectors/eigenvalue labellings before Galois contraction; non-symmetric block/tensor data; cross-level couplings between different `(m,n)` Hessians; infinite limits or asymptotic determinant organizations; and the global uniformization/monodromy branch. Those require independent novelty audits rather than being inferred from the present integrality theorem.
