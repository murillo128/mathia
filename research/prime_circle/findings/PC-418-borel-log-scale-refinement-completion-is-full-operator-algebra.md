# PC-418 — Borel log-scale/refinement completion is the full operator algebra

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-WEYL-IRREDUCIBILITY` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the unrestricted bounded-Borel / von-Neumann completion left open by PC-417. On the canonical shell-2 Hilbert space, the unitary log-scale group already used in PC-417 together with only two intrinsic prime-refinement directions generates an irreducible Weyl system. Consequently its von Neumann algebra is

\[
\boxed{B\!\left(L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx/x)\right).}
\]

Equivalently, adjoining arbitrary bounded Borel functions of the intrinsic log-position operator `Q` to the source-forced descendant/refinement operators does not produce a more selective Prime-Circle spectral mechanism: after strong/weak operator closure it produces **every bounded operator on the ambient Hilbert space**. The apparent gap left by the finite-measure Fourier-Stieltjes class in PC-417 therefore has a sharp opposite failure mode. The bounded Fourier-Stieltjes calculus is restrictive enough to remain on the one-shell Mellin boundary, whereas the unrestricted Borel/von-Neumann completion is so expressive that arbitrary spectral structure can be inserted.

This is a closure statement, not a no-go for every individually source-forced nonlocal operator. A specific bounded Borel function, singular kernel, proper operator algebra, or topology independently forced by Prime-Circle geometry could still be meaningful. What is ruled out is treating **arbitrary** bounded Borel functional calculus, or the unrestricted strong/weak closure of the PC-417 generators, as evidence for a new RH mechanism.

## 1. The exact descendant law contains the prime dilations themselves

Use the Hilbert representation from PC-407--PC-417,

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx/x),
\qquad
(V_a f)(x)=f(ax),
\tag{1}
\]

where every `V_a` is unitary. For a fixed parent shell `n>1`, the exact prime-child operators are

\[
C_{n,p}=
\begin{cases}
V_p,&p\mid n,\\[1mm]
V_p-I,&p\nmid n.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

This is the source-forced refinement law, not an added operator choice. In any unital operator algebra containing the descendant operators, (2) recovers

\[
\boxed{
V_p=
\begin{cases}
C_{n,p},&p\mid n,\\[1mm]
C_{n,p}+I,&p\nmid n.
\end{cases}}
\tag{3}
\]

for **every** rational prime `p`. Thus passing from descendant differences to their generated unital algebra automatically includes the underlying prime dilations. Since a von Neumann algebra is `*`-closed, it also includes `V_p^*=V_{1/p}` and hence

\[
V_q\qquad(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}).
\tag{4}
\]

No arithmetic limit has entered here; (3) is an exact algebraic consequence of the same refinement operators already used by PC-405--PC-417.

## 2. Logarithmic radius turns the intrinsic refinements into a dense translation group

Put

\[
u=\log x
\]

and let

\[
(\mathcal Uf)(u):=f(e^u).
\tag{5}
\]

Then `U` is unitary from `H` to `L^2(R,du)`. Under this conjugacy,

\[
\mathcal U V_a\mathcal U^{-1}=S_{\log a},
\qquad
(S_hg)(u)=g(u+h).
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore the intrinsic rational dilations (4) become translations by

\[
D:=\log\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\tag{7}
\]

The subgroup `D` is dense in `R`, simply because `Q_{>0}` is dense in `R_{>0}` and `log` is a homeomorphism. In fact only the two prime directions `2` and `3` are already enough: `log 2/log 3` is irrational, since a rational relation would imply `2^r=3^s` for nonzero integers `r,s`, and the additive group

\[
\{m\log2+n\log3:m,n\in\mathbb Z\}
\tag{8}
\]

is dense in `R`.

The important point is structural. The dense translation group does **not** come from granting arbitrary continuous scale motion by hand. It is generated by two exact prime-child refinement directions plus adjoints/closure.

## 3. The PC-417 log-position unitary group supplies the conjugate Weyl direction

The intrinsic log-position operator is

\[

Together with (6), these satisfy the Weyl relation

\[
M_aS_h=e^{-iah}S_hM_a.

PC-417 deliberately studied only finite words and finite-measure Fourier-Stieltjes superpositions of these operations. In that restricted class the normal form remained a finite-measure integral of real Mellin translates and therefore never moved the zeta argument off `Re(s)=1`.

The open question was whether allowing the full bounded Borel calculus of `Q`, or a stronger infinite-order completion, could escape that boundary. The answer is not another one-shell factorization. Instead, the unrestricted operator completion becomes universal.

## 4. Arbitrary bounded Borel calculus of `Q` is the full multiplication algebra

By the spectral theorem, bounded Borel functional calculus of `Q` is, after (5), exactly multiplication by arbitrary essentially bounded measurable functions:

\[
\phi(Q)
\longleftrightarrow
M_\phi,
\qquad
(M_\phi g)(u)=\phi(u)g(u),
\qquad
\phi\in L^\infty(\mathbb R).
\tag{13}
\]

Every `L^infinity` class has a Borel representative modulo Lebesgue-null sets, so there is no smaller measurable multiplier algebra hidden in the wording “bounded Borel”. Equivalently, the von Neumann algebra generated by the unitary group `{e^{iaQ}:a in R}` is already this multiplication algebra:

\[
W^*(e^{iaQ}:a\in\mathbb R)=L^\infty(\mathbb R)
\tag{14}
\]

acting by multiplication.

Thus the stronger closure does not merely add a few kernels beyond PC-417's Fourier-Stieltjes class. It adds all spectral projections of `Q` and hence the entire position MASA.

## 5. Dense intrinsic translations plus the Borel `Q` calculus have scalar commutant

Define on `L^2(R)`

\[
\mathfrak M
:=W^*\!\left(
\{M_\phi:\phi\in L^\infty(\mathbb R)\}
\cup
\{S_h:h\in D\}
\right),
\qquad D=\log\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\tag{15}
\]

We compute its commutant directly.

Let `A in M'`. Since `A` commutes with every multiplication operator, and the multiplication algebra on `L^2(R)` is maximal abelian, there exists `g in L^infinity(R)` such that

\[
A=M_g.
\tag{16}
\]

Commutation with each intrinsic translation gives

\[
M_gS_h=S_hM_g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g(u+h)=g(u)
\quad\text{for a.e. }u,
\qquad h\in D.
\tag{17}
\]

Because `D` is countable, one may choose a common full-measure set on which (17) holds for all `h in D`. To make the density step insensitive to representatives, convolve `g` with any smooth compactly supported test function `eta`. The convolution `g*eta` is continuous and inherits every period in `D`; since `D` is dense, continuity makes `g*eta` invariant under **all** real translations, hence constant. Applying an approximate identity `eta_epsilon` then gives

\[
g*\eta_\varepsilon\to g
\quad\text{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}
\tag{18}
\]

while every `g*eta_epsilon` is constant and uniformly bounded by `||g||_infinity`. A convergent subsequence of those constants shows that `g` itself is constant almost everywhere. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak M'=\mathbb C I.}
\tag{19}
\]

This is the elementary commutant form of irreducibility for the Schrödinger/Weyl representation. The only project-specific input is that Prime-Circle's exact child law supplies a dense translation subgroup without inserting continuous translations externally.

Since `M` in (15) is already a von Neumann algebra, the bicommutant theorem and (19) yield

\[
\boxed{

\tag{20}
\]

Conjugating back by (5),

\[
\boxed{

\[
\boxed{

\{e^{iaQ}:a\in\mathbb R\}

Hence the distinction between PC-417 and the present result is a **topological closure boundary**. Finite words and finite-measure superpositions are still rigid enough to have the PC-417 normal form; unrestricted strong/weak closure of the same generators is not rigid at all.

## 10. Boundary and falsification tests

The claim is deliberately about the **von Neumann / strong-weak closure**. It does not classify the norm closure of the finite Weyl-descendant algebra, intermediate Banach algebras, Schatten/trace-class ideals, or a particular source-forced integral kernel. Those may retain meaningful restrictions and would require their own topology and source-specificity audits.

Likewise, (21) does not say that every bounded Borel `phi(Q)` is useless. It says that granting the whole Borel calculus and unrestricted operator closure gives no selection principle: the ambient algebra becomes all of `B(H)`. A specific `phi` could still matter if its formula is independently forced by roots-of-unity geometry and survives matched controls; it cannot be credited merely because it belongs to the completed algebra.

The exact theorem has simple falsification points. It would fail if the prime-child operators did not recover `V_2` and `V_3` through (3), if their logarithmic translation subgroup were not dense, if bounded Borel functions of `Q` did not give the full multiplication algebra, or if a nonconstant essentially bounded function were invariant under every translation in the dense subgroup (8). Equations (2)--(18) exclude those possibilities without analytic continuation, asymptotics, or numerical evidence.

The radial shell-2 operator route now has a sharp completion boundary. **Increasing the log-scale calculus from PC-417 all the way to arbitrary bounded Borel/von-Neumann closure does not uncover hidden Prime-Circle spectral information; it destroys selectivity by generating the full bounded-operator algebra.**

Merely invoking a larger bounded functional calculus of `Q`, strong limits of the existing Weyl/refinement generators, or a spectral object available only because the closure is `B(H)` is now closed as a source-specific RH route.
