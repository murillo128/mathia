# PC-089 — two-step cotangent shell paths collapse to endpoint cyclotomic potentials

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the most direct shell-aware two-step use of the Prime-Circle cotangent geometry. A path that starts on one exact-order shell, propagates through one intermediate shell with the intrinsic oriented cotangent kernel, and exits to another shell can look like a genuinely two-dimensional relational observable because the intermediate vertices are summed before any Fourier or character diagonalization. In fact it collapses exactly to the direct endpoint cotangent block multiplied by two scalar endpoint potentials, plus a rank-one cardinality term.

For a primitive intermediate shell those scalar potentials are just logarithmic derivatives of its cyclotomic polynomial. Consequently a nonzero two-path defect around a divisor square is **not** a new prime-refinement curvature: it is an endpoint-only cyclotomic displacement. This closes the simplest exact-order-projector / two-operator escape left open after PC-087 and PC-088. It does not classify paths with two or more genuinely weighted intermediate shells, nonlinear tensors not generated by one intermediate cotangent contraction, Hardy higher traces, or the global uniformization/monodromy branch.

## 1. The cotangent kernel has an exact three-point composition law

For distinct points `z=e^{i theta_z}` and `w=e^{i theta_w}` on the unit circle define the oriented Prime-Circle cotangent kernel

\[
\boxed{
K(z,w)=i\cot\!\left(\frac{\theta_z-\theta_w}{2}\right).
}
\]

This is the same kernel used in PC-045--PC-053: when `z=zeta_N^a` and `w=zeta_N^b`,

\[
K(z,w)=i\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi(a-b)}N\right).
\]

For three pairwise distinct points `z,u,w`, the elementary cotangent addition formula gives

\[
\cot x\cot y
=1+\cot(x+y)(\cot x+\cot y).
\]

With

\[
x=\frac{\theta_z-\theta_u}{2},
\qquad
y=\frac{\theta_u-\theta_w}{2},
\]

this becomes the exact kernel identity

\[
\boxed{
K(z,u)K(u,w)
=K(z,w)\bigl(K(z,u)+K(u,w)\bigr)-1.
}
\]

No arithmetic hypothesis, limiting argument, spectral parameter, or choice of coordinates beyond the oriented circle is involved.

## 2. Summing one intermediate shell leaves only endpoint potentials

Let `A,B,C` be pairwise disjoint finite subsets of the unit circle. Write `K_{A,B}` for the rectangular matrix obtained by restricting `K` to rows in `A` and columns in `B`. Define the scalar potential of the intermediate set `B` by

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_B(z)=\sum_{u\in B}K(z,u),
\qquad z\notin B.
}
\]

Let `D_B^A` and `D_B^C` be the diagonal matrices with entries `sigma_B(z)` on `A` and `C`, respectively, and let `J_{A,C}` be the all-ones rectangular matrix. Summing the three-point identity over `u in B` gives entry by entry

\[
\begin{aligned}
(K_{A,B}K_{B,C})(z,w)
&=K(z,w)
\left(
\sum_{u\in B}K(z,u)
+
\sum_{u\in B}K(u,w)
\right)
-|B|\\
&=K(z,w)\bigl(\sigma_B(z)-\sigma_B(w)\bigr)-|B|,
\end{aligned}
\]

because `K(u,w)=-K(w,u)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{A,B}K_{B,C}
=D_B^A K_{A,C}
-K_{A,C}D_B^C
-|B|J_{A,C}.
}
\]

This is the decisive structural collapse. The full matrix of two-step paths through every vertex of `B` contains no irreducible interior tensor. Once the direct endpoint block `K_{A,C}` is known, the intermediate set enters only through one scalar value at each endpoint and its cardinality.

The identity is stronger than a rank estimate. The left side may have maximal rank and may differ strongly for two choices of intermediate shell, yet all of that variation is forced by endpoint multiplication of the direct block.

## 3. Primitive intermediate shells are cyclotomic logarithmic derivatives

Now take

\[
B=P_r^*
\]

for an exact primitive shell. For `u` and `z` on the circle,

\[
K(z,u)
=-\frac{z+u}{z-u}
=1-\frac{2z}{z-u}.
\]

Since

\[
\Phi_r(z)=\prod_{u\in P_r^*}(z-u),
\qquad
\frac{\Phi_r'(z)}{\Phi_r(z)}
=\sum_{u\in P_r^*}\frac1{z-u},
\]

we get the exact endpoint formula

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_r(z)
:=\sigma_{P_r^*}(z)
=\varphi(r)-2z\frac{\Phi_r'(z)}{\Phi_r(z)},
\qquad z\notin P_r^*.
}
\]

Thus every two-step exact-shell contraction is already in the classical cyclotomic rational-function field generated by `Phi_r'/Phi_r` evaluated at the endpoint roots.

The common anchor is especially rigid. For every `r>1`, cyclotomic reciprocity

\[
\Phi_r(z)=z^{\varphi(r)}\Phi_r(z^{-1})
\]

implies

\[
2\frac{\Phi_r'(1)}{\Phi_r(1)}=\varphi(r),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\sigma_r(1)=0.}
\]

Therefore a two-step path from an arbitrary disjoint endpoint set `A` through `P_r^*` to the common anchor has the even simpler form

\[
\boxed{
K_{A,P_r^*}K_{P_r^*,\{1\}}
=D_r^A K_{A,\{1\}}
-\varphi(r)\mathbf 1_A,
}
\]

where `D_r^A=diag(sigma_r(z):z in A)`. The intermediate shell has become a scalar cyclotomic endpoint potential multiplying the original pointed cotangent profile.

## 4. The natural two-prime divisor-square defect is endpoint-only

Let `d>1` and let `p,q` be distinct primes coprime to `d`. Inside the common circle of level

\[
n=dpq
\]

consider the four exact-order shells

\[
P_d^*,\qquad P_{dp}^*,\qquad P_{dq}^*,\qquad P_{dpq}^*.
\]

Write `H_{e,f}` for the cotangent block from `P_{df}^*` to `P_{de}^*`; equivalently it is the restriction of the single ambient kernel `K`. There are two canonical two-step paths from the base shell to the top shell,

\[
M_p=H_{pq,p}H_{p,1},
\qquad
M_q=H_{pq,q}H_{q,1}.
\]

Let

\[
B=H_{pq,1}
\]

be the direct top-to-base block. Applying the general identity with intermediate shells `P_{dp}^*` and `P_{dq}^*` gives

\[
\boxed{
M_p-M_q
=
D_{\Delta\sigma}^{\rm top}B
-BD_{\Delta\sigma}^{\rm base}
-\bigl(\varphi(dp)-\varphi(dq)\bigr)J,
}
\]

where

\[
\Delta\sigma(z)
=
\sigma_{dp}(z)-\sigma_{dq}(z)
\]

and every `sigma` is the explicit cyclotomic logarithmic derivative from Section 3.

The raw path difference is generally nonzero. That nonzero value, however, is not evidence for a two-prime curvature or holonomy: it is exactly a left/right endpoint-potential displacement of the direct block plus a cardinality term. There is no residual operator depending on the internal order in which the vertices of the intermediate shell are traversed.

The pointed version is stronger still. If the lower endpoint is the common anchor `1`, then both intermediate potentials vanish there and

\[
\boxed{
M_p^{(1)}-M_q^{(1)}
=
D_{\Delta\sigma}^{\rm top}K_{P_{dpq}^*,\{1\}}
-\bigl(\varphi(dp)-\varphi(dq)\bigr)\mathbf1.
}
\]

So the most natural anchored two-route test retains only scalar cyclotomic data at the top endpoints.

## 6. What remains outside the obstruction

The collapse is deliberately sharp rather than global.

- A path with **two or more weighted intermediate shells** is not covered. After the first contraction, the next shell sees the nonconstant weight `sigma_B`, so the elementary unweighted identity no longer closes automatically.
- A genuinely nonlinear tensor built from several distinct geometric operators is not classified unless it reduces to one intermediate cotangent contraction.
- Shell-dependent kernels other than the canonical oriented cotangent kernel are outside the theorem.

A directed literature search around cotangent-matrix products, cotangent kernels, cyclotomic logarithmic derivatives, and cotangent-sum scale identities located the classical surrounding ingredients but not a need to treat the displayed Prime-Circle shell-path identity as a new general theorem.
