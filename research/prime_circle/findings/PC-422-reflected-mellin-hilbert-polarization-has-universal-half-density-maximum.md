# PC-422 — reflected Mellin Hilbert polarization has a universal half-density maximum

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-MELLIN-PARSEVAL` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the canonical `sigma <-> 1-sigma` Hilbert-commutator repair left open by PC-421. Pairing the two reflected continuous-scale commutators does produce an exact zeta-bearing `s <-> 1-s` vertical-line product, and for every strictly positive radial source profile the pairing is uniquely maximized at `sigma=1/2`. However, both effects are universal: the unweighted reflected product is the classical Mellin Parseval formula, while the strict half-density maximum follows from an elementary `cosh` identity and is reproduced by the zero-free nonarithmetic control `rho_0(x)=e^{-x}`. Thus this natural reflected-line compact coupling does not supply an RH critical-line selector.

This result is deliberately narrower than a no-go for all cross-weight operators. It closes the direct Hilbert--Schmidt polarization of the PC-421 family and any interpretation of its `1/2` extremum as zeta-specific. A surviving mechanism must couple reflected Mellin weights in a way that is not already forced by Mellin Parseval / homogeneous Sobolev polarization and that fails an appropriate nonarithmetic matched control.

## 1. The canonical reflected-line operator pairing

PC-421 defines, from the exact Prime-Circle radial flux of PC-179,

\[
\rho_n(x)
=-\frac{d}{dx}\log\Phi_n(e^{-x}),
\qquad
\mathcal R_n(s)=\int_0^\infty \rho_n(x)x^{s-1}\,dx,
\tag{1}
\]

and for `sigma>0`

\[
h_{n,\sigma}(u)=e^{\sigma u}\rho_n(e^u),
\qquad
\widehat h_{n,\sigma}(t)
=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\mathcal R_n(\sigma-it).
\tag{2}
\]

Let `H` be the real-line Hilbert transform with multiplier `-i sgn(t)` and

\[
C_{n,\sigma}:=[H,M_{h_{n,\sigma}}].
\tag{3}
\]

PC-421 proves that every `C_{n,sigma}` is self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt. The most direct way to exploit the missing functional-equation symmetry is therefore to pair the reflected weights before taking a scalar invariant:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_n(\sigma)
:=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(C_{n,\sigma}C_{n,1-\sigma}\right),
\qquad 0<\sigma<1.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The product is trace class because both factors are Hilbert--Schmidt. Polarizing the exact PC-421 Hilbert--Schmidt identity gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(C_fC_g)
=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|t|\,
\overline{\widehat f(t)}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\tag{5}
\]

Since `rho_n` is real, equations (2) and (5) yield

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_n(\sigma)
=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R}|t|\,
\mathcal R_n(\sigma+it)
\mathcal R_n(1-\sigma-it)\,dt.
}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the exact reflected vertical-line coupling that PC-421 itself left open.

For

\[
A_n(s):=n^{1-s}\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{s-1}),
\qquad
\mathcal R_n(s)=-\Gamma(s)\zeta(s)A_n(s),
\tag{7}
\]

(6) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_n(\sigma)
=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R}|t|\,
\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)
\zeta(s)\zeta(1-s)
A_n(s)A_n(1-s)\,dt,
\quad s=\sigma+it.
}
\tag{8}
\]

So the canonical compact pairing really does display the functional-equation involution `s <-> 1-s`. The question is whether that involution has been generated by the Prime-Circle geometry in a restrictive way or merely inherited from transform duality.

## 2. The reflected product before the commutator is exactly Mellin Parseval

The answer is already visible without the `|t|` multiplier. From (2),

\[
h_{n,\sigma}(u)h_{n,1-\sigma}(u)
=e^u\rho_n(e^u)^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R}h_{n,\sigma}(u)h_{n,1-\sigma}(u)\,du
=\int_0^\infty \rho_n(x)^2\,dx,
}
\tag{10}
\]

independently of `sigma`. Fourier Plancherel then gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
\mathcal R_n(\sigma+it)
\mathcal R_n(1-\sigma-it)\,dt
=\int_0^\infty \rho_n(x)^2\,dx.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Equation (11) is not a new functional equation. It is exactly the classical Mellin Parseval-type identity specialized to `f=g=rho_n` and `y=1`; see NIST DLMF §1.14(iv), Eq. 1.14.36, <https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14.E36>. The hypotheses hold here for `0<sigma<1`: `rho_n` is bounded at the origin and exponentially decaying at infinity.

Thus the reflected product `R_n(s)R_n(1-s)` is forced before any zeta-specific reasoning enters. The Hilbert commutator in (6) adds the homogeneous frequency weight `|t|`, equivalently the standard homogeneous `H^{1/2}`/Besov quadratic polarization familiar from Hilbert-transform commutator theory. It does not create the reflection involution.

## 3. Positive source profiles force a strict maximum at one half

The remaining possible signal is the `|t|`-weighted pairing itself. It has an exact real-space form that settles the issue.

For an arbitrary real source profile `rho` with the same regularity and decay as above, set

\[
g(u):=e^{u/2}\rho(e^u),
\qquad
\delta:=\sigma-\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]

Then

\[
h_\sigma(u)=e^{\delta u}g(u),
\qquad
h_{1-\sigma}(u)=e^{-\delta u}g(u).
\tag{13}
\]

Using the commutator kernel

\[
K_h(u,v)=\frac{h(v)-h(u)}{\pi(u-v)},
\tag{14}
\]

one obtains pointwise

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(h_\sigma(v)-h_\sigma(u)\bigr)
 \bigl(h_{1-\sigma}(v)-h_{1-\sigma}(u)\bigr)\\
&\qquad =g(v)^2+g(u)^2
-2\cosh\!\bigl(\delta(v-u)\bigr)g(u)g(v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Subtracting the `delta=0` case gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_\rho\!\left(\frac12\right)-\mathcal P_\rho(\sigma)
=\frac{2}{\pi^2}
\iint_{\mathbb R^2}
\frac{\cosh(\delta(v-u))-1}{(u-v)^2}
\,g(u)g(v)\,du\,dv.
}
\tag{16}
\]

If `rho(x)>0` for all `x>0`, then `g(u)>0`, so the right side is nonnegative and is strictly positive for every `sigma != 1/2`. Moreover `cosh(a(v-u))-1` increases pointwise with `|a|`, hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_\rho(\sigma)
=\mathcal P_\rho(1-\sigma),
\qquad
\mathcal P_\rho(\sigma)
<\mathcal P_\rho(1/2)
\quad(\sigma\neq1/2),
}
\tag{17}
\]

with strict decrease as `|sigma-1/2|` increases inside `(0,1)`.

Prime-power Prime-Circle fluxes are strictly positive, so (17) applies in particular to every `n=p^a`. The smallest shell is already decisive:

\[
\rho_2(x)=\frac1{e^x+1},
\qquad
\mathcal R_2(s)=\Gamma(s)(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s).
\tag{18}
\]

Thus a source whose Mellin transform contains all nontrivial zeta zeros already has a unique reflected-Hilbert maximum at `1/2`, but the proof of that maximum uses only positivity of the real-space profile and the reciprocal weights in (13). It does not use the location of a single zeta zero. Even if zeta possessed off-critical zeros, (16)--(17) would remain unchanged.

This is stronger than merely observing the half-density gauge of PC-374: here the canonical nonlocal compact operator and an explicit `sigma <-> 1-sigma` pairing do create a strict extremum at `1/2`, but the extremum is still universal rather than arithmetic.

## 4. A zero-free nonarithmetic control has exactly the same selector

Take the matched positive control

\[
\rho_0(x):=e^{-x}.
\tag{19}
\]

Its Mellin transform is

\[
\mathcal R_0(s)=\Gamma(s),
\tag{20}
\]

which has no zeros anywhere. Nevertheless the same construction gives

\[
h_{0,\sigma}(u)=e^{\sigma u-e^u},
\tag{21}
\]

and (16)--(17) apply verbatim. Hence the reflected Hilbert pairing is again uniquely maximized at `sigma=1/2` despite there being no zero set to select.

At the maximum one can evaluate the invariant exactly. Since

\[
|\Gamma(1/2+it)|^2=\frac{\pi}{\cosh(\pi t)},
\tag{22}
\]

(6) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_0(1/2)
=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R}|t|
|\Gamma(1/2+it)|^2\,dt
=\frac{4G}{\pi^3},
}
\tag{23}
\]

where `G` is Catalan's constant. This provides an independent exact normalization check on the constants in (5)--(6): expand `sech y=2 sum_{k>=0}(-1)^k e^{-(2k+1)y}` and integrate `y sech y` termwise to obtain `2G`.

The zero-free control is the decisive falsification test. Any interpretation of the maximum in (17) as evidence that the zeta zeros are constrained to `Re(s)=1/2` would make the identical claim for the Gamma transform, where no zeros exist at all.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The reflected transform identity is classical. NIST DLMF §1.14(iv), Eq. 1.14.36 records the Mellin Parseval-type formula with the complementary arguments `s` and `1-s`; equation (11) is its direct specialization. There is therefore no novelty claim for the appearance of `s <-> 1-s` itself.

The operator-theoretic weighting is classical as well. Janson and Wolff, **Schatten classes and commutators of singular integral operators**, *Arkiv for Matematik* 20 (1982), 301--310, DOI `10.1007/BF02390515`, is a classical source for Schatten-class commutators. Lacey, Li and Wick, **Schatten Classes and Commutator in the Two Weight Setting, I. Hilbert Transform**, *Potential Analysis* 60 (2024), 875--894, DOI `10.1007/s11118-023-10072-x`, gives a modern Hilbert--Schmidt/Besov characterization. These results place the `|t|` energy in (5) within standard harmonic/operator analysis.

The project-specific exact contribution is the boundary classification obtained by combining that classical machinery with the PC-179 source: the canonical reflected polarization proposed by the open frontier of PC-421 has formula (6), while (16) proves that its apparently critical `1/2` extremum is a positivity theorem for arbitrary positive profiles. The zero-free control (19)--(23) separates that extremum from zeta arithmetic.

This does not duplicate PC-421, which tested one vertical weight at a time and explicitly left source-forced reflected coupling open. It also does not duplicate PC-374, whose `1/2` arose from half-density normalization alone. Here two different Hilbert--Schmidt operators are coupled, the `s <-> 1-s` product appears exactly, and the resulting strict half-line extremum is shown to be universal.

## 6. Boundary, falsification, and surviving frontier

The strict inequality (17) requires a positive real-space profile. Prime-power radial fluxes satisfy that hypothesis; general composite-shell fluxes need not. Therefore this finding does **not** claim that every shell-by-shell reflected pairing is maximized at one half, nor does it rule out matrix-valued or sign-sensitive cross-shell constructions that deliberately exploit non-prime-power structure.

What is ruled out is the most canonical repair of PC-421: take the source-native family `C_{n,sigma}`, pair the reflected weights by the Hilbert--Schmidt trace, and interpret the resulting symmetry or its `1/2` maximum as a zeta critical-line mechanism. The reflected symmetry is Mellin Parseval, and the extremum already holds for a zero-free positive control.

The load-bearing audit points are independent: (6) follows by Fourier polarization of the Hilbert commutator, while (16) follows directly from its real-space kernel. A sign or normalization error in one route is therefore detectable by the other; (23) supplies a third exact constant check. A falsification of the classification would require either failure of one of these identities under the stated regularity hypotheses or a demonstrated zeta-specific invariant of the same reflected pairing that is absent from the matched Gamma control.

The surviving route is correspondingly narrower. A serious `sigma <-> 1-sigma` candidate must add structure before scalar Mellin pairing -- for example a shell-dependent matrix/noncommutative interaction, an angular or old/new coupling mixed with radial scale, or another source-forced nonlocal kernel -- and its critical behavior must survive comparison against nonarithmetic positive profiles rather than follow from reciprocal half-density weights alone.
