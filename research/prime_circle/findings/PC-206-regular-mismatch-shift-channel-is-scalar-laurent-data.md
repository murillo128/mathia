## 1. Source packet and the signed mismatch

Take any fixed finite product of the source-native Cauchy--Poisson character packets used in PC-197--PC-205 at strictly positive packet depths. After expanding and collecting the common angular variable,

\[
F(\theta)=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb Z} a_\Delta e^{i\Delta\theta}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here `Delta` is the total positive-frequency sum minus the total negative-frequency sum. Because every packet depth is positive, each one-sided character series is exponentially damped. Their fixed finite product therefore has

\[
\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb Z}|a_\Delta|<\infty.
\tag{2}
\]

This absolute summability is important: it makes the operator channel below intrinsic without any formal summation or regularization at unit mismatch radius.

Let

\[
\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb Z),
\qquad
S e_j=e_{j+1}
\tag{3}
\]

be the bilateral regular representation of the additive mismatch group. For `0<r<=1`, define

\[
A_r
=
\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb Z}

\tag{4}

By (2), the series converges absolutely in operator norm, including at `r=1`. In particular, `A_1` records every coefficient `a_Delta` as a distinct translation channel. This is exactly the simplest infinite-dimensional realization suggested by the frontier of PC-205, with no extra boundary, graph, or basis choice added to the signed mismatch itself.

For `r<1`, the same operator can be written as an operator-valued anchor contraction. Define

\[

\tag{5}

Let

f_r(\varphi)=(P_r*F)(-\varphi),

\tag{12}

## 3. Spectral data are exactly scalar-symbol data

Because `f_r` is continuous on the compact circle, multiplication-operator theory gives

\[
\operatorname{Spec}(A_r)=f_r(\mathbb T),
\qquad
\|A_r\|=\|f_r\|_\infty.
\tag{13}

Retaining `Delta` as an infinite basis index is faithful, but it is exactly a change of representation of scalar Fourier data, not a new spectral carrier.

There is also a direct obstruction to a Hilbert--Polya-style discrete spectrum. For any `lambda` outside (13),

\[
(\lambda I-A_r)^{-1}
=
U^{-1}M_{(\lambda-f_r)^{-1}}U.

Indeed, on a positive-measure set where the multiplier has modulus at least some `epsilon>0`, split that set into infinitely many disjoint positive-measure pieces and use their normalized indicator functions. \[
\boxed{\text{the regular mismatch channel has no compact resolvent.}}
\tag{15}

This conclusion is intrinsic to the regular `Z` channel and does not depend on the special arithmetic form of `F`.
