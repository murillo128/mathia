# PC-419 — norm-closed Weyl-descendant algebra has no compact sector

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-WEYL/CCR-BOUNDARY` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the norm-closed operator-algebra loophole left explicitly open by PC-418. On the canonical shell-2 Hilbert space, let

\[
\mathcal A_n
:=C^*\!\left(
\{e^{iaQ}:a\in\mathbb R\}
\cup
\{C_{n,p}:p\text{ prime}\}
\right)
\subset B\!\left(L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx/x)\right),
\]

where `Qf(x)=(\log x)f(x)` and the exact descendant operators `C_{n,p}` are those of PC-405--PC-418. Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_n\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H)=\{0\}.}
\]

Equivalently, the Calkin quotient is faithful on `A_n`. Hence every bounded element of `A_n` has ordinary spectrum equal to essential spectrum, no nonzero finite-rank or trace-class element belongs to `A_n`, and no self-adjoint operator whose nonreal resolvent lies in `A_n` can have an isolated eigenvalue of finite multiplicity. In particular, the norm closure cannot provide a Hilbert--Pólya-style discrete spectral carrier or a nontrivial trace-class/Fredholm-determinant channel from these generators alone.

This complements rather than contradicts PC-418. The **norm closure** is too rigid: it has no compact sector and therefore no intrinsic discrete finite-multiplicity spectrum. The **von Neumann / strong-weak closure** of the same generators is too large: PC-418 proves that it is all of `B(H)`. Thus merely changing the closure topology between the finite/Fourier--Stieltjes calculus of PC-417 and the unrestricted von Neumann completion does not produce the missing arithmetic selection principle.

## 1. Exact reduction to a norm-closed Weyl algebra

Use the logarithmic coordinate

\[
u=\log x,
\qquad
\mathcal H\simeq L^2(\mathbb R,du).
\]

Write

\[
(M_a f)(u)=e^{iau}f(u),
\qquad
(S_h f)(u)=f(u+h).
\]

PC-418 proves from the exact prime-child law that the unital `*`-algebra generated by the descendants contains every rational dilation `V_q`, `q>0`, hence every translation

\[
S_h,
\qquad
h\in D:=\log\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\]

The exponentiated log-position group is exactly `{M_a:a in R}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_n
=C^*\!\left(M_a,S_h:a\in\mathbb R,\ h\in D\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]

The Weyl relation is

\[
M_aS_h=e^{-iah}S_hM_a.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently every algebraic word normal-orders to a scalar multiple of one `M_aS_h`, and the algebraic span is the finite Weyl-polynomial space

\[
\mathcal W_0
=
\left\{
\sum_{j=1}^N c_jM_{a_j}S_{h_j}
:N<\infty
\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal A_n=\overline{\mathcal W_0}^{\|\cdot\|}.
\tag{3}
\]

No source profile enters (1)--(3). The only Prime-Circle input is that the exact descendant law supplies the translation subgroup `D` while `Q` supplies the modulation direction.

## 2. Every element has arbitrarily large norm-recurrences under translation conjugacy

For `t in R`, define the external conjugation automorphism

\[
\alpha_t(T):=S_{-t}TS_t.
\tag{4}
\]

The unitary `S_t` need not itself belong to `A_n`; conjugation nevertheless preserves `A_n` because

\[
\alpha_t(M_aS_h)=e^{-iat}M_aS_h.
\tag{5}
\]

Let

\[
T=\sum_{j=1}^N c_jM_{a_j}S_{h_j}\in\mathcal W_0.
\]

For the finite frequency set `{a_1,...,a_N}`, simultaneous Dirichlet/Kronecker recurrence gives arbitrarily large `t` such that

\[
\max_j|e^{-ia_jt}-1|<\varepsilon.
\]

Hence

\[
\|\alpha_t(T)-T\|
\le
\sum_{j=1}^N |c_j|\,|e^{-ia_jt}-1|
\longrightarrow0
\]

along an unbounded recurrence sequence.

The same is true for **every** `A in A_n`. Choose `T_m in W_0` with

\[
\|A-T_m\|<1/m.
\]

For each `m`, choose `t_m>m` so that

\[
\|\alpha_{t_m}(T_m)-T_m\|<1/m.
\]

Since every `alpha_t` is isometric,

\[
\boxed{
\|\alpha_{t_m}(A)-A\|
\le
2\|A-T_m\|
+
\|\alpha_{t_m}(T_m)-T_m\|
<3/m.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus every element of the norm-closed Weyl-descendant algebra is norm recurrent under conjugation by translations escaping to infinity.

## 3. A compact operator cannot have that recurrence unless it vanishes

For every `f in L^2(R)`, translations escape weakly:

\[
S_t f\rightharpoonup0
\qquad(|t|\to\infty).
\tag{7}
\]

This follows first for compactly supported `L^2` functions by separating supports and then for general vectors by density; equivalently, the matrix coefficient `\langle S_tf,g\rangle` is a Fourier transform of an `L^1` function and vanishes at infinity.

Assume now that

\[
0\ne K\in\mathcal A_n\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H).
\]

Choose `f` with `Kf != 0`, and choose the recurrence sequence `t_m` from (6). Since `K` is compact and `S_{t_m}f` is bounded and weakly null,

\[
\|KS_{t_m}f\|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]

But

\[
KS_{t_m}
=S_{t_m}\alpha_{t_m}(K),
\]

so unitary invariance of the norm and (6) give

\[
\|KS_{t_m}f\|
=
\|\alpha_{t_m}(K)f\|
\longrightarrow
\|Kf\|>0,
\tag{9}
\]

contradicting (8). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_n\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal H)=\{0\}.}
\tag{10}
\]

The proof is stronger than a dimension or source-specific argument: it applies to the whole norm closure and uses only the Weyl covariance already forced by the PC-417/PC-418 representation.

## 4. The Calkin representation is faithful, so spectrum is purely essential

Let

\[
\pi_{\mathrm C}:B(\mathcal H)\to
B(\mathcal H)/\mathcal K(\mathcal H)
\]

be the Calkin quotient. Equation (10) says exactly that

\[
\ker(\pi_{\mathrm C}|_{\mathcal A_n})=0.
\]

Thus `pi_C` restricts to an injective unital `*`-homomorphism on `A_n`. By spectral permanence for C*-subalgebras,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{B(\mathcal H)}(A)
=
\sigma_{\mathcal A_n}(A)
=
\sigma_{\mathrm Calkin}(\pi_{\mathrm C}(A))
=:\sigma_{\mathrm ess}(A)
}
\tag{11}
\]

for every `A in A_n`.

So the norm-closed algebra has **no discrete spectral sector separated from its essential spectrum**. In particular, if a normal `A in A_n` had an isolated eigenvalue of finite nonzero multiplicity, the corresponding continuous-functional-calculus spectral projection would be a nonzero finite-rank element of `A_n`, contradicting (10).

The same obstruction applies to an unbounded self-adjoint operator `H` whenever a nonreal resolvent belongs to the algebra,

\[
(H-i)^{-1}\in\mathcal A_n.
\tag{12}
\]

An isolated finite-multiplicity eigenvalue `lambda` of `H` becomes an isolated nonzero finite-multiplicity eigenvalue `(lambda-i)^{-1}` of the normal element `(H-i)^{-1}`. Its spectral projection would again be a nonzero finite-rank element of `A_n`. Hence no such `H` can have the discrete finite-multiplicity zero spectrum required by a direct Hilbert--Pólya interpretation.

Compact resolvent is excluded a fortiori: if `(H-i)^{-1}` were compact and belonged to `A_n`, (10) would force it to vanish, impossible for a resolvent.

## 5. Trace-class and Fredholm determinant repairs also vanish

Every trace-class, Hilbert--Schmidt, or finite-rank operator is compact. Therefore

\[
\mathcal A_n\cap\mathcal S_p=\{0\}
\qquad(1\le p<\infty),
\tag{13}
\]

and two elements of `A_n` cannot differ by a nonzero compact operator.

Thus a Fredholm-determinant repair of the form

\[
\det(I+T),
\qquad T\in\mathcal A_n\cap\mathcal S_1,
\]

is necessarily trivial because `T=0`. Likewise, no nonzero source-forced trace-class correction can be obtained merely by taking norm limits of the same Weyl-descendant generators. A trace-class determinant can still be introduced by enlarging the algebra or by inserting an additional smoothing/resolvent kernel, but then that extra structure must itself be forced and audited; it is not produced by the norm closure.

## 6. Matched controls and RH relevance

The obstruction is source-blind. Replace the cyclotomic shell vector by any nonzero matched profile in `L^2(R_+,dx/x)` while keeping the exact scale representation and the same descendant operators. The algebra (1), the recurrence (6), and the no-compact theorem (10) are unchanged.

Therefore any discrete finite-multiplicity spectrum that appears only after adding a compact, trace-class, resolvent, or smoothing ingredient comes from that **additional ingredient**, not from the norm-closed Prime-Circle Weyl-descendant algebra itself. This does not rule out angular/chord/old-new operators before shell-2 radial reduction, nonlinear source-dependent constructions, a geometrically forced proper smoothing algebra, or a different source-native state space. It rules out only the natural proposal left by PC-418: use the operator-norm closure of the existing log-scale/refinement Weyl generators as the missing spectral completion.

Together PC-417--PC-419 now give a sharp three-regime boundary:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{finite/Fourier--Stieltjes calculus}
&\Rightarrow&\text{one-shell real Mellin translates},\\[1mm]

\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]

The middle completion therefore does not repair either endpoint in the way needed for a direct RH spectral mechanism.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The operator-algebraic phenomenon is classical. J. Slawny, **On factor representations and the C*-algebra of canonical commutation relations**, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 24 (1972), 151--170, DOI `10.1007/BF01878451`, is an early C*-algebraic CCR/Weyl reference. D. Buchholz and H. Grundling, **The Resolvent Algebra: A New Approach to Canonical Quantum Systems**, *Journal of Functional Analysis* 254 (2008), 2725--2779, DOI `10.1016/j.jfa.2008.02.011`, explicitly contrast the simple Weyl algebra with a resolvent algebra designed to contain the compact/ideal structure and affiliated Hamiltonians missing from the Weyl setting. D. Buchholz and T. D. H. van Nuland, **The basic resolvents of position and momentum operators form a total set in the resolvent algebra**, *Letters in Mathematical Physics* 113 (2023), article 119, DOI `10.1007/s11005-023-01741-4`, further shows that the resolvent algebra norm-generates compact operators from basic resolvents, illustrating exactly the kind of additional generator class absent here.

No historical novelty is claimed for Weyl/CCR simplicity, almost-periodic recurrence, Calkin spectral permanence, or the general fact that compact-resolvent operators require compact resolvents. The exact result persisted here is the **line-local closure classification** for the mixed Prime-Circle generator set of PC-418. Because that set uses all log modulations but only the source-forced rational-log translation subgroup, the proof above is given directly and does not import a stronger full-continuous-CCR identification than the project actually supplies.

The novelty audit therefore classifies PC-419 as a project-specific negative consequence inside classical Weyl/CCR operator theory, not as a new theorem about CCR algebras.

## 8. Falsification and boundary tests

The main claim has a direct falsification test: exhibit a nonzero compact operator that is a norm limit of finite sums `sum c_j M_{a_j}S_{h_j}` with `h_j in log Q_{>0}`. Such an operator would contradict the recurrence argument (6)--(9). Equivalently, exhibit `A in A_n` whose Calkin image has smaller spectrum than `A`; injectivity in (10) and C*-spectral permanence exclude this.

The boundary is equally explicit. Strong/weak limits are not covered and in fact give the opposite result `B(H)` by PC-418. Norm closures after adjoining a genuinely new source-forced resolvent or smoothing kernel are also not covered; the modern resolvent-algebra literature shows that such additions can create compact ideals. Any future repair along that route must therefore derive the new kernel from Prime-Circle geometry **before** invoking its compact/Fredholm consequences, and then compare it against matched nonarithmetic controls.
