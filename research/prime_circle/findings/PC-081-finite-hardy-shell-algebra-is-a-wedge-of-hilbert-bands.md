# PC-081 — finite Hardy shell algebra is a wedge of universal Hilbert bands

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` + `PRIOR-ART-REDIRECTION` for finite algebraic cross-shell essential-spectrum mechanisms in the cyclotomic Hardy/Hankel branch. The exact wedge-algebra classification below is a direct consequence of PC-075 and PC-080 plus standard C*-algebra functional calculus. The general localization of disjoint Hankel singularities is classical operator theory; no theorem-level historical novelty is claimed.

PC-080 proves that two distinct primitive-shell Hardy operators have trace-class product,

\[
\Gamma_m\Gamma_n\in\mathcal S_1\qquad(m\neq n),
\]

while PC-075 proves, for every `n>2`,

\[
\sigma_{\rm ess}(\Gamma_n)=[-\pi,\pi].
\]

Taken together these statements classify **all finite algebraic combinations of distinct shell operators at the noncompact level**, not merely pairwise products. If `S={n_1,...,n_r}` is any finite set of distinct conductors with every `n_i>2`, then the C*-algebra generated by their images in the Calkin algebra is exactly the algebra of continuous functions on `r` copies of `[-pi,pi]` glued only at the common point `0`.

Equivalently, the noncompact Hardy data have no point at which two different shell coordinates are simultaneously nonzero. Every mixed monomial is trace class, every finite polynomial essential spectrum reduces branch-by-branch to a **single-shell Hilbert band**, and even finite block-matrix constructions built algebraically from the canonical shell operators have only one-shell matrix symbols on each branch.

Thus the route

\[
\boxed{
\text{finitely many primitive Hardy shells}
\to
\text{algebraic/noncommutative cross-shell coupling}
\to
\text{new essential spectrum}
\to
\text{RH}
}
\]

is closed. Any surviving cross-shell arithmetic must live below the essential-spectrum level — in trace-class relative data such as PC-080's resultant trace, higher cyclic traces/determinants, or in a genuinely new cross-level operator not generated algebraically by the completed single-shell `\Gamma_n`.

## 1. Every mixed noncommutative monomial is trace class

Let

\[
S=\{n_1,\ldots,n_r\},
\qquad n_i>2,
\qquad n_i\neq n_j\ (i\neq j).
\]

Consider a word

\[
W=\Gamma_{i_1}\Gamma_{i_2}\cdots\Gamma_{i_k}
\]

using at least two distinct shell labels. Somewhere in the word there is an adjacent transition

\[
i_j\neq i_{j+1}.
\]

Factor the word there:

\[
W=A\,(\Gamma_{i_j}\Gamma_{i_{j+1}})\,B,
\]

where `A` and `B` are bounded products of Hardy shell operators. PC-080 gives

\[
\Gamma_{i_j}\Gamma_{i_{j+1}}\in\mathcal S_1,
\]

and `S_1` is a two-sided ideal in the bounded operators. Hence

\[
\boxed{W\in\mathcal S_1}
\]

for **every** finite word containing more than one shell label.

Therefore any scalar noncommutative polynomial `P` admits, after evaluation on the shell operators, the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
P(\Gamma_{n_1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n_r})
=cI+\sum_{i=1}^r p_i(\Gamma_{n_i})+K,
\qquad K\in\mathcal S_1,
}
\]

where

\[
c=P(0,\ldots,0)
\]

and each one-variable polynomial is

\[
p_i(t)=P(0,\ldots,0,t,0,\ldots,0)-c,
\qquad p_i(0)=0.
\]

This is stronger than commutation modulo compact operators. Every genuinely mixed finite algebraic term is already **nuclear**.

For example, for distinct `m,n`,

\[
\Gamma_m\Gamma_n+\Gamma_n\Gamma_m\in\mathcal S_1,
\qquad
[\Gamma_m,\Gamma_n]\in\mathcal S_1,
\]

and

\[
(\Gamma_m+\Gamma_n)^k
-\Gamma_m^k-\Gamma_n^k
\in\mathcal S_1
\]

for every integer `k>=2`.

## 2. Exact Calkin algebra: a wedge of intervals

Let

\[
\pi:\mathcal B(\ell^2)\to\mathcal Q
=\mathcal B(\ell^2)/\mathcal K
\]

be the Calkin quotient and put

\[
x_n=\pi(\Gamma_n).
\]

PC-080 gives the exact orthogonality relations

\[
\boxed{x_mx_n=0\qquad(m\neq n).}
\]

Each `x_n` is self-adjoint, and PC-075 gives

\[
\boxed{\sigma(x_n)=[-\pi,\pi]\qquad(n>2).}
\]

For a continuous function `f` on `[-pi,pi]` with `f(0)=0`, polynomial approximation shows that `f(x_m)g(x_n)=0` whenever `m!=n` and `g(0)=0`. Thus the nonunital ideals generated by the different `x_n` are pairwise orthogonal.

Let `X_S` be the compact space obtained by taking one copy

\[
I_n=[-\pi,\pi]
\]

for every `n in S` and identifying all their zero points. Write

\[
X_S=\bigvee_{n\in S}(I_n,0).
\]

On this wedge define the coordinate `xi_n` to equal `t` on the `n`-th interval and `0` on every other interval. The assignment

\[
\xi_n\longmapsto x_n
\]

extends to a surjective *-homomorphism

\[
C(X_S)\longrightarrow C^*(1,x_n:n\in S).
\]

It is injective. Indeed, if

\[
c1+\sum_{n\in S}f_n(x_n)=0,
\qquad f_n(0)=0,
\]

then multiplying by `x_k` kills every branch except `k` and gives

\[
\bigl(c+f_k(x_k)\bigr)x_k=0.
\]

Faithful continuous functional calculus for the self-adjoint element `x_k`, whose spectrum is the full interval `[-pi,pi]`, implies

\[
(c+f_k(t))t=0
\]

for all `t` in that interval. Hence `c+f_k(t)=0` for `t!=0`; continuity at zero and `f_k(0)=0` force `c=0` and then `f_k=0`. This holds for every `k`.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
C^*(1,\pi(\Gamma_n):n\in S)
\cong
C\!\left(\bigvee_{n\in S}[-\pi,\pi]\right).
}
\]

The geometry of the joint essential spectrum is therefore literally a wedge: **there is no Calkin point carrying two nonzero shell coordinates simultaneously**.

## 3. Essential spectrum of every finite polynomial is branchwise one-dimensional

Let `P` be any scalar noncommutative polynomial. Mixed words vanish in the Calkin algebra, so its quotient image is the continuous wedge function whose restriction to the `i`-th branch is simply

\[
t\longmapsto
P(0,\ldots,0,t,0,\ldots,0).
\]

Since the spectrum of a continuous function in `C(X_S)` is its range,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm ess}
\bigl(P(\Gamma_{n_1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n_r})\bigr)
=
\bigcup_{i=1}^r
\left\{
P(0,\ldots,t,\ldots,0):
-\pi\le t\le\pi
\right\}.
}
\]

No mixed-shell value appears on the right-hand side.

The simplest control is a finite linear superposition. For real coefficients `a_i`,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\Gamma_{n_i}\right)
=
\bigcup_{i=1}^r a_i[-\pi,\pi]
=
[-\pi A,\pi A],
\qquad
A=\max_i|a_i|.
}
\]

In particular, for the unweighted sum of **any** finite set of distinct shells `n_i>2`,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(\sum_i\Gamma_{n_i}\right)
=[-\pi,\pi].
}
\]

Adding more exact-order shells does not broaden, split, shift, or otherwise reshape the essential spectral set. The shell labels disappear completely from that set.

This is precisely the finite-shell manifestation of the localization principle already visible in PC-075/PC-080, but the wedge classification shows that it survives arbitrary finite algebraic manipulation, not only addition or pairwise multiplication.

## 4. Finite block operators do not restore essential cross-shell mixing

One possible escape from PC-080 was to retain several shell labels as a finite block system rather than multiply them immediately to one scalar operator. The same obstruction persists whenever the blocks are built algebraically from the canonical `Gamma_n`.

Let

\[
\mathbf P=[P_{ab}]_{1\le a,b\le q}
\]

be a `q x q` matrix whose entries are noncommutative polynomials in the shell operators, acting on

\[
\ell^2(\mathbb Z_{\ge0})\otimes\mathbb C^q.
\]

Modulo compact operators this is a matrix-valued continuous function on the same wedge. On the `i`-th branch its symbol is exactly

\[
\mathbf P_i(t)
=
\mathbf P(0,\ldots,0,t,0,\ldots,0).
\]

Standard matrix-valued functional calculus then gives

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm ess}(\mathbf P)
=
\bigcup_{i=1}^r
\bigcup_{t\in[-\pi,\pi]}
\sigma\bigl(\mathbf P_i(t)\bigr).
}
\]

Thus finite matrix bookkeeping does not create a joint point with two live shell variables either. Any new essential band produced by such a block construction is already obtainable from a **one-shell matrix polynomial** on the universal Hilbert interval.

This does not rule out a new cross-level operator whose off-diagonal blocks are independently forced by Prime-Circle geometry and are not elements of the algebra generated by the completed single-shell `Gamma_n`. It rules out only the natural attempt to manufacture cross-level noncompactness by algebraically stacking the existing Hardy shell operators.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract operator-theoretic mechanism is classical.

1. PC-075 already anchors Magnus/Rosenblum Hilbert-matrix spectral theory and Pushnitski--Yafaev multichannel Hankel theory. In the latter framework, disjoint symbol singularities generate independent propagation channels rather than a new shared absolutely continuous band.
2. PC-080 proves the much more specific Prime-Circle input needed here: exact-order shell singularities are disjoint, their cross products are trace class, and their first mixed trace is the classical cyclotomic resultant.
3. Once pairwise products vanish in the Calkin algebra, the wedge description is standard commutative C*-algebra/continuous-functional-calculus reasoning. A directed check of the general operator-theory neighborhood confirms that unions of essential spectra for self-adjoint families with pairwise compact products are established territory. No abstract spectral theorem is claimed as new.

The durable contribution is therefore a **research-object classification**: the canonical finite family of cyclotomic Hardy shell operators has a completely separated noncompact algebra. This sharply limits where genuinely arithmetic multi-shell information can still live.

## 6. What this rules out and what survives

The result closes every finite construction of the form

\[
\text{distinct completed Hardy shells}
\to
\text{finite sums/products/commutators/polynomial blocks}
\to
\text{essential spectrum}
\]

as a source of new RH-sensitive cross-shell structure. Mixed monomials are trace class, and the quotient algebra is only a wedge of universal Hilbert bands.

It does **not** say the trace-class mixed sector is trivial. In particular:

- PC-080 already shows `Tr(Gamma_m Gamma_n)` can carry the classical resultant;
- higher cyclic quantities such as `Tr(Gamma_a Gamma_b Gamma_c)` remain outside this finding and can be nontrivial even though they cannot affect the essential spectrum;
- Fredholm/perturbation determinants, trace norms, singular values, and isolated eigenvalues created by trace-class shell interactions remain open;
- countably infinite shell families are not covered without a separate convergence/control theorem;
- a genuinely new refinement/cross-level operator with geometrically forced off-diagonal maps is not required to belong to this generated algebra and is not ruled out;
- the old/new cotangent branch and the global nonlinear uniformization/monodromy branch rooted in PC-017 remain unrelated to this Hardy Calkin calculation.

The important boundary is therefore exact: **finite algebraic cross-shell information survives only in relative/trace-class data, not in noncompact spectral geometry.**

## 7. Falsification surface

The classification has four direct failure points.

1. PC-080 must hold with `Gamma_m Gamma_n in S_1` for every pair of distinct exact-order shells in `S`.
2. PC-075 must give the full essential interval `[-pi,pi]` for every `n>2`; otherwise the wedge branches have different spectra.
3. Every mixed polynomial word must contain an adjacent distinct-shell pair, allowing the two-sided `S_1` ideal argument.
4. In the Calkin algebra, continuous functions vanishing at zero on different shell coordinates must multiply to zero; failure would invalidate the wedge functional calculus.

All four points reduce directly to the exact prior findings plus standard ideal/functional-calculus identities. No numerical approximation or limiting interchange is required.

## Research consequence

The multi-shell Hardy branch has now been localized one step further than PC-080:

\[
\boxed{
C^*(1,\pi(\Gamma_n):n\in S)
\cong
C\!\left(\bigvee_{n\in S}[-\pi,\pi]\right),
\qquad |S|<\infty,\ n>2.
}
\]

So finite algebraic stacking of primitive Hardy shells cannot produce a joint essential spectral geometry capable of encoding zeta zeros or a critical line. The next meaningful cross-shell Hardy question is necessarily **relative** — higher cyclic traces/determinants or another trace-class invariant — or must introduce a genuinely new geometrically forced cross-level operator before the completed shell operators are compressed into this wedge algebra.
