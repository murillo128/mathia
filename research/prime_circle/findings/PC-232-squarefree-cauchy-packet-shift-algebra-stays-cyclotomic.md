# PC-232 — squarefree Cauchy-packet shift algebra stays in classical cyclotomic torsion

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-IDENTITY` + `DECISIVE-BOUNDARY` for the simultaneous-squarefree-growth escape left open by PC-231. Starting from the source-forced PC-227 Cauchy packets, any finite expression built from the two canonical Fourier-dual bilinear operations—pointwise multiplication and bilateral convolution—can raise pole order, alter amplitudes, create integer-shift punctures, and create finitely supported resonant terms, but its nonintegral packet shifts never leave the additive subgroup generated by the input shifts. At squarefree conductor `N`, that subgroup is `(1/N) Z / Z`; over all squarefree conductors its direct limit is exactly the squarefree-torsion subgroup `⊕_p Z/pZ` of `Q/Z`.

Consequently, allowing the nonlinear expression depth to grow with a squarefree conductor does **not** synthesize a new shift carrier: in particular it cannot manufacture `p^2`-torsion from squarefree packet shifts. The growing shift support is real, infinite in the direct limit, and dense on the circle, but it is still the classical cyclotomic torsion already fenced by the Prime-Circle refinement/Bost--Connes audit. This does not close simultaneous squarefree growth as a whole: growing amplitudes, pole orders, support cardinalities, numerator/normalization state, non-translation-invariant old/new operators, and genuinely coupled multi-prime dynamics remain outside the theorem.

## 1. Confluent packets are the natural closure class

For a nonintegral rational shift `a` and integer `m >= 1`, write

\[
f_{a,m}(k):=\frac{1}{(k+a)^m},
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{1}
\]

PC-227 supplies the simple case `m=1` with shifts `a=s/N`, and PC-231 proves the first bilinear closure laws. To classify arbitrary finite operation depth, one must also retain the objects created at resonances. Let

\[
h_{m,c}(k):=
\begin{cases}
0,&k=-c,\\
(k+c)^{-m},&k\ne-c,
\end{cases}
\qquad c\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{2}
\]

and allow finitely supported lattice deltas. For an additive subgroup `Gamma <= Q/Z`, let `R_Gamma` be the finite linear span of integer translates of the `f_{a,m}` with `a in Gamma\setminus{0}`, the punctured integer packets (2), and finitely supported deltas.

Ordinary confluent partial fractions already show that pointwise multiplication preserves `R_Gamma`: equal shifts add pole orders, unequal shifts decompose into poles at the two original shifts, and any discrepancy caused by a puncture is finitely supported. The nontrivial question is convolution.

## 2. General confluent convolution adds shifts and nothing else

For positive integers `m,r`, put

\[
(f_{a,m}*f_{b,r})(n)
:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\frac{1}{(k+a)^m(n-k+b)^r}.
\tag{3}
\]

The original series is absolutely convergent because `m+r >= 2`. With

\[
x=k+a,
\qquad
D=n+a+b,
\tag{4}
\]

the exact rational partial-fraction identity, valid for `D != 0`, is

\[
\frac{1}{x^m(D-x)^r}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{m}
\binom{m+r-j-1}{r-1}
\frac{1}{D^{m+r-j}x^j}
+
\sum_{j=1}^{r}
\binom{m+r-j-1}{m-1}
\frac{1}{D^{m+r-j}(D-x)^j}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the symmetric lattice sums

\[
S_1(a):=\operatorname{PV}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{k+a}
=\pi\cot(\pi a),
\tag{6}
\]

and, for `j >= 2`,

\[
S_j(a):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(k+a)^j}
=
\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}
\frac{d^{j-1}}{da^{j-1}}\bigl[\pi\cot(\pi a)\bigr].
\tag{7}
\]

Summing (5) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
(f_{a,m}*f_{b,r})(n)
={}&
\sum_{j=1}^{m}
\binom{m+r-j-1}{r-1}
\frac{S_j(a)}{(n+a+b)^{m+r-j}}\\
&+
\sum_{j=1}^{r}
\binom{m+r-j-1}{m-1}
\frac{S_j(b)}{(n+a+b)^{m+r-j}} ,
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8}
\]

whenever `n+a+b != 0`. The principal values in (6) only evaluate the separated partial fractions; (3) itself is absolutely convergent.

Equation (8) is the higher-pole version of PC-231. Every nonresonant convolution is a finite linear combination of confluent packets whose fractional shift is

\[
\boxed{a+b\pmod 1.}
\tag{9}
\]

No other fractional shift appears.

## 3. Resonances create punctures and deltas, not new torsion

If `a+b=1`, then the unique resonant lattice coordinate is `n=-1`. There

\[
(f_{a,m}*f_{1-a,r})(-1)
=
(-1)^r S_{m+r}(a).
\tag{10}
\]

For every `n != -1`, equation (8) remains valid and is a finite linear combination of powers `(n+1)^{-q}`. Thus the resonant output is a finite combination of punctured integer-shift packets plus a delta at `-1`. PC-231's simple-pole identity

\[
f_{a,1}*f_{1-a,1}
=-\pi^2\csc^2(\pi a)\,\delta_{-1}
\tag{11}
\]

is the maximally collapsed first member of this general family.

The punctured terms do not open a hidden escape at later depth. Convolving a punctured integer packet with a nonintegral packet is evaluated by the same partial fractions with one lattice summand removed; the removed summand is itself a rational function of `n+b`, so the output remains at shift `b`. Convolving two punctured integer packets gives only punctured integer powers plus finitely supported corrections. Delta convolution merely translates the lattice coordinate. Hence

\[
\boxed{
R_\Gamma\cdot R_\Gamma\subseteq R_\Gamma,
\qquad
R_\Gamma*R_\Gamma\subseteq R_\Gamma.
}
\tag{12}
\]

By induction on an arbitrary finite expression tree, every state obtained from initial shifts in `Gamma` by pointwise multiplication and bilateral convolution remains in `R_Gamma`. The expression depth may depend on the conductor; no uniform depth bound is required for the shift-support statement.

## 4. Squarefree growth gives exactly squarefree cyclotomic torsion

At a fixed PC-227 coarse conductor `N`, every source-forced packet shift lies in

\[
\Gamma_N:=\frac1N\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z.
\tag{13}
\]

Equations (9) and (12) therefore prove that arbitrary finite packet-algebra depth cannot leave `Gamma_N`. If `N` is squarefree, the Chinese remainder theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_N\cong\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z
\cong
\bigoplus_{p\mid N}\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Taking the directed union over squarefree conductors yields

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}
:=\bigcup_{N\ \mathrm{squarefree}}\Gamma_N
=
\{x\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z:\operatorname{ord}(x)\ \text{is squarefree}\}
\cong
\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is a proper subgroup of `Q/Z`: it contains no element of order `p^2`. The obstruction is visible without taking a limit. Starting from the shift `1/p`, repeated convolution can only visit

\[
\frac1p,\frac2p,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{p},0\pmod1;
\tag{16}
\]

it can never produce `1/p^2`. Likewise, at `N=6`, combining shifts `1/2` and `1/3` produces `5/6`, still inside `Gamma_6`.

The union (15) is nevertheless infinite and dense in the circle under the usual embedding. The result is therefore not a finite-state claim after `N -> infinity`; it is a structural claim about **which torsion can be generated** by this packet algebra.

## 5. The structure constants are also classical cyclotomic data

For rational `a=s/N`, equations (6)--(7) express every `S_j(a)` as `pi^j` times an algebraic number obtained from a root of unity. Explicitly,

\[
\cot(\pi a)
=i\,\frac{e^{2\pi i a}+1}{e^{2\pi i a}-1},
\tag{17}
\]

and repeated derivatives are rational functions of the same trigonometric values. Thus the convolution coefficients lie in powers of `pi` times cyclotomic number fields (one may take a conductor dividing `4N`); pointwise partial-fraction coefficients are rational functions of rational shift differences.

So neither the labels nor the algebraic coefficients reveal a new analytic parameter. The entire finite-depth shift algebra is assembled from classical rational partial fractions, cotangent/Mittag--Leffler sums, and cyclotomic torsion.
