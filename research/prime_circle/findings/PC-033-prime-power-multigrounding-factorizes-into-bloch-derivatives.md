# PC-033 — multi-grounded inverse-square chord spectra reduce to Bloch sectors; prime powers remain derivative spectra

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the prime-power extension of the canonical multi-grounded inverse-square chord-Laplacian route. The full `csc^2` spectrum and circulant/Fourier decomposition are classical; no novelty is claimed for those ingredients.

## 1. The composite-level question left open by PC-032

PC-032 studies the full regular-polygon inverse-square chord Laplacian

\[
(\mathcal L_n f)_a
=
\sum_{b\ne a}
\frac{f_a-f_b}{|z_a-z_b|^2},
\qquad
z_a=e^{2\pi i a/n},
\]

whose full Fourier spectrum is

\[
\lambda_k^{(n)}
=
\frac{k(n-k)}2,
\qquad 0\le k<n.
\]

At prime level, grounding the common old vertex leaves exactly the primitive/new vertices, and PC-032 proves that the resulting characteristic polynomial is `P_p'(t)/p`.

For composite `n`, the exact analogue is to ground **every old/imprimitive vertex** and keep only the primitive exponents

\[
U(n):=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]

Define

\[
A_n
:=
\mathcal L_n[U(n),U(n)].
\]

This is the Dirichlet principal block obtained by setting every nonunit coordinate to zero. It is the natural multi-grounded version explicitly left open by PC-032.

The apparent hope is that the irregular reduced-residue pattern might create a genuinely arithmetic spectrum even though the full regular polygon is elementary.

## 2. The unit pattern is periodic with the squarefree radical

Let

\[
R=\operatorname{rad}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p,
\qquad
m=\frac nR.
\]

The unit condition depends only on the residue modulo `R`:

\[
a\in U(n)
\iff
a\bmod R\in U(R).
\]

Hence translation by `R` preserves both the primitive set and its complement. Since `\mathcal L_n` is circulant, it commutes with the translation

\[
T_R f(a)=f(a+R).
\]

The cyclic group generated by `T_R` has order `m`. Decompose into its Bloch eigenspaces

\[
E_h
=
\{f:T_Rf=e^{2\pi i h/m}f\},
\qquad
0\le h<m.
\]

Each `E_h` has dimension `R`. In the full Fourier basis it is spanned by the frequencies

\[
k=h+jm,
\qquad
0\le j<R,
\]

because

\[
e^{2\pi i kR/n}=e^{2\pi i k/m}.
\]

Therefore the full operator on `E_h` has the explicit eigenvalue multiset

\[
\boxed{
\left\{
\lambda_{h+jm}^{(n)}
=
\frac{(h+jm)(n-h-jm)}2
:
0\le j<R
\right\}.
}
\]

Now use the position basis on `E_h` indexed by residues `r mod R`. Grounding the nonunits deletes exactly the coordinates

\[
r\notin U(R)
\]

in every Bloch sector. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
A_n
\ \simeq\
\bigoplus_{h=0}^{m-1}
B_{n,h},
}
\]

where `B_{n,h}` is the `U(R)` principal block of an `R x R` twisted-circulant matrix whose complete spectrum is the explicit set above.

This is already a useful reduction: **all combinatorial complexity of which vertices are grounded is carried by the squarefree radical `R`**. Prime-power exponents only create Bloch sectors and change their elementary frequency weights.

This statement does not say that `B_{n,h}` is identical to the corresponding block at level `R`; the Bloch weights still depend on `n` and `h`. It says that repeated prime powers create no new deletion pattern.

## 3. Prime powers have only one grounded coordinate per Bloch sector

Now let

\[
n=p^r,
\qquad
m=p^{r-1}.
\]

Then `R=p`, and the nonunits are precisely the single residue class `0 mod p`. Thus every Bloch sector has dimension `p`, and multi-grounding deletes **one coordinate** from each sector.

Write `C_h` for the full `p x p` block on `E_h`. Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
Q_h(t)
=
\det(tI-C_h)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}
\left(
t-\lambda_{h+jm}^{(n)}
\right).
\]

Translation by one vertex commutes with `\mathcal L_n` and preserves `E_h`. In the residue basis it acts as a cyclic monomial matrix: it permutes the `p` coordinate lines transitively, with only the Bloch phase appearing when the cycle wraps around.

Therefore all `p` principal cofactors of `tI-C_h` are equal. Jacobi's derivative identity gives

\[
Q_h'(t)
=
\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{adj}(tI-C_h)
=
p\,
\det(tI-B_{n,h}).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI-B_{n,h})
=
\frac1p Q_h'(t).
}
\]

Multiplying the `m` independent sectors gives the complete primitive-layer characteristic polynomial:

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI-A_{p^r})
=
p^{-m}
\prod_{h=0}^{m-1}
Q_h'(t),
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
Q_h(t)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}
\left[
t-\frac{(h+jm)(p^r-h-jm)}2
\right].
}
\]

Thus **every eigenvalue of the multi-grounded primitive layer at a prime-power level is a critical point of one of a finite family of completely explicit degree-`p` polynomials built from the universal regular-polygon spectrum**.

PC-032 is the special case `r=1`, where `m=1` and there is a single derivative polynomial.

## 4. Exact audit examples

For `n=8=2^3`, `m=4`, every sector is two-dimensional before grounding and one-dimensional after grounding. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Spec}(A_8)
=
\left\{
\frac{\lambda_h^{(8)}+\lambda_{h+4}^{(8)}}2
:
0\le h<4
\right\}
=
\left\{
4,\frac{11}{2},6,\frac{11}{2}
\right\}.
\]

In particular,

\[
\det A_8
=
4\cdot\frac{11}{2}\cdot6\cdot\frac{11}{2}
=
726.
\]

For `n=9=3^2`, `m=3`, the three sector polynomials use the frequency triples

\[
\{0,3,6\},
\qquad
\{1,4,7\},
\qquad
\{2,5,8\}.
\]

The derivative blocks give

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(A_9)
=
\{3,9,7-\sqrt3,7-\sqrt3,7+\sqrt3,7+\sqrt3\},
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\det A_9
=
27(49-3)^2
=
57132.
\]

These checks are exact consequences of the factorization above, not numerical pattern matching.

## 5. Why this closes the prime-power multi-grounding escape

The composite loophole in PC-032 was real: after deleting several old vertices, a principal block of a circulant matrix need not in general be controlled by one derivative of the full characteristic polynomial.

Prime powers looked like the first natural place where that extra structure might matter because the birth layer is already a proper reduced-residue subset.

But the hierarchy of old vertices at `p^r` has an exact periodic symmetry. Bloch decomposition converts the many grounded vertices into **one grounded coordinate in each sector**, and the PC-032 derivative argument then applies sector by sector.

So the route

\[
\boxed{
\text{prime-power birth shell}
\to
\text{ground all older vertices}
\to
1/\text{chord}^2\text{ Laplacian}
\to
\text{new arithmetic spectrum}
}
\]

still collapses to elementary regular-polygon spectral data.

In particular, summing these spectra or determinants over prime powers would not explain Riemann zeros by itself: the only prime-power arithmetic would already have been inserted through the external choice of levels `p^r`, while the operator at each such level is explicitly resolved by the formulas above.

## 6. Prior art and novelty audit

The ingredients are standard:

- Calogero--Perelomov is already the source anchor in PC-032 for the full `csc^2` regular-polygon spectrum.
- Fourier/Bloch decomposition of a circulant operator under a periodic subgroup is standard structured-matrix theory.
- The derivative-of-characteristic-polynomial identity is the same elementary cofactor/Jacobi mechanism used in PC-032.

Searches for the closest combinations -- `csc^2` principal submatrices, reduced-residue restrictions, cyclotomic-root inverse-square matrices, and prime-power circulant principal blocks -- did not identify a source in which this exact prime-power primitive-layer factorization is used as an RH mechanism. That absence is **not** treated as a novelty proof.

No broad theorem novelty is claimed. The durable result is the project-specific obstruction: the most immediate composite extension of PC-032 is still spectrally universal on the entire prime-power subfamily.

## 7. Boundary of the no-go

The radical reduction identifies the surviving case sharply.

If `rad(n)` has at least two prime factors, then every Bloch sector keeps `phi(R)` coordinates and deletes several residue classes. Those multi-coordinate principal minors are not determined by `Q_h'(t)` alone.

Therefore this finding does **not** classify squarefree composite radicals such as `pq`, nor operators that couple different levels before spectralization.

What it does establish is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{new single-level complexity, if any, begins at squarefree radicals with at least two primes.}
}
\]

That is the next nontrivial boundary for this particular nonlocal operator. Global uniformization/monodromy and genuinely cross-level operators remain outside the result.
