# PC-303 — finite oriented-frame tensors are joint Fourier/polyspectral data

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-BOUNDARY` for the finite higher-order labelled-frame escape left open by PC-302.

PC-302 shows that retaining the oriented `+m/-m` frames before same-source Gram formation genuinely recovers reflection-odd information, but its first quadratic average is only sine/cosine Fourier covariance. It therefore leaves higher joint observables open.

At finite tensor degree that escape can be classified exactly. A monomial in entries of the oriented frames is itself one additive character of the source residues. Averaging such a monomial against an arbitrary joint source law gives exactly one coefficient of the joint additive Fourier transform. If one instead averages the frames first and then forms polynomial invariants, the result is a finite polynomial in the ordinary source Fourier coefficients; the translation-invariant part is precisely the classical zero-sum polyspectral algebra. The first phase-sensitive translation-invariant scalar is the cubic bispectrum.

Thus higher order **does** recover phase that PC-302's quadratic positive geometry loses; there is no further reflection-blindness theorem. But the recovered information is not a new Prime-Circle carrier: at fixed degree it is exactly joint Fourier/polyspectral source data. For the canonical distinct-prime source, even the full fixed-order joint transform reduces by partition-lattice inclusion-exclusion to a finite polynomial in the ordinary prime exponential sums `S_q(a)` already exposed in PC-302.

This closes the direct proposal “keep the labelled oriented frames and go to a fixed higher tensor order” as a source of new zeta spectral structure. It does **not** close growing tensor order, cross-level constructions involving several moduli, or an additional geometry-forced operation that acts before the frame phases are reduced to additive characters.

Let `q` be an odd prime,

\[
H_B=\{-B,\ldots,B\},\qquad N=2B+1\le q,
\]

and retain the PC-296--302 oriented frame packet

\[
S_M=\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm M\},\qquad 2M<q,
\]

with

\[
x_{m,s}(h)=N^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i msh/q},
\qquad h\in H_B,\ m\in S_M.
\tag{1}
\]

Deterministic boundary-log weights such as `sqrt(w_m)` may be attached to the columns throughout; they only multiply the formulas below by known coefficients and do not change the information classification.

## 1. Every direct finite frame tensor moment is one joint Fourier coefficient

Let

\[
\pi(s_1,\ldots,s_r)
\]

be an arbitrary probability law on `(Z/qZ)^r`. No independence, exchangeability, distinctness, or prime assumption is imposed.

Choose `d` frame entries. For each factor choose a source coordinate `j_alpha in {1,...,r}`, a window coordinate `h_alpha`, a mode `m_alpha`, and a sign `sigma_alpha in {+1,-1}`; `sigma_alpha=+1` means use `x`, while `sigma_alpha=-1` means use its complex conjugate. Define

\[
T(s_1,\ldots,s_r)
:=
\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}
\begin{cases}
 x_{m_\alpha,s_{j_\alpha}}(h_\alpha),&\sigma_\alpha=+1,\\
 \overline{x_{m_\alpha,s_{j_\alpha}}(h_\alpha)},&\sigma_\alpha=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

For each source coordinate put

\[
\kappa_j
:=
\sum_{\alpha:\,j_\alpha=j}
\sigma_\alpha m_\alpha h_\alpha
\pmod q.
\tag{3}
\]

Substitution of (1) into (2) gives pointwise

\[
\boxed{
T(s_1,\ldots,s_r)
=N^{-d/2}
\exp\!\left(
\frac{2\pi i}{q}
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\kappa_j s_j
\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Define the joint additive Fourier transform

\[
\widehat\pi(k_1,\ldots,k_r)
:=
\sum_{s_1,\ldots,s_r}
\pi(s_1,\ldots,s_r)
\exp\!\left(
\frac{2\pi i}{q}
\sum_{j=1}^{r}k_js_j
\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\pi T
=N^{-d/2}\widehat\pi(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_r).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Equation (6) is the basic classification. Every fixed finite tensor contraction of the oriented frames is a finite linear combination of joint Fourier coefficients of the source law. Correlation and source reuse can change which joint coefficients occur, but they do not create an additional carrier beyond the joint additive transform.

This is strictly stronger than the reflection quotient of PC-301 and deliberately avoids repeating it. If `pi` contains orientation-sensitive phase, (6) retains it. The point is that the retained phase is completely identified.

## 2. Average-then-multiply gives the classical polyspectral algebra

For one source law `mu` on `Z/qZ`, let

\[
\overline X_\mu:=\mathbb E_{s\sim\mu}X_s.
\]

With the Fourier convention of PC-302,

\[
\widehat\mu(a)
:=\sum_{s\bmod q}\mu(s)e^{2\pi i as/q},
\]

we have entrywise

\[
\boxed{
(\overline X_\mu)_{h,m}
=N^{-1/2}\widehat\mu(mh).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Hence every fixed polynomial in entries of `overline X_mu` and their conjugates belongs to the finite polynomial algebra generated by the represented Fourier coefficients `widehat mu(a)`.

There is a sharper statement when one asks for invariance under a common translation of the source law. If

\[
(\tau_c\mu)(s)=\mu(s-c),
\]

then

\[
\widehat{\tau_c\mu}(a)
=e^{2\pi i ac/q}\widehat\mu(a).
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore a Fourier monomial

\[
M=
\prod_{u=1}^{r}\widehat\mu(a_u)
\prod_{v=1}^{t}\overline{\widehat\mu(b_v)}
\tag{9}
\]

is translation invariant exactly when

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u=1}^{r}a_u-
\sum_{v=1}^{t}b_v
\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Because translation acts diagonally by the characters of the cyclic group, any invariant polynomial is a linear combination of such weight-zero monomials. This is precisely the finite cyclic **polyspectral** algebra. Passing from quadratic to cubic, quartic, or any other fixed order can restore Fourier phase, but it remains classical higher-order Fourier data.

No claim that global translation is a gauge of the Prime-Circle source is needed for (6)--(7). Equations (8)--(10) only identify what the standard translation-invariant higher-order repair becomes if that symmetry is imposed.

## 3. The first phase-sensitive invariant is the ordinary bispectrum

For represented frequencies `a`, `b`, and `a+b`, define

\[
\boxed{
B_\mu(a,b)
:=
\widehat\mu(a)\widehat\mu(b)
\overline{\widehat\mu(a+b)}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

This is a cubic polynomial in entries of the averaged oriented frame by (7). Equation (8) shows that it is translation invariant. For a real probability law define the reflected law

\[
\mu^\vee(s)=\mu(-s).
\]

Then

\[
\widehat{\mu^\vee}(a)
=\overline{\widehat\mu(a)},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
B_{\mu^\vee}(a,b)=\overline{B_\mu(a,b)}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently `Im B_mu(a,b)` is a genuine reflection-odd, translation-invariant orientation carrier. This matters conceptually: higher order is not merely another positive compression. It can recover precisely the phase whose loss motivated PC-302.

The repair is nevertheless textbook bispectral data. Ramakrishna Kakarala's **Bispectrum on finite groups**, ICASSP 2009, DOI `10.1109/ICASSP.2009.4960328`, develops the finite-group bispectrum, and the later phase-sensitive invariant literature treats its completeness properties. Tamir Bendory, Dan Edidin and Shay Kreymer, **Signal recovery from a few linear measurements of its high-order spectra**, arXiv:2103.01551, explicitly places the bispectrum and higher-order spectra in circular-shift orbit recovery and shows that order at least three can generically determine a signal up to shift. These references confirm the relevant novelty boundary: the phase recovery is expected classical behavior, not a new Prime-Circle spectral phenomenon.

## 4. The actual prime source gives products of ordinary prime exponential sums

Let

\[
P_q=\{\ell:\ell\text{ prime},\ 2<\ell<q\},
\qquad L_q=|P_q|,
\]

and let `nu_q` be the uniform law on `P_q`. As in PC-302 write

\[
S_q(a):=\sum_{\ell\in P_q}e^{2\pi i a\ell/q}.
\tag{13}
\]

Then

\[
\widehat\nu_q(a)=\frac{S_q(a)}{L_q},
\]

and the bispectrum is exactly

\[
\boxed{
B_{\nu_q}(a,b)
=
\frac{S_q(a)S_q(b)\overline{S_q(a+b)}}{L_q^3}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus the first higher-order orientation-sensitive invariant is a cubic product of ordinary additive exponential sums over primes. This is richer than PC-302's quadratic covariance because it retains relative phase, but no new spectral parameter, gamma factor, functional-equation symmetry, or critical-line condition has been created.

The effect is genuinely nonzero on the native source, not merely formal. For `q=13`,

\[
P_{13}=\{3,5,7,11\}.
\]

Already with `B=1` and `M=2`, the represented frequencies include `-1`, `1`, and `2`. Direct evaluation gives

\[
B_{\nu_{13}}(-1,2)
\approx
0.0198500830-0.0196923212\,i.
\tag{15}
\]

Reflection conjugates this value and reverses its imaginary part while the quadratic reflection-even data of PC-302 is unchanged. So the higher-order repair passes the information-gain test; it fails the novelty test because the information gained is exactly classical Fourier phase.

## 5. Fixed-order distinct-prime joint moments still reduce to the same `S_q` packet

Prime-Circle often samples several distinct lower primes rather than independent samples with replacement. Equation (6) still applies directly to that joint law, but in the prime case the resulting joint Fourier coefficients can be reduced further.

For fixed `r`, let `pi_{q,r}` be the uniform law on ordered `r`-tuples of pairwise distinct elements of `P_q`. Its joint Fourier transform is

\[
\widehat\pi_{q,r}(k_1,\ldots,k_r)
=
\frac{1}{(L_q)_r}
\sum_{\substack{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r\in P_q\\
\ell_i\ne\ell_j}}
\exp\!\left(
\frac{2\pi i}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{r}k_j\ell_j
\right),
\tag{16}
\]

where `(L_q)_r=L_q(L_q-1)\cdots(L_q-r+1)`.

Apply Möbius inversion on the partition lattice of `{1,...,r}`. If `Pi_r` denotes that lattice and

\[
\mu_{\Pi}(\mathcal P)
=
\prod_{C\in\mathcal P}
(-1)^{|C|-1}(|C|-1)!,
\tag{17}
\]

then the injective sum in (16) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\pi_{q,r}(k_1,\ldots,k_r)
=
\frac{1}{(L_q)_r}
\sum_{\mathcal P\in\Pi_r}
\mu_{\Pi}(\mathcal P)
\prod_{C\in\mathcal P}
S_q\!\left(\sum_{j\in C}k_j\right).
}
\tag{18}
\]

For `r=2` this is simply

\[
\widehat\pi_{q,2}(a,b)
=
\frac{S_q(a)S_q(b)-S_q(a+b)}{L_q(L_q-1)}.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus without-replacement coupling does create genuine finite-order correlations, but every fixed-order correlation is still a finite polynomial in the same ordinary prime exponential sums. There is no hidden mixed source variable beyond the additive Fourier packet at fixed `r`.

Equation (18) is an exact finite identity. It uses only inclusion-exclusion/Möbius inversion on set partitions; no prime-number theorem, asymptotic independence, or probabilistic approximation is involved.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract higher-order mechanism is classical signal/harmonic analysis. Finite-group bispectra, higher-order spectra, and phase-sensitive translation invariants were developed precisely to recover information lost by power spectra and quadratic Gram data. The literature cited above therefore predicts rather than merely resembles the transition from PC-302's reflection-even quadratic covariance to the phase-sensitive cubic invariant (11).

The arithmetic endpoint is also classical. PC-302 already identifies `S_q(a)` as the ordinary additive prime exponential-sum packet and audits the Vinogradov/Vaughan/circle-method boundary, including recent work of Maynard, Pandey and Radziwill on exponential sums over primes. Equations (14) and (18) do not add a new arithmetic object; they form finite products and inclusion-exclusion combinations of those same sums.

The project-local exact delta is the placement of this classicalization boundary inside the Prime-Circle frame construction: **before any Gram compression, every fixed finite oriented-frame tensor moment under an arbitrary source coupling is already a joint additive Fourier coefficient; for distinct prime sources, every fixed-order joint coefficient is a partition polynomial in the one-source prime exponential sums.**

The derivation is self-contained and the external references are neighboring prior art rather than load-bearing dependencies, so no new `SOURCES.md` anchor is required.

## 7. Falsification controls

Equation (6) can be tested at finite `q` by choosing an arbitrary joint probability table `pi`, enumerating any tensor monomial (2), and comparing its exact average with the corresponding joint DFT coefficient. Any mismatch refutes the central classification.

Equation (10) can be tested by translating an arbitrary nonuniform `mu`: weight-zero monomials must remain fixed and every nonzero-weight monomial must acquire exactly the predicted root-of-unity phase.

Equation (12) gives a matched reflection control. A generic law with nonreal bispectrum must have the same quadratic power data after reflection but opposite bispectral imaginary part. The native `q=13` prime example in (15) supplies one explicit check.

Equation (18) can be tested independently for small prime sets by enumerating injective tuples and comparing with the partition formula. For `r=2`, (19) is immediate by subtracting the diagonal `ell_1=ell_2`; higher `r` is the standard partition-lattice inclusion-exclusion.

## 8. Consequence for the research frontier

PC-302's higher-order escape is real but narrower than it first appears. Moving from quadratic to cubic or any other **fixed finite tensor degree** can restore orientation and source phase, yet if the construction uses only the labelled oriented frame entries and their finite contractions, all recovered information is already joint additive Fourier/polyspectral data. For the distinct-prime source it is generated by the same `S_q(a)` packet.

Therefore a new Prime-Circle mechanism cannot consist merely of “take a higher tensor/bispectrum of the oriented frames.” A surviving route must introduce something not captured by the fixed finite Fourier tensor algebra: a geometry-forced cross-level operation involving genuinely different moduli, a tensor degree/window/mode range growing in a load-bearing joint limit, an order-sensitive operation whose normalization is not a finite polyspectral readout, or a non-Fourier geometric carrier imposed before the root phases are converted into characters.

This finding does not say that prime exponential sums or their higher correlations are analytically shallow; they can encode deep prime and zero information. It says only that recovering them from the Prime-Circle frames is a representation of already-classical source Fourier data, not by itself a new bridge to RH.
