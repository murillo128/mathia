# PC-077 — the Hardy/Hilbert second relative trace is radical-divisor harmonic data

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` + `PRIOR-ART-REDIRECTION`. The exact Prime-Circle specialization and radical-divisor reduction below are derived here. The generalized Hilbert-matrix background, digamma/trigamma identities, and Ramanujan-sum product arithmetic are classical. No theorem-level novelty is claimed for those ingredients or for the final formula as an abstract number-theoretic identity.

PC-075 decomposes the canonical Hardy interior/exterior coupling of the cyclotomic logarithmic potential as

\[
W\Gamma_nW^*=-\frac1n C_n\otimes H+T_n,
\qquad T_n\in\mathcal S_1,
\]

and leaves the trace-class remainder `T_n` as the possible carrier of arithmetic beyond the universal Hilbert channels. PC-076 then shows that the first relative trace `Tr(T_n)` collapses to the two scalar cyclotomic endpoint values at `+1` and `-1`.

The next genuinely nonlinear spectral statistic can also be evaluated exactly. Since `T_n` is self-adjoint and trace class,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)=\|T_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2.
\]

It does retain more than the two endpoint values, but not a new spectral scale. If

\[
\rho=\operatorname{rad}(n),
\qquad
A(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}p(p-2),
\]

and

\[
g(q)=-q\log q+2qH_{q-1}-2(q-1),
\qquad g(1)=0,
\]

then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)
=
\frac{n}{\rho^2}
\sum_{d\mid\rho}A(d)\,g(\rho/d).
}
\]

Thus the second relative moment of the infinite-dimensional nonlocal remainder is a finite function of the squarefree radical, multiplied by the elementary scale factor `n`. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)}{n}
\text{ depends only on }\operatorname{rad}(n).
}
\]

Repeated prime powers therefore introduce no new normalized second-moment structure. The special-function content generated by the generalized Hilbert blocks cancels down to ordinary harmonic numbers and logarithms before the arithmetic sum is completed.

## 1. Hilbert–Schmidt square of one generalized-Hilbert difference

PC-075 uses

\[
H_\alpha=\left(\frac1{a+b+\alpha}\right)_{a,b\ge0},
\qquad H=H_1,
\]

and the `(r,s)` block of the remainder is

\[
(T_n)_{rs}
=-\frac{c_n(r+s+1)}n
\left(H_{(r+s+1)/n}-H_1\right).
\]

Set

\[
D_\alpha:=H_\alpha-H_1.
\]

Grouping its matrix entries by `m=a+b`, for `alpha>0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(\alpha)
&:=\|D_\alpha\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2\\
&=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)
\left(\frac1{m+\alpha}-\frac1{m+1}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The summand has the exact partial-fraction form

\[
\frac{(\alpha-1)^2}{(m+\alpha)^2(m+1)}
=
\frac1{m+1}-\frac1{m+\alpha}
-\frac{\alpha-1}{(m+\alpha)^2}.
\]

Using the standard series for the digamma `psi` and trigamma `psi_1` functions gives

\[
\boxed{
S(\alpha)
=\psi(\alpha)+\gamma-(\alpha-1)\psi_1(\alpha).
}
\]

In particular `S(1)=0`, as it must.

## 2. The full second trace is initially a finite Ramanujan-weighted special-function sum

Put

\[
t=r+s+1,
\qquad 1\le t\le2n-1.
\]

The number of residue pairs `(r,s)` producing `t` is

\[
w_t=\min(t,2n-t).
\]

Since the Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the sum of the block Hilbert–Schmidt norms,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)
=
\frac1{n^2}
\sum_{t=1}^{2n-1}
 w_t c_n(t)^2 S(t/n).
}
\]

The `t=n` term vanishes because `S(1)=0`. Pairing `t` with `2n-t`, and then symmetrizing `t` with `n-t`, removes the trigamma terms completely. Define, for `0<x<1`,

\[
R(x)=
\psi(x)+\psi(1-x)+2\gamma
+2\left(\frac{x}{1-x}+\frac{1-x}{x}\right).
\]

The digamma/trigamma recurrences

\[
\psi(z+1)=\psi(z)+\frac1z,
\qquad
\psi_1(z+1)=\psi_1(z)-\frac1{z^2}
\]

imply directly

\[
x\bigl(S(x)+S(2-x)\bigr)
+(1-x)\bigl(S(1-x)+S(1+x)\bigr)
=R(x).
\]

Because `c_n(n-t)=c_n(t)`, the second trace becomes

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)
=
\frac1{2n}
\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}c_n(t)^2R(t/n).
}
\]

This is already an exact finite reduction of the nonlinear operator statistic, but it still appears to contain nontrivial special-function geometry.

## 3. A complete rational grid has an elementary closed sum

For an integer `q>=2`, Gauss multiplication for the digamma function gives

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}\psi(k/q)
=-(q-1)\gamma-q\log q.
\]

The rational part of `R` gives

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}
\left(
\frac{k}{q-k}+\frac{q-k}{k}
\right)
=2qH_{q-1}-2(q-1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}R(k/q)
=-2q\log q+4qH_{q-1}-4(q-1)
=2g(q),
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
g(q)=-q\log q+2qH_{q-1}-2(q-1).}
\]

So once the Ramanujan-square weight is decomposed into divisibility indicators, every surviving rational grid can be summed exactly with no remaining polygamma functions.

## 4. Squared Ramanujan sums depend only on radical divisibility after one scale factor

Write

\[
n=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{a_p},
\qquad
\rho=\operatorname{rad}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p,
\qquad
m=\frac n\rho.
\]

For a prime power `p^a`, the classical prime-power formula for Ramanujan sums implies

\[
\boxed{
c_{p^a}(t)^2
=p^{2a-2}\mathbf1_{p^{a-1}\mid t}
\left(1+p(p-2)\mathbf1_{p^a\mid t}\right).}
\]

Multiplicativity over the distinct primes therefore gives the exact finite divisor expansion

\[
\boxed{
c_n(t)^2
=m^2\sum_{d\mid\rho}
A(d)\,\mathbf1_{md\mid t},
\qquad
A(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}p(p-2).}
\]

The factor for `p=2` vanishes when `2|d`, which is an intrinsic simplification rather than a separate parity convention.

Substitute this expansion into the finite formula of the previous section. For fixed `d|rho`, write

\[
t=mdk,
\qquad
1\le k\le\rho/d-1.
\]

Then

\[
\frac tn=\frac{k}{\rho/d},
\]

so the inner sum is exactly the complete grid sum `2g(rho/d)`. Since `m^2/n=n/rho^2`, this proves

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)
=
\frac{n}{\rho^2}
\sum_{d\mid\rho}
\left(\prod_{p\mid d}p(p-2)\right)
 g(\rho/d).
}
\]

No limit, asymptotic replacement, or numerical fitting is used.

## 5. Prime powers and same-radical families

If `n=p^a`, then `rho=p`. The divisor `d=p` contributes `g(1)=0`, so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_{p^a}^2)
=p^{a-2}g(p).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_{p^a}^2)
=p^{a-1}
\left(
2H_{p-1}-2+\frac2p-\log p
\right).
}
\]

At prime level,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(T_p^2)
=2H_{p-1}-2+\frac2p-\log p.
}
\]

More generally, if two levels have the same radical, then their normalized second relative traces agree exactly:

\[
\operatorname{rad}(n_1)=\operatorname{rad}(n_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(T_{n_1}^2)}{n_1}
=
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(T_{n_2}^2)}{n_2}.}
\]

Thus the second relative moment does detect squarefree divisor structure, but repeated prime powers are only a linear inflation after the radical has been fixed.

## 7. What this rules out, and what it does not

PC-076 left open the possibility that higher relative spectral data might recover information lost by the first trace. The second moment is the first serious test because it is nonlinear in the full trace-class remainder and depends on all matrix entries, not merely the diagonal. Nevertheless,

\[
\text{Hardy/Hilbert remainder}
\to
\operatorname{Tr}(T_n^2)
\to
\text{Ramanujan-square grids}
\to
\text{radical/divisor harmonic data}.
\]

There is no intrinsic complex spectral parameter `s`, no gamma-factor completion, no `s <-> 1-s` symmetry, and no critical-line selector in this statistic. Any subsequent Dirichlet or Mellin aggregation would be a transform of the explicit radical/divisor sequence above and requires its own analysis; it must not be credited as a new RH mechanism merely because zeta functions later appear.

This finding does **not** determine the full spectrum of `T_n`, nor does it prove that all higher moments collapse in the same way. It leaves open:

- `Tr(T_n^k)` for `k>=3`, where several independent residue indices interact;
- the Fredholm/perturbation determinant of `T_n`;
- isolated eigenvalues or threshold phenomena of `Gamma_n` relative to the Hilbert core;
- cross-level Hardy operators coupling distinct shells before residue decomposition;
- shell-dependent/refinement-forced nonlinear operators;
- the old/new cotangent coupling of PC-047 and the global uniformization/monodromy branch rooted in PC-017.

There is also a normalization boundary: `T_n` is the canonical remainder relative to the specific universal `H_1` core fixed in PC-075. `Tr(T_n^2)` is therefore the second moment of that canonical decomposition, not an invariant under an arbitrary re-choice of trace-class reference core.

1. The PC-075 block formula for `T_n` must be used with exactly its stated normalization.

4. The prime-power Ramanujan-square indicator formula must hold, and its multiplicative expansion must give the divisor coefficient `A(d)=prod_{p|d}p(p-2)`.
5. The complete rational-grid sum of `R` must equal `2g(q)`.
