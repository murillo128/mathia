# PC-168 — full refinement covariance rigidifies local differential operators to universal cone form

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `CLASSICAL-STRUCTURE` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for obtaining a new Prime-Circle/RH mechanism from a finite-order local differential or Schrödinger operator whose coefficients are intrinsically compatible with the complete power-refinement semigroup. PC-167 classified the narrower class coming from local Riemannian homothety metrics and explicitly left geometry-forced singular potentials and more general local operators outside its theorem. At the coefficient level the full refinement system is already rigid enough to close that local escape: exact covariance under every power map forces every coefficient to be angle-independent and homogeneous in `t=|log|z||`. For a second-order operator the leading coefficients are constant, first-order coefficients are proportional to `t^{-1}`, and the only scalar potential is `c/t^2`. None of these coefficients can carry primitive-shell arithmetic.

The result is deliberately about **local finite-order coefficients**, not domains or nonlocal operators. Self-adjoint extension data at the singular circle can introduce additional scales for an inverse-square potential, but those are extra domain/boundary data and are not generated by the refinement-covariant local coefficients proved here.

## 1. Refinement covariance directly at the operator level

Work on either open side of the root circle in the same coordinates as PC-167,

\[
t=|\log|z||>0,
\qquad
\theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z,
\]

so every intrinsic power refinement is

\[
D_n(t,\theta)=(nt,n\theta),
\qquad n\ge2.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `U_n` denote pullback on functions,

\[
(U_nf)(t,\theta)=f(nt,n\theta).
\tag{2}
\]

Consider a scalar finite-order local differential operator

\[
L=
\sum_{a+b\le m}
A_{ab}(t,\theta)\,
\partial_t^a\partial_\theta^b,
\tag{3}
\]

with continuous `2pi`-periodic coefficients. The same argument applies entrywise to a fixed finite matrix bundle, so scalar notation loses no rigidity relevant here.

Assume exact homogeneous refinement covariance with some real weight `sigma`,

\[
\boxed{
L U_n=n^\sigma U_n L
}
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge2.
\tag{4}
\]

This is the direct operator analogue of the full-semigroup compatibility tested for metrics in PC-167. It does not assume rotational invariance of the coefficients.

Because `D_n` is locally linear,

\[
\partial_t^a\partial_\theta^b(U_nf)
=
n^{a+b}
U_n(\partial_t^a\partial_\theta^b f).
\tag{5}
\]

At any point, arbitrary local jets of `f` may be prescribed at its image under `D_n`, so comparison of coefficients in (4) gives, with `r=a+b`,

\[
\boxed{
A_{ab}(nt,n\theta)
=
n^{r-\sigma}A_{ab}(t,\theta).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the classification of the whole operator reduces to one exact scalar functional equation for each coefficient.

## 2. A full-refinement invariant continuous coefficient is constant

Normalize each nonzero coefficient by its forced radial weight,

\[
F_{ab}(t,\theta)
=
t^{\sigma-r}A_{ab}(t,\theta).
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (6) becomes

\[
\boxed{
F_{ab}(nt,n\theta)=F_{ab}(t,\theta)
}
\qquad(n\ge2).
\tag{8}
\]

There is a short Fourier proof that no angular information survives. For fixed `t`, write

\[
F(t,\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat F_k(t)e^{ik\theta}
\tag{9}
\]

in the usual `L^2` sense; continuity is enough for uniqueness of Fourier coefficients. The left side of (8), as a function of `theta`, contains only modes divisible by `n`. Hence

\[
\widehat F_k(t)=0
\qquad\text{whenever }n\nmid k.
\tag{10}
\]

For every nonzero integer `k`, choose `n>|k|`. Then `n` does not divide `k`, so every nonzero angular mode vanishes. Therefore `F(t,theta)=F_0(t)` is angle-independent.

Equation (8) now says

\[
F_0(nt)=F_0(t)
\qquad(n\ge2).
\tag{11}
\]

Applying the same equation at `t/n` gives invariance under division by `n`; finite compositions give

\[
F_0(qt)=F_0(t)
\qquad(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}).
\tag{12}
\]

Positive rational scalings are dense in `R_{>0}`, so continuity forces `F_0` to be constant. Consequently every coefficient in (3) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
A_{ab}(t,\theta)
=
C_{ab}\,t^{a+b-\sigma}
}
\tag{13}
\]

for one constant `C_ab`.

This is the complete coefficient classification under (4). No angular modulation, preferred prime, log-periodic factor, primitive residue class, or shell label survives. A single preferred scale could retain a radial log-periodic modulation; simultaneous compatibility with all integer refinements removes it by the same dense-rational mechanism seen in PC-167.

## 3. Second order forces the inverse-square singularity

The natural scaling weight for a second-order spectral operator is `sigma=2`. Equation (13) then gives the most general local operator in this class:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
L={}&
C_{20}\,\partial_t^2
+C_{11}\,\partial_t\partial_\theta
+C_{02}\,\partial_\theta^2\\
&+t^{-1}
\left(
C_{10}\,\partial_t+C_{01}\,\partial_\theta
\right)
+C_{00}\,t^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus a Schrödinger-type attempt with a fixed scale-covariant kinetic term has only one possible scalar local singularity:

\[
\boxed{V(t)=\frac{c}{t^2}.}
\tag{15}
\]

The common root circle is `t=0`, so (15) is precisely the inverse-square singularity in logarithmic radial distance. Making the potential singular therefore does not create a new family capable of encoding `phi(n)`, `Lambda(n)`, Ramanujan modes, old/new incidence, or cyclotomic resultants. It produces the universal scale-invariant potential already singled out by ordinary dimensional homogeneity.

PC-167 is recovered as an internal consistency check. Its metric family has

\[
\Delta_g
=
t^{-\kappa}
\left(
\alpha\partial_t^2
+2\beta\partial_t\partial_\theta
+\gamma\partial_\theta^2
\right).
\tag{16}
\]

This satisfies (4) with `sigma=kappa+2`; equation (13) then predicts exponent `2-(kappa+2)=-kappa` for each second-order coefficient, exactly as in (16). The present result is broader because no metric origin, ellipticity, self-adjointness, conformality, or rotational hypothesis was assumed.

More generally, if `sigma=m` for an operator of order `m`, its top-order coefficients are constant and every term of derivative order `r` has the universal radial factor `t^{r-m}`. This is the familiar homogeneous/cone pattern rather than an arithmetic refinement law.

## 4. The matched control removes the roots entirely

The strongest control is the same one that made PC-167 decisive. Erase every primitive/new-vertex label and retain only the punctured plane together with the covering semigroup `z -> z^n`. Equations (1)--(15) are unchanged. The coefficient classification therefore cannot know whether a level is prime, composite, squarefree, or a prime power.

In particular, choosing the inverse-square constant `c`, the leading symbol `C_20,C_11,C_02`, or one of the lower-order constants after seeing arithmetic data would merely insert that information by hand. Exact local refinement covariance does not derive such dependence. A level-dependent family `L_n`, a shell-dependent potential, or an operator whose coefficients are built from actual old/new incidence is outside assumption (4) and must be audited separately.

This also distinguishes the result from Bost--Connes/cyclotomic dynamics. There the semigroup action is supplemented by arithmetic algebra, representation, and state data. Here the naked power maps alone rigidify the local coefficients, and the matched control proves that they generate no cyclotomic arithmetic by themselves.

## 5. Domain anomalies do not repair the coefficient no-go automatically

The inverse-square endpoint is not spectrally trivial in general. Singular inverse-square Hamiltonians have classical self-adjoint-extension and renormalization phenomena; in supercritical regimes a boundary condition can break continuous scale invariance and generate a dimensional scale or limit-cycle behavior. Bawin and Coon, *Singular inverse square potential, limit cycles, and self-adjoint extensions*, Phys. Rev. A 67, 042712 (2003), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.67.042712`, is a direct prior-art anchor.

That fact does **not** restore arithmetic to (15). The extension parameter or boundary scale is additional domain data at `t=0`; it is not determined by the constants in (14). This distinction is especially important for RH-oriented spectral wrappers because the inverse-square system now has an explicit literature connection to the Berry--Keating dilation Hamiltonian: S. Sundaram, C. P. Burgess and D. H. J. O'Dell, *Duality between the quantum inverted harmonic oscillator and inverse square potentials*, New J. Phys. 26 (2024) 053023, DOI `10.1088/1367-2630/ad3a91`, maps the supercritical inverse-square problem to the Berry--Keating `xp` system and emphasizes the surviving boundary-condition ambiguity.

Accordingly, encountering `c/t^2`, dilation eigenfunctions, or a Berry--Keating-like generator after imposing refinement covariance is not a new Prime-Circle bridge to zeta. It is classical scale-invariant operator structure. To become line-relevant, a domain condition would itself have to be derived from the actual roots-of-unity incidence, remain compatible with the required refinement maps, survive matched controls, and pass an independent novelty audit.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The general analytic shape is classical. Homogeneous differential operators under dilations and Fuchs/cone operators near singular boundaries are established subjects; Matthias Lesch, *Differential operators of Fuchs type, conical singularities, and asymptotic methods*, arXiv:`dg-ga/9607005`, is a direct structural anchor for local differential operators with homogeneous radial singularities and their spectral/index analysis. No novelty is claimed for homogeneous coefficient laws, inverse-square potentials, cone operators, self-adjoint extensions, scale anomalies, or Berry--Keating dilation mechanics.

The line-specific derivation is the simultaneous use of **all** Prime-Circle covering refinements. It upgrades a generic scale-covariance ansatz into the exact coefficient law (13) without assuming rotational symmetry: the covering map kills every nonzero angular Fourier mode, and dense rational radial refinements kill the residual log-periodic freedom. Targeted searches across scale-invariant differential operators, cone/Fuchs operators, inverse-square quantum mechanics, self-adjoint extensions, and Berry--Keating-related formulations found the classical structures above, not a Prime-Circle/RH mechanism supplied by these local coefficients. Absence of a wording match is not treated as evidence of historical novelty.

The durable contribution is therefore a **negative classification**, not a positive novelty claim: the explicit local-operator escape left outside PC-167 collapses to universal homogeneous cone coefficients before any arithmetic data can enter.

## 7. Scope, falsifiers, and surviving frontier

The assumptions carrying the result are explicit:

1. `L` is a finite-order local differential operator on one open side of the root circle, with continuous periodic coefficients;
2. the same operator is required to transform covariantly under every intrinsic power refinement by (4), with one fixed homogeneity weight `sigma`;
3. arithmetic information is not inserted separately into a level-indexed family of coefficients or domains.

The theorem does **not** classify nonlocal pseudodifferential or boundary operators, shell-dependent or cross-level operator families, domains/self-adjoint extensions derived from old/new root incidence, distributional coefficients supported exactly on the root circle, or nonlinear operators. It also does not touch the global uniformization/monodromy branch retained elsewhere in the line.

What it rules out is the natural local singular continuation

\[
\text{full power refinement}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite-order local covariant operator}
\longrightarrow
\text{singular prime-sensitive spectrum}
\longrightarrow
\text{RH}.
\]

The middle object is already forced to (13), and in second order to (14)--(15). Any surviving mechanism must obtain its arithmetic content from structure that this local coefficient compression does not contain: genuine primitive/old incidence, level dependence, nonlocality, a geometry-forced domain, cross-level coupling, or global monodromy.
