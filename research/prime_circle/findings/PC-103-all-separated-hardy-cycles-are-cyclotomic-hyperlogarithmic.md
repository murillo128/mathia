# PC-103 — every finite cyclically separated Hardy cycle is cyclotomic-hyperlogarithmic

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-BOUNDARY`. Every finite cyclically separated Hardy root-channel period from PC-082 is linearly reducible in an explicit parity-adapted integration order. After polynomial reduction, every singular letter is cyclotomic; for odd cycle length the only possible degree-two obstruction is a terminal binomial whose roots are still roots of unity. Consequently every fixed finite separated Hardy trace lies in a finite cyclotomic hyperlogarithm / multiple-polylogarithm algebra. This closes the period-class boundary left open beyond the cubic and quartic calculations of PC-100 and PC-102.

The result does **not** cover repeated-shell words with reciprocal adjacent root channels, infinite-shell generating constructions, Fredholm/global Hardy determinants, or the global uniformization/monodromy branch. It also does not claim a sharp pure weight in every length: the general bound proved here is hyperlogarithmic weight at most the cycle length, while PC-100 and PC-102 retain their sharper low-length statements.

## 1. Starting point: the exact separated cycle cube

For roots of unity `alpha_1,...,alpha_k` with cyclic indices and

\[
\alpha_i\alpha_{i+1}\neq1
\qquad (i\bmod k),
\]

PC-082 gives the ordinary trace

\[
\mathcal P_k(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)
=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal H_{\alpha_1}\cdots\mathcal H_{\alpha_k})
\]

as the regular cube integral

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_k
=\left(\prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_i\right)
\int_{[0,1]^k}
\prod_{i=1}^k
\frac{dx_1\cdots dx_k}
{1-q_i x_i x_{i+1}},
\qquad
q_i:=\alpha_i\alpha_{i+1}.
}
\]

Every `q_i` is a nontrivial root of unity. Hence no denominator vanishes on the real cube and this is an ordinary convergent Euler integral; the conditional cone presentations of PC-082/PC-101 are not needed for the reduction below.

Let `N` be a common order of the `alpha_i`. Then all `q_i` lie in `mu_N`. The question left by PC-100/PC-102 is whether increasing `k` eventually forces a non-polylogarithmic period class. For the cyclically separated finite family, it does not.

## 2. Polynomial-reduction class

Use the standard hyperlogarithmic polynomial-reduction test. When integrating a variable `x`, one tracks irreducible factors of leading coefficients, values at `x=0,1`, and pairwise resultants of the singular polynomials that are linear in `x`.

Let `M` be the multiplicative group generated by the edge phases `q_i`. Consider, on the variables that remain, factors of the following three forms:

\[
\boxed{
 x,
\qquad
 A-Bx,
\qquad
 Ax-By,
\qquad A,B\in M.
}
\]

This class is closed under one-variable reduction. Indeed, for two link factors

\[
Ax-By,
\qquad
Cx-Dz,
\]

their resultant in `x` is again a link,

\[
\boxed{ADz-CBy.}
\]

A link against an affine factor gives an affine factor in the remaining variable, two affine factors give only a constant, and the `x=0,1` specializations give coordinates or affine factors. Thus once the cycle has been reduced to this affine/link class, every subsequent variable is hyperlogarithmically linear and every nonzero one-variable zero is a ratio of monomials in the `q_i`, hence a root of unity.

## 3. Even cycles are linearly reducible without algebraic letters

Let `k=2m`. Integrate first the independent set

\[
x_1,x_3,\ldots,x_{2m-1}.
\]

Each eliminated odd vertex meets exactly two edge factors. Eliminating `x_{2j-1}` replaces its two incident factors, as far as polynomial reduction is concerned, by endpoint/coordinate factors together with the link between its two even neighbors,

\[
\boxed{
q_{2j-2}x_{2j-2}-q_{2j-1}x_{2j},
}
\]

with cyclic interpretation at `j=1`. Because every edge of an even cycle meets one of these odd vertices, after this first phase there is no bilinear edge factor left. The entire singular set on

\[
x_2,x_4,\ldots,x_{2m}
\]

belongs to the affine/link class of Section 2.

Now integrate the even variables successively. Closure of that class proves linear reducibility all the way to the end. Every final letter is a ratio of monomials in the `q_i`, so it lies in `mu_N`.

Thus **all even separated Hardy cycles are hyperlogarithmic**. PC-102's explicit quartic reduction is the first nontrivial instance and gives a sharper weight-three description at `k=4`; no analogous sharp weight claim is needed here.

## 4. Odd cycles have only a terminal cyclotomic quadratic

Let `k=2m+1`. Keep the last vertex `x_k` to the end and first integrate

\[
x_1,x_3,\ldots,x_{2m-1}.
\]

All edges are eliminated into affine/link factors except the single edge

\[
\boxed{1-q_{k-1}x_{k-1}x_k.}
\]

Then integrate

\[
x_2,x_4,\ldots,x_{k-3}
\]

while holding `x_{k-1}` and `x_k`. The affine/link class remains closed, and the bilinear factor above is independent of every variable in this intermediate phase.

At the `x_{k-1}` step all other singular factors involving that variable are affine or links to `x_k`. A resultant with an affine factor remains affine in `x_k`. The only possible increase in degree comes from a link

\[
Ax_{k-1}-Bx_k
\]

against the remaining bilinear edge; their resultant is

\[
\boxed{
q_{k-1}B x_k^2-A.
}
\]

Here `A/B` is a monomial ratio in the `q_i`. Therefore every such terminal quadratic is a binomial whose two roots are in `mu_{2N}`. After factorization over the cyclotomic field, the last variable is again hyperlogarithmically linear.

The chain-closing link makes the cancellation especially transparent. Propagating the links around the odd cycle produces

\[
\frac{q_k q_2q_4\cdots q_{k-1}}
{q_1q_3\cdots q_{k-2}}
=\alpha_k^2,
\]

because `q_i=alpha_i alpha_{i+1}`. Hence its terminal factor is proportional to

\[
\boxed{
1-\alpha_k^2x_k^2
=(1-\alpha_kx_k)(1+\alpha_kx_k).
}
\]

So the apparent square-root obstruction at odd length is not elliptic or non-cyclotomic: it is exactly the pair of cyclotomic letters `+alpha_k^{-1}` and `-alpha_k^{-1}`.

## 5. Consequence: a uniform cyclotomic-MPL bound for every finite length

Panzer's hyperlogarithmic integration framework applies once the successive singular polynomials split linearly. Starting from a rational integrand, each of the `k` integrations raises iterated-integral weight by at most one. Endpoint regularization of intermediate expressions, when a spurious letter collides with `0` or `1`, stays inside the same hyperlogarithm algebra; the original separated cube integral supplies the canonical finite specialization.

Define `MPV_<=k(2N)` to be the span of regularized hyperlogarithm values

\[
G(a_1,\ldots,a_r;1),
\qquad
r\le k,
\qquad
a_j\in\{0\}\cup\mu_{2N},
\]

and their standard shuffle products. The reduction above gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_k(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)
\in
\mathbb Q(\mu_{2N})\cdot \operatorname{MPV}_{\le k}(2N).
}
\]

For even `k` the polynomial alphabet already lies in `mu_N`; the harmless level doubling is only needed for a uniform statement covering the odd terminal square roots.

If `n_1,...,n_k>1` are cyclically adjacent distinct primitive-shell orders, rootwise separation is automatic. Since

\[
\Gamma_n=-\sum_{\alpha\in P_n^*}\mathcal H_\alpha,
\]

the completed shell trace is a finite sum of these root-channel periods. With

\[
N=\operatorname{lcm}(n_1,\ldots,n_k),
\]

we therefore have

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(\Gamma_{n_1}\cdots\Gamma_{n_k})
\in
\mathbb Q(\mu_{2N})\cdot \operatorname{MPV}_{\le k}(2N)
}
\]

for every finite cyclically separated shell word.

## 6. Controls and falsification surface

The parity mechanism gives direct low-length controls.

1. At `k=2` there is no quadratic and the reduction terminates at `-Log(1-alpha_1 alpha_2)`, recovering PC-080.
2. At `k=3` the terminal polynomial is `-q_1+q_2q_3 x_3^2`; because `q_2q_3/q_1=alpha_3^2`, it factors into the two cyclotomic letters predicted above. PC-100 gives the sharper weight-three horn decomposition of this case.
3. At `k=4` the odd-vertex elimination removes every bilinear edge and all remaining resultants are affine/link factors, agreeing with the explicit hyperlogarithmic reduction of PC-102.
4. At `k=5` the only terminal degree-two factor in the generic full reduction is

\[
-q_1q_3+q_2q_4q_5x_5^2
=q_1q_3(\alpha_5^2x_5^2-1).
\]

5. At `k=7` the corresponding factor is

\[
-q_1q_3q_5+q_2q_4q_6q_7x_7^2
=q_1q_3q_5(\alpha_7^2x_7^2-1).
\]

Symbolic polynomial-reduction checks through lengths `3` to `10` reproduce the stated parity pattern. Those checks are controls only; the evidence is the closure argument of Sections 2--4.

The theorem is falsified if a reduction step in the stated order produces, before the final odd variable, an irreducible factor outside the coordinate/affine/link class, or if the odd terminal resultant has a non-binomial algebraic factor. The displayed resultant identities show why neither occurs.

Special coincidences among the `q_i` can make generic partial-fraction coefficients vanish or merge letters. They do not invalidate the result: prove the identity for generic cyclotomic parameters in the same reduction class and take the finite specialization supplied by the original regular cube integral. By contrast, dropping cyclic separation (`q_i=1`) changes the analytic problem and is explicitly outside the theorem.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The function class is classical.

- Erik Panzer, **Algorithms for the symbolic integration of hyperlogarithms with applications to Feynman integrals**, *Computer Physics Communications* 188 (2015), 148--166, DOI `10.1016/j.cpc.2014.10.019`, supplies the standard symbolic-integration and polynomial-reduction framework used here. Linear reducibility is precisely the mechanism that keeps an Euler integral inside hyperlogarithms rather than forcing a new algebraic/elliptic function class.
- A. B. Goncharov, **Multiple polylogarithms, cyclotomy and modular complexes**, *Mathematical Research Letters* 5 (1998), 497--516, DOI `10.4310/MRL.1998.v5.n4.a7`, is the standard cyclotomic multiple-polylogarithm setting for iterated integrals whose letters are roots of unity.
- Terasoma's rational-cone theorem and the Guo--Paycha--Zhang conical-zeta literature already recorded for PC-082 remain the neighboring arithmetic framework. The point here is that the ordinary cube integral gives a direct hyperlogarithmic route for the separated finite Hardy family, so the critical conditional lattice presentation no longer leaves an unexplained period-class loophole.

Directed searches around linearly reducible cycle/Euler integrals, cyclotomic conical sums, and cyclic Mordell--Tornheim reductions found extensive general hyperlogarithmic and multiple-`L` technology, but no need to claim a new historical function class. The durable Prime-Circle result is the exact all-length reduction of its PC-082 cycle family and the closure of the previously explicit `k>=5` novelty boundary.

## 8. RH consequence and remaining boundary

PC-082 established that higher Hardy traces contain relational information not determined by pairwise cyclotomic resultants. PC-100 and PC-102 showed that the first odd and even cases nevertheless land in classical cyclotomic multiple-polylogarithm spaces. The present result makes that conclusion uniform:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every fixed finite cyclically separated Hardy cycle}
\longrightarrow
\text{linearly reducible cyclotomic Euler integral}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite cyclotomic hyperlogarithm period}.
}
\]

Therefore increasing the **finite separated cycle length alone** cannot escape to an elliptic or otherwise new period class, and it does not by itself produce the missing RH ingredients: there is still no geometry-forced free complex parameter, gamma factor, `s\leftrightarrow1-s` symmetry, positivity criterion, or Riemann-zero divisor.

The surviving Hardy boundary is now materially narrower. It consists of words that fail rootwise cyclic separation (especially repeated-shell channels where reciprocal roots occur), or constructions that organize infinitely many shells/traces before taking the relevant analytic limit, such as a genuinely intrinsic Fredholm/global Hardy determinant. Those cases are not consequences of this finite hyperlogarithmic theorem and must be analyzed separately.
