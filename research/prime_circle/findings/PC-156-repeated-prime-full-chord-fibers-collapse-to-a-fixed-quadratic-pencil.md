# PC-156 — repeated-prime full-chord fibers collapse to a fixed quadratic metric–cotangent pencil

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `PRIOR-ART-CLASSICALIZATION` + `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for extracting a new RH-sensitive spectrum from the **full fine primitive fiber** of the inverse-square chord Laplacian when the refinement adds no new prime support. PC-155 classifies the fiber-constant compression for genuinely new squarefree primes but explicitly leaves repeated-prime refinement and fine zero-mean fiber modes open. PC-051 previously showed that repeated-prime fibers of the oriented cotangent operator are only affine copies of base data.

For the positive inverse-square operator the fine fiber is slightly richer, but still closes exactly. If `m` has no prime divisor outside the coarse level `d`, then `U(dm) -> U(d)` is a full cyclic `m`-sheeted fiber. Fiber Fourier transform diagonalizes the **entire** fine primitive-shell Laplacian. After the natural `(dm)^2` normalization, its `k`-th sector is unitarily equivalent to one value of the fixed base-level Hermitian pencil

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_d(t)
=\frac1{d^2}
\left(
L_d^{\rm int}+\frac t2\,(H_d+J_d)-\frac{t^2}{2}I
\right),
\qquad t=\frac{k}{m}.
}
\]

Thus repeated-prime depth introduces no new operator coefficients: it only samples one finite-dimensional algebraic spectral curve on progressively finer rational grids. The metric/cotangent noncommutativity that survives primitive compression is inherited from the base level rather than generated by the extra depth. Uniformly in the depth and fiber sector, every normalized sector is moreover `O(1/d)` from the coarse normalized inverse-square operator. This closes the repeated-prime full-fiber loophole of PC-155 in the canonical inverse-square geometry.

## 1. No-new-prime-support refinement is a full cyclic fiber

Write

\[
U(N)=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

and let

\[
\kappa_N(r)
:=\frac1{4\sin^2(\pi r/N)}.
\]

On the primitive shell use the induced inverse-square chord Laplacian

\[
(L_N^{\rm int}f)(a)
=\sum_{\substack{b\in U(N)\\b\ne a}}
\kappa_N(a-b)(f(a)-f(b)).
\]

Also retain the intrinsic oriented cotangent block from PC-045,

\[
H_N(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
i\cot\!\left(\dfrac{\pi(a-b)}N\right),&a\ne b,\\[2mm]0,&a=b,
\end{cases}
\]

and define the augmented Hermitian matrix

\[
\boxed{C_N:=H_N+J_N,}
\]

where `J_N` is the all-ones matrix on `U(N)`. This is the same augmentation that closes the repeated-prime cotangent recursion in PC-051.

Now let `m>=1` satisfy

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rad}(m)\mid\operatorname{rad}(d).}
\tag{1}
\]

Every prime dividing `m` already divides `d`. Hence for every `a in U(d)` all residues

\[
a+td,\qquad t\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,
\]

remain coprime to `dm`, and they are exactly the lifts of `a`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
U(dm)\cong U(d)\times\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z
}
\tag{2}
\]

as a fibered set over `U(d)`. Translation by `d` in the exponent is an exact order-`m` rotation of the fine root configuration and preserves `U(dm)`. Since the chord kernel depends only on exponent differences, `L_{dm}^{int}` commutes with this cyclic fiber translation.

Consequently the full fine primitive space, not merely the fiber-constant part, splits exactly into the `m` fiber Fourier sectors. There is no coarse/detail mixing in this no-new-prime-support refinement.

## 2. The weighted `csc^2` fiber transform closes on inverse-square plus cotangent data

Let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/m},
\qquad 0\le k<m,
\]

and fix two distinct coarse residues `a,b in U(d)`. Put `delta=a-b`,

\[
\alpha=e^{2\pi i\delta/(dm)},
\qquad
\beta=\alpha^m=e^{2\pi i\delta/d}.
\]

The `k`-th Fourier coefficient of the fine conductance between the two fibers is

\[
S_k(\delta)
:=
\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}
\omega^{-kt}\,
\kappa_{dm}(\delta+dt).
\tag{3}
\]

The elementary root-of-unity identity

\[
\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}
\frac{\omega^{-kt}}{1-\alpha\omega^t}
=\frac{m\alpha^k}{1-\alpha^m}
\qquad(0\le k<m)
\tag{4}
\]

and

\[
\kappa_{dm}(\delta+dt)
=-\frac{\alpha\omega^t}{(1-\alpha\omega^t)^2}
\]

show, after differentiating (4) in `alpha`, that

\[
\boxed{
S_k(\delta)
=m\alpha^k
\left(
 m\kappa_d(\delta)
-\frac{k}{1-\beta}
\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

This includes `k=0`, where (5) is the ordinary differentiated multiplication formula

\[
S_0(\delta)=m^2\kappa_d(\delta).
\tag{6}
\]

For `k>0` the apparent new term is still the canonical circle Cauchy/cotangent kernel, because

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{1-e^{2\pi i\delta/d}}
=\frac12
\left(
1+i\cot\frac{\pi\delta}{d}
\right).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus fiber Fourier transformation of the inverse-square kernel does not generate a third analytic kernel: it closes exactly on the base inverse-square kernel and the already-classified augmented cotangent kernel `C_d`.

## 3. Exact full-fiber block decomposition

Choose the standard representatives `0<=a<d` for `U(d)` and define the diagonal unitary

\[
D_{m,k}(a,a)
:=e^{2\pi i k a/(dm)}.
\tag{8}
\]

Changing the section of the reduction map only changes these matrices by the corresponding fiber gauge, so the spectral statements below are section-independent.

For `a!=b`, equation (5) gives the off-diagonal entry of the `k`-th Laplacian block. On one coarse fiber, the remaining fine edges form the full `m`-gon inverse-square Laplacian, whose classical Fourier eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda_k^{(m)}=\frac{k(m-k)}2.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining the off-diagonal and diagonal terms yields the exact operator identity

\[
\boxed{
L_{dm}^{\rm int}
\cong
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m-1}
D_{m,k}
\left[
 m^2L_d^{\rm int}
 +\frac{mk}{2}C_d
 -\frac{k^2}{2}I
\right]
D_{m,k}^{-1}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

The constant sector is especially rigid:

\[
\boxed{
L_{dm}^{(0)}=m^2L_d^{\rm int}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the naturally normalized fiber-constant compression is exactly the coarse normalized inverse-square operator, with no analogue of the new-prime correction in PC-155.

Put

\[
A_N:=N^{-2}L_N^{\rm int},
\qquad
t_k:=\frac{k}{m}.
\]

Dividing (10) by `(dm)^2` gives the central classification:

\[
\boxed{
A_{dm}
\cong
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m-1}
D_{m,k}\,\mathcal P_d(t_k)\,D_{m,k}^{-1},
}
\tag{12}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_d(t)
=\frac1{d^2}
\left(
L_d^{\rm int}
+\frac t2 C_d
-\frac{t^2}{2}I
\right),
\qquad 0\le t<1.
}
\tag{13}
\]

All dependence on the repeated-prime depth `m` is therefore reduced to the rational sampling set

\[
\left\{0,\frac1m,\ldots,\frac{m-1}{m}\right\}.
\]

No new matrix coefficient, character family, or refinement-order datum appears as the old prime powers deepen.

## 4. The joint metric–cotangent defect is a covariant base invariant

The same full cyclic fiber transform applied to the augmented cotangent operator gives the direct `m`-fold extension of PC-051:

\[
\boxed{
C_{dm}
\cong
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m-1}
D_{m,k}
\left(mC_d-2kI\right)
D_{m,k}^{-1}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Equations (10) and (14) contain an exact cancellation. Sector by sector,

\[
\boxed{
8L_{dm}^{(k)}+(C_{dm}^{(k)})^2
=
m^2D_{m,k}
\left(8L_d^{\rm int}+C_d^2\right)
D_{m,k}^{-1}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Likewise the commutator is simply inherited:

\[
\boxed{
[L_{dm}^{(k)},C_{dm}^{(k)}]
=
m^3D_{m,k}[L_d^{\rm int},C_d]D_{m,k}^{-1}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

This matters because primitive compression can leave genuinely noncommuting metric/chiral data; PC-047 shows that the corresponding old/new cotangent defect can even be full rank. Equations (15)--(16) do **not** declare that base-level joint data trivial. They say that adding only further powers of primes already present in the conductor creates no new joint defect: every sector carries a scaled/gauged copy of the same base invariant and the same base noncommutativity.

## 5. After geometric normalization every fine sector is uniformly close to the coarse operator

The full-polygon cotangent matrix has eigenvalues `0` and `d-2r`, so its operator norm is at most `d`. Since `H_d` is a principal Hermitian compression,

\[
\|H_d\|\le d.
\]

Also

\[
\|J_d\|=\varphi(d)\le d,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\|C_d\|\le2d.}
\tag{17}
\]

For every `t in [0,1]`, equation (13) now gives the uniform estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal P_d(t)-A_d\|
\le
\frac1d+\frac1{2d^2}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The bound is independent of `m` and `k`. By Weyl's inequality, every eigenvalue in every normalized fine fiber sector lies within the same distance of some eigenvalue of the coarse normalized operator. Hence no amount of repeated-prime depth can create an order-one normalized spectral band separated from the coarse primitive-shell spectrum once the coarse conductor is large.

This is stronger than saying that the average forgets prime-power depth. The **entire fine spectrum** is a collection of finite-pencil samples whose displacement from the coarse spectrum is uniformly `O(1/d)`.

## 6. Repeated depth samples one finite algebraic spectral curve

For fixed `d`, define

\[
F_d(z,t)
:=\det\!\left(zI-\mathcal P_d(t)\right).
\tag{19}
\]

This is a finite polynomial in `z` and `t` with coefficients determined completely by the two base matrices `L_d^{int}` and `C_d`. Equation (12) gives

\[
\boxed{
\det(zI-A_{dm})
=
\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}
F_d\!\left(z,\frac{k}{m}\right).
}
\tag{20}
\]

As old-prime-supported `m` grows, the fiber spectrum therefore becomes a denser sampling of the finitely many real eigenvalue branches of the single Hermitian pencil `mathcal P_d(t)`. The refinement depth does not generate a new discrete zero divisor; it refines the quasimomentum grid on a spectral curve already fixed at the coarse level.

In particular, interpreting the rational fiber frequency `t=k/m` as a new complex spectral parameter would be an external analytic continuation. The intrinsic construction supplies only a real Bloch parameter in `[0,1)` and a finite algebraic characteristic polynomial. The pencil does have the exact antiunitary `t <-> 1-t` reflection classified in PC-159, but that symmetry is the ordinary opposite-quasimomentum/time-reversal pairing of a real cyclic Bloch decomposition and already holds for arbitrary finite root subsets. It therefore carries no prime discriminator. The construction still supplies no archimedean gamma factor, no intrinsic complex `s`-plane, and no zero-localizing mechanism selecting `Re(s)=1/2`; identifying the real Bloch coordinate with Riemann's complex spectral variable remains external.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The analytic identities are in classical or already-audited territory. Calogero--Perelomov is the existing Prime-Circle anchor for the full regular-polygon `csc^2` spectrum used in (9). Matthias Beck, **Dedekind cotangent sums**, *Acta Arithmetica* 109 (2003), 109--130, places cotangent derivatives and Petersson--Knopp distribution identities in a standard finite trigonometric framework. More recently Jiahang Liu and Guoce Xin, **Root-of-unity weighted trigonometric power sums: a constant term approach**, arXiv:2607.29130 (2026), gives systematic root-of-unity-weighted formulas for even powers of cosecant and the neighboring cotangent families; equation (5) is a low-power member of precisely that finite-trigonometric landscape. These sources are already anchored in `research/prime_circle/SOURCES.md`.

Finite Fourier diagonalization of a block-circulant cyclic fiber is standard representation theory, and PC-051 already derives the affine repeated-prime fiber decomposition for the cotangent operator alone. Directed searches across weighted `csc^2` Fourier sums, primitive-root trigonometric matrices, block-circulant refinements, and cotangent/cosecant distribution identities did not locate the exact Prime-Circle operator formula (10)--(13). That absence is not evidence of historical priority, and no theorem-level novelty claim is made. PC-159 separately identifies the pencil's exact `t <-> 1-t` reflection as the standard Bloch time-reversal mechanism specialized to this circle gauge.

The durable contribution is the line-specific classification boundary: retaining **all** fine primitive degrees of freedom in a refinement that adds no new prime support still does not escape the finite base inverse-square/cotangent algebra. The new depth only supplies Bloch samples of one fixed quadratic pencil.

## 8. RH consequence, boundaries, and falsification surface

Under the stated hypotheses, the route

\[
\boxed{
\text{repeated/old-prime primitive refinement}
\to
\text{retain the full inverse-square fine fiber}
\to
\text{new depth-generated normalized spectrum}
\to
\text{RH mechanism}
}
\]

is ruled out. This strengthens PC-155 only on its repeated-prime boundary. It does **not** classify the full fine fibers of a genuinely new prime `q not| d`, where the fiber has `q-1` points and the missing residue breaks the cyclic translation symmetry; PC-155 currently controls only the fiber-constant channel there. It also does not classify rectangular multi-level operators retaining several conductors simultaneously, nonlinear interactions between fiber sectors, base conductors growing by genuinely new primes, growing-support linked-cluster resummations, or the global uniformization/monodromy branch.

The exact claim has direct finite falsifiers:

1. whenever `rad(m)|rad(d)`, ordering `U(dm)` by coarse residue and lift coordinate must make the fine primitive operator block-circulant in the lift difference;
2. equation (5) must hold for every nonzero coarse difference and every fiber character;
3. fiber Fourier transform must give (10) entry by entry, including the same-fiber eigenvalue `k(m-k)/2`;
4. the normalized spectrum must factor exactly as (12) and the characteristic polynomial as (20);
5. the quadratic covariance (15) and commutator scaling (16) must hold in every sector;
6. one counterexample to the uniform bound (18), after the stated normalization, would falsify the asymptotic collapse claim.

The central distinction is therefore sharp: **old-prime depth is only a Bloch sampling variable; genuinely new prime support is where unresolved fine-fiber information can still enter.**
