## Claim

PC-356 leaves a legitimate escape from its transported-metric obstruction. Instead of recomputing a source-derived radial Gram metric on the reciprocal control, suppose the Prime-Circle geometry supplies a positive Hilbert metric **before** the radial reciprocal flip — for example from an independently justified angular/chord ambient structure — and the same fixed metric is used for source and control. Such a metric need not commute with the centered parity

\[
Jc=c,\qquad Jb_n=-b_n.
\]

Then the PC-355 unitary equivalence can indeed fail. However the failure has an exact classification: it is only a nonunitary similarity effect.

Let `K` be a finite centered shell alphabet, let `H>0` be any fixed Hermitian form on `K`, and let

\[
\mathcal F_H^{\le N}
=\bigoplus_{m=0}^N K^{\otimes m}
\]

carry the tensor-product metric `H^{\otimes m}` on degree `m`, optionally multiplied by any positive scalar degree weight `w_m`. If `\mathcal D` is the regularized Prime-Circle inversion defect and

\[
\widetilde{\mathcal D}=\theta_J(\mathcal D)
\]

is the matched reciprocal control, their truncated left multipliers satisfy the algebraic identity

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde L_N=\Gamma_N(J)L_N\Gamma_N(J),
\qquad
\Gamma_N(J)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^N J^{\otimes m}.
}
\]

This identity is independent of the chosen metric. After whitening the common Hilbert metric, put

\[
S:=H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2},
\qquad
S_N:=\bigoplus_{m=0}^N S^{\otimes m}.
\]

Then `S^2=I`, and the Euclidean representatives `A_N` and `\widetilde A_N` obey

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde A_N=S_NA_NS_N^{-1}.
}
\]

Thus a fixed common metric cannot alter the ordinary spectrum at all. In particular, the PC-355 finite-cutoff result remains

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}A_N
=\operatorname{Spec}\widetilde A_N
=\{1\}.
}
\]

What the metric can alter are unitary-sensitive quantities such as singular values and pseudospectra, because `S_N` need not be unitary. Their possible separation is controlled completely by the one-particle metric/parity mismatch

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_H(J)
:=\kappa_2\!\left(H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2}\right)\ge1.
}
\]

Indeed

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_2(S_N)=\kappa_H(J)^N,
}
\]

and for every singular value,

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_H(J)^{-N}\,\sigma_k(A_N)
\le
\sigma_k(\widetilde A_N)
\le
\kappa_H(J)^N\,\sigma_k(A_N).
}
\]

The same factor controls resolvents and hence pseudospectra. Therefore an apparent source/control singular or pseudospectral separation — even one that grows rapidly with tensor depth — is not by itself a new arithmetic channel. It can arise purely from repeatedly tensoring one fixed nonunitary involution.

The fixed-metric escape from PC-356 is therefore real but much narrower than it first appears. A viable continuation must prove a **specific arithmetic correlation between the independently fixed geometric metric and the actual cyclotomic defect** that fails admissible reciprocal controls. Merely exhibiting `J^*HJ != H`, different singular values, or growing pseudospectral separation does not cross the Prime-Circle matched-control boundary.

## 1. The source/control relation is algebraic before a Hilbert metric is chosen

PC-350--PC-355 give the centered alphabet decomposition

\[
K=K_+\oplus K_-,
\qquad
J|_{K_+}=+I,
\qquad
J|_{K_-}=-I,
\]

with the calibration direction in `K_+` and the transverse shell directions in `K_-`. The full regularized defects satisfy

\[
\widetilde{\mathcal D}=\theta_J(\mathcal D)
\]

degree by degree.

For left multiplication on the truncated tensor algebra, this immediately gives

\[
\widetilde L_N
=\Gamma_N(J)L_N\Gamma_N(J)^{-1}.
\]

Since `J^2=I`, also `\Gamma_N(J)^2=I`, so the inverse equals the same operator. No inner product is used in this statement. Consequently every fixed common Hilbertization leaves source and control **similar**, even when it stops making them unitarily equivalent.

This already settles ordinary eigenvalues. The scalar term of every defect is `1`, while every positive-degree term strictly raises tensor degree. Hence

\[
L_N=I+Q_N,
\qquad Q_N^{N+1}=0,
\]

and the same holds for the control. The choice of a common metric cannot change this algebraic triangularity or its spectrum.

## 2. Whitening exposes the exact nonunitary intertwiner

Let the one-particle metric be

\[
\langle x,y\rangle_H=x^*Hy,
\qquad H>0.
\]

On degree `m`, allow the metric

\[
w_m H^{\otimes m},
\qquad w_m>0.
\]

Define the block whitening operator

\[
R_N
=\bigoplus_{m=0}^N
\sqrt{w_m}\,(H^{1/2})^{\otimes m}.
\]

The Euclidean representatives are

\[
A_N=R_NL_NR_N^{-1},
\qquad
\widetilde A_N=R_N\widetilde L_NR_N^{-1}.
\]

Because `\Gamma_N(J)` preserves degree, the scalar weights cancel from its whitened representative:

\[
R_N\Gamma_N(J)R_N^{-1}
=
\bigoplus_{m=0}^N
\left(H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2}\right)^{\otimes m}
=:S_N.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde A_N=S_NA_NS_N^{-1}.
}
\]

The common fixed metric has done exactly one thing: it has replaced the coordinate parity `J` by the similar involution

\[
S=H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2}.
\]

Moreover

\[
S^*S
=H^{-1/2}J^*HJH^{-1/2}.
\]

Hence `S` is unitary exactly when

\[
\boxed{J^*HJ=H.}
\]

This recovers PC-355 as the metric-compatible case. PC-356 instead let the control metric transform to `J^*HJ`, making `J` an isometry **between two different Hilbert spaces**. The present fixed-metric setting is the complementary case: source and control use one common Hilbert space, so parity becomes a generally nonunitary similarity.

## 3. Tensor depth amplifies only the static metric mismatch

Because `S^2=I`, its spectral radius is one and `\|S\|_2\ge1`. Also `S^{-1}=S`, so

\[
\kappa_2(S)
=\|S\|_2\|S^{-1}\|_2
=\|S\|_2^2.
\]

Tensor-product singular values multiply, giving

\[
\kappa_2(S^{\otimes m})
=\kappa_2(S)^m.
\]

Since `S_N` is the direct sum of the tensor powers through degree `N`,

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_2(S_N)=\kappa_2(S)^N
=\kappa_H(J)^N.
}
\]

This exponent is unaffected by arbitrary positive degree weights `w_m`; those weights disappeared from the intertwiner before the condition number was taken.

For any matrices `P,A,Q`, the min-max characterization of singular values gives

\[
\sigma_k(PAQ)
\le \|P\|_2\,\|Q\|_2\,\sigma_k(A).
\]

Apply this first to `\widetilde A_N=S_NA_NS_N^{-1}` and then to the inverse similarity. One obtains

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_H(J)^{-N}\sigma_k(A_N)
\le\sigma_k(\widetilde A_N)
\le\kappa_H(J)^N\sigma_k(A_N).
}
\]

Thus deeper tensor truncations can magnify a fixed one-particle metric asymmetry exponentially without introducing any new shell arithmetic at those deeper levels. This is an important control for numerical or asymptotic experiments: depth-dependent singular separation can be an ill-conditioning effect of second-quantized similarity rather than evidence that higher Chen words have become more arithmetically selective.

For every `z` outside the common spectrum,

\[

Hence

\[
\boxed{

\le
\|(zI-\widetilde A_N)^{-1}\|_2

\kappa_H(J)^N

With the standard resolvent definition of the `epsilon`-pseudospectrum this implies the two-sided inclusions

\[
\Lambda_\epsilon(\widetilde A_N)

\Lambda_{\kappa_H(J)^N\epsilon}(A_N),

\[
\Lambda_\epsilon(A_N)

Again, the only universal source/control amplification factor is the condition number of the whitened parity. No zeta parameter, gamma factor, critical-line symmetry, or prime-power selector enters this relation.

## 5. What this says about an independently fixed angular/chord metric

PC-356 correctly left open a metric or boundary datum that is fixed by roots-of-unity geometry **before** the reciprocal radial profile flip. The present theorem does not say such a datum is impossible. It says precisely what a fixed Hilbert metric can and cannot achieve if the operator being spectralized is still the PC-355 left-regular signature multiplier.

Suppose an angular/chord construction supplies `H` and the same `H` is used on source and matched control. If `J^*HJ=H`, nothing changes from PC-355: the two problems are unitarily equivalent. If `J^*HJ != H`, singular values and pseudospectra may differ, but their difference is mediated entirely by the fixed involution `S=H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2}` and inherits the depth amplification above.

Therefore the statement

\[
\text{"the roots metric breaks parity, so source/control singular spectra differ"}
\]

is not yet an arithmetic mechanism. The same operator-theoretic phenomenon occurs for any pair of tensor defects related by the same letter involution in the same non-invariant metric. To become Prime-Circle evidence, a candidate must derive an additional relation tying the particular roots-of-unity `H` to the particular cyclotomic coefficients of `\mathcal D` and then show that the resulting invariant is absent from an admissible matched reciprocal control.

This requirement is stronger than nonunitarity. It asks for a source-specific **correlation between two independently forced structures** rather than a generic consequence of comparing a matrix with a nonunitarily similar copy.

## Falsification / disproof audit

- **Metric check:** `H` is required to be one fixed positive-definite Hermitian form used identically for source and control. If the control recomputes its own metric, PC-356 rather than this theorem applies.
- **Parity check:** the result assumes the exact PC-355 algebraic law `\widetilde{\mathcal D}=\theta_J(\mathcal D)`; changing the operator, domain, or multiplication law in a way not transported by `theta_J` is outside the theorem.
- **Whitening check:** positive scalar degree weights cancel because parity preserves tensor degree; shell/word-dependent weights that do not factor through one common one-particle metric are not covered.
- **Unitary boundary:** `S` is unitary iff `J^*HJ=H`. This exactly reproduces the prior unitary obstruction rather than assuming it.
- **Tensor check:** `kappa(S^{\otimes m})=kappa(S)^m`, so the exponential depth factor is exact, not an asymptotic estimate.
- **Spectrum check:** similarity preserves ordinary eigenvalues independently of the metric, and finite left multiplication remains identity plus nilpotent.
- **Singular-value check:** the two-sided bound follows by applying the standard product inequality to the similarity and its inverse; it does not claim that the bound is always attained.
- **Pseudospectrum check:** the resolvent relation is exact and yields the stated epsilon rescaling inclusions.
- **Control interpretation:** different singular values do not prove arithmetic blindness of every possible joint invariant of `(H,\mathcal D)`. They prove only that metric-induced separation by itself is generic similarity distortion. A special cyclotomic correlation may survive, but it requires an additional theorem and matched-control test.
- **Infinite-depth boundary:** if the tensor completion makes `S_N` unbounded in the limit, the finite-cutoff theorem still holds but no bounded infinite-dimensional similarity is asserted. Such an unbounded-domain phenomenon requires its own operator-domain analysis and cannot be inferred from finite truncations.
- **Geometry boundary:** a source-forced domain, boundary condition, angular/chord operator, changing state space, or non-tensor Hilbert structure that alters the algebraic control law is outside the theorem.
- **RH check:** no intrinsic complex spectral parameter, completed-zeta functional equation, zero condition, or critical-line positivity is produced.

## Boundary assessment

PC-356 showed that a metric constructed covariantly from the radial source is transported to `J^*HJ` on the matched control and therefore restores exact unitary equivalence. PC-357 treats the complementary natural repair: **freeze one independently justified geometric metric and use it on both sides**. This can break unitary equivalence, but it cannot break algebraic similarity. Every ordinary eigenvalue remains control-blind, while singular and pseudospectral distinctions are governed by the fixed nonunitary parity `H^{1/2}JH^{-1/2}` and can be amplified as `kappa_H(J)^N` solely by tensor depth.

The surviving frontier is therefore narrower. A fixed roots-of-unity angular/chord metric is potentially useful only if one can prove a source-specific correlation with the cyclotomic signature that is not a generic consequence of nonunitary similarity. Alternatively the construction must change something deeper — a canonical domain or boundary condition, an asymmetric coupling, a non-tensor state space, or another operator datum that is not still related to the reciprocal control by the same algebraic parity automorphism. Merely freezing a non-parity-invariant Hilbert metric and observing spectral separation is not enough.
