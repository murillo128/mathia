# ANF-156 — positive bow spectrum is a finite-rank boundary perturbation of Fejer Toeplitz geometry

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + DUAL-GRAM + POSITIVE-FINITE-RANK-BOUNDARY + FEJER-TOEPLITZ-REDUCTION + SOURCE-GATE-REFINEMENT`. `ANF-153` freezes the bow geometry, `ANF-154` identifies the sharp first positive scale `Theta(K/N^2)`, and `ANF-155` proves that many detuned box-null directions occur above that scale on the cofinal family `N=mK`. The remaining technical nuisance in that picture is the diagonal edge normalization: the exact normalized Gram matrix is not Toeplitz, so the Fourier characters used in `ANF-155` are trial directions rather than exact eigenvectors. Dualizing the Gram removes most of that nuisance. For every integer `1<=K<=N`, the `N` positive eigenvalues of the exact normalized bow Gram are exactly the eigenvalues of an `N x N` matrix `B_{N,K}` which is the Fejer Toeplitz matrix plus a **positive boundary correction of rank `min(N,2K-2)` and trace exactly `K-1`**. Consequently its positive eigenvalue counting function differs from that of the Fejer Toeplitz matrix by at most `2K-2` at every threshold. In the bow regime `K=o(N)`, the entire empirical positive spectrum is therefore Toeplitz-controlled up to `o(N)` directions, uniformly in the spectral threshold. Moreover the dual quadratic form splits exactly into Fejer-frequency energy plus a positive prefix/suffix boundary tax. Thus low positive modes must simultaneously concentrate near box-filter null frequencies and cancel the finite-horizon edges. The source problem remains directional, but the fixed geometry is no longer an opaque edge-weighted Gram matrix: away from only `O(K)` boundary-sensitive directions it is the classical Fejer finite section.

## 1. Dualizing removes the exact kernel and preserves every positive eigenvalue

Keep the integer moving-window operator of `ANF-146`--`ANF-155`,

\[
A=A_{N,K}:\mathbb C^{N+K-1}\to\mathbb C^N,
\qquad
(Ab)_j=\sum_{r=1}^{K}b_{j+r},
\quad 0\le j<N.
\tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
d_n:=\#\{(j,r):0\le j<N,\ 1\le r\le K,\ j+r=n\},
\qquad
D:=\operatorname{diag}(d_n),
\tag{2}
\]

and `W=D^{1/2}`. The normalized untwisted Gram matrix of `ANF-153` is

\[
C_0=D^{-1/2}A^*AD^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Set

\[
S:=AD^{-1/2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then `C_0=S^*S`. Since `A` has rank `N` and every `d_n>0`, `S` also has rank `N`. Hence `C_0` has the established `(K-1)`-dimensional zero eigenspace and exactly `N` positive eigenvalues.

Now form the dual Gram matrix

\[
\boxed{
B:=SS^*=AD^{-1}A^*\in\mathbb C^{N\times N}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

The standard singular-value identity for `S^*S` and `SS^*` gives, with multiplicities,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}_{>0}(C_0)=\operatorname{spec}(B).
}
\tag{6}
\]

No asymptotic argument or divisibility hypothesis is present here. Thus every question about the **positive** bow spectrum may be transferred exactly from the `(N+K-1)`-dimensional normalized coefficient Gram to the positive-definite `N`-dimensional matrix `B`. The exact periodic kernel stays on the coefficient side, while all positive spectral scales survive unchanged on the output side.

## 2. The dual Gram is exactly Fejer Toeplitz plus a positive edge correction

Because `K<=N`, the moving multiplicities are

\[
d_n=
\begin{cases}
n,&1\le n\le K-1,\\
K,&K\le n\le N,\\
N+K-n,&N+1\le n\le N+K-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

Compare `B` with

\[
T:=\frac1KAA^*.
\tag{8}
\]

Two output windows overlap in exactly `(K-|j-k|)_+` coefficient sites, so

\[
\boxed{
T_{jk}=\left(1-\frac{|j-k|}{K}\right)_+,
\qquad 0\le j,k<N.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus `T` is the finite Toeplitz matrix generated by the Fejer symbol

\[
\boxed{
F_K(\theta)
:=\frac1K\left|\sum_{r=1}^{K}e^{ir\theta}\right|^2
=\sum_{|h|<K}\left(1-\frac{|h|}{K}\right)e^{ih\theta}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

The difference from the exact normalized geometry is

\[
\boxed{
E:=B-T
=A\left(D^{-1}-\frac1K I\right)A^*.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Since `d_n<=K`, the diagonal matrix in parentheses is positive semidefinite. It vanishes identically on the plateau `K<=n<=N` and is strictly positive only on the `2(K-1)` edge sites. Therefore

\[
\boxed{E\ge0.}
\tag{12}
\]

The range can also be read off exactly. The left edge columns of `A`, indexed by `1<=n<K`, are the nested prefix indicators

\[
(1,0,\ldots),\ (1,1,0,\ldots),\ \ldots,
\tag{13}
\]

and span the first `K-1` output coordinates. The right edge columns are the reversed nested suffix indicators and span the last `K-1` output coordinates. Since all corresponding diagonal coefficients in (11) are positive,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}E=\min\{N,2K-2\}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

In particular, in the bow regime `K=o(N)` one eventually has

\[
\operatorname{rank}E=2K-2=o(N).
\tag{15}
\]

The total correction is equally explicit. Cyclicity of trace gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}B
=\operatorname{tr}(D^{-1}A^*A)
=\sum_{n=1}^{N+K-1}\frac{d_n}{d_n}
=N+K-1,
\tag{16}
\]

whereas `tr T=N` because every diagonal entry in (9) is one. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}E=K-1.
}
\tag{17}
\]

So diagonal normalization does not create a new bulk operator. It adds a positive boundary perturbation supported on at most `2K-2` output directions and carrying total spectral mass exactly `K-1`.

## 3. The positive spectral counting function is Toeplitz-controlled up to O(K) directions

Let

\[
0<\mu_1\le\cdots\le\mu_N
\tag{18}
\]

be the eigenvalues of `T`, and

\[
0<\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_N
\tag{19}
\]

those of `B`, equivalently the positive eigenvalues of `C_0`. Positivity of `E` gives monotonicity

\[
\boxed{\mu_j\le\lambda_j\qquad(1\le j\le N).}
\tag{20}
\]

Let `r=rank E`. Finite-rank interlacing also gives

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_j\le\mu_{j+r}
\qquad(1\le j\le N-r),
}
\tag{21}
\]

with the statement vacuous if `r=N`. Define

\[
\nu_T(\tau):=\#\{j:\mu_j\le\tau\},
\qquad
\nu_+^{C_0}(\tau):=\#\{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(C_0):0<\lambda\le\tau\}.
\tag{22}
\]

Equations (6), (20), and (21) imply, for every real `tau`,

\[
\boxed{
\max\{0,\nu_T(\tau)-r\}
\le
\nu_+^{C_0}(\tau)
\le
\nu_T(\tau).
}
\tag{23}
\]

Using (14),

\[
\boxed{
\left|\nu_+^{C_0}(\tau)-\nu_T(\tau)\right|
\le 2K-2
}
\tag{24}
\]

whenever the right side is interpreted as the harmless larger bound if `2K-2>N`. This comparison is **uniform in the threshold `tau`**, including thresholds that themselves move with `N` and `K`.

Consequently, if `K=o(N)`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\left|
\frac{\nu_+^{C_0}(\tau)}N-
\frac{\nu_T(\tau)}N
\right|
\le\frac{2K-2}{N}
=o(1).
}
\tag{25}
\]

This removes the divisibility restriction of `ANF-155` at the level of **bulk spectral comparison**: for arbitrary integer `N,K`, all but `O(K)` positive eigenvalue counts are governed by the Fejer Toeplitz finite section. It does not make the bottom `O(K)` directions asymptotically invisible. At the sharp first scale `K/N^2`, `ANF-155` already finds only `Theta(K)` trial multiplicity, exactly the size at which the boundary rank can still matter. Thus the new reduction complements rather than replaces that bottom-scale argument.

There is also an exact total-shift identity. From (16)--(17), (18)--(20),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_j-\mu_j)=K-1.
}
\tag{26}
\]

The edge correction can raise positive eigenvalues but has only `K-1` total trace budget.

## 4. Low dual modes are Fejer-null-frequency modes with an additional boundary tax

The finite-rank statement has a more concrete quadratic-form version. For `g=(g_0,\ldots,g_{N-1})` put

\[
G(\theta):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g_j e^{ij\theta}.
\tag{27}
\]

Toeplitz Fourier inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
\langle Tg,g\rangle
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}F_K(\theta)|G(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.
}
\tag{28}
\]

The correction term can be written without matrices. For `1<=r<K`, let

\[
P_r(g):=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}g_j,
\qquad
S_r(g):=\sum_{j=N-r}^{N-1}g_j.
\tag{29}
\]

Expanding only the edge sites in (11) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\langle Eg,g\rangle
=\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}
\left(\frac1r-\frac1K\right)
\left(|P_r(g)|^2+|S_r(g)|^2\right).
}
\tag{30}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\langle Bg,g\rangle
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}F_K(\theta)|G(\theta)|^2\,d\theta
+
\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}
\left(\frac1r-\frac1K\right)
\left(|P_r(g)|^2+|S_r(g)|^2\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]

Both pieces are nonnegative. A genuine low positive eigenvector of the normalized bow Gram, transported to the dual side through singular-value duality, must therefore satisfy **two simultaneous conditions**: its Fourier mass must live where the Fejer response is small, and its nested prefix/suffix sums must pay little boundary energy.

This can be made quantitative without any Toeplitz asymptotics. Let `Z_K={2pi ell/K:1<=ell<=K-1}` be the nonzero box-filter nulls on the circle, and for fixed `0<eta<=1/2` remove circular neighborhoods of radius `2pi eta/K` around `Z_K`. On the remaining set `Omega_eta`, the elementary sine formula for (10), together with the high value in the Voronoi cell around zero, gives

\[
F_K(\theta)\ge\frac{\sin^2(\pi\eta)}{K}
\qquad(\theta\in\Omega_\eta,\ K\ge2).
\tag{32}
\]

Hence any nonzero `g` with dual Rayleigh quotient

\[
\lambda(g):=\frac{\langle Bg,g\rangle}{\|g\|_2^2}
\tag{33}
\]

obeys

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{2\pi\|g\|_2^2}
\int_{\Omega_\eta}|G(\theta)|^2\,d\theta
\le
\frac{K\lambda(g)}{\sin^2(\pi\eta)}.
}
\tag{34}
\]

It also obeys directly from (30)--(31)

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}
\left(\frac1r-\frac1K\right)
\left(|P_r(g)|^2+|S_r(g)|^2\right)
\le
\lambda(g)\|g\|_2^2.
}
\tag{35}
\]

Thus `lambda=o(1/K)` forces asymptotically all dual Fourier energy into `O(1/K)` tubes around the nonzero `K`th-root nulls, while also forcing the edge tax to vanish. At the sharp bottom scale from `ANF-154`, `lambda=O(K/N^2)`, the right side of (34) is `O((K/N)^2)`, which tends to zero in the bow regime. This supplies an actual geometric description of positive near-null modes rather than only their count.

## 5. What this changes for the arithmetic source gate

For every positive eigenpair

\[
C_0u=\lambda u,
\qquad \lambda>0,
\tag{36}
\]

the vector

\[
g:=\lambda^{-1/2}Su
\tag{37}
\]

is a unit eigenvector of `B` with the same eigenvalue. Hence (31)--(35) describe the left-singular geometry associated to every positive near-null direction of the fixed bow operator. The low-spectrum sector exposed in `ANF-152` is therefore not an unstructured collection of edge-weighted vectors: on the dual side it is a Fejer-null-frequency sector constrained by a finite-horizon boundary tax.

This does **not** prove the bow. The actual source remains

\[
y_U=D_UWr_X,
\tag{38}
\]

and neither (23) nor (34) shows that its coefficient-side spectral mass lands in the required positive low eigenspaces or in the exact kernel. A rank-`O(K)` perturbation can still matter at the very bottom scale, and eigenvalue-counting equivalence does not imply projector or eigenvector equivalence. In particular, ordinary Szego-type spectral distribution information would be insufficient for the source-specific alignment demanded by `ANF-152`.

The reduction does, however, sharpen the next analytic interface. Geometry-only work can now be divided cleanly into the classical growing-symbol finite section `T_N(F_K)` and an explicit positive edge correction. Any proposed arithmetic theorem should then address **directional coupling** of the demodulated post-sieve innovation to the corresponding low singular sector, rather than treating the diagonal normalization itself as an unknown source of spectral complexity. Conversely, a negative bow theorem could try to show that a fixed fraction of the source-induced positive-spectrum component necessarily pays either Fejer energy away from the box-null tubes or the boundary tax in (35).

## 6. Prior-art boundary, stress tests, and next test

The ingredients on the geometry side are classical: boxcar convolution produces the Fejer/Dirichlet-kernel symbol, nonzero spectra of `S^*S` and `SS^*` agree, and positive finite-rank perturbations satisfy min--max interlacing. A literature audit recovered the standard Grenander--Szego Toeplitz framework and the familiar principle that low-rank perturbations do not change bulk spectral distributions. None of those external results is load-bearing here: equations (5)--(17), the rank calculation, the counting comparison, and the boundary quadratic form are finite-dimensional identities proved directly above. The Mathia-specific content is their exact combination for the diagonally normalized bow moving-window Gram. No new durable literature dependency is therefore needed in `SOURCES.md`.

The main stress tests are explicit. When `K=1`, `D=I`, `E=0`, and `B=T=I`, so every correction formula collapses correctly. When `K` is comparable to `N`, the edge rank can be all of `N`, and (25) gives no small-error bulk conclusion; the bow application uses `K=o(N)`. At the first positive scale `K/N^2`, an `O(K)` rank perturbation is not negligible relative to the `Omega(K)` multiplicity found by `ANF-155`, so no bottom-eigenvalue asymptotic is claimed. Finally, the dual Fourier localization concerns the left singular vectors `g`, not the arithmetic coefficient vector `y_U` directly; transporting source information across that singular geometry is still an unresolved step.

For `analytic_frontier`, the next decisive test is therefore more focused than a generic spectral-counting problem. Characterize enough of the **low projectors** of the growing Fejer Toeplitz finite section, including the explicit edge tax, to translate a projection of `y_U` into a concrete twisted prime/Ramanujan correlation statement. If that translation still demands information beyond known short-interval or Hardy--Littlewood local statistics, it will isolate the precise new arithmetic datum the bow requires; if the source structure forces mass away from these null tubes, it would instead provide a direct obstruction to the bow route.
