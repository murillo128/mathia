# ANF-003 — common-translation vector features scalarize to one Montgomery--Taylor kernel

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-IDENTITY + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT` for attempts to beat the Montgomery--Taylor constant by replacing Lamzouri's single scalar feature with finitely many vector components while keeping one common translation character and then consuming only their scalar Hilbert-space Gram kernel.

## 1. Exact statement

Let `I=(-1/2,1/2)` and let `E` be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. Take a measurable feature seed

\[
a:I\to E,
\qquad
q(u):=\|a(u)\|_E^2,
\]

with `q` integrable, nonnegative, even, supported in `I`, and normalized by

\[
\int_I q(u)\,du=1.
\tag{1}
\]

For every complex point `z`, define the translation-covariant vector feature

\[
\Phi_z(u):=a(u)e^{-2\pi iuz}
\in L^2(I;E).
\tag{2}
\]

Then for all `z,s in C`,

\[
\boxed{
\langle \Phi_z,\Phi_s\rangle
=
\int_I q(u)e^{-2\pi iu(z-\bar s)}\,du
=
\widehat q(z-\bar s).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every scalar Gram quantity generated by the whole vector feature family depends only on the single nonnegative spectral density `q`; the internal feature dimension and the factorization of `q` into components are invisible.

In particular, if the Lamzouri tensor/Bessel construction is repeated with `Phi_z` in place of the scalar feature `eta(u)e^{-2 pi iuz}`, its pair statistic is

\[
\sum_{z,s\in\mathcal Z}
\langle\Phi_z,\Phi_s\rangle^2
=
\sum_{z,s\in\mathcal Z}
\widehat q(z-\bar s)^2.
\tag{4}
\]

Put `eta=sqrt(q)`. Then `eta in L^2(I)`, `eta` is real and even, and `eta^2=q`. Equations (3)--(4) are therefore **exactly the one-feature class of Lamzouri Proposition 2.1**, not a larger semidefinite class.

Consequently the Carneiro--Chandee--Littmann--Milinovich extremal bound applies to the normalized quadratic constant and gives

\[
\boxed{C_q\ge C_{\rm MT}.}
\tag{5}
\]

A common-translation vector-valued feature lift of this form cannot improve the unconditional simple-critical proportion `2-C_MT=0.6725007...`.

## 2. The scalarization is representation-theoretic, not an artifact of coordinates

Writing `a=(a_1,...,a_m)` in an orthonormal basis makes (3) transparent:

\[
\langle\Phi_z,\Phi_s\rangle
=
\sum_{j=1}^m
\int_I |a_j(u)|^2e^{-2\pi iu(z-\bar s)}\,du
=
\widehat{\sum_j|a_j|^2}(z-\bar s).
\tag{6}
\]

But this is not merely a coordinate cancellation. The orbit `z -> Phi_z` is generated from one seed vector by the scalar character action `e^{-2 pi iuz}`. Its cyclic subspace therefore has scalar spectral measure `q(u)du`. Any two vector seeds with the same norm profile `q` produce identical Gram data for every complex configuration `Z`, even if their component decompositions are completely different.

This gives a matched falsification control for proposed multi-feature certificates: if changing the component directions of `a(u)` while keeping `||a(u)||^2` fixed leaves the complete proposed zero statistic unchanged, then the construction has not added pair-correlation information beyond one scalar kernel.

## 3. Constant positive-semidefinite mixing also collapses

Suppose instead that one begins with finitely many scalar features collected into a vector `a(u)` and inserts a fixed positive-semidefinite matrix `A>=0` in the feature-space inner product. Replacing `a` by `A^{1/2}a` gives

\[
q_A(u)=a(u)^*Aa(u)\ge0,
\qquad
\langle\Phi_z,\Phi_s\rangle_A
=
\widehat{q_A}(z-\bar s).
\tag{7}
\]

After normalization, this is again one admissible nonnegative spectral density. Hence a finite SDP whose only freedom is a constant PSD mixing of common-translation features cannot escape the Montgomery--Taylor extremal problem.

Likewise, taking positive convex combinations of separately normalized Lamzouri inequalities cannot help. If certificates `j` have constants `C_j>=C_MT`, then any convex combination has constant

\[
\sum_j\lambda_j C_j\ge C_{\rm MT},
\qquad
\lambda_j\ge0,
\quad
\sum_j\lambda_j=1.
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore the smallest natural interpretations of “multi-feature” and “PSD lift” in `CLUE-semidefinite-pair-correlation-horizontal-lift` are closed.

## 4. Why this does not kill a genuine matrix-valued or signed lift

The no-go depends on **scalar compression of the feature geometry**. Operator-valued Bochner theory is genuinely richer: a translation-invariant matrix kernel can carry a positive operator-valued spectral measure rather than one scalar measure. Carmeli--De Vito--Toigo--Umanità characterize translation-invariant vector-valued reproducing kernels on abelian groups in precisely this operator-valued spectral framework.

Accordingly, (5) does **not** rule out a certificate that retains several matrix entries or several pair observables separately and feeds them into a new counting inequality before taking a scalar trace. It also does not rule out signed combinations whose validity comes from a global inequality rather than from a positive convex combination of known scalar certificates.

This distinction is important for the neighboring semidefinite prior art. Chirre--Gonçalves--de Laat do not obtain their RH-conditional gain merely by writing the same bandlimited scalar feature in more coordinates; they enlarge the auxiliary-function/sign class and optimize genuinely additional constraints. A useful unconditional analogue must likewise preserve additional information after the feature representation is quotiented by Gram equivalence.

## 5. Compatibility with unconditional BGSST evaluation

The scalarization occurs before any number-theoretic estimate. If `q` is regular enough that

\[
Q=q*q
\]

is an admissible real-even support-one BGSST profile, then

\[
\widehat Q=\widehat q^{\,2}
\tag{9}
\]

and Lamzouri's derivative correction removes the Montgomery weight exactly as in `ANF-002`. No smoothness of `sqrt(q)` is needed for this analytic observation: BGSST evaluates the final support-one profile `Q`, while Proposition 2.1 itself only requires the scalar square root in `L^2`.

Thus vectorization does not create a hidden new unconditional evaluation channel. Once the scalar Gram kernel is formed, both the zero-side counting inequality and the prime-side support-one asymptotic see only `q`.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty assessment

The general feature-map identity `K(x,t)=gamma_x^* gamma_t`, the spectral description of translation-invariant kernels on abelian groups, and operator-valued Bochner theory are classical. A direct modern reference is C. Carmeli, E. De Vito, A. Toigo and V. Umanità, **Vector valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and universality**, *Analysis and Applications* 8 (2010), 19--61, DOI `10.1142/S0219530510001503`. Their framework explicitly represents translation-invariant operator-valued kernels through positive operator-valued spectral measures.

Lamzouri's Proposition 2.1 supplies the scalar Hilbert counting inequality, and Corollary 14 of Carneiro--Chandee--Littmann--Milinovich supplies the extremal lower bound for the resulting nonnegative admissible scalar class. No novelty is claimed for those ingredients or for Bochner scalarization itself.

The Mathia-specific contribution is the exact boundary relevant to the live clue: **adding feature multiplicity without retaining operator-valued information is pure representation redundancy**. The common-translation cyclic feature orbit is completely classified by one nonnegative spectral density, so the apparent finite-dimensional SDP enlargement has no mathematical room to beat `C_MT`.

## 7. Consequence for the analytic frontier

`ANF-002` reduced the next unconditional gain to finding more usable quadratic information than one factorized squared kernel. The present result rules out the most immediate implementation of that idea: a vector-valued direct sum, a constant PSD mixing matrix, or a positive convex ensemble whose final zero observable is still one scalar Gram square.

The surviving second-order route must therefore do at least one of the following:

- retain a genuinely matrix-valued translation-invariant kernel and use its operator structure in the counting inequality before scalarization;
- evaluate several scalar pair observables jointly and exploit constraints not implied by their positive convex hull;
- use a signed global inequality whose validity is not obtained by termwise positivity or convex averaging;
- justify information beyond the current support-one BGSST channel.

This is consistent with `WI-118`: support-one termwise positivity is already screened, while the present result shows that merely packaging the same positive spectral density into more feature coordinates is also inert.

### Decisive falsification boundary

This finding would be falsified by a construction of the exact form (2), possibly with constant PSD feature mixing, whose scalar Gram-based Lamzouri certificate has normalized constant `< C_MT`; equations (3)--(7) reduce such a construction to the cited scalar extremal problem.

It does not apply when different matrix entries remain separate observables, when the feature action is not one common translation character, when the counting inequality uses operator structure not determined by the scalar Gram kernel, or when signed combinations are certified by additional arithmetic information. Those are the precise surviving branches of the clue.
