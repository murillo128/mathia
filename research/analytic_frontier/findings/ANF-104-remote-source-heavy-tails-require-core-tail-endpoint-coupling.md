# ANF-104 — remote source-heavy tails require core-tail endpoint coupling

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CORE-TAIL-POLARIZATION + REMOTE-DECOUPLING + ENDPOINT-HEIGHT-BARRIER + NOTCH-ENVELOPE-NARROWING`. `ANF-103` shows that a geometrically remote `o(N)` tail is irrelevant whenever its Montgomery--Taylor source norm is `o(N)`, but it leaves a source-heavy sparse tail as a genuine escape. That escape is more rigid than source-heaviness alone. Because Lamzouri's Montgomery--Taylor floor applies separately to the core and to the tail, a source-heavy sparse tail inside a globally near-extremal configuration must cancel its own positive source excess through a **macroscopic negative core--tail cross term**. The explicit Montgomery--Taylor kernel then turns horizontal remoteness into a quantitative obstruction to that cancellation: the cross term decays like the inverse square of the horizontal gap, up to the endpoint exponential moments generated by the imaginary heights.

Consequently, a sparse tail separated from its core by a gap `R` is automatically source-negligible, and hence removable by `ANF-103`, whenever its endpoint coupling is too small. In the coarse max-height form, if the tail has `m` points and the core and tail heights are bounded by `H_A,H_B`, respectively, then a source-heavy tail in a near-extremizer necessarily satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{m e^{2\pi(H_A+H_B)}}{R^2}\not\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{1}
\]

More quantitatively, if the normalized tail source mass has a positive lower limit `lambda`, then

\[
\boxed{
H_A+H_B
\ge
\frac1\pi\log\frac{R}{\sqrt m}+O_\lambda(1).
}
\tag{2}
\]

This is independent of the self-energy height threshold in `ANF-103`. A sub-square-root source-heavy tail must now pay **both** its own endpoint visibility cost and enough endpoint mass to remain coupled to the core across its horizontal separation.

## 1. A source-heavy sparse tail must anti-align with its actual core

Retain the Montgomery--Taylor source notation

\[
J_0:=J_{\rm MT},
\qquad
\|f\|_0^2:=\int_{-1}^{1}J_0(\alpha)|f(\alpha)|^2\,d\alpha,
\qquad
S_X(\alpha):=\sum_{z\in X}e^{-2\pi i\alpha z},
\]

and define

\[
L(X):=2|X|-\sigma(X),
\qquad
\Delta(X):=\|S_X\|_0^2-L(X)\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

Let a finite conjugation-invariant multiset split as

\[
W=A\sqcup B,
\tag{4}
\]

where the split is complete on every real-part fiber. In particular no real support site is divided between the two pieces, so the affine bookkeeping is exactly additive:

\[
L(W)=L(A)+L(B).
\tag{5}
\]

Put

\[
\mathcal C_0(A,B)
:=\operatorname{Re}\langle S_A,S_B\rangle_0.
\tag{6}
\]

Hilbert-space polarization and (5) give the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(W)=\Delta(A)+\Delta(B)+2\mathcal C_0(A,B).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Since Lamzouri's floor applies independently to both pieces, `Delta(A),Delta(B)>=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(B)-\Delta(W)
\le -2\mathcal C_0(A,B)
\le2|\mathcal C_0(A,B)|.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This already identifies the missing mechanism in `ANF-103`. Let `W_n=A_n sqcup B_n` be near-extremal, let `L_n=L(W_n)`, and suppose

\[
\frac{\Delta(W_n)}{L_n}\to0,
\qquad
\mu_n:=\frac{L(B_n)}{L_n}\to0.
\tag{9}
\]

If the tail is source-heavy in the sense that

\[
\liminf_n
\tau_n^2
:=
\liminf_n\frac{\|S_{B_n}\|_0^2}{L_n}
\ge\lambda>0,
\tag{10}
\]

then `Delta(B_n)/L_n=tau_n^2-mu_n`, and (7) implies

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n
\frac{\mathcal C_0(A_n,B_n)}{L_n}
\le-\frac\lambda2.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus a source-heavy sparse tail cannot merely carry a macroscopic positive self-energy. Global near-extremality forces it to be cancelled by a macroscopic **negative** interaction with the retained core. This is the core--tail analogue of the separable-comparator anti-alignment mechanism in `ANF-097`, but it requires no choice of separable carrier.

## 2. The explicit Montgomery--Taylor kernel gives quantitative remote decoupling

Use the explicit factor from `ANF-087`, with `theta=2^{-1/2}`,

\[
K(z)
=
\frac{
\cos(\pi z)-\sqrt2\,\pi z\cot\theta\,\sin(\pi z)
}{1-2\pi^2z^2},
\qquad
\widehat J_0(z)=K(z)^2.
\tag{12}
\]

For every complex `z` with `|z|>=1`, the elementary bounds

\[
|\cos(\pi z)|,|\sin(\pi z)|
\le e^{\pi|\operatorname{Im}z|}
\tag{13}
\]

and

\[
|1-2\pi^2z^2|
\ge(2\pi^2-1)|z|^2
\tag{14}
\]

give

\[
\boxed{
|K(z)|
\le
\kappa\,
\frac{e^{\pi|\operatorname{Im}z|}}{|z|},
\qquad
\kappa
:=
\frac{1+\sqrt2\,\pi\cot(2^{-1/2})}
     {2\pi^2-1}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Numerically `kappa=0.3308214...`; its exact value is not important.

Define the horizontal gap

\[
R(A,B)
:=
\min_{a\in A,\,b\in B}
|\operatorname{Re}a-\operatorname{Re}b|
\tag{16}
\]

and the endpoint exponential mass

\[
\mathfrak M(X)
:=
\sum_{z\in X}e^{2\pi|\operatorname{Im}z|}.
\tag{17}
\]

Assume `R(A,B)>=1`. Directly from the source inner product,

\[
\langle S_A,S_B\rangle_0
=
\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}
K(a-\overline b)^2.
\tag{18}
\]

The real part of every `a-bar(b)` has magnitude at least `R(A,B)`, while

\[
|\operatorname{Im}(a-\overline b)|
\le|\operatorname{Im}a|+|\operatorname{Im}b|.
\tag{19}
\]

Applying (15) pairwise and factorizing the resulting positive sum gives the finite bound

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal C_0(A,B)|
\le
\frac{\kappa^2}{R(A,B)^2}
\mathfrak M(A)\mathfrak M(B).
}
\tag{20}
\]

No separation inside `A` or inside `B` is assumed. Multiplicities, collisions, arbitrary signs of the complex kernel values, and arbitrary internal horizontal geometry are all allowed. Only the gap between the two conjugation-invariant components enters.

Combining (8) and (20) yields the exact source-transfer estimate

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(B)
\le
\Delta(W)
+
\frac{2\kappa^2}{R(A,B)^2}
\mathfrak M(A)\mathfrak M(B).
}
\tag{21}
\]

After division by `L(W)`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|S_B\|_0^2}{L(W)}
\le
\frac{\Delta(W)}{L(W)}
+
\frac{L(B)}{L(W)}
+
\frac{2\kappa^2\mathfrak M(A)\mathfrak M(B)}
     {R(A,B)^2L(W)}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Equation (22) is the useful form for the near-extremizer frontier: it turns the qualitative phrase “source-heavy remote tail” into one explicit coupling budget.

## 3. Remote tails below the endpoint-coupling threshold are automatically trimmable

Let `N_n=|W_n|`, `m_n=|B_n|`, and assume

\[
N_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta(W_n)}{L(W_n)}\to0,
\qquad
m_n=o(N_n).
\tag{23}
\]

Then `L(B_n)/L(W_n)->0`. If the components are separated by gaps `R_n>=1` and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathfrak M(A_n)\mathfrak M(B_n)}
     {R_n^2L(W_n)}
\longrightarrow0,
}
\tag{24}
\]

then (22) forces

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|S_{B_n}\|_0^2}{L(W_n)}\to0.
}
\tag{25}
\]

The tail is therefore source-negligible, exactly the hypothesis required by `ANF-103`; if the retained core lies in an `O(N_n)` horizontal interval, it can be trimmed without changing either near-extremality or fixed-notch exposure, and `ANF-101` applies to the resulting core.

Conversely, if (10) holds, then (22)--(23) force

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_n
\frac{\mathfrak M(A_n)\mathfrak M(B_n)}
     {R_n^2L(W_n)}
\ge
\frac{\lambda}{2\kappa^2}.
}
\tag{26}
\]

Thus the endpoint exponential mass is not merely a convenient upper bound: a macroscopically source-visible sparse tail must keep a quantitatively nonvanishing endpoint channel open to its core across the horizontal gap.

For a coarser but transparent corollary, put

\[
H_A:=\max_{a\in A}|\operatorname{Im}a|,
\qquad
H_B:=\max_{b\in B}|\operatorname{Im}b|.
\tag{27}
\]

Since

\[
\mathfrak M(A)\le|A|e^{2\pi H_A},
\qquad
\mathfrak M(B)\le m e^{2\pi H_B},
\qquad
\frac{|A|}{L(W)}\le1,
\tag{28}
\]

(22) gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|S_B\|_0^2}{L(W)}
\le
\frac{\Delta(W)}{L(W)}
+
\frac{L(B)}{L(W)}
+
2\kappa^2
\frac{m e^{2\pi(H_A+H_B)}}{R^2}.
}
\tag{29}
\]

Hence (10) implies

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_n
\frac{m_ne^{2\pi(H_{A,n}+H_{B,n})}}{R_n^2}
\ge
\frac{\lambda}{2\kappa^2},
}
\tag{30}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_{A,n}+H_{B,n}
\ge
\frac1\pi\log\frac{R_n}{\sqrt{m_n}}
+O_\lambda(1).
}
\tag{31}
\]

This is a **coupling** threshold, not merely a tail self-energy threshold. It says that a tail can remain source-heavy only if the combined vertical endpoint amplification of core and tail is large enough to overcome the `R^{-2}` decay of the Montgomery--Taylor interaction.

## 4. The new barrier is independent of the ANF-103 self-energy barrier

For a sub-square-root tail, `ANF-103` already proves that source-heaviness requires

\[
4\pi H_B
\ge
\log\frac{L(W)}{m^2}
+2\log\log\frac{L(W)}{m^2}
+O(1).
\tag{32}
\]

Equation (31) is a second necessary condition. The first asks whether the tail can carry macroscopic Montgomery--Taylor energy **by itself**. The second asks whether that positive excess can be cancelled strongly enough by the core for the union to remain near-extremal. Neither condition implies the other in general.

A useful power-law calibration makes the distinction visible. Suppose

\[
m_n=N_n^a,
\qquad
R_n=N_n^b,
\qquad
a<\frac12,
\tag{33}
\]

and the retained core has `H_{A,n}=o(log N_n)`. Any source-heavy tail then needs, from (31),

\[
\boxed{
H_{B,n}
\ge
\frac{b-a/2}{\pi}\log N_n
-o(\log N_n).
}
\tag{34}
\]

The self-energy requirement (32) has leading coefficient `(1-2a)/(4pi)`. Thus once `b>1/4`, the core--tail coupling constraint is already stronger at leading logarithmic order; for a genuinely superlinear remote gap `b>1` it is much stronger. A geometrically extreme outlier therefore cannot exploit the minimal self-energy height from `ANF-103` while remaining dynamically decoupled from its core.

The concrete arithmetic surgery of `ANF-096` is recovered as a stress test rather than used as evidence. There `|A|asymp n^3`, `m asymp n`, the padding/block gap is `R=n^{10}`, the padding height is negligible, and the lifted block was restricted to `H_B<=log n`. Equation (29) gives the same qualitative remote-decoupling conclusion as its direct estimate: after normalizing by the carrier size, the cross term is bounded by a constant times

\[
\frac{n e^{2\pi H_B}}{n^{20}}=o(1).
\tag{35}
\]

The present result removes the arithmetic-comb assumptions and shows exactly which quantity controls that decoupling for an arbitrary physical core and tail.

## 5. Prior-art boundary, adversarial checks, and remaining frontier

The neighboring general principles are classical. Compact spectral support gives Fourier--Laplace transforms of exponential type in the Paley--Wiener theory, and concentration--compactness arguments routinely distinguish compactness from splitting into remote profiles. Neither principle is load-bearing here. The useful `R^{-1}` bound for `K`, the resulting `R^{-2}` interaction decay, and the affine source-excess inequality (21)--(22) are obtained directly from the explicit Montgomery--Taylor kernel already anchored through Carneiro--Chandee--Littmann--Milinovich and Lamzouri. A targeted literature check did not locate a theorem giving this line-specific core--tail cancellation threshold, so `SOURCES.md` is unchanged and no generic concentration--compactness novelty is claimed.

Several failure modes are explicit. The gap assumption concerns separation **between components**, not the total diameter of `W`; a diffuse superlinear bulk with no sparse separated component remains untouched. If `R=O(1)`, (20) gives no useful decoupling. If the core itself has enormous endpoint exponential mass, the max-height version (31) can be vacuous; the sharper invariant is (26), not `H_B` alone. Nor does source-heaviness by itself imply a fixed-notch obstruction: `ANF-097` still requires a fatal configuration to anti-align with every compatible separable carrier, while (11) only records the additional anti-alignment forced against the actual retained core.

The mathematical frontier from `ANF-103` is therefore narrowed but not closed. A tail-driven superlinear escape must now be both source-heavy **and** core-coupled at the endpoint scale (26). For a sub-square-root tail it must simultaneously cross the self-energy height barrier (32). What remains is to control the endpoint mass of the linearly spanned core strongly enough to make (26) impossible for remote tails, or to build a physical sequence that satisfies both constraints while also meeting the all-comparator anti-alignment of `ANF-097`. The genuinely diffuse superlinear-bulk branch remains separate, and no inequality here by itself proves `beta_eta<B_eta`, improves the simple-critical proportion, or proves RH.
