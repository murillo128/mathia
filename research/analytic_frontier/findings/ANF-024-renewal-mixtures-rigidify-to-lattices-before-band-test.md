# ANF-024 — renewal mixtures rigidify to lattices before the band test

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + EXACT-RIGIDITY + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + DIFFRACTION-DUAL`. The diffraction target of `ANF-020` cannot be reached by stationary renewal processes, and convexifying over renewal laws does not restore the missing freedom at the Montgomery--Taylor contraction. The obstruction occurs in two stages. First, the exact renewal diffraction formula shows that **any vanishing long-wavelength diffuse structure factor forces the iid gap law to be deterministic**. Second, after convexification every surviving component is therefore a lattice, so the Möbius dilation certificate of `ANF-022` applies.

Let

\[
\nu_a:=a\,\delta_0+a|h|\,dh
\qquad (0<a<1)
\]

on the open band `(-1,1)`. For a stationary renewal process with iid positive gaps `G`, assume

\[
0<m:=\mathbb E G<\infty
\]

and, as in the classical diffraction theorem of Baake--Kösters, that `G` has a finite moment of order `1+epsilon` for some `epsilon>0`. Write `rho=1/m` for its intensity and use the per-particle diffraction normalization of `ANF-020`--`ANF-023`.

Then:

1. **No single such renewal process satisfies** `mu<=nu_a` on `(-1,1)` for any `a<1`.
2. More generally, let `pi` be any measurable probability mixture of such renewal diffraction profiles whose barycenter is locally finite. If

\[
\overline\mu:=\int \mu_\theta\,d\pi(\theta)
\le \nu_a,
\]

then `pi`-almost every renewal gap law is deterministic. Hence `overline mu` is a scale mixture of lattice diffractions.
3. The exact finite dual certificate already constructed in `ANF-022` then yields the necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
a\ge a_{50}:=\frac1{1+B_{50}}
=0.755534723837469\ldots,
\qquad
B_{50}=0.323565904318542\ldots .
}
\]

Since

\[
a_{\rm MT}=C_{\rm MT}^{-1}
=0.753296067856070\ldots<a_{50},
\]

**no convex mixture of stationary renewal-process diffraction profiles can realize the Montgomery--Taylor order interval.** This excludes not only Poisson-like iid spacings but the entire classical renewal/random-tiling family covered by the diffraction theorem. Any stochastic scalar witness that survives this filter must use genuine correlations between successive gaps (or leave the renewal framework altogether).

## 1. Classical renewal diffraction in the present normalization

Baake--Kösters treat the mean-one stationary renewal process generated by a probability measure on the positive half-line with a finite `1+epsilon` moment. If `phi` is the characteristic function of the gap law in the Fourier convention `exp(-2 pi i h x)`, their renewal relation gives an absolutely continuous diffraction density which, away from the discrete set where `phi(h)=1`, can be written as

\[
\boxed{
S(h)=\frac{1-|\phi(h)|^2}{|1-\phi(h)|^2}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Their theorem also classifies the pure-point part: in the non-arithmetic case only the forward peak remains, while an arithmetic gap law can carry reciprocal-lattice Bragg peaks. The formula follows equivalently from their displayed density `1-H`, where

\[
H(h)=
\frac{2\bigl(|\phi(h)|^2-\operatorname{Re}\phi(h)\bigr)}
{|1-\phi(h)|^2}.
\]

Indeed `1-H=(1-|phi|^2)/|1-phi|^2`.

Rescale now from mean gap one to mean gap `m`. The intensity is `rho=1/m`; after dividing the usual diffraction by the intensity, as in the finite-configuration normalization of `ANF-020`, the forward atom has mass `rho` while the diffuse part still has density (1), with

\[
\phi(h)=\mathbb E e^{-2\pi i hG}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus one may write

\[
\mu
=\rho\,\delta_0+\Pi+S(h)\,dh,
\tag{3}
\]

where `Pi>=0` contains any nonzero pure-point diffraction. No explicit description of `Pi` will be needed for the rigidity argument.

## 2. The zero-frequency diffuse floor is the squared gap coefficient of variation

The key point is that (1) cannot approach zero for a genuinely random iid gap law. Only the first moment is needed for the denominator. From (2) and dominated convergence,

\[
\frac{1-\phi(h)}h\longrightarrow 2\pi i m
\qquad(h\to0),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|1-\phi(h)|^2}{h^2}
\longrightarrow 4\pi^2m^2.
}
\tag{4}
\]

For the numerator, let `G'` be an independent copy of `G`. Since `G-G'` is symmetric,

\[
1-|\phi(h)|^2
=
\mathbb E\!\left[1-\cos\bigl(2\pi h(G-G')\bigr)\right].
\tag{5}
\]

Fatou's lemma gives, in the extended nonnegative reals,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\liminf_{h\to0}
\frac{1-|\phi(h)|^2}{h^2}
&\ge
\mathbb E\left[
\liminf_{h\to0}
\frac{1-\cos(2\pi h(G-G'))}{h^2}
\right]\\
&=
2\pi^2\,\mathbb E(G-G')^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Combining (1), (4), and (6),

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{h\to0}S(h)
\ge
c(G):=
\frac{\mathbb E(G-G')^2}{2m^2}
\in[0,\infty].
}
\tag{7}
\]

If the second moment is finite, this is the familiar lower bound

\[
c(G)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(G)}{m^2}.
\tag{8}
\]

But (7) is stronger for the present purpose: no second moment is assumed. If the second moment diverges, the right side is `+infinity`; if it is finite and the law is non-degenerate, it is strictly positive. In all cases

\[
\boxed{c(G)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G=m\ \text{almost surely}.}
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed `E(G-G')^2=0` forces two independent copies to coincide almost surely, which is possible only for a point-mass law.

The countable exceptional frequencies of an arithmetic renewal law cause no issue in (7). Their positive set is a reciprocal lattice and therefore has a positive first frequency; sufficiently close to zero, every nonzero `h` lies in the regular branch of (1).

## 3. A single renewal witness fails for every contraction below one

Suppose a single renewal profile satisfies

\[
\mu\le\nu_a
\qquad\text{on }(-1,1)
\tag{10}
\]

with `a<1`. The absolutely continuous part of the domination gives

\[
S(h)\le a|h|
\quad\text{for almost every }|h|<1.
\tag{11}
\]

Choose any sequence of regular points `h_n->0` on which (11) holds. Then (7) implies `c(G)=0`, hence the gap is deterministic. The process is a lattice of spacing `m=1/rho`, whose per-particle diffraction is

\[
\mu_\rho
=\rho\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\delta_{k\rho}.
\tag{12}
\]

The forward atom in (10) gives `rho<=a<1`, so the first nonzero Bragg peak is located at

\[
h=\rho\in(0,1).
\]

It has positive mass `rho`, whereas `nu_a` is atomless away from zero. This contradicts (10). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{every stationary renewal process in the stated class fails }\nu_a
\text{ for every }a<1.
}
\tag{13}
\]

This is a stronger single-process obstruction than the Montgomery--Taylor constant requires. The only reason convex mixtures need additional work is that averaging over a continuum of lattice scales can smear the nonzero Bragg peaks, exactly as in `ANF-022`.

## 4. Convexification cannot hide non-deterministic renewal gaps

Now let `theta` index a measurable family of renewal laws and let `pi` be a probability measure on that family. Write

\[
\mu_\theta
=
\rho_\theta\delta_0+\Pi_\theta+S_\theta(h)dh,
\]

with gap mean `m_theta=1/rho_theta`. Assume the barycenter

\[
\overline\mu=\int\mu_\theta\,d\pi(\theta)
\]

is locally finite and satisfies

\[
\overline\mu\le\nu_a.
\tag{14}
\]

The measure

\[
\left(\int S_\theta(h)d\pi(\theta)\right)dh
\]

is a nonnegative absolutely continuous submeasure of `overline mu`. Hence for almost every `0<h<1`,

\[
\overline S(h):=
\int S_\theta(h)d\pi(\theta)
\le ah.
\tag{15}
\]

Choose a sequence `h_n downarrow0` entirely inside this full-measure set. For every fixed `theta`, the argument of Section 2 applies along this sequence once `n` is large enough to avoid its possible first reciprocal frequency, so

\[
c_\theta
:=
\frac{\mathbb E(G_\theta-G_\theta')^2}{2m_\theta^2}
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}S_\theta(h_n).
\tag{16}
\]

All quantities are nonnegative. Applying Fatou once more, now in the mixture variable,

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
\le\int c_\theta\,d\pi(\theta)
&\le
\int\liminf_n S_\theta(h_n)d\pi(\theta)\\
&\le
\liminf_n\int S_\theta(h_n)d\pi(\theta)\\
&\le
\liminf_n ah_n
=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{c_\theta=0\quad\text{for }\pi\text{-almost every }\theta,}
\tag{18}
\]

so almost every component has deterministic gaps. Convexification can average lattice scales, but it cannot average away the infrared diffuse floor of any genuinely random renewal component: positivity prevents cancellation.

Consequently every putative renewal-mixture witness for (14) reduces exactly to a scale mixture of the lattice measures (12), with `pi` pushed forward by `theta -> rho_theta`.

## 5. ANF-022 gives a slightly stronger universal contraction threshold

`ANF-022` specialized its lattice-mixture dual certificate to `a=a_MT`, but the final algebra does not depend on that substitution. Retain its exact nonnegative dual function with

\[
B_{50}
:=\int_0^1\lambda(x)dx
=
\frac{1529210663723939914090488978899221253}
{4726118059146527924423342245547351250}
=0.323565904318542\ldots .
\tag{19}
\]

For a scale mixture dominated by `nu_a`, the same notation as `ANF-022` gives the scale deficit

\[
D=\int_0^1(1-\rho)w(\rho)d\rho.
\]

The forward-atom budget gives

\[
D\ge1-a,
\tag{20}
\]

while the harmonic-dilation constraint and the feasible dual certificate give, after normalizing `v=w/a`,

\[
\frac Da\le B_{50}.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence every dominated lattice-scale mixture must satisfy

\[
1-a\le aB_{50},
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
a\ge a_{50}:=\frac1{1+B_{50}}
=0.755534723837469\ldots .}
\tag{22}
\]

This is only the threshold certified by the particular `N=50` dual witness of `ANF-022`; it is not asserted to be optimal for lattice mixtures. It is nevertheless already stronger than needed:

\[
a_{\rm MT}
=0.753296067856070\ldots
<a_{50}.
\tag{23}
\]

Combining (18) and (22) proves the convex-renewal claim stated at the start.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty boundary

The renewal-process diffraction machinery is classical. Michael Baake and Holger Kösters, *Random point sets and their diffraction*, *Philosophical Magazine* 91 (2011), 2671--2679, DOI `10.1080/14786435.2010.513696`, arXiv:1007.3084, state the stationary renewal diffraction theorem under the mean-one and `1+epsilon`-moment hypotheses, derive the renewal Fourier relation, give the diffuse density equivalent to (1), and classify the non-arithmetic versus lattice-supported pure-point parts. Their paper in turn points to the more general treatment of Baake--Birkner--Moody.

For finite second moment, the appearance of the normalized gap variance as the zero-frequency renewal structure factor is standard renewal/number-variance behavior. The argument (5)--(9) is therefore not presented as a new hyperuniformity theorem. Its useful specialization here is that Fatou makes the rigidity one-sided and still decisive without assuming a second moment: any `O(|h|)` target forces the gap difference to vanish identically.

A targeted search across renewal diffraction, random tilings, iid-gap point processes, hyperuniformity, and structure-factor realizability did not locate the specific convex-mixture reduction (14)--(18) or its combination with the Montgomery--Taylor lattice-dilation certificate. No publication-level novelty claim is made. The durable Mathia content is the exact interface between the classical renewal formula and the `ANF-020` order interval, together with the inherited explicit threshold (22).

## 7. Evidence boundary and consequence for the scalar frontier

The `1+epsilon` moment hypothesis is retained because it is the hypothesis under which the cited renewal diffraction theorem guarantees the locally integrable diffuse density used in (1). The rigidity calculation itself needs only the first moment once that formula is available. This finding does **not** claim the same theorem for every finite-mean renewal law outside the source theorem's regularity class.

The convex result concerns barycenters of stationary renewal diffraction profiles. As with the negative point-process filters in `ANF-020`--`ANF-023`, no claim that every infinite-volume expected profile is automatically an element of the finite-configuration convex body `K` is needed: even granting the whole renewal family as candidate witnesses, the required band domination fails.

Most importantly, the iid-gap assumption is load-bearing. Correlated gaps can suppress long-wavelength number fluctuations through cancellation between successive spacings and are not constrained by (5) through a single characteristic function. Markov-renewal, Gibbsian, block-correlated, or other genuinely interacting spacing processes therefore remain viable candidate classes, as do direct nonstationary finite-cluster convexifications and separating-spectrum arguments.

The scalar search has now eliminated free-fermion DPPs (`ANF-020`), symplectic scale mixtures (`ANF-021`), scale-randomized crystals (`ANF-022`), iid site perturbations of a lattice (`ANF-023`), and iid **gap** disorder (this finding). The common lesson is increasingly sharp: the Montgomery--Taylor contraction cannot be obtained by independently randomizing a classical one-dimensional template. A surviving stochastic construction must encode correlations that are visible in the full support-one diffraction budget, not merely improve a local cusp or smear Bragg peaks.

The configuration-level escape of `ANF-006` remains outside this scalar diffraction duality and is unaffected.
