# ANF-123 — transverse interior-saddle mirrors pay an exponential unsigned source budget

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + PROFILE-TRANSVERSE-COST + FIXED-GAUSSIAN-BAND + SADDLE-LOCKING + EXPONENTIAL-UNSIGNED-SOURCE-BUDGET + RATE-SHARP-CONTROL`. `ANF-122` eliminates the most direct completion of a bounded interior-saddle packet: a positive translation cloud generated by the same `ANF-115` profile can anti-align almost perfectly at the saddle, but once it does so the whole cloud becomes source-negligible and is globally excisable. The live alternative was a profile-transverse companion. For the natural next class — translated packets from **different** `ANF-115` interior-saddle families — transverse mirroring has an explicit cost.

Fix a compact parameter interval

\[
\Gamma=[\gamma_-,\gamma_+]\Subset(0,\infty),
\]

and write `P_A^(gamma)` for the packet of `ANF-115`, with

\[
F_\gamma(\alpha)
=\alpha+2\gamma\log\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}{2},
\qquad
a_\gamma
=\frac2\pi\arctan\frac1{\pi\gamma},
\qquad
f_\gamma=F_\gamma(a_\gamma),
\]

and source energy `E_A^(gamma)=E_0(P_A^(gamma))`. Let the distinguished packet be

\[
P_A=P_A^{(\gamma_0)},
\qquad a_0=a_{\gamma_0},
\qquad \gamma_0\in\Gamma,
\]

inside a global Montgomery--Taylor near-extremizer

\[
W_A=P_A\sqcup R_A,
\qquad
\frac{E_0(W_A)}{L_A}=1+o(1),
\qquad
0<\lambda\le\frac{E_A^{(\gamma_0)}}{L_A}\le\Lambda<\infty.
\tag{1}
\]

There is a **fixed** number `S=S(Gamma,lambda,Lambda)>0` such that the Gaussian-width band

\[
B_A(S):=
\left\{\alpha\in[-1,1]:
\bigl||\alpha|-a_0\bigr|\le\frac S{\sqrt A}
\right\}
\tag{2}
\]

already forces a macroscopic amount of complement source energy:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E_{B_A(S)}(R_A)}{L_A}\ge c_0>0
}
\tag{3}
\]

for all sufficiently large `A`, where `c_0` depends only on the displayed compact data. Thus the external companion required by near-extremality is visible on a fixed `A^(-1/2)` saddle window; one does not need a widening mesoscopic band to detect a constant amount of it.

Now suppose the part of the remainder that carries this band energy is assembled from translated `ANF-115` packet families,

\[
C_A
=
\bigsqcup_{r=1}^{N_A}
 w_{r,A}\bigl(P_A^{(\gamma_{r,A})}+\tau_{r,A}\bigr),
\qquad
\gamma_{r,A}\in\Gamma,
\tag{4}
\]

with positive integer multiplicities `w_{r,A}` and arbitrary real translations. Allow an additional remainder `D_A` with

\[
R_A=C_A\sqcup D_A,
\qquad
E_{B_A(S)}(D_A)=o(L_A).
\tag{5}
\]

Define the nearest transverse saddle distance and the **unsigned packet source budget**

\[
d_A:=\min_r|a_{\gamma_{r,A}}-a_0|,
\qquad
\mathfrak U_A
:=
\left(
\sum_r w_{r,A}
\sqrt{\frac{E_A^{(\gamma_{r,A})}}{L_A}}
\right)^2.
\tag{6}
\]

The budget `mathfrak U_A` deliberately ignores cancellation between companion pieces. It is the source norm that would be available if their individual Hilbert norms were added before interference. Then there are constants `c_Gamma,C>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak U_A
\ge
C^{-1}
\exp\!\left[
 c_\Gamma A
\left(d_A-\frac S{\sqrt A}\right)_+^2
\right].
}
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
d_A
\le
\frac S{\sqrt A}
+
C\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+\mathfrak U_A)}A}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This gives the new completion dichotomy. A profile-transverse mirror with `log mathfrak U_A=o(A)` must have a saddle converging to the original one. If `mathfrak U_A<=A^b`, then

\[
\boxed{
d_A=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log A}{A}}\right),}
\tag{9}
\]

and if `mathfrak U_A=O(1)`, then

\[
\boxed{d_A=O(A^{-1/2}).}
\tag{10}
\]

Conversely, keeping every companion saddle a fixed distance `delta>0` from `a_0` requires

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak U_A\ge \exp(c_{\Gamma,\delta}A).
}
\tag{11}
\]

So a genuinely separated transverse mirror is possible only by hiding an **exponentially large unsigned source reservoir** behind further destructive interference. It is not a bounded-mass completion of the type left open after `ANF-122`; it creates a new cancellation cascade.

## 1. Near-extremality forces companion energy in one fixed Gaussian window

Write

\[
M_A=\frac{E_A^{(\gamma_0)}}{L_A},
\qquad
X_A=\frac{\Delta(R_A)}{L_A}\ge0.
\]

The exact affine polarization identity of `ANF-119` gives

\[
\frac{2\mathcal C_0(P_A,R_A)}{L_A}
=-(M_A+X_A)+o(1).
\tag{12}
\]

In particular the total source cross term has magnitude bounded below uniformly because `M_A>=lambda`. The same finding also gives

\[
\frac{E_0(R_A)}{L_A}
\le
\bigl(1+\sqrt{M_A}+o(1)\bigr)^2
\le C_R
\tag{13}
\]

for a fixed `C_R`.

The `ANF-118` Gaussian tail bound for the distinguished packet says

\[
\frac{E_{B_A(S)^c}(P_A)}{E_A^{(\gamma_0)}}
\le Ce^{-cS^2}
\tag{14}
\]

for every fixed sufficiently large `S` and all large `A`. Choose `S` once so that

\[
\sqrt{\Lambda C_RCe^{-cS^2}}<\frac\lambda8.
\tag{15}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz then gives

\[
\frac{|\mathcal C_{B_A(S)^c}(P_A,R_A)|}{L_A}
<\frac\lambda8.
\tag{16}
\]

while (12) gives, for large `A`,

\[
\frac{|\mathcal C_0(P_A,R_A)|}{L_A}
>\frac{3\lambda}{8}.
\tag{17}
\]

Hence the band cross term itself satisfies

\[
|\mathcal C_{B_A(S)}(P_A,R_A)|
\ge\frac\lambda4L_A.
\tag{18}
\]

A second Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, using

\[
E_{B_A(S)}(P_A)\le E_0(P_A)\le\Lambda L_A,
\]

proves (3), for example with any fixed `c_0<lambda^2/(16Lambda)` after increasing the starting value of `A`. This is a useful strengthening of the qualitative localization in `ANF-119`: the companion cannot spread all required cancellation into the tails of a widening window. A constant amount is already forced at the natural Gaussian resolution.

If (5) holds, the Hilbert triangle inequality in the restricted band gives

\[
\sqrt{E_{B_A(S)}(C_A)}
\ge
\sqrt{E_{B_A(S)}(R_A)}
-
\sqrt{E_{B_A(S)}(D_A)},
\]

so after reducing `c_0` by a fixed factor,

\[
\boxed{
E_{B_A(S)}(C_A)\ge c_1L_A
}
\tag{19}
\]

for some `c_1>0`.

## 2. A wrong-saddle packet has exponentially little source in that window

The decisive estimate is already encoded in the exact `ANF-115` exponent. For every `gamma in Gamma`,

\[
F_\gamma''(\alpha)
=-\frac{\gamma\pi^2}{2}
\sec^2\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}
\le
-\frac{\gamma_-\pi^2}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]

Since `F_gamma'(a_gamma)=0`, strong concavity gives the global quadratic bound

\[
\boxed{
F_\gamma(\alpha)
\le
f_\gamma
-
\frac{\gamma_-\pi^2}{4}
(\alpha-a_\gamma)^2.
}
\tag{21}
\]

The exact physical density formula of `ANF-115`, together with the uniformly bounded prefactor analysis used in `ANF-118`, is uniform when `gamma` ranges in the compact set `Gamma`. A lower Laplace bound on an `A^(-1/2)` neighborhood of `a_gamma` and (21) on the fixed band (2) therefore give constants `C_Gamma,c_Gamma>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E_{B_A(S)}(P_A^{(\gamma)})}
{E_A^{(\gamma)}}
\le
C_\Gamma
\exp\!\left[
-c_\Gamma A
\left(
|a_\gamma-a_0|-\frac S{\sqrt A}
\right)_+^2
\right].
}
\tag{22}
\]

Translations do not change this estimate because they multiply the exponential sum by a unimodular phase.

Apply Minkowski in the restricted Montgomery--Taylor Hilbert norm to (4). If all companion saddles are at distance at least `d_A`, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{\frac{E_{B_A(S)}(C_A)}{L_A}}
&\le
\sum_r w_{r,A}
\sqrt{\frac{E_{B_A(S)}(P_A^{(\gamma_{r,A})})}{L_A}}\\
&\le
C_\Gamma^{1/2}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{c_\Gamma A}{2}
\left(d_A-\frac S{\sqrt A}\right)_+^2
\right]
\sum_r w_{r,A}
\sqrt{\frac{E_A^{(\gamma_{r,A})}}{L_A}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Squaring (23) and combining it with (19) proves (7), hence (8)--(11).

The important point is that no assumption is made about cancellation **inside** `C_A`. Such cancellation can make its actual total source energy small, but it cannot make the unsigned budget in (6) disappear. If the companion uses saddles separated by a fixed amount, that hidden budget must itself be exponential.

## 3. The exponential cost has the exact Laplace rate

The quadratic exponent in (7) is a uniform compact-family bound. For one fixed transverse family `gamma_1!=gamma_0`, the exact large-deviation rate is sharper. Define

\[
I_{\gamma_1}(a_0)
:=
f_{\gamma_1}-F_{\gamma_1}(a_0)>0.
\tag{24}
\]

For every fixed `S>0`, the same upper/lower Laplace comparison gives

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{A\to\infty}
\frac1A
\log
\frac{E_{B_A(S)}(P_A^{(\gamma_1)})}
{E_A^{(\gamma_1)}}
=
-I_{\gamma_1}(a_0).
}
\tag{25}
\]

Moreover (21) implies

\[
I_{\gamma_1}(a_0)
\ge
\frac{\gamma_-\pi^2}{4}
|a_{\gamma_1}-a_0|^2.
\tag{26}
\]

Thus a fixed wrong-saddle packet whose own source energy is only `Theta(L_A)` needs multiplicity-squared of exponential rate at least `I_{gamma_1}(a_0)` merely to make `Theta(L_A)` source energy **available** in the base saddle band. The rate is not an artifact of Cauchy--Schwarz or of the compact quadratic bound: multiplying that packet by `exp((I_{gamma_1}(a_0)/2+o(1))A)` copies exactly compensates the band large-deviation exponent at the level of source availability.

This is only a matched rate control, not a construction of a full mirror. A translation can tune phase at the center of the band, but a different family also has a different mesoscopic amplitude profile. Therefore (25) shows that the exponential **budget exponent** is sharp; it does not claim that paying it is sufficient for the complete physical anti-alignment required by `ANF-119`.

## 4. Saddle locking is also parameter locking

The saddle map is explicit and nondegenerate:

\[
a_\gamma
=\frac2\pi\arctan\frac1{\pi\gamma},
\qquad
\boxed{
\frac{da_\gamma}{d\gamma}
=-\frac2{1+\pi^2\gamma^2}.
}
\tag{27}
\]

On the compact interval `Gamma`, saddle distance and parameter distance are therefore uniformly comparable. The polynomial-budget conclusion (9) can equivalently be written

\[
\min_r|\gamma_{r,A}-\gamma_0|
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log A}{A}}\right),
\tag{28}
\]

and bounded unsigned budget forces `O(A^(-1/2))` parameter locking.

This identifies the only low-budget transverse chamber not excluded by the present argument: **coalescing-saddle profiles**. At the `A^(-1/2)` parameter scale, two different `ANF-115` families can have saddle centers separated by only `O(1)` in the Gaussian coordinate `u=sqrt(A)(alpha-a_0)`. Their overlap is then order one, so fixed-separation large-deviation arguments cannot distinguish them. This is the natural next object for a two-profile Gaussian Gram analysis.

## 5. Scope, prior-art boundary, and the new live gate

The finding does not classify arbitrary physical complements. It applies when the part responsible for base-band cancellation is a positive multiset union of translated `ANF-115` packet families with parameters in one fixed compact subset of `(0,infinity)`, up to a remainder carrying `o(L_A)` energy in that band. It permits arbitrarily many companion packets, arbitrary translations, repeated sites, and arbitrarily severe cancellation among the companion pieces. The price of that generality is that the controlling quantity is the unsigned budget `mathfrak U_A`, not the actual source norm of `C_A`.

A targeted prior-art audit was made against classical Laplace/steepest-descent separation of saddles, uniform asymptotics for coalescing saddles in the Chester--Friedman--Ursell/Olver tradition, and Agmon-type exponentially small overlap estimates for separated semiclassical wells. Those theories supply broad analogues of the exponential localization in (22)--(25), but no result found in that audit combines it with the Montgomery--Taylor affine polarization identity to force the source-budget law (7). No external theorem is load-bearing: the proof uses the exact `ANF-115` packet exponent, the canonical `ANF-118` Gaussian tail, and `ANF-119` Hilbert bookkeeping, so `SOURCES.md` is unchanged.

Together with `ANF-122`, the completion frontier is now sharper. A bounded interior-saddle packet cannot be saved by a low-budget same-saddle mirror, because that aggregate is excisable; and it cannot be saved by a low-budget **separated** transverse `ANF-115` family, because the wrong saddle has exponentially little source in the required band. A surviving packetized completion must therefore either

1. use transverse families whose saddles coalesce with the base saddle at the Gaussian/moderate-deviation scale, or
2. hide an exponentially large unsigned source reservoir behind a further cancellation cascade.

The next useful test is the first branch: derive the joint mesoscopic Gram form for `gamma_A=gamma_0+kappa/sqrt(A)+o(A^(-1/2))` and decide whether such coalescing-saddle transverse profiles genuinely escape the same global-excision mechanism as `ANF-122`, or merely enlarge its finite-dimensional Gaussian mirror orbit.
