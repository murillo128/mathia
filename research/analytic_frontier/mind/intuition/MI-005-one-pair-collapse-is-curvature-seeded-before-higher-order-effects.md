# MI-005 — One-pair vertical geometry reduces to amplitude-normalized real multiplicity

**Evidence level:** exact through ANF-074, with classical positive-spectrum Gram geometry

## Core intuition

The six-point one-pair obstruction is no longer primarily a complex-height problem. ANF-072 shows that whenever a central notch creates a dangerous one-pair configuration, collapsing the conjugate pair to the real axis cannot improve the affine inequality: any counterexample would already appear in a real multiplicity configuration. The first unresolved information is therefore the multiplicity profile of a real Gram configuration, not the vertical placement of one complex pair.

The apparent failure of the minimum-slack central notch is also a normalization failure rather than a shape failure. ANF-073 gives an exact doubled-site obstruction for the fixed amplitude, while ANF-074 proves that after optimizing the amplitude the whole viable small-notch shape ray survives every configuration supported on at most two distinct real sites. The first open multiplicity geometry is three distinct real support sites.

## Strongest justified principle

ANF-070 already screens the complete base-profile six-point collapse-reversal branch by a positive affine floor. ANF-071 confines any notch-created reversal to a shrinking neighborhood of the collapsed real configuration. ANF-072 then proves that complex vertical displacement adds no new danger there: a violating one-pair configuration would force a violating collapsed real multiset.

For a profile `F`, ANF-073 isolates the exact two-site multiplicity gate through `D_2(F)=F(0)+2 inf F`. The minimum-elementary-slack normalization of the central notch fails this gate because the profile has a negative real value. ANF-074 solves the full positive-spectrum problem for all real multisets with at most two support sites: double/double is the strongest configuration, and amplitude reoptimization makes `M(F_s)/D_2(F_s)` strictly better than the Montgomery--Taylor target throughout the small viable notch-width range.

Thus the durable frontier is **amplitude-normalized real multiplicity with at least three distinct support sites**. Reopening complex height before that gate is solved would reintroduce a variable already shown not to be load-bearing for the first obstruction.

## What remains possible

Three-site real multiplicity can carry interactions that are invisible to the exact two-site positive-spectrum reduction. Higher cardinality may also expose the first one-pair higher-order reversal not seeded by negative curvature; the existing coefficient argument first loses automatic control at total cardinality eleven. Ordered or multi-pair information remains a genuinely different category and is not ruled out by the scalar real-multiplicity reductions.

Amplitude optimization must remain part of the admissible profile design. A fixed normalization that fails the doubled-site gate is not evidence that the underlying notch shape is unusable.

## Status / novelty

The Gram positivity, collapse comparison, and amplitude optimization are exact line-level results built from classical positive-definite kernel geometry. The durable synthesis is the reduction: **the first unresolved six-point notch mechanism is a three-support real-multiplicity problem after amplitude optimization, not a vertical one-pair problem.**

## Falsification criterion

Produce a one-pair central-notch counterexample whose collapsed real multiset satisfies the same affine inequality, invalidate the two-site positive-spectrum extremal reduction, or find an admissible small-notch shape for which amplitude optimization still fails a supported-on-at-most-two-sites configuration. Otherwise the next scalar gate should be attacked at three real support sites before complex height is reopened.
