## Control the endpoint Fejer scale-slope defect with source amplitude/placement information, not relative uniformity alone

**Linked intuition:** `MI-007-bow-variance-needs-source-specific-coherence-at-the-distinguished-twist`.

ANF-150--ANF-172 reduce bow variance to a phase-sensitive endpoint problem. Target-sized completion forces a large positive real dyadic shifted correlation, and that signed shell is exactly a dyadic defect in the scale slope of a positive endpoint Fejer family. A single moving-window energy is only a necessary shadow because phase orthogonality can make the energy critical while killing the real completion.

ANF-173 removes a range artifact but sharpens the obstruction. By raising the fixed Taylor degree, the available exact-twist discorrelation extends through the full endpoint horizon `X^(5/8+eta) <= H <= K`. Nevertheless arbitrary fixed-log savings, qualitative fixed-order Gowers uniformity, and bounded-test relative decorrelation can coexist with target-sized endpoint charge: a tiny carrier-aligned drift can be accumulated and then remembered by the endpoint primitive.

The live theorem must therefore control the exact Fejer scale-slope defect, or a source-faithful equivalent, at an **absolute power-scale tolerance**. A relative-uniformity-only route would need roughly `rho_X K=o(sqrt(X log X))`; otherwise it must use the actual prime/rough amplitude and placement law to rule out the adaptive small-amplitude charging mechanism. Extending the same fixed-order uniformity theorem to more interval lengths is no longer the missing step.
