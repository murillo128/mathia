# MI-048 — Cubic averaging loses the leading arithmetic fluctuation, but not the zeta-zero Mellin layer

**Evidence level:** exact continuation-kernel synthesis from [PL-401](../../findings/PL-401-cubic-transition-even-gap-gallagher-collapse.md) plus literature-backed/derived Mellin synthesis from [PL-402](../../findings/PL-402-singular-series-riesz-zero-layer-cubic-mellin.md). The Goldston--Suriajaya zero layer belongs to the classical pair singular-series transform; its transfer to the exact Prime-Lattice continuation statistic is not yet proved.

The parity mismatch of PL-400 is not permanent. Retaining the next incomplete-gamma transition phase gives a cubic rotation `exp(-i pi d^3/(6r))`, and at `d~r^(1/3)` the real even-gap channel returns at full `1/d` scale. PL-401 then shows that the first natural cubic-window average still uses only Gallagher's mean of the pair singular series. Its leading sine-integral limit is reproduced by the parity-only control `2*1_(2|d)`.

PL-402 shows that this leading collapse must not be overgeneralized. The classical Dirichlet transform of the same pair singular series contains the factor `1/zeta(2s+2)`, so every nontrivial zeta zero `rho` appears at

`s_rho = rho/2 - 1`.

For the Riesz means analyzed by Goldston--Suriajaya, normalization by the leading `x^(m+1)` scale makes the zero contribution `x^(rho/2-2)`. On RH the radial exponent is `-7/4`; a zero with real part `>1/2` produces a strictly larger power. This is an existing arithmetic explicit-formula mechanism, not a new spectral criterion generated by the lattice representation.

Crucially, the ideal cubic transition weight `g(u)=sin(pi u^3/6)/u` does not erase those pole locations. Its oscillatory Mellin transform

`g_hat(s)=(1/3)(pi/6)^(-(s-1)/3) Gamma((s-1)/3) sin(pi(s-1)/6)`

is nonzero at every `s_rho`, because the sine zeros are real and Gamma has no zeros. The cubic phase is therefore spectrally compatible with the classical zero layer even though its **leading** singular-series average classicalizes to the mean.

The unresolved gate is scale separation. The singular-series transform also has a deterministic lower layer at `s=0`, seen by the cubic kernel at order `H^-1`, while the RH-sensitive contribution starts at `H^-7/4`. The current PL-401 transition control aggregates only to `H^-1`, so it cannot resolve the zero residues. Exposing them requires renormalizing every larger deterministic term and improving the continuation remainder by roughly three quarters of a power, with a cutoff or other regulator that makes the Mellin argument rigorous.

**Boundary.** PL-402 reopens a linear fluctuation route only at the singular-series model level. It does not prove the unregularized discrete Mellin identity for the ideal cubic kernel, an averaged Hardy--Littlewood theorem for actual prime pairs, or an RH consequence for the exact continuation statistic. The correct distinction is now leading-mean universality versus a subleading zero-sensitive Mellin layer below the present error floor.
