# MI-024 — Fixed-window certification requires frequency-dependent tail control

**Evidence level:** mixed but sharply separated synthesis from [PL-328](../../findings/PL-328-certified-weil-positivity-beyond-unit-window.md). The `a=17/16` coercivity constant is a recent non-peer-reviewed computer-assisted literature claim whose public package has not been independently reproduced by Mathia; the tail-dropping obstruction recorded in the same finding is an exact structural argument. No RH consequence is claimed.

The localized Weil problem distinguishes two very different uses of a positive tail. A finite certificate may retain enough of the high-frequency complement to prove positivity on one fixed aperture, while a purported scalable localization can fail if that tail is replaced by a coarse scalar reserve. PL-328 places these two facts side by side at the first window where several prime powers are simultaneously active.

The reported certificate at `a=17/16` keeps the pole term, every active source `2,3,4,5,7,8`, the infinite-dimensional complement, cross-block control, and a positive rank-two tail contribution before reducing the finite compression to exact integer residual checks. Conditional on the external package being correct, this gives coercivity of the canonical completed Weil form at that fixed aperture and, by nested-domain monotonicity, at every smaller aperture. The result shows that activation of additional prime powers does **not** by itself force negativity.

The independent obstruction concerns a different operation. If the high-frequency remainder is replaced by the unit-window lower comparison, then once the `8` channel is available there are high-carrier tests whose continuous defect vanishes while the `log 8` arithmetic phase remains locked. The normalized comparison defect tends to

`1/16 - log(2)/(2 sqrt(2)) < -1/8`.

High-frequency modulation is weakly null, so every fixed bounded compact self-adjoint correction disappears on the same sequence. No fixed compact patch can therefore restore positivity of that **tail-dropped comparison** for every test.

The reusable point is that **a positive tail is not summarized by its smallest scalar floor when arithmetic losses are frequency selective**. A coarse floor can be numerically positive and still discard the directional information that offsets a resonant prime-power channel. Retaining a finite number of source atoms exactly is likewise not enough if the complementary positive sector is collapsed in the wrong topology.

This also changes how fixed-window certificates should be interpreted. The reported `17/16` result, if independently reproduced, would move the finite positivity frontier through six active prime powers, but the extremely small fixed lower constant and large fixed compression do not give an aperture-uniform mechanism. What matters for RH is not another isolated positive matrix but an argument whose positive compensation remains source-exact and frequency sensitive as new prime-power channels enter.

A scalable proposal should therefore expose, separately, the active arithmetic translations, the positive high-frequency remainder, the topology in which that remainder is retained, and the uniformity cost as the aperture grows. Replacing the tail by a scalar semibound is admissible only if a separate theorem shows that all destination-relevant resonances remain controlled after that compression.

**Boundary.** The numerical `17/16` coercivity statement remains at literature status until the frozen certificate is independently reproduced; it must not be used as proved Mathia evidence. The high-frequency obstruction rules out only the specified tail-dropping localization plus fixed bounded compact repair. It does not prove that the full Weil form becomes negative beyond any aperture, does not rule out other source-exact decompositions, and does not supply an aperture-uniform positivity theorem.
