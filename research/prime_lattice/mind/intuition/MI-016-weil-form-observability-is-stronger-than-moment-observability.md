# MI-016 — Actual localized-Weil eigenstates need source-specific regularity and source-specific sign

**Evidence level:** exact for the source-static all-log hierarchy and its generic boundaries through PL-292--PL-295, conditional on the PL-283--PL-284 finite spectral certificate at `a=0.8` where that endpoint is invoked. PL-295 proves that `H^log` or `H^s`, `s<=1/2`, continuity cannot protect sign by topology alone, and that eventual positivity strong enough to help already contains the missing ground-sign theorem. Positive-order regularity and fixed sign of the actual selected ground state remain open.

PL-285--PL-290 identify generic regularity ceilings for the localized Weil form and graph domains. PL-291--PL-292 show that actual source-static eigenstates are much smaller: they lie in every finite logarithmic Fourier scale, with an optimized translation modulus faster than every fixed inverse-log power but still slower than every Hölder power. Generic cutoff costs prevent this all-log bootstrap from automatically becoming positive-order Sobolev regularity.

PL-293 identifies the sharp universal boundary obstruction. The optimal zero-exterior Dirichlet logarithmic-Laplacian profile has amplitude `ell(t)^(1/2)`, and a nonvanishing channel at that scale excludes `H^s` for every `s>=1/2`. Thus a routine first-frequency-moment differentiation route cannot come from the principal operator alone.

PL-294 separately closes ordinary semigroup positivity at the certified aperture: the exact Beurling--Deny window ends at `a_BD=0.140599787...`, before the first prime-power translation. Failure of this criterion does not say that the actual arithmetic ground state changes sign.

PL-295 now rules out two generic ways of bridging that gap. First, fixed sign is **not open** in `H^log`, nor in any `H^s` with `0<=s<=1/2`: even parity-preserving, norm-preserving boundary perturbations can vanish in those topologies while creating a negative layer. Therefore obtaining subcritical Sobolev continuity may improve the moving-translation modulus, but it cannot by itself continue the ground-state sign.

Second, eventual or asymptotic positivity is not a weaker substitute. For a simple isolated ground state with a gap, the shifted semigroup converges in operator norm to the rank-one ground projection. If positive data become asymptotically positive, that projection must be positive, which is equivalent to the ground vector having fixed sign. An eventual-positivity theorem strong enough for the intended use therefore already proves the missing sign property.

The two open resources must be kept separate. A source-specific `H^s`, `0<s<1/2`, gain can still be valuable for non-absolute translation differentiation; it just does not solve sign. The sign gate needs information from the **actual rational-prime eigen-equation**: a boundary-weighted lower profile, a source-specific oscillation/maximum principle, an absolute-value defect identity, or another order mechanism unavailable to matched generic operators. A sufficiently strong supercritical or weighted topology could also change the topological argument, but would itself require new source structure.

**Boundary.** PL-295 is a method obstruction, not a sign-change theorem. Its perturbations are not eigenfunctions, and no claim is made that the selected `a=0.8` eigenbranch lacks positive-order regularity.
