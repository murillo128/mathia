# MI-044 — Zero-faithful continuation can be invisible to both `B^2` and fixed-`N` Jessen means

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-aware synthesis from PL-379--[PL-393](../../findings/PL-393-jessen-mean-forgets-euler-maclaurin-tail.md), including [PL-391](../../findings/PL-391-finite-jessen-half-line-density-is-not-rh.md) and [PL-392](../../findings/PL-392-euler-maclaurin-renormalization-besicovitch-null.md). No RH conclusion is claimed.

PL-392 gives the matched pair: `Q_N(s)=P_N(s)+N^(1-s)/(s-1)` converges locally uniformly to zeta on `Re(s)>0` away from the pole and is therefore fixed-zero faithful, while the Euler--Maclaurin tail is `B^2`-null on every fixed interior vertical line. Thus the corrected representative and the raw Dirichlet polynomial are identical in the native mean-square quotient.

PL-393 shows that moving from quadratic mean to the standard nonlinear two-sided Jessen logarithmic mean does not repair this at fixed `N`. For every `0<sigma<1`, the height-decaying continuation tail tends locally uniformly to zero under large translations, so the hull limits of `Q_N(sigma+it)` are the same as those of `P_N`. Consequently their infinite-height means of `log|.|` are equal even though only `Q_N` is the continuation-faithful approximant.

The obstruction is therefore broader than linear/quadratic `B^2`: any fixed-`N`, infinite-height observable determined only by the translation hull can forget a continuation correction lying in `C_0` of height. A viable zero-sensitive representation must retain the distinguished continuation representative in a way that is not quotiented out by large-height translation. Coupled limits `N=N(T)`, finite-height/local zero statistics, or another representative-sensitive analytic category remain logically open.

**Boundary.** PL-393 does not say Jessen theory is irrelevant and does not cover coupled cutoff/height limits. It says the ordinary fixed-`N` two-sided Jessen mean cannot distinguish exactly the Euler--Maclaurin tail that restores local-uniform zeta continuation.
