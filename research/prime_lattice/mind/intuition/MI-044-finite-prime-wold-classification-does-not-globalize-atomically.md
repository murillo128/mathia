# MI-044 — Completed continuation turns the sharp cutoff collapse into a near-diagonal saddle--transition layer

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-aware synthesis from PL-379--[PL-396](../../findings/PL-396-smooth-transition-reopens-fixed-gap-pairs.md), including PL-391--PL-395. No RH conclusion is claimed.

PL-392--PL-393 give the first representation warning: Euler--Maclaurin continuation can be fixed-zero faithful while disappearing in both the native `B^2` quotient and the fixed-`N` Jessen mean. PL-394 then finds a continuation-sensitive reflected/Gamma-phase cross channel with nominal pair saddle `t approx 2 pi nm`, while PL-395 restores the sharp moving Riemann--Siegel support and shows that every off-diagonal pair reaches that saddle before the larger term is admitted. In the hard main sum, active interior stationary geometry therefore collapses to the diagonal boundary.

PL-396 shows that this sharp-support exclusion is not invariant under exact analytic continuation. In the absolutely convergent Paris--Cang representation, each Dirichlet mode is weighted by a normalized incomplete gamma function rather than a hard indicator. At the nominal saddle of a fixed-gap pair `q=r+d`, the two transition weights approach complementary error-function constants of order one. Thus the larger mode is not absent there even though the corresponding sharp main-sum term has not switched on.

The scale match is decisive. For fixed `d`, the sharp activation offset `2 pi q^2-2 pi qr`, the incomplete-gamma transition width and the quadratic stationary-phase width are all `Theta(r)`. Phase and amplitude therefore have to be treated together in one uniform saddle--transition problem; freezing the amplitude or interpreting the hard cutoff as an intrinsic event changes the completed observable.

The escape is narrow, not a revival of the broad stationary shell. If `q/r` stays separated from one, the larger incomplete-gamma weight tends to zero. The order-one regime is the shrinking layer `log(q/r)=O(t^(-1/2))`, including fixed additive gaps. In prime-exponent coordinates this is only a thin slab in the same scalar log-energy projection already present throughout the line.

That last point is the matched-control gate. The fixed-gap calculation uses `log n`, the conductor and the archimedean smoothing kernel, but no genuinely multidimensional feature of the prime factor vector. An abstract energy-labelled system with the same scalar spectrum reproduces the local transition. Prime-lattice leverage must therefore come from a completed quantity that retains factor labels, a nonquadratic/cross-level coupling, or another source-specific structure after the uniform transition is assembled—not from the existence of the layer itself.

**Boundary.** PL-396 proves neither a nonzero completed off-diagonal contribution nor a zero-localizing mechanism. Complex transition weights and the explicit archimedean term can alter or cancel naive saddle contributions. The durable correction to PL-395 is only that hard-support diagonal collapse is chart-dependent, while the representation-faithful surviving problem is a near-diagonal coalescing saddle/transition layer whose arithmetic content remains to be established.
