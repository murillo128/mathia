# MI-044 — Zero-faithful continuation survives the quotient averages, but the moving Riemann–Siegel cutoff collapses the broad quadratic saddle to the diagonal boundary

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-aware synthesis from PL-379--[PL-395](../../findings/PL-395-riemann-siegel-moving-cutoff-diagonal-stationary-boundary.md), including [PL-391](../../findings/PL-391-finite-jessen-half-line-density-is-not-rh.md), [PL-392](../../findings/PL-392-euler-maclaurin-renormalization-besicovitch-null.md), [PL-393](../../findings/PL-393-jessen-mean-forgets-euler-maclaurin-tail.md), and [PL-394](../../findings/PL-394-critical-conductor-functional-equation-shell.md). No RH conclusion is claimed.

PL-392 gives the matched pair: `Q_N(s)=P_N(s)+N^(1-s)/(s-1)` converges locally uniformly to zeta on `Re(s)>0` away from the pole and is therefore fixed-zero faithful, while the Euler--Maclaurin tail is `B^2`-null on every fixed interior vertical line. PL-393 shows that the standard fixed-`N` two-sided Jessen logarithmic mean also forgets this tail because corrected and uncorrected representatives have the same large-translation hull. Continuation information can therefore be decisive for zeros while disappearing in the native quotient averages.

PL-394 tests the canonical coupled-height one-sided escape. For `N asymp sqrt(T)`, the frozen positive Dirichlet packet still has Gram `I+O(T^(-1/2))`, so merely growing the same-sign cutoff at the conductor does not recover continuation. The balanced approximate functional equation does retain additional structure: a reflected negative packet and the archimedean phase `chi(1/2+it)=e^(-2i theta(t))`. If the cutoff is frozen while differentiating pair phases, its quadratic cross channel has nominal stationary condition `nm approx t/(2 pi)`.

PL-395 restores the actual moving Riemann--Siegel support and shows that this broad stationary shell is not an active interior saddle. A fixed pair `(n,m)` enters only at `t_on=2 pi max(n,m)^2`, while its nominal saddle is `t_nm=2 pi nm+O((nm)^(-1))`. For `n!=m`, the saddle lies before activation; after activation the phase derivative is already positive. Only `n=m` has its saddle at the moving support boundary, `t=2 pi n^2+O(n^(-2))`. Thus the scalar quadratic cross channel reduces its active stationary geometry to the diagonal/perfect-square boundary.

The finite convolution identity `A_M(t)^2=sum_{k<=M^2} d_M(k) k^(-1/2-it)` exposes a second compression: after scalar pair summation, `(v(n),v(m))` is pushed to `v(n)+v(m)` plus the truncated divisor multiplicity. The nominal product saddle sits at the upper product boundary, and the actual stationary fiber asymptotically reduces to the square factorization. The broad two-body sum-energy shell from PL-394 is therefore a useful representation diagnostic but not a surviving bulk stationary resource once canonical support is restored.

What remains genuinely live is narrower: near-diagonal cutoff activation where `log(q/r)` is small, an observable that keeps ordered factor labels rather than quotienting through the product, a nonquadratic continuation interaction, or a global/argument-principle coupling that is individually zero-sensitive. Each must still retain the reflected/Gamma-phase continuation information and beat the matched controls; none follows merely from the existence of the diagonal boundary saddle.

**Boundary.** PL-395 excludes high-energy off-diagonal interior stationary points for the canonical moving quadratic cross channel, not all off-diagonal contributions. Nonstationary integrals, transition-layer/endpoint terms and near-diagonal activation remain. The product quotient applies only after scalar pair summation, and the Riemann--Siegel main sum is an asymptotic representation. The result narrows the continuation carrier; it does not localize all zeta zeros on the critical line.
