# MI-014 — Prime exchangeability cannot substitute for a rational-prime source relation

**Evidence level:** exact normal-state, de Finetti-completion, and finite-color symmetry no-go results through PL-235

## Core intuition

Treating infinitely many prime coordinates as interchangeable is not a neutral way to create all-prime coupling. On the finite-support integer representation it destroys non-vacuum normal equilibrium; on the ordinary quasi-local completion it leaves only exchangeable product-mixture structure. A fixed finite arithmetic coloring does not cure the problem because every color still contains an infinite symmetry orbit.

## Strongest justified principle

PL-233 removes additivity from the Gibbs obstruction: every positive trace-class operator invariant under all finite prime permutations is vacuum-supported. PL-234 shows that passing to the standard infinite tensor completion changes normality but not source specificity: Størmer/de Finetti makes every symmetric state a mixture of identical product states. PL-235 generalizes the normal orbit argument to within-color symmetry and applies it to fixed congruence and Frobenius labels.

## Program consequence

A viable thermodynamic/operator route must break exchangeability with sufficiently fine **source-forced** rational-prime data before equilibrium or quotienting. The burden is then to prove that the retained relation, not a freely chosen state, causes the desired cross-prime effect.

## Counterevidence / boundary

The no-go does not cover scale-dependent colorings whose classes refine with observation scale, nonlocal arithmetic algebras whose defining relations already couple primes, or other representations not equivalent to the ordinary tensor completion.

## Epistemic status

**Exact category boundary for full and fixed-finite-color exchangeability; no claim is made against source-forced symmetry breaking.**

## Falsification criterion

Produce a non-vacuum positive normal state on the finite-support integer representation invariant under an infinite prime-permutation block, or an extreme symmetric state in the ordinary infinite tensor product that is not an identical product state. A positive escape should instead identify the source relation that breaks those symmetries.
