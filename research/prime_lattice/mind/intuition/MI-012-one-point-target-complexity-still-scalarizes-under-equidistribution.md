# MI-012 — One-point target complexity still scalarizes when an equidistribution theorem sees it

**Evidence level:** literature-backed exact reductions through PL-185

## Core intuition

A target can be rough, discontinuous, or explicitly arithmetic and still carry no useful relational information if the final observable samples only one moving prime and a classical equidistribution theorem averages that target to a scalar. The relevant discriminator is not visual or combinatorial complexity of the weight, but whether the target changes the information category seen by the averaging theorem.

## Strongest justified principle

PL-184 replaces the Lipschitz hypothesis of PL-183 by bounded variation. Below the short-interval resolution budget, arbitrary jumps and step masks remain continuum-density weights; their local complexity is paid exactly through total variation. PL-185 then treats a class with enormous real-variable variation that lies outside that theorem: bounded residue-class labels for polylogarithmic moduli. Siegel--Walfisz annihilates every centered residue pattern, leaving only the reduced-residue mean multiplied by the same one-point continuum phase profile.

The two mechanisms are different but their information consequence agrees. Smoothness is not the fundamental issue. **Whenever the target belongs to a class for which the rational primes satisfy a sufficiently uniform one-point equidistribution theorem, the affine Kronecker average remembers at most the corresponding coarse scalar mean.** A nonzero local congruence bias is therefore not an RH-sensitive survivor.

## What remains possible

Factorization conditions such as Möbius/Liouville or large-prime-factor data of `q+h`, growing moduli beyond the available pointwise progression theorem, joint conditions on several moving primes, sparse source-forced correlations, and nonlocal/completed operators remain outside the present scalarization results. They become interesting only if the final carrier preserves their relation rather than averaging them back to a one-point law.

## Status / novelty

Short-interval prime counting, bounded-variation quadrature, Siegel--Walfisz, partial summation, and Dirichlet-character orthogonality are classical inputs. The durable synthesis is the boundary: **one-point target complexity is not arithmetic survival; a target must outrun the applicable equidistribution quotient or change the observable to a genuinely relational one**.

## Falsification criterion

Produce an admissible bounded-variation target violating PL-184's continuum limit, or a polylogarithmic residue target in PL-185's phase window whose centered contribution does not vanish. Alternatively, prove that one of the currently excluded factorization/joint target classes is already determined by the same one-point density data.
