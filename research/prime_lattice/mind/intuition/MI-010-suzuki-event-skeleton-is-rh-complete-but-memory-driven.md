# MI-010 — Suzuki's completed scalar is RH-complete on very thin observation sets, but the mechanism remains cumulative arithmetic memory

**Evidence level:** supported by PL-150, PL-153--PL-157; PL-154 is current evidence after adversarial acceptance

## Core intuition

Suzuki's completed scalar can be sampled on prime-power events without losing the RH sign problem, but even that observation set is far richer than necessary for asymptotic boundedness and growth. The completed state itself carries the zero-sensitive information; once it has been formed, both a fixed two-prime face and broad exogenous meshes can be sufficient observation domains for different RH-equivalent readouts.

The event/basis geometry is therefore an exact **carrier of a completed criterion**, not yet a generative mechanism. The load-bearing resource is cumulative rational-prime/global-completion history before sampling, not terminal recovery shape, sampling density, or the dimensionality of the observation set.

## Strongest justified principle

PL-150 shows that the terminal recovery drawdown after a prime-power event vanishes in the stated unconditional regime. PL-153 proves that either one-sided boundedness of the completed prime-power checkpoint sequence is already equivalent to RH; failure of RH makes both one-sided tails unbounded.

PL-154, now adversarially accepted, sharpens the state variable further: the one-sided exponential growth exponents of the completed scalar recover the rightmost-zero frontier `Theta-1/2`. Thus the checkpoint carrier records not only the RH dichotomy but the horizontal zero frontier.

PL-155 shows that completed screw positivity can be tested on any fixed two-prime exponent face. Because the difference group generated by `log p` and `log q` is dense, positivity there extends by continuity to the full real screw kernel. This is a generic dense-subgroup sampling fact once the completed scalar kernel already exists; it does not make two prime coordinates a source mechanism.

PL-156 shows that the canonical ordinary-prime basis is an RH-complete tail sampling set for the completed scalar, with chord interpolation errors tending to zero. PL-157 supplies the decisive matched control: any exogenous mesh with gaps `O(x^kappa)`, `kappa<3/4`, has the same one-sided boundedness and growth-exponent information, including simple non-prime meshes such as `x_j=j^3`. Ordinary-prime basis sufficiency is therefore a mesh property **after completion**, not evidence that the prime basis generates the zero selector.

## What remains possible

A positive prime-lattice mechanism must act upstream of these sampling reductions: on the cumulative forcing/history, on a non-scalar structure, or on a source-specific relation that fails for generic dense subgroups and cubic meshes. Prime-power checkpoints remain useful for exact finite sign certification, where an `o(1)` interpolation error is insufficient, but asymptotic sampling completeness itself should no longer be treated as explanatory.

## Status / novelty

Suzuki's completed criteria are literature-backed; the event, growth, face-sampling, and mesh reductions are persisted findings. The synthesis is the mechanistic separation: **after scalar completion, surprisingly small or non-prime observation sets can remain RH-complete, so observation-set rigidity is not the missing arithmetic polarization**.

## Falsification criterion

Derive a one-sided or growth bound from source data before the completed scalar criterion is assumed, or exhibit a source-forced observation invariant that distinguishes the rational-prime lattice from the PL-155 dense-face and PL-157 non-prime mesh controls while still carrying the required zero information.

## Lean-formalizable core

- Prime-power checkpoint one-sided boundedness.
- Rightmost-zero growth exponent.
- Dense two-prime-face extension by continuity.
- Sub-`3/4` mesh chord interpolation and transfer of one-sided bounds.
