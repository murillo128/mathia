# MI-015 — Mixed-prime reciprocity is genuine source structure, but principal zeta selection must preserve it rather than project it away

**Evidence level:** literature-backed exact reciprocity, multiple-Dirichlet-series, and principal-residue boundaries through PL-242

## Core intuition

Prime Lattice has now found a nonempty class of genuinely source-forced mixed-prime relations: quadratic reciprocity couples actual rational-prime labels in a way that is not reducible to exchangeability, abstract order, a fixed congruence coloring, or one-dimensional log geometry. That alone is not a zeta mechanism.

The decisive new gate is **relation survival under principal-channel extraction**. The canonical analytic completion of reciprocity is family-valued, and the cleanest residue that really isolates the Riemann zeta divisor does so precisely by collapsing the nontrivial quadratic family to its principal squarefree kernel. Recovering zeta after erasing the richer relation supplies no new localization theorem.

## Strongest justified principle

PL-240 attaches the classical Rédei matrix to every finite squarefree prime support. Its extra nullity is exactly the 4-rank of the corresponding narrow quadratic class group. This is authentic mixed-prime arithmetic and genuinely escapes the earlier fixed-label and generic-order no-go hypotheses, but each row naturally belongs to a varying quadratic Dirichlet character and hence to a family of quadratic `L`-functions.

PL-241 identifies the established family completion in quadratic double/multiple Dirichlet series. Quadratic reciprocity exchanges Dirichlet variables and Weyl-type functional equations yield multivariable continuation with an explicit principal zeta correction. Yet the affine zeta argument is moved among correction factors, its Weyl self-dual locus is not the Riemann `1/2` line, and even the displayed zeta pole can be canceled/repackaged in the global continuation. The visible factor is not an invariant zero divisor.

PL-242 tests the most direct residue escape exactly. On Blomer's `s=1` polar face, the residue is `(1/2) zeta_2(2w)`, so the Riemann zero divisor genuinely survives after a scalar rescaling. But the pole comes only from squarefree kernel `d_0=1`: every nontrivial quadratic character carrying the reciprocity-family information is holomorphic there and contributes nothing to the residue. The extracted zeta channel is therefore the principal square/imprimitive sector, not a mixed-prime rigidity theorem.

## Program consequence

A successful principal-channel construction must satisfy two conditions simultaneously: the Riemann zero divisor must be an invariant of the final object, and a nontrivial source-forced mixed-prime relation must remain present **after** the projection/residue/quotient that isolates that divisor. The retained relation must then do mathematical work—positivity, self-adjoint spectral restriction, trace identity, or another independently proved localization principle.

Merely generating a quadratic `L`-family, exhibiting a zeta correction factor, or taking a residue equal to `zeta(affine variable)` is now a control.

## Counterevidence / boundary

PL-242 closes only the canonical `s=1` principal residue in the tested rank-two quadratic model. Other polar faces, periods, quotients, operator-valued lifts, higher-rank multiple Dirichlet series, or nonquadratic source relations may retain nontrivial arithmetic while isolating a principal channel. Their existence and usefulness are not established.

## Epistemic status

**Exact source-relation and principal-projection boundary with classical prior-art identification; no zeta localization beyond scalar RH equivalence is established.**

## Falsification criterion

Find nontrivial reciprocity-family data surviving the PL-242 `s=1` residue, or show that the PL-241 Weyl geometry already makes the Riemann divisor invariant with the correct critical symmetry. A positive continuation should instead exhibit a source-derived principal projection that demonstrably retains nontrivial mixed-prime structure and converts it into an independent localization theorem.
