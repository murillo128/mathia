# MI-004 — Prime-shift symmetry is scaffolding; a fixed resolvent edge is either programmable or trivial

**Evidence level:** exact gauge/programmability results, singular dense-range controls, exact range obstruction, and two-parameter resolvent-family rigidity through PL-206

## Core intuition

The multiplicative prime action is canonical on the exponent lattice, but exact covariance is much easier to manufacture than arithmetic rigidity. Flat scalar, unitary, nonunitary invertible, and operator-valued edge fields can be gauged away or used to program arbitrary spectral data. PL-203 shows that merely dropping bounded invertibility does not fix this: an injective dense-range non-surjective tail can be appended while an arbitrary spectral field remains a reducing summand.

PL-205--PL-206 sharpen the category boundary. At one spectral parameter, bounded exact self-adjoint resolvent covariance forces dense edge range but can still admit nontrivial dense-range singular transport. Across a genuine resolvent family, however, the same bounded left edge cannot remain nontrivial: exact same-parameter transport at two distinct spectral points forces the edge to be `I` and the neighboring generators to coincide. A meaningful prime resolvent action must therefore **move or depend on the spectral parameter**, or leave this fixed-edge architecture altogether.

## Strongest justified principle

PL-193--PL-197 close exact scalar/unitary flatness by gauge trivialization and show explicit programmability of vertex spectral fields. PL-201--PL-202 extend that collapse to nowhere-zero scalar nonunitary and invertible operator-valued weights.

PL-203 constructs a bounded flat edge family that is injective, dense-range, non-surjective, and not boundedly invertible while exact resolvent covariance still carries arbitrary self-adjoint data on a reducing summand. Hence proper dense range alone is non-rigid. PL-205 gives the complementary impossibility theorem: from one exact fiberwise covariance identity between self-adjoint resolvents, every bounded edge range is dense, so closed-range/cokernel/isometric unilateral defects are impossible.

PL-206 then distinguishes single-point factorization from resolvent-family transport. If one bounded `A` satisfies `R_1(z)=A R_0(z)` and `R_1(w)=A R_0(w)` at two distinct common resolvent points, Hilbert's resolvent identity forces `A=I` and `H_1=H_0`. If the transfer is allowed to depend on `z`, its defect `A(z)-I` obeys a forced resolvent propagation law; if the prime operation is an additive energy shift such as `log p`, the natural covariance moves the spectral parameter instead of using a fixed left multiplier.

## What remains possible

A spectral-parameter-dependent transfer whose dependence is source-forced, a canonical parameter shift, an unbounded/domain-changing relation, approximate or compact/Schatten covariance, a target-relative non-fiberwise compression, genuinely noncommuting source actions, or another source-forced singular relation remains outside the fixed-edge results.

Each is only a category exit. It must survive a programmability audit and then supply a replacement sign/coercivity theorem tied to rational-prime/global-completion data.

## Epistemic status

**Exact collapse of flat invertible and generic dense-range single-point covariance, plus exact triviality of parameter-independent same-parameter transport across two resolvent points.** Arithmetic rigidity remains open only in category exits that carry additional source structure.

## Falsification criterion

Construct a nonidentity bounded fixed edge transporting the same two closed operators' resolvents at two distinct common spectral parameters, or invalidate PL-203's programmable dense-range single-point construction. A positive escape must instead make spectral-parameter dependence, parameter motion, domain structure, approximation defect, or noncommutation both source-forced and target-coercive.
