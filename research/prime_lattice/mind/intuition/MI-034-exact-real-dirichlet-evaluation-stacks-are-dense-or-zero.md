# MI-034 — Exact positive-real Dirichlet evaluation stacks are dense or zero

**Evidence level:** exact synthesis from [PL-353](../../findings/PL-353-finite-coefficient-constraints-dual-interpolation-gate.md) and [PL-354](../../findings/PL-354-real-dirichlet-evaluation-stack-dense-or-zero.md). The identity theorem/dominant-term argument for finite Dirichlet polynomials is classical; the Mathia content is its interaction with the local Sobolev closure gate.

Exact positive-real evaluations do not produce a gradual hierarchy of proper closed Prime-Lattice test families. On the finite-prime algebraic span, any finite collection of relations

`L_sigma(f)=0`

falls under the PL-353 finite-functional gate. Unless a nonzero combination extends to a continuous dual trace of the local form topology, its joint kernel remains dense. The natural exact real-evaluation weights therefore remove no closed directions at any finite stage.

The infinite exact stack behaves in the opposite extreme. A nonzero finite Dirichlet polynomial cannot vanish at infinitely many distinct positive real points: if the points are unbounded, the least exponent dominates; if they have a finite accumulation point, analyticity gives the same conclusion. Hence the common algebraic kernel of an infinite distinct positive-real evaluation stack is `{0}`.

The literal exact stack therefore jumps from **topologically invisible** to **algebraically annihilating**. Letting the number of exact evaluations grow with aperture does not by itself create an intermediate object either: at each finite aperture the constraint remains locally dense, while imposing the full infinite stack at one fixed algebraic stage leaves only zero.

This rules out a common repair strategy for the complete modulation family. An intermediate arithmetic restriction cannot be obtained merely by accumulating more exact real Dirichlet equalities. The relation has to change category: for example an approximate/asymptotic infinite law in a completed coefficient topology, a genuine continuous dual trace, a nonlinear or positivity constraint, or a cross-aperture condition whose content is not equivalent to exact vanishing of one finite Dirichlet polynomial at more and more points.

**Boundary.** The dichotomy concerns exact positive-real evaluation constraints on the finite-prime algebraic span and its local subcritical completion. It does not classify approximate evaluation laws, infinite-support completed coefficient vectors, nonlinear conditions, other ambient topologies or cross-aperture source relations, and it does not itself give an RH criterion.
