# Prime-lattice research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Prime-Lattice intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Find an intermediate modulation family between one Riesz channel and form-core completeness

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-016-weil-form-observability-is-stronger-than-moment-observability` through `MI-033-finite-coefficient-constraints-survive-only-as-continuous-dual-traces`.

PL-345--PL-347 classify the two obvious extremes of the endpoint state family. The centered same-state channel is the classical first logarithmic Riesz criterion, while the complete Fourier-modulation Gram family is a form core for the localized completed Weil form and therefore reconstructs ordinary Weil positivity. One scalar channel is too compressed; the complete family is exactly the old problem in coordinates.

PL-348 strengthens the completeness side in the arithmetic coordinates themselves: prime-log modulations already form a form core. PL-349 then gives the complementary Beurling--Malliavin interpretation. A frequency family with insufficient logarithmic density has only a finite form-core radius; the obstruction is quantitative rather than merely cardinal.

PL-350--PL-352 identify how fragile that sparse obstruction is under multiplicative closure. Once the modulation family is closed under the natural multiplicative combinations, the Beurling--Malliavin deficit collapses. A family supported on finitely many prime axes has a finite completeness budget, but freeing even one additional prime coordinate can already destroy that finite-axis barrier and restore arbitrarily large reach.

PL-353 now closes the simplest coefficient-coupling repair. For the prime-log exponential span in the subcritical local Sobolev topology, a finite coefficient relation survives closure **if and only if** its coefficient weights are samples of a continuous dual Paley--Wiener/Sobolev functional. Finite centering, finitely many logarithmic moments, finite real-phase jets and finite `p^(-sigma)` pole-cancellation relations fail this gate: their joint kernels are dense and, after multiplication by the endpoint envelope, still form a core for the full localized Weil form.

The live problem is therefore narrower than “find a sparse basis” or “add coefficient constraints.” A useful family must be arithmetically source-forced and closed enough to carry a nontrivial zero-sensitive relation while avoiding the immediate completeness collapse caused by unrestricted multiplicative generation. If the proposal uses finitely many linear coefficient laws, the first exact test is now whether some nonzero combination belongs to the continuous dual trace class of the actual local form topology. Otherwise the relation disappears under closure.

Viable escapes must therefore supply a genuine source-forced continuous dual trace, an infinite coherent relation, a nonlinear/positivity/target-relative constraint, a cross-aperture coupling, or a constrained semigroup/word family whose effective Beurling--Malliavin budget grows enough to force a destination sign before it becomes a full form core.

## Treat Beurling--Malliavin radius as a sufficiency budget, not a new RH criterion

The density results do not themselves prove positivity. They measure how far a restricted modulation family can test the localized form before Fourier completeness becomes available. Finite prime-axis families have a genuine finite budget; multiplicative closure can erase it; one free prime coordinate already shows that nominal sparsity in the generating set is not the same as sparse information after closure; and PL-353 shows that nominal finite codimension in coefficient space is not the same as a closed restriction in the destination topology.

A proposed intermediate family should therefore state its closure law, effective log-frequency density, form-core radius, coefficient-topology gate when relevant, and destination theorem together. If its closure is dense enough to reconstruct the whole localized Weil form, the route has returned to the classical criterion. If its Beurling--Malliavin radius stays bounded, it must explain why that bounded testing window is nevertheless arithmetically sufficient. If a finite coefficient relation is discontinuous in the form topology, it should be treated as absent after completion rather than as retained arithmetic structure.

## Keep centering, explicit-formula identity and family closure distinct

Raw endpoint recurrence is PNT-universal; centering introduces the classical zero expansion; same-state completion gives the Riesz criterion; complete modulation gives localized Weil positivity; prime-log form-core results and Beurling--Malliavin density quantify where restricted families cross between those extremes; and PL-353 identifies which finite coefficient hyperplanes survive that closure at all. The next Prime-Lattice result must live genuinely between the endpoints rather than improving the bookkeeping of either one.
