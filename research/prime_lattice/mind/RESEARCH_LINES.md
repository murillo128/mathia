# Prime-lattice research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Prime-Lattice intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Find a source-forced intermediate family that survives the completion topology

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-016-weil-form-observability-is-stronger-than-moment-observability` through `MI-035-l2-completion-turns-exact-boundary-zero-stacks-into-a-large-nullspace`.

PL-345--PL-347 classify the two obvious endpoint extremes. The centered same-state channel is the classical first logarithmic Riesz criterion, while the complete Fourier-modulation Gram family is a form core for the localized completed Weil form and therefore reconstructs ordinary Weil positivity. PL-348--PL-352 quantify how finite-axis sparsity loses its Beurling--Malliavin barrier once natural multiplicative closure is admitted.

PL-353 then shows that finite coefficient restrictions create a genuine closed intermediate family only when they are continuous in the destination Sobolev/form topology. Finite centering, finite logarithmic moments, finite real-phase jets and finite `p^(-sigma)` pole-cancellation relations disappear after closure. PL-354 gives the finite-support opposite extreme: finitely many exact positive-real Dirichlet evaluations remain topologically invisible, whereas an infinite distinct exact stack kills every finite Dirichlet polynomial.

PL-355 shows that the last statement is **not stable under the natural prime `l2` completion**. In the prime-supported Hilbert space `sum_p c_p p^(-s)` with `c in l2(P)`, point evaluation is continuous exactly for `Re s>1/2`, and classical Seip zero-set theory produces nonzero pure-prime series vanishing on bounded real Blaschke stacks accumulating at `1/2`, for example `1/2+2^(-j)`. The boundary reproducing kernel satisfies `K(sigma_u,sigma_v)=min(u,v)+O(1)` after logarithmic approach to `1/2`; that Brownian geometry is classical and is not an RH discriminator.

PL-356 strengthens the topology change. Seip's tail-supported construction makes the common zero-stack nullspace infinite-dimensional, so intersecting it with **any finite family of bounded homogeneous coefficient constraints** still leaves an infinite-dimensional space. Finite low-prime exclusions, finitely many Hilbert moments or finite orthogonality repairs therefore cannot restore the finite-support rigidity of PL-354.

The live intermediate-family problem is now topology-specific. A useful source-forced restriction must be stronger than finitely many bounded `l2` coefficient equations and must prevent the boundary interpolation freedom without simply returning to the complete Weil form core or annihilating the algebraic source. Plausible classes include an infinite closed relation with a nontrivial range, nonlinear/positivity structure, cross-degree Euler-product compatibility, target-relative Nyman/Weil coupling, or cross-aperture coherence; each requires its own exact sufficiency and nontriviality test.

## Treat completeness radius, coefficient topology and boundary interpolation as separate budgets

Beurling--Malliavin density measures how far a restricted modulation family tests the localized form. PL-353 measures whether algebraic coefficient restrictions survive the destination topology. PL-354--PL-356 add a third budget: changing the source completion can turn an infinite exact evaluation stack from algebraically fatal into a large closed nullspace.

A proposed intermediate family should therefore state its closure law, effective log-frequency density, coefficient topology, evaluation/interpolation boundary, and destination theorem together. Finite support, local Sobolev closure and native prime `l2` completion have genuinely different nullspaces; a conclusion proved in one cannot be transferred to another by formal analogy.

## Keep centering, explicit-formula identity, family closure and source completion distinct

Raw endpoint recurrence is PNT-universal; centering introduces the classical zero expansion; same-state completion gives the Riesz criterion; complete modulation gives localized Weil positivity. Prime-log density and coefficient-dual results classify many nominally sparse repairs. PL-354--PL-356 now show that exact evaluation rigidity itself depends sharply on the source topology: finite support gives the dense-or-zero stack dichotomy, while `l2(P)` admits large critical-boundary zero-stack nullspaces. The next Prime-Lattice mechanism must add canonical arithmetic coupling beyond that ambient interpolation freedom rather than treating the critical evaluation boundary or its Brownian kernel as zero selection.
