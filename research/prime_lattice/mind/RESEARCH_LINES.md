## Find a source-forced intermediate family that survives completion, continuation and the required cancellation mechanism

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-016-weil-form-observability-is-stronger-than-moment-observability` through `MI-039-canonical-cutoff-continuation-forces-absolute-convergence`.

PL-345--PL-347 classify the two obvious endpoint extremes. The centered same-state channel is the classical first logarithmic Riesz criterion, while the complete Fourier-modulation Gram family is a form core for the localized completed Weil form and therefore reconstructs ordinary Weil positivity. PL-348--PL-352 quantify how finite-axis sparsity loses its Beurling--Malliavin barrier once natural multiplicative closure is admitted.

PL-353--PL-356 show that finite coefficient restrictions survive only when continuous in the destination topology, and that exact evaluation stacks change character under completion: algebraically fatal stacks can reopen large nullspaces in prime `l2`. PL-357--PL-358 bracket the most canonical nonlinear Euler-product repair in native Dirichlet `H^2`: complete multiplicativity is zero-free while ordinary multiplicativity permits one-prime zero programming.

PL-359 tests exact conductor-one Riemann symmetry. Hamburger rigidity forces the Dirichlet series to be a scalar multiple of zeta, but native `H^2` excludes the nonzero zeta vector, so the exact functional equation overshoots the desired intermediate family all the way to zero.

PL-360 closes the direct diagonal-metric repair. No positive diagonal coefficient Hilbert space can both contain the zeta coefficient vector and keep ordinary bounded point evaluation at any `Re s<=1`; Cauchy--Schwarz reconstructs the absolute-convergence wall.

PL-361 shows that this is not merely a diagonal-RKHS pathology. In any Hausdorff topological vector space, if the ordinary zeta partial sums converge in the space and point evaluation at `s_0` is continuous, their scalar values must converge, forcing `Re(s_0)>1`. The same applies when the Dirichlet monomials form a Schauder basis and the zeta vector has coefficient sequence `(1,1,...)`.

PL-362 closes the natural “smooth the cutoff positively” escape. Every coefficientwise-positive approximate identity `w_i(n)->1` satisfies `sum_n w_i(n)n^-sigma -> +infinity` at each real `sigma<=1`. Positive Riesz/Cesàro/heat-like energy filters therefore do not supply continuation by themselves.

PL-363 then tests the first finite signed escape. A fixed finite lattice-shift filter `T=sum_m c_m S_m` sends the coefficient-one vector to the periodic sequence `b_k=sum_(m|k)c_m`, with Dirichlet series

`B(s)=P(s) zeta(s)`, where `P(s)=sum_m c_m m^-s`.

If `P(1)=0`, the periodic coefficients have mean zero and their ordinary Dirichlet series converges locally uniformly exactly on `Re s>0`; Hurwitz-zeta continuation then removes the remaining pole globally. Thus finite sign-indefinite filtering really does cross the positive-cone obstruction of PL-362, but only by manufacturing a classical periodic continuation. The one-prime filter `1-p S_p` gives the familiar eta-type example; its extra factor does not select the critical line. More generally a finite `P` may add its own zeros, but supplies no independent mechanism forcing them to encode the Riemann critical geometry.

The surviving problem is narrower again. A viable intermediate family must alter the continuation/readout protocol **and** identify a source-forced cancellation that is not merely a chosen finite pole-cancelling filter: an infinite or scale-dependent signed/complex summation law, non-coefficientwise mixing, a nontrivial counterterm/finite part, rigged/distributional evaluation, target-relative quotient geometry, or another global arithmetic correlation with an independently zero-sensitive destination theorem. Crossing the positive cone is necessary for this route but is not itself zero selection.

## Treat completeness radius, coefficient topology, cutoff protocol, positivity cone, evaluation geometry and global symmetry as separate budgets

Beurling--Malliavin density measures how far a restricted modulation family tests the localized form. PL-353 measures whether algebraic coefficient restrictions survive the destination topology. PL-354--PL-356 show that source completion can turn an infinite exact evaluation stack from algebraically fatal into a large closed nullspace. PL-357--PL-359 show that nonlinear multiplicative or global symmetry constraints can be too local, too rigid, or incompatible with the chosen Hilbert class.

PL-360 adds the primal/dual warning that making a target vector cheap can make evaluation expensive. PL-361 strengthens it: even a fully non-diagonal topology cannot cross the Dirichlet convergence wall if it retains both canonical coefficient-partial-sum convergence and ordinary continuous point evaluation. PL-362 then separates summation geometry from sign structure: changing the approximating family still fails if coefficient recovery stays positive and unrenormalized on the real axis. PL-363 supplies the complementary control: allowing finitely many signs can cross to `Re s>0`, but the resulting periodic continuation remains a finite Dirichlet-polynomial multiple of zeta and does not create a new critical selector.

“Change the topology,” “smooth the cutoff,” or “allow signs” is therefore incomplete unless one specifies the approximating family, its sign/complex structure, convergence mode, any divergent counterterm, the destination functional, how continuation is realized and why the cancellation is forced by the arithmetic source rather than inserted as a known analytic device. A conclusion proved in finite support, local form, diagonal weighted `l2`, native `H^2`, a canonical Schauder completion, a positive coefficientwise summation method, a fixed finite signed lattice filter or completed zeta categories cannot be transferred to another by formal analogy.

## Keep centering, explicit-formula identity, family closure, source completion and analytic renormalization distinct

Raw endpoint recurrence is PNT-universal; centering introduces the classical zero expansion; same-state completion gives the Riesz criterion; complete modulation gives localized Weil positivity. The later results classify several attempted intermediate source laws, topologies and summation protocols.

The next Prime-Lattice mechanism must therefore exhibit a genuinely nontrivial completed family and a continuation protocol simultaneously, then explain **where the necessary sign-indefinite or renormalized contribution is forced by rational-prime structure rather than inserted from the known continuation**. Neither the critical evaluation boundary, multiplicativity, the bare Riemann functional equation, diagonal coefficient reweighting, canonical coefficient-cutoff completion, positive coefficientwise smoothing nor a fixed finite signed shift filter should be treated as zero selection in isolation.
