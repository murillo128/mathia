# Prime-lattice research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Prime-Lattice intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Find an intermediate modulation family between one Riesz channel and form-core completeness

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-016-weil-form-observability-is-stronger-than-moment-observability` through `MI-034-exact-real-dirichlet-evaluation-stacks-are-dense-or-zero`.

PL-345--PL-347 classify the two obvious extremes of the endpoint state family. The centered same-state channel is the classical first logarithmic Riesz criterion, while the complete Fourier-modulation Gram family is a form core for the localized completed Weil form and therefore reconstructs ordinary Weil positivity. One scalar channel is too compressed; the complete family is exactly the old problem in coordinates.

PL-348 strengthens the completeness side in the arithmetic coordinates themselves: prime-log modulations already form a form core. PL-349 then gives the complementary Beurling--Malliavin interpretation. A frequency family with insufficient logarithmic density has only a finite form-core radius; the obstruction is quantitative rather than merely cardinal.

PL-350--PL-352 identify how fragile that sparse obstruction is under multiplicative closure. Once the modulation family is closed under the natural multiplicative combinations, the Beurling--Malliavin deficit collapses. A family supported on finitely many prime axes has a finite completeness budget, but freeing even one additional prime coordinate can already destroy that finite-axis barrier and restore arbitrarily large reach.

PL-353 closes the simplest finite coefficient-coupling repair. For the prime-log exponential span in the subcritical local Sobolev topology, a finite coefficient relation survives closure if and only if its coefficient weights are samples of a continuous dual Paley--Wiener/Sobolev functional. Finite centering, finitely many logarithmic moments, finite real-phase jets and finite `p^(-sigma)` pole-cancellation relations fail this gate: their joint kernels are dense and still form a core for the full localized Weil form.

PL-354 now closes the literal exact positive-real Dirichlet-evaluation stack as an intermediate family. Any finite stack remains invisible after local completion by the PL-353 mechanism, while an infinite stack at distinct positive real points annihilates every finite Dirichlet polynomial and leaves only the zero algebraic vector. Thus the exact stack has a **dense-or-zero dichotomy**: finite exact evaluation constraints remove no closed directions; an infinite exact stack removes all algebraic directions. There is no nonzero proper intermediate closed family of this literal type.

The live problem is therefore narrower than “find a sparse basis,” “add coefficient constraints,” or “use infinitely many exact real evaluations.” A useful family must be arithmetically source-forced and closed enough to carry a nontrivial zero-sensitive relation while avoiding both unrestricted form-core completeness and the exact-stack zero collapse. Viable escapes include a genuine source-forced continuous dual trace, an approximate/asymptotic infinite relation in a completed coefficient topology, a nonlinear/positivity/target-relative constraint, a cross-aperture coupling, or a constrained semigroup/word family whose effective Beurling--Malliavin budget grows enough to force a destination sign before it becomes a full form core.

## Treat Beurling--Malliavin radius and coefficient topology as sufficiency budgets, not new RH criteria

The density results do not themselves prove positivity. They measure how far a restricted modulation family can test the localized form before Fourier completeness becomes available. Finite prime-axis families have a genuine finite budget; multiplicative closure can erase it; one free prime coordinate already shows that nominal sparsity in the generating set is not the same as sparse information after closure; PL-353 shows that nominal finite codimension in coefficient space is not the same as a closed restriction in the destination topology; and PL-354 shows that passing naively from finitely many exact Dirichlet evaluations to infinitely many jumps directly from dense closure to algebraic triviality.

A proposed intermediate family should therefore state its closure law, effective log-frequency density, form-core radius, coefficient-topology gate when relevant, and destination theorem together. If its closure is dense enough to reconstruct the whole localized Weil form, the route has returned to the classical criterion. If its exact constraints annihilate the algebraic family, it has overshot in the other direction. The target is a proper source-forced closed structure whose destination sufficiency is independent of these two automatic closures.

## Keep centering, explicit-formula identity and family closure distinct

Raw endpoint recurrence is PNT-universal; centering introduces the classical zero expansion; same-state completion gives the Riesz criterion; complete modulation gives localized Weil positivity; prime-log form-core results and Beurling--Malliavin density quantify where restricted families cross between those extremes; PL-353 identifies which finite coefficient hyperplanes survive that closure; and PL-354 shows that a literal infinite exact real-evaluation stack is too rigid rather than intermediate. The next Prime-Lattice result must live genuinely between these endpoints rather than improving the bookkeeping of either automatic closure.
