## Retain the continuation tail in a zero-sensitive category that is not translation-hull blind

**Linked intuition:** `MI-044-finite-prime-wold-classification-does-not-globalize-atomically`.

PL-391 shows that finite Dirichlet-polynomial Jessen zero density can concentrate on the critical half-line for an `L^2` coefficient-energy reason while remaining too coarse to track the actual zeta divisor. PL-392 makes the representation loss exact: the Euler--Maclaurin tail that restores local-uniform convergence to zeta is Besicovitch-null on fixed interior vertical lines.

PL-393 now rules out the standard fixed-`N` two-sided Jessen mean as the obvious nonlinear repair. The same height-decaying continuation tail leaves the infinite-height logarithmic mean unchanged, because it disappears in all large-translation hull limits. Thus both the `B^2` quotient and fixed-cutoff Jessen averaging erase the distinguished representative needed for fixed-zero recovery.

The live object must therefore be individual-zero-sensitive and representative-sensitive before the `C_0` tail is lost. Coupled limits `N=N(T)`, finite-height/local zero statistics, or another analytic/Fredholm construction remain viable only if they demonstrably distinguish `Q_N` from `P_N` while retaining the numerical prime frequencies.

## Keep zero density, continuation and divisor fidelity separate

A half-line density statement is not an RH statement, and a nonlinear height mean is not automatically continuation-sensitive. Every proposed bridge should state which representative is retained, which topology supplies continuation, and why the target observable sees an individual zero rather than only the common translation hull or an averaged density.
