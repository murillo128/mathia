# Prime-lattice research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Prime-Lattice evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Transfer the repaired moving endpoint through a noncircular shifted scalar channel

PL-407--PL-421 reduce the actual-prime target to one fixed signed supertrace and identify the exact image-cone gate: after local-factor deletion, positivity on the admissible insertion image is equivalent at `p=5` to the normalized residue correlation matrix having `lambda_min>=1/4`. PL-424--PL-426 show that residue/character marginals and the published Fourier-diagonal mixed-zero observables do not determine that verdict. PL-427 shows that arbitrary complex polarization can recover the full fixed-modulus matrix, but only under a GRH hypothesis that already contains RH through the principal character.

PL-428 identifies the remaining same-shift representation loss exactly. For a real mod-5 residue signal, variances of every real character combination determine only the real part of the Hermitian character covariance; the three imaginary coherences `Im K_01`, `Im K_21` and `Im K_13` remain invisible and can change the `1/4` cone verdict. Ordinary same-shift products of integer powers of Dirichlet `L`-functions therefore cannot repair the blind sector.

PL-429 shows that this obstruction is not intrinsic to scalarization once the canonical vertical flow is retained. For a finite prime-window response, the cross term `C(t)` has only positive frequencies `log n`, while the observable scalar polarization response is `R(t)=2 Re C(t)`. Positive-frequency projection, equivalently the Hilbert-transform quadrature, recovers the full complex zero-shift coherence from the real function `t -> R(t)`. In Dirichlet-series coordinates the modulation is exactly the legitimate shift `L(s+it,chi)`, so no complex powers are being inserted by hand.

PL-430 removes the apparent close-frequency conditioning barrier for the quantity the cone test actually needs. The aggregate quadrature `Im C(0)` has the finite-aperture estimator

`Q_(epsilon,T)[R]=-(1/(2pi)) int_(-T)^T R(t) t/(t^2+epsilon^2) dt`

with deterministic error bounded by `(epsilon log N + 4/(pi T log 2)) B`, where `B=sum |Im beta_n|` may be bounded by the window operator energy. A uniform response error `eta` is amplified only by `(eta/(2pi)) log(1+T^2/epsilon^2)`. For fixed target accuracy the shift aperture can therefore remain bounded independently of `N`; the load-bearing spectral gap is the distance from zero frequency `log 2`, not the minimum spacing among adjacent `log n` frequencies.

PL-431 makes the actual dyadic short-prime source substantially cheaper than that broad-band reconstruction. On support `[X,CX]`, the single vanishing shift `t_X=-pi/(2 log X)` rotates every `log n` carrier by an approximate quarter turn, so `R(0)` together with `R(t_X)` recovers the missing complex covariance with deterministic error `O(B_X/log X)`. The continuum Hilbert aperture is therefore unnecessary for this localized source, although a theorem at exactly `t=0` still does not determine the oriented second sample.

PL-432 removes the artificial `ell^1` coefficient-mass bottleneck from that quarter-turn conditioning. Abel--Stieltjes transport controls the deformation by ordinary multiscale short-interval `L^2` norms, and it shifts the deterministic principal-character subtraction coherently with the prime source. Under natural Selberg-type second-moment bounds the transport error is lower order on the natural variance scale. The remaining analytic obligation is thus the oriented scalar response itself, not a coefficientwise mass estimate.

PL-433 identifies why the fact that `t_X->0` does not make that obligation perturbative on the zero side. For polynomial short intervals `h=X^(theta+o(1))`, the natural local zero spacing is also of order `1/log X`, and the quarter-turn corresponds to a fixed microscopic displacement `-(1-theta)/4`. A zero-side theorem known only at the unshifted point cannot therefore be continued to the required sample by ordinary unscaled continuity. Either the oriented prime-side response must be controlled directly, or mixed zero correlations must be controlled uniformly at that fixed microscopic offset without assuming RH/GRH.

The live positive route is now sharply analytic rather than representational. It needs a noncircular estimate for the model-subtracted scalar short-interval response at the two source scales `t=0` and `t=-pi/(2 log X)`, together with ordinary multiscale diagonal norms strong enough that the PL-432 transport error and the source/model replacement stay below the explicit PL-421 spectral margin. A direct prime-side theorem is preferable because the available zero-correlation transfers are conditional on RH/GRH. Full fixed-aperture tomography, coefficientwise Fourier recovery and same-shift matrix reconstruction are no longer necessary for the actual dyadic source.

## Keep scalar tomography, conditioning, source replacement and theorem hypotheses distinct

The current frontier has four separate gates. PL-428 is a coefficient-field obstruction only for same-shift real scalar probes; PL-429 proves exact representation completeness after vertical modulation; PL-430 gives a broad-band finite-aperture reconstruction; PL-431 replaces that by a two-sample `1/log X` quarter turn for the actual dyadic source; PL-432 conditions the deformation using source-native `L^2` norms; and PL-433 shows that this vanishing physical shift is still order one on the microscopic zero scale. None of those statements supplies the arithmetic variance estimate or source/model replacement needed by PL-421. A valid argument must state which shifted scalar response is controlled, with what normalization and error, under which hypotheses, and how it reaches the actual source-subtracted covariance in operator norm. Representation completeness and good source-side conditioning do not weaken the noncircular analytic requirement.
