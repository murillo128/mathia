# Prime-lattice research lines

This file holds the current mathematical lines of investigation suggested by the durable prime-lattice intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Force a source-determined, divisor-complete self-adjoint relation rather than a programmable selector

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-011-addition-is-first-nonmultiplicative-escape-but-affine-traces-hit-analytic-boundaries`, `MI-014-prime-exchangeability-erases-normal-arithmetic-equilibrium`, `MI-015-reciprocity-family-coupling-is-not-principal-zero-selection`.

PL-240--PL-253 show that canonical arithmetic/global constructions may retain rich structure while the principal zeta block stays factorized or while ambient positivity leaves a signed remainder. PL-254--PL-255 show the complementary selector failure: self-adjoint spectral reality can hold for a designed sub-divisor, and formally squaring a Dirac realization does not supply completeness.

PL-256 tests the remaining domain-change escape. Boundary-triple/Weyl theory confirms that changing the first-order domain can genuinely change the spectrum, but generalized Weyl data are broad enough to be programmable. For zeta, the natural completed logarithmic derivative is Herglotz exactly under RH, so prescribing a divisor-complete Weyl function does not generate the missing positivity. The elementary parameter `lambda=s(1-s)` is real for a nonreal zero exactly on the critical line, but this becomes useful only if one independently constructs a self-adjoint model that captures the full principal divisor.

The live construction must therefore derive the boundary/Weyl data from rational-prime or global arithmetic structure, prove self-adjoint/Herglotz positivity without assuming RH, and prove two-way divisor completeness with multiplicity. Abstract extension existence or a designed secular equation is a control, not the mechanism.

## Treat positivity, spectral reality, and divisor completeness as separate obligations

A positive completed distribution can hide the target behind a signed complement, and a self-adjoint operator can select only a sub-divisor or encode a prescribed Weyl function. A successful spectral route must prove source-forced coupling, sign/reality, and completeness independently and then show that they meet in the same target-bearing object.
