# Positive-cone and growing-depth Gram data reduce to classical sampling controls

[[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-072-positive-cone-dirichlet-resolution-universality|PL-072]]--[[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-099-subnyquist-midpoint-s1-control|PL-099]] establish that fixed-lag pointing, local sharp kernels, mean-gap bulk and the first growing-depth coherent regime reduce to classical additive correlations, Poisson/prolate sampling and PNT/log-uniform envelopes. The strongest unresolved raw boundary was trace norm at and near Nyquist plus possible hard-edge/determinant information.

That raw boundary is now substantially classicalized. [[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-100-critical-nyquist-midpoint-s1-control|PL-100]] and [[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-101-near-critical-nyquist-midpoint-s1-control|PL-101]] give a completely non-arithmetic midpoint cloud whose covariance converges in `S_1` to the continuum comparator at exact Nyquist and along every `r_N->1` approach from either side. [[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-102-critical-prime-nyquist-poisson-s1-excess|PL-102]] shows that the prime cloud can still have a positive critical `S_1` excess, but under the same local Hardy--Littlewood hierarchy that excess is exactly the generic Poisson self-correlation contribution, not a rational-prime selector. [[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-103-poisson-bulk-logdet-decay|PL-103]] similarly routes exponential raw Gram-determinant decay to strict concavity against the same nondegenerate Poisson bulk.

The source-backed conclusion remains scoped: ordinary bulk, fixed moments, first growing-depth covariance, raw near-Nyquist trace norm and the most immediate log-determinant signal all admit deterministic or Poisson matched controls. The live frontier is information beyond that sampling model—microscopic/hard-edge inverse statistics, condition numbers, target-relative Schur complements, singular/growing tests, depth-selective observables or completed indefinite Weil data—and any positive claim must separate rational primes from the relevant control in the same statistic and norm.
