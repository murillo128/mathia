## Claim

Let

\[
\mathcal H=L^2((0,1),dx)
\]

and let the normalized dilation isometries be

\[
(D_r f)(x)
=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt r\,f(rx),&0<x\le 1/r,\\
0,&1/r<x\le1,
\end{cases}
\qquad r\ge1,
\tag{PL383-1}
\]

as in `PL-382`. Then

\[
\boxed{
W^*(D_2,D_3)
=
W^*(\{D_r:r\ge1\})
=
\mathcal B(\mathcal H).
}
\tag{PL383-2}
\]

More generally, if `a,b>1` and

\[
\frac{\log a}{\log b}\notin\mathbb Q,
\tag{PL383-3}
\]

then

\[
W^*(D_a,D_b)=\mathcal B(\mathcal H).
\tag{PL383-4}
\]

Thus the self-adjoint strong closure of the ambient Nyman dilation representation no longer remembers the discrete exponent lattice, the integer sampling set, or even the full set of primes. Two incommensurate sampled times already generate the maximal possible von Neumann algebra.

The conclusion is deliberately topology-sensitive. It is a statement about the von Neumann/strong-operator closure after adjoining adjoints. It does **not** assert that the norm-closed `C*`-algebra generated by `D_2,D_3` contains every `D_r`, and it does **not** assert that the nonselfadjoint semigroup algebra generated by the positive integer dilations has the same closure.

## 1. Logarithmic coordinates reduce the statement to two sampled right shifts

Use the unitary from `PL-382`,

\[
(Jf)(y)=e^{-y/2}f(e^{-y}),
\qquad
J:\mathcal H\to L^2(\mathbb R_+),
\tag{PL383-5}
\]

under which

\[
JD_{e^t}J^{-1}=S_t,
\tag{PL383-6}
\]

where

\[
(S_tg)(y)
=
\begin{cases}
g(y-t),&y\ge t,\\
0,&0\le y<t.
\end{cases}
\qquad t\ge0
\tag{PL383-7}
\]

is the standard strongly continuous right-shift semigroup.

For `0\le s\le t`, direct calculation gives

\[
S_s^*S_t=S_{t-s}.
\tag{PL383-8}
\]

This is the logarithmic form of the identity already established in `PL-382`,

\[
D_a^*D_b=D_{b/a}
\qquad(1\le a\le b).
\tag{PL383-9}
\]

The important new consequence is that adjoining adjoints converts **differences of sampled times** into genuine positive-time shifts even though the individual isometries are not invertible.

## 2. Two prime times recover a dense set of continuous times

Put

\[
\alpha=\log2,
\qquad
\beta=\log3.
\tag{PL383-10}
\]

Their ratio is irrational. Indeed, if `alpha/beta=m/n` with positive integers `m,n`, then

\[
2^n=3^m,
\tag{PL383-11}
\]

contradicting unique factorization.

Hence the additive subgroup

\[
G=\alpha\mathbb Z+\beta\mathbb Z
\tag{PL383-12}
\]

is dense in `R`. Every nonnegative `g\in G` can be written

\[
g=A-B,
\qquad
A,B\in\alpha\mathbb N_0+\beta\mathbb N_0,
\qquad
A\ge B.
\tag{PL383-13}
\]

Both `S_A` and `S_B` lie in the semigroup generated by `S_alpha,S_beta`, and (PL383-8) gives

\[
S_g=S_B^*S_A.
\tag{PL383-14}
\]

Therefore the unital `*`-algebra generated by `S_alpha,S_beta` already contains

\[
\{S_g:g\in G\cap\mathbb R_+\}.
\tag{PL383-15}
\]

Because `G\cap R_+` is dense in `R_+` and `t\mapsto S_t` is strongly continuous, for every `t\ge0` one can choose `g_k\in G\cap R_+` with `g_k\to t` and obtain

\[
S_{g_k}\xrightarrow[\ k\to\infty\ ]{\mathrm{SOT}}S_t.
\tag{PL383-16}
\]

It follows that

\[
W^*(S_\alpha,S_\beta)
=
W^*(\{S_t:t\ge0\}).
\tag{PL383-17}
\]

Conjugating by `J` proves the first equality in (PL383-2). The same argument proves (PL383-4) for any pair satisfying (PL383-3).

A weaker but useful reformulation uses all integer dilations. Since

\[
D_n=\prod_pD_p^{v_p(n)},
\tag{PL383-18}
\]

for `m\ge n` one has

\[
D_n^*D_m=D_{m/n}.
\tag{PL383-19}
\]

Thus the `*`-algebra generated by the prime dilations contains every rational dilation `D_q` with `q\in Q`, `q\ge1`. Since `log Q_{>0}` is dense in `R`, strong continuity again recovers all real dilations. The two-prime argument is sharper: the rest of the prime family is already unnecessary after self-adjoint closure.

## 3. The full right-shift semigroup generates `B(L2(R+))`

It remains to identify the von Neumann algebra of the continuous right-shift semigroup. Let

\[
\mathcal M=W^*(\{S_t:t\ge0\})
\subseteq
\mathcal B(L^2(\mathbb R_+)).
\tag{PL383-20}
\]

For each `t\ge0`,

\[
Q_t:=I-S_tS_t^*
\tag{PL383-21}
\]

is multiplication by the indicator of `[0,t)`. Therefore `M` contains every `Q_t`, and the differences of these projections give multiplication by indicators of half-open bounded intervals. Their von Neumann closure is the standard multiplication masa

\[
L^\infty(\mathbb R_+)
\subseteq
\mathcal B(L^2(\mathbb R_+)).
\tag{PL383-22}
\]

Now take `X\in M'`. Because `X` commutes with every `Q_t`, it commutes with the multiplication masa and hence itself is a multiplication operator,

\[
X=M_\varphi
\qquad
\text{for some }\varphi\in L^\infty(\mathbb R_+).
\tag{PL383-23}
\]

Commutation with every shift gives, for each `t\ge0`,

\[
\varphi(y)=\varphi(y-t)
\quad\text{for a.e. }y\ge t.
\tag{PL383-24}
\]

A measurable essentially bounded function invariant under all positive translations is constant almost everywhere. Hence

\[
\mathcal M'=\mathbb CI.
\tag{PL383-25}
\]

and the bicommutant theorem yields

\[
\mathcal M=\mathcal B(L^2(\mathbb R_+)).
\tag{PL383-26}
\]

Together with (PL383-17), this proves (PL383-2).

This is consistent with the classical operator theory already used in `PL-382`: the strongly continuous pure isometric semigroup is the standard right-shift model. The present point is not a new shift-semigroup theorem; it is the exact consequence of combining that model with the incommensurate prime sampling and then taking a self-adjoint strong closure.

## 4. Why this closes an ambient operator-algebra escape

`PL-018` observed an important obstruction to a naive density argument. Although

\[
\log\mathbb Q_{>0}
=
\log\mathbb N-\log\mathbb N
\tag{PL383-27}
\]

is dense in `R`, the Nyman integer dilations are unilateral isometries rather than unitaries, so one cannot simply insert inverse shifts and declare the discrete semigroup equal to the continuous one.

`PL-382` then identified the exact adjoint identity (PL383-9). That identity shows what happens once a self-adjoint operator algebra is formed: **adjoints supply the positive differences without supplying genuine inverses**. This is enough to turn the discrete sampled family into the whole continuous semigroup in strong closure.

Consequently, any proposed RH mechanism that first replaces the distinguished prime/integer dilation tuple by only its generated von Neumann algebra has already discarded the feature that distinguishes the Báez-Duarte integer criterion from the continuous Beurling dilation family. In the ambient space it sees only

\[
W^*(D_p:p\text{ prime})
=
\mathcal B(L^2(0,1)),
\tag{PL383-28}
\]

which is independent of the arithmetic sampling once two incommensurate times are present.

The correct negative statement is therefore about **forgetful closure**, not about every operator construction involving the tuple. The distinguished generators `D_p`, their nonselfadjoint positive semigroup, their ordering, and their action on a distinguished invariant subspace can still contain information even though the von Neumann algebra they generate is maximal.

## 5. Adversarial controls and scope limits

### Generic-frequency control

Nothing in the closure argument is specific to rational primes beyond incommensurability. Any two positive times `alpha,beta` with `alpha/beta` irrational satisfy the same proof. Therefore the maximal von Neumann algebra cannot by itself distinguish `{log p}` from a generic incommensurate pair of frequencies. This is a decisive negative under the line's generic-frequency falsification rule.

### Norm-topology control

Strong continuity does not imply norm continuity for the right-shift semigroup. The approximation in (PL383-16) is therefore only strong (and hence weak), not operator-norm. No claim is made that

\[
C^*(D_2,D_3)
\tag{PL383-29}
\]

contains every real dilation. The distinction is essential: this finding rules out a **von Neumann/SOT-closed self-adjoint** escape, not every `C*`-algebraic or norm-sensitive one.

### Nonselfadjoint control

Without adjoints, the positive semigroup generated by the prime dilations is only

\[
\{D_n:n\in\mathbb N\},
\tag{PL383-30}
\]

with times `{log n}`. Those times are not dense in `R_+`; in fact their gaps near a fixed finite region do not disappear by taking products inside the positive semigroup. The difference operation in (PL383-14) is exactly what creates the dense group. Hence the finding does not collapse the one-sided nonselfadjoint problem that remains central to `PL-017`--`PL-020`.

### Nyman-step-space control

The equality (PL383-2) is an **ambient `L^2(0,1)` statement**. If `M` is Bagchi's invariant Nyman step space and

\[
T_n=D_n|_M,
\tag{PL383-31}
\]

then the adjoint inside `M` is the compression

\[
T_n^*=P_M D_n^*|_M,
\tag{PL383-32}
\]

not generally the restriction of the ambient adjoint. In particular there is no automatic identity

\[
T_n^*T_m=T_{m/n}
\tag{PL383-33}
\]

because `m/n` need not even be an integer dilation preserving `M`. Therefore (PL383-2) does **not** prove

\[
W^*(T_2,T_3)=\mathcal B(M),
\tag{PL383-34}
\]

and it does not solve or trivialize the RH-equivalent Nyman totality problem.

This boundary is important rather than cosmetic: it says that whatever arithmetic rigidity survives must live in the distinguished invariant subspace/target, the oriented integer semigroup, Mellin continuation, or another structure not erased by ambient self-adjoint closure.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The ingredients are classical or elementary:

- `PL-382` already identifies `D_r` with the standard strongly continuous right-shift semigroup and derives `D_a^*D_b=D_{b/a}` for `a\le b`.
- Strong continuity of the standard shift semigroup and the bicommutant description of von Neumann closure are classical operator theory.
- Density of `alpha Z+beta Z` for irrational `alpha/beta` is elementary Kronecker/Diophantine approximation.
- The fact that `log 2/log 3` is irrational is an immediate consequence of unique factorization.

A targeted literature search around right-shift semigroups, their commutants, Wiener-Hopf/translation algebras, and incommensurate sampled shifts found no basis for claiming theorem novelty for these ingredients. Modern right-shift-semigroup literature treats the continuous model as standard, and the operator-algebra closure argument above is short enough to derive directly.

The durable contribution is therefore **not** a new operator-theory theorem. It is the route-specific synthesis and obstruction:

\[
\text{two prime samples}
\xrightarrow{\ *\text{-closure}\ }
\text{dense continuous times}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SOT}}
\text{full shift semigroup}
\xrightarrow{W^*}
\mathcal B(L^2(0,1)).
\tag{PL383-35}
\]

That chain closes a concrete escape left by `PL-382`: the non-double-commuting overlap geometry cannot be made more arithmetic merely by passing to the ambient self-adjoint strong operator algebra. Doing so maximally forgets the discrete sampling.

## 7. Falsification tests

This finding should be corrected or withdrawn if any of the following fails:

1. the logarithmic unitary equivalence (PL383-5)--(PL383-7) or the adjoint product (PL383-8) is incorrect;
2. `log 2/log 3` is rational or the positive part of `alpha Z+beta Z` is not dense in `R_+`;
3. the right-shift semigroup is not strongly continuous, invalidating (PL383-16);
4. the projections `I-S_tS_t^*` fail to generate the multiplication masa;
5. an operator commuting with the full right-shift semigroup and its adjoints can be non-scalar, contradicting (PL383-25);
6. the ambient adjoint identity transfers automatically to the Nyman step-space compression, invalidating the scope limit in (PL383-32)--(PL383-34).

Items 1--5 are direct standard calculations. Item 6 is intentionally a warning against overextension rather than an asserted unresolved theorem.

## Consequence for `prime_lattice`

The current frontier after `PL-382` can be sharpened. There are now three distinct levels that must not be conflated:

\[
\text{oriented integer dilation semigroup}
\subset
\text{distinguished tuple plus adjoints}
\subset
\text{ambient von Neumann closure}.
\tag{PL383-36}
\]

At the last level, the arithmetic sampling is gone completely: two prime directions already give `B(L^2(0,1))`. Therefore an RH-relevant continuation should not search for an invariant of the **ambient generated von Neumann algebra**. The viable information must remain generator-relative or target-relative: the nonselfadjoint one-sided semigroup, the Nyman invariant step space and target, compressed adjoint geometry, Mellin continuation, or another source-forced structure that is not preserved by the forgetful passage to (PL383-28).

In particular, the next sharp operator question is no longer whether the ambient overlaps generate a rich self-adjoint algebra—they generate the richest possible one. The question is whether the **compressed/nonselfadjoint Nyman representation retains a rigid arithmetic invariant that disappears in the ambient closure**, and whether that invariant couples to the zero-sensitive Mellin totality problem rather than being reproducible by a generic pair of incommensurate shifts.
