## Exact reduction from the log-prime Kronecker character

For `f_t(m)=m^{it}`, the general identity in `PL-179` gives

\[
\mathcal C_t(n;r,q)
=\overline{r^{it}}\,\overline{q^{it}}(q+r-1)^{it}.
\]

Since `h=r-1`,

\[
\overline{q^{it}}(q+h)^{it}
=\left(\frac{q+h}{q}\right)^{it}
=\exp\!\left(it\log\left(1+\frac hq\right)\right),
\]

which proves the displayed formula. In exponent coordinates this is precisely the additive-energy character generated by `(log p)_p`; no auxiliary phase assignment has been introduced.

The factor `r^{-it}` is not a regularization or fitted counterterm. It is the explicit phase of the frozen source direction and is already visible before averaging. Multiplying the average by `r^{it}` merely asks whether the residual coupling to the moving prime `q` contains information beyond that known source phase.

## PNT scaling limit

Define the probability measure

\[
\mu_X
=
\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{q\le X}\delta_{q/X}
\]

on `[0,1]`, with the sum over primes. The prime number theorem implies, for every fixed `0<u<=1`,

\[
\mu_X([0,u])
=
\frac{\pi(uX)}{\pi(X)}
\longrightarrow u.
\]

Hence `mu_X` converges weakly to Lebesgue measure on `[0,1]`.

Assume `h t_X/X -> tau`. Fix `epsilon>0`. Uniformly for primes `q>=epsilon X`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
t_X\log\left(1+\frac hq\right)
&=t_X\frac hq
+O\!\left(\frac{|t_X|h^2}{q^2}\right)\\
&=\frac{h t_X/X}{q/X}+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]

because `r`, and therefore `h`, is fixed and `|t_X|=O(X/h)` in the finite-`tau` regime. Thus on `[epsilon,1]` the phase converges uniformly to `e^{i tau/u}`. PNT weak convergence gives

\[
\frac1{\pi(X)}
\sum_{\epsilon X<q\le X}
\exp\!\left(it_X\log\left(1+\frac hq\right)\right)
\longrightarrow
\int_\epsilon^1 e^{i\tau/u}\,du.
\]

The discarded primes `q<=epsilon X` have relative mass

\[
\frac{\pi(\epsilon X)}{\pi(X)}\longrightarrow\epsilon,
\]

and every phase has modulus one. Letting `epsilon -> 0` proves

\[
B_X(t_X)\to\int_0^1e^{i\tau/u}\,du.
\]

The second integral representation follows from `v=1/u`.

The case `tau=0` gives `J(0)=1`. Therefore a nontrivial residual phase cannot appear at any fixed source `r` while `|t_X|=o(X/h)`.

The proof does not use unique factorization, prime gaps, Euler products, Möbius cancellation, or zeta zeros after the exact affine reduction. It uses only the rescaled counting law

\[

## Adversarial checks and limitations

- **The frozen source is fixed.** If `r=r(X)` grows with `X`, then `h/X` can remain macroscopic and the limiting phase changes. The present result does not classify that joint-source regime.
- **The source average is broad.** Joint conditioning of `(r,q)`, a thin arithmetic subset of primes, or a target-relative weight can destroy the uniform rescaled source law used above.
- **The high-frequency regime remains open.** When `|t_X|h/X -> infinity`, the argument no longer yields `J`; one obtains a genuinely oscillatory reciprocal-prime sum. This finding does not claim cancellation or universality there.
- **The demodulation is exact source data.** Removing `r^{-it}` cannot remove any dependence on `q` or on the shifted integer; it only factors out the known frozen-axis phase.
- **PNT strength is the point.** The limit is unconditional but far too coarse to encode the critical zero divisor. It would persist for matched non-zeta source systems with the same first-order counting law.
- **No analytic continuation is used.** The argument is a finite prime average followed by `X -> infinity`; it does not continue an Euler product through `Re(s)=1`.
- **No RH implication is obtained.** A universal PNT kernel cannot by itself select `Re(s)=1/2` or impose Weil positivity.
