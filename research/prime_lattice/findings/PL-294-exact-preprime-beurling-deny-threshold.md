# PL-294 — The localized Weil semigroup loses positivity at an exact pre-prime aperture

**Evidence:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/ROUTE-RESTRICTION`

**Status:** `EXACT-BEURLING-DENY-THRESHOLD + PRE-PRIME-OBSTRUCTION + HOPF-GATE-NARROWED`

## Claim

Let `q_a` be Suzuki's scaled closed localized-Weil form on `I=(-1,1)` and `A_a` its Friedrichs operator. Write `rho_pl>1` for the plastic constant, the unique real root

\[
\rho_{\rm pl}^3-\rho_{\rm pl}-1=0,
\qquad
\rho_{\rm pl}=1.324717957\ldots,
\]

and set

\[
\boxed{
a_{\rm BD}:=\frac12\log\rho_{\rm pl}
=0.140599787\ldots .
}
\]

Then `a_BD` is the exact threshold for the standard first Beurling--Deny positivity criterion of the full localized Weil form:

\[
\boxed{
q_a(|u|)\le q_a(u)\quad\text{for every real }u\in\mathfrak D(\bar q_a)
\iff
0<a\le a_{\rm BD}.
}
\]

Consequently the self-adjoint semigroup generated by `A_a` is positivity preserving precisely on this aperture range. In fact, for `a<=a_BD` the effective off-diagonal jump coefficient is strictly negative almost everywhere, so the usual irreducibility argument gives positivity improving and hence a simple fixed-sign ground state whenever the bottom eigenfunction exists. This sharpens Suzuki's qualitative `sufficiently small a` sign/simplicity mechanism, but does **not** extend his positivity theorem for the numerical eigenvalue itself.

The key arithmetic consequence is negative: the threshold occurs strictly before the first prime-power translation can enter,

\[
a_{\rm BD}=0.140599787\ldots
<\frac12\log 2=0.346573590\ldots .
\]

Thus the standard positivity-improving/Perron--Frobenius route has already failed in the purely archimedean window. In particular it cannot be used to promote the certified simple-even ground state at `a=0.8` from `PL-284` to a nonnegative state, which is exactly the missing hypothesis needed to apply the Hopf boundary obstruction from `PL-293`. Any such sign theorem at `a=0.8` must use additional structure beyond ordinary Beurling--Deny positivity.

## Exact absolute-value defect

Suzuki's fixed-interval formula is

\[
q_a(u)=c_a\|u\|_2^2+\mathcal L(u)+P_a(u)
-a\iint_{I^2}r''(a(x-y))u(y)u(x)\,dx\,dy,
\]

where `P_a` is the finite prime-power translation contribution and

\[
\mathcal L(u)
=
\frac14\iint_{I^2}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy
-rac12\int_I |u(x)|^2\log(1-x^2)\,dx.
\]

For real `u`, put

\[
D_u(x,y):=|u(x)u(y)|-u(x)u(y)\ge0.
\]

The scalar and killing terms are unchanged by `u->|u|`. The principal jump term contributes

\[
-\iint_{I^2}\frac{D_u(x,y)}{2|x-y|}\,dx\,dy,
\]

while the smooth remainder contributes

\[
a\iint_{I^2}\bigl(-r''(a|x-y|)\bigr)D_u(x,y)\,dx\,dy.
\]

Each prime-power term has a negative correlation coefficient, hence

\[
P_a(|u|)-P_a(u)\le0.
\]

Therefore the continuous off-diagonal coefficient governing the competition between the principal logarithmic jump and the archimedean remainder is

\[
C_a(d)
:=a\bigl(-r''(ad)\bigr)-\frac1{2d},
\qquad 0<d<2.
\]

Suzuki gives, for `t>0`,

\[
r''(t)
=-2\cosh(t/2)
+\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}
-\frac1{2t}.
\]

With `t=ad`, the singular terms cancel exactly:

\[
\boxed{
C_a(d)
=a\left[
2\cosh(t/2)
-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}
\right].
}
\]

Hence the sign change is an elementary algebraic equation. Setting `x=e^{t/2}` gives

\[
2\cosh(t/2)=\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}
\iff
x^6-x^2-1=0.
\]

Writing `y=x^2=e^t`, this becomes

\[
y^3-y-1=0.
\]

Since `y^3-y-1` is strictly increasing for `y>1`, there is one positive threshold

\[
t_*=\log\rho_{\rm pl}=0.281199574\ldots,
\]

with `C_a(d)<0` for `ad<t_*` and `C_a(d)>0` for `ad>t_*`.

For `a<=t_*/2`, every interior separation satisfies `ad<2a<=t_*`. Moreover `2a<=log rho_pl<log 2`, so there are no active prime-power translations. Thus `q_a(|u|)<=q_a(u)` throughout the form domain. The effective jump coefficient is strictly negative for almost every pair, including at `a=a_BD` because equality would require the unattained separation `|x-y|=2`; irreducibility therefore remains available at the endpoint.

## Failure above the threshold

Let `a>a_BD`. Choose `t_0` with

\[
t_*<t_0<2a
\]

and, when prime translations are active, choose it away from the finite set `\{\log n:n<=e^{2a},\Lambda(n)\ne0\}`. Put `d_0=t_0/a<2`. There are two small disjoint intervals inside `(-1,1)` whose centers are separated by `d_0`; choose nonnegative smooth bumps `f,g` supported there and set `u=f-g`.

The supports can be made small enough that `C_a(|x-y|)>0` on every cross pair `(x,y) in supp(f) x supp(g)`, while no active prime shift maps either support into the other (or into itself). Hence the prime absolute-value defect vanishes for this test function and

\[
q_a(|u|)-q_a(u)
=
\iint C_a(|x-y|)D_u(x,y)\,dx\,dy
>0.
\]

So the first Beurling--Deny criterion fails for every `a>a_BD`. Adding a scalar to make the form nonnegative changes neither side of this inequality, so the usual equivalence between the absolute-value contraction criterion and positivity preservation applies to the lower-semibounded closed form.

This obstruction is robust after primes enter: the prime correlations individually favor the absolute-value inequality, but their lags are discrete and finite at fixed aperture, whereas the positive archimedean defect occupies an open set of separations. They therefore cannot restore positivity preservation of the full semigroup.

## Relation to the current boundary-regularity target

`PL-293` left open the possibility that the actual localized-Weil ground state is nonnegative and hence subject to the sharp `ell(t)^{1/2}` Hopf lower law, which would rule out zero-extension `H^{1/2}`. `PL-284` supplies simplicity and evenness at `a=0.8`, but not fixed sign.

The present threshold shows that ordinary semigroup positivity cannot bridge that gap. At `a=0.8`, the absolute-value contraction property has been absent since `a` passed `0.1406`, well before any rational-prime event. Therefore one cannot infer positivity of the `a=0.8` ground state by extending Suzuki's Dirichlet-form/Perron--Frobenius argument. A successful Hopf attack must instead prove a **source-specific sign property** of the actual eigenfunction, derive the boundary channel without a global sign hypothesis, or bypass the Hopf route entirely.

Conversely, the result prevents an overinterpretation of the exact constant. `a_BD` is entirely archimedean and precedes `log 2/2`; it is not an RH-sensitive scale and carries no rational-prime specificity. Its value is useful as a sharp structural boundary for a proof method, not as a candidate critical aperture for zeta.

The durable redirect is therefore precise: **the generic positive-ground-state/Hopf route is unavailable at the certified `a=0.8` window unless one first proves an additional zeta-specific sign theorem.** The remaining boundary question from `PL-293` cannot be settled by routine Beurling--Deny continuation from the small-aperture principal operator.
