## Claim

Let `(S_t)_{t>=0}` be the right-shift semigroup on `L^2(R_+)`,

\[
(S_tf)(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x-t),&x\ge t,\\
0,&0\le x<t.
\end{cases}
\]

For an additive subgroup `G subset R`, write

\[
G^+=G\cap[0,\infty),
\qquad
\mathcal T_G=C^*(S_g:g\in G^+).
\tag{PL387-1}
\]

Then for every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}(S_t,\mathcal T_G)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\in G,\\
1,&t\notin G.
\end{cases}}
\tag{PL387-2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{S_t\in\mathcal T_G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\in G^+.}
\tag{PL387-3}
\]

Thus the caveat left explicitly open in `PL-386` does not occur: norm completion adds no pure half-line translation whose time lies outside the sampled additive group.

For the prime-frequency system,

\[
G_{\mathbb P}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{\log p:p\text{ prime}\}
=\log\mathbb Q_{>0},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
S_t\in C^*(S_{\log p}:p\text{ prime})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e^t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
}
\qquad(t\ge0),
\tag{PL387-4}
\]

and every excluded time is separated from the algebra by the maximal possible nontrivial distance `1`:

\[
\boxed{
e^t\notin\mathbb Q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(S_t,C^*(S_{\log p})\right)=1.}
\tag{PL387-5}
\]

This makes the closure hierarchy from `PL-383`, `PL-385`, and `PL-386` exact at the level of pure shifts: nonselfadjoint norm closure sees the positive integer monoid, selfadjoint norm closure sees the positive cone of `log Q_{>0}`, while selfadjoint strong closure uses density and strong continuity to recover every real time.

## 1. Coburn–Douglas gives a canonical almost-periodic symbol quotient

Let

\[
\mathcal P=C^*(S_t:t\ge0)
\]

be the full selfadjoint half-line translation algebra and let `\mathcal C` be its closed commutator ideal. Coburn and Douglas proved that

\[
\mathcal P/\mathcal C\cong AP(\mathbb R)
\tag{PL387-6}
\]

isometrically as a `C*`-algebra, where `AP(R)` is the algebra of continuous Bohr almost-periodic functions. Under their Fourier/Wiener-Hopf representation the translation `S_t` has symbol

\[
\chi_t(x)=e^{itx}.
\tag{PL387-7}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
\sigma:\mathcal P\longrightarrow AP(\mathbb R)
\]

denotes the quotient-symbol map, then

\[
\sigma(S_t)=\chi_t.
\tag{PL387-8}
\]

For a subgroup `G subset R`, let

\[
AP_G
=\overline{\operatorname{span}}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}
\{\chi_g:g\in G\}.
\tag{PL387-9}
\]

Because `\mathcal T_G` is generated by `S_g` for `g in G^+` together with their adjoints, (PL387-8) gives exactly

\[
\boxed{\sigma(\mathcal T_G)=AP_G.}
\tag{PL387-10}
\]

This is the missing norm-separation tool in `PL-386`: the full operator algebra may be noncommutative, but every pure translation retains its frequency in the classical almost-periodic quotient.

## 2. A frequency outside `G` is at uniform distance one from `AP_G`

Let `\mathsf M` denote the Bohr invariant mean on `AP(R)`. Its characters satisfy

\[
\mathsf M(\chi_a\overline{\chi_b})
=\begin{cases}
1,&a=b,\\
0,&a\ne b.
\end{cases}
\tag{PL387-11}
\]

If `t notin G` and `f` is an almost-periodic polynomial whose frequencies lie in `G`, then every frequency of `\overline{\chi_t}f` is nonzero, hence

\[
\mathsf M(\overline{\chi_t}f)=0.
\]

Uniform approximation extends this to every `f in AP_G`. Therefore

\[
\mathsf M\bigl(|\chi_t-f|^2\bigr)
=1+\mathsf M(|f|^2)
\ge1.
\tag{PL387-12}
\]

Since the invariant mean is a state,

\[
\mathsf M(|h|^2)\le\|h\|_\infty^2,
\]

so

\[
\|\chi_t-f\|_\infty\ge1
\qquad(f\in AP_G).
\tag{PL387-13}
\]

Taking `f=0` shows the bound is sharp:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}(\chi_t,AP_G)=1\qquad(t\notin G).}
\tag{PL387-14}
\]

Now take any `A in \mathcal T_G`. Quotient maps are contractive and (PL387-10) gives `sigma(A) in AP_G`; hence

\[
\|S_t-A\|
\ge
\|\sigma(S_t-A)\|_\infty
=
\|\chi_t-\sigma(A)\|_\infty
\ge1.
\tag{PL387-15}
\]

Again `A=0` gives the matching upper bound because `\|S_t\|=1`. This proves the `t notin G` branch of (PL387-2). The `t in G^+` branch is already the exact adjoint-product inclusion proved in `PL-386`, so (PL387-2)--(PL387-3) follow.

A useful stress test is a non-pure subgroup extension. Take `G=log Q_{>0}` and

\[
t=\tfrac12\log2.
\]

Then `2t=log2 in G` but `t notin G` because `sqrt 2` is irrational. Even though a positive integer multiple of the missing time lies in the algebraic frequency group, (PL387-15) still gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}(S_{(\log2)/2},\mathcal T_G)=1.
\]

Thus divisibility in `R` or density of `G` cannot create fractional/limit shifts in operator norm.

## 3. Prime specialization completes the three closure regimes

`PL-385` proves for the nonselfadjoint norm algebra generated by the prime shifts that a pure shift belongs exactly when its time is `log n`, `n in N`; any other `S_t` has distance one from that analytic algebra. `PL-386` shows that adjoining adjoints before norm closure enlarges the exact algebraic times from the positive monoid to the positive cone of the integer span, which for the rational primes is

\[
\log\mathbb Q_{>0}\cap[0,\infty).
\]

The present result proves that this description is not merely an inclusion: **there are no additional pure shifts hidden in the `C*`-completion**.

By contrast, `PL-383` shows that once adjoints and **strong** operator closure are admitted, two incommensurate prime times generate the full continuous right-shift semigroup and then `B(L^2(R_+))`. At the pure-shift level the hierarchy is therefore exact:

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathbb N
\ \subsetneq\ 
(\log\mathbb Q_{>0})\cap[0,\infty)
\ \subsetneq\ 
\mathbb R_+,
}
\tag{PL387-16}
\]

corresponding respectively to analytic norm closure, selfadjoint norm closure, and selfadjoint strong closure.

This identifies precisely which topological operation destroys which part of the sampled exponent-lattice timing data. In particular, norm-selfadjoint completion retains the rational multiplicative group exactly, whereas strong closure erases its discreteness completely.

## 4. Generic-frequency control and RH relevance

The proof of (PL387-2) never uses primes. It applies verbatim to **every** additive subgroup `G subset R`. The almost-periodic symbol quotient detects whether a candidate pure shift frequency belongs to the subgroup, and Bohr orthogonality forces unit distance when it does not.

Consequently the exact recognition of `log Q_{>0}` is not itself rational-prime rigidity of the sort needed by the line mandate. It is the generic harmonic duality between a sampled additive subgroup and its character algebra. Replacing `{log p}` by any other frequency family simply replaces `G` by that family's integer span and yields the same theorem.

The result therefore closes a structural ambiguity but supplies no bridge to analytic continuation, the functional equation, Möbius cancellation, the explicit formula, or the zero divisor of zeta. Any RH-facing use of the norm-selfadjoint algebra must still add structure not determined by the bare subgroup-symbol pair, such as the positive-energy covariance, a Mellin/continuation target, or a distinguished Nyman/Weil compression already isolated elsewhere in the line.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The load-bearing operator theorem is classical. L. A. Coburn and R. G. Douglas, “Translation operators on the half-line,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* **62** (1969), 1010--1013, DOI https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.62.4.1010, study exactly the selfadjoint `C*`-algebra generated by the full half-line translation semigroup. Their Theorems 1--2 identify the quotient by the commutator ideal with the `C*`-algebra of Bohr almost-periodic functions and identify translations with exponential symbols. This is stronger than the abstract statement needed here.

The ordered-group setting is also classical prior art, as already audited in `PL-386`: G. J. Murphy, “Ordered groups and Toeplitz algebras,” *Journal of Operator Theory* **18** (1987), 303--326, and S. Adji, M. Laca, M. Nilsen and I. Raeburn, “Crossed products by semigroups of endomorphisms and the Toeplitz algebras of ordered groups,” *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **122** (1994), 1133--1141, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1994-1215024-1, place the sampled positive-cone algebras inside the standard Toeplitz theory of totally ordered abelian groups.

No novelty is claimed for these operator algebras, the almost-periodic quotient, or character orthogonality. The Mathia delta is the exact route-specific consequence (PL387-2), derived here from those classical ingredients: it closes the explicit accidental-pure-shift caveat in `PL-386` and makes the norm/strong closure hierarchy exact for the prime-frequency system.

A targeted novelty search did not locate a source stating this exact distance-one corollary in the prime-frequency notation. That absence is **not** used as an originality claim; (PL387-14)--(PL387-15) are an immediate classical-symbol consequence once the question is posed.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification conditions

This result classifies only **pure translation operators `S_t` inside the represented algebra**. It does not classify every element of `\mathcal T_G`, identify the unlabelled abstract `C*`-algebra across different ordered groups, or prove that the internal commutator ideal of `\mathcal T_G` has any particular form. Those stronger questions are not needed for (PL387-2).

The argument also does not infer norm separation from strong discontinuity. Its decisive ingredient is the Coburn--Douglas symbol quotient, so density of `G` in `R` is irrelevant. This is why a dense subgroup such as `log Q_{>0}` can still have unit operator-norm gaps to every omitted pure shift.

The finding should be corrected or withdrawn if any of the following fails:

1. the Coburn--Douglas quotient sends `S_t` to the almost-periodic character `e^{itx}`;
2. `sigma(\mathcal T_G)=AP_G` for the represented sampled shift algebra;
3. Bohr invariant-mean orthogonality (PL387-11) and its extension from trigonometric polynomials to `AP_G`;
4. quotient contractivity in (PL387-15);
5. the `PL-386` identity `\mathcal T_G=C^*(S_g:g in G^+)` for the sampled positive cone;
6. `span_Z{log p}=log Q_{>0}`.

All six items are exact or classical; no analytic-continuation assumption enters the proof.

## Consequence for the research line

The selfadjoint norm-closure branch is now sharply classified at the level of its most natural geometric observables, the pure half-line translations. It neither forgets the sampled subgroup as completely as the analytic polydisk algebra of `PL-385` nor fills its real closure as the strong operator topology does in `PL-383`. It remembers **exactly** the positive cone of the ordered integer span and keeps every omitted time at norm distance one.

For the rational-prime system this means that bare `C*` completion recovers the multiplicative rationals, but nothing in this exact recovery distinguishes their zeta function from a generic ordered-frequency Toeplitz system. Further operator-algebra work is useful for RH only if it introduces a zeta-sensitive enrichment before the classical almost-periodic symbol quotient scalarizes the construction to subgroup membership.
