# PL-023 — Full prime-shift invariance collapses normal operators to scalars

## Claim

Let `H^2` denote the standard Hardy Hilbert space of Dirichlet series, with orthonormal basis

```text
e_n(s) = n^(-s),  n >= 1,
```

and for each prime `p` let

```text
S_p = M_(p^(-s)),
S_p e_n = e_(pn).
```

In exponent coordinates, `S_p` is exactly translation by the prime basis vector `e_p`:

```text
v(n) -> v(n) + e_p.
```

Fernandez Vidal, Galicer, and Sevilla-Peris prove that a bounded operator on a Hardy space of Dirichlet series commutes with every prime-monomial multiplier `S_p` if and only if it is itself a multiplication operator `M_D` by a bounded Dirichlet series `D`.

For `H^2`, this has the following exact consequence:

```text
T bounded + [T,S_p]=0 for every prime p + T normal
    => T is a scalar multiple of the identity.
```

Consequently, if `H` is a self-adjoint (possibly unbounded) operator on `H^2` and one resolvent, for example `(H-i)^(-1)`, commutes with every `S_p`, then `H` is scalar. Thus **a nontrivial Hilbert–Pólya operator on the standard Bohr/Dirichlet Hardy Hilbert space cannot preserve the full positive prime-lattice translation symmetry in this strong commutant sense**.

Dropping normality does not produce a point-spectrum route inside the same bounded commutant: the same paper proves that a nonconstant multiplication operator has empty point spectrum, while its operator spectrum is the closure of the multiplier's image on `Re(s)>0`.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the route "construct a normal/self-adjoint zero operator inside the full prime-shift commutant." The commutant and multiplier-spectrum theorems are literature. The scalar-normal and self-adjoint-resolvent consequences below are elementary exact deductions in `H^2`; no novelty claim is made for them as abstract operator theory.

## The prime shifts are the coordinate translations of the exponent lattice

For

```text
n = product_q q^(v_q(n)),
```

multiplication by `p^(-s)` sends the Dirichlet monomial `n^(-s)` to `(pn)^(-s)`. Hence

```text
S_p : v(n) -> v(n)+e_p.
```

The family `{S_p}` therefore represents the free positive semigroup generated by all prime-coordinate translations. Requiring a bounded operator `T` to commute with every `S_p` is the most literal operator-theoretic implementation of invariance under the full positive exponent-lattice action.

Theorem 4.1 of Fernandez Vidal–Galicer–Sevilla-Peris states, for `1<=p,q<=infinity`, that a bounded operator `T:H_p->H_q` is a multiplication operator if and only if it commutes with all multipliers by the prime monomials `p_i^(-s)`. In the Hilbert case this gives

```text
{S_p : p prime}' = { M_D : D in H^infinity }.
```

So the entire bounded commutant is already the analytic multiplier algebra. There is no hidden larger class of bounded prime-translation-invariant operators in which a new self-adjoint spectrum might live.

## Normality forces a multiplier to be constant

Write

```text
D(s) = sum_(n>=1) a_n n^(-s),
T = M_D.
```

Since `e_1=1`,

```text
T e_1 = D,
||T e_1||_2^2 = sum_(n>=1) |a_n|^2.
```

On the other hand, for `n>1` the product `D n^(-s)` has no constant coefficient. Therefore

```text
T^* e_1 = conjugate(a_1) e_1,
||T^* e_1||_2^2 = |a_1|^2.
```

If `T` is normal, then for every vector `x`,

```text
||Tx|| = ||T^*x||.
```

Applying this to `e_1` gives

```text
sum_(n>=1) |a_n|^2 = |a_1|^2,
```

hence `a_n=0` for every `n>1`. Thus

```text
boxed:  T normal and [T,S_p]=0 for all p  =>  T=a_1 I.
```

In particular, the self-adjoint, unitary, and positive parts of the full prime-shift commutant are trivial up to scalars.

This is stronger than merely observing that the bare Kronecker flow has pure-point spectrum (`PL-011`). It says that even after allowing **every bounded operator compatible with all positive prime-coordinate shifts**, normality destroys all nontrivial spectral content.

## Unbounded self-adjoint operators do not escape if their resolvent has the symmetry

A Hilbert–Pólya candidate would naturally be unbounded, so boundedness of the preceding theorem is not by itself enough. Let `H` be self-adjoint on `H^2`. Its resolvent

```text
R = (H-i)^(-1)
```

is a bounded normal operator defined on all of `H^2`.

Assume the strong prime-translation invariance condition

```text
R S_p = S_p R  for every prime p.
```

The commutant theorem gives `R=M_D`. The scalar-normal argument then gives

```text
R=c I.
```

A resolvent is injective, so `c != 0`, and consequently

```text
H-i = c^(-1) I,
H = (i+c^(-1)) I.
```

Since `H` is self-adjoint, the final scalar is real. Hence

```text
boxed: self-adjoint H + one commuting nonreal resolvent
       => H is scalar.
```

This is the relevant unbounded obstruction. It does **not** assert that every operator whose formula mentions prime coordinates must commute with the shifts; it says that exact full translation invariance is incompatible with a nontrivial self-adjoint spectrum.

## Nonnormal multipliers still do not provide zero eigenvalues

Theorem 5.1 of the same paper describes the spectrum of a nonzero multiplier `M_D` on `H_p`. In particular,

```text
sigma(M_D) = closure(D(C_0)),
C_0 = {s : Re(s)>0},
```

and, when `D` is nonconstant,

```text
sigma_point(M_D) = empty.
```

Thus dropping normality leaves many nontrivial prime-shift-invariant operators, but **their spectrum is multiplier-value geometry rather than a discrete eigenvalue set**. A hypothetical zeta-related zero encoded in a multiplier cannot become an eigenvalue merely because the multiplier belongs to the prime-lattice commutant.

This should not be overread: approximate, residual, or continuous spectrum may still be nontrivial, and the theorem does not say that every spectral construction from multipliers is useless. The decisive negative is specifically against using the full prime-shift commutant as a direct normal/self-adjoint Hilbert–Pólya class or as a nonconstant point-spectrum class.

## Why this is not the same obstruction as PL-007 or PL-011

The canonical logarithmic generator from `PL-007`,

```text
A e_n = (log n)e_n,
```

does **not** commute with the prime shifts. Instead, on the natural common domain,

```text
[A,S_p] e_n
 = (log(pn)-log n)e_(pn)
 = (log p) S_p e_n,
```

so

```text
[A,S_p] = (log p) S_p.
```

This covariance relation is exactly how the energy functional

```text
log n = <v(n),(log p)_p>
```

survives: translating one prime coordinate changes the energy by `log p`. Therefore `A` escapes the new obstruction for a principled reason — it is covariant, not invariant.

Likewise `PL-011` concerns the Koopman spectrum of the compact prime Kronecker rotation, whereas the present result concerns the much larger operator commutant of all analytic coordinate shifts. The two negatives point in the same direction but are mathematically distinct.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The direct Dirichlet-series prior art is:

- Tomas Fernandez Vidal, Daniel Galicer, Pablo Sevilla-Peris, “Multipliers for Hardy spaces of Dirichlet series,” *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 75(2) (2025), 541–577, DOI `10.5802/aif.3658`, arXiv `2205.07961`.
- Their Theorem 4.1 characterizes bounded operators commuting with all prime monomial multipliers as multiplication operators.
- Their Theorem 5.1 identifies the multiplier spectrum and notes that nonconstant multipliers have empty point spectrum.

The fact that analytic multiplication/Toeplitz operators have severe normality constraints is also part of classical Hardy-space operator theory. The scalar-normal deduction here is therefore not presented as a new operator theorem. The durable contribution to this research line is the **specialized obstruction**: in exponent coordinates, the exact symmetry one would most naturally call "translation invariance of the full prime lattice" is already too strong to coexist with any nontrivial self-adjoint spectral candidate.

Searches by the equivalent formulations "commutant of prime monomial shifts," "coordinate-shift commutant," and "normal analytic multiplication operator" found no basis for treating this mechanism as novel. The 2025 theorem is a direct modern Dirichlet-series anchor and is sufficient for the reduction.

## Boundary conditions and adversarial checks

### This is a commutant obstruction, not a ban on lattice operators

A self-adjoint operator may be built from the prime lattice while failing to commute with `S_p`. The exact logarithmic generator `A` is the canonical example. Weighted shifts, Hankel/Helson operators, scattering constructions, boundary conditions, and adelic operators can all break or deform the symmetry and are not ruled out.

### Resolvent commutation is a strong and explicit hypothesis

For an unbounded `H`, the statement uses commutation of a bounded nonreal resolvent with every `S_p`. Merely having some formal core on which `[H,S_p]=0` is written is not automatically equivalent without domain/strong-commutation hypotheses. No such upgrade is assumed here.

### The result is specific to the standard Hardy/Bohr representation

The commutant theorem is applied on the standard Hardy space of Dirichlet series. A singular Hilbert space, an adelic completion, a quotient/model space, or a representation in which the prime translations act differently is additional structure outside this obstruction.

### Empty point spectrum is not empty operator spectrum

For a nonconstant multiplier, the point spectrum is empty, but the full spectrum is generally a nontrivial closed set. The result rules out a direct eigenvalue interpretation, not every possible resonance or boundary-value construction.

### No analytic-continuation claim is smuggled in

The commutant theorem is a functional-analytic statement about the Hardy space and its multipliers. The argument does not continue an Euler product across `Re(s)=1`. Any later proposal that inserts zeta itself as a multiplier must separately verify that it belongs to the required multiplier class; ordinary zeta does not become a bounded `H^infinity(C_0)` multiplier by analytic continuation.

## Falsification / audit tests

Withdraw or narrow this finding if any of the following fails:

1. the cited commutant theorem really characterizes all bounded `T:H^2->H^2` commuting with every prime monomial multiplier;
2. `M_D^*1=conjugate(a_1)1` in the Dirichlet coefficient Hilbert space;
3. normality implies `||T1||=||T^*1||`;
4. a nonconstant multiplier has empty point spectrum in the cited theorem;
5. a self-adjoint resolvent is bounded and normal, so the bounded scalar-normal argument applies when it commutes with every `S_p`.

All five are unconditional operator-theoretic statements and do not assume RH.

## Consequence for the research line

The natural spectral design space is now narrower:

```text
full prime-coordinate shift invariance
        + normal/self-adjoint spectrum
        => scalar triviality.
```

So a credible Hilbert–Pólya mechanism based on the exponent lattice must **break invariance while retaining structured covariance**, or introduce genuinely new analytic/arithmetic structure. The commutator

```text
[A,S_p] = (log p) S_p
```

provides a canonical model for what such a nontrivial relation can look like: the exact prime energies appear through failure of translation invariance, not through the commutant.

This redirects future searches away from operators that simply commute with all prime directions and toward operators whose controlled noncommutativity couples the prime-coordinate action to global analytic data capable of seeing the critical strip.
