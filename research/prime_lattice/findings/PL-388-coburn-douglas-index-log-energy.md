# PL-388 — Coburn–Douglas Breuer index identifies log n with half-line shift defect dimension but exits before the critical strip

## Claim

The natural index theory of the half-line translation algebra used in `PL-387` gives a rigorous operator interpretation of the prime-lattice energy
\[
\log n=\langle v(n),(\log p)_p\rangle:
\]
for the right translation \(S_t\) on \(L^2(\mathbb R_+)\), the Coburn–Douglas–Schaeffer–Singer real-valued Fredholm/Breuer index satisfies
\[
\operatorname{ind}(S_t)=-t.
\]
Hence
\[
-\operatorname{ind}(S_{\log n})
=\log n
=\sum_p v_p(n)\log p.
\]

This is exact but not RH-sensitive. It is a classical index invariant of the ambient half-line translation algebra, works for arbitrary frequency tuples, and on every Euler-product line \(\Re s=\sigma>1\) the almost-periodic zeta symbol has index zero. The standard almost-periodic Wiener–Hopf index therefore recovers prime-lattice energy but supplies no continuation-sensitive mechanism toward the critical strip.

## Exact derivation

Let \(S_t\) be the unilateral translation semigroup
\[
(S_t f)(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le x<t,\\
f(x-t),&x\ge t.
\end{cases}
\qquad t\ge0.
\]
Coburn–Douglas identify the C*-algebra generated by these Wiener–Hopf translations modulo its commutator ideal with the algebra \(AP(\mathbb R)\) of uniformly almost-periodic symbols. Coburn–Douglas–Schaeffer–Singer then classify connected components of the invertible symbols by mean motion: an invertible almost-periodic function can be written
\[
\phi(x)=e^{isx}e^{\psi(x)},
\]
and their topological index, equal to the analytic semifinite Fredholm index of the associated Wiener–Hopf operator, is
\[
\mathrm{t\!-​ind}(W_\phi)=-s.
\]

For the character \(\phi_t(x)=e^{itx}\), \(W_{\phi_t}\) is the right shift \(S_t\), so
\[
\operatorname{ind}(S_t)=-t.
\]
The same number is visible directly as the relative defect dimension: \(S_t\) is an isometry and
\[
I-S_tS_t^*=1_{[0,t)}.
\]
This defect projection has infinite ordinary rank but semifinite trace/dimension \(t\) in the translation-invariant von Neumann framework underlying the index theorem.

Now put \(t=\log n\). Since
\[
S_{\log n}
=\prod_p S_{\log p}^{\,v_p(n)}
\]
and the index is additive under products,
\[
-\operatorname{ind}(S_{\log n})
=\sum_p v_p(n)\bigl(-\operatorname{ind}(S_{\log p})\bigr)
=\sum_p v_p(n)\log p
=\log n.
\]
Thus the basic prime-lattice functional is exactly a Breuer–Fredholm/mean-motion invariant of the corresponding half-line shift.

## Zeta stress test

For fixed \(\sigma>1\),
\[
\log\zeta(\sigma+it)
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p^{-k\sigma}}{k}e^{-itk\log p}
\]
converges absolutely and uniformly in \(t\). Therefore it is a uniformly almost-periodic function, and
\[
\zeta(\sigma+it)=\exp(\log\zeta(\sigma+it))
\]
is an invertible element of \(AP(\mathbb R)\) lying in the identity component. Its Coburn–Douglas topological index is consequently
\[
\mathrm{t\!-​ind}\bigl(\zeta(\sigma+i\,\cdot)\bigr)=0.
\]
The same holds for \(1/\zeta(\sigma+i\,\cdot)\).

This statement is deliberately restricted to \(\sigma>1\). It uses the absolutely convergent Euler product/prime-power expansion there and gives no analytic continuation of the almost-periodic symbol across \(\Re s=1\). The index machinery therefore becomes trivial on the zeta symbol exactly in the region where the Euler product is safest, and by itself supplies no information about zeros in the critical strip.

## Adversarial controls

The construction is not specific to rational primes. For any positive frequency family \(b_j\) and any finitely supported \(\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^{(J)}\),
\[
-\operatorname{ind}\!\left(S_{\sum_j\alpha_jb_j}\right)
=\sum_j\alpha_jb_j.
\]
Hence the invariant survives arbitrary generic-frequency and Beurling-style replacements. It records the embedded additive time/energy, not arithmetic prime structure.

Helson phase twists likewise do not create a new index mechanism: on \(\sigma>1\) an absolutely convergent twisted Euler product is still the exponential of an almost-periodic logarithm and remains in the identity component whenever it is nonvanishing. The standard index is therefore zero there as well.

## Prior-art / novelty audit

The real-valued topological/analytic index and its value on exponential characters are classical prior art:

- L. A. Coburn and R. G. Douglas, “Translation operators on the half-line”, *Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA* **62** (1969), 1010–1013.
- L. A. Coburn, R. G. Douglas, D. G. Schaeffer, and I. M. Singer, “C*-algebras of operators on a half-space, II. Index theory”, *Publ. Math. IHÉS* **40** (1971), 69–79, https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1971__40__69_0/.

The specialization \(t=\log n\) and the prime-exponent identity are exact consequences of that theory and are not claimed as new mathematics. The useful content for this research line is the boundary they establish: after `PL-386` and `PL-387`, the natural C*-topological invariant left in the ambient half-line shift algebra is already known, is universally frequency-sensitive rather than prime-sensitive, and is zero on the zeta almost-periodic symbol throughout \(\Re s>1\).

## Implication for the line

This closes the route
\[
\text{ambient prime-shift C* algebra}
\longrightarrow
\text{standard AP/Wiener--Hopf index}
\longrightarrow
\text{RH-sensitive invariant}.
\]
The first arrow is mathematically meaningful: the index packages \(\log n\) as a spectral defect dimension. The second does not occur. Any surviving index-theoretic program must add structure that is not already determined by mean motion of the ambient almost-periodic symbol algebra—most plausibly an analytic-continuation-sensitive symbol class, a target-relative/Nyman component, or another arithmetic coupling that fails the generic-frequency control.
