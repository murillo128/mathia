# PL-047 — The Lapidus–Herichi spectral operator already quantizes the prime-log translation semigroup; continuation reduces to scalar functional calculus

## Claim

A direct operator construction suggested by the prime-exponent lattice is substantial prior art. Herichi and Lapidus rigorously define the infinitesimal shift

```text
partial_c = d/dt
```

on the weighted Hilbert space

```text
H_c = L^2(R, e^(-2ct) dt)
```

and the spectral operator

```text
a_c = zeta(partial_c).
```

For `c>1`, this is literally the operator generated by the positive prime-exponent lattice:

```text
n^(-partial_c) f(t) = f(t-log n),
a_c = sum_(n>=1) n^(-partial_c)
    = product_p (1-p^(-partial_c))^(-1)
```

with convergence in `B(H_c)`. Since

```text
log n = sum_p v_p(n) log p,
```

one has the exact finite-support factorization

```text
n^(-partial_c)
  = product_p p^(-v_p(n) partial_c).
```

Thus the route

```text
prime-exponent vector
 -> additive log-energy
 -> commuting prime translations
 -> operator-valued Dirichlet series / Euler product
 -> zeta operator
```

was already constructed and studied rigorously. It is not a new `prime_lattice` mechanism.

The important negative boundary is what happens after crossing `Re(s)=1`. In the cited functional-analytic framework the ordinary operator-valued Dirichlet series and Euler product are rigorously norm-convergent for `c>1`; the 2014 paper explicitly says that convergence of the operator-valued Euler product in the critical strip was conjectural there. The spectral operator in the critical strip is instead defined through the **meromorphic continuation of the scalar zeta function and the functional calculus for the normal operator `partial_c`**. Its spectrum is exactly

```text
sigma(a_c)
  = closure { zeta(c+it) : t in R }.
```

Therefore the continued operator does not provide an independent geometric continuation mechanism for the prime lattice. It transports the already-continued scalar function onto the vertical-line spectrum of `partial_c`.

This distinction is decisive for RH interpretation. The same papers prove that the truncated operators are invertible exactly when the corresponding vertical segment contains no zeta zero, and hence that quasi-invertibility of the family is equivalent to zero-freeness on the relevant vertical line. This is a rigorous operator reformulation of the zero problem, but the spectral mapping theorem shows why it is not a Hilbert–Pólya mechanism: **a zero ordinate `gamma` is not an eigenvalue of `a_c`; it is a parameter value at which the scalar multiplier `zeta(c+i gamma)` vanishes.**

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` for the route

```text
bare log-prime translation semigroup
 + one normal infinitesimal generator
 + functional calculus zeta(partial)
 + operator-valued Euler product / analytic continuation
 -> new spectral mechanism forcing RH.
```

The finding does not criticize the fractal-string inverse spectral program and does not say that operator-valued zeta constructions are vacuous. It identifies the exact information boundary relevant to this research line: once the construction is a scalar function of the single translation generator, its critical-strip spectrum is fixed by ordinary spectral mapping and inherits, rather than explains, the scalar zeta divisor.

## Exact prime-lattice realization in the convergent half-plane

Herichi–Lapidus use the logarithmic variable `x=e^t`. Their spectral operator acts additively as

```text
a_c f(t) = sum_(n>=1) f(t-log n).
```

For each prime,

```text
a_p f(t) = sum_(m>=0) f(t-m log p),
```

and they write

```text
a_c = product_p a_p
    = product_p (1-p^(-partial_c))^(-1).
```

The associated semigroup is

```text
(exp(-u partial_c) f)(t)=f(t-u),
||exp(-u partial_c)||=exp(-cu).
```

Putting `u=log n` gives

```text
||n^(-partial_c)|| = n^(-c).
```

Consequently the operator Dirichlet series converges absolutely in operator norm for `c>1`, exactly mirroring

```text
sum_n n^(-c)<infinity.
```

For a lattice point `alpha=v(n)`, define its energy

```text
E(alpha)=<alpha,(log p)_p>=log n.
```

Then the semigroup representation is simply

```text
T_alpha = exp(-E(alpha) partial_c),
T_alpha T_beta = T_(alpha+beta).
```

This is a one-dimensional representation of the full positive exponent lattice: all prime-coordinate information is compressed through the linear functional `E(alpha)`. Unique factorization guarantees that the distinct finite-support exponent vectors still give distinct translation lengths, but the operator architecture depends on them only through `log n`.

This is precisely the kind of prime-log operator sum that the research line had been treating as a natural candidate. It must therefore be regarded as established prior art.

## The Fourier model exposes the information content

There is an elementary unitary model that makes the limitation transparent. Define

```text
(U_c f)(t)=e^(-ct) f(t),
```

which sends `H_c` unitarily to ordinary `L^2(R)`. On the natural Sobolev domain,

```text
U_c partial_c U_c^(-1) = c + d/dt.
```

After the ordinary Fourier transform, `d/dt` is multiplication by `i xi` (up to the Fourier-sign convention). Hence

```text
partial_c  ~  multiplication by c+i xi.
```

and the measurable functional calculus gives

```text
a_c=zeta(partial_c)
  ~ multiplication by zeta(c+i xi).
```

This directly reproduces Herichi–Lapidus's spectral theorem

```text
sigma(a_c)=closure zeta(c+iR).
```

It also gives the correct interpretation of a zeta zero. If

```text
rho=c+i gamma,
zeta(rho)=0,
```

then the multiplier vanishes at `xi=gamma`. Thus `0` enters the spectrum of `a_c` (and the corresponding truncation loses invertibility), but `gamma` itself is not thereby an operator eigenvalue. The spectral variable of `a_c` is the **value** `zeta(c+i xi)`, not the ordinate `xi`.

This is structurally different from the Lax–Phillips realization in `PL-033`, where zeta zeros themselves occur as eigenvalues of a generator. Here normality and spectral mapping are exact but do not create a zero-ordinate spectrum.

## The critical line is an adjoint-fixed axis, but that is still transported symmetry

The same theory proves

```text
partial_c^* = 2c - partial_c,
sigma(partial_c)=c+iR.
```

At the distinguished value `c=1/2`,

```text
partial_(1/2)^* = 1-partial_(1/2).
```

Therefore the involution appearing in the completed zeta functional equation,

```text
s -> 1-s,
```

matches the adjoint reflection of the infinitesimal shift exactly at `1/2`. This is a genuine operator-geometric reason that the critical line is a self-dual axis in this representation and is closely aligned with the adelic self-duality already recorded in `PL-014`.

However, it does not force RH. If `Lambda(s)` denotes the completed zeta factor with functional equation `Lambda(s)=Lambda(1-s)`, then on the critical line its values are real, and in the Fourier model

```text
Lambda(partial_(1/2))
  ~ multiplication by Lambda(1/2+i xi),
```

so the completed functional-calculus operator is self-adjoint on its natural domain. This self-adjointness is obtained because the **scalar functional equation has already been inserted into the functional calculus**. Its spectrum is the closure of the real value set of `Lambda(1/2+it)`, not the set of Riemann-zero ordinates. Thus even this canonical self-adjoint critical-line operator does not supply a Hilbert–Pólya implication.

No claim is made here about an operator-valued Euler product on the critical line. The 2014 source explicitly separates the norm-convergent `c>1` Euler product from the critical-strip continuation of `zeta(partial_c)`.

## Analytic-continuation boundary

This point is central enough to state as a strict ledger.

For `c>1`:

```text
a_c
 = sum_n n^(-partial_c)
 = product_p (1-p^(-partial_c))^(-1)
```

is justified in operator norm. The exponent-lattice / Euler-product interpretation is literal.

For `0<c<1`:

```text
a_c=zeta(partial_c)
```

is defined through the normal functional calculus using the meromorphically continued scalar zeta function. Herichi–Lapidus obtain its spectrum from spectral mapping. Their 2014 article states that convergence of the operator-valued Euler product in the critical strip was conjectural in that framework.

Therefore one must not infer

```text
critical-strip functional-calculus operator
 => critical-strip Euler product
```

or treat the former as a continuation derived internally from independent prime-coordinate operators. The rigorous continuation that supports the RH criteria is scalar-zeta continuation transported operatorially.

## Adversarial controls

### Arbitrary scalar functional-calculus control

The operator architecture

```text
F(partial_c)
```

is available for any scalar function `F` admitted by the normal functional calculus. Its spectrum is correspondingly controlled by the image `F(c+iR)`. Hence self-adjointness on an adjoint-fixed line, invertibility, spectral gaps, and range geometry can all be inherited from scalar properties of `F` without supplying a new mechanism for those properties.

This is the decisive matched control: **functional calculus is a representation layer, not arithmetic rigidity.** Zeta-specific information enters through the choice `F=zeta` (and, in `c>1`, through the rational-prime Euler factorization), not through the one-generator operator geometry itself.

### Beurling/generalized-prime control

If a generalized-prime system supplies a generalized zeta `Z(s)` with suitable continuation, the same formal architecture `Z(partial_c)` and the same spectral-mapping logic apply. The rational primes distinguish the concrete scalar function and its analytic theory, but a theorem using only

```text
normal translation generator
+ scalar functional calculus
+ vertical-line spectral mapping
```

cannot discriminate the ordinary prime system from a matched generalized one.

### Relation to existing Prime-Lattice negatives

`PL-007` found that canonical diagonal/translation prime-flow operators naturally reproduce summability boundaries but do not produce the Riemann-zero spectrum. `PL-023` showed that demanding full prime-shift commutation together with normality collapses bounded operators on the Bohr coefficient space to scalars. `PL-024` showed that the Bost–Connes Hamiltonian already realizes exact `log p` covariance. The present finding closes a complementary route on a different Hilbert space: **even allowing the nontrivial normal infinitesimal translation generator and applying zeta itself to it is classical, and the critical-strip zero information then enters by spectral mapping.**

## Falsification and escape tests

The negative boundary would be materially narrowed by any of the following:

1. an operator-valued Euler-product continuation into the critical strip obtained from prime-coordinate operators by a mechanism that is not equivalent to first inserting scalar zeta continuation;
2. a canonical additional operator `B` not lying in the scalar functional calculus of `partial_c`, with a trace/commutator/positivity relation coupling the prime translations and forcing zero localization;
3. a construction in which the Riemann ordinates occur as genuine eigenvalues/resonances rather than merely as zeros of the Fourier multiplier `zeta(c+i xi)`;
4. a target-relative or adelic completion whose positivity/self-adjointness follows from independent arithmetic structure rather than from the already-known scalar functional equation;
5. a theorem exploiting interactions among multiple prime-coordinate operators that are destroyed by compression to the single energy `log n`.

These are genuine escape routes. They are precisely outside the one-generator functional-calculus construction audited here.

## Consequence for the research line

The operator search can now discard another broad class of rediscoveries:

```text
prime directions e_p
    -> translation lengths log p
    -> one infinitesimal shift partial_c
    -> zeta(partial_c)
    -> spectral mapping / invertibility criterion.
```

That chain already exists, including an operator-valued Euler product in the convergent half-plane and rigorous RH-equivalent invertibility statements after analytic continuation.

The durable lesson is not that operator methods fail. It is that **compressing the exponent lattice to the single generator `partial_c` and then applying scalar zeta cannot explain the divisor that was used to define the continued operator.** Future work should require extra structure that does not reduce to `F(partial_c)`: a genuinely multi-prime operator relation, a target-relative Nyman/Weil coupling, an adelic trace/positivity identity, or another arithmetic observable whose rigidity survives matched functional-calculus and generalized-prime controls.
