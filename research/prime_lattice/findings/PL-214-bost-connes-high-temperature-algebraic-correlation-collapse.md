# PL-214 — High-temperature Bost–Connes algebraic KMS correlations collapse to finite Dirichlet polynomials

## Claim

The boundary left explicitly open by `PL-212` — whether finite **noncommuting** products involving the Bost–Connes semigroup isometries can restore Riemann-zero sensitivity after the high-temperature KMS symmetry has removed it from the cyclotomic one-point channel — has a clean negative answer on the canonical dense algebraic core.

Let `H_BC` be the classical dense `*`-subalgebra of the rational Bost–Connes algebra generated by the multiplicative isometries `mu_n` and the cyclotomic unitaries `e(r)`, `r in Q/Z`. Bost–Connes' algebraic presentation gives a linear basis

\[
\mu_m e(r)\mu_n^*,\qquad (m,n)=1,
\]

and the time evolution acts diagonally on that basis,

\[
\sigma_t\!\left(\mu_m e(r)\mu_n^*\right)
=\left(\frac mn\right)^{it}\mu_m e(r)\mu_n^*.
\]

For every real `0<beta<=1`, let `phi_beta` be the unique Bost–Connes `KMS_beta` state. Then for every fixed `x in H_BC` there is a **finite Dirichlet polynomial**

\[
P_x(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{J} c_j N_j^{-s},
\qquad N_j\in\mathbb N,
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{\phi_\beta(x)=P_x(\beta)\qquad(0<\beta\le1).}
\]

The statement persists for every fixed finite equilibrium correlation. If `x_1,...,x_k in H_BC` and `t_1,...,t_k in R`, then there is another finite Dirichlet polynomial `P_{x,t}` with

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta\!\left(
\sigma_{t_1}(x_1)\cdots\sigma_{t_k}(x_k)
\right)
=P_{x,t}(\beta).
}
\]

Thus every finite algebraic high-temperature KMS observable/correlation extends in the complexified temperature variable to an **entire finite exponential polynomial**. The canonical high-temperature state evaluation contributes no factor `zeta(s)`, `1/zeta(s)`, completed zeta divisor, Euler-product singularity, or analytic-continuation mechanism. It can of course have zeros of its own, but those are zeros of a finite exponential polynomial determined by the chosen finite observable; they are not a source-forced realization of the Riemann zero divisor.

Equivalently on the dynamical side, for fixed algebraic `x`,

\[
t\longmapsto \phi_\beta(x^*\sigma_t(x))
\]

is a finite trigonometric polynomial whose frequencies lie in

\[
\log\mathbb Q_+^\times
=\left\{
\left\langle \alpha,(\log p)_p\right\rangle:
\alpha\in\mathbb Z^{(\mathcal P)}
\right\}.
\]

So a finite algebraic probe sees only finitely many signed prime-lattice frequencies. The type-`III_1` completion of `PL-213` and any genuinely infinite spectral behavior arise only after an infinite completion; they are not already hidden in finite algebraic KMS correlations.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The negative is deliberately restricted to **fixed finite elements and fixed finite correlations in the canonical dense Bost–Connes algebraic core, evaluated in the unique high-temperature KMS state**. It does not rule out source-forced norm limits, unbounded observables, relative modular operators, distributional/renormalized limits, low-temperature broken-symmetry data, or the separate adelic/Weil trace-formula completions.

## 1. The algebraic core is graded by signed prime-lattice energy

The rational Bost–Connes algebra is generated by `mu_n` and `e(r)`. The classical relations imply that its dense algebraic Hecke core has basis

\[
x_{m,n,r}=\mu_m e(r)\mu_n^*,
\qquad (m,n)=1.
\]

The time evolution fixes `e(r)` and satisfies

\[
\sigma_t(\mu_n)=n^{it}\mu_n.
\]

Hence every basis vector is a time-eigenoperator:

\[
\sigma_t(x_{m,n,r})
=\exp\!\left(it(\log m-\log n)\right)x_{m,n,r}.
\]

In exponent coordinates,

\[
\log m-\log n
=\left\langle v(m)-v(n),(\log p)_p\right\rangle.
\]

This is the group-completed version of the positive prime-lattice energy used throughout the line. The important point for the present test is that every algebraic element contains only **finitely many** such grades.

## 2. KMS invariance kills every off-diagonal grade

Every KMS state is invariant under the underlying time evolution. Therefore

\[
\phi_\beta(x_{m,n,r})
=\phi_\beta(\sigma_t(x_{m,n,r}))
=\left(\frac mn\right)^{it}\phi_\beta(x_{m,n,r})
\]

for every real `t`. If `m!=n`, choose `t` for which `(m/n)^(it) != 1`; then

\[
\boxed{\phi_\beta(\mu_m e(r)\mu_n^*)=0\qquad(m\ne n).}
\]

Because the basis is chosen with `(m,n)=1`, the only basis elements with `m=n` have `m=n=1`. Thus the restriction of the unique high-temperature KMS state to the entire algebraic basis is already determined by the cyclotomic coefficients

\[
\phi_\beta(e(r)).
\]

This closes the most direct loophole in `PL-212`: inserting a finite number of noncommuting semigroup isometries does not create a new surviving high-temperature channel. Their nonzero time grades are annihilated by equilibrium invariance before any Riemann-zero-sensitive analytic structure can appear.

For completeness, without reducing to the coprime basis one also has the exact diagonal formula

\[
\phi_\beta(\mu_k e(r)\mu_k^*)
=k^{-\beta}\phi_\beta(e(r)).
\]

Indeed the KMS identity with `a=mu_k` and `b=e(r)mu_k^*`, together with

\[
\sigma_{i\beta}(\mu_k)=k^{-\beta}\mu_k,
\qquad \mu_k^*\mu_k=1,
\]

gives the formula directly. Even diagonal semigroup insertions therefore add only another finite Dirichlet monomial.

## 3. The unique high-temperature cyclotomic coefficient is already finite

Write `r=a/b` in lowest terms. The Bost–Connes high-temperature classification gives, for every `0<beta<=1`,

\[
\phi_\beta(e(a/b))
=\frac{f_{1-\beta}(b)}{f_1(b)},
\]

where

\[
f_k(b)=\sum_{d\mid b}\mu(d)\left(\frac bd\right)^k.
\]

Since `f_1(b)=varphi(b)` is the Euler totient, this is

\[
\phi_\beta(e(a/b))
=\frac1{\varphi(b)}
\sum_{d\mid b}\mu(d)\frac bd
\left(\frac bd\right)^{-\beta}.
\]

The sum is finite. Therefore it is literally a finite Dirichlet polynomial in the temperature variable. Equivalently,

\[
\phi_\beta(e(a/b))
=b^{-\beta}
\prod_{p\mid b}
\frac{1-p^{\beta-1}}{1-p^{-1}},
\]

again a finite Euler expression involving only the primes dividing the fixed denominator `b`.

Combining this formula with the basis reduction proves the first boxed claim. No infinite Euler product is being continued, and no identity from `Re(s)>1` has been transported into the critical strip. The formula is the actual KMS-state value in the real high-temperature regime and happens, because it is finite, to possess an elementary entire extension in a complex variable.

The `beta=1/2` point has no new analytic status here. For each fixed algebraic observable it is just one regular evaluation point of a finite exponential polynomial. This is stronger than merely observing, as in `PL-213`, that the factor type is constant through `beta=1/2`: the entire finite algebraic correlation hierarchy also has no zeta-divisor singularity waiting at that value.

## 4. Every finite equilibrium correlation remains finite-frequency

Take fixed `x_1,...,x_k in H_BC` and real times `t_1,...,t_k`. The algebraic core is a `*`-algebra and is preserved by `sigma_t`, so

\[
y=\sigma_{t_1}(x_1)\cdots\sigma_{t_k}(x_k)
\]

is again an element of `H_BC`. Reducing `y` in the classical basis yields a finite sum. The off-diagonal grades vanish under `phi_beta`, and each surviving cyclotomic coefficient is the finite divisor sum above. Hence

\[
\phi_\beta(y)
=\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_j(t_1,\ldots,t_k)N_j^{-\beta}
\]

for finitely many positive integers `N_j`.

In particular, the two-point function of a fixed algebraic observable has the form

\[
\phi_\beta(x^*\sigma_t(x))
=\sum_{j=1}^{J} a_j e^{it\lambda_j},
\qquad
\lambda_j\in\log\mathbb Q_+^\times.
\]

Its Fourier spectral measure is finite atomic. The frequencies are exactly signed combinations of the prime energies `log p`, but finite algebraic correlation does not turn them into the ordinates of zeta zeros, a determinant over the zero divisor, or a trace formula with an infinite prime/zero duality.

This provides a useful reconciliation with `PL-213`. The high-temperature GNS factor is type `III_1` and has full ratio set only after completion. The dense algebraic vectors are built from finite rational-scale grades; the full modular scale is an infinite-completion phenomenon. Coarse type-III structure and finite algebraic correlation data fail for different but compatible reasons to single out the critical half.

## 5. Why the low-temperature `1/zeta` factor does not contradict the collapse

For `beta>1`, an extremal Gibbs/KMS state indexed by arithmetic symmetry data `rho` satisfies the classical formula

\[
\phi_{\beta,\rho}(e(r))
=\frac{\operatorname{Li}_\beta(\rho(\zeta_r))}{\zeta(\beta)}.
\]

Thus the low-temperature broken-symmetry channel really does contain a `1/zeta(beta)` normalization, as used in `PL-212`. The present result says that this denominator does **not** survive as the evaluation formula of the unique positive KMS state after crossing the phase transition at `beta=1`.

There is no contradiction because these are different equilibrium-state regimes. The high-temperature formula

\[
\frac{f_{1-\beta}(b)}{f_1(b)}
\]

is not obtained by analytically continuing

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Li}_\beta(\rho(\zeta_r))}{\zeta(\beta)}.
\]

The first is the actual unique KMS state for `0<beta<=1`; the second belongs to the broken-symmetry Gibbs family for `beta>1`. Continuing the second meromorphically into the critical strip produces scalar functions, not positive high-temperature KMS states. This is the same analytic-continuation boundary diagnosed for one cyclotomic harmonic in `PL-212`, now extended to the whole finite noncommutative algebraic core.

## 7. Adversarial boundaries and matched controls

**Finite does not mean zero-free.** A finite Dirichlet polynomial can have complex zeros, and arbitrary complex coefficients can be chosen to place finite sets of zeros almost at will. That is exactly why such zeros cannot count as a Riemann mechanism without an independent source-forcing theorem. The claim is not that `P_x` has no zeros; it is that the Bost–Connes high-temperature evaluation map itself contributes no Riemann zeta divisor or infinite analytic continuation.

**A beta-dependent observable can program any answer.** The theorem fixes `x` before varying `beta`. If the coefficients of `x` are allowed to depend on `beta`, on `zeta(beta)`, or on known zero data, one can manufacture arbitrary readouts. Such programming is excluded by the research contract and by the controls developed in `PL-208`--`PL-210`.

**The full C*-completion is not covered by the finite-polynomial statement.** A fixed norm-limit of algebraic observables can involve infinitely many lattice grades. Although each `phi_beta` is continuous at fixed `beta`, the dependence of `phi_beta(x)` on `beta` for a nonalgebraic limit need not remain a finite Dirichlet polynomial. This is the principal surviving Bost–Connes escape and must be audited for convergence, source-forcing, and programmability rather than inferred from finite truncations.

**Unbounded and relative-modular constructions are outside the negative.** An unbounded affiliated observable, a relative modular operator between states, or an infinite scattering/determinant limit could have much richer spectral data. `PL-213` only removed the coarse factor-type/ratio-set selector; the present finding only removes the finite algebraic-correlation selector.

**The adelic/Weil trace-formula route is a different completion.** Connes' adele-class trace formula and completed Weil positivity import global harmonic structure absent from a fixed finite Bost–Connes algebraic observable. Their zero-sensitive terms are not contradicted by the present finite-core collapse.

## Falsification / audit tests

The finding would be materially falsified or narrowed if any of the following failed:

1. the dense Bost–Connes algebraic core were not finitely spanned by the standard coprime monomials `mu_m e(r) mu_n^*`;
2. the time evolution did not grade those monomials by `(m/n)^(it)`;
3. a `KMS_beta` state were not invariant under that time evolution, allowing a nonzero expectation on an off-diagonal grade `m!=n`;
4. the unique high-temperature formula for `phi_beta(e(a/b))` were not the finite arithmetic expression `f_(1-beta)(b)/f_1(b)` for `0<beta<=1`;
5. multiplication/time translation of finitely many algebraic elements could leave the algebraic core and generate an infinite expansion; or
6. there existed a fixed finite algebraic Bost–Connes correlation whose actual high-temperature KMS evaluation contained a source-forced Riemann `zeta` divisor not reducible to the finite formulas above.

Items 1--4 are classical Bost–Connes structure/KMS facts. Item 5 is immediate from the definition of the algebraic `*`-core. Item 6 is excluded by their finite linear combination unless additional nonalgebraic data have been inserted.
