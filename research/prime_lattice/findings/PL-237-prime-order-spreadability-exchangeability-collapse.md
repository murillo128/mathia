# PL-237 — Generic prime-order spreadability collapses back to full exchangeability

## Claim

`PL-234`--`PL-236` show that full prime-permutation symmetry is too coarse: on the ordinary quasi-local prime tensor product it yields de Finetti product mixtures, and finite-color variants still remove finite-prime entanglement. A natural remaining escape is to keep the **order** of the rational primes while abandoning arbitrary permutations. Write

\[
p_0<p_1<p_2<\cdots
\]

and regard the prime-coordinate algebra as the ordinary infinite tensor product

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathcal P}=\bigotimes_{j\in\mathbb N}^{\min} A,
\]

with site `j` corresponding to `p_j`. Suppose a state `Phi` is invariant only under passing to ordered subsequences: for every strictly increasing map `f:N->N`, every finite family of distinct indices `j_1,...,j_k`, and local observables `a_1,...,a_k in A`, the finite-dimensional distribution is unchanged after `j -> f(j)`. This is the standard notion of **spreadability**.

For the ordinary tensor-product completion, this apparently weaker order symmetry does **not** retain any extra prime-order structure. Aiello--Del Vecchio--Rossi prove a Ryll--Nardzewski theorem for quantum stochastic processes whose distribution factors through suitable quotients of the infinite free product; they state explicitly that the theorem applies to ordinary infinite tensor products. In this setting spreadability implies exchangeability. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{prime-order spreadability}
\Longrightarrow
\text{full prime exchangeability}.
}
\]

Combining this with Størmer's classification used in `PL-234`, every such state is a de Finetti mixture

\[
\boxed{
\Phi=\int_{S(A)}\rho^{\otimes\mathcal P}\,d\nu(\rho),
}
\]

so every finite-prime marginal is separable and all correlations are classical mixing correlations. Therefore **the order type of the rational primes, used only through generic subsequence invariance, cannot supply the source-forced arithmetic relation sought after `PL-236`**. A surviving order-based mechanism must use numerical scale data such as `log p`, prime gaps, ratios, adjacency with its actual labels, or another arithmetic relation that is not invariant under arbitrary increasing re-embeddings.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The spreadability/exchangeability theorem is prior art, published in 2026. The only derived step is its specialization to the increasing enumeration of rational-prime tensor factors and composition with the already stored Størmer/de Finetti control of `PL-234`. No novelty is claimed for either theorem.

## 1. Exact order-only model

The bare exponent lattice has countably many prime-coordinate directions. After ordering the rational primes increasingly, a completion with identical one-prime observable algebra `A` is canonically a one-sided chain

\[
A^{\otimes\mathbb N}.
\]

There are two very different ways in which the prime order might enter this chain.

The first is **order type only**: the state is required to look the same after replacing the prime sequence by any ordered subsequence. Formally, if `i_j:A->A^(tensor N)` denotes the `j`-th embedding, spreadability requires

\[
\Phi\!\left(i_{j_1}(a_1)\cdots i_{j_k}(a_k)\right)
=
\Phi\!\left(i_{f(j_1)}(a_1)\cdots i_{f(j_k)}(a_k)\right)
\]

for every strictly increasing `f:N->N`.

The second is genuinely arithmetic: the dynamics or observable may depend on the actual values `p_j`, `log p_j`, `p_{j+1}-p_j`, `log(p_{j+1}/p_j)`, congruence/Frobenius data, or another source-defined relation. Such a model is generally **not** spreadable, because an arbitrary increasing injection changes those labels and gaps.

The present finding concerns only the first case. This distinction is load-bearing. It closes the generic idea that merely replacing permutation symmetry by order-preserving symmetry could retain enough arithmetic information while preserving a strong infinite-prime invariance.

## 2. Why the tensor-product quotient satisfies the 2026 theorem

Aiello, Del Vecchio, and Rossi formulate quantum stochastic processes as states on the infinite free product of a fixed sample `C*`-algebra and study distributions factoring through a quotient

\[
*_{\mathbb N}A/I.
\]

Their Ryll--Nardzewski theorem gives spreadability `=>` exchangeability under two structural hypotheses on the quotient: the defining ideal is invariant under finite permutations of the indices, and the quotient is norm-generated by ordered monomials. The paper notes explicitly that ordinary infinite tensor products satisfy the theorem.

For the usual tensor-product quotient these hypotheses are transparent. The ideal imposing commutation of disjoint tensor factors is invariant under relabelling the sites. Because observables at distinct tensor sites commute, every local monomial can be reordered into increasing site order, so ordered monomials span the quasi-local algebra densely. Therefore a spreadable state on `A^(tensor N)` is exchangeable.

This is precisely the regime of `PL-234`: identical one-site prime algebras with the ordinary quasi-local tensor completion. No trace-class assumption, normality in `ell^2(N)`, Gibbs form, finite-dimensional local Hilbert space, or product-state ansatz is needed for the implication from spreadability to exchangeability.

## 3. Composition with Størmer gives the full collapse

Once spreadability has promoted itself to full exchangeability, `PL-234` applies verbatim. Størmer's theorem classifies the symmetric state space of the infinite tensor power: its extreme points are identical product states `rho^(tensor N)`, and every symmetric state is a barycentric mixture of them.

Consequently, for distinct primes `p_{j_1},...,p_{j_k}`,

\[
\Phi\!\left(a_1^{(p_{j_1})}\cdots a_k^{(p_{j_k})}\right)
=
\int_{S(A)}\prod_{r=1}^k\rho(a_r)\,d\nu(\rho).
\]

Thus generic order-subsequence invariance does not merely fail to encode prime gaps: it erases the order altogether at the level of the state. Finite-dimensional distributions become invariant under arbitrary finite permutations of the sampled prime sites.

In particular, if the one-site system is the hard-core/square-free coordinate `C^2`, or any fixed finite occupation cutoff, every finite-prime marginal is a convex combination of product states and hence fully separable. For infinite-dimensional one-site algebras the same state-level product-mixture formula remains the relevant Størmer conclusion in the ordinary tensor product setting.

## 4. Arithmetic interpretation: order type is not prime order data

The increasing enumeration `p_0<p_1<...` contains arithmetic information only when the **values attached to the indices** survive. The abstract ordered set `(P,<)` has the same order type as `(N,<)`. Every infinite subsequence is again an ordered copy of `N`, and spreadability explicitly declares all such copies distributionally identical.

The 2026 Ryll--Nardzewski theorem makes the resulting information loss exact in the noncommutative tensor model: on an ordinary tensor product, there is no residual quantum state invariant that can distinguish the weaker subsequence symmetry from full exchangeability.

This supplies a useful matched control for the frontier identified after `PL-236`. Replacing full prime permutations by generic order-preserving injections sounds arithmetically less destructive, but the ordinary tensor completion does not notice the difference. Any putative invariant that survives only because sites have a left-to-right order is therefore generic sequence theory, not a rational-prime mechanism.

The conclusion is stronger than the elementary observation that the abstract order of the primes is isomorphic to `N`. One might have hoped that a noncommutative state could retain order-sensitive correlations while being invariant under subsequence selection. That possibility exists for general noncommutative stochastic processes, but the ordinary tensor-product relations rule it out.

## 5. Important boundary: noncommutative spreadability can differ from exchangeability

The negative is intentionally not stated for arbitrary noncommutative completions. Köstler's noncommutative extended de Finetti theory already showed that, in general, spreadability is strictly weaker than exchangeability. The 2026 Aiello--Del Vecchio--Rossi paper sharpens the boundary and constructs spreadable but nonexchangeable product states on suitable **twisted tensor products**, where disjoint-site commutation is modified by a bicharacter.

This is not an escape for free. A twisted tensor relation is an additional coupling supplied before state evaluation. In the present research contract it would have to be derived from rational-prime arithmetic and survive the generalized-prime/programmability controls; choosing a twist merely because it leaves room for nonexchangeable states would be an arbitrary re-encoding.

The literature therefore gives both sides of the falsification test:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{ordinary prime tensor product + spreadability}
&&\Longrightarrow\text{exchangeability/de Finetti},\\
&\text{general twisted/nonlocal completion}
&&\not\Longrightarrow\text{exchangeability in general}.
\end{aligned}
\]

What remains potentially interesting is exactly the second line **only when the non-tensor relation is source-forced by the arithmetic problem**.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The primary new literature anchor for this pass is:

Valeriano Aiello, Simone Del Vecchio, Stefano Rossi, “On the Ryll-Nardzewski Theorem for Quantum Stochastic Processes,” *Advances in Mathematics* **501** (2026), 111119, DOI `10.1016/j.aim.2026.111119`; arXiv:2509.02270v2 (30 June 2026). The paper proves a Ryll--Nardzewski theorem for quotient models of quantum stochastic processes, explicitly includes ordinary infinite tensor products among the cases where spreadability and exchangeability coincide, and also supplies twisted-product counterexamples outside the hypotheses.

The exchangeable-state classification used after that implication is the classical theorem already audited in `PL-234`:

Erling Størmer, “Symmetric states of infinite tensor products of C*-algebras,” *Journal of Functional Analysis* **3** (1969), 48--68, DOI `10.1016/0022-1236(69)90050-0`.

A bounded search combining prime numbers/Riemann zeta with spreadability, Ryll--Nardzewski, quantum stochastic processes, and de Finetti did not locate a distinct RH or rational-prime application of the 2026 theorem. That absence is not evidence of novelty. The finding records only a rigorous route-closing specialization: the generic order-symmetry escape from `PL-234` is already controlled by published noncommutative probability.

No claim here concerns analytic continuation of zeta, its zero divisor, the functional equation, or Weil positivity. The result is a structural negative about which prime-coordinate symmetries can plausibly preserve the arithmetic information those mechanisms would need.

## 7. Falsification boundaries

This finding must not be used to claim that **all prime-order constructions** collapse. It does not cover a state or dynamics depending on the actual prime values, `log p_j`, gaps `p_{j+1}-p_j`, ratios, or any label-sensitive relation. Those data break generic spreadability and are precisely what remains live.

It also does not cover a finite prime truncation, where exact infinite spreadability and Størmer's theorem are unavailable; nor does it cover arbitrary free, braided, twisted, crossed-product, adelic, or other nonlocal completions. The 2026 paper explicitly demonstrates that noncommutative spreadability need not imply exchangeability outside the quotient hypotheses.

Finally, invariance only under one special shift `j->j+1` is stationarity, not spreadability. A stationary prime-chain model can retain nearest-neighbor or longer-range correlations and is not closed by this argument. The no-go requires invariance under **all** increasing subsequence embeddings.

The finding should be narrowed or withdrawn if the ordinary infinite tensor product fails the hypotheses of the Aiello--Del Vecchio--Rossi Ryll--Nardzewski theorem, or if a spreadable state on that tensor product exists that is not exchangeable. The cited paper explicitly identifies ordinary infinite tensor products as covered examples, so these are direct literature-level falsification conditions.

## Consequence for the research line

The candidate escape “retain prime order but keep a generic infinite-prime subsequence symmetry” is no longer live in the ordinary quasi-local tensor completion. It collapses to the already-controlled exchangeable/de Finetti sector.

The frontier after `PL-236` can therefore be sharpened from “prime ordering/gaps may break exchangeability” to a stricter requirement: **the source must retain quantitative arithmetic labels or relations, not merely the abstract order type of the primes**. Actual `log p`, prime gaps or ratios, a source-defined adjacency coupling, or a genuinely arithmetic non-tensor relation remain admissible. Generic order-spreadability does not.
