# PL-353 — Finite coefficient constraints survive prime-log overcompleteness only through a dual Paley–Wiener interpolant

**Evidence:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-FUNCTIONAL-ANALYSIS + CLASSICAL-PALEY-WIENER + DECISIVE-BOUNDARY`

**Status:** `COEFFICIENT-CONSTRAINT-SURVIVAL-IFF-DUAL-INTERPOLANT + FINITE-LOG-MOMENT-AND-POWER-WEIGHT-CONSTRAINTS-ARE-INVISIBLE + INTERMEDIATE-FAMILY-NEEDS-CONTINUOUS-DUAL-OR-NONFINITE-LINEAR-COUPLING`

## Precise claim

`PL-352` left coefficient coupling as one of the few ways a source-selected Prime-Lattice family might avoid the automatic form-core collapse caused by the overcomplete prime-log frequencies. A finite number of linear coefficient relations is not enough by itself. There is an exact functional-analytic gate determining whether such a relation survives closure in the local Sobolev/form topology.

Fix `A>0`, `0<s<1/2`, `I=(-A,A)`, and

\[
\Lambda_{\mathbb P}:=\{\log p:p\text{ prime}\}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal E:=\operatorname{span}_{\rm alg}\{e^{i\lambda x}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbb P}\}
\subset H^s(I).
\]

By `PL-348`, `mathcal E` is dense in `H^r(I)` for every finite Sobolev order `r`, hence in particular in `H^s(I)`. Distinct exponentials are linearly independent on `I`, so for any weight family `w=(w_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbb P}}` the coefficient functional

\[
L_w\!\left(\sum_{\lambda\in F}c_\lambda e^{i\lambda x}\right)
:=\sum_{\lambda\in F}c_\lambda w_\lambda
\tag{PL353-1}
\]

is well-defined on `mathcal E`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\ker L_w}^{\,H^s(I)}=H^s(I)
\iff
L_w\text{ is not continuous for the }H^s(I)\text{ norm on }\mathcal E.
}
\tag{PL353-2}
\]

Equivalently, the coefficient relation survives as a genuine closed restriction exactly when there is a dual distribution `T in (H^s(I))^*` whose Fourier-Laplace transform

\[
F_T(z):=\langle T,e^{izx}\rangle
\tag{PL353-3}
\]

interpolates the prescribed weights:

\[
\boxed{F_T(\lambda)=w_\lambda\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbb P}).}
\tag{PL353-4}
\]

For finitely many coefficient relations `L_{w^(1)},...,L_{w^(m)}`, their joint kernel is dense if and only if **no nonzero linear combination** of the weight families admits such a continuous-dual interpolant.

This immediately rules out several natural finite couplings. For any finite set of real points `x_a in I`, finite derivative orders, and any finite set of positive numbers `sigma_b`, the joint constraints generated by weights of the forms

\[
w_{p,a,j}=(i\log p)^j p^{ix_a}
\tag{PL353-5}
\]

and

\[
w_{p,b,P}=P(\log p)p^{-\sigma_b},
\qquad \sigma_b>0,
\tag{PL353-6}
\]

with fixed polynomials `P`, have dense joint kernel in `H^s(I)` provided the listed constraints are algebraically independent. In particular, each of the following still leaves a form-core modulation family:

\[
\sum_p c_p=0,
\qquad
\sum_p c_p(\log p)^j=0\quad(0\le j\le m),
\qquad
\sum_p\frac{c_p}{p^\sigma}=0\quad(\sigma>0),
\tag{PL353-7}
\]

and any finite independent collection of their real-phase/log-polynomial variants covered by (PL353-5)--(PL353-6).

Therefore **finite centering, finitely many logarithmic moments, and finite Nyman-like `p^{-sigma}` pole-cancellation relations do not by themselves create an intermediate endpoint-modulation family**. After multiplication by the endpoint envelope `u_A`, their states are still a form core for the completed localized Weil form. A coefficient-based escape from `PL-352` must cross the sharper gate (PL353-4): it must produce a relation that is continuous in the actual local form topology, or else use an essentially infinite, nonlinear, positivity/cone, target-relative, or cross-aperture coupling not reducible to finitely many discontinuous coefficient functionals.

## 1. Dense-kernel lemma

Let `H` be a Hilbert space, `E subset H` a dense linear subspace, and `L:E->C` a nonzero linear functional. Then

\[
\overline{\ker L}=H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L\text{ is not continuous in the norm inherited from }H.
\tag{PL353-8}
\]

If `L` is continuous, it extends to a nonzero continuous functional on `H`, so its kernel has proper closed closure. Conversely, suppose `overline{ker L}` is proper. Hahn--Banach gives a nonzero `phi in H^*` vanishing on `overline{ker L}`. On `E`, `ker L subset ker phi`. Since `L` is nonzero and scalar-valued, this forces

\[
\phi|_E=cL
\]

for some nonzero scalar `c`; hence `L` is continuous on `E`.

For a finite map

\[
L=(L_1,\ldots,L_m):E\to\mathbb C^m,
\]

the same quotient argument gives

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\bigcap_{j=1}^m\ker L_j}=H
\iff
\operatorname{span}\{L_1,\ldots,L_m\}\cap H^*|_E=\{0\},
}
\tag{PL353-9}
\]

where algebraically redundant relations are first removed. Thus finite coefficient coupling can create a proper closed family only if some nontrivial linear combination of the coefficient functionals is already visible to the ambient Hilbert topology.

Applied to `E=mathcal E subset H^s(I)`, every continuous functional is represented by a dual Sobolev distribution. After extending it as a compactly supported distribution on `[-A,A]`, Paley--Wiener--Schwartz makes (PL353-3) an entire function of exponential type at most `A`, with polynomial growth on the real axis. This proves the interpolation characterization (PL353-4).

The criterion is not vacuous. If `h in C_c^infty(I)` and one chooses

\[
w_\lambda=\int_I h(x)e^{i\lambda x}\,dx,
\tag{PL353-10}
\]

then `L_w` is exactly the continuous functional `f mapsto integral h f`, restricted to `mathcal E`; its kernel has proper closure. So some coefficient laws genuinely survive. The issue is whether the arithmetic source forces weights lying in this dual trace class.

## 2. Finite real-point jet constraints are invisible below the trace threshold

Take one of the weights in (PL353-5). If a continuous dual interpolant existed, its entire transform `F_T` would satisfy

\[
F_T(\log p)
=(i\log p)^j e^{ix_a\log p}
\qquad(p\text{ prime}).
\tag{PL353-11}
\]

The explicit entire function

\[
W_{a,j}(z)=(iz)^j e^{ix_a z}
\tag{PL353-12}
\]

has the same values. Hence `F_T-W_{a,j}` is an entire function of exponential type with zeros at every `log p`. By `PL-348`, the zero set has counting function

\[
\pi(e^R)\sim e^R/R,
\]

whereas every nonzero entire function of finite exponential type has only `O(R)` zeros in `|z|<=R`. Therefore

\[
F_T\equiv W_{a,j}.
\tag{PL353-13}
\]

The inverse Fourier distribution of (PL353-12) is, up to the Fourier convention, the point jet `delta_{x_a}^{(j)}`. But in one dimension

\[
\delta_{x_a}^{(j)}\in H^{-s}
\iff
s>j+\frac12.
\tag{PL353-14}
\]

Indeed its Fourier transform has magnitude comparable to `|xi|^j`, so the defining integral at infinity behaves like

\[
\int^\infty \xi^{2j-2s}\,d\xi.
\]

For the form-core bridge we deliberately work with `0<s<1/2`, so no nonzero point jet belongs to the dual. Thus every functional (PL353-5) is discontinuous on `mathcal E` in the `H^s` norm, and (PL353-2) makes its kernel dense.

The same argument handles any finite family of jets at finitely many real points. A nonzero linear combination has entire transform

\[
\sum_a P_a(z)e^{ix_a z},
\]

which is the transform of a nonzero finite sum of delta derivatives. Such a distribution is not in `H^{-s}` when `s<1/2`. Hence no nonzero combination becomes a continuous dual functional, and (PL353-9) gives density of the joint kernel.

There is also a constructive interpretation. A smooth bump supported in an `epsilon`-neighborhood of `x_a`, equal near `x_a` to `(x-x_a)^j/j!`, has the prescribed `j`-jet while its `H^s` norm scales like `epsilon^{j+1/2-s}`. For `s<1/2` this tends to zero even for `j=0`. `PL-348` gives prime-log approximation in arbitrarily high Sobolev order, so these arbitrarily small jet-correction bumps can themselves be approximated by prime-log exponential sums while retaining an invertible finite jet matrix. This is the geometric reason the finite constraints disappear after closure: points have no continuous trace at the subcritical topology used to reach the Weil form.

## 3. Exponentially weighted pole-cancellation constraints are also invisible

Now let

\[
w_p=P(\log p)p^{-\sigma},
\qquad \sigma>0.
\]

If (PL353-4) held, `F_T` would agree at every prime-log point with

\[
W_{\sigma,P}(z)=P(z)e^{-\sigma z}.
\tag{PL353-15}
\]

Again the superlinear zero count forces

\[
F_T\equiv W_{\sigma,P}.
\tag{PL353-16}
\]

But a Fourier-Laplace transform of a compactly supported distribution has only polynomial growth on the real axis, while for `x->-infinity`

\[
|P(x)e^{-\sigma x}|
\]

grows exponentially unless `P=0`. Therefore no nonzero weight of the form (PL353-6) can come from a continuous `H^s(I)` functional. Its coefficient constraint is again dense-kernel rather than a genuine closed restriction.

The conclusion remains true for any finite independent family combining real-point jet weights and finitely many distinct positive `sigma_b`. In a nonzero linear combination, the term with largest `sigma_b` dominates exponentially as `x->-infinity` unless all coefficients at that exponent vanish; stripping these terms recursively leaves only a finite real-point jet distribution, which is excluded by (PL353-14). Thus no nonzero linear combination lies in the continuous dual trace class required by (PL353-9).

The special case

\[
\sum_p c_p/p=0
\tag{PL353-17}
\]

is worth recording because it superficially resembles the pole-cancellation relation in Nyman--Beurling/Balazard formulations. In the present **local endpoint-modulation topology**, however, (PL353-17) is invisible after closure. This does not contradict `PL-017`--`PL-020`: those results live in a half-plane Hardy/Mellin geometry in which analytic continuation and the zeta divisor are part of the ambient structure. Transporting only the scalar coefficient relation into a local real-window modulation model does not transport that topology.

## 4. Consequence for the localized Weil form

Let `K subset mathcal E` be any of the dense joint kernels above. `PL-348` gives the form-domain bridge for the endpoint envelope

\[
u_A(x)=
\frac{e^{-(A-|x|)/2}}{\sqrt{2(1-e^{-A})}}
\mathbf 1_{|x|<A}.
\]

Because `K` is dense in `H^s(I)` for some `0<s<1/2`, the same divide-by-`u_A`, approximate, and multiply-back argument as in `PL-348` yields

\[
\boxed{
\overline{u_A K}^{\,\mathrm{form}}=D(Q_W^A).
}
\tag{PL353-18}
\]

Therefore imposing finitely many relations of the types above before taking arbitrary finite linear combinations does **not** produce a smaller closed test family. Positivity of the completed localized Weil form on those constrained states is still positivity on a form core and hence recovers the full localized Weil problem.

This sharpens the surviving escape clause in `PL-352`. It is not enough to say “impose coefficient constraints.” At least for finite linear constraints, the relevant question is now exact and falsifiable: **does the coefficient weight vector interpolate to a continuous dual functional of the local form topology?** If not, overcompleteness of the prime-log family absorbs the relation completely.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract kernel lemma (PL353-8)--(PL353-9) is elementary functional analysis. The Paley--Wiener--Schwartz representation and exponential-type zero-count bound are classical and were already audited in `PL-348`; the local trace threshold `s=j+1/2` for a point `j`-jet is the standard one-dimensional Sobolev threshold and is also verified directly by the Fourier integral in (PL353-14). Classical nonharmonic Fourier theory likewise identifies completeness of exponentials with uniqueness/interpolation properties of Paley--Wiener transforms. None of those ingredients is claimed as new.

A targeted literature audit on 18 September 2026 searched for coefficient-functional constraints on complete exponential systems, moment-constrained exponential sums, Paley--Wiener interpolation of coefficient weights, and versions specialized to logarithms of rational primes. It found the standard nonharmonic-Fourier/Paley--Wiener framework but did not locate this exact prime-log, subcritical-Sobolev specialization or the explicit comparison between real-jet and `p^{-sigma}` coefficient laws. That absence is **not** used as evidence of priority. The durable value is the route restriction relative to `PL-348`--`PL-352`: it converts the vague “coefficient coupling may survive” escape into the precise dual-interpolation gate (PL353-4).

The result is generic once the frequency set is sufficiently overcomplete. Replacing `log p` by any real frequency set whose counting forces uniqueness for finite-exponential-type entire functions gives the same mechanism. Rational-prime specificity enters through the canonical Prime-Lattice frequency support and its superlinear logarithmic counting, not through the dense-kernel lemma itself. This generic matched control is why the finding is classified as a boundary/negative rather than a new RH mechanism.

## 6. Adversarial boundaries

1. **Finite linear constraints only.** Infinite systems of relations can define a closed subspace even when every individual relation is discontinuous; nonlinear constraints, positivity cones, normalization conditions coupled to the quadratic form, and operator-domain restrictions are outside (PL353-9).

2. **The criterion depends on the ambient topology.** A coefficient law invisible in `H^s(-A,A)` can be decisive in a Hardy, de Branges, Mellin, or graph norm. The Nyman comparison above is exactly such a warning.

3. **A surviving dual interpolant is not an RH mechanism.** If the weights happen to be samples of a compactly supported `H^{-s}` distribution, the constrained closure is genuinely proper, but that only avoids automatic form-core collapse. Arithmetic sufficiency for the missing directions still needs a separate theorem.

4. **The prime-log uniqueness input is aperture-local and nonuniform.** Nothing here gives frame bounds, stable coefficient recovery, or uniform control as `A->infinity`.

5. **No claim is made for the critical Sobolev endpoint.** The localized Weil bridge inherited from `PL-348` uses some `0<s<1/2`; the proof deliberately stays in that regime. Borderline `H^{1/2}` capacity phenomena are unnecessary here.

6. **Scalar weights without coefficient restrictions remain irrelevant.** Rescaling generators by nonzero weights changes neither their algebraic span nor this classification. The finding concerns actual linear relations among coefficients.

7. **No analytic-continuation shortcut occurs.** The `p^{-sigma}` calculation is an interpolation statement about coefficient weights, not an Euler product or Dirichlet-series continuation. All zeta-zero content remains inside the completed Weil form inherited from the earlier findings.

## Research consequence

The coefficient-coupling branch of the live intermediate-sufficiency problem now has a concrete first test. For a proposed finite family of source relations, form the weight vectors on the selected log frequencies and ask whether some nonzero combination is the restriction of a continuous dual Paley--Wiener/Sobolev functional on the physical window. If the answer is no, the relations disappear under closure and the endpoint states remain a full Weil form core.

This eliminates finite centering, finitely many logarithmic moment constraints, finite real-phase jet constraints, and finite `p^{-sigma}` pole-cancellation conditions as escapes from the `PL-352` collapse. A viable next candidate must therefore supply either a **genuine continuous dual trace forced by arithmetic**, an infinite coherent relation whose closure cannot be reduced to finitely many coefficient hyperplanes, or a nonlinear/target-relative/cross-aperture mechanism that changes the problem before unrestricted polarization recreates the full form core.
