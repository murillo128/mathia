# PL-012 — Tanaka's ergodic Hardy extension makes the Riemann orbit a Haar-null exception

## Claim

A rigorous critical-strip prime-torus interpretation substantially stronger than bare Bohr point evaluation was already developed by Jun-ichi Tanaka (2008).

For every `u>1/2`, Tanaka constructs analytic Hardy-space objects on the infinite prime torus `T^omega` whose Fourier coefficients are the zeta and Möbius coefficients,

```text
Z_u(x)      = sum_{n>=1} n^{-u} chi_{log n}(x),
Z_u^{-1}(x) = sum_{n>=1} mu(n)n^{-u} chi_{log n}(x),
```

and proves that both are outer in the corresponding ergodic `H^q` spaces for every finite `q`. For Haar-almost every prime phase `x`, the associated twisted Euler product

```text
z(x,s)=prod_p (1-a_p p^{-s})^{-1}
```

is analytic and zero-free throughout `Re(s)>1/2`, and typical such twists occur as compact-uniform limits of vertical translates of the Riemann zeta function on that half-plane.

However, the distinguished Riemann orbit is not Haar-typical. Tanaka explicitly notes that because `zeta` has its pole at `s=1`, the identity orbit `O(0)` is wholly contained in the exceptional Haar-null set for the relevant outer-function boundary theorem. Consequently, **Haar-a.e. zero-freeness, ordinary ergodic typicality, or torus-average Hardy norms cannot by themselves determine the zero divisor of the Riemann zeta function**: the arithmetic observable whose zeros matter lies precisely in the exceptional orbit excluded by those generic statements.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` — classical prior-art redirect plus an exact scope obstruction to any route based only on Haar-typical prime phases.

No novelty is claimed for Tanaka's construction or the ergodic-Hardy theory. The Mathia-specific consequence is the explicit separation between the generic prime-torus geometry and the exceptional arithmetic orbit that must be controlled for RH.

## The ergodic Hardy extension

Write the compact prime torus as

```text
T^omega = product_p T,
```

with characters indexed by the additive frequency group generated by `log p`. Tanaka uses the flow

```text
T_t(x)_p = e^{i t log p} x_p.
```

For a Dirichlet series with suitably small coefficients, restriction of the associated torus Hardy function to the identity orbit recovers the corresponding vertical Dirichlet series where the latter converges. For zeta, when `u>1/2`, square summability gives

```text
||Z_u||_2^2 = sum_{n>=1} n^{-2u} = zeta(2u) < infinity,
```

while for the reciprocal

```text
||Z_u^{-1}||_2^2
  = sum_{n>=1} |mu(n)| n^{-2u}
  = zeta(2u)/zeta(4u) < infinity.
```

Tanaka strengthens this to membership in every finite ergodic `H^q` and proves both `Z_u` and `Z_u^{-1}` are outer. Thus there really is an infinite-torus Hardy object attached simultaneously to zeta and its Möbius inverse for every `u>1/2`.

This is a different construction from ordinary point evaluation of the Bohr power series at

```text
(p^{-s})_p.
```

`PL-001` shows that such point evaluation stops at `Re(s)=1/2`. Tanaka does not evade that theorem by claiming convergence of the ordinary Euler product in the critical strip; he passes to an `L^q`/ergodic Hardy boundary object and its analytic extensions along almost every flow line.

## Almost-everywhere zero-freeness and the exceptional Riemann orbit

Tanaka's Proposition 2.1 states that there is a Haar-null set `N subset T^omega` such that, for every `x` outside `N`, both `z(x,s)` and its inverse are analytic in

```text
Re(s)>1/2.
```

In particular the generic twist is zero-free there.

The crucial boundary condition appears immediately before that proposition. Restricting `Z_u` to the identity orbit represents the Riemann zeta continuation along the corresponding half-plane, but the pole of `zeta` at `1` prevents that orbit from satisfying the generic Hardy boundary hypothesis. Tanaka therefore states that

```text
O(0) is wholly contained in the exceptional null set.
```

This is not a small technical caveat. It means the Haar-almost-everywhere theorem deliberately excludes the one prime-phase orbit whose deterministic zero set is the RH problem.

The same paper also emphasizes that the exceptional set is mathematically rich rather than negligible in a topological sense: finite modifications of the identity phase form a dense subgroup with zeta-like behavior, and Proposition 2.2 constructs dense sets of twists with prescribed poles inside `1/2<Re(s)<1`. Later Helson-zeta results recorded in `PL-003` make this exceptional flexibility much stronger, but Tanaka already shows why measure-generic behavior cannot be transferred to the distinguished arithmetic point.

## Vertical-shift compactification

Tanaka's Theorem 4.1 gives a second, unusually direct bridge to the prime-lattice dynamics. For Haar-almost every `x in T^omega`, there exists a sequence `T_n` such that

```text
zeta(s+iT_n) -> z(x,s)
```

uniformly on every compact subset of `Re(s)>1/2`. Conversely, whenever a sequence of vertical zeta shifts is a normal family there, each accumulation function is of the form `z(x,s)` for some prime phase `x`.

Thus the infinite prime torus is not merely an Euler-product parametrization valid in `Re(s)>1`: it also acts as a genuine compactification/model for normal-family limits of vertical zeta shifts in the critical half-plane.

This overlaps conceptually with `PL-005` but does not replace Bagchi's RH equivalence. Bagchi asks whether the **distinguished zeta function itself** strongly returns to itself; Tanaka characterizes generic phase limits and the exceptional status of the Riemann orbit. The distinction is exactly what matters here.

## Weak Lindelöf mean values and the exceptional-set bottleneck

Tanaka further transfers Haar/ergodic moment information back to the actual zeta orbit after deleting a sparse exceptional subset of heights. In Corollary 5.2 he obtains one density-zero set `A subset [1,infinity)`, independent of the integer moment order `k` and of `sigma`, such that for every `sigma>1/2`,

```text
(1/T) integral_1^T |zeta(sigma+it)|^(2k) 1_{A^c}(t) dt
    ~ sum_{n>=1} d_k(n)^2 n^(-2 sigma).
```

This is deliberately only a weak form of the classical mean-value statement equivalent to Lindelöf: the density-zero deletion may contain rare but arbitrarily large contributions. It therefore does not prove Lindelöf and supplies no RH proof.

For the prime-lattice line, this sharpens the obstruction. Haar typicality can recover the expected critical-strip behavior on density-one sets of heights, yet the unresolved arithmetic information can remain concentrated on a measure/density exceptional set. That is structurally consistent with the identity orbit itself being exceptional in the torus model.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

- Tanaka (2008) is direct prior art for extending zeta to outer functions in ergodic Hardy spaces on the infinite prime torus and for using Haar measure to study critical-strip behavior.
- His Proposition 2.1 supplies the a.e. zero-free twist statement; Theorem 4.1 supplies the vertical-shift/prime-phase compactification; Corollary 5.2 supplies the density-zero-deleted moment theorem.
- `PL-003` records later and stronger Helson-zeta freedom of exceptional twists. That later work strengthens rather than invalidates the generic-versus-exceptional separation here.
- `PL-005` records Bagchi's exact RH/strong-recurrence equivalence. Tanaka's theorem gives a nearby but different function-space dynamical picture and makes explicit why generic torus behavior is insufficient for the distinguished zeta orbit.
- The statement that this separates generic torus geometry from the arithmetic RH orbit is a derived consequence for Mathia, not a claim that the distinction itself is a new theorem.

## Boundary conditions and counterarguments

- `Z_u` being outer in an ergodic Hardy space does **not** imply that the Riemann zeta function is zero-free on `Re(s)>1/2`; the identity orbit is explicitly exceptional.
- The ordinary Euler product for zeta is still not asserted to converge pointwise in the critical strip.
- A Haar-null exceptional set can be dense and can contain arithmetically distinguished points. Measure zero is therefore not evidence of irrelevance to RH.
- Theorem 4.1 does not say every prime phase is a limit of zeta shifts; the forward assertion is almost-everywhere, and the converse assumes normality.
- The density-zero deletion in the weak mean-value theorem is essential. Removing a set of density zero does not control the high moments needed for the ordinary Lindelöf-equivalent mean-value theorem.
- This finding does not rule out using Haar/ergodic methods as one component of a stronger argument. It rules out **genericity alone** as the missing RH mechanism.

## Audit criterion

The finding is falsified if Tanaka's paper does not support all three structural statements used here:

1. for `u>1/2`, `Z_u` and `Z_u^{-1}` are outer in every finite ergodic `H^q`, with a.e. twists analytic and zero-free on `Re(s)>1/2`;
2. the identity zeta orbit is explicitly contained in the exceptional null set because of the pole at `1`;
3. typical twists arise as compact-uniform limits of vertical zeta shifts on `Re(s)>1/2`, and the mean-value transfer to zeta requires deletion of a density-zero set.

Any future proposal based on Haar typicality must therefore exhibit a separate theorem controlling the exceptional identity orbit. Showing that a statement holds for almost every prime phase is not enough.

## Consequence for the research line

The prime torus already has a rigorous critical-strip Hardy/ergodic realization:

```text
prime phases + log-prime flow
    -> outer ergodic Hardy functions for u>1/2
    -> generic twists zero-free in Re(s)>1/2
    -> normal-family limits of vertical zeta shifts
    -> density-one weak moment behavior.
```

But the Riemann zeta function sits in the exceptional set at the first genericity step. The remaining RH problem is therefore not to establish a generic critical-strip prime-torus theory; that prior art exists. It is to identify **additional arithmetic structure that selects and controls the exceptional identity orbit**, precisely where Haar-a.e. and ordinary ergodic arguments stop applying.
