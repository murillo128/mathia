# PL-017 — Nyman–Beurling already realizes the prime-exponent lattice as a Mellin-diagonal isometric semigroup; RH is a totality problem

## Claim

The Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte Hilbert-space formulation already contains a precise operator realization of the positive prime-exponent lattice that matches the prime-frequency geometry sought in this research line.

Let `M` be the closed subspace of `L^2((0,1],dx)` consisting of functions constant on each interval `(1/(n+1),1/n]`. For each positive integer `m`, Bagchi defines

```text
(T_m f)(x) = m^(1/2) f(mx),   0 < x <= 1/m,
             0,               1/m < x <= 1.
```

He proves

```text
T_m T_n = T_(mn)
```

and each `T_m` is an isometry. Hence unique factorization gives the exact prime-exponent representation

```text
T_m = product_p T_p^(v_p(m)),
```

with only finitely many nontrivial factors.

Under the Fourier–Mellin isometry

```text
F(f)(s) = integral_0^1 x^(s-1) f(x) dx,
```

one has, by direct change of variables,

```text
F(T_m f)(s) = m^(1/2-s) F(f)(s).
```

Writing `s=1/2+it` therefore gives the boundary character

```text
m^(1/2-s)
  = m^(-it)
  = exp(-it log m)
  = exp(-it <v(m),(log p)_p>).
```

Thus the positive exponent lattice acts by commuting isometries, and Mellin duality sends its basis directions to the exact Kronecker frequencies `log p` on the critical boundary. This is not a new Mathia mechanism: it is already present, in essentially this operator form, in the classical Nyman–Beurling/Báez-Duarte theory as organized by Bagchi.

The RH-level statement is also operator-theoretic but is **not an eigenvalue statement**. For the Nyman vectors `g_l`, Bagchi proves

```text
RH
<=> 1 belongs to closure(span{g_l : l>=1}) in M
<=> {g_l : l>=1} is total in M.
```

The closed span `K=closure(span{g_l})` is invariant under every `T_m`. The constant function `1` is cyclic for this semigroup because

```text
T_m 1 = m^(1/2) 1_(0,1/m]
```

and the interval indicators `1_(0,1/m]` are total in `M`. Consequently

```text
RH <=> 1 in K <=> K=M.
```

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` — material prior-art redirect. The semigroup, its invariance argument, the Mellin isometry, the Nyman–Beurling/Báez-Duarte totality criterion, and the square-free thinning are literature results. The explicit exponent-vector factorization and the displayed `log p` boundary-character dictionary are exact consequences of those formulas. No novelty is claimed.

## Exact exponent-lattice / Mellin dictionary

The identity `T_mT_n=T_(mn)` means that the representation depends on multiplication exactly as the exponent cone does. If

```text
m = product_p p^(v_p(m)),
```

then commutativity and unique factorization give

```text
T_m = product_p T_p^(v_p(m)).
```

For the Mellin transform,

```text
F(T_m f)(s)
 = integral_0^(1/m) x^(s-1) m^(1/2) f(mx) dx
 = m^(1/2-s) integral_0^1 u^(s-1) f(u) du
 = m^(1/2-s) F(f)(s).
```

Equivalently,

```text
m^(1/2-s)
 = exp((1/2-s) sum_p v_p(m) log p).
```

On `Re(s)=1/2` this becomes a pure phase. For `Re(s)>1/2` and `m>1`, its modulus is strictly below one. Bagchi expresses the same Hardy-space action using the inner multipliers `mu^(s-1/2)` for `0<mu<=1`; setting `mu=1/m` gives exactly the multiplier above.

This makes the connection

```text
positive exponent cone
    -> multiplicative isometric semigroup {T_m}
    -> Mellin multipliers m^(1/2-s)
    -> boundary characters exp(-it <v(m),log p>)
```

fully rigorous.

## How zeta zeros enter: common evaluation obstruction, not operator spectrum

Bagchi defines, on `Omega={Re(s)>1/2}`,

```text
G_l(s) = (l^(-s)-l^(-1)) zeta(s)/s,
E(s)   = 1/s.
```

The zero of the first factor at `s=1` cancels the zeta pole, so each `G_l` is analytic in `Omega`. His Hardy-space theorem gives

```text
RH <=> E in closure(span{G_l}) in H^2(Omega).
```

The obstruction created by a hypothetical off-line zero is exact. If `rho in Omega` and `zeta(rho)=0`, then

```text
G_l(rho)=0 for every l,
E(rho)=1/rho != 0.
```

Point evaluation is continuous in `H^2(Omega)`, so all of `closure(span{G_l})` lies in the proper closed hyperplane `{F:F(rho)=0}`, while `E` does not. Thus an off-critical-line zero appears as a **common evaluation obstruction to cyclicity/totality**, not as an eigenvalue of one of the dilation operators.

This distinction matters for the spectral interpretation: the operators `T_m` already realize the arithmetic lattice, but their spectrum is not being identified with Riemann zeros. RH is encoded in whether a distinguished invariant subspace generated by the Nyman vectors fills the ambient Hilbert space.

## The analytic-continuation step is genuine

The Mellin identity relating the real-space Nyman functions to zeta is not obtained by silently extending an Euler-product formula. For

```text
f_lambda(x) = {lambda/x} - lambda {1/x},
```

Bagchi first computes, for `Re(s)>1`,

```text
F(f_lambda)(s)
 = -(lambda^s-lambda) zeta(s)/s.
```

He then notes that both sides are analytic on the larger half-plane `Omega={Re(s)>1/2}`, so the equality continues there. For `lambda=1/l`, this gives

```text
F(g_l) = -G_l
```

throughout `Omega`.

Therefore the totality criterion genuinely lives in the critical half-plane after analytic continuation; it is not merely a relabeling of the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series in `Re(s)>1`.

## Square-free hypercube thinning

Bagchi also records that the Hardy closure criterion can be thinned to square-free indices because the Möbius coefficients used in the RH-to-closure direction vanish off the square-free integers. Equivalently,

```text
RH
<=> E in closure(span{G_l : l square-free}).
```

In exponent coordinates these are precisely the finite-support `{0,1}` vertices. His sequence-space formulation likewise admits the square-free thinning.

This reinforces `PL-008`, rather than superseding it: the oriented square-free hypercube is already built into a serious RH-equivalent approximation theory. What `PL-017` adds to the durable picture is that the **multiplicative semigroup action and its Mellin/Fourier dual are also already present**, so the sought prime-lattice operator interpretation is not missing prior art.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

- Nyman and Beurling supply the original `L^2` closure criterion.
- Báez-Duarte proves the crucial strengthening that the continuous dilation family can be replaced by the integer-indexed family; this is exactly what makes the multiplicative integer semigroup relevant.
- Bagchi (2006) organizes the theory explicitly in Hardy/Mellin language, defines the integer isometries `T_m`, states `T_mT_n=T_(mn)`, proves invariance of the Nyman subspace, and reformulates RH as totality.
- The factorization `T_m=product_p T_p^(v_p(m))` and the equality of the critical-boundary multiplier with `exp(-it<v(m),log p>)` are immediate exact consequences of the literature formulas. They are useful for Mathia's coordinates but are not new mathematics.

Accordingly, a proposal of the form

```text
integer multiplication
 -> dilation operators
 -> Mellin/Fourier transform
 -> log-prime phases on Re(s)=1/2
 -> RH as a Hilbert-space completeness condition
```

fails the novelty audit: this route is already classical prior art.

## Boundary conditions and counterarguments

### The `1/2` here is not forced by the abstract lattice alone

The factor `m^(1/2)` in `T_m` is exactly the normalization making dilation isometric in `L^2((0,1],dx)`. It is this Hilbert measure/normalization that makes `Re(s)=1/2` the unit-modulus Mellin boundary. With a different Mellin Hilbert measure, the unitary axis is shifted. Therefore this construction does **not** establish that the bare exponent cone intrinsically forces `1/2` independently of analytic structure.

### The operators are isometries, not a unitary group

For `m>1`, `T_m` is not surjective on `M`; the cutoff to `(0,1/m]` is essential. Likewise, multiplication by the corresponding nonconstant inner function on `H^2(Omega)` is an isometry but not an onto unitary operator. Calling this a Hilbert–Pólya self-adjoint spectral realization would be incorrect.

### Totality is exactly the hard part

The semigroup law, prime factorization, Mellin diagonalization, and critical-line phases are unconditional. What is equivalent to RH is the assertion that the special invariant subspace generated by the `g_l` is all of `M`. The semigroup representation by itself does not force that equality.

### Square-free sufficiency does not make the Boolean geometry a proof

The square-free thinning uses Möbius-weighted approximation and analytic estimates. It does not say that Boolean-hypercube combinatorics alone imply RH; `PL-008` records the corresponding analytic bottleneck.

## Audit / falsification criterion

The stored claim is auditable by checking four independent statements in Bagchi's paper:

1. the Fourier–Mellin transform is an isometry from `L^2((0,1])` into `H^2(Re(s)>1/2)`;
2. `F(g_l)=-G_l` is first computed in `Re(s)>1` and then analytically continued to `Re(s)>1/2`;
3. the integer operators `T_m` are isometries satisfying `T_mT_n=T_(mn)` and leave `K=closure(span{g_l})` invariant;
4. RH is equivalent to totality of `{g_l}` in `M`, with a square-free thinning available in the equivalent formulations.

Given those statements, the exponent-vector/Mellin phase identity follows by one change of variables and unique factorization. The interpretation would be falsified if any proposed stronger conclusion — self-adjointness, unitary surjectivity, or zeta zeros as eigenvalues — were attributed to this semigroup; none follows.

## Consequence for the research line

The bare operator bridge from the exponent lattice to `log p` Fourier phases and an RH-equivalent Hilbert-space condition has already been built. The remaining mathematical problem is not to invent that bridge but to explain or prove the missing **totality/cyclicity** of the distinguished Nyman subspace.

For `prime_lattice`, this redirects further work toward structure that can force that totality — for example a new positivity principle, quantitative Gram/invariant-subspace control, or genuinely global arithmetic input — rather than another reformulation of multiplication as commuting dilations or another observation that Mellin duality produces the frequencies `log p`.
