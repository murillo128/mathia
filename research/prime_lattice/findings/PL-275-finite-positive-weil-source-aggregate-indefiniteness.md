# PL-275 — Every finite positive interior Weil source is eventually indefinite

**Evidence:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`

**Status:** `PROVED-STRUCTURAL + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + FINITE-AGGREGATE-SIGN-CLASSIFICATION + PRIOR-ART-ADJACENT`

## Claim

`PL-273` proves that every individual interior source atom is eventually indefinite in the even Galerkin tower, but it deliberately leaves open a possible escape: perhaps the canonical positive von-Mangoldt atoms become positive after they are accumulated together at a fixed prime cutoff. That escape also fails.

Let

\[
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\,\delta_{\omega_j},
\qquad a_j>0,\qquad 0<\omega_j<1,
\]

be any nonzero finite positive atomic source. Let `Q_N[mu]` be Groskin's level-`N` source matrix and restrict to the normalized even Fourier modes

\[
T_0(t)=1,
\qquad
T_k(t)=\sqrt2\cos(2\pi k t),\quad k\ge1.
\]

Then there is a finite `N_0(mu)` such that

\[
\boxed{
Q_N[\mu]\text{ is indefinite for every }N\ge N_0(\mu).
}
\]

Atoms at `omega=0` may be added without changing the statement because their source matrix is zero. The exclusion of `omega=1` is essential: `Q_N[delta_1]=2I` on the even tower.

Consequently, for every fixed prime cutoff `c>2`, the entire positive rational-prime source

\[
\nu_c^+
=
\sum_{q=p^a<c}
\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\sqrt q}
\,\delta_{\,1-\log q/\log c}
\]

is eventually indefinite as the Galerkin band grows. If `c` itself is a prime power, its threshold atom sits at `omega=0` and contributes zero, so the same conclusion holds with `q<=c`. Multiplying by the overall negative sign with which the prime source enters the usual Weil formula preserves indefiniteness.

Thus **collective cancellation among finitely many positive von-Mangoldt source atoms cannot turn the uncompleted prime-source block into a positive-semidefinite operator at sufficiently fine Galerkin resolution**. Any surviving positivity mechanism must use something outside the positive finite source block itself: pole/archimedean completion, a genuinely signed or nonlinear source relation, or another structure that is not shared by arbitrary finite positive atomic measures.

## 1. Exact aggregate cosine values

Groskin's finite source calculus is linear in the source measure. For one atom,

\[
K_k(\omega)
=
2\omega\cos(2\pi k\omega)
+
\frac{\sin(2\pi k\omega)}{\pi k},
\qquad k\ge1,
\]

while the constant mode gives `K_0(omega)=2omega`. Therefore

\[
\langle T_0,Q_N[\mu]T_0\rangle
=
2\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j
>0
\]

and, whenever `N>=k`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_k
&:=\langle T_k,Q_N[\mu]T_k\rangle\\
&=2\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\cos(2\pi k\omega_j)
 +\frac1{\pi k}\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\sin(2\pi k\omega_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

Write

\[
F(k)=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\cos(2\pi k\omega_j),
\qquad
R(k)=\frac1{\pi k}\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\sin(2\pi k\omega_j),
\]

so `C_k=2F(k)+R(k)` and

\[
|R(k)|\le \frac{A}{\pi k},
\qquad
A:=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j.
\]

The issue is therefore whether the finite almost-periodic cosine sum `F(k)` can stay nonnegative for all positive integer `k`. It cannot.

## 2. Compact-torus sign lemma

Set

\[
g=(e^{2\pi i\omega_1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\omega_r})\in\mathbb T^r
\]

and let `H` be the compact subgroup generated by `g`, equivalently the closure of `{g^k:k in Z}`. Define the continuous real function

\[
\Phi(z_1,\ldots,z_r)
=
\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\,\Re z_j
\qquad (z\in H).
\]

For each `j`, the coordinate character `z -> z_j` is nontrivial on `H`: indeed its value at the generator is `e^{2 pi i omega_j} != 1` because `0<omega_j<1`. Character orthogonality on compact groups therefore gives

\[
\int_H \Phi\,dm_H=0.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
\Phi(1,\ldots,1)=\sum_j a_j\omega_j>0.
\]

If `Phi` were nonnegative on all of `H`, continuity would make it strictly positive on a nonempty open neighborhood of the identity; Haar measure gives every nonempty open set positive mass, contradicting the zero integral. Hence there is `z_* in H` and `delta>0` such that `Phi(z_*)<-2delta`, and by continuity an open neighborhood `U` of `z_*` on which `Phi<-delta`.

The positive powers of a generator are dense in the same compact monothetic group `H`; every tail is dense as well. Consequently there are arbitrarily large positive integers `k` with `g^k in U`, and for all of them

\[
F(k)=\Phi(g^k)<-\delta.
\]

Choose one such `k` also satisfying `A/(pi k)<delta`. Then

\[
C_k=2F(k)+R(k)<-2\delta+\delta<0.
\]

The constant mode has positive quadratic value and this cosine mode has negative quadratic value. Therefore the compression containing `T_0` and `T_k` is indefinite. Once that mode has entered the Galerkin space, the same two test directions remain available at every larger `N`, proving eventual permanent indefiniteness.

This argument handles rational, irrational, and rationally dependent collections of source frequencies uniformly. No simultaneous rational independence is assumed: all relations are absorbed into the compact orbit closure `H`.

## 3. Application to the complete finite von-Mangoldt source

Fix `c>2` and put `L=log c`. The positive prime source at that cutoff is finite and has atoms

\[
\omega_q(c)=1-\frac{\log q}{L}
\]

with masses `Lambda(q)/sqrt(q)>0` for prime powers `q<c`. Every such frequency lies strictly in `(0,1)`, and at least `q=2` is present. Thus the theorem applies directly:

\[
\boxed{
\exists N_0(c)\ \forall N\ge N_0(c):
Q_N[\nu_c^+]\text{ is indefinite.}
}
\]

This is stronger than the atomwise result `PL-273`. The negative direction here is a **single common Fourier mode for the already-summed source**, not a different witness chosen separately for each atom. Therefore interactions among finitely many prime-power atoms cannot rescue positivity of the source block at fixed cutoff.

The theorem is intentionally qualitative. The first negative common mode can be very large when the torus orbit must simultaneously approximate a small negative region, so no uniform or practical bound for `N_0(c)` is claimed. That quantitative recurrence problem is separate from the sign obstruction.

The statement also remains generic with respect to the arithmetic weights. It uses only positivity, finite atomicity, and the fact that all active normalized frequencies are interior. The geometric prime-power-ray relation `q=p^a` and the exact ratios among the masses are not needed. Hence those within-source arithmetic relations, by themselves, cannot be the missing order mechanism in this finite source calculus.

## 4. What this closes, and what it does not

The sequence `PL-270`--`PL-274` progressively separated source observability from order: one band sees a finite quotient; the full tower recovers the source; positive source atoms need not map to positive matrices; every individual interior atom is eventually indefinite; and cross-cutoff affine transport is universal. The only source-side escape left explicitly by `PL-273` was that the **sum of the actual positive atoms** might have a different sign behavior from its constituents. The compact-torus argument above closes that escape for every fixed finite cutoff.

It does **not** prove that the completed Weil form is indefinite. The pole and archimedean pieces are omitted here, and Groskin's finite dictionary identifies the completed form, not the prime-source block alone, with the relevant Guinand--Weil zero sum. Those completion terms can change the sign. The result also says nothing about RH directly and does not contradict numerical positivity of completed finite Weil matrices.

Likewise, it does not cover arbitrary continuous positive source measures. Finite atomicity is used through compact almost-periodic recurrence. That is exactly the regime of the rational-prime source at any finite cutoff, so no extension to continuous measures is required for the arithmetic consequence.

The surviving target is therefore narrower than after `PL-274`: a proof cannot come from positivity of the complete finite von-Mangoldt source, even after all its atoms are allowed to interact at arbitrarily high Galerkin resolution. It must use a **completed** zeta-specific law coupling that source to the pole/archimedean terms, or another relation that changes the ordered object rather than merely identifying the source more accurately.

## 5. Prior-art and adversarial audit

The load-bearing source identity is prior art. Groskin's Lemma 2.3 in *A finite Guinand--Weil dictionary and archimedean tail order for the truncated Weil quadratic form* gives the exact source kernel used above. `PL-272` and `PL-273` already record adjacent work of de Andrade Silva showing mixed/parity-dependent signs for localized prime contributions.

A second, very close 2026 source is Groskin's *A matrix-valued von Mangoldt measure in the finite Connes--van Suijlekom path* (Zenodo concept DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21242028`, corrected v2 DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21612746`). It proves that prime-power threshold events form an exact rank-one matrix-valued von-Mangoldt measure and that the local singular jet carries precisely the scalar `Lambda(q)` times a universal matrix. The paper explicitly makes no positivity claim and separates the sign-definite derivative event from positivity of the full path. This is strong adjacent prior art for the conclusion that local arithmetic rigidity is not itself the missing Weil order.

The literature audit found no statement in these sources of the aggregate theorem proved here: that **every nonzero finite positive atomic source supported away from `omega=1` has an eventually indefinite even Galerkin image**. The harmonic step is classical compact-group character orthogonality/Kronecker recurrence, so no novelty is claimed for that machinery. The durable delta is its application to the exact Groskin source kernel, which closes the explicit multi-atom cancellation escape left by `PL-273`.

Adversarial controls are built into the hypotheses. The positive endpoint `delta_1` is a counterexample if the endpoint exclusion is dropped. A zero-frequency atom is harmless because its source matrix vanishes. Signed weights are outside the theorem and can change the conclusion. The finite cutoff is essential to the atomic proof. Finally, multiplying the source block by the conventional minus sign cannot repair it because the negative of an indefinite form is still indefinite.
