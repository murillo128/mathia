# PL-124 — Poisson–Newton already turns frequency lattices into divisor trace formulas; for zeta the full lattice collapses to prime-power rays

## Claim

A natural target of the `prime_lattice` program is already present in classical prior art in essentially maximal generality: **a Dirichlet-frequency lattice can be transformed into an exact distributional trace formula whose dual side is the zero/pole divisor.** Muñoz and Pérez-Marco's Poisson–Newton formula does this for every finite-order meromorphic Dirichlet series satisfying the standard hypotheses, and specializes to the Riemann explicit formula.

For zeta, the specialization is especially informative in prime-exponent coordinates. The logarithm of the Euler product kills the mixed-prime interior of the exponent cone and leaves only the axis rays

`k e_p`,

with frequencies

`<k e_p,(log q)_q> = k log p`.

After centering at `1/2`, the resulting symmetric distributional identity is

`sum_rho n_rho exp((rho-1/2)|t|) = origin term - sum_(p,k>=1) (log p) p^(-k/2) (delta_(k log p)+delta_(-k log p))`.

This is already the desired-looking chain

`additive frequency geometry -> logarithm/Newton coefficients -> atomic prime-period distribution -> zero divisor / trace formula`.

But it is **not** an RH-localization mechanism. The Poisson–Newton trace architecture is universal for Dirichlet series, and a one-frequency Dirichlet polynomial has the same architecture while its zeros can lie on an arbitrary vertical line. For zeta, the functional equation supplies the distinguished symmetry center `1/2`; it does not turn the factors `exp((rho-1/2)|t|)` into pure phases. That last step is exactly the missing positivity/unitarity/Hodge content already isolated in `PL-118`--`PL-120`.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the route

`prime/exponent frequency lattice + exact trace/Poisson formula -> geometry itself forces Re(rho)=1/2`.

The finding does **not** say explicit formulas are irrelevant. It says that the existence of a lattice-to-divisor trace formula, even with the exact rational-prime periods and the natural `1/2` centering, is prior art and is too universal to provide the missing RH rigidity without an additional sign, metric, unitarity, or self-duality theorem.

## The general Poisson–Newton mechanism

Let

`f(s)=1+sum_(n>=1) a_n exp(-lambda_n s)`

be a Dirichlet series absolutely convergent in a right half-plane and meromorphically continued to finite order, with positive increasing frequencies `lambda_n`. Write its logarithm in the convergent half-plane as

`-log f(s) = sum_(k in Lambda) b_k exp(-<lambda,k> s)`,

where `k` is a finite-support nonnegative multi-index and

`<lambda,k> = sum_j lambda_j k_j`.

Muñoz and Pérez-Marco define the Newton–Cramer distribution from the divisor of `f`, schematically

`W(f)(t)=sum_rho n_rho exp(rho t)`,

with the canonical distributional extension at the origin supplied by the Hadamard discrepancy polynomial. Their symmetric Poisson–Newton formula, after the affine shift parameter `beta`, gives for genus one the structure

`sum_rho n_rho exp((rho-beta)|t|)`

`= 2 c_0(f,sigma) delta_0`

`  + sum_(k in Lambda union -Lambda) <lambda,|k|> exp(-beta <lambda,|k|>) b_|k| delta_(<lambda,k>).`

The precise origin term depends on the Hadamard normalization, but the nonzero support is canonical: it is the additive semigroup generated by the Dirichlet frequencies, with weights determined by the logarithmic/Newton coefficients `b_k`.

This is exactly the kind of Fourier/trace duality suggested by the exponent-lattice picture. The zeros and poles appear on one side; atomic masses at additive combinations of primitive frequencies appear on the other. The same paper derives ordinary Poisson summation, Newton identities, the Riemann explicit formula, and the Selberg trace formula as instances of this single mechanism.

The important novelty audit is therefore immediate: **constructing a trace formula from an additive frequency cone is not by itself a new zeta mechanism.** It is a general theorem about meromorphic Dirichlet series.

## Zeta specialization in prime-exponent coordinates

For `Re(s)>1`,

`zeta(s)=prod_p (1-p^(-s))^(-1)`

and hence

`-log zeta(s) = -sum_p sum_(k>=1) p^(-ks)/k`.

Muñoz and Pérez-Marco apply the general theorem with the Dirichlet frequencies of zeta and observe that the logarithmic coefficient is zero except when the resulting frequency is

`k log p`.

For such a frequency the coefficient is `-1/k`. Multiplication by the Poisson–Newton frequency factor therefore gives

`(k log p)(-1/k) = -log p`.

With the symmetric shift `beta=1/2`, Theorem 7.1 becomes

`sum_rho n_rho exp((rho-1/2)|t|)`

`= 2 c_0(zeta,1/2) delta_0`

`  - sum_(p,k>=1) (log p) p^(-k/2) (delta_(k log p)+delta_(-k log p)).`

Their Theorem 7.3 pairs this distribution with test functions and recovers the classical explicit formula, including the archimedean/trivial-divisor contribution.

In the canonical prime-exponent coordinates this specialization is particularly transparent:

`k log p = <k e_p,(log q)_q>`.

Thus the non-archimedean trace side sees precisely

`{k e_p : p prime, k>=1}`,

not the mixed-prime vectors of the full cone `N_0^(P)`. The apparent full-lattice geometry has been passed through `log zeta` / the logarithmic derivative, and that operation extracts the primitive prime directions and their repetitions. Mixed composites do not survive as independent trace atoms.

This independently explains the repeated axis-ray compression already encountered in `PL-013`, `PL-044`, and `PL-120`: it is not an accident of a particular Weil operator. It is the generic logarithmic/determinant structure behind the explicit formula itself.

## Why the critical half-axis is not forced

The formula above is unconditional. If a nontrivial zero is written

`rho = beta_rho + i gamma_rho`,

then after centering at `1/2` its contribution is

`exp((rho-1/2)|t|)`

`= exp((beta_rho-1/2)|t|) exp(i gamma_rho |t|)`.

Under RH the first factor is `1`, so the zero side becomes oscillatory/pure-phase. Off RH it acquires exponential growth or decay. **The trace identity itself allows both.** It records the defect; it does not prohibit it.

The role of the functional equation must also be kept precise. The general Poisson–Newton formula permits arbitrary affine centering parameters. For zeta, `s <-> 1-s` makes `1/2` the canonical symmetric center and lets the origin/discrepancy contribution be computed and simplified. Muñoz and Pérez-Marco explicitly remark after the zeta derivation that the functional equation is only needed there to compute the constant at the origin; the general explicit-formula structure persists for order-one Dirichlet series more broadly.

Therefore

`functional-equation symmetry about 1/2 + prime-period trace formula`

still falls short of

`unitary/positive spectrum on Re(s)=1/2`.

The missing implication is exactly a **metric or positivity theorem** that rules out the real exponential factors `exp((beta_rho-1/2)|t|)`.

## Exact one-frequency matched control

The universality can be seen without any sophisticated example. Fix `lambda>0` and `a>0`, and consider the one-frequency Dirichlet polynomial

`f_(a,lambda)(s)=1-a exp(-lambda s)`.

It is entire of finite order and has zeros

`s_k = (log a)/lambda + 2 pi i k/lambda`,  `k in Z`

(up to reindexing the sign of `k`). Thus its complete zero divisor lies on the arbitrary vertical line

`Re(s) = (log a)/lambda`.

In its right half-plane of logarithmic convergence,

`-log f_(a,lambda)(s) = sum_(m>=1) a^m exp(-m lambda s)/m`.

The Poisson–Newton weights are therefore

`(m lambda)(a^m/m)=lambda a^m`

at the repeated frequencies `m lambda`. The same primitive-frequency/repetition/zero-divisor trace architecture is present for **every** value of `(log a)/lambda`.

So the structural package

`one primitive frequency + all repetitions + exact Newton/trace formula + vertical zero spectrum`

does not select any preferred real part. The zeta-specific datum `1/2` comes from its global functional equation/completion, while localization *onto* that symmetry axis requires extra structure.

This matched control is deliberately weaker than zeta because it lacks zeta's arithmetic functional equation. Its purpose is narrower and sufficient: it falsifies any argument in which the Poisson–Newton/trace geometry itself is supposed to create a critical axis. The stronger statement that functional-equation symmetry still needs positivity is already independently controlled by the Weil, Meyer, Deninger, and Suzuki findings `PL-118`--`PL-120`.

## Analytic-continuation boundary

There is no illicit continuation of the Euler product here.

The identities

`zeta(s)=prod_p(1-p^(-s))^(-1)`

and

`-log zeta(s)=-sum_(p,k) p^(-ks)/k`

are used only in `Re(s)>1`, where they converge absolutely, to identify the Dirichlet/logarithmic coefficients on the frequency side. The Poisson–Newton theorem then uses the **meromorphic continuation and Hadamard divisor of zeta** to produce the global distributional identity. The passage to the critical strip is therefore made by the theorem for the continued meromorphic function, not by pretending that the Euler product converges there.

Likewise, the `beta=1/2` shift is an affine re-centering of the already-defined Newton–Cramer distribution. It is not a substitution into a divergent prime product.

## Falsification and surviving escape routes

The negative conclusion would be materially narrowed by a mechanism that adds structure not present in the general Poisson–Newton identity. In particular, a viable escape may:

1. derive a positive-definite or unitary representation in which the zero-side exponentials must have modulus one;
2. produce a Hodge/polarization identity coupling the `s <-> 1-s` symmetry to a positive metric, rather than merely centering the trace formula;
3. use a genuinely full-lattice observable in which mixed exponent vectors survive and prove that this extra information controls the zero divisor;
4. extract a cross-scale invariant from the rational-prime activation sequence that fails for general Dirichlet-series frequency systems; or
5. establish a determinant/scattering identity whose reality or positivity is not automatic in the one-frequency and generic Dirichlet-series controls.

By contrast, another exact formula of the shape

`zero divisor <-> atomic sums at additive combinations of logarithmic frequencies`

is not enough: Poisson–Newton already guarantees such a structure in great generality.

## Consequence for the research line

This finding sharpens the current funnel. The line no longer needs to ask whether the prime-frequency lattice **can** have a trace formula: it can, and the general mechanism is classical. Nor should the appearance of `k log p` on one side and zeta zeros on the other be treated as evidence that the lattice has produced a Hilbert–Pólya operator.

The real target is now the missing additional law that converts the unconditional distribution

`sum_rho n_rho exp((rho-1/2)|t|)`

into a genuinely unitary/positive object. Under RH this distribution is oscillatory in the nontrivial-zero sector; without RH it contains exponential radial defects. A successful prime-lattice mechanism must **forbid those radial defects**, not merely encode them in an exact trace formula.

That conclusion is consistent with, and conceptually unifies, the strongest recent redirects in the line: Deninger's Hodge picture needs a positive polarization (`PL-118`), Meyer's exact adelic zero spectrum still leaves positivity equivalent to RH (`PL-119`), and Suzuki's unconditional positive auxiliary Hilbert space still needs identification with the Weil metric (`PL-120`). Poisson–Newton shows why the trace-formula side alone cannot close that gap.
