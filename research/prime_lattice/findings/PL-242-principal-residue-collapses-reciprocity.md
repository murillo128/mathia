# PL-242 — The canonical `s=1` residue isolates zeta only by collapsing the reciprocity family to the principal squarefree kernel

## Claim

The most direct residue escape left open by `PL-241` can be evaluated exactly in Blomer's quadratic double Dirichlet series, and it does isolate the Riemann-zeta divisor. However, it does so only after the mixed-prime quadratic-reciprocity data have disappeared.

For real characters `psi, psi'` of conductor dividing `8`, Blomer defines, initially for `Re(s), Re(w)` sufficiently large,

`Z(s,w;psi,psi') = zeta_2(2s+2w-1) sum_(d odd) L_2(s,chi_d psi) psi'(d) d^(-w)`.

When `psi=psi_1` is trivial, the residue on the polar hyperplane `s=1` is

`Res_(s=1) Z(s,w;psi_1,psi') = (1/2) zeta_2(2w)`

for every `psi'`. The identity is first obtained in Blomer's region `Re(w)>1` from his squarefree-kernel expansion and then extends meromorphically in `w`. If `psi` is nontrivial, Blomer's continuation has no `s=1` polar line in that region.

Thus a canonical polar residue of the reciprocity-coupled two-variable object really does recover the principal Riemann channel, up to the intentionally removed Euler factor at `2`. Its nontrivial Riemann-zero component is

`w = rho/2`, `zeta(rho)=0`,

so RH is equivalent to these residue zeros lying on `Re(w)=1/4`.

The key negative is the mechanism of this extraction. Writing `d=d_0 d_1^2` with `d_0` odd and squarefree, the pole at `s=1` comes **only from `d_0=1`**, where the primitive quadratic character has become the trivial character. Every nontrivial squarefree kernel, and hence every varying quadratic field/character carrying the reciprocity-family information of `PL-240`--`PL-241`, is holomorphic at `s=1` and contributes zero to the residue. The surviving `zeta_2(2w)` is generated by the square/imprimitive principal sector `d=d_1^2`, not by a retained interaction among nontrivial quadratic characters.

Consequently the residue does not transfer mixed-prime reciprocity rigidity to the Riemann zero divisor. It projects the family onto the principal squarefree kernel and leaves an ordinary rescaled zeta function whose functional equation, zero symmetry, and unresolved RH content are exactly those of zeta itself.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + NEGATIVE-BOUNDARY + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`. Decisive only against the canonical `s=1` principal polar residue of this quadratic double-series model as a new RH-localization mechanism. It does not rule out every other residue, quotient, period, specialization, or operator realization of a multiple Dirichlet series.

## Exact residue calculation

Blomer's equation (29), initially valid in the domain from which he continues the series, is

`Z(s,w;psi,psi')`

`= zeta_2(2s+2w-1) sum_(d_0 odd, mu^2(d_0)=1)`

`    [L_2(s,chi_(d_0) psi) psi'(d_0) zeta_2(2w)]`

`    / [d_0^w L_2(s+2w,chi_(d_0) psi)]`.

For `psi=psi_1`, the only numerator `L`-function with a pole at `s=1` is the `d_0=1` term. There

`L_2(s,chi_1)=zeta_2(s)`,

`psi'(1)=1`,

and the term becomes

`zeta_2(2s+2w-1) zeta_2(s) zeta_2(2w) / zeta_2(s+2w)`.

Taking `s -> 1`, the two finite factors with argument `2w+1` cancel exactly. Since

`zeta_2(s)=(1-2^(-s)) zeta(s)`

has residue `1/2` at `s=1`, one gets

`Res_(s=1) Z(s,w;psi_1,psi') = (1/2) zeta_2(2w)`.

This derivation is legitimate before continuation: for `Re(w)>1` all relevant displayed expressions lie in Blomer's controlled region, and the cancellation is between ordinary holomorphic nonzero factors there. The resulting equality of meromorphic residue functions then extends by uniqueness. No Euler product has been continued term-by-term into the critical strip.

The independence from `psi'` is also structural rather than accidental. In the decomposition `d=d_0 d_1^2`, every real mod-`8` character satisfies `psi'(d_1^2)=1` for odd `d_1`, so the square part disappears from the twisting character. Once the residue forces `d_0=1`, no nontrivial `psi'`-dependent quadratic-family datum survives.

## What survives in the prime lattice

The double series of `PL-241` couples two arithmetic supports through the symbols `chi_d(n)=(d/n)` before analytic continuation. That was a genuine source-forced mixed-prime relation: quadratic reciprocity exchanges the two Dirichlet variables, and varying `d_0` varies the primitive quadratic character and its conductor.

The `s=1` residue destroys precisely that part. The condition `d_0=1` means that the squarefree support of the discriminant variable is empty. In exponent-vector language, the residue keeps only even exponent vectors in the `d`-variable,

`v_p(d)=2 v_p(d_1)`,

while the primitive quadratic-character label is trivial. Summing this surviving square sector produces the ordinary factor `zeta_2(2w)`.

This is therefore a useful matched boundary case for future mixed-prime proposals. A principal-channel projection is not enough merely because it returns zeta: one must check whether the operation preserves any of the arithmetic relation that made the larger construction richer than zeta in the first place. Here it does not.

## Zero divisor and critical-line accounting

Restoring the omitted local factor at `2` turns the residue, up to the harmless constant `1/2`, into `zeta(2w)`. Hence every nontrivial Riemann zero `rho` produces a residue zero at

`w=rho/2`.

The known factor `1-2^(-2w)` in `zeta_2(2w)` contributes its own elementary local zeros, so these must not be confused with the Riemann divisor. After separating that finite Euler factor, the Riemann-zero statement is exact.

The completed one-variable residue inherits the ordinary zeta functional equation under

`2w -> 1-2w`,

or equivalently

`w -> 1/2-w`.

Its self-dual vertical line is therefore `Re(w)=1/4`. This is exactly the rescaled form of the Riemann critical line, not a new line selected by the two-variable Weyl geometry. Proving that all nontrivial residue zeros lie there is simply RH after the change of variable `rho=2w`.

This sharpens the distinction from the joint double-series critical locus in `PL-241`. The full two-variable object has its natural central geometry around `Re(s)=Re(w)=1/2`, while the residue's zeta divisor lives in a lower-rank scalar object with its own inherited center `Re(w)=1/4`. The act of taking the residue has reduced the problem rather than supplied a positivity or self-adjointness principle linking those two centers.

## Adversarial controls

**The residue is not literally only the single term `d=1`.** It selects the trivial *squarefree kernel* `d_0=1`, so all odd squares `d=d_1^2` remain. Their characters are imprimitive principal characters with local zeros at primes dividing `d_1`; summing those square corrections is exactly what leaves `zeta_2(2w)`. The negative conclusion is therefore about loss of the nontrivial primitive quadratic-family coupling, not about collapse to one summand.

**Recovering zeta is real, not dismissed as a formal factor.** Unlike the visible correction factor in the original definition, the residue identity is an equality of one-variable meromorphic functions. The Riemann zero divisor genuinely survives this operation, modulo the explicit local factor at `2`.

**But divisor survival alone is insufficient.** The residue supplies neither a self-adjoint operator whose spectrum forces reality, nor a positive Weil form, nor a new trace identity. Its functional equation is just the ordinary zeta functional equation after `s=2w`. Treating this as a Hilbert--Polya mechanism would therefore reinsert the unresolved problem at lower rank.

**Other polar faces are not closed here.** Blomer's global continuation also permits poles on `w=1` and `s+w=3/2`, and the Weyl functional equations mix the character components. This finding makes no claim that every residue or iterated residue has the same scalar form. It closes only the clean principal `s=1` extraction that is the most direct realization of the residue caveat left by `PL-241`.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

No novelty is claimed for the double Dirichlet series, its polar hyperplanes, or the squarefree-kernel expansion. They are explicit in Blomer and belong to the established quadratic multiple-Dirichlet-series framework of Diaconu--Goldfeld--Hoffstein.

The exact residue formula above is an elementary consequence of Blomer's equation (29): isolate the unique `d_0=1` pole and cancel the identical `zeta_2(2w+1)` factors. The research value here is only the RH-facing boundary interpretation: the first canonical residue that really preserves a Riemann-zeta divisor does so by eliminating the nontrivial reciprocity family that motivated the construction.

A targeted literature audit around quadratic/Weyl multiple-Dirichlet-series residues confirms that residue calculus is itself an established part of that theory. Accordingly, neither the operation nor the reduction to lower-rank arithmetic data is treated as a discovery. The durable result is the exact no-shortcut statement for this particular principal polar face.

## Consequences for `prime_lattice`

`PL-240` found a genuine source-dependent mixed-prime relation but its direct analytic completion opened a quadratic `L`-family. `PL-241` then found the classical family average that couples this reciprocity data to an explicit zeta correction and derives multivariable continuation, while leaving open whether a canonical residue or quotient could isolate the principal divisor more rigidly.

For the simplest canonical residue the answer is now exact: **yes, zeta is isolated, but only because the nontrivial quadratic family is projected away.** Future work should therefore not count recovery of `zeta(affine variable)` from a polar face as evidence of a new prime-lattice RH mechanism unless the same operation demonstrably retains a nontrivial source-forced relation among rational-prime coordinates.

A surviving construction must couple the principal Riemann divisor to arithmetic data that remains nontrivial after the projection, and then use that retained coupling to prove something not already equivalent to RH by a scalar change of variables -- for example positivity, a self-adjoint spectral constraint, or a trace/determinant identity whose localization property is independently derived.
