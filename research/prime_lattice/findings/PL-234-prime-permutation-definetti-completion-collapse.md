# PL-234 — Full prime-permutation quasi-local completion is a de Finetti product mixture

## Claim

`PL-233` closed the normal-state escape for exact full permutation symmetry on the finite-support integer Hilbert space, but deliberately left open a natural completion escape: pass to an infinite-coordinate quasi-local algebra, allow non-normal states in the original `ell^2(N)` representation, and ask whether a genuinely correlated prime-symmetric equilibrium can survive there.

For the ordinary tensor-product completion of independent prime coordinates, classical noncommutative de Finetti theory closes that escape much more strongly than a Gibbs trace argument.

Let `A` be any unital one-prime `C*`-algebra and let

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathcal P}
=\bigotimes_{p\in\mathcal P}^{\min} A
\]

be the quasi-local infinite minimal tensor product, with the finite symmetric group on the rational-prime labels acting by permutation of tensor factors. A state `Phi` is prime-permutation symmetric when

\[
\Phi\circ\alpha_\pi=\Phi
\qquad
(\pi\in S_{\rm fin}(\mathcal P)).
\]

Størmer's de Finetti theorem for symmetric states on infinite tensor products implies that there is a probability measure `nu` on the one-site state space `S(A)` such that, for distinct primes `p_1,...,p_k` and local observables `a_1,...,a_k in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi\!\left(a_1^{(p_1)}\cdots a_k^{(p_k)}\right)
=
\int_{S(A)}\prod_{j=1}^k\rho(a_j)\,d\nu(\rho).
}
\]

Moreover the extreme symmetric states are exactly the identical product states

\[
\boxed{
\Phi=\rho^{\otimes\mathcal P}.
}
\]

Thus **full prime-coordinate exchangeability does not produce irreducible cross-prime quantum structure in the natural quasi-local tensor completion**. Every finite-prime marginal is a mixture of identical product marginals and is therefore separable. Correlations can remain, but only through the classical de Finetti mixing variable `rho`; conditional on that variable, distinct prime sites are independent and identically distributed.

This is a decisive negative for the completion escape left by `PL-233`. Passing from the finite-support integer representation to the ordinary infinite prime tensor product can evade normality and can support nontrivial symmetric states, but it does so by replacing rational-prime geometry with an exchangeable product-mixture structure. Full symmetry itself cannot generate a coupling that distinguishes `p`, `q`, `pq`, `log p`, the norm map, or any other rational-prime label.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The de Finetti classification is classical prior art due to Størmer, with Hudson--Moody giving the locally normal quantum analogue in an infinite-dimensional setting. The prime-lattice specialization and its use to close the precise completion boundary of `PL-233` are derived here; no novelty is claimed for the symmetric-state theorem itself.

## 1. The natural prime completion is an infinite tensor product

The finite-support exponent lattice can be written as the occupation basis of a countable collection of one-prime sites. At a single prime, the occupation number lies in `N_0`; if operator shifts are retained, a natural local algebra is for example the unital Toeplitz algebra generated by the unilateral shift on `ell^2(N_0)`. Nothing below depends on that specific choice: the theorem applies to any fixed unital one-site `C*`-algebra `A`.

For a finite prime set `F`, the local algebra is

\[
\mathcal A_F=\bigotimes_{p\in F}^{\min}A.
\]

The inductive limit over finite `F` gives the quasi-local algebra `A_P`. Since the prime set is countably infinite, choose any enumeration to identify this with the usual infinite tensor power of `A`; the action of finite prime permutations is independent of that enumeration.

This is precisely the most direct operator-algebraic version of the completion discussed after `PL-232` and `PL-233`: local observables still inspect only finitely many exponent coordinates, but states need no longer arise from trace-class density operators on the finite-support Fock representation. In particular, a symmetric state on `A_P` may be singular relative to `ell^2(N)` and may describe configurations with infinitely many occupied prime coordinates.

The question is therefore whether leaving the type-I finite-support representation creates a genuinely new all-prime correlation mechanism. Størmer's theorem answers that question for this tensor-product completion.

## 2. Størmer de Finetti classification

For the infinite minimal tensor product of a unital `C*`-algebra with itself, Størmer proved that the convex set of states invariant under finite permutations of tensor factors is a Choquet simplex whose extreme points are exactly the infinite identical product states. Equivalently, every symmetric state admits a barycentric representation by one-site states.

Specializing the tensor-factor labels to rational primes gives the formula in the claim. If `Phi` is extreme among prime-permutation-invariant states, there is a single `rho in S(A)` such that

\[
\Phi\!\left(a_1^{(p_1)}\cdots a_k^{(p_k)}\right)
=
\prod_{j=1}^k\rho(a_j)
\]

for every finite set of distinct prime sites. If `Phi` is not extreme, it is an integral of these product states and therefore

\[
\Phi\!\left(a_1^{(p_1)}\cdots a_k^{(p_k)}\right)
=
\int\prod_{j=1}^k\rho(a_j)\,d\nu(\rho).
\]

The measure `nu` is the de Finetti mixing data. It can create nonzero connected correlations. For example, with the same local observable `a` at two different primes,

\[
\Phi(a^{(p)}a^{(q)})-\Phi(a^{(p)})\Phi(a^{(q)})
=
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(\rho(a)).
\]

So the conclusion is **not** that every symmetric state is an uncorrelated product state. The precise conclusion is stronger and more useful for the present audit: all cross-prime correlation is classical mixture correlation. Once the latent one-site state is fixed, the prime directions factorize completely.

For any finite collection of prime sites, the marginal is consequently a convex mixture of states `rho^(tensor k)`. In the ordinary quantum-information/operator-algebraic sense it is separable across the prime tensor factors. Exact infinite exchangeability therefore excludes prime-site entanglement as the missing global coupling within this quasi-local tensor model.

## 3. The classical diagonal shadow forces nontrivial symmetric completions to infinite support

The same structure can be seen without operator algebras on the diagonal occupation variables. Let `(X_p)_p` be an exchangeable family of `N_0`-valued random variables. Classical de Finetti theory represents its law as a mixture of iid product laws. Conditional on a one-site law `lambda`, set

\[
q_\lambda=\lambda(\{1,2,\ldots\}).
\]

If `q_lambda>0`, then under the corresponding iid product law the events `{X_p>0}` are independent with a fixed positive probability. The second Borel--Cantelli lemma gives

\[
\Pr_\lambda(X_p>0\text{ for infinitely many }p)=1.
\]

Hence an exchangeable law on the completed product `N_0^P` that is supported on finite-support exponent vectors must have `q_lambda=0` for almost every de Finetti component. That forces `lambda=delta_0` almost surely, so the law is the vacuum point mass.

This recovers the diagonal content of `PL-233` from the opposite side and extends the interpretation in `PL-232`: a nontrivial fully symmetric completion is possible only because its typical configurations leave the positive-integer lattice and occupy infinitely many prime coordinates. Allowing arbitrary exchangeable correlations does not change that conclusion; de Finetti says those correlations are mixtures of iid tails.

The operator-algebraic theorem is stronger than this diagonal observation because it applies to noncommuting local observables as well. A non-normal symmetric state can indeed live on the quasi-local completion, but its finite-site quantum marginals still have the product-mixture form.

## 4. Why this completion cannot recover rational-prime arithmetic by symmetry alone

The bare exponent orthant treats all prime coordinates as isomorphic. A fully permutation-invariant state therefore contains no distinguished label that can tell the site `p=2` from `p=3` or from a large prime. Størmer's representation makes that information loss exact: the entire state is specified by a probability measure on **one** site-state space, used identically at every coordinate.

Consequently, any finite-prime observable whose definition itself is permutation-covariant can depend only on the number and local arrangement of the sampled sites together with moments of the common latent state. It cannot manufacture the arithmetic norm weights

\[
p\longmapsto\log p
\]

or relations involving the actual values of distinct rational primes. Those data have to be inserted through the observable algebra, dynamics, a source relation, or explicit symmetry breaking before the symmetric-state evaluation.

This is the same distinction that the mandate's Beurling/generalized-prime controls repeatedly expose. Relabel the countable sites by a generic countable set and the entire de Finetti theorem and every displayed factorization remain unchanged. The result is therefore a **generic exchangeability no-go**, not evidence about RH. Its value is to prevent a generic infinite-system completion from being misread as an arithmetic all-prime coupling.

A de Finetti mixing measure can of course be chosen to encode arbitrarily complicated external data. That does not evade the obstruction: such information is programmable state-selection input unless an independent arithmetic mechanism derives the measure from the rational-prime problem. Full coordinate symmetry supplies no such derivation.

## 5. Relation to PL-232 and PL-233

`PL-232` showed, for additive diagonal Hamiltonians, that full prime-permutation symmetry creates infinite one-particle degeneracy and destroys the trace-class Gibbs state on `ell^2(N)`. It also showed in the corresponding independent geometric product completion that the thermodynamic measure is almost surely infinite-support when the one-prime occupation probability is not summable.

`PL-233` removed the additivity restriction. Every positive trace-class operator on the finite-support integer Hilbert space that is invariant under all finite prime permutations is supported on the vacuum, so no interacting fully symmetric Hamiltonian can repair the normal finite-temperature Gibbs problem there. It deliberately left open non-normal KMS states and completed infinite-coordinate representations.

The present finding closes the **ordinary quasi-local tensor-product version** of that remaining escape. Non-normal symmetric states certainly exist after completion, so there is no contradiction with `PL-233`. But they are completely classified at the exchangeability level: extremal states are identical products and general states are their mixtures. Interactions or thermodynamic limiting procedures may select a nontrivial de Finetti measure, including mixtures describing phase coexistence, but exact prime-permutation symmetry still forces the resulting state to have this product-mixture structure.

Therefore the sequence of no-go results is now sharper:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{finite-support integer representation + full symmetry}
&&\Longrightarrow\text{ only vacuum normal state},\\
&\text{ordinary quasi-local tensor completion + full symmetry}
&&\Longrightarrow\text{ de Finetti mixture of identical product states}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second line allows nontrivial states but not an irreducible prime-specific coupling.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty audit

The main theorem is classical. Erling Størmer, “Symmetric states of infinite tensor products of C*-algebras,” *Journal of Functional Analysis* **3** (1969), 48--68, DOI `10.1016/0022-1236(69)90050-0`, proves the symmetric-state structure for infinite tensor products. Modern operator-algebra references routinely state the result in the form used here: extreme symmetric states are infinite product states and the symmetric state space is a Choquet simplex.

R. L. Hudson and G. R. Moody, “Locally normal symmetric states and an analogue of de Finetti's theorem,” *Zeitschrift für Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie und Verwandte Gebiete* **33** (1976), 343--351, DOI `10.1007/BF00534784`, gives the locally normal quantum de Finetti analogue and is close prior art for infinite-dimensional one-site Hilbert spaces. Modern quantum de Finetti literature explicitly treats these papers as the foundational infinite-exchangeability results.

A bounded search for a rational-prime/exponent-lattice use of this theorem did not identify a distinct RH localization mechanism. That absence is **not** used as evidence of novelty. The durable mathematical content is only the exact specialization to the live `PL-233` completion boundary and the resulting negative: the most natural fully symmetric quasi-local completion is already classified by generic de Finetti structure.

The argument contains no zeta continuation, zero divisor, functional equation, or positivity theorem. It therefore cannot be promoted to an RH mechanism. It is a route-closing control.

## 7. Exact boundaries and falsification tests

The conclusion depends on **full finite-permutation invariance** of the prime tensor factors. The arithmetic energy `log p`, a distinguished additive successor, congruence data, a local-global reciprocity relation, or any other source-forced prime label breaks this symmetry and lies outside the theorem. That is not a loophole to patch away; introducing such structure before state selection is exactly what a surviving arithmetic mechanism must do.

The conclusion also depends on the **ordinary tensor-product quasi-local completion** with identical one-site algebras. It does not classify arbitrary nonlocal `C*`-algebras, crossed products, adelic quotients, Bost--Connes-type algebras with additional arithmetic relations, or other completions whose defining relations already couple sites globally. Such a construction remains live only if those extra relations are source-forced rather than tuned to reproduce zeta.

No assertion is made that the de Finetti product components of a symmetric KMS state are themselves KMS states for a chosen interacting dynamics. The state-level classification alone is what is needed here: whatever dynamics selected the symmetric state, its finite-site correlations still admit the de Finetti product-mixture representation.

Finite-prime systems are also outside the exact theorem. Finite exchangeability permits entangled symmetric states and only approximate finite de Finetti statements are available. The obstruction is an infinite-prime result and therefore matches the completed prime lattice, not its finite truncations.

Finally, the phrase “no irreducible cross-prime quantum structure” means precisely the displayed product-mixture identity and separability of finite-prime marginals. It does not mean that all connected correlations vanish; variation of the latent one-site state can produce nonzero classical correlations.

The finding should be withdrawn or narrowed if any of the following fails: (1) Størmer's theorem does not apply to the chosen infinite minimal tensor product; (2) an extreme fully permutation-invariant state exists there that is not an identical product state; (3) a symmetric state has a finite-prime marginal outside the convex closure of identical product marginals; or (4) the classical exchangeable finite-support corollary admits a non-vacuum law. The classical theorem and the elementary Borel--Cantelli check rule out all four under the stated hypotheses.

## Consequence for the research line

The generic “leave `ell^2(N)` and hope that a fully prime-symmetric infinite-system state acquires the missing global coupling” route is no longer live for the standard quasi-local tensor completion. Completion removes the trace-class obstruction of `PL-233`, but de Finetti immediately classicalizes the all-prime correlations.

A surviving operator/thermodynamic construction must therefore introduce **rational-prime-sensitive structure before the final state or equilibrium is formed**. It may break permutation symmetry through the norm map, impose a source-forced non-tensor relation, use an adelic/local-global coupling, or retain a distinguished target such as the Nyman/Weil observable. What it cannot do is rely on full exchangeability itself to create the arithmetic coupling after completion.
