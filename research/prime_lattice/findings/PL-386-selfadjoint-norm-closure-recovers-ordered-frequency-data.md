## Claim

Let `(S_t)_{t>=0}` be the right-shift semigroup on `L^2(R_+)`,

\[
(S_tf)(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x-t),&x\ge t,\\
0,&0\le x<t.
\end{cases}
\]

For a positive frequency family `b=(b_j)`, let

\[
G_b=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{b_j\}\subset\mathbb R,
\qquad
G_b^+=G_b\cap[0,\infty),
\]

with the order inherited from `R`, and define the selfadjoint norm-closed algebra

\[
\mathcal T_b=C^*(S_{b_j}:j).
\tag{PL386-1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_b=C^*(S_g:g\in G_b^+).}
\tag{PL386-2}
\]

Thus adjoining adjoints fills the entire positive cone of the additive group generated by the sampled times, even though norm topology does not invoke the strong-continuity collapse of `PL-383`.

More importantly, with the distinguished shift representation retained, the range projections

\[
Q_g=S_gS_g^*=M_{\mathbf 1_{[g,\infty)}}
\tag{PL386-3}
\]

encode the inherited total order exactly:

\[
\boxed{Q_h\le Q_g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\le h.}
\tag{PL386-4}
\]

Consequently the represented semigroup system recovers all rational comparisons among the original frequencies. For two positive rationally independent generators `b_i,b_j`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_i}{b_j}
=
\sup\left\{\frac mn:\ m,n\in\mathbb N,
\ Q_{n b_i}\le Q_{m b_j}\right\}.
}
\tag{PL386-5}
\]

Equivalently, the ordered subgroup `G_b subset R` determines its numerical geometry up to one common positive scale. This is the exact norm-selfadjoint boundary missing from `PL-385`: the nonselfadjoint analytic norm algebra forgets the values of the rationally independent times, while the distinguished selfadjoint shift system remembers their Archimedean order through range projections.

For the rational primes,

\[
G_{\mathbb P}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{\log p:p\text{ prime}\}
=\log\mathbb Q_{>0},
\tag{PL386-6}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
C^*(S_{\log p}:p\text{ prime})
=
C^*(S_{\log q}:q\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\ q\ge1).
}
\tag{PL386-7}
\]

This is precisely the kind of Toeplitz/Wiener-Hopf `C*`-algebra attached classically to the positive cone of a totally ordered abelian group. The prime-specific content at this level is therefore the ordered subgroup `log Q_{>0}`, not a new spectral or zeta-zero mechanism.

## 1. Adjoint products recover group differences

For `a,b>=0`, direct calculation gives

\[
S_a^*S_b=
\begin{cases}
S_{b-a},&b\ge a,\\
S_{a-b}^*,&a>b.
\end{cases}
\tag{PL386-8}
\]

Now take `g in G_b^+`. Write

\[
g=\sum_j z_jb_j=a-c,
\qquad z_j\in\mathbb Z,
\]

where `a` is the sum of the positive parts of the integer coefficients and `c` the sum of the negative parts, so both `a,c` lie in the original positive monoid generated by the `b_j`. Since `g>=0`, `a>=c`, and therefore

\[
S_c^*S_a=S_{a-c}=S_g\in\mathcal T_b.
\tag{PL386-9}
\]

This proves the nontrivial inclusion in (PL386-2); the reverse inclusion is immediate because each original generator belongs to `G_b^+`.

The difference from `PL-383` is topological. Strong continuity can turn a dense subgroup of times into the full continuous semigroup. Equation (PL386-9) requires an **exact integer relation**: adjoints supply differences already present in `G_b`, but no limiting argument has been used to create arbitrary real times.

## 2. Range projections recover the Archimedean order

The range of `S_g` consists of the functions supported on `[g,infinity)`, so (PL386-3) is immediate. Hence `h>=g` exactly when `Ran(S_h) subset Ran(S_g)`, proving (PL386-4).

For positive `b_i,b_j` and integers `m,n>=1`,

\[
Q_{n b_i}\le Q_{m b_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m b_j\le n b_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac mn\le\frac{b_i}{b_j}.
\tag{PL386-10}
\]

Density of the positive rationals gives (PL386-5). Rational independence merely removes equality ambiguities between distinct coordinates; the order cut itself is the essential datum.

The same observation yields a useful abstract statement. Let `G,H subset R` be nonzero additive subgroups and let `phi:G->H` be an order-preserving group isomorphism. Fix `x>0` in `G`. For every `y>0` and positive integers `m,n`,

\[
mx<ny
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m\phi(x)<n\phi(y).
\tag{PL386-11}
\]

Thus `y/x` and `phi(y)/phi(x)` define the same Dedekind cut in `Q`, so

\[
\frac{\phi(y)}{\phi(x)}=\frac yx.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\phi(g)=c g\quad(g\in G)}
\tag{PL386-12}
\]

for one `c>0`. A common rescaling is genuinely invisible in the shift representation: the unitary

\[
(U_cf)(x)=c^{1/2}f(cx)
\]

satisfies

\[
U_cS_tU_c^{-1}=S_{t/c}.
\tag{PL386-13}
\]

So the natural invariant carried by the ordered represented semigroup is exactly relative frequency geometry, not an absolute choice of time unit.

## 3. Prime specialization and the generic-frequency control

Unique factorization gives

\[
\sum_p z_p\log p
=
\log\left(\prod_pp^{z_p}\right),
\]

with finite support, proving (PL386-6). The positive cone is

\[
G_{\mathbb P}^+
=
\{\log q:q\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\ q\ge1\}.
\tag{PL386-14}
\]

This does distinguish the prime-frequency system from the completely universal generator-preserving polydisk algebra of `PL-385`: after adjoints are admitted, integer differences allow order comparisons and hence numerical ratios to reappear.

But the matched generic-frequency control remains decisive. For **every** positive family `b`, the same calculation constructs the ordered subgroup `(G_b,G_b^+)` and its half-line shift Toeplitz algebra. Nothing in (PL386-2)--(PL386-13) uses primes, the Euler product, Möbius inversion, the functional equation, or analytic continuation. Generic rationally independent frequencies retain their own ratio data in exactly the same manner. The rational primes contribute the particular ordered subgroup `log Q_{>0}`, but the operator mechanism that reads the order is universal.

Accordingly this is a boundary result, not an RH mechanism. An RH-relevant enrichment would still need to explain why the specific arithmetic embedding `log Q_{>0} subset R`, together with some additional target or analytic structure, forces a zeta-specific cancellation or positivity statement.

## 4. Relation to the three closure regimes

Together `PL-383`, `PL-385`, and the present finding isolate three different losses of information.

The norm-closed **nonselfadjoint** algebra of `PL-385` retains the oriented free positive monoid but is isometrically the same polydisk algebra for every rationally independent frequency basis; numerical time ratios disappear. The norm-closed **selfadjoint** system here admits group differences and its canonical range projections recover the inherited order, so frequency ratios return up to common scale. The **strong/selfadjoint** closure of `PL-383`, by contrast, uses density and strong continuity: two incommensurate times recover the entire continuous shift semigroup and generate `B(L^2(R_+))`, erasing the discrete subgroup again.

Thus the norm-selfadjoint category is the narrow intermediate regime in which discrete Archimedean frequency geometry remains visible. This resolves the `C*`-structure branch left open after `PL-385`, but what survives is ordered-group data rather than zeta continuation or zero data.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The selfadjoint half-line translation algebra is classical. L. A. Coburn and R. G. Douglas, “Translation operators on the half-line,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* **62** (1969), 1010--1013, DOI https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.62.4.1010, explicitly study the selfadjoint algebra generated by the translation semigroup on the half-line and its Wiener-Hopf/almost-periodic structure.

The ordered-group formulation is also established prior art. G. J. Murphy, “Ordered groups and Toeplitz algebras,” *Journal of Operator Theory* **18** (1987), 303--326, develops Toeplitz algebras for ordered groups. S. Adji, M. Laca, M. Nilsen and I. Raeburn, “Crossed products by semigroups of endomorphisms and the Toeplitz algebras of ordered groups,” *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **122** (1994), 1133--1141, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1994-1215024-1, prove in particular that for a totally ordered abelian group `Gamma` the `C*`-algebras generated by nonunitary isometric representations of `Gamma^+` are canonically isomorphic. This places (PL386-7) squarely inside classical Toeplitz/Wiener-Hopf theory once the ordered group `log Q_{>0}` is identified.

No novelty is claimed for ordered-group Toeplitz algebras or half-line translation `C*`-algebras. The exact calculations (PL386-8)--(PL386-13) are elementary. The durable Mathia delta is the **route-specific classification boundary** relative to `PL-383`--`PL-385`: adjoining adjoints in norm closure does recover numerical relative-frequency information that the analytic polydisk algebra loses, but the recovered structure is precisely classical ordered-group data and therefore does not itself supply an RH bridge.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification conditions

The strongest claim here is about the **represented shift system with its distinguished generators/range projections**. It must not be upgraded without proof to the statement that the unlabelled abstract `C*`-algebra `mathcal T_b` alone reconstructs the embedded ordered subgroup or the individual frequency ratios. The cited ordered-group results establish a canonical algebra for a fixed ordered group; they do not, by themselves, prove the converse classification of arbitrary such `C*`-algebras across different ordered groups.

Likewise, (PL386-2) does not assert that no additional `S_t` with `t notin G_b^+` can belong accidentally to the completed `C*`-algebra. The present conclusion needs only the exact inclusion of every `G_b^+` shift and the order information carried by their canonical range projections. Any stronger spectral-synthesis statement would require a separate proof.

This finding should be corrected or withdrawn if any of the following fails:

1. the adjoint-product identity (PL386-8) for the half-line shifts;
2. the positive/negative decomposition proving (PL386-2);
3. the range-projection order equivalence (PL386-4);
4. the ratio reconstruction (PL386-5), or the claim that order-preserving isomorphisms between real additive subgroups are common scalings;
5. the identification `span_Z{log p}=log Q_{>0}`;
6. the applicability of the classical ordered-group Toeplitz/Wiener-Hopf literature to the nonunitary half-line shift representation.
