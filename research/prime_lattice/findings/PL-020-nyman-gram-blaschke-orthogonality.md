# PL-020 — Nyman generator Gram geometry is blind to the off-line Blaschke factor; the RH obstruction splits orthogonally from the arithmetic Gram defect

## Claim

The quantitative Gram/frame route suggested by the discrete Nyman problem in `PL-019` has an exact structural limitation. In the Hardy formulation of Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte, the common inner factor generated by hypothetical zeta zeros in `Re(s)>1/2` is invisible to every pairwise Gram quantity of the Nyman generators.

Let

```text
H = H^2({Re(s)>1/2})
```

with the standard boundary norm, and let

```text
psi_n(s) = (n^(-s)-n^(-1)) zeta(s)/s,   n>=2.
```

These are the Bagchi/Nyman Hardy generators from `PL-017`. Let `Z_+` be the multiset of zeta zeros `rho` with `Re(rho)>1/2`, including multiplicity, and let `B_Z` be the corresponding half-plane Blaschke product, normalized by `B_Z(1)>0`. Since every `psi_n` contains the zeta factor and belongs to `H`, `B_Z` divides every `psi_n`. Write

```text
psi_n = B_Z phi_n,
phi_n in H.
```

Multiplication by an inner function is an isometry on `H`, so for every `m,n`

```text
<psi_m,psi_n> = <phi_m,phi_n>.
```

Hence every finite generator Gram matrix, and every quantity depending only on that matrix — eigenvalues, singular values, condition number, frame/Riesz bounds, spectral whitening, off-diagonal decay, block compressibility — is unchanged after removing the entire off-line-zero Blaschke factor.

There is also an exact orthogonal decomposition of the finite Nyman approximation error. Let

```text
k_1(s)=1/s
```

be the reproducing kernel at `s=1`, so `||k_1||=1` in the standard normalization. For

```text
A_M   = span{phi_n : 2<=n<=M},
Psi_M = span{psi_n : 2<=n<=M} = B_Z A_M,
```

orthogonal projection onto `B_Z H` gives

```text
P_(B_Z H) k_1 = conjugate(B_Z(1)) B_Z k_1.
```

Therefore

```text
dist(k_1,Psi_M)^2
 = 1-|B_Z(1)|^2
   + |B_Z(1)|^2 dist(k_1,A_M)^2.                  (1)
```

The first term is a rigid model-space obstruction depending only on the off-line zero divisor; the second is the residual discrete/arithmetic approximation defect after the common Blaschke factor has been removed. The cross-term is exactly zero because

```text
H = (H \ominus B_Z H) orthogonal_sum B_Z H.
```

Thus a **generator-Gram-only** program cannot force RH: its entire input is invariant under removal of precisely the inner factor whose triviality is equivalent to the absence of zeros in `Re(s)>1/2`. Quantitative Gram analysis can still be useful for the second term of (1), but it needs additional target/embedding data or an independent argument forcing `B_Z=1` before it can address the RH obstruction itself.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the specifically stated generator-Gram-only route. The Hardy/Nyman setup and inner-function/model-space identities are classical. A May 2026 preprint by T. A. Ziad independently states essentially the same Blaschke-skeleton/arithmetic-Gram orthogonal split; because that source is a recent unrefereed preprint, the exact identities above are re-derived here directly from standard Hardy-space projection rather than taken on trust. No novelty is claimed.

## Exact Hardy-space derivation

For the half-plane `Re(s)>1/2`, the reproducing kernel can be normalized as

```text
k_w(s) = 1/(s + conjugate(w) - 1).
```

At `w=1`,

```text
k_1(s)=1/s,
||k_1||^2=k_1(1)=1.
```

Let `M_B` denote multiplication by an inner function `B`. Then `M_B` is an isometry and

```text
M_B^* k_w = conjugate(B(w)) k_w.
```

Consequently the projection onto `BH` is

```text
P_(BH) k_w
 = M_B M_B^* k_w
 = conjugate(B(w)) B k_w.
```

Specializing to `B=B_Z` and `w=1`,

```text
||(I-P_(B_Z H))k_1||^2
 = ||k_1||^2 - ||P_(B_Z H)k_1||^2
 = 1-|B_Z(1)|^2.
```

Since `Psi_M=B_Z A_M` lies inside `B_Z H`, Pythagoras gives

```text
dist(k_1,Psi_M)^2
 = ||(I-P_(B_Z H))k_1||^2
   + dist(P_(B_Z H)k_1,B_Z A_M)^2.
```

Using the isometry of multiplication by `B_Z` and linearity of `A_M`,

```text
dist(P_(B_Z H)k_1,B_Z A_M)^2
 = dist(conjugate(B_Z(1)) k_1,A_M)^2
 = |B_Z(1)|^2 dist(k_1,A_M)^2,
```

which proves (1).

No Euler product or identity restricted to `Re(s)>1` enters this argument. The `psi_n` are the analytically continued Hardy functions of the Nyman formulation, and all operations occur inside `H^2(Re(s)>1/2)`.

## The zero energy is explicit

A half-plane Blaschke factor for `rho in Z_+` has, up to a unimodular normalization, modulus at `s=1`

```text
|b_rho(1)| = |1-rho|/|rho|.
```

Hence, counting multiplicity `m_rho`,

```text
|B_Z(1)|^2
 = product_(rho in Z_+) ( |1-rho|/|rho| )^(2 m_rho),
```

and the rigid term in (1) is

```text
E_Z = 1-|B_Z(1)|^2.
```

For every `rho=beta+i gamma` with `beta>1/2`,

```text
|rho|^2-|1-rho|^2 = 2 beta-1 > 0,
```

so each such factor has modulus strictly below one. Thus any off-line zero makes `E_Z>0`. Under RH the right-half zero multiset is empty, the normalized product is `B_Z=1`, and `E_Z=0`.

This is consistent with the classical zero-based lower bounds for Nyman approximation due to Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias and Burnol. The point here is the sharper structural separation from the generator Gram geometry.

## Why the generator Gram matrix cannot see the RH obstruction

For any finite index set `I`, define

```text
G_I = ( <psi_m,psi_n> )_(m,n in I).
```

After deflation by the full off-line-zero Blaschke product,

```text
G_I
 = ( <B_Z phi_m,B_Z phi_n> )
 = ( <phi_m,phi_n> ).
```

Therefore a putative certificate of the form

```text
F(G_I) -> some limiting spectral/frame property
```

cannot, without extra non-Gram input, distinguish the original Nyman system from its de-Blaschke image. In particular, the following properties are invariant under the common inner factor:

- eigenvalues and singular values of each finite generator Gram matrix;
- condition numbers and spectral gaps of those Gram matrices;
- frame or Riesz bounds for a fixed indexed family;
- any whitening or regularization that is a function only of the generator Gram matrix;
- sparsity, off-diagonal decay, or block structure measured only through pairwise generator inner products.

This is an information-loss statement, not a numerical objection. Better conditioning or a beautiful prime-lattice pattern in `G_I` may be mathematically useful, but such a pattern cannot by itself certify that `Z_+` is empty because multiplication by `B_Z` erases no Gram data at all.

## What Gram methods can still contribute

The negative result is deliberately narrow. Finite Nyman approximation is not determined by the generator Gram matrix alone: solving the projection problem also uses the target cross-vector

```text
b_n = <k_1,psi_n>.
```

Equivalently, formula (1) shows that after de-Blaschke factorization the target changes relative to the generator span. Thus a Gram-based method augmented by target evaluations/cross-correlations, divisibility information, model-space projections, or another structure not invariant under common inner multiplication is **not** ruled out.

Likewise, quantitative control of the de-Blaschke span `A_M` could still settle the arithmetic term in (1). What it cannot do alone is make the orthogonal model-space term `1-|B_Z(1)|^2` disappear.

This distinction matters because recent work genuinely studies nontrivial Nyman Gram structure. Alouges–Darses–Hillion obtain structured Gram matrices, including a block-Hankel form, in generalized Nyman approximation; Carvill proves polynomial off-diagonal Gram decay and block compressibility for a `2^{-j}3^{-k}` multiplicative ladder. Those results remain potentially useful for arithmetic approximation, but their generator-only spectral data do not by themselves overcome the inner-factor obstruction proved above.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The route was searched structurally through Nyman–Beurling finite-distance formulas, Gram matrices, Vasyunin/cotangent-sum Gram formulas, Hardy inner/outer factorization, model spaces, reproducing-kernel projections, and recent Gram-spectral work.

- **Nikolski (1995)** already supplies the continuous-scale model-space/Blaschke framework recorded in `PL-018`.
- **Burnol (2002)** derives zero-dependent lower bounds for the Nyman approximation distance and constructs Hilbert-space vectors tied to zeros, so a rigid zero obstruction to approximation is classical.
- **Alouges–Darses–Hillion (2022)** explicitly identify Gram-matrix control as a major quantitative difficulty in a generalized Nyman criterion and derive special Gram structures.
- **Carvill (2025 preprint)** studies unconditional spectral/decay structure of Nyman Gram matrices on a multiplicative ladder.
- **T. A. Ziad (19 May 2026 preprint)** is the closest direct prior art found: its abstract explicitly separates a rigid Blaschke skeleton from an arithmetic Gram projection defect and states an orthogonal splitting with zero cross-term. This essentially classicalizes the organization proposed here. The preprint is used as a novelty boundary, not as sole proof authority.

Accordingly, neither the Gram-invariance observation nor the orthogonal split should be presented as a new RH mechanism. Their value for `prime_lattice` is as a **route-killing distinction**: they tell us exactly which quantitative information can and cannot carry the off-line zero divisor.

## Boundary conditions and adversarial checks

### The negative result concerns generator-only Gram data

A method using `G_I` together with target cross-correlations, evaluations, Mellin divisibility, an explicit model-space component, or other non-isometry-invariant information is not covered by the obstruction. It would be incorrect to conclude that all Gram-based Nyman methods are useless.

### Deflating by `B_Z` does not solve the discrete classification

The functions `phi_n=psi_n/B_Z` still encode the difficult sparse semigroup geometry from `PL-019`. Their closed span may have additional defect. Formula (1) separates the known zero inner factor from that residual question; it does not classify `closure(span{phi_n})`.

### No assumption that the discrete common inner factor is only `B_Z`

`B_Z` is a common inner divisor because of the off-line zeta zeros. The discrete family could in principle possess additional common inner structure; Balazard's classification problem is precisely not settled. The argument needs only divisibility by `B_Z`, not equality with the greatest common inner divisor.

### The Blaschke product is legitimate

Because each `psi_n` is an `H^2` function, its zeros satisfy the half-plane Blaschke condition. The zeta zeros in `Re(s)>1/2`, with their multiplicities, form a subset of those zeros for every `psi_n`, so the corresponding `B_Z` is a valid inner divisor.

### The critical-strip identity is not an Euler-product extrapolation

The Hardy generators are the analytically continued Mellin images from the Nyman theory, as audited in `PL-017`. The present proof uses only Hilbert-space operations on those functions in `Re(s)>1/2`.

## Falsification / audit test

The finding is falsified if any one of these exact statements fails:

1. every Bagchi/Nyman generator `psi_n` is in `H^2(Re(s)>1/2)` and vanishes to at least the zeta multiplicity at each `rho` with `Re(rho)>1/2`;
2. the resulting Blaschke product `B_Z` is therefore an inner divisor of every `psi_n`;
3. multiplication by `B_Z` is isometric, hence leaves all generator Gram matrices unchanged;
4. the standard projection identity `P_(B H)k_w=conjugate(B(w))B k_w` holds;
5. substituting `w=1` and `Psi_M=B_Z A_M` yields equation (1).

A future paper that obtains RH from a matrix called a “Gram matrix” would not contradict this result unless its decisive information is genuinely a function only of pairwise inner products among the common-inner-divisible generators. If it also uses a target vector or another external observable, it lies outside the negative claim.

## Consequence for the research line

`PL-019` left quantitative frame/Riesz or Gram control as one plausible way to attack the sparse integer Nyman frontier. This finding narrows that suggestion materially:

```text
prime-exponent / integer Nyman generators
       |
       | common off-line-zero inner divisor B_Z
       v
psi_n = B_Z phi_n
       |
       +--> generator Gram spectrum: unchanged by B_Z
       |
       +--> target approximation:
            rigid zero energy  +  de-Blaschke arithmetic defect
```

The next useful target is therefore **not** a spectral invariant of the generator Gram matrix by itself. A viable lattice mechanism must either

```text
(a) force B_Z=1 by information external to generator-only Gram geometry,
```

or

```text
(b) couple a quantitative analysis of the de-Blaschke discrete span to the distinguished target/model-space component strongly enough to eliminate the first term of (1).
```

This preserves the discrete prime-semigroup problem as a legitimate frontier while ruling out the tempting idea that increasingly refined Gram conditioning or generator-spectrum analysis alone can reveal why the off-line zero divisor is absent.
