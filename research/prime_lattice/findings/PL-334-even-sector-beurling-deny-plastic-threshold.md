# PL-334 — Even-sector Beurling–Deny positivity fails at the plastic wall before the first prime event

**Evidence:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/ROUTE-RESTRICTION`

**Status:** `EXACT-EVEN-SECTOR-BEURLING-DENY-THRESHOLD + PRE-PRIME-OBSTRUCTION + PARITY-LOOPHOLE-CLOSED`

## Claim

Let `Q_W^a` be Suzuki's closed localized Weil form on `(-a,a)`, restricted to the real even subspace. Write `rho_pl>1` for the plastic constant,

\[
\rho_{\rm pl}^3-\rho_{\rm pl}-1=0,
\qquad
\rho_{\rm pl}=1.324717957\ldots,
\]

and set

\[
\boxed{
a_{\rm even}:=\log\rho_{\rm pl}=0.281199574\ldots .
}
\]

Then the first Beurling--Deny modulus criterion for the even restriction has the exact threshold

\[
\boxed{
Q_W^a(|u|)\le Q_W^a(u)\quad\text{for every real even }u
\iff
0<a\le a_{\rm even}.
}
\]

Hence the self-adjoint semigroup generated by the even restriction is positivity preserving exactly through `a=log rho_pl`. This threshold is still strictly before the first rational-prime translation can enter:

\[
\log\rho_{\rm pl}
<\frac12\log2,
\]

because `rho_pl<sqrt(2)`.

This closes the natural parity loophole left by `PL-294` and sharpened by `PL-324`. The full-space modulus criterion fails already at `(1/2)log rho_pl`; odd parity repairs the continuous obstruction all the way to the first-prime wall `(1/2)log2`; but even parity only doubles the full-space threshold to `log rho_pl` and still loses order positivity in a purely archimedean window. Therefore the evenness of a localized-Weil ground state cannot by itself restore the standard positivity-preserving-semigroup/Hopf route near the first-prime threshold.

## 1. Fold the continuous defect into the even sector

Use the unscaled continuous defect function isolated in `PL-324`,

\[
F(t)
:=-r''(t)-\frac1{2t}
=
2\cosh(t/2)-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}},
\qquad t>0.
\]

`PL-294` shows that `F` has the unique positive zero

\[
t_*:=\log\rho_{\rm pl},
\]

with `F(t)<0` for `0<t<t_*` and `F(t)>0` for `t>t_*`.

For an even function write

\[
u(x)=f(|x|),\qquad 0<|x|<a,
\]

and define

\[
D_f(x,y):=|f(x)f(y)|-f(x)f(y)\ge0.
\]

Folding the full continuous absolute-value defect over the two half intervals gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm cont}(|u|)-Q_{\rm cont}(u)
=
2\int_0^a\!\int_0^a
\bigl[F(|x-y|)+F(x+y)\bigr]
D_f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.
}
\]

The sign problem is therefore not the full-space coefficient `F(d)` and not the odd-sector difference `F(|x-y|)-F(x+y)` from `PL-324`, but the parity-paired sum

\[
F(|x-y|)+F(x+y).
\]

## 2. Concavity forces the paired sum negative up to `a=log rho_pl`

The needed concavity is local and exact. Put `z=e^{t/2}>1`. Then

\[
F(t)=z+z^{-1}-\frac{z^3}{z^4-1},
\]

and direct differentiation gives

\[
F''(t)
=
\frac{
 z^{14}-3z^{10}-25z^8+3z^6-6z^4-z^2-1
}{
4z(z-1)^3(z+1)^3(z^2+1)^3
}.
\]

For `0<t<=2t_*` one has `1<z<=rho_pl<3/2`. Dividing the numerator by `z^8` and discarding negative terms gives

\[
\frac{N(z)}{z^8}
=
z^6-3z^2-25+3z^{-2}-6z^{-4}-z^{-6}-z^{-8}
<z^6-22<0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{F''(t)<0\qquad(0<t\le2t_*).}
\]

Now let `0<x<y<a<=t_*`. Set

\[
d=y-x,\qquad s=x+y.
\]

Then `0<d<s<2t_*` and `(d+s)/2=y`. Concavity gives

\[
F(d)+F(s)
\le2F(y)
<2F(t_*)=0.
\]

Hence the continuous even-sector modulus defect is nonpositive for every real even `u`, and is strictly favorable on every nontrivial sign-changing cross set.

There are no prime-power translations in this entire range. Indeed `rho_pl<sqrt(2)` because `x^3-x-1` is increasing for `x>1` and is already positive at `x=sqrt(2)`; therefore

\[
2a\le2\log\rho_{\rm pl}<\log2.
\]

The scalar and killing terms are unchanged by modulus, so the first Beurling--Deny criterion holds for the even restriction throughout `0<a<=log rho_pl`.

## 3. Above the plastic wall an even two-bump test defeats the criterion

Let `a>t_*`. Choose

\[
y_0\in(t_*,a)
\]

and then choose `x_0>0` small enough that

\[
y_0-x_0>t_*,
\qquad
y_0+x_0>t_*.
\]

Therefore

\[
F(y_0-x_0)+F(y_0+x_0)>0.
\]

Choose two narrow nonnegative smooth bumps centered at `x_0` and `y_0`, give them opposite signs in `f`, and extend `f` evenly to `u` on `(-a,a)`. For sufficiently small bump width the continuous cross interaction has strictly positive modulus defect of order the product of the two support widths.

If prime-power translations are active, there are only finitely many lags `log n<2a`. Choose `(x_0,y_0)` generically so that none of those lags equals any separation among the four reflected bump centers `+-x_0,+-y_0`. Shrinking the supports then makes every prime-translation modulus defect vanish on this test. This is possible because only finitely many resonance equalities are excluded.

Consequently

\[
Q_W^a(|u|)-Q_W^a(u)>0
\]

for every `a>t_*`. Thus prime terms cannot repair the even-sector order property after the continuous defect changes sign; the exact threshold is `a=t_*`.

## 4. Parity hierarchy and the live boundary problem

Together `PL-294`, `PL-324`, and the present result give three distinct exact order-positivity thresholds:

\[
\boxed{
 a_{\rm full}=\frac12\log\rho_{\rm pl}
 <
 a_{\rm even}=\log\rho_{\rm pl}
 <
 a_{\rm odd}=\frac12\log2.
}
\]

The distinction is structural. In the full space one sees a single continuous coefficient `F(d)`. Even parity pairs the same-half and opposite-half coefficients by addition, and local concavity postpones failure. Odd parity pairs them by subtraction; monotonicity makes the continuous defect favorable at every aperture, so the first rational-prime translation becomes the exact obstruction.

For the endpoint program in `PL-329`--`PL-333`, the important consequence is negative. `PL-333` showed that the full threshold operator is not Markov, but its simplest counterexample is compatible with the concern that parity restriction might restore order positivity for an even state. The present theorem removes that loophole: the even restricted semigroup has already lost the modulus property at `a=log rho_pl`, strictly before the first-prime aperture `a_0=(1/2)log2` where the current boundary-flux analysis is centered.

This still does **not** imply that the relevant even eigenfunction changes sign, nor does it prove `C_0=0` or `C_0!=0` in the endpoint expansion. It says only that fixed sign cannot be obtained from the standard Beurling--Deny/Perron--Frobenius mechanism after the plastic wall. Any proof of a one-sign threshold state or nonzero boundary coefficient must use state-specific information or a genuinely non-Markov comparison principle.

## Novelty and adversarial audit

Literature input is Suzuki's localized Weil form and its parity invariance (source 56 in `research/prime_lattice/SOURCES.md`) together with classical Beurling--Deny theory. `PL-294` already derived the scalar plastic wall for the unrestricted form, and `PL-324` derived the exact odd-sector folding identity and first-prime threshold. The new line-local content here is the even folding identity, the local concavity argument on `(0,2 log rho_pl]`, and the converse parity-compatible two-bump construction proving the exact threshold `a=log rho_pl`.

A targeted structure search around Suzuki's form, even/odd parity, the plastic curvature wall, modulus contraction, and positivity-preserving semigroups located recent parity-polarized/event-cell work that independently uses the same curvature wall `t=log rho_pl`, but no statement of this exact even-sector Beurling--Deny threshold. Accordingly no novelty is claimed for the plastic constant or curvature event; the classification is `EXACT-DERIVED` for this order-semigroup consequence.

Adversarial checks:

- **Not Weil positivity.** Failure of the modulus criterion does not imply that `Q_W^a` is indefinite or that any zeta zero is off the critical line.
- **No ground-state sign claim.** The theorem removes a generic sign mechanism; it does not prove sign change of the actual eigenstate.
- **Endpoint is included.** At `a=t_*`, interior points satisfy `max(x,y)<t_*`, so the concavity estimate is strictly negative away from null/zero-defect sets.
- **No hidden prime obstruction below threshold.** `2 log rho_pl<log2`, so the entire positive side of the theorem is prime-free.
- **Prime terms cannot rescue failure.** The converse test chooses its reflected bump centers away from the finitely many active translation lags, making the prime defect identically zero for sufficiently narrow supports.
- **Concavity is only used where proved.** No global concavity of `F` is asserted; `F''` eventually changes sign, but the proof only needs `0<t<=2 log rho_pl`.
