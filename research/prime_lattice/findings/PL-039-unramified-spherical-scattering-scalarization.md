# PL-039 — Unramified spherical scattering scalarizes the Riemann-zeta channel

## Claim

The most canonical operator-valued local-global scattering escape left open by `PL-038` collapses on the channel that carries the ordinary Riemann zeta function.

For a rank-one unramified principal series over `Q_p`, the `K_p`-fixed spherical subspace is one-dimensional. The standard local intertwining operator therefore has no genuinely operator-valued freedom on that line. In the usual rank-one parameter `u`, the Gindikin–Karpelevich formula gives

```text
M_p(u) f^o_(p,u)
  = c_p(u) f^o_(p,-u),

c_p(u)
  = L_p(u) / L_p(u+1).
```

For the trivial unramified character,

```text
L_p(u)=zeta_p(u)=(1-p^(-u))^(-1),
```

so, initially for `Re(u)>1`,

```text
product_p c_p(u)
  = zeta(u) / zeta(u+1).
```

In the classical modular-Eisenstein parameter `u=2s-1`, the finite factor is

```text
zeta(2s-1)/zeta(2s),
```

and the archimedean factor completes it to the scalar scattering coefficient

```text
phi(s)=Lambda(2s-1)/Lambda(2s).
```

This scalar is the same completed-zeta scattering data that underlies the automorphic zero-spectrum mechanism audited in `PL-033`.

Now divide the local intertwiner by its spherical scalar wherever that meromorphic normalization is defined:

```text
R_p(u)=c_p(u)^(-1) M_p(u).
```

Then exactly

```text
R_p(u) f^o_(p,u)=f^o_(p,-u).
```

Thus on the unramified spherical line the normalization removes **all** local zeta-factor singularity from the operator and leaves only the canonical Weyl transport of the spherical vector. Equivalently, if one keeps the unnormalized operator, every zeta-sensitive datum on that line is already contained in the scalar normalizing factor `c_p(u)`.

Hence the proposed escape

```text
canonically arithmetic local prime operators
+ adelic/global scattering composition
    -> non-scalar operator-valued rigidity for the Riemann zeta divisor
```

fails in the most canonical unramified spherical realization: the Riemann-zeta channel is one-dimensional locally, so its global intertwining/scattering datum is scalar. Passing from scalar scattering to the normalized operator does not uncover an additional hidden prime-lattice invariant; it removes the L-factor carrying the divisor.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the route

```text
unramified spherical GL(2)/rank-one standard intertwiners
+ operator-valued local-global completion
    -> new RH-sensitive joint prime rigidity beyond the scalar scattering factor.
```

The Gindikin–Karpelevich action on spherical vectors, normalization of intertwining operators by L-factors, and the global scalar action on spherical vectors are classical prior art. No novelty is claimed for these constructions. The derived contribution is the obstruction for the narrowed `prime_lattice` search: the operator-valued escape left after `PL-038` does not survive restriction to the canonical spherical channel that actually realizes the ordinary zeta Euler factors.

## Exact local rank-one calculation

Let `G=GL_2(Q_p)`, `B` its upper triangular Borel, and `I_p(chi)` an unramified normalized principal series. The hyperspecial-fixed space

```text
I_p(chi)^(GL_2(Z_p))
```

is one-dimensional, generated by the normalized spherical vector `f^o_chi`.

For the nontrivial Weyl element `w`, the standard intertwining integral `M_p(w,chi)` maps the spherical line in `I_p(chi)` to the spherical line in `I_p(w chi)`. Hundley records the rank-one formula

```text
M_p(w,chi) f^o_chi
  = [L_p(<chi,alpha^vee>)/L_p(<chi,alpha^vee>+1)]
    f^o_(w chi).
```

Thus the restriction is necessarily scalar even before using the explicit formula; the formula identifies that scalar with a ratio of local L-factors.

For the trivial unramified arithmetic channel write

```text
u=<chi,alpha^vee>.
```

Then

```text
c_p(u)
 = zeta_p(u)/zeta_p(u+1)
 = (1-p^(-u-1))/(1-p^(-u)).
```

The spherical-normalized operator

```text
R_p(u)=c_p(u)^(-1)M_p(u)
```

therefore satisfies

```text
R_p(u)f^o_(p,u)=f^o_(p,-u).
```

The point is not that every convention in the Langlands–Shahidi literature uses exactly this displayed scalar; normalizations may redistribute nonzero unit/gamma factors. The invariant conclusion is convention-independent:

```text
on a one-dimensional spherical line,
all unnormalized L-factor singularity is scalar,
and a normalization that removes that scalar leaves no residual matrix datum.
```

Keys–Shahidi and the subsequent normalized-intertwiner literature are classical prior art for precisely this separation of L-function normalizing factors from the regularized operator.

## Global product and the modular scattering coefficient

For `Re(u)>1`, absolute Euler-product convergence permits multiplication of the finite local scalars:

```text
product_p c_p(u)
 = product_p zeta_p(u)/zeta_p(u+1)
 = zeta(u)/zeta(u+1).
```

For the classical Eisenstein series of `SL_2(Z)`, put

```text
u=2s-1.
```

The constant term is

```text
E(z,s)
 = y^s + phi(s)y^(1-s) + nonconstant terms,
```

with

```text
phi(s)=Lambda(2s-1)/Lambda(2s),
Lambda(s)=pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2) zeta(s).
```

Thus the finite-prime ratio above is exactly the finite Euler part of the global scattering scalar, while the real place supplies the gamma factor.

This global scalarization is not an artifact of the elementary `GL_2` example. Heiermann studies global standard intertwining operators for everywhere-unramified data and explicitly formulates their action on a global spherical vector as a meromorphic scalar computed from Langlands–Shahidi L-data. The Riemann-zeta case is the rank-one trivial-character specialization.

## Analytic-continuation boundary

The identity

```text
product_p c_p(2s-1)
 = zeta(2s-1)/zeta(2s)
```

is an Euler-product identity only in its initial absolute-convergence region, e.g. `Re(s)>1` for the standard modular Eisenstein normalization.

Its continuation into the critical strip is supplied by the meromorphic continuation and functional equation of Eisenstein series/intertwining operators. The completed scattering coefficient

```text
phi(s)=Lambda(2s-1)/Lambda(2s)
```

is therefore legitimate meromorphic data outside the Euler-product half-plane, but not because the product over primes continues term-by-term.

This distinction is essential. The local scalarization theorem is algebraic/representation-theoretic; the passage to the global zeta divisor in the strip uses automorphic analytic continuation, exactly as in `PL-033`.

## Why normalization does not solve the localization problem

There are only two choices on the spherical zeta channel.

### Keep the unnormalized intertwiner

Then the operator acts by the scalar

```text
c_p(u)=L_p(u)/L_p(u+1)
```

locally and by the completed scalar `phi(s)` globally. One has recovered the classical scalar automorphic scattering factor. As `PL-033` shows, completed automorphic scattering can realize the zeta divisor very strongly — even as eigenvalues of a Lax–Phillips generator — but RH remains the assertion that the resulting spectrum lies on the distinguished symmetry axis.

### Normalize away the L-factor

Then on each spherical line

```text
R_p f^o=f^o,
```

up to the canonical Weyl identification of source and target parameters. The singular scalar carrying the L-function divisor has been removed. There is no residual prime-pair matrix curvature or scattering phase on this one-dimensional channel from which a stronger localization theorem could be extracted.

So the decomposition is structurally

```text
standard spherical intertwiner
   = scalar L-factor ratio
     x normalized spherical transport.

zeta-sensitive part
   = scalar factor;

operator-valued residual on the zeta spherical line
   = trivial transport.
```

This is the opposite of the hoped-for escape after `PL-038`: making the local-global object genuinely operator-valued does not preserve extra zeta information on the canonical unramified channel.

## Adversarial checks

### Off-spherical operator structure is real, but it is extra data

The full induced representation is not one-dimensional. Standard intertwiners can act nontrivially on other `K`-types, and ramified principal series have richer local structure. This finding does **not** claim that automorphic scattering is globally scalar as an operator.

The narrower statement is the relevant one for this line: the ordinary Riemann zeta Euler product comes from the everywhere-unramified trivial spherical channel. Once one leaves that line, one has introduced representation/K-type/ramification data not encoded by the bare positive-integer exponent vector.

A future construction may use such extra data, but it must prove why it is canonically forced by the Riemann-zeta problem and why its non-spherical structure constrains the spherical scalar rather than merely accompanying it.

### Operator unitarity on the symmetry axis does not imply RH

Normalized intertwiners are designed to have good functional-equation/unitarity properties. Those properties are classical and coexist with the unresolved RH. Likewise the scalar modular scattering coefficient satisfies its functional equation and has modulus one on the unitary axis away from singularities. This symmetry identifies the critical axis but does not force every zero/pole onto it.

Thus replacing an unnormalized scalar by a unitary normalized operator cannot by itself provide the missing localization theorem.

### This does not refute Tate's additive Fourier mechanism

`PL-014` uses additive Fourier transform, self-dual measures, the archimedean place, and Poisson summation to obtain continuation and the `s -> 1-s` duality. The present finding only says that **standard unramified intertwining/scattering, once decomposed into L-factor normalization plus normalized operator, contains no additional operator-valued zeta rigidity on its spherical line**.

### Beurling comparison

A generic Beurling system has no canonical `GL_2(Q_p)` local representation theory, so this construction does pass the weak test of using genuine rational-prime local arithmetic rather than merely an arbitrary frequency list.

But `PL-038` already showed that passing the Beurling-discrimination test is not sufficient. Here the exact rational local structure is canonical, yet on the zeta channel it scalarizes. The obstruction is therefore stronger than “Beurling can imitate it”: the canonical arithmetic operator itself has rank one on the relevant local state.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The core literature is classical and explicit:

- **Joseph Hundley**, “Holomorphy of adjoint L functions for quasisplit A2,” *Research in Number Theory* **4**, 44 (2018), DOI `10.1007/s40993-018-0136-8`. Lemma 2.1 records the `GL_2` rank-one local spherical formula `M_v f^o = L_v(u)/L_v(u+1) f^o` and normalized-intertwiner analyticity.
- **C. David Keys, Freydoon Shahidi**, “Artin L-functions and normalization of intertwining operators,” *Annales scientifiques de l'École Normale Supérieure* **21**(1) (1988), 67–89, DOI `10.24033/asens.1551`. Classical source for L-function normalization of intertwining operators.
- **Volker Heiermann**, “The value of the global intertwining operators on spherical vectors,” *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **147**(1) (2019), 115–124, DOI `10.1090/proc/14208`, arXiv:`1709.09107`. Computes the meromorphic scalar defined by a global standard intertwining operator acting on an everywhere-unramified spherical vector.

As a modern adjacent check, Qiyuan Chen's 2026 preprint *Spectral Intertwining Operators* again separates canonical normalized intertwiners from unnormalized ones by ratios of L-functions in a newer spectral/categorical setting. It is unreviewed and is not needed for the proof; it only confirms that the normalized-operator/L-factor separation is not an obsolete convention.

No novelty is claimed for any of this representation theory. The durable negative is the consequence for the current Mathia clue: **the canonical spherical operator-valued completion does not retain a non-scalar residue after the zeta normalizing factor is removed.**

## Relation to PL-033, PL-038, and the accepted clue

`PL-033` established that automorphic Lax–Phillips scattering already turns the completed zeta divisor into genuine generator eigenvalues. It left open whether a prime-local operator family might contain extra joint structure capable of forcing the critical axis.

`PL-038` then showed that canonical scalar reciprocity data can cancel globally and explicitly left **operator-valued local-global scattering** as the most plausible next escape for `CLUE-trace-class-prime-resolvent-cocycle`.

The present finding closes the most canonical version of that escape:

```text
everywhere-unramified spherical prime channel
    -> one-dimensional local state
    -> local standard intertwiner is scalar L-ratio
    -> global scalar is the classical zeta scattering coefficient
    -> normalized residual is trivial on the spherical state.
```

The clue should therefore no longer search for hidden RH rigidity in the normalized unramified spherical standard intertwiner itself.

## Falsification / escape tests

A future operator-valued local-global proposal escapes this negative only if it proves at least one of the following:

1. a canonically forced **non-spherical or ramified** local component is intrinsic to the ordinary Riemann-zeta problem and constrains the spherical scattering scalar;
2. an operator-valued target-relative construction (for example Nyman/model-space data) couples to the prime directions in a way not reducible to the one-dimensional unramified spherical line;
3. a global trace/positivity relation uses matrix coefficients outside the spherical line and proves a condition on the zeta divisor that is stronger than the existing functional equation/scattering unitarity;
4. the extra operator structure survives normalization, is not freely engineerable as in `PL-034`–`PL-036`, and remains nontrivial after the reciprocity/product-formula cancellations of `PL-038`.

Conversely, if a proposal starts from the ordinary unramified `GL_2` spherical channel and its only zeta dependence is the standard intertwining normalizing factor, it has reduced to classical scalar scattering and does not supply a new localization mechanism.
