# PL-005 — Bagchi strong recurrence gives an exact RH equivalence for the prime-frequency flow

## Claim

There is already a rigorous dynamical formulation of the Riemann hypothesis that is much closer to the prime-lattice / Bohr-flow viewpoint than a bare spectral analogy:

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RH}\iff \zeta \text{ is strongly recurrent under vertical translation in }1/2<\Re s<1.}
\]

In the prime coordinates of the Bohr lift, vertical translation is generated by the frequencies `log p`. Thus Bagchi's recurrence theorem supplies an exact known bridge between the distinguished zeta observable and the prime-frequency flow, but the bridge lives in a function-space universality/recurrence theory after analytic continuation, not in the pointwise Euler product.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED` — exact published equivalence plus its specialization to the prime-lattice coordinates.

## Exact recurrence theorem

Let

\[
D=\{s\in\mathbb C:1/2<\Re s<1\}.
\]

For a compact set \(K\subset D\) with connected complement and \(\varepsilon>0\), consider the return set

\[
R(K,\varepsilon)
=
\left\{\tau\ge 0:
\sup_{s\in K}|\zeta(s+i\tau)-\zeta(s)|<\varepsilon
\right\}.
\]

Bagchi's recurrence theorem states that RH is equivalent to these self-returns having positive asymptotic density in the strong-recurrence sense. A standard modern formulation is

\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}
\operatorname{meas}\big(R(K,\varepsilon)\cap[0,T]\big)>0
\]

for every such \(K\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\).

This is an equivalence, not merely a consequence of RH.

## Prime-torus theorem bridge

On the Bohr curve,

\[
z_p(s)=p^{-s},
\]

vertical translation acts coordinatewise by

\[
z_p(s+i\tau)
=
p^{-s}e^{-i\tau\log p}.
\]

For any finite set of distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_N\), the numbers

\[
\log p_1,\ldots,\log p_N
\]

are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\): an integer relation would exponentiate to a nontrivial multiplicative relation among distinct primes. Hence Kronecker/Weyl theory gives equidistribution of the finite-dimensional phase orbit

\[
\tau\longmapsto
(e^{-i\tau\log p_1},\ldots,e^{-i\tau\log p_N})
\]

on \(\mathbb T^N\).

Bagchi's probabilistic universality theory is the nontrivial extension of this prime-phase picture to analytic functions in the critical strip. The limiting function-space law is described using independent Haar prime phases / random Euler products, while the deterministic vertical shifts of zeta sample that law.

The important distinction is that the rigorous critical-strip statement is a limit theorem and support/recurrence statement in a space of analytic functions. It is **not** the assertion that the ordinary Euler product converges pointwise for \(1/2<\Re s<1\).

## Mathia-specific consequence

This prior art materially redirects the prime-lattice question.

The relevant established dynamical object is not merely

```text
the infinite torus + the frequency multiset {log p},
```

which `PL-003` already shows is too flexible. It is

```text
the vertical-translation orbit of the distinguished zeta observable
inside a function space after analytic continuation,
together with the support/recurrence properties of its limiting law.
```

Therefore a new prime-lattice mechanism should not aim only to rediscover that the `log p` flow is dense or almost periodic. To add mathematical content beyond Bagchi, it would need to explain or control the **strong recurrence/support property** in a way that is genuinely simpler, more rigid, or more structural than the known equivalence to RH.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The RH/strong-recurrence equivalence is classical prior art due to Bhaskar Bagchi. Bagchi's earlier probabilistic universality work supplies the function-space distributional framework behind the recurrence result. Later literature states the equivalence explicitly and develops ergodic universality variants.

No novelty claim is made for the equivalence, the Kronecker flow, or the random-prime-phase representation.

The durable contribution of this finding is the identification that the exact dynamical bridge sought by the prime-lattice line **already exists**, and that it lives one level above the bare prime torus: in recurrence of the zeta function as an analytic function under vertical translation.

## Boundary conditions and counterarguments

- This finding does not prove RH. It gives an exact equivalent condition.
- The ordinary Euler product does not converge in the critical strip. The torus-to-critical-strip bridge is probabilistic/universality theory, not pointwise continuation of \(\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\).
- `PL-003` is not contradicted. Generic or exceptional prime-phase twists can have different zero behavior; Bagchi's theorem concerns recurrence of the **distinguished** Riemann zeta function.
- Finite-dimensional equidistribution of prime phases is elementary compared with the full function-space universality theorem and is not by itself enough to obtain RH.
- A reformulation that merely replaces `s -> s+i tau` by prime-coordinate rotations without proving new structure is only a change of coordinates.

## Audit criterion

A proposed extension passes beyond this prior art only if it provides a precise statement that is not simply Bagchi recurrence written in prime coordinates.

A decisive test is whether the proposal yields a verifiable sufficient or necessary condition for strong recurrence that can be stated and checked independently of knowing the zeta zero set. If every step reduces to the existing universality/recurrence equivalence, it adds no new RH mechanism.

## Consequence for the research line

Prime-lattice dynamics does have a rigorous RH-level target:

\[
\text{prime-frequency vertical flow}
\longrightarrow
\text{function-space recurrence of }\zeta
\longleftrightarrow
\mathrm{RH}.
\]

The open mathematical burden is not to invent the dynamical interpretation, but to find additional rigid structure that makes the recurrence side tractable.
