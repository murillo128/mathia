# PL-276 — Finite positive Weil sources remain indefinite under every compact correction

**Evidence:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`

**Status:** `PROVED-STRUCTURAL + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + COMPACT-COMPLETION-OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-ADJACENT`

## Claim

`PL-275` proves that every nonzero finite positive atomic source supported strictly inside the normalized Weil window has an eventually indefinite even Galerkin image. That result can be strengthened in a way that separates the two completion channels left open there: the sign oscillation is not a fragile finite-dimensional accident and cannot be repaired by any fixed compact correction.

Let

\[
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\,\delta_{\omega_j},
\qquad a_j>0,\qquad 0<\omega_j<1,
\]

be nonzero, let `Q_N[mu]` be Groskin's level-`N` source matrix, and use the normalized even Fourier basis

\[
T_0(t)=1,
\qquad
T_k(t)=\sqrt2\cos(2\pi k t),\quad k\ge1.
\]

Let `H_even` be the Hilbert completion of the span of these modes, let `P_N` project onto `span{T_0,...,T_N}`, and let `K` be any self-adjoint compact operator on `H_even`. Then there are constants `eta_+,eta_->0`, `delta_+,delta_->0`, and sets `A_+,A_- subset N` of positive lower natural density such that, for all sufficiently large `k in A_+` and every `N>=k`,

\[
\langle T_k,(Q_N[\mu]+P_NKP_N)T_k\rangle>\delta_+,
\]

while for all sufficiently large `k in A_-`,

\[
\langle T_k,(Q_N[\mu]+P_NKP_N)T_k\rangle<-\delta_-.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
Q_N[\mu]+P_NKP_N
\text{ is indefinite for every sufficiently large }N.
}
\]

The same conclusion holds after multiplying the source block by `-1`, since the positive- and negative-density witness sets simply exchange roles.

Applied at a fixed rational-prime cutoff `c>2`, this gives a sharp completion obstruction. The complete positive von-Mangoldt source

\[
\nu_c^+
=
\sum_{q=p^a<c}
\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\sqrt q}\,
\delta_{1-\log q/\log c}
\]

remains indefinite after **every fixed compact correction**. In particular, the zeta pole contribution cannot repair the sign: at fixed aperture it is a finite-rank form, hence compact. Therefore any positivity of the fully completed localized Weil form must use an essentially noncompact channel — in the standard decomposition, the archimedean/scalar sector — or another noncompact coupling not present in the positive finite source plus pole block.

This is a route restriction, not an RH theorem. It does not claim that the archimedean term has the required sign, nor that the completed Weil form is indefinite.

## 1. Positive and negative source witnesses occur with positive density

For `k>=1`, `PL-275` records the exact diagonal source value

\[
C_k
:=
\langle T_k,Q_N[\mu]T_k\rangle
=
2\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\cos(2\pi k\omega_j)
+
\frac1{\pi k}\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\sin(2\pi k\omega_j),
\]

valid for every `N>=k`. Write

\[
F(k)=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\cos(2\pi k\omega_j),
\qquad
R(k)=\frac1{\pi k}\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\sin(2\pi k\omega_j),
\]

so `C_k=2F(k)+R(k)` and `R(k)=O(1/k)`.

Set

\[
g=(e^{2\pi i\omega_1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\omega_r})
\]

and let `H` be the compact monothetic subgroup generated by `g`. Define

\[
\Phi(z)=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega_j\,\Re z_j,
\qquad z\in H.
\]

Every coordinate character is nontrivial on `H`, because `0<omega_j<1`. Hence character orthogonality gives

\[
\int_H\Phi\,dm_H=0.
\]

At the identity,

\[
\Phi(1,\ldots,1)=\sum_j a_j\omega_j>0.
\]

Therefore `Phi` is positive on a nonempty open set. It must also be negative somewhere: if it were nonnegative everywhere, continuity and strict positivity near the identity would force a positive Haar mean, contradicting the zero integral. Thus there are nonempty open sets `U_+` and `U_-` and numbers `epsilon_+,epsilon_->0` such that

\[
\Phi>2\epsilon_+\quad\text{on }U_+,
\qquad
\Phi<-2\epsilon_-\quad\text{on }U_-.
\]

Rotation by `g` on `H` is a minimal rotation of a compact group and is uniquely ergodic with Haar measure. To avoid any boundary-measure issue, choose nonzero continuous functions `0<=psi_\pm<=1` supported inside `U_\pm`. Then

\[
\frac1M\sum_{k=1}^M\psi_\pm(g^k)
\longrightarrow
\int_H\psi_\pm\,dm_H>0.
\]

Hence the visit sets

\[
A_\pm=\{k\ge1:g^k\in U_\pm\}
\]

have positive lower natural density. This statement includes the completely rational case: then `H` is finite and the orbit is simply periodic with the Haar frequency of each orbit point.

Since `F(k)=Phi(g^k)` and `R(k)->0`, after deleting finitely many indices one gets uniform sign margins

\[
C_k>2\epsilon_+\quad(k\in A_+),
\qquad
C_k<-2\epsilon_-\quad(k\in A_-).
\]

Thus the aggregate theorem of `PL-275` is substantially more recurrent than the single-witness proof needed there: both signs occur on positive-density sets of arbitrarily high Fourier modes.

## 2. Compact corrections vanish on the recurrent witness modes

The orthonormal sequence `T_k` converges weakly to zero in `H_even`. Compactness therefore implies

\[
\|KT_k\|\longrightarrow0,
\]

and hence

\[
|\langle T_k,KT_k\rangle|
\le \|KT_k\|
\longrightarrow0.
\]

For `N>=k`, compression does not change this diagonal value:

\[
\langle T_k,P_NKP_NT_k\rangle
=
\langle T_k,KT_k\rangle.
\]

Choose `k` large enough that the compact diagonal is smaller than `epsilon_+` or `epsilon_-`. The source margins from the previous section then survive:

\[
\langle T_k,(Q_N[\mu]+P_NKP_N)T_k\rangle
>\epsilon_+
\quad(k\in A_+),
\]

and

\[
\langle T_k,(Q_N[\mu]+P_NKP_N)T_k\rangle
<-\epsilon_-
\quad(k\in A_-).
\]

Because both visit sets contain arbitrarily large indices, choose one sufficiently large positive witness and one sufficiently large negative witness. Once both have entered the Galerkin space, they remain available at every larger level. This proves eventual permanent indefiniteness of the compactly corrected compressions.

No boundedness or infinite-matrix realization of the source block itself is required. The argument uses only the exact stable diagonal values of the finite source matrices and a fixed compact correction on the ambient Fourier Hilbert space.

## 3. The zeta pole is too small in operator category

At fixed localization radius, the pole term in the completed Weil form factors through finitely many Laplace evaluation functionals. `PL-059` writes it explicitly as

\[
Q_{\rm pole}(v)
=
2\Re\bigl(V_{1/2}(v)\,\overline{V_{-1/2}(v)}\bigr),
\qquad
V_s(v)=\int v(x)e^{sx}\,dx.
\]

On a fixed finite interval these are bounded functionals, so the associated self-adjoint operator has finite-dimensional range. It is therefore compact. Equivalently, the current finite CvS implementation decomposes the completed matrix into an archimedean Mellin-multiplier block, a finite-rank pole correction, and the finite von-Mangoldt block.

Take `K` to be that pole operator. The theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm prime}^{(N)}(c)+D_{\rm pole}^{(N)}(c)
\text{ remains indefinite for all sufficiently large }N,
}
\]

with the same statement for the opposite source-sign convention. Therefore the pole can cancel particular low-dimensional or boundary asymptotics — as it does in `PL-059` — but it cannot globally turn the fixed-cutoff positive finite source block into a semidefinite form as Fourier resolution tends to infinity.

This distinguishes two roles that were still bundled together after `PL-275`. The pole is structurally essential in the completed explicit formula but belongs to a compact operator category at fixed aperture. The archimedean/scalar sector is not compact and is therefore the only standard completion channel capable of changing the recurrent high-mode sign ledger established here.

## 4. Relation to the current Weil frontier

`PL-275` leaves open the possibility that the pole/archimedean completion could supply the missing order even though the entire positive finite von-Mangoldt source is itself eventually indefinite. The present result removes the compact half of that escape.

The fixed-cutoff hierarchy is now:

\[
\text{positive finite von-Mangoldt source}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{positive-density high-mode witnesses of both signs};
\]

\[
\text{add any fixed compact correction, including the pole}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{both signs survive};
\]

\[
\text{full completed Weil form}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{any sign repair must use noncompact completion structure.}
\]

This is complementary to the moving-boundary result `PL-059`. There the pole cancels the universal PNT rank-one boundary profile but leaves the essential prime-log recurrence untouched because finite-rank corrections disappear in the Calkin quotient. Here the variable is different — the cutoff is fixed and the Galerkin frequency tends to infinity — yet the operator-category conclusion is parallel: finite-rank/compact pole completion cannot remove the arithmetic sign recurrence.

The surviving target is therefore sharper than “use completion.” A useful RH-facing theorem must exploit the **specific noncompact archimedean/scalar coupling to the rational-prime source**, or another noncompact completed relation, and must show why that coupling supplies the Weil sign rather than merely dominating generic finite-source oscillation at high frequency.

## 5. Prior-art and adversarial audit

The load-bearing finite-source kernel is Groskin's Lemma 2.3 in *A finite Guinand--Weil dictionary and archimedean tail order for the truncated Weil quadratic form*, already anchored in `research/prime_lattice/SOURCES.md` and used in `PL-270`--`PL-275`. `PL-059` supplies the exact finite-rank factorization of the zeta pole in the localized completed form.

The recurrence input is classical compact-group dynamics: a dense cyclic rotation on its compact orbit closure is uniquely ergodic with Haar measure. Walters' *An Introduction to Ergodic Theory*, already anchored in `SOURCES.md`, is sufficient background. The compact-operator step is the standard fact that a compact operator sends weakly null orthonormal sequences to norm-null sequences.

A targeted literature search found no statement in the current CvS/Groskin/Suzuki sources of the fixed-cutoff theorem above: positive-density two-sided source witnesses and their persistence under every compact correction. No novelty is claimed for either the unique-ergodic or compactness lemma. The durable delta is their application to the exact finite Weil source kernel, which proves that **compact completion is categorically incapable of repairing the source sign**.

The main adversarial boundaries are essential:

- The endpoint `omega=1` is excluded. For that atom the source matrix is `2I`, so the zero-mean torus argument fails and compact perturbations need not create two-sided high-mode signs.
- Atoms at `omega=0` are harmless because their source matrix vanishes.
- Signed source weights are outside the theorem; positivity of the coefficients is used to guarantee a strictly positive value at the identity before the zero-mean argument.
- The theorem is finite-atomic. It is exactly sufficient for every fixed rational-prime cutoff, but no claim is made for arbitrary continuous or infinite source measures.
- The correction must be the compression of one fixed compact operator. An `N`-dependent family with no compact ambient limit can act with order-one strength on moving Fourier modes and is not covered.
- The identity/scalar channel and the archimedean Mellin multiplier are noncompact and are deliberately not ruled out.
- No conclusion about RH or the sign of the fully completed Weil form follows. The theorem only proves that one standard component of completion — the pole, and more generally every compact correction — cannot be the missing order mechanism by itself.

## Research implication

The next completion-sensitive question should no longer ask whether the finite von-Mangoldt source becomes positive after adding the pole or another compact regularizer. That is impossible. The mathematically live question is whether the noncompact archimedean/scalar sector has a **zeta-specific coupling** to the prime-power source strong enough to force the completed Weil orientation, and whether that coupling survives matched generalized-prime or phase-twisted controls.

A useful next lemma would therefore compare the recurrent source witness sets above with the high-frequency symbol of the archimedean Weil operator, not merely with another finite-rank or compact correction. A positive result must identify an arithmetic relation beyond generic coercive domination; a negative matched control showing the same repair for arbitrary finite positive sources would demonstrate that high-frequency completion is universal and push the RH-sensitive content back to a mesoscopic/global regime.
