# PL-141 — Möbius inverse small-sum rigidity is pole-oriented and prime-phase tautological

## Claim

Recent inverse-structure theorems for multiplicative functions with small partial sums do not provide a new RH-localization mechanism when specialized to the canonical Möbius point of the prime-exponent lattice.

For `f=mu`,

`L(s,f)=sum_(n>=1) mu(n)n^(-s)=1/zeta(s)`

in `Re(s)>1`, and the generalized von Mangoldt coefficients defined by `f log = f * Lambda_f` satisfy the exact identity

`Lambda_f(n)=-Lambda(n)`.

Thus Möbius is the extremal `D=1` case of the Koukoulopoulos small-partial-sum framework. But its prime data are already exactly

`f(p)=-1`,

and, more generally, for the Archimedean twist `f_gamma(n)=mu(n)n^(i gamma)`,

`f_gamma(p)=-p^(i gamma)`.

Consequently the inverse theorem's prime-phase conclusion recovers a phase pattern already built into the Möbius coefficients. More importantly, the theorem's zero-transition mechanism is oriented toward **zeros of `L(s,f)` near `s=1`**, whereas nontrivial Riemann zeros are **poles** of `L(s,mu)=1/zeta(s)`. The canonical zero of `1/zeta` at `s=1` comes instead from the pole of `zeta` and is known independently.

Any RH-strength zero-free consequence obtained from a hypothesis such as `M(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n)=O(x^(1/2+epsilon))` enters already through direct analytic continuation of `1/zeta` by partial summation; it is not supplied by the inverse prime-phase/zero-transition theorem.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-DELIMITED`.

The inverse structure theorems are literature. The Möbius specialization, divisor-orientation check, and separation between direct continuation and inverse phase recovery are exact derived consequences. No novelty is claimed for the underlying small-sum theory or for the standard Möbius formulation of RH.

## Exact Möbius specialization

Let

`F(s)=sum_(n>=1) f(n)n^(-s)`.

If `Lambda_f` is defined by

`f log = f * Lambda_f`,

then in every half-plane of absolute convergence,

`-F'(s)/F(s)=sum_(n>=1) Lambda_f(n)n^(-s)`.

For `f=mu`,

`F(s)=1/zeta(s)`

for `Re(s)>1`. Hence

`-F'(s)/F(s)=zeta'(s)/zeta(s)=-sum_(n>=1) Lambda(n)n^(-s)`,

so coefficient uniqueness gives

`Lambda_mu(n)=-Lambda(n)`.

Therefore `|Lambda_mu|=Lambda`, exactly the hypothesis `|Lambda_f|<=D Lambda` with `D=1`.

At every prime,

`mu(p)=-1`.

For the vertical/Kronecker twist

`f_gamma(n)=mu(n)n^(i gamma)`,

we likewise have

`f_gamma(p)=-p^(i gamma)`

and

`L(s,f_gamma)=1/zeta(s-i gamma)`

in the absolute-convergence half-plane. In exponent coordinates this twist is exactly the one-parameter phase translation generated by the energy

`log n=<v(n),(log p)_p>`.

Thus the prime-phase model singled out by inverse pretentious theory is not hidden information for the Möbius point: it is already an exact coordinate description of that point and its vertical translates.

## What the current inverse theorem adds in general

Koukoulopoulos's 2026 theorem studies multiplicative functions with `|Lambda_f|<=D Lambda` and sufficiently small partial sums. Theorem 1.1 produces logarithmic prime-scale regimes on which the average of `Re(f(p))` is close to negative integers. Under a substantially stronger partial-sum hypothesis, Theorem 1.2 relates the transition scales to zeros of `L(s,f)` in a small disk around `s=1`.

This is a genuine structural theorem for general multiplicative functions. It extends a line of work in which Koukoulopoulos--Soundararajan explicitly identify `mu(n)n^(i gamma)` as the basic inverse-structure model for small partial sums. The result is therefore strong prior art for any proposal that tries to infer a new prime-torus phase law merely from cancellation of a multiplicative function.

For `D=1` and the canonical Möbius function, however, the weak/logarithmic theorem degenerates to the already exact prime identity `mu(p)=-1`. The associated Dirichlet series has a simple zero at `s=1` because `zeta` has a simple pole there. This is precisely the zero at the PNT boundary that the reciprocal series naturally carries.

The stronger 2026 zero-location theorem cannot be applied unconditionally to Möbius at RH strength: its hypothesis contains a fixed power saving `x^(1-1/log Q)` (up to logarithms), far stronger than the unconditional information on `M(x)`. If one assumes a sufficiently strong Möbius bound to enter such a half-plane, the relevant zero-free continuation of `1/zeta` has already been obtained from that assumption by elementary Dirichlet-series theory.

## Divisor-orientation obstruction

The divisor of the reciprocal series has the opposite orientation from the Riemann-zero divisor. If `rho` is a nontrivial zero of `zeta`, then

`1/zeta(s)`

has a pole at `s=rho`, with matching order. Conversely, the pole of `zeta` at `s=1` produces the simple zero of `1/zeta` at `s=1`.

For a twist `f_gamma(n)=mu(n)n^(i gamma)`, the same statement is translated vertically:

`L(s,f_gamma)=1/zeta(s-i gamma)`.

The known zeta pole gives a zero of `L(s,f_gamma)` at `1+i gamma`, while every nontrivial Riemann zero `rho` gives a pole at `rho+i gamma`.

Koukoulopoulos's zero-transition mechanism is formulated in terms of zeros of `L(s,f)` near `1`; its logarithmic derivative contains terms of the form `1/(s-rho)` for those zeros. Applied canonically to Möbius, this channel therefore points at the reciprocal zero created by the zeta pole, not at the nontrivial Riemann zeros. Detecting the latter through the reciprocal series would require a **pole-sensitive** inverse statement or an equivalent control of the reciprocal's meromorphic continuation, which is a different mechanism.

This sign/orientation distinction is not semantic. Replacing `zeta` by `1/zeta` changes the relevant divisor from zeros to poles, and a theorem that classifies prime phases from zeros of the associated Dirichlet series does not automatically convert into a theorem localizing poles.

## Where RH information actually enters

Let

`M(x)=sum_(n<=x) mu(n)`.

For `Re(s)>1`, partial summation gives

`1/zeta(s)=s integral_1^infinity M(x)x^(-s-1) dx`.

If one assumes `M(x)=O(x^theta)` for some `theta<1`, the integral converges locally uniformly and defines a holomorphic continuation of the reciprocal series to `Re(s)>theta`. Since it agrees with `1/zeta(s)` on `Re(s)>1`, uniqueness of analytic continuation forces `zeta` to be zero-free in that half-plane (with the zero of the reciprocal at `s=1` canceling the zeta pole in the usual way).

In particular, the family of bounds

`M(x)=O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon))`

is the standard Möbius formulation equivalent to RH. The logical route is direct: cancellation of Möbius sums gives continuation/holomorphy of the reciprocal Dirichlet series, which excludes poles there, hence excludes zeta zeros. No inverse prime-phase classification is required.

This separates two very different uses of small partial sums:

- **direct analytic continuation**, which can carry RH-strength information because poles of `1/zeta` are exactly zeta zeros; and
- **inverse prime-phase structure**, which for Möbius recovers the already fixed phase `-1` (or `-p^(i gamma)` after twisting) and organizes zeros of the reciprocal near `1`.

Only the first route is naturally pole-sensitive to the Riemann divisor.

## Adversarial controls and boundary of the negative result

The result does **not** say that small-partial-sum methods are irrelevant to RH. The Möbius partial-sum criterion is one of the classical equivalent forms of RH, and any genuinely new way to prove a square-root bound for `M(x)` would of course be decisive.

It also does not say that Koukoulopoulos's inverse theorem is weak or tautological for general multiplicative functions. Its force is precisely that, for a general `f`, small global sums constrain previously unknown prime behavior and, under stronger hypotheses, tie logarithmic phase changes to nearby zeros. The negative result is only about the canonical RH specialization `f=mu`, where the prime values and the reciprocal divisor orientation are already known exactly.

The twist control `mu(n)n^(i gamma)` prevents a false escape through the Kronecker direction: the inverse phase `-p^(i gamma)` is inserted exactly by the twist, while the nontrivial zeta zeros remain poles of the shifted reciprocal series. No new localization of their real parts appears.

Nor is the result a generic Beurling or Helson impossibility theorem. It identifies a narrower design failure: an inverse theorem whose arithmetic output is a prime phase and whose spectral transition is indexed by **zeros of the same Dirichlet series** does not, without an additional pole-sensitive/global input, turn the Möbius reciprocal into an RH mechanism.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

Koukoulopoulos--Soundararajan (2020) already formulate the inverse problem for multiplicative functions with small partial sums and explicitly use `mu(n)n^(i gamma)` as the motivating phase model. Koukoulopoulos (2026) sharpens this into logarithmic prime-scale regimes and, under a stronger hypothesis, relates the transition parameters to zeros of the associated Dirichlet series near `s=1`.

`PL-137` and `PL-138` audit the forward pretentious direction: bounded-frequency distances, mean-value decay, and transfer of cancellation from a comparator. `PL-139` and `PL-140` audit what information is or is not recovered by power-enriched phase observables. The present finding closes the complementary **inverse** escape: starting from small Möbius sums and asking inverse pretentious theory to manufacture the missing RH structure only returns the canonical Möbius phase, while its zero channel has the wrong divisor orientation for the nontrivial zeta zeros.

A targeted search for the current inverse theorem, its D=1 ancestry, Möbius twists, and the reciprocal `1/zeta` specialization found the underlying ingredients as established prior art but no separate theorem claiming that this specialization itself yields critical-line localization. That absence is not used as evidence of novelty. The durable contribution here is the exact line-specific obstruction and the separation of where RH information actually enters.

## Consequence for `prime_lattice`

Do not pursue the chain “small Möbius sums -> inverse pretentious prime-phase transition -> critical-line localization” as if the inverse theorem supplied a new zero mechanism. For the Möbius point, the phase output is already exact and the theorem's nearby-zero channel is pointed at zeros of `1/zeta`, not at the nontrivial zeros of `zeta`.

A viable continuation would need one of two genuinely additional ingredients: a pole-sensitive inverse theory for the reciprocal Dirichlet series that does more than restate analytic continuation, or a different canonical prime-lattice observable whose own zeros (rather than reciprocal poles) encode the Riemann divisor and whose localization is forced by independent global structure. Without such an ingredient, current inverse small-sum rigidity is a prior-art redirect, not a new RH mechanism.
