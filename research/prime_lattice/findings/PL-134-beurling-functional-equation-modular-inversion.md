# PL-134 — Beurling functional equations are equivalent to global modular inversion data, not bare exponent-lattice geometry

## Claim

Hilberdink and Lapidus give a precise converse-style characterization of generalized functional equations for Beurling zeta functions. Under their Mellin-decay and meromorphic-continuation hypotheses, a completed relation of the form

`G_1(1-s) zeta_1(1-s) = G_2(s) zeta_2(s)`

is equivalent to a global reciprocal identity for the corresponding smoothed generalized-integer sums

`F_1(x) = x^(-1) F_2(1/x) + H(x)`,

where

`F_r(x) = sum_(n in N_r) g_r(n x)`

and `H(x)` is a finite residual sum determined by the poles. In exponent coordinates for a Beurling system with norm map

`nu_r(alpha) = exp(<alpha, lambda^(r)>),    lambda_j^(r)=log q_j^(r)`,

this becomes a relation between the **full weighted lattice sums**

`F_r(x) = sum_alpha g_r(x exp(<alpha,lambda^(r)>))`.

Thus a zeta-type reflection law is not generated by the free commutative exponent cone, its local Euler factors, or the prime-coordinate torus alone. It is equivalent, in this broad generalized-prime setting, to an additional global inversion/modular relation coupling all lattice points at reciprocal scales. The theorem even permits `zeta_1` and `zeta_2` to arise from two different generalized-prime systems, so the reflection can be duality data between two normed lattices rather than an intrinsic symmetry of one bare prime lattice.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`.

The equivalence theorem is established prior art. The Mathia-specific consequence is the adversarial interpretation for `prime_lattice`: after `PL-133` showed that a reflection axis can be imposed formally without selecting the Riemann center, Hilberdink--Lapidus identifies what a genuine analytic functional equation requires in a generalized-prime control class — a global reciprocal summation law. This sharpens the surviving target from “find a lattice reflection” to “derive the arithmetic global inversion/self-duality that distinguishes the rational-prime norm map.”

## The Mellin identity starts only in the honest convergence strip

Let `P` be a Beurling prime system, `N` its generalized integers, and let `g:(0,infinity)->C` satisfy the decay assumptions used by Hilberdink--Lapidus. Define

`F(x)=sum_(n in N) g(n x)`

and

`G(s)=integral_0^infinity x^(s-1) g(x) dx`.

Their Proposition 3.1 proves, under the stated hypotheses, that for `1<Re(s)<beta`,

`G(s) zeta_P(s) = integral_0^infinity x^(s-1) F(x) dx`.

The proof explicitly uses absolute convergence to interchange the generalized-integer sum and the Mellin integral. Meromorphic continuation then comes from the asymptotic expansion of `F(x)` near `x=0`, not from formally continuing the Euler product. This distinction is essential for the research mandate: the theorem crosses the `Re(s)=1` barrier only after additional global information about the smoothed integer sum has been supplied.

Primary source:

- Titus W. Hilberdink, Michel L. Lapidus, “Beurling Zeta Functions, Generalised Primes, and Fractal Membranes,” *Acta Applicandae Mathematicae* **94**(1) (2006), 21–48. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10440-006-9063-0. arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410270. Proposition 3.1 gives the Mellin continuation mechanism; Section 3.2 and Theorem 3.2 give the functional-equation/modular-inversion equivalence.

## The functional equation is equivalent to reciprocal global summation

Hilberdink--Lapidus consider two Mellin transforms `Phi_r` and prove in Theorem 3.2, under mild decay conditions and a finite-poles-per-strip hypothesis, the equivalence between:

`Phi_1(1-s)=Phi_2(s)`

with the required meromorphic continuations and vertical decay, and

`F_1(x)=x^(-1)F_2(1/x)+H(x)`,

where `H` is a finite sum of terms `a_k x^(mu_k) (log x)^(nu_k)` arising from residues. For the Beurling specialization they take

`F_r(x)=sum_(n in N_r) g_r(n x)`

and obtain

`Phi_r(s)=G_r(s) zeta_r(s)`

in the original Mellin convergence strip. Consequently the generalized completed functional equation

`G_1(1-s) zeta_1(1-s)=G_2(s) zeta_2(s)`

is equivalent, under the theorem's hypotheses, to the reciprocal-scale relation for the `F_r`.

The authors explicitly note that the classical Riemann functional equation is equivalent to the Jacobi theta modular identity and present their result as a generalization of precisely that equivalence. Their addendum also points to an earlier related theorem of Bochner, with a more general residual term when infinitely many poles per strip are allowed. The conceptual point is therefore classical: **functional equation and global modular inversion are two faces of the same added structure**.

## Translation into the prime-exponent lattice

For a generalized-prime sequence `q_j` with unique formal exponent coordinates, write

`lambda_j=log q_j`,

`nu(alpha)=product_j q_j^(alpha_j)=exp(<alpha,lambda>)`.

Then the smoothing sum is

`F(x)=sum_(alpha in N_0^(N), finite support) g(x exp(<alpha,lambda>))`.

The free monoid structure determines which exponent vectors exist and the norm vector `lambda` assigns their logarithmic energies. But the modular identity compares the **entire sum over all exponent vectors** at `x` with another entire sum at the reciprocal scale `1/x`, plus the residual polar contribution. Nothing in the coordinatewise law

`alpha+beta <-> nu(alpha)nu(beta)`

or in the local Euler factors

`(1-exp(-s lambda_j))^(-1)`

forces such a reciprocal identity. Indeed, Hilberdink--Lapidus emphasize that analytic continuation of a generic Beurling zeta function is non-generic and that `Re(s)=1` can naturally be a boundary; the functional equation is an additional condition to be characterized, not a consequence of possessing an Euler product.

This is stronger than merely saying “the archimedean place matters.” It gives a precise falsifiable boundary: a proposed lattice mechanism that claims to derive the Riemann functional equation must, after suitable Mellin smoothing, produce the corresponding reciprocal global summation law (or an equivalent Poisson/Tate self-duality). If it only produces local prime factors, torus phases, or a formal reflection quotient, it has not supplied the analytic content of the functional equation.

## Two-system duality prevents an intrinsic-lattice interpretation

A particularly useful adversarial feature of the theorem is that `zeta_1` and `zeta_2` may belong to **different generalized-prime systems**. The functional equation can therefore be a correspondence

`(P_1,g_1) <-> (P_2,g_2)`

rather than a self-map of one prime lattice. Self-duality is the special case in which the two sides are identified by additional arithmetic structure.

For ordinary Riemann zeta, Tate's thesis supplies exactly such extra structure: additive Fourier transform on the adeles, the product formula, self-dual measures, and Poisson summation produce the completed involution `s <-> 1-s`. This is the mechanism already isolated in `PL-014`. Hilberdink--Lapidus provides a complementary Beurling-side converse: once one asks for a completed functional equation in a generalized multiplicative system, the hidden datum is a reciprocal global summation identity.

Thus `PL-014` and the present finding meet from opposite directions:

- Tate constructs the modular/Fourier self-duality for the rational arithmetic system;
- Hilberdink--Lapidus characterizes a broad class in which such a functional equation is equivalent to having global inversion data in the first place.

## Relation to the Beurling flexibility control

`PL-015` showed that the abstract exponent cone, square-root-scale generalized-integer regularity, and arbitrarily long finite agreement with the rational primes do not determine RH-type zero geometry. The present result identifies one important structure that those controls need not preserve: a completed modular inversion law.

This does **not** negate the Beurling control. It explains why a candidate capable of deriving the exact Riemann functional equation may escape that control: it uses global information absent from the bare lattice/comparable counting data. Conversely, a proposal that remains valid for arbitrary Beurling norm assignments but never derives a modular relation cannot claim that its reflection geometry is the Riemann functional equation in disguise.

`PL-126` gives a different rigidity statement. Hamburger converse theory says that, inside the ordinary integer Dirichlet-series finite-order class, imposing the exact Riemann functional equation collapses certain deformations back to `zeta` itself. Hilberdink--Lapidus is broader and weaker in a useful way: it does not identify the Riemann zeta function, but shows what extra **global summation structure** a generalized-prime functional equation encodes. The two results therefore delimit different stages: modular inversion supplies/refines the global reflection law, while Hamburger rigidity can identify the distinguished ordinary Dirichlet series once the Riemann completion is fixed.

## Adversarial limits

Several stronger claims would be invalid.

First, the theorem does not prove that no exponent-lattice construction could ever derive a modular relation. A special norm map together with additional arithmetic operations could do so. The negative statement is narrower: **the free multiplicative lattice/Euler-product structure does not itself contain the required reciprocal relation**; a successful mechanism must exhibit the extra global datum explicitly.

Second, the theorem does not show that every Beurling system admits a useful functional equation. Quite the opposite: the paper treats continuation and completion as restrictive phenomena, and the equivalence requires explicit analytic hypotheses.

Third, the theorem does not produce a Beurling counterexample having the exact Riemann gamma factor, the exact self-dual Riemann functional equation, and off-critical zeros. It therefore cannot be used to argue that the full Riemann functional equation plus ordinary arithmetic fails as an RH mechanism. `PL-126` in fact shows strong converse rigidity in the ordinary Dirichlet class.

Fourth, modular inversion explains the **reflection symmetry** but not RH. Even for ordinary zeta, the functional equation only pairs zeros across `Re(s)=1/2`; it does not force them onto that line. An independent positivity, Hodge, unitarity-with-localization, or equivalent mechanism is still required.

Finally, the theorem's residual term `H(x)` is part of the exact statement. Dropping it would incorrectly erase the contribution of poles encountered when shifting Mellin contours. In the Riemann/theta case these residual terms encode familiar polar/constant pieces.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

No novelty is claimed for the Mellin method, the theta-modular interpretation of the Riemann functional equation, generalized primes, or Hilberdink--Lapidus Theorem 3.2. The paper's abstract already advertises a characterization of generalized functional equations, and Section 3.2 explicitly states the modular-equivalence objective.

The durable Mathia contribution is the **matched-control consequence for the current prime-lattice program**. `PL-133` had isolated that reflection-axis unitarity is formally universal once one chooses an axis; the present literature theorem identifies the missing nonformal ingredient in a generalized-prime control class as reciprocal global summation. This is a prior-art redirect, not a new theorem.

The closest existing `prime_lattice` results do not subsume this exact statement. `PL-014` gives the positive adelic/Tate construction for ordinary zeta; `PL-015` gives Beurling zero-flexibility controls; `PL-126` gives Hamburger uniqueness after the Riemann functional equation is imposed; and `PL-133` rules out reflection-axis unitarity as an axis selector. The present finding adds the generalized-prime **functional-equation iff modular-inversion bridge** linking those four observations.

## Audit test and consequence for the line

For any future claim that prime-exponent geometry intrinsically generates the completed Riemann reflection, perform the following audit. Identify the smoothed full-lattice sum `F(x)` whose Mellin transform is the proposed completion in its honest convergence strip. Then ask whether the construction proves a reciprocal relation of the form

`F(x)=x^(-1)F^dual(1/x)+residual terms`

with enough hypotheses to justify meromorphic continuation and contour movement. If not, the proposal has only repackaged the Euler product or imposed a reflection algebraically; it has not derived the global analytic mechanism.

The surviving RH target is consequently narrower. The bare exponent lattice is universal multiplicative scaffolding. What can still distinguish ordinary zeta is a **global self-dual norm/Fourier structure** that produces the modular relation and selects `s <-> 1-s`, followed by a separate mechanism forcing the already symmetric zero divisor onto the self-dual axis. Functional-equation geometry without that global summation bridge should no longer be treated as a candidate explanation.
