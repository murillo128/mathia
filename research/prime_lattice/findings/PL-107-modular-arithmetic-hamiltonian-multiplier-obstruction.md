# PL-107 — The proposed modular-arithmetic prime Hamiltonian is a translation-invariant Fourier multiplier before its unspecified boundary/renormalization step

## Claim

A July 2026 working paper by Payam Danesh proposes a Hilbert–Pólya program in logarithmic space with

```text
P = -i d/du,
H_0 = 1/4 + P^2,
T_p = exp(-i (log p) P),
```

and, for a regulator `epsilon>1/2`, the prime interaction

```text
V_P^(epsilon)
 = - sum_p sum_(k>=1)
     (log p) p^(-k(1/2+epsilon))
     (T_p^k + T_p^(-k)).
```

The paper then asks for a critical renormalized limit, an arithmetic self-adjoint domain, a prime-power trace formula, and a determinant identity with the completed zeta function in the quadratic spectral coordinate `lambda=s(1-s)`.

For the concrete absolutely convergent Hamiltonian displayed above, however, the prime term is not a confining or mixing interaction. Every `T_p` is a bounded function of the **same** momentum operator `P`, so

```text
[H_0,T_p]=0,
[V_P^(epsilon),P]=0,
[H_RH^(epsilon),T_p]=0
```

for every prime. On the natural translation representation `L^2(R,du)`, Fourier transform diagonalizes the whole family simultaneously. Writing

```text
a = 1/2 + epsilon > 1,
```

the exact multiplier is

```text
h_a(xi)
 = 1/4 + xi^2
   - 2 sum_p sum_(k>=1)
       (log p) p^(-ka) cos(k xi log p)

 = 1/4 + xi^2
   + 2 Re [ zeta'(a+i xi) / zeta(a+i xi) ].
```

The second equality is legitimate **only for `a>1`**, where the Euler/log-derivative series converges absolutely.

Consequently the regularized bulk Hamiltonian is unitarily equivalent to multiplication by the real function `h_a`. The prime interaction only modifies the free dispersion relation. It does not couple momentum modes, create standing waves, or produce the required discrete Riemann-zero point spectrum. Since `h_a` is a nonconstant real-analytic function and `h_a(xi)=xi^2+O_a(1)`, every level set `{xi:h_a(xi)=E}` has Lebesgue measure zero, hence

```text
sigma_p(H_RH^(epsilon)) = empty
```

on `L^2(R)`. Its spectrum is the continuous interval

```text
[min_R h_a, infinity).
```

Thus the paper's missing `epsilon -> 0+` renormalization and arithmetic boundary domain are not technical finishing steps around an already confining arithmetic Hamiltonian: **they must supply essentially all of the mechanism that could turn the translation-invariant multiplier into a discrete self-adjoint spectrum and determinant.** Any renormalization that preserves the full translation symmetry and remains a scalar functional calculus of `P` stays in the same multiplier class and cannot generate normalizable zero levels.

The critical-strip continuation also cannot be obtained by simply extending the displayed prime series. At `a<=1` that series is no longer absolutely convergent. Replacing it by

```text
2 Re zeta'(a+i xi)/zeta(a+i xi)
```

through meromorphic continuation imports the zero divisor explicitly through the poles of `zeta'/zeta`; it is not a derivation of those poles from the bare prime-translation operator.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE` for the concrete route

```text
logarithmic free Hamiltonian P^2
+ prime translations generated by the same P
+ absolutely convergent symmetric prime-power interaction
    -> confinement / point spectrum / independent zeta-zero localization.
```

The finding does **not** refute Danesh's stated conjecture, because the paper explicitly leaves the arithmetic domain, critical renormalization, point-spectrum realization, and determinant closure as conjectural requirements. It identifies where the actual proof burden lies: those additional structures must break or transcend the bulk multiplier degeneracy without inserting the zeta divisor by hand.

## 1. The prime interaction is exactly a function of one momentum operator

Take the natural logarithmic configuration space `R` with Lebesgue measure and the self-adjoint momentum

```text
P=-i d/du
```

on its standard domain. The spectral theorem gives

```text
F P F^(-1) = M_xi,
```

where `F` is Fourier transform and `M_xi` is multiplication by `xi`.

The translation used in the proposal is

```text
T_p = exp(-i (log p) P).
```

Therefore

```text
F T_p^k F^(-1)
 = exp(-i k (log p) xi),
```

and

```text
F (T_p^k+T_p^(-k)) F^(-1)
 = 2 cos(k xi log p).
```

For `a=1/2+epsilon>1`,

```text
sum_p sum_(k>=1) (log p) p^(-ka)
```

converges. Hence the double operator series converges in norm and can be Fourier transformed term by term. This yields

```text
F V_P^(epsilon) F^(-1)
 = M_(v_a),

v_a(xi)
 = -2 sum_p sum_(k>=1)
      (log p) p^(-ka) cos(k xi log p).
```

The free term is already diagonal:

```text
F H_0 F^(-1)
 = M_(1/4+xi^2).
```

Thus

```text
boxed:
F H_RH^(epsilon) F^(-1)
 = M_(h_a),

h_a(xi)=1/4+xi^2+v_a(xi).
```

This is stronger than merely observing that the prime shifts commute. The entire regularized Hamiltonian belongs to the abelian von Neumann algebra generated by `P`. There is no operator-valued interaction among prime directions after the logarithmic coordinate has compressed them into their frequencies `log p`.

In prime-exponent language, the construction uses

```text
log(p^k)=k log p
```

as a list of Fourier frequencies, but it does not retain the multidimensional incidence geometry of `v(n)`. All prime powers are sampled through one scalar spectral variable `xi`.

## 2. The exact multiplier is the classical logarithmic derivative of zeta

For `Re(s)>1`, the standard absolutely convergent Euler/log-derivative identity is

```text
-zeta'(s)/zeta(s)
 = sum_p sum_(k>=1) (log p) p^(-ks)
 = sum_n Lambda(n) n^(-s).
```

Put

```text
s=a+i xi,
a>1.
```

Taking real parts gives

```text
Re[-zeta'(a+i xi)/zeta(a+i xi)]
 = sum_p sum_(k>=1)
     (log p) p^(-ka) cos(k xi log p).
```

Therefore

```text
boxed:
v_a(xi)
 = 2 Re[zeta'(a+i xi)/zeta(a+i xi)].
```

This identity clarifies what the symmetric prime-power term does. It is a bounded almost-periodic correction to the dispersion relation on every fixed line `a>1`. It is not yet the explicit formula, because no spectral sum over zeta zeros has been derived from the Hamiltonian; it is simply the convergent prime-side logarithmic derivative evaluated along a vertical line where the Euler expansion is valid.

The convergence boundary matters. Nothing in this calculation permits the prime sum to be transported termwise to `a=1/2`. The continued meromorphic function `zeta'/zeta` exists there, but its poles are precisely the zeros one is trying to obtain spectrally.

## 3. The absolutely convergent bulk has no point spectrum on the line

For fixed `a>1`, the prime correction satisfies the uniform bound

```text
|v_a(xi)|
 <= 2 sum_p sum_(k>=1)(log p)p^(-ka)
 =: C_a < infinity.
```

Hence

```text
h_a(xi)=xi^2+O_a(1)
```

and in particular

```text
h_a(xi) -> +infinity
```

as `|xi|->infinity`.

The same absolute convergence with arbitrary powers of `k log p` shows that `v_a` is real analytic on `R` (equivalently, it is the restriction of the analytic log derivative on a vertical strip inside `Re(s)>1`). Thus `h_a` is real analytic and nonconstant.

For a multiplication operator `M_h` on `L^2(R)`, a number `E` is an eigenvalue exactly when the level set

```text
{xi : h(xi)=E}
```

has positive Lebesgue measure. A nonconstant real-analytic function has discrete zero sets for `h-E` unless it is identically `E`; therefore every such level set here has measure zero. It follows that

```text
boxed:
sigma_p(H_RH^(epsilon))=empty.
```

Since `h_a` is continuous, tends to `+infinity` at both ends, and `R` is connected, its range is

```text
[min_R h_a, infinity),
```

which is the spectrum of the multiplication operator.

The conclusion is independent of the detailed prime arithmetic. If one replaces the prime frequencies and amplitudes by any absolutely summable family

```text
sum_j c_j (exp(-i ell_j P)+exp(+i ell_j P)),
```

the result is again a scalar Fourier multiplier. Prime specificity changes the dispersion function, but not the spectral type needed for a Hilbert–Pólya point-spectrum construction.

## 4. The regularized interaction does not create standing-wave selection

The paper motivates the final construction through standing waves in logarithmic arithmetic space, with phases indexed by `k log p`. The displayed regularized bulk operator by itself cannot perform that selection.

For every real translation parameter `t`, define

```text
U_t=exp(-itP).
```

Then

```text
[H_RH^(epsilon),U_t]=0
```

for **all** real `t`, not merely for `t=log p`. Thus the regularized operator has a larger continuous translation symmetry than the arithmetic semigroup it was intended to encode.

In position space the prime term is a sum of finite translations, but because the coefficients are constant and both directions are included, it is translation invariant. In momentum space this is exactly the statement that it only alters the scalar dispersion `h_a(xi)`.

A standing-wave or bound-state mechanism needs a boundary, defect, quotient, nonconstant coefficient, compression, or another structure that couples momentum channels or restricts the domain. The paper recognizes this by introducing a later conjectural arithmetic boundary functional `B_xi[psi]=0`. The present calculation shows that this boundary is mathematically load-bearing rather than auxiliary.

## 5. Translation-preserving renormalization cannot repair the spectral type

The paper correctly notes that the limit cannot be obtained by simply setting `epsilon=0` in the prime series and writes schematically

```text
H_RH = lim_(epsilon->0+) R_epsilon(H_RH^(epsilon)),
```

with a renormalization intended to subtract a divergent smooth component while preserving arithmetic oscillation.

There is a sharp design boundary here. If `R_epsilon` consists only of scalar subtraction, scalar rescaling, or another functional operation that keeps the operator inside the abelian algebra generated by `P`, then every renormalized operator remains a Fourier multiplier:

```text
R_epsilon(H_RH^(epsilon)) = F^(-1) M_(r_epsilon(h_a)) F
```

for the relevant scalar functions. Any strong-resolvent limit obtained by pointwise multiplier convergence is again decomposable in the same momentum representation. It therefore does not acquire a discrete Riemann-zero point spectrum merely from removing the divergence.

This does not rule out a genuinely nontranslation-invariant renormalization or a self-adjoint extension with an arithmetic boundary. It says exactly what such a repair must do: **leave the scalar-multiplier category.**

That requirement creates a consistency problem that must be solved, not assumed. If the boundary domain is not invariant under the translations `T_p`, then the prime translations are no longer symmetries of the final self-adjoint operator on its physical domain. If it is invariant under the whole continuous translation group generated by `P`, the multiplier obstruction remains. A viable boundary must therefore explain a precise arithmetic symmetry breaking/compression and prove self-adjointness, rather than merely append a quantization condition.

## 6. Meromorphic continuation of the multiplier would import the divisor

The exact identification

```text
v_a(xi)=2 Re zeta'(a+i xi)/zeta(a+i xi)
```

is established from the prime-power sum only for `a>1`.

One could formally define a new object for `a<=1` by using the meromorphic continuation of `zeta'/zeta`. That object already contains the zeta zeros as poles:

```text
zeta(rho)=0
    -> zeta'(s)/zeta(s) has a pole at s=rho
```

with the usual multiplicity statement. On the critical line, the continued multiplier is therefore singular at known critical zeros rather than producing those zeros as eigenvalues of an independently defined self-adjoint operator.

This is analogous to several earlier `prime_lattice` obstructions: a spectral object is not explanatory merely because a continued zeta function has been inserted into its coefficient or determinant. The hard bridge is to construct the continuation/determinant from an operator whose definition does not already contain the desired divisor.

The paper itself does not make the invalid termwise-continuation step; it explicitly labels the critical limit as a renormalization problem. The point here is to prevent the unresolved renormalization from being treated as if the convergent prime Hamiltonians already carried the needed discrete spectrum.

## 7. The quadratic spectral coordinate is an exact RH reformulation, not the missing mechanism

For a nonreal zero

```text
rho=sigma+it,
```

the proposed quadratic energy is

```text
E_rho=rho(1-rho).
```

Its imaginary part is

```text
Im E_rho = t(1-2 sigma).
```

Hence, for `t != 0`,

```text
E_rho real <=> sigma=1/2.
```

This is exact and useful as a target. Likewise, if one constructs a self-adjoint operator `H` with a determinant identity

```text
det_reg(H-s(1-s)) = C xi(s),
```

where the zeros of the determinant correspond to point spectrum, then self-adjointness would force all `rho(1-rho)` to be real and RH follows.

But the determinant identity is precisely the missing theorem. The free choice

```text
H_0=1/4+P^2
```

already parametrizes real free energies as `1/4+t^2`, i.e. the image of the critical line under `s(1-s)`. It does not force the zeta divisor to map into that free spectrum. The regularized prime multiplier above also has no point spectrum. Therefore the conditional theorem does not reduce the remaining problem to a routine self-adjointness check: it requires the construction of the very determinant/point-spectrum bridge whose existence would encode RH.

## 8. Relation to existing Prime-Lattice obstructions

This current-literature audit fits several earlier findings but is not identical to them.

- `PL-004` already classifies `log p` one-particle energies and zeta partition functions as classical Riemann-gas prior art. The Danesh model goes beyond a partition function by proposing a symmetric prime-translation operator and determinant target.
- `PL-023` shows that normal operators commuting with all **unilateral prime shifts on the Hardy/positive exponent lattice** collapse to scalars. The present representation is different: the `T_p` are unitary translations generated by a single continuous momentum, whose commutant contains all Fourier multipliers. The failure is therefore not scalar collapse but continuous multiplier spectrum.
- `PL-025` rules out exact **log-energy covariance** under reversible prime shifts by forcing full real spectrum. Here the relation is instead exact commutation with translations; again the obstruction is different.
- `PL-033`, `PL-043`, and related findings emphasize that a genuine trace/scattering/canonical-system realization can contain zeta zeros, but the missing issue is a non-universal positivity or localization theorem. The Danesh determinant conjecture has the same logical burden once the concrete bulk is diagonalized.
- `PL-106` shows that a different 2026 quantum construction is a programmable Dirichlet-series readout. The present finding identifies a complementary failure mode: the prime data are not merely programmable coefficients; rather, the proposed symmetric prime interaction is **too translation invariant to create bound states**.

Thus this is a reusable new audit rule for the line:

```text
prime powers inserted only as constant-coefficient translations
all generated by one logarithmic momentum
    -> Fourier multiplier
    -> arithmetic dispersion but no discrete spectral selector.
```

## 10. Adversarial boundaries

### The paper does not claim the fixed-`epsilon` bulk already has the final point spectrum

Correct. It explicitly introduces an arithmetic boundary domain and renormalized critical limit later. This finding therefore does not accuse the paper of claiming a theorem it does not state. It establishes that those later conjectural ingredients are the entire possible escape from the exact bulk obstruction.

### A different configuration space could change the spectral type

Yes. If `X` is compact, has a boundary, is a quotient, or carries a non-translation-invariant measure/domain, `P` may have discrete spectrum or different self-adjoint extensions. But then the choice of `X`, measure, boundary, and compatibility of every `T_p` with the domain become the substantive arithmetic construction. The formulas `P=-i d/du` and `T_p=exp(-i log p P)` alone no longer prove that this structure is canonical or that its determinant is xi.

On a compact circle, there is an additional elementary tension: a continuous translation group has discrete integer momentum but the free `P^2` counting law is of one-dimensional Weyl type rather than the Riemann-von Mangoldt `T log T` law in the zero-height variable. A nontrivial arithmetic domain/geometry would again have to supply the missing growth.

### Continuous spectrum could encode zeros as resonances

Yes. The no-point-spectrum result rules out the paper's stated requirement that the quadratic zero energies be genuine normalizable point-spectrum levels of the displayed bulk Hamiltonian. It does not rule out scattering poles, resonances, absorption spectra, or trace defects. Those are different mechanisms and require their own continuation and positivity analysis.

### A singular critical limit can change spectral type

In principle, yes. Singular limits can create new boundary conditions or spectral components. The claim is not that this is impossible. The claim is that it cannot be inferred from the absolutely convergent family, and a translation-preserving scalar multiplier renormalization cannot do it. The singular mechanism must be specified and proved.

### Could the prime frequencies themselves make a multiplier level set have positive measure?

No for every fixed `a>1`. The prime correction is real analytic and bounded, while the `xi^2` term makes `h_a` nonconstant and coercive. Arithmetic recurrence can create many local extrema, but not a positive-measure constant plateau of a nonconstant analytic function.

### Does the log-derivative identity already use analytic continuation?

No. The equality used in the derivation is restricted to `a>1`, exactly the paper's absolutely convergent regime. The finding explicitly refuses to transport the prime sum past that boundary. The critical-strip statement is only the negative observation that using continued `zeta'/zeta` as a replacement would already import the zero poles.

## Consequence for the research line

The new 2026 proposal does not open a surviving route based on the displayed constant-coefficient prime translations. It sharpens the design constraint:

```text
logarithmic coordinate
+ one self-adjoint momentum P
+ every prime action T_p = exp(-i log(p) P)
+ translation-invariant prime-power coefficients
    -> one scalar dispersion h(P)
    -> no independent point-spectrum mechanism.
```

A viable `prime_lattice` Hamiltonian must therefore introduce a genuinely arithmetic noncommuting/boundary/global structure **before** determinant closure becomes meaningful. The structure must be defined without the zeta zeros, survive the line's Beurling/Helson and universality controls, have a legitimate continuation mechanism across `Re(s)=1`, and prove rather than assume the point-spectrum/determinant bridge.

The most informative remaining question is not whether prime translations can be written into a Hamiltonian—they can—but what canonical arithmetic structure breaks their continuous multiplier degeneracy while still coupling all prime directions and forcing a zeta-specific positivity or localization law.
