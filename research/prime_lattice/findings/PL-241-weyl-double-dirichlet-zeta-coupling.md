# PL-241 — Quadratic double Dirichlet series already couple reciprocity families to zeta, but the Weyl completion does not localize the Riemann zero divisor

## Claim

The first natural escape left open by `PL-240` is already classical prior art: if one averages the support-dependent quadratic-character data over discriminants instead of freezing one finite Rédei matrix, quadratic reciprocity leads to a **perfect double Dirichlet series** whose correction factors include an explicit Riemann-zeta factor and whose analytic continuation is generated by a finite Weyl-type functional-equation group.

A concrete rank-two model is Blomer's double Dirichlet series. For `Re(s)` and `Re(w)` sufficiently large and real characters `psi, psi'` of conductor dividing `8`, let

`Z(s,w;psi,psi') = zeta_2(2s+2w-1) sum_(d odd) L_2(s,chi_d psi) psi'(d) / d^w`,

where `chi_d(n)=(d/n)` and the subscript `2` removes the Euler factor at `2`. Expanding the inner `L`-series gives

`Z = zeta_2(2s+2w-1) sum_(d,n odd) chi_d(n) psi(n) psi'(d) / (d^w n^s)`.

Quadratic reciprocity converts `chi_d(n)` into the reciprocal quadratic character in the other variable, so changing the order of summation produces a functional equation exchanging the two Dirichlet variables. Combining this with the ordinary functional equation of each quadratic `L(s,chi_d psi)` gives a group isomorphic to `D_12` and meromorphic continuation to `C^2`. Thus the proposed bridge

`source-forced mixed-prime reciprocity -> family averaging -> principal zeta coupling -> derived continuation`

exists in established multiple-Dirichlet-series theory.

The bridge does **not**, however, supply an RH selector. Put

`u = 2s+2w-1`.

One Weyl generator is

`beta(s,w)=(1-s, s+w-1/2)`.

Blomer's exact derivation notes that this map interchanges the correction-factor arguments

`u <-> 2w`

while leaving `s+2w` invariant. The longer symmetry roughly sending `(s,w)` to `(1-s,1-w)` sends

`u -> 2-u`.

Consequently the self-dual hyperplane of this affine zeta argument is `Re(u)=1`, whereas the Riemann functional equation for `zeta(u)` is centered at `Re(u)=1/2`. Equivalently, the pullback of the Riemann critical line is

`Re(s+w)=3/4`,

while the joint double-series critical locus `Re(s)=Re(w)=1/2` has `Re(u)=1`.

There is an even sharper divisor-level control. The defining formula contains `zeta_2(u)`, which has a pole at `u=1`, so at `(s,w)=(1/2,1/2)` the visible scalar factor is singular. Yet the meromorphically continued `Z` has possible polar lines only at

`s=1`, `w=1`, `s+w=3/2`.

Hence `Z` is holomorphic at `(1/2,1/2)`: the pole of the displayed zeta correction does **not** survive as a pole of the global two-variable object. The continuation reorganizes the scalar factors with the quadratic family rather than preserving `zeta_2(u)` as an independent meromorphic divisor.

Therefore a principal-zeta factor occurring in a reciprocity-coupled family average is not by itself a zeta spectral mechanism. The exact classical construction gives the missing analytic coupling from `PL-240`, but its Weyl symmetry permutes the zeta correction among other affine factors and does not turn the nontrivial zeros of `zeta` into an invariant spectrum, resonance set, or positivity boundary.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + NEGATIVE-BOUNDARY + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`. Decisive against treating quadratic-reciprocity family averaging plus an explicit scalar zeta correction as a new or sufficient RH-localization mechanism. It does not rule out a further residue, quotient, operator realization, or positivity theorem that proves a genuine transfer from the multiple-series structure to the Riemann zero divisor.

## Prime-lattice meaning of the double series

For a fixed discriminant `d`, the values `chi_d(n)` are not generic phases attached independently to prime coordinates. They come from the quadratic residue relation between the actual prime divisors of `d` and those of `n`. In exponent coordinates, the double sum therefore couples two arithmetic supports through a reciprocity law before any state, averaging measure, or spectralization is chosen.

This is precisely the kind of source-forced relation demanded by the current `prime_lattice` frontier. A finite Rédei matrix in `PL-240` packages pairwise quadratic-residue incidences among the primes ramified in one quadratic field. The double Dirichlet series instead lets the discriminant/support vary and forms an analytic generating object whose coefficients are themselves quadratic `L`-functions. It is therefore a genuine global completion of the same broad mixed-prime arithmetic source, not a fixed congruence coloring or a generic label kernel.

The key point is that the completion is already family-valued. Varying `d` varies the quadratic character and conductor, and the second Dirichlet variable records that family. The principal `zeta_2(2s+2w-1)` factor is one of the correction factors needed to make the resulting double series have the desired functional equations; it is not evidence that the two-variable object has collapsed back to the principal Riemann channel.

## Exact reciprocity and functional-equation mechanism

Initially in the domain of absolute convergence,

`Z(s,w;psi,psi') = zeta_2(u) sum_(d,n odd) chi_d(n) psi(n) psi'(d) d^(-w)n^(-s)`.

Blomer rewrites the quadratic symbol using the reciprocal character `tilde(chi)_n(d)` and obtains

`Z(s,w;psi,psi') = zeta_2(u) sum_(n odd) L_2(w,tilde(chi)_n psi') psi(n) / n^s`.

This is the exact arithmetic source of the variable-exchange functional equation: quadratic reciprocity makes the two summation directions dual. It is not obtained by analytically continuing an Euler product term-by-term.

A second representation separates `d=d_0 d_1^2`, with `d_0` square-free, and gives in its valid region

`Z = zeta_2(u) sum_(d_0 odd, squarefree) [L_2(s,chi_(d_0)psi) psi'(d_0) zeta_2(2w)] / [d_0^w L_2(s+2w,chi_(d_0)psi)]`.

This identity makes the correction structure explicit. The transformation

`beta(s,w)=(1-s,s+w-1/2)`

leaves `s+2w` fixed and interchanges `u=2s+2w-1` with `2w`. The corresponding functional equation is vector-valued because the mod-`8` character components mix, but the important point for this line is already visible at the scalar-factor level: there is no Weyl-invariant distinguished copy of the argument `u` carrying the Riemann zero divisor.

The two basic functional equations generate `D_12`. By iterating them from the initial convergence region, Blomer proves that

`(s-1)(w-1)(s+w-3/2) Z(s,w;psi,psi')`

extends holomorphically to `C^2`. This continuation is therefore a theorem of the coupled double-series system. It is not an inference from the formal presence of `zeta_2(u)` in the initial definition.

## Why the visible zeta factor is not a zero selector

There are three separate obstructions to reading the scalar correction as an RH mechanism.

First, **the affine argument is not preserved by the Weyl group**. Under `beta`, `u` becomes `2w`; the same functional-equation orbit contains several correction-factor directions. A zero of one displayed scalar factor is therefore not intrinsically a zero of a Weyl-invariant determinant.

Second, **the self-duality is misaligned with the Riemann critical line**. Under the combined reflection `(s,w)->(1-s,1-w)`,

`u -> 2-u`.

The fixed real hyperplane is `Re(u)=1`. By contrast, the completed Riemann zeta function pairs `u` with `1-u` and has self-dual line `Re(u)=1/2`. The linear pullback of that Riemann line, `Re(s+w)=3/4`, is not the central double-series locus `Re(s)=Re(w)=1/2`.

Third, **even the pole divisor of the explicit zeta factor is not inherited literally**. At the central point `(1/2,1/2)`, `u=1`, so `zeta_2(u)` has its pole. But the continued double series is holomorphic there because its only possible polar hyperplanes are `s=1`, `w=1`, and `s+w=3/2`. This is an exact cancellation/repackaging control: the displayed zeta correction cannot be treated as a global independent factor with a nonvanishing holomorphic cofactor.

These facts do not establish that every nontrivial zero of `zeta_2(u)` is canceled in every specialization, and no such claim is made. They establish the narrower point needed here: the initial product notation does not transport the Riemann zero divisor into the global analytic divisor of `Z` in a way from which RH localization could be read off.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The construction is classical multiple-Dirichlet-series theory, not a new prime-lattice object. Diaconu--Goldfeld--Hoffstein developed the several-complex-variable framework for perfect multiple Dirichlet series associated with quadratic `L`-families, emphasizing correction factors, hidden functional equations obtained by changing the order of summation, meromorphic continuation, polar divisors, and applications to moments of zeta and quadratic `L`-functions. Their introduction traces the basic quadratic double-series continuation back to Siegel and earlier automorphic work.

Blomer gives the exact rank-two formula used here, the two functional equations, the `D_12` group, the correction-factor representation, and the full meromorphic continuation with the three possible polar hyperplanes above. The argument explicitly states that the transformation `beta` interchanges `2s+2w-1` and `2w`, making the non-invariance of the displayed zeta argument part of the classical formula rather than a speculative interpretation.

Chinta--Gunnells show that the phenomenon is much broader: multiple Dirichlet series whose coefficients involve quadratic residue symbols admit analytic continuation and Weyl-group functional equations in higher rank. Thus replacing the rank-two family by a larger quadratic-residue tensor or Weyl system is also established prior art at the level of construction and continuation.

The local derived observation retained for this line is the RH-facing boundary: in the concrete Riemann-coupled factor `zeta_2(2s+2w-1)`, the Weyl self-duality and the Riemann critical symmetry are not aligned, and the scalar pole at the joint critical point is absent from the continued double-series divisor. No novelty is claimed for the underlying multiple Dirichlet series, their functional equations, or their analytic continuation.

## Adversarial controls and failure modes

**This does not say family averaging is arithmetically empty.** Multiple Dirichlet series are powerful precisely because residues and specializations recover moments and other global information about `L`-families. The negative conclusion is only about using the visible scalar zeta correction as an RH-localizing spectrum or divisor without an additional theorem.

**The continuation is not being attributed to the exponent lattice alone.** It uses quadratic reciprocity, one-variable quadratic-`L` functional equations, correction factors, and several-complex-variable continuation. These are extra arithmetic/analytic structures, which is why the construction passes the source-specificity control while simultaneously becoming prior art.

**The absence of a zero-selector is not a proof that no specialization can isolate one.** A residue, quotient, period, trace, or operator-valued lift might conceivably produce a Riemann-specific object. Such a proposal must show that the Riemann zero divisor survives the operation invariantly and that the resulting self-duality/positivity actually centers it at `Re=1/2`; this finding does not rule that out in advance.

**A larger Weyl group is not automatically an escape.** Quadratic-residue Weyl-group multiple Dirichlet series already exist in arbitrary finite root-system ranks. Adding more variables or more quadratic-symbol incidences without a new principal-channel invariant is therefore a change of size, not yet a new RH mechanism.

## Consequences for `prime_lattice`

`PL-240` left open the possibility that averaging the support-dependent quadratic reciprocity data could couple a family-valued invariant back to the principal zeta channel. `PL-241` answers the first version of that question positively but classically: **yes, there is a canonical reciprocity-driven family average with an explicit zeta correction and derived multivariable continuation.**

The result therefore advances the boundary rather than killing mixed-prime arithmetic altogether. Future work should not spend further passes merely constructing quadratic-character family generating series or pointing to the occurrence of a zeta factor in their correction terms. Those are established controls.

A surviving mechanism must prove a stronger kind of principal-channel coupling: the Riemann zero divisor must be an invariant of the global construction rather than one scalar factor in a Weyl orbit; its `1/2` symmetry must align with the construction's genuine self-dual locus; and some positivity, self-adjointness, trace identity, or target-relative argument must convert that invariant into zero localization rather than merely analytic continuation or moment information.
