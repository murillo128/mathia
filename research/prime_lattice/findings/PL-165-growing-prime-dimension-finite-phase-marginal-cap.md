# PL-165 — Growing prime dimension cannot turn fixed phase pinning into robust positive-harmonic amplification

## Claim

The increasing-dimensional escape left open by `PL-164` cannot be justified from phase information on only a **fixed finite set of prime coordinates**. Haar marginalization onto the controlled coordinates reduces every normalized positive full-strip detector, regardless of its total active prime dimension, to a detector covered by the fixed-prime cap of `PL-164`.

Let `P` be any finite set of active primes and let `Q subseteq P` be fixed. Consider

`F(theta)=1+2 Re sum_(n in E) a_n exp(i <v_P(n),theta>)`,

where `E` is a finite subset of the positive semigroup generated by `P`, and assume `F>=0` on `T^P` with Haar mean `1`. Let `H_F(x,theta)` be the symmetric full-critical-strip harmonic extension used in `PL-164`:

`H_F(x,theta)`
` = 1 + 2 Re sum_(n in E) a_n`
`       [cosh(x log n)/cosh((log n)/2)]`
`       exp(i <v_P(n),theta>)`,

for `|x|<=1/2`.

Assume RH fails but the rightmost zero abscissa satisfies

`1/2 < Theta < 1`,

and put `B=Theta-1/2`, `delta=1-Theta>0`. For fixed `theta_Q in T^Q`, average over all uncontrolled prime phases:

`F_Q(theta_Q)`
` = integral_(T^(P\Q)) F(theta_Q,phi) dphi`.

Then `F_Q>=0`, has mean `1`, and contains only modes supported on the `Q`-smooth semigroup. Moreover harmonic extension commutes with the marginal:

`integral_(T^(P\Q)) H_F(x,(theta_Q,phi)) dphi`
` = H_(F_Q)(x,theta_Q)`.

Applying `PL-164` to the fixed prime set `Q` gives, uniformly in the ambient active set `P`,

`0 <= H_(F_Q)(x,theta_Q) <= C_(Q,Theta)`

for `|x|<=B`, where

`C_(Q,Theta)`
` = 1 + 4 [ product_(q in Q) (1-q^(-delta))^(-1) - 1 ]`.

Consequently

`inf_(phi in T^(P\Q)) H_F(x,(theta_Q,phi)) <= C_(Q,Theta)`,

and, because `H_F>=0`, for every `M>0`,

`m_(P\Q){phi : H_F(x,(theta_Q,phi)) >= M}`
` <= C_(Q,Theta)/M`.

Thus if a sequence of growing-dimensional normalized positive full-strip detectors has response `M_j -> infinity` while only the phases in one fixed `Q` are controlled, the uncontrolled phase vector must lie in fiberwise Haar-exceptional sets whose measures are `O_(Q,Theta)(M_j^(-1))`.

This does **not** prove that the actual zeta-zero phases avoid those exceptional sets. It identifies the exact missing input. Ambient dimension growth by itself does not upgrade the one-prime or fixed-finite-prime phase pinning available from `PL-161` into a robust amplification mechanism. To exploit growing dimension one must also obtain arithmetic or dynamical control of a growing set of prime phases, or otherwise prove that the relevant zero phases systematically hit the shrinking exceptional fibers.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + DIMENSION-GROWTH-REDIRECT`. The argument is finite-dimensional Haar conditional expectation plus the exact fixed-prime cap of `PL-164`; no external novelty claim is made.

## Haar marginalization removes every uncontrolled prime mode

Write `theta=(theta_Q,phi)` according to the decomposition `P=Q union (P\Q)`. For a Fourier mode indexed by an integer `n` in the `P`-smooth semigroup,

`exp(i <v_P(n),theta>)`
` = exp(i <v_Q(n),theta_Q>)`
`   exp(i <v_(P\Q)(n),phi>)`.

Haar integration in `phi` annihilates the mode unless

`v_p(n)=0` for every `p in P\Q`.

Hence the marginal is exactly

`F_Q(theta_Q)`
` = 1 + 2 Re sum_(n in E, supp(v(n)) subseteq Q)`
`       a_n exp(i <v_Q(n),theta_Q>)`.

It remains nonnegative because it is an average of the nonnegative function `F`, and its constant coefficient remains `1`. Therefore it lies precisely in the fixed-`Q` detector class of `PL-164`.

The full-strip extension is diagonal on the same Fourier characters: the `n`th coefficient is multiplied by

`cosh(x log n)/cosh((log n)/2)`.

Since this scalar does not depend on the torus variables, taking the Haar marginal before or after harmonic extension gives the same result. No limiting argument is involved because every detector is a finite trigonometric polynomial.

## The fixed-coordinate marginal remains uniformly capped when the ambient dimension grows

The cap in `PL-164` depends only on the prime support of the detector to which it is applied. For the marginal, that support is contained in the fixed set `Q`, irrespective of how many additional primes occur in `P`. Thus for `|x|<=Theta-1/2`,

`H_(F_Q)(x,theta_Q)`
` <= 1 + 4 sum_(n in N_Q, n>1) n^(-delta)`
` = 1 + 4 [ product_(q in Q) (1-q^(-delta))^(-1) - 1 ]`.

The right-hand side is finite because `Q` is finite and `delta>0`.

Since `H_F` is nonnegative throughout the strip by the orbitwise Poisson argument of `PL-164`, its fiber average is a genuine positive average, not a cancellation. Therefore, for each fixed controlled phase `theta_Q`, a value much larger than `C_(Q,Theta)` can occur only on a small subset of the remaining torus. Markov's inequality gives the quantitative statement

`m_(P\Q){H_F(x,(theta_Q,phi)) >= M}`
` <= H_(F_Q)(x,theta_Q)/M`
` <= C_(Q,Theta)/M`.

This estimate is dimension-free: the measure is normalized Haar measure on whatever finite torus `T^(P\Q)` the detector happens to use.

The conclusion is intentionally weaker than a pointwise upper bound on `H_F`. Growing dimensions can create arbitrarily sharp positive peaks in the uncontrolled coordinates. What the marginal identity proves is that **fixed-coordinate phase pinning supplies no deterministic reason for a zeta zero to land on those peaks**. Any such mechanism must contain new information about the additional coordinates.

## Relation to PL-161 through PL-164

`PL-161` shows that a Pringsheim-type boundary mechanism can pin a phase on one prime axis along a near-frontier zero sequence. Taking `Q={p}` here shows that allowing the detector to use arbitrarily many additional prime axes does not convert that one-coordinate information into a robust large response: any response `M` is confined, conditional on the pinned `p`-phase, to an uncontrolled-phase set of Haar measure at most

`[1+4/(p^(1-Theta)-1)]/M`.

`PL-162` proves vertical genericity/equidistribution of the full zero population on every **fixed finite prime torus**. That does not fill the present gap. The exceptional sets above depend on the detector and may shrink as `M` grows, while the relevant zeros are additionally conditioned to approach the hypothetical rightmost frontier `beta -> Theta`. Fixed-dimensional equidistribution of the unconditioned zero population is not a theorem about such growing-dimensional, shrinking-target, frontier-conditioned events.

`PL-164` left growth of the active prime set as the first positive-detector escape after proving a uniform cap for fixed finite support. The present marginal argument separates that escape into two logically different requirements. Growing the **ambient detector dimension** is easy, but is inert relative to phase information already available on a fixed `Q`. What could still matter is growth of the **arithmetically controlled phase dimension**: a theorem simultaneously constraining `gamma log p (mod 2 pi)` for a set `Q=Q_j` whose size grows with the detector or target zero.

This distinction prevents a misleading repair in which one simply adds prime axes to the detector and attributes any resulting narrow high-dimensional peak to the phase pinning already established in one or finitely many coordinates.

## Adversarial audit

1. **There is no pointwise dimension-independent cap for the full detector.** The theorem controls its conditional Haar average and therefore the measure of high-response fibers. A specially chosen uncontrolled phase can still carry an arbitrarily large peak as the ambient dimension or degree grows.

2. **The actual zeta-zero orbit is not modeled as random.** Haar measure is used only to quantify the geometric size of the exceptional fiber. No independence, randomness, or genericity of near-frontier zero phases is assumed or inferred.

3. **A growing controlled set remains an escape.** If `Q=Q_j` itself grows, then

   `C_(Q_j,Theta)=1+4[product_(q in Q_j)(1-q^(-delta))^(-1)-1]`

   can grow without bound. The argument does not rule out simultaneous phase rigidity on increasingly many prime axes.

4. **The strict interior hypothesis `Theta<1` is essential.** At `Theta=1`, `delta=0` and even the fixed-prime geometric bound of `PL-164` diverges.

5. **Positivity on the full critical strip is load-bearing.** The exceptional-set estimate uses `H_F>=0`. Cancellation-sensitive or nonpositive observables are outside the theorem.

6. **Only the positive integer semigroup is covered.** Mixed-sign exponent vectors correspond to rational frequencies and are outside the `PL-164` class inherited by this argument.

7. **No continuation of an Euler product is used.** The finite product in `C_(Q,Theta)` is merely the convergent geometric sum over a finitely generated prime semigroup at exponent `delta>0`. The new step is exact finite-torus conditional expectation.

8. **The result is not an RH implication.** It is a route restriction: it says precisely what extra phase information a growing-dimensional positive detector would have to acquire before dimension growth becomes relevant to excluding a sparse off-line frontier.

A falsification would require failure of Haar mode elimination, failure of harmonic extension to commute with that finite Fourier projection, failure of the `PL-164` fixed-prime cap, or failure of positivity needed for Markov's inequality.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The analytical operation used here is classical conditional expectation on a compact abelian group: integrating over a coordinate subtorus projects a trigonometric polynomial onto the characters trivial on that subtorus, preserves positivity, and preserves its Haar mean. The quantitative cap is exactly the internal result `PL-164` applied after that projection.

A targeted literature audit around positive trigonometric polynomials, Haar marginals/conditional expectation on tori, Harnack/Poisson bounds on polydiscs, Bohr lifts, and positive Dirichlet polynomials found only the standard harmonic-analysis ingredients, not a reason to claim this prime-lattice specialization as a new theorem. The result is therefore recorded as an exact derived obstruction and research-route refinement, not as external novelty.

The closest Mathia prior art is `PL-161`--`PL-164`. None of those findings distinguishes ambient prime-dimension growth from growth of the number of phases actually controlled on a near-frontier zero sequence. That distinction, and the dimension-free conditional high-response bound above, are the durable content here.

## Consequence for the research line

The naive increasing-dimensional repair of `PL-164` is now too weakly specified. **Adding prime axes is not itself new arithmetic information.** With only one-prime or fixed-finite-prime phase pinning, every normalized positive full-strip detector has a uniformly bounded conditional mean on the uncontrolled torus, so unbounded amplification can occur only on shrinking exceptional fibers.

Within this detector family, the next nontrivial target is therefore a theorem that couples near-frontier zeros to increasingly many prime phases, or a shrinking-target result strong enough to show systematic intersection with the exceptional fibers. If no such coupling exists, the remaining escapes are the same structural ones already separated by `PL-164`: leave full-strip positivity, use mixed-sign/rational characters, or bring in arithmetic information beyond first Landau--Gonek moments.
