# PL-013 — Weil’s prime-power axis form already feeds a self-adjoint zeta spectral route, but convergence to `Xi` remains open

## Claim

The most canonical way in which the exponent lattice reaches the zeta zero divisor is already present in the classical explicit formula and in recent spectral-triple work of Connes–Consani–Moscovici.

For `Re(s)>1`,

```text
-zeta'(s)/zeta(s) = sum_{n>=1} Lambda(n)n^{-s}
                   = sum_p sum_{m>=1} (log p) p^{-m s}.
```

Under the Bohr lift this becomes

```text
sum_p sum_{m>=1} (log p) z_p^m.
```

Thus its monomial support is not the full exponent lattice: `Lambda(n)` is nonzero exactly on prime powers, whose exponent vectors are the coordinate-axis points

```text
v(p^m) = m e_p.
```

Weil’s completed explicit formula carries precisely this prime-power data, together with the archimedean and pole terms, into a distributional/quadratic-form identity involving the nontrivial zero divisor. In a compact multiplicative window `[lambda^-1,lambda]`, Connes–Consani–Moscovici write the semilocal Weil quadratic form as

```text
QW_lambda(f,f)
  = archimedean term
    + pole-evaluation term
    - sum_{1<n<=lambda^2} Lambda(n) <f,T(n)f>.
```

Consequently its non-archimedean input consists only of the finite set of axis-ray points `m e_p` with `p^m<=lambda^2`.

Recent work then uses finite-dimensional restrictions of this Weil form to construct self-adjoint rank-one perturbations of the logarithmic scaling operator. Under an explicit finite-truncation hypothesis (the smallest eigenvalue is simple and its eigenvector is even), the regularized determinant is an entire function whose zeros are all real and are exactly the spectrum of the self-adjoint operator. Numerically these spectra approximate the low Riemann zeros with very high precision, even from small prime cutoffs.

However, the limit statement needed for RH is **not proved**. The authors explicitly identify missing steps: in particular one must control the smallest-eigenvector structure of the semilocal Weil form and justify strongly enough that the prolate-wave model approximates that extremal eigenvector so that the corresponding determinants/zeros converge to the Riemann `Xi` function.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED` for the explicit-formula support, semilocal Weil form, finite-cutoff self-adjoint construction, and real-zero determinant theorem; `CONJECTURAL` for convergence of those spectra/determinants to the Riemann zero set / `Xi`.

This is a **prior-art redirect**, not a Mathia novelty claim.

## Axis support in prime-exponent coordinates

Write

```text
n = product_p p^{v_p(n)}.
```

The von Mangoldt function satisfies

```text
Lambda(n) = log p   if n=p^m for some prime p and m>=1,
Lambda(n) = 0       otherwise.
```

Therefore

```text
Lambda(n) != 0  =>  v(n)=m e_p.
```

In particular every genuinely mixed-prime lattice point — a vector with at least two nonzero prime coordinates — has zero coefficient in the logarithmic derivative and in the non-archimedean term of the classical explicit formula.

This gives an exact structural compression:

```text
full exponent lattice N_0^(P)
        |
        | logarithmic derivative / von Mangoldt weight
        v
union of coordinate rays {m e_p}
        |
        | completed Weil explicit formula
        v
zero-divisor / archimedean dual expression.
```

The first equality is initially a Dirichlet-series identity only for `Re(s)>1`. What crosses the analytic-continuation boundary is **not** that Euler series itself; it is Weil’s completed explicit formula, with its archimedean contribution and pole terms included.

## Weil positivity is already an RH-level spectral target

The full Weil quadratic functional is classical. In standard formulations its positive semidefiniteness is equivalent to RH. Bombieri’s treatment makes this criterion and the associated finite truncations explicit.

This matters for the prime-lattice program because it rules out presenting

```text
prime-power Fourier data -> quadratic form -> positivity/spectrum -> RH
```

as a new mechanism. That bridge is already a central classical formulation of RH.

The remaining question is whether the exponent-lattice viewpoint supplies extra structure that makes the positivity/extremal-vector problem tractable rather than merely rewriting the known criterion.

## The current self-adjoint construction

Connes–Consani–Moscovici restrict the Weil form to

```text
L^2([lambda^-1,lambda], d*u)
```

and then to a finite-dimensional space `E_N` generated by the lowest-frequency modes of the logarithmic scaling operator. Their equation for the semilocal form has the arithmetic part

```text
-sum_{1<n<=lambda^2} Lambda(n) <f,T(n)f>,
```

so the finite prime data entering the form is exactly the bounded set of prime powers `p^m<=lambda^2`.

Let `epsilon_N` be the smallest eigenvalue of the finite form. Their Theorem 1.1 assumes that `epsilon_N` is simple and that its normalized eigenvector `xi` is even. Under those hypotheses they construct

```text
D_log^(lambda,N)
  = D_log^(lambda) - |D_log^(lambda) xi><delta_N|
```

as a self-adjoint operator on the indicated quotient/direct-sum Hilbert space, and prove

```text
det_reg(D_log^(lambda,N)-z)
  = -i lambda^(-i z) xi_hat(z).
```

They also prove that `xi_hat` is entire and all of its zeros are real, coinciding with the spectrum of that self-adjoint operator.

This is substantially stronger than an arbitrary Hilbert–Pólya analogy: at each controlled finite stage there is a genuine self-adjoint operator and a genuine determinant/spectrum statement.

## What is numerical and what remains unproved

The identification of the finite operator spectrum with Riemann zeros is numerical, not a theorem. The paper reports striking agreement and proposes the limiting picture

```text
N -> infinity, lambda -> infinity
    => spectrum(D_log^(lambda,N)) -> ordinates of zeta zeros
```

or, at the determinant level, convergence after normalization toward `Xi`.

The paper explicitly states that a rigorous proof of this convergence would prove RH. It also isolates essential missing steps. Among them:

1. establish the needed simplicity/evenness property for the extremal eigenvector of the semilocal Weil form at the limiting level;
2. prove that the explicit prolate-spheroidal model `k_lambda` approximates the actual extremal eigenvector `xi_lambda` strongly enough to transport zero convergence.

They do prove that the Fourier transform of the model `k_lambda` converges to the Riemann `Xi` function uniformly on closed substrips of `|Im z|<1/2`. The unproved bridge is from that model function to the actual extremal eigenvector/determinant governing the self-adjoint spectral triple.

No inference from the numerical agreement to RH is made here.

## Mathia-specific consequence

This prior art materially sharpens what a useful prime-lattice contribution would have to add.

First, the canonical completed explicit-formula channel does **not** use the full combinatorics of mixed-prime exponent vectors directly. Its arithmetic support is the union of prime-power coordinate rays. Therefore any claim that mixed-prime hypercube/lattice geometry is essential to a zeta spectral mechanism must identify a mathematically justified operation by which that extra information survives into, or improves on, the Weil/explicit-formula channel.

Second, the requested architecture

```text
log-prime arithmetic data
  -> finite spectral object
  -> self-adjoint operator
  -> determinant with real zeros
  -> candidate limit toward zeta zeros
```

already exists in serious prior art. Constructing another operator from `{log p}` or another finite prime matrix is not, by itself, a new RH mechanism.

Third, the construction needs structure absent from the bare Bohr torus: the **completed** Weil form, including the archimedean gamma-place contribution and pole term. This is consistent with `PL-003`, `PL-010`, `PL-011`, and `PL-012`: prime phases/frequencies alone repeatedly fail to select the exceptional Riemann object, while completed arithmetic/analytic structure can reach an RH-equivalent spectral statement.

The productive residual target is therefore much narrower: explain, from additional canonical structure, the extremal-eigenvector/positivity behavior that the Weil spectral route currently assumes or observes numerically.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

- Weil’s explicit formula and positivity criterion are classical.
- Bombieri gives a detailed analysis of Weil’s quadratic functional and finite truncations; positivity of the full functional is an RH equivalence.
- Connes–Consani (2023) already connect compactly supported Weil forms, prolate spheroidal functions, and perturbed spectral triples, with numerical matching to zeta zeros.
- Connes–Consani–Moscovici (2025/2026) give the current self-adjoint rank-one construction, determinant theorem, numerical experiments, and explicit statement of the missing convergence steps.
- The identification `Lambda`-support = prime-power coordinate rays is an exact specialization to exponent-vector coordinates, but it is immediate from the classical von Mangoldt support and is not claimed as a new theorem.

Thus the result is stored because it redirects the search toward a very specific unresolved bridge, not because the spectral construction itself is novel to Mathia.

## Boundary conditions and counterarguments

- The Dirichlet-series identity for `-zeta'/zeta` holds absolutely only in `Re(s)>1`; it must not be treated as a convergent Euler expansion in the critical strip.
- The Weil explicit formula is the legitimate continuation/completion bridge and includes archimedean and pole contributions. Omitting them changes the object.
- Axis-ray support does not imply that mixed-prime arithmetic is globally irrelevant to zeta. It says only that mixed-prime lattice points do not appear **directly** in the von-Mangoldt/non-archimedean term of this canonical linear explicit-formula channel.
- Self-adjointness and real zeros at finite truncation do not prove convergence to the Riemann zeros.
- Theorem 1.1 has explicit simplicity/evenness hypotheses for the finite extremal eigenvector. Numerical verification of those hypotheses at tested parameters is not a general proof.
- The fact that the model `k_lambda` has Fourier transform tending to `Xi` does not prove that the actual determinant does so; controlling `xi_lambda-k_lambda` is the missing bridge.

## Audit / falsification criterion

This finding can be audited through four independent checks:

1. verify `Lambda(n)` restricts the non-archimedean explicit-formula support to `n=p^m`, hence to exponent vectors `m e_p`;
2. verify the semilocal Weil form contains `-sum_{1<n<=lambda^2} Lambda(n)<f,T(n)f>` plus the archimedean and pole terms;
3. verify the finite self-adjoint/determinant theorem is conditional on simplicity and evenness of the lowest eigenvector and gives an entire determinant factor with only real zeros;
4. verify that convergence of the actual spectral determinants/zeros to `Xi`/Riemann zeros remains explicitly unproved.

A future prime-lattice proposal genuinely advances this route only if it proves a new structural statement that closes one of those missing analytic/spectral steps, or introduces additional lattice information with a demonstrable effect not already encoded by the Weil form. Merely reproducing the explicit formula or its finite prime matrices is prior art.

## Consequence for the research line

There is already a rigorous, highly relevant spectral pipeline whose arithmetic input is the prime-power axis skeleton of the exponent lattice and whose completed output is tied to Weil’s RH criterion. At finite cutoff, recent work produces honest self-adjoint operators with real spectral determinants and striking numerical agreement with zeta zeros. The decisive RH limit remains open.

For `prime_lattice`, this is a strong prior-art redirect: the main opportunity is no longer to invent a generic spectral operator from prime coordinates, but to determine whether the exponent-lattice viewpoint supplies genuinely new control of the completed Weil form’s extremal eigenvectors/positivity or of the unproved determinant-convergence bridge.
