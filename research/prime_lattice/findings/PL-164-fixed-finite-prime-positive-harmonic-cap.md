# PL-164 — Fixed finite-prime positive Landau–Gonek localization has a uniform interior cap

## Claim

The one-prime full-strip resolution obstruction of `PL-163` survives **every fixed finite family of prime axes** and does not depend on the Fejer coefficients.

Fix a finite set of distinct rational primes

`P={p_1,...,p_r}`

and write

`N_P={ product_(p in P) p^(k_p) : k_p in N_0 }`.

Let `E` be any finite subset of `N_P \ {1}` and let

`F(theta)=1+2 Re sum_(n in E) a_n exp(i <v_P(n),theta>)`,

`theta in T^r`,

be a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial with normalized constant coefficient `1`. Define its symmetric full-critical-strip extension by

`H_F(x,theta)`
` = 1 + 2 Re sum_(n in E) a_n`
`       [cosh(x log n)/cosh((log n)/2)]`
`       exp(i <v_P(n),theta>),`

for `|x|<=1/2`.

Then:

1. `H_F(x,theta)>=0` throughout the whole strip `|x|<=1/2`;
2. for zeta zeros `rho=beta+i gamma`, evaluating at

   `x=beta-1/2`, `theta_p=gamma log p`,

   is controlled exactly by the integer Landau--Gonek moments `sum n^rho`;
3. if RH fails but the rightmost zero abscissa satisfies `Theta<1`, then every such normalized detector obeys the degree-independent bound

`0 <= H_F(x,theta)`
`   <= 1 + 4 [ product_(p in P) (1-p^(-(1-Theta)))^(-1) - 1 ]`

throughout `|x|<=Theta-1/2`.

Consequently, no sequence of normalized positive full-strip detectors supported on the semigroup generated by a **fixed finite set of prime axes** can acquire unbounded response on a sparse phase-pinned zero sequence with `beta_j->Theta<1`, no matter how its degree or finite support grows.

This is a strict strengthening of the natural finite-prime repair left after `PL-162` and `PL-163`: adding finitely many prime coordinates cannot overcome the interior smoothing while retaining positivity on the full critical strip. The remaining escape within this family must let the number of active prime axes grow, leave the positive-exponent integer semigroup, abandon full-strip positivity, or use arithmetic information beyond first Landau--Gonek moments.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + FINITE-PRIME-CLOSURE`. The arithmetic moment theorem is the classical Landau--Gonek formula already audited in `PL-163`. Positivity and the interior cap are elementary Poisson/harmonic and Fourier-coefficient deductions. No novelty claim is made; the durable contribution is the exact finite-prime closure of the previously one-axis obstruction.

## The full-strip extension is positive along every prime Kronecker orbit

Put

`lambda_P=(log p_1,...,log p_r)`.

For a fixed torus point `theta_0`, restrict `H_F` to the real prime-flow orbit by

`u_(theta_0)(x,t)=H_F(x,theta_0+t lambda_P)`.

For `n in N_P`, unique factorization gives

`<v_P(n),lambda_P>=log n`.

Hence the `n`th mode of `u_(theta_0)` is a scalar multiple of

`cosh(x log n) exp(i t log n)`,

which is harmonic for `partial_x^2+partial_t^2`. At both strip boundaries,

`u_(theta_0)(+/-1/2,t)=F(theta_0+t lambda_P)>=0`.

The bounded harmonic extension of these nonnegative boundary data is their strip Poisson integral, so it is nonnegative in the interior. Evaluating at `t=0` gives

`H_F(x,theta_0)>=0`

for every `theta_0` and `|x|<=1/2`.

This argument is deliberately orbitwise. It does not require the multivariable polynomial to be harmonic for the ordinary Euclidean Laplacian on `R x T^r`; the relevant differential operator is the one generated by the actual Kronecker frequency vector. It also makes clear why `log n=<v(n),(log p)_p>` is the load-bearing exponent-lattice identity.

## Every mode is an ordinary integer Landau–Gonek moment

For an integer `n>1`, let

`S_n(T)=sum_(zeta(rho)=0, 0<Im rho<=T) n^rho`,

with multiplicity. Functional-equation reflection preserves the positive-ordinate zero multiset under

`rho=beta+i gamma -> 1-conj(rho)=1-beta+i gamma`.

Therefore

`n^(-1/2) S_n(T)`
` = sum_(0<gamma<=T)`
`     cosh((beta-1/2) log n) exp(i gamma log n).`

The identity is exact: reflection changes only the sign of `beta-1/2`, so averaging the two equal zero sums produces the hyperbolic cosine while retaining the vertical phase.

At the prime-torus point

`theta_rho=(gamma log p_1,...,gamma log p_r)`,

we also have

`exp(i <v_P(n),theta_rho>)=exp(i gamma log n)`.

Summing `H_F` over the zero divisor consequently gives

`sum_(0<gamma<=T) H_F(beta-1/2,theta_rho)`
` = N(T) + 4 Re sum_(n in E) a_n S_n(T)/(n+1),`

because

`n^(-1/2)/cosh((log n)/2)=2/(n+1)`.

Gonek's uniform integer formula then gives

`S_n(T)`
` = -(T/(2 pi)) Lambda(n)`
`   + O(n log(2nT) log log(3n)).`

Mixed finite-prime modes therefore do not create a new zero-moment theory. They are already the classical integer Landau--Gonek channel. The arithmetic main term vanishes unless `n` is a prime power, while the same full-strip normalization turns the factor `n` in the standard error into `n/(n+1)=O(1)` before the remaining logarithms.

## Positivity forces a coefficient bound, and fixed finite prime support makes the damping summable

Because `F>=0` and its Haar mean is `1`, every Fourier coefficient satisfies

`|a_n|`
` = | integral_(T^r) F(theta) exp(-i <v_P(n),theta>) dtheta |`
` <= integral_(T^r) F(theta) dtheta`
` = 1`.

Assume now that

`Theta=sup {Re rho : zeta(rho)=0}`

satisfies `1/2<Theta<1`, and put `B=Theta-1/2`. For every `|x|<=B`,

`cosh(x log n)/cosh((log n)/2)`
` <= 2 n^(-(1-Theta)).`

Hence

`H_F(x,theta)`
` <= 1+4 sum_(n in E) |a_n| n^(-(1-Theta))`
` <= 1+4 sum_(n in N_P, n>1) n^(-(1-Theta)).`

Since `P` is fixed and finite, the last sum is a finite Euler product:

`sum_(n in N_P) n^(-delta)`
` = product_(p in P) (1-p^(-delta))^(-1)`

for every `delta>0`. Taking `delta=1-Theta` yields the claimed cap.

The essential point is that the cap does **not** depend on the number of modes, their degrees, or the shape of the positive polynomial. A Fejer kernel is only one extremizing-looking choice inside a much larger class. Full-strip positivity plus fixed finite-prime semigroup support already forces enough Fourier control that the harmonic damping is absolutely summable at every strict interior frontier.

For one prime, the estimate reduces to the same geometric-series scale found explicitly in `PL-163`:

`1+4/(p^(1-Theta)-1)`.

Thus the earlier one-axis bound was not a special feature of Fejer weights; it is the rank-one instance of a finite-prime semigroup bound.

## Narrowing the positivity strip restores resolution only by restoring the Landau–Gonek loss

The full-strip denominator `cosh((log n)/2)` is what simultaneously makes the detector positive on the unconditional critical strip and cancels the linear `n` size in Gonek's uniform error.

If one instead normalizes the same mode at the hypothetical frontier width `B=Theta-1/2`, the coefficient multiplying the `S_n(T)` error is proportional to

`n^(-1/2)/cosh(B log n)`.

After multiplying by Gonek's `O(n ...)` remainder, the modewise loss is

`n^(1/2)/cosh(B log n) asymp n^(1-Theta)`.

So the finite-prime enlargement does not change the tradeoff identified in `PL-163`: moving the positivity boundary inward permits sharper response near `beta=Theta`, but the available uniform explicit-formula estimate loses precisely a positive power of the mode size. Mixed composite modes have no compensating von-Mangoldt main term, since `Lambda(n)=0` away from prime powers.

This does not prove that the true remainder must have this size. It proves only that the standard first-moment Landau--Gonek theorem, even after arbitrary fixed-finite-prime positive assembly, does not provide a sparse-frontier exclusion theorem.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The arithmetic input is exactly the Landau--Gonek theory already anchored and audited in `PL-163`; allowing `n` to range over the semigroup generated by finitely many primes is an immediate specialization of the integer formula, not a new theorem.

The analytical input is classical harmonic analysis. A nonnegative normalized trigonometric polynomial has Fourier coefficients bounded by its constant coefficient, and symmetric strip extension is a positive Poisson operator. The orbit restriction above is the ordinary strip Dirichlet problem applied to the Kronecker flow generated by the frequencies `log p`. A targeted audit around positive trigonometric polynomials, strip Poisson/Harnack bounds, Bohr lifts, and Dirichlet-polynomial positivity found only this classical background and no reason to regard the finite-prime cap as a new harmonic-analysis mechanism. The exact zeta specialization is therefore classified as a derived obstruction rather than a novelty claim.

The closest Mathia prior art is internal and decisive:

- `PL-162` proves that simultaneous identity recurrence in every **fixed finite prime torus** is vertically generic for the full zero population, so simply adding finitely many phases cannot anti-alias the `PL-161` frontier;
- `PL-163` introduces the horizontal Landau--Gonek weight and proves the one-prime Fejer full-strip/frontier tradeoff;
- the present result closes the obvious combination of those two ideas: finitely many prime coordinates plus an arbitrary normalized positive semigroup polynomial still have a uniform interior cap.

## Adversarial boundaries

1. **The prime set must be fixed.** The cap contains

   `product_(p in P)(1-p^(-(1-Theta)))^(-1)`.

   If `P=P_K` grows with the detector, this constant can diverge. The result does not rule out a genuinely increasing-dimensional prime-lattice construction.

2. **The frequency support is the positive integer semigroup.** Characters with mixed-sign exponent vectors correspond to rational ratios rather than integers. They leave the direct integer Landau--Gonek channel used here and are not covered by the stated moment identity.

3. **Full-strip positivity is load-bearing.** A detector required to be positive only on a narrower hypothetical zero strip can have much sharper boundary response, but then the standard Landau--Gonek remainder acquires the `n^(1-Theta)` loss above.

4. **The case `Theta=1` remains open to this argument.** Then `1-Theta=0`, the finite-prime geometric series no longer converges, and there is no positive harmonic distance between the hypothetical frontier and the trivial boundary.

5. **This is not a density theorem for off-line zeros.** The result says that one broad detector class cannot amplify a sparse near-frontier sequence. It neither constructs such a sequence nor proves that one can exist.

6. **No Euler product is continued into the critical strip.** The finite Euler product in the cap is merely the convergent geometric sum over the finitely generated semigroup at exponent `1-Theta>0`. Zeta information enters through the completed zero symmetry and Landau--Gonek explicit formula.

7. **This does not conflict with `PL-155`.** Two-prime positivity there concerns Suzuki's translation-invariant screw kernel on a dense signed additive group and can be RH-equivalent. The present result concerns a different object: positive first-moment trigonometric detectors assembled from integer Landau--Gonek modes.

A falsification would require failure of the orbitwise strip Poisson positivity, failure of the normalized Fourier coefficient bound, an error in the functional-equation symmetrization of `S_n(T)`, or failure of the finite-semigroup geometric product.

## Consequence for the research line

After `PL-162`--`PL-164`, the fixed-finite-dimensional version of the weighted prime-phase repair is closed quite broadly. The missing information cannot come merely from taking more Fourier modes on one prime axis or from replacing that axis by any fixed finite collection of primes while retaining a normalized positive full-strip Landau--Gonek detector.

Within this family the first genuinely different escape is **dimension growth**: let the active prime set itself increase so that the interior cap is no longer uniform, and ask whether the resulting gain can beat the simultaneously growing explicit-formula cost without collapsing to ordinary prime-product statistics. Mixed-sign frequencies, nonpositive/cancellation-sensitive observables, or arithmetic inputs beyond first zero moments are separate escapes. None is established here.
