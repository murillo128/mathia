# PL-171 — Affine congruence projections recover Dirichlet L-channels, but periodic cancellation does not cross the trace-class wall

## Claim

`PL-169`--`PL-170` identify ordinary addition as genuine extra rational-integer structure beyond the free multiplicative prime lattice, and test the cheapest affine relative trace. The same `ax+b` representation contains a richer canonical arithmetic layer that the tail-difference calculation does not use: **congruence projections**. That layer is classical prior art in Cuntz's `ax+b` algebra and canonically generates the profinite integer algebra `C(hat Z)`.

On the positive-energy space `ell^2(N_{>=1})`, keep

```text
H e_n = (log n)e_n,
S_h e_n = e_(n+h),
mu_m e_n = e_(mn),
```

and let

```text
P_m = mu_m mu_m^*.
```

Then `P_m` is the projection onto multiples of `m`. For `h=0,...,m-1`, with `S_0=I`,

```text
E_(m,h) := S_h^* P_m S_h
```

is the exact residue-class projection

```text
E_(m,h)e_n = 1_(m | n+h) e_n.
```

The `E_(m,h)` are pairwise orthogonal and

```text
sum_(h=0)^(m-1) E_(m,h)=I.
```

Thus the same affine completion that produced the finite-head relative traces of `PL-170` also recovers all finite congruence observables. For `a in {1,...,m}`, write `E_(m,a)` for the projection onto `n congruent a (mod m)`. For `Re(s)>1`,

```text
boxed:
Tr(E_(m,a) exp(-sH))
 = sum_(k>=0) (a+km)^(-s)
 = m^(-s) zeta(s,a/m).
```

Consequently every periodic function `f mod m` gives a bounded diagonal observable

```text
M_f = sum_(a=1)^m f(a) E_(m,a)
```

with

```text
boxed:
Tr(M_f exp(-sH))
 = sum_(n>=1) f(n)n^(-s),
qquad Re(s)>1.
```

For a Dirichlet character `chi mod m`, this is exactly

```text
boxed:
Tr(M_chi exp(-sH)) = L(s,chi),
\qquad Re(s)>1.
```

This is a genuine arithmetic enrichment of the bare exponent lattice: unlike an unlabeled Beurling/free-monoid control, the ordinary integer semiring canonically carries residue classes and the profinite completion. But it does **not** solve the analytic-continuation/critical-line problem at the level of ordinary operator ideals.

For every nonzero periodic `f` and every `q>=1`, if `sigma=Re(s)>0`,

```text
boxed:
M_f exp(-sH) in S_q
    iff q sigma > 1.
```

In particular,

```text
M_f exp(-sH) in S_1  iff Re(s)>1,
M_f exp(-sH) in S_2  iff Re(s)>1/2.
```

The phases and cancellations in `f` are invisible to Schatten membership because singular values use `|f(n)|`. Thus a nonprincipal Dirichlet character can make the scalar Dirichlet series converge conditionally already for `Re(s)>0`, and its classical analytic continuation can go much farther, while the corresponding affine heat operator remains non-trace-class throughout `0<Re(s)<=1`.

At the critical line the Hilbert--Schmidt divergence is explicit:

```text
sum_(n<=N) |f(n)|^2/n
 = [(1/m) sum_(a=1)^m |f(a)|^2] log N + O_f,m(1).
```

For a Dirichlet character this coefficient is `phi(m)/m`. Hence the `1/2` boundary here is the universal density/Schatten threshold of a nonzero periodic diagonal channel, not a zero-sensitive mechanism.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + PRIOR-ART/REDIRECT + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` for the route

```text
prime-exponent lattice
+ canonical integer addition / ax+b completion
+ congruence/profinite projections
+ periodic or Dirichlet-character heat twist
    -> ordinary trace-class continuation into the critical strip
       or a new mechanism localizing zeta zeros.
```

No novelty is claimed for the `ax+b` algebra, congruence projections, profinite completion, Hurwitz zeta decomposition, Dirichlet `L`-functions, or their operator-trace realizations. The durable line-specific point is the exact separation between two natural affine repairs: **finite differences gain one power of absolute decay but erase the infinite zeta tail (`PL-170`), while periodic arithmetic twists retain an infinite `L`-series but do not gain any ordinary trace-class decay.**

## 1. Congruence geometry is already intrinsic to the affine semigroup

Cuntz's canonical representation of the `ax+b` semigroup uses multiplicative isometries and additive shifts satisfying

```text
s_n v^k = v^(nk) s_n.
```

After passing to his boundary quotient `Q_N`, the range projection `e_n=s_ns_n^*` satisfies the characteristic relation

```text
sum_(k=0)^(n-1) u^k e_n u^(-k)=1,
```

which Cuntz explicitly identifies with the partition of the integers into congruence classes modulo `n`. The commutative algebra `D` generated by these projections has

```text
Spec(D)=hat Z.
```

Thus the passage from the free multiplicative lattice to the integer `ax+b` semiring does add a canonical zero-dimensional arithmetic geometry: the profinite residue space.

The positive-energy Toeplitz representation used in `PL-170` sees the same residue information before quotienting. Indeed

```text
P_m e_n = 1_(m|n)e_n,
```

and therefore

```text
S_h^*P_mS_h e_n
 = 1_(m|n+h)e_n.
```

For fixed `m`, exactly one residue `h mod m` satisfies `m|n+h`, so the projections are orthogonal and sum to the identity. Varying `m` and refining residue partitions produces the usual locally constant functions on the profinite integers. This part of the construction is arithmetic and canonical; the negative result below is not a claim that congruence data are generic free-monoid structure.

## 2. Residue heat traces are Hurwitz zeta functions

Let `E_(m,a)` denote the projection onto the basis vectors with

```text
n congruent a (mod m),
1<=a<=m,
```

where `a=m` denotes residue `0`. Since

```text
exp(-sH)e_n=n^(-s)e_n,
```

one has, for `sigma=Re(s)>1`,

```text
Tr(E_(m,a)exp(-sH))
 = sum_(k>=0)(a+km)^(-s)
 = m^(-s) zeta(s,a/m).
```

The restriction `sigma>1` is an **operator trace-class statement**, not merely a convenient starting half-plane. The projection selects one residue class of positive density, so

```text
||E_(m,a)exp(-sH)||_(S_1)
 = sum_(k>=0)(a+km)^(-sigma),
```

which diverges for `sigma<=1`.

For a bounded periodic `f`, finite linearity gives

```text
M_f=sum_a f(a)E_(m,a)
```

and hence

```text
Tr(M_f exp(-sH))
 = m^(-s) sum_(a=1)^m f(a) zeta(s,a/m)
 = sum_n f(n)n^(-s)
```

in the common absolutely convergent half-plane. The finite Hurwitz-zeta formula is classical and supplies scalar meromorphic continuation after the fact, but the individual operator trace above has not thereby been continued as an ordinary trace.

For a Dirichlet character `chi mod m`, this specializes to the classical identity

```text
L(s,chi)
 = m^(-s) sum_(a=1)^m chi(a) zeta(s,a/m).
```

Thus the affine/profinite algebra contains the **coefficient observable** for an entire family of degree-one arithmetic `L`-channels, not merely the principal zeta channel.

## 3. Exact Schatten threshold: periodic cancellation cannot help

Let `f` be a nonzero periodic function modulo `m`. Since `M_f exp(-sH)` is diagonal in the standard basis, its singular values are the absolute diagonal entries

```text
|f(n)| n^(-sigma),
qquad sigma=Re(s).
```

Therefore for every `q>=1`,

```text
||M_f exp(-sH)||_(S_q)^q
 = sum_(n>=1)|f(n)|^q n^(-q sigma).
```

If `q sigma>1`, this converges by comparison with `sum n^(-q sigma)`. Conversely, because `f` is nonzero, there is a residue class `a mod m` and a constant `c>0` such that

```text
|f(a+km)|=c
```

for every `k>=0`. Hence

```text
sum_n |f(n)|^q n^(-q sigma)
 >= c^q sum_(k>=0)(a+km)^(-q sigma),
```

which diverges when `q sigma<=1`. This proves the exact equivalence

```text
M_f exp(-sH) in S_q iff q Re(s)>1.
```

The result is deliberately stronger than the character case. **No fixed periodic sign or phase pattern can improve the ordinary Schatten boundary**, regardless of mean zero, multiplicativity, orthogonality relations, or Gauss-sum structure.

For `q=2` and `sigma=1/2`, elementary summation over residue classes gives

```text
sum_(n<=N)|f(n)|^2/n
 = (1/m) [sum_(a=1)^m |f(a)|^2] log N + O(1).
```

If `f=chi` is a Dirichlet character, `|chi(a)|^2` is the indicator of `(a,m)=1`, so the coefficient becomes

```text
phi(m)/m.
```

This critical Hilbert--Schmidt divergence records only the density of residues on which the character is nonzero. It has no dependence on the zeros of `L(s,chi)`.

## 4. Conditional Dirichlet convergence is not an ordinary operator trace

For a nonprincipal Dirichlet character, the partial sums

```text
sum_(n<=x) chi(n)
```

are bounded because `chi` is periodic with mean zero over a complete period. Dirichlet summation therefore gives convergence of

```text
sum_n chi(n)n^(-s)
```

for every `Re(s)>0` (with the usual boundary qualifications at `s=0`). Classical analytic continuation then extends `L(s,chi)` still further; for primitive nonprincipal characters it is entire.

None of this changes the operator-ideal statement. For `0<Re(s)<=1`,

```text
M_chi exp(-sH) notin S_1.
```

The naturally ordered diagonal sum may converge as a scalar series, but the operator does not possess the canonical unitary-invariant trace of a trace-class operator. Reordering the diagonal terms can change a merely conditionally convergent scalar sum, whereas an ordinary operator trace is absolutely summable and basis independent.

This is the exact obstruction to using congruence cancellation as a cheap operator-theoretic continuation mechanism. The arithmetic phase cancellation lives **after signs are retained**; Schatten norms discard those signs before summing.

The contrast with `PL-170` is sharp. There the finite difference

```text
(n+h)^(-s)-n^(-s)
```

has absolute size `O(n^(-sigma-1))`, so the relative heat operator is genuinely trace class for `sigma>0`; but exact telescoping deletes the entire infinite tail and leaves a finite Dirichlet polynomial. Here the periodic twist keeps the infinite `L`-series and its arithmetic cancellation, but the absolute decay remains `n^(-sigma)`, so ordinary trace class stops at `sigma=1`.

## 5. The critical `S_2` boundary is not a hidden GRH mechanism

Because every nonzero periodic channel satisfies

```text
M_f exp(-sH) in S_2 iff Re(s)>1/2,
```

one might try to interpret the common boundary `1/2` as evidence for the critical line. The exact formula above rules out that interpretation in this model. The same threshold holds for an arbitrary nonzero periodic function, whether or not it is multiplicative and whether or not its Dirichlet series has any functional equation.

For characters, the threshold depends only on the positive density of nonzero residue classes. It is therefore the same square-summability geometry already encountered in several Bohr/Hardy and Schatten manifestations of the prime lattice: `1/2` appears because the squared coefficients behave like `1/n`, not because the `L`-zero divisor has been localized.

A Hilbert--Schmidt regularization also does not restore the lost first-order trace automatically. For example, whenever `T=M_chi exp(-sH)` is Hilbert--Schmidt, the standard regularized determinant `det_2(I-zT)` removes the linear trace term by definition. Its small-`z` logarithm begins with traces of `T^k`, `k>=2`, i.e. scalar channels of the form

```text
sum_n chi(n)^k n^(-ks)=L(ks,chi^k)
```

where those traces converge. Thus the regularization that becomes available past `Re(s)=1/2` does not canonically retain `L(s,chi)` itself as the missing trace term; recovering that term would require an extra renormalization prescription.

This is the same methodological warning as `PL-009`: crossing an operator-ideal threshold by regularization is not a derivation of the desired analytic continuation unless the counterterm/linear functional is independently forced.

## 6. Functional equation and zero localization remain additional global structure

The finite congruence algebra is rich enough to encode Dirichlet characters and finite additive Fourier transforms, so Gauss sums are not alien to the integer semiring. But the completed functional equation for a primitive character also couples this finite arithmetic to the archimedean gamma factor and the reflection `s -> 1-s`.

The diagonal heat-trace calculation proves none of that. It gives `L(s,chi)` as an ordinary trace only for `Re(s)>1`; beyond that half-plane one must use classical scalar continuation, a Hurwitz-zeta formula, a summation theorem, or a new operator-relative/renormalized construction. Importing the completed Dirichlet functional equation then imports the classical zero symmetry; it does not show that the affine congruence operator has independently forced the zeros onto `Re(s)=1/2`.

This distinction is especially important for the Riemann channel. Taking modulus `1` or the principal character recovers `zeta(s)` (up to finitely many Euler factors), but the ordinary trace boundary remains exactly `Re(s)>1`. The fact that nonprincipal characters have stronger coefficient cancellation does not produce a trace-class mechanism for the principal zeta channel.

## Prior art and novelty audit

The operator-algebraic congruence structure is classical.

- **Joachim Cuntz**, “C*-algebras associated with the `ax+b`-semigroup over `N`,” in *K-Theory and Noncommutative Geometry*, EMS Series of Congress Reports, 2008, DOI `10.4171/060-1/8`, arXiv `math/0611541`. Section 2 gives the canonical additive/multiplicative representation; Section 3 gives the congruence relation `sum u^k e_n u^(-k)=1`, the commuting congruence projections, and identifies the spectrum of their diagonal algebra with `hat Z`. The introduction and later crossed-product description place this algebra as an additive extension of Bost--Connes.
- **NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions**, §§25.11 and 25.15, version 1.2.7 (release 15 June 2026), records the Hurwitz-zeta and Dirichlet-`L` formulas, the defining series in `Re(s)>1`, and the classical analytic/functional-equation structure. These formulas are used only to identify the scalar channels after the exact operator calculation.
- **Parikshit Dutta, Debashis Ghoshal**, “Pseudodifferential Operators on `Q_p` and `L`-Series,” arXiv:2003.00901 (2020), constructs a different `p`-adic operator framework in which Riemann and Dirichlet `L`-functions are expressed as traces of pseudodifferential operators. This is a broad novelty control: realizing an `L`-function as an operator trace is itself established prior art and is not claimed here as new.
- `PL-114` already shows that fixed-modulus **prime-pair** congruence couplings scalarize under finite character harmonic analysis. The present finding addresses a different question left live by `PL-169`--`PL-170`: what the canonical full-integer affine semigroup contributes at the level of congruence projections and operator ideals.

The exact Schatten equivalence above is an elementary diagonal calculation. No novelty claim is made for it as an abstract theorem. Its durable value is the route classification: the most canonical profinite/congruence enrichment of the affine prime-lattice representation retains Dirichlet arithmetic, but ordinary trace ideals cannot exploit the cancellation that analytically improves nonprincipal Dirichlet series.

## Adversarial boundaries

1. **Fixed periodic modulus is load-bearing.** A conductor `m=m(T)` growing with a spectral or spatial scale is a different mesoscopic problem. The pointwise `S_q` criterion remains true for each fixed nonzero periodic observable, but no uniform statement about a moving family is claimed.
2. **Only diagonal congruence twists are classified.** Noncommuting operators coupling the profinite algebra to additive shifts, prime shifts, Nyman targets, or Weil/archimedean data are outside the no-go.
3. **The result concerns ordinary Schatten traces.** Dixmier traces, cyclic cocycles, zeta-regularized traces, scattering determinants, and other renormalized functionals are not ruled out. They must independently justify why their subtraction/ordering is canonical and why it preserves rather than inserts the desired `L`-divisor.
4. **Conditional scalar convergence is not denied.** For nonprincipal `chi`, `L(s,chi)` already converges conditionally in `Re(s)>0`. The claim is specifically that this cancellation does not make `M_chi exp(-sH)` trace class there.
5. **The `1/2` Hilbert--Schmidt wall is not claimed to be irrelevant in every enriched model.** It is non-discriminating for this periodic diagonal family because the same threshold holds for arbitrary nonzero periodic coefficients.
6. **Congruence arithmetic is genuinely rational-integer-specific.** A generic Beurling system need not have a canonical additive quotient modulo `m`. The negative is not obtained by replacing arithmetic with a generic free monoid; it comes after adding precisely the canonical integer semiring structure that distinguishes rational integers.
7. **Dirichlet functional equations are not produced by the trace identity.** The finite residue algebra can host character/Gauss-sum data, but the completed reflection also requires the archimedean factor and a continuation theorem. No zero-location claim follows from the uncompleted diagonal trace.

A falsification of the main operator statement would require a nonzero fixed periodic `f` for which `sum |f(n)|^q n^(-q sigma)` converges at `q sigma<=1`, or a failure of the exact residue projection/heat-trace identities. Neither depends on RH, GRH, Euler-product continuation, or asymptotic prime distribution.

## Consequence for the research line

The canonical rational-semiring enrichment now has two sharply different continuation behaviors:

```text
additive finite difference
    -> gains absolute decay
    -> relative S_1 trace exists for Re(s)>0
    -> infinite zeta tail cancels to a finite head          [PL-170]

congruence / character twist
    -> retains an infinite Dirichlet L-series
    -> no gain in absolute Schatten decay
    -> ordinary S_1 trace still requires Re(s)>1            [PL-171]
```

This closes the next cheapest `ax+b` repair after `PL-170`. Merely exploiting the canonical profinite congruence algebra does not turn arithmetic sign cancellation into a basis-independent trace-class continuation, and its `S_2` boundary at `1/2` is generic periodic square-summability.

A surviving affine/semiring route therefore has to use a **non-diagonal, target-sensitive coupling** between addition, multiplication, and completion data. It must explain how arithmetic cancellation survives in a canonical operator invariant without either telescoping away the zeta bulk or being reduced to a scalar conditionally convergent `L`-series whose continuation and functional equation are already classical.
