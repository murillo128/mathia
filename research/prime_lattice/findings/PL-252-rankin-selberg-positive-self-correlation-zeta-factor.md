# PL-252 — Rankin–Selberg self-correlation is positive and global, yet its zeta factor still carries arbitrary off-line zeros

## Claim

`PL-251` leaves a materially narrower escape than finite-dimensional Artin functors: perhaps a **global positive quadratic construction**, rather than a representation-ring operation, could couple richer arithmetic data to the principal Riemann-zeta channel strongly enough to force critical-line localization. The classical Rankin–Selberg self-correlation of a level-one Hecke eigenform is a matched control for that proposal, and it fails in an exact way.

Let `f` be a normalized level-one holomorphic Hecke cusp eigenform, with real normalized Hecke eigenvalues `lambda_f(n)`. In `Re(s)>1`, Rankin–Selberg unfolding gives the positive-coefficient Dirichlet series

`D_f(s) = sum_(n>=1) lambda_f(n)^2 n^(-s)`

and the exact factorization

`D_f(s) = zeta(s) L(s,sym^2 f) / zeta(2s)`.

Define the full self-convolution Euler product

`R_f(s) := zeta(2s) D_f(s) = zeta(s) L(s,sym^2 f)`

initially in the common absolute-convergence half-plane. Its Dirichlet coefficients are also nonnegative:

`R_f(s) = sum_(m>=1) r_f(m) m^(-s)`,

`r_f(m) = sum_(d^2 | m) lambda_f(m/d^2)^2 >= 0`.

Thus the construction has all of the elementary positivity one could ask for at coefficient level, is generated by a canonical quadratic automorphic self-pairing, has a Rankin–Selberg integral realization, has meromorphic continuation and a completed functional equation, and contains the Riemann zeta function as an exact global factor. Nevertheless, because `L(s,sym^2 f)` is entire, **every nontrivial zero of `zeta(s)`, wherever it lies in the critical strip, is automatically a zero of `R_f(s)`**. Positivity of the coefficients and of the defining Rankin–Selberg integral for real `s>1` therefore imposes no critical-line localization on that inherited zeta divisor.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` for the route

`canonical automorphic self-correlation -> nonnegative exponent-lattice weights / positive Rankin-Selberg integral -> continuation + functional equation -> inherited zeta divisor forced to Re(s)=1/2`.

All Rankin–Selberg and symmetric-square ingredients are classical. The durable line-specific delta is that the most canonical global quadratic positivity escape left after `PL-251` is already too weak: it can coexist with the **entire unknown Riemann zero set as a direct factor**.

## 1. The quadratic weights live exactly on the exponent lattice

For `Re(s)>1`, Petridis–Risager record the standard normalized Rankin–Selberg series

`D_f(s)=sum_n lambda_f(n)^2 n^(-s)`

and, for level `q`, the relation

`D_f(s)=zeta_(q)(s) L(s,sym^2 f) / zeta_(q)(2s)`.

Taking level `q=1` removes all bad-prime bookkeeping and gives the formula above with ordinary Riemann zeta. Multiplying by

`zeta(2s)=sum_(d>=1) d^(-2s)`

and taking the Dirichlet convolution yields

`r_f(m)=sum_(d^2|m) lambda_f(m/d^2)^2`.

Every summand is a square, so `r_f(m)>=0`. Since both factors are multiplicative in the absolute-convergence region, `r_f` is multiplicative. In prime-exponent coordinates this means

`r_f(n)=product_p r_(f,p)(v_p(n))`,

with

`r_(f,p)(k)=sum_(0<=j<=floor(k/2)) lambda_f(p^(k-2j))^2 >= 0`.

The same exponent vector therefore carries the usual energy

`log n = sum_p v_p(n) log p`

and a canonical nonnegative automorphic occupation weight. This is not an invented lattice kernel: the weights come from the self-correlation of one automorphic eigenform.

The calculation also shows a limitation immediately relevant to the mandate. Despite being quadratic in the automorphic data, the resulting arithmetic weight is still Euler-factorizable across prime axes. The global positivity does not by itself create a nonseparable interaction between distinct prime coordinates.

## 2. The positivity is genuinely geometric, but only in the physical half-plane

Rankin–Selberg unfolding supplies more than coefficientwise positivity. For real `s>1`, the standard nonholomorphic Eisenstein series is positive pointwise and Petridis–Risager record

`integral_(Gamma_0(q)\H) E_infinity(z,s) y^2 |f(z)|^2 dmu(z)
 = Gamma(s+1)/(4pi)^(s+1) D_f(s)`.

At level one, every factor on the left is nonnegative and the integral is strictly positive for nonzero `f`. Thus the positivity has a source-derived automorphic/geometric realization rather than being a post-hoc choice of coefficients.

But this order structure is a statement for **real `s>1`**, where the integral and Dirichlet series are represented by positive quantities. Meromorphic continuation is an analytic identity, not a continuation of the inequality `D_f(s)>0` into complex `s`. In particular there is no meaningful complex sign that survives merely because the function was represented by a positive integral in its initial half-plane.

This distinction is exactly the analytic-continuation gate of the present research line. A positivity theorem that lives only where the Euler/Rankin–Selberg expansion converges cannot be promoted to critical-strip zero localization without an additional global Hermitian or test-function positivity statement.

## 3. Removing the harmless square factor exposes the exact Riemann divisor

The positive Dirichlet series `D_f` contains a denominator `zeta(2s)`, so one must not infer naively that every zero of `zeta(s)` is a zero of `D_f(s)` after meromorphic continuation. A simultaneous zero of the denominator could make such a statement invalid.

The correct matched object is therefore the standard full self-convolution factor

`R_f(s)=zeta(2s)D_f(s)=zeta(s)L(s,sym^2 f)`.

Gelbart–Jacquet prove the automorphy/analytic continuation of the degree-three symmetric-square lift of a cuspidal `GL(2)` representation; in particular the symmetric-square `L`-function in this level-one cuspidal case is entire. Hence the equality, first obtained in `Re(s)>1`, extends by uniqueness of meromorphic continuation. For every nontrivial Riemann zero `rho`,

`R_f(rho)=zeta(rho)L(rho,sym^2 f)=0`.

There is no cancellation issue here: the second factor is holomorphic at `rho`. It may itself vanish, which only increases the order of vanishing.

Consequently a hypothetical zero with `Re(rho) != 1/2` is perfectly compatible with this canonical positive self-correlation. It would simply appear as an off-line zero of `R_f` as well.

## 4. Functional-equation symmetry does not repair the positivity gap

Rankin–Selberg `L`-functions admit meromorphic continuation and a completed functional equation relating `s` and `1-s` (with the contragredient on the dual side). In the present self-dual level-one setting, the completed self-convolution therefore has the same central symmetry `Re(s)=1/2` that motivates the RH target.

But a functional equation supplies reflection symmetry, not localization. The factorization

`R_f(s)=zeta(s)L(s,sym^2 f)`

is fully compatible with reflected pairs of hypothetical off-line Riemann zeros. The nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients do not remove those pairs.

Modern zero-free-region work makes the boundary explicit from another angle. Humphries, with an appendix by Brumley, obtains standard de la Vallée Poussin-type zero-free regions for self-dual Rankin–Selberg `L`-functions. Such positivity-driven arguments control a neighborhood of `Re(s)=1`; they are not critical-line theorems. This is consistent with the `PL-137` audit, where prime-coordinate/Rankin–Selberg positivity supports PNT-edge exclusion but not an RH-strength mechanism.

## 5. Adversarial controls

1. **This does not rule out Weil positivity.** Weil's quadratic criterion is a positivity statement on a global test-function/divisor pairing and is equivalent to RH. It is categorically stronger than nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients or positivity of a Rankin–Selberg integral for real `s>1`.
2. **This does not say automorphic forms are irrelevant to RH.** A trace formula, period relation, relative trace formula, or geometric correspondence could impose a genuinely global sign or spectral constraint linking the factors. The present no-go concerns the ordinary positive self-convolution and its standard analytic continuation alone.
3. **The zeta factor is not manufactured by a denominator cancellation.** The object `R_f`, not merely `D_f`, is used for the inherited-zero claim. Its factorization has `zeta(s)` in the numerator and an entire symmetric-square companion.
4. **Level one is deliberate.** It removes ramified Euler factors and character complications. A single exact matched control is enough to disprove the general inference that canonical quadratic automorphic positivity plus functional-equation symmetry forces the zeta factor onto the critical line.
5. **Coefficient positivity is stronger here than a finite Artin direct sum but still insufficient.** `PL-251` used finite-dimensional semisimplicity to split the trivial constituent. Here the source is an infinite automorphic quadratic integral with nonnegative coefficients; the same principal-factor obstruction survives at global analytic level.
6. **No zero-statistical independence is claimed.** The statement is only logical: all possible Riemann zeros are inherited by `R_f`, while the classical positivity properties remain valid. No assertion is made about correlations between the zeta and symmetric-square zero sets.

The ingredients are standard and no mathematical novelty is claimed.

This is the main exact formula/integral anchor.

Used only as a modern matched control showing the scale naturally obtained from Rankin–Selberg positivity is a standard zero-free region at the `Re(s)=1` edge, not RH localization.

Internal duplication was checked against the recent `PL-249`–`PL-251` class-field/Artin sequence and against `PL-137`. `PL-251` explicitly left a global positivity theorem as a possible escape but did not test a positive automorphic self-correlation. `PL-137` uses Rankin–Selberg logarithmic derivatives inside a metric zero-free-region mechanism near `Re(s)=1`; it does not record the present exact counterexample in which a coefficient-positive, geometrically positive self-convolution contains the full unknown Riemann zero divisor as an entire companion factor. The present finding is therefore a route classification, not a claim of a new Rankin–Selberg identity.

## Research implication

After `PL-251`, “replace finite Artin functors by a canonical positive automorphic quadratic self-pairing” is no longer a live escape by itself. The Rankin–Selberg construction already gives a source-derived global quadratic object with nonnegative exponent-lattice weights, a positive geometric integral in the physical half-plane, meromorphic continuation, and central functional-equation symmetry, while accepting every hypothetical off-line Riemann zero unchanged through the factor `zeta(s)`.

A viable positivity route must therefore be **divisor-sensitive after continuation**, not merely coefficient-positive before continuation. In the language of this line, it needs a source-derived Hermitian/test-function form, trace identity, or spectral theorem whose positivity is defined on the analytically continued global object and whose failure can be tied specifically to an off-line zero. Anything weaker remains compatible with the exact Rankin–Selberg matched control above.
