# PL-217 — One-prime Bost–Connes projections make high-temperature scalar profiles programmable

## Claim

The scalar-temperature-profile escape left open by the Bost–Connes branch is already too flexible on the **finite algebraic core**. No passage to a norm-limit, nonalgebraic observable, or many-prime construction is needed to reproduce an arbitrary finite pattern — or uniformly approximate an arbitrary continuous pattern — along the real high-temperature KMS interval.

Let `S_n` be the Bost–Connes multiplicative isometries and

\[
E_n=S_nS_n^*.
\]

As used in `PL-211`, every `KMS_beta` state satisfies the exact relation

\[
\phi_\beta(E_n)=n^{-\beta},
\qquad \beta>0.
\]

Fix a single rational prime `p` and put `E_{p^0}=1`. Then

\[
\phi_\beta(E_{p^j})=p^{-j\beta}.
\]

Consequently every polynomial

\[
Q(x)=\sum_{j=0}^J c_jx^j
\]

is represented by the fixed algebraic observable

\[
X_Q=\sum_{j=0}^J c_jE_{p^j},
\]

with exact temperature profile

\[
\boxed{\phi_\beta(X_Q)=Q(p^{-\beta}).}
\]

This has two sharp consequences on every compact interval `I=[a,b]` with `0<a<b<=1`.

First, for any distinct temperatures `beta_1,...,beta_m in I` and arbitrary prescribed complex values `y_1,...,y_m`, there is one fixed finite algebraic observable `X` such that

\[
\boxed{\phi_{\beta_i}(X)=y_i\qquad(1\le i\le m).}
\]

Second, for every continuous `f:I->C` and every `epsilon>0`, there is one fixed finite algebraic observable `X` such that

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\beta\in I}|\phi_\beta(X)-f(\beta)|<\epsilon.
}
\]

For real-valued `f`, `X` may be chosen self-adjoint. Thus the scalar KMS profiles of finite algebraic observables generated by **one prime ray** are uniformly dense in `C(I)`. In particular, any finite set of desired values involving `beta=1/2` can be interpolated exactly without using a zeta zero, a functional equation, Möbius cancellation, an adelic completion, or any interaction between distinct primes.

This strengthens the programmability boundary of `PL-214`. That finding proves that every fixed finite high-temperature algebraic correlation is only a finite Dirichlet polynomial and leaves a richer `C*`-completion as a possible source of more complicated temperature dependence. The present result shows that **rich scalar dependence itself is not the missing ingredient**: arbitrary continuous profiles can already be approximated by one-prime finite algebraic controls. A viable Bost–Connes route must therefore obtain its content from a source-forced operator relation, positivity/trace identity, or canonical infinite object that survives matched controls; it cannot use the mere shape of `beta -> phi_beta(X)` for a freely selected observable as an RH selector.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/CONTROL + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## 1. Exact KMS monomials on one prime ray

The Bost–Connes time evolution satisfies

\[
\sigma_t(S_n)=n^{it}S_n.
\]

For an analytic isometry `S_n`, the KMS identity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_\beta(S_nS_n^*)
&=\phi_\beta\!\left(S_n^*\sigma_{i\beta}(S_n)\right)\\
&=n^{-\beta}\phi_\beta(S_n^*S_n)\\
&=n^{-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the projection formula already established and used in `PL-211`; no continuation of the Gibbs partition function is involved. It holds directly at real inverse temperature for every Bost–Connes KMS state.

Restrict now to the exponent-lattice ray

\[
0,e_p,2e_p,3e_p,\ldots .
\]

The corresponding range projections are `E_1,E_p,E_{p^2},...`, and their KMS expectations are the ordinary monomials in

\[
x_\beta=p^{-\beta}.
\]

Therefore finite linear combinations of this one coordinate already produce all polynomial functions of `x_beta`. No coupling between different prime directions enters the construction.

## 2. Exact interpolation at finitely many temperatures

Choose distinct `beta_1,...,beta_m>0` and set

\[
x_i=p^{-\beta_i}.
\]

The `x_i` are distinct because `beta -> p^{-beta}` is strictly monotone. Given arbitrary values `y_i`, ordinary Lagrange interpolation supplies a polynomial `Q` of degree at most `m-1` with

\[
Q(x_i)=y_i.
\]

The associated finite algebraic observable

\[
X_Q=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_jE_{p^j}
\]

then satisfies

\[
\phi_{\beta_i}(X_Q)=Q(x_i)=y_i
\]

exactly. If all `y_i` are real, the interpolating polynomial may be taken with real coefficients, and because every `E_{p^j}` is a projection, `X_Q=X_Q^*`.

This is a particularly strong matched control for any proposal based on a distinguished finite set of temperatures. For example, assigning a zero, a sign, or a special value at `beta=1/2` while prescribing unrelated values at finitely many other points can be done exactly using only the ray generated by one arbitrary prime.

## 3. Uniform density of scalar profiles on compact high-temperature intervals

Fix

\[
I=[a,b]\subset(0,1]
\]

with `a<b`, and let

\[
J=[p^{-b},p^{-a}]\subset(0,1).
\]

The map

\[
\beta\longmapsto x=p^{-\beta}
\]

is a homeomorphism from `I` onto `J`. For a continuous function `f:I->C`, define

\[
h(x)=f\!\left(-\frac{\log x}{\log p}\right),
\qquad x\in J.
\]

Then `h` is continuous on the compact real interval `J`. By the classical Weierstrass approximation theorem, for every `epsilon>0` there is a polynomial `Q` with

\[
\sup_{x\in J}|Q(x)-h(x)|<\epsilon.
\]

Using the observable `X_Q` from the previous section gives

\[
\sup_{\beta\in I}
|\phi_\beta(X_Q)-f(\beta)|
=
\sup_{x\in J}|Q(x)-h(x)|
<\epsilon.
\]

Thus one-prime algebraic KMS profiles are uniformly dense in the continuous scalar profiles on every compact subinterval of the high-temperature regime. The argument actually works on any compact interval of positive inverse temperatures on which the relevant KMS states are considered; the restriction to `b<=1` is made because the present research question concerns the unique high-temperature phase and the critical half.

The density statement is about **choosing an observable for a desired profile**. It is therefore a programmability control, not a positive construction.

## 4. What this does and does not rule out

The result blocks a specific but broad class of false selectors. If the proposed mechanism is merely that some fixed observable can be found whose scalar KMS expectation has a special value, zero, extremum, sign pattern, or continuous profile near `beta=1/2`, then that property has no arithmetic rigidity by itself. A one-prime algebraic observable can match arbitrary finite samples exactly and approximate arbitrary continuous real profiles uniformly.

Several stronger requirements are deliberately outside the claim.

**Source-forcing remains decisive.** A canonically specified arithmetic observable can still be meaningful. The present control says that its scalar profile is evidence only if the rule selecting that observable supplies additional structure that the one-prime interpolants do not share.

**Positivity and norm budgets are not preserved automatically.** For real target profiles the control observable is self-adjoint, but the interpolation/Weierstrass argument does not guarantee a positive contraction or a uniform operator-norm bound independent of approximation accuracy. A future mechanism with a source-forced positivity or norm constraint must be tested against controls satisfying that same constraint rather than against the unrestricted family here.

**Uniform profile approximation does not produce an arbitrary exact `C*`-limit.** The approximating observables depend on `epsilon`, and uniform convergence of their scalar expectation profiles does not imply that the observables are Cauchy in operator norm. The theorem therefore does not classify the image of the full Bost–Connes `C*`-algebra under the map `X -> (beta -> phi_beta(X))`.

**No complex-temperature continuation is asserted.** Each finite control has the elementary entire continuation `Q(p^{-s})`, but the approximation theorem is only on a compact interval of real inverse temperatures. It neither constructs nor approximates the Riemann zero divisor in a complex strip.

**The mechanism is not rational-prime specific.** The proof uses one isometric semigroup direction with energy `log p` and the KMS relation. Replacing the rational prime by any generic positive energy `lambda` would produce the same polynomial family in `e^{-beta lambda}`. This is exactly why scalar-profile flexibility cannot satisfy the line's rational-prime specificity control.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The arithmetic/dynamical inputs are classical Bost–Connes theory. The primary source already recorded as source 5 in `research/prime_lattice/SOURCES.md` is Jean-Benoît Bost and Alain Connes, “Hecke algebras, type III factors and phase transitions with spontaneous symmetry breaking in number theory,” *Selecta Mathematica, New Series* **1** (1995), 411–457, DOI `10.1007/BF01589495`. The time evolution, multiplicative isometries, and KMS structure come from that work.

The exact projection expectation `phi_beta(E_n)=n^{-beta}` is already persisted and audited in `PL-211`; `PL-214` already proves the finite-Dirichlet-polynomial collapse for arbitrary finite algebraic high-temperature correlations. The only additional analytic ingredient here is the classical polynomial interpolation/Weierstrass approximation theorem.

A targeted literature search around Bost–Connes KMS observables, temperature dependence, polynomial approximation, and Stone–Weierstrass did not expose a dedicated theorem that should be treated as a new arithmetic mechanism. Absence of such a search hit is not novelty evidence. The density statement is an elementary consequence of classical ingredients, and **no novelty claim is made**. Its durable value is as a line-specific matched control that sharpens what `PL-214` leaves open.

## 6. Adversarial checks

The argument has four essential inputs, each independently checkable.

First, `E_{p^j}` must be a fixed element of the Bost–Connes algebraic core rather than a temperature-dependent choice. It is: `E_{p^j}=S_{p^j}S_{p^j}^*` is a finite product of the standard generators and their adjoints.

Second, the KMS value must be exactly `p^{-j beta}` throughout the interval. This follows directly from the KMS relation and `S_n^*S_n=1`; it does not use uniqueness of the high-temperature state or analytic continuation.

Third, a single prime must separate distinct temperatures. Since `p^{-beta}` is strictly decreasing for `p>1`, the Vandermonde/Lagrange interpolation matrix is nonsingular for distinct temperatures.

Fourth, uniform approximation must not smuggle temperature dependence into the observable. The polynomial `Q` is chosen once for the prescribed function and tolerance; the resulting `X_Q` is then a single fixed algebraic element evaluated against the entire family of states.

A counterexample to any of these four inputs would invalidate or narrow the result. Constraints not present in the theorem — positivity, a fixed norm budget, canonical source selection, exact operator-norm limiting behavior, or complex analytic continuation — do not falsify it; they define stronger candidate mechanisms that must be audited separately.

## Consequence for the research line

`PL-214` correctly leaves the full `C*`-completion capable of richer scalar dependence on temperature than a single finite Dirichlet polynomial. But **scalar richness is no longer a plausible reason by itself to pursue that completion**. On every compact high-temperature interval, the finite one-prime algebraic core already approximates every continuous scalar profile.

The surviving Bost–Connes question is therefore narrower and better aligned with the line's current frontier. A genuinely useful infinite or same-sector construction must carry a **source-forced relation among prime channels, a positivity/trace identity, or another operator invariant that cannot be reproduced by one-prime polynomial interpolation**. If its only output is a distinguished graph of `beta -> phi_beta(X)` with a feature at `1/2`, the matched control above shows that the feature is programmable before any Riemann-specific geometry enters.
