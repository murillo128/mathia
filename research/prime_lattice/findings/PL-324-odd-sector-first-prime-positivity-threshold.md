# PL-324 — Odd-sector Beurling–Deny positivity survives exactly to the first prime event

**Evidence:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED + SOURCE-SPECIFIC-ROUTE-BOUNDARY`

**Status:** `EXACT-ODD-SECTOR-BEURLING-DENY-THRESHOLD + FIRST-PRIME-OBSTRUCTION + ARITHMETIC-SIGN-GATE-NARROWED`

## Claim

Let `Q_W^a` be Suzuki's closed localized Weil form on `(-a,a)`, and restrict it to the real odd subspace. Identify that subspace with `L^2(0,a)` by writing

\[
u(x)=f(x),\qquad u(-x)=-f(x),\qquad 0<x<a,
\]

and let the positive-cone modulus on the odd sector be

\[
u^{\#}(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)|u(x)|.
\]

Then the first Beurling--Deny modulus criterion for the restricted form has the exact threshold

\[
\boxed{
Q_W^a(u^{\#})\le Q_W^a(u)\quad\text{for every real odd }u
\iff
0<a\le \frac12\log 2.
}
\]

Equivalently, after the natural identification of the odd subspace with `L^2(0,a)`, the self-adjoint semigroup generated by the odd restriction is positivity preserving exactly through the first rational-prime event. At the endpoint `a=(1/2)log 2`, the `n=2` translation touches only at separation `2a` and has no positive-measure overlap, so the criterion still holds.

This is sharply different from the full-space criterion in `PL-294`, which already fails at

\[
a_{\rm BD}=0.140599787\ldots < \frac12\log 2.
\]

Odd parity therefore removes the purely archimedean Beurling--Deny obstruction all the way to the first prime-power lag. But once any prime-power lag is active in the genuinely cross-half regime, that arithmetic term has the wrong sign for the odd-sector modulus and destroys positivity preservation immediately.

This is a route boundary, not an RH proof. Suzuki records Yoshida's result that positivity of the Weil form on the odd class is already sufficient to force RH, so the odd sector is an RH-relevant restriction. The theorem here says only that the **standard positivity-preserving-semigroup mechanism** is available precisely up to the first prime event and cannot propagate through it.

## 1. Continuous odd-sector defect has the favorable sign at every aperture

The absolute-value calculation in `PL-294` isolates the continuous off-diagonal defect coefficient, in unscaled separation `t>0`, as

\[
F(t)
:=-r''(t)-\frac1{2t}
=
2\cosh(t/2)-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}.
\]

The important point for the odd sector is not the sign of `F` itself but its monotonicity. Set `x=e^{t/2}>1`. Then

\[
F(t)=x+x^{-1}-\frac{x^3}{x^4-1},
\]

and

\[
\frac{dF}{dx}
=
1-x^{-2}
+\frac{x^6+3x^2}{(x^4-1)^2}
>0.
\]

Thus `F` is strictly increasing on `(0,infinity)`.

For `x,y in (0,a)`, define

\[
D_f(x,y):=|f(x)f(y)|-f(x)f(y)\ge0.
\]

Pairing the two same-half contributions with the two opposite-half contributions gives the exact continuous odd-modulus defect

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm cont}(u^{\#})-Q_{\rm cont}(u)
=
2\int_0^a\!\int_0^a
\bigl[F(|x-y|)-F(x+y)\bigr]
D_f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.
}
\]

Because `x+y>|x-y|` for positive `x,y` and `F` is strictly increasing,

\[
F(|x-y|)-F(x+y)<0
\]

throughout the interior. Scalar and killing terms are unchanged by `u -> u#`. Therefore the entire archimedean part satisfies the odd-sector modulus inequality for **every** aperture `a>0`.

This is the mechanism that the full space cannot exploit. In the full cone, `PL-294` compares the kernel only at one separation and the coefficient eventually changes sign. In the odd cone, parity forces every same-half separation `|x-y|` to be paired against the larger opposite-half separation `x+y`; monotonicity of `F` then fixes the sign without any small-aperture assumption.

## 2. Before the first prime event there is no arithmetic obstruction

Suzuki's arithmetic contribution is a finite sum over prime-power lags

\[
\ell=\log n,
\qquad
w_\ell=\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}>0,
\]

with correlations of the form

\[
-2w_\ell C_\ell(u),
\qquad
C_\ell(u)
=
\int_{-a}^{a-\ell}u(x+\ell)u(x)\,dx,
\]

whenever `ell<2a`. The smallest possible lag is `log 2`. Hence for

\[
0<a\le\frac12\log2
\]

there is no positive-measure arithmetic overlap. At equality the `n=2` lag is exactly the diameter and contributes only at an endpoint of measure zero.

Combining this with the strict continuous inequality above proves

\[
Q_W^a(u^{\#})\le Q_W^a(u)
\]

on a form core throughout the pre-prime range. Closure of the restricted lower-bounded form then gives the first Beurling--Deny criterion on its full form domain, and therefore positivity preservation of the odd-sector semigroup after the usual harmless scalar shift.

No positivity-improving claim is needed here. The durable statement is only the exact positivity-preserving threshold.

## 3. Every aperture after the first prime event contains a wrong-sign cross-half lag

Let

\[
a>\frac12\log2.
\]

There is always a prime-power lag `ell` satisfying

\[
a<\ell<2a.
\]

Indeed:

- if `(1/2)log2<a<log2`, take `ell=log2`;
- if `a=log2`, take `ell=log3`;
- if `a>log2`, put `X=e^a>2` and choose the smallest power `2^k>X`. Then `X<2^k\le2X<X^2`, hence `a<k log2<2a`.

For such a lag, only opposite halves of an odd function meet. Writing `x=-y` in the correlation gives

\[
C_\ell(u)
=-\int_{\ell-a}^{a} f(\ell-y)f(y)\,dy.
\]

Under odd modulus,

\[
C_\ell(u^{\#})-C_\ell(u)
=-\int_{\ell-a}^{a}
D_f(\ell-y,y)\,dy
\le0.
\]

Because the Weil prime coefficient is `-2w_ell`, this produces the **opposite** sign from Beurling--Deny:

\[
\boxed{
P_\ell(u^{\#})-P_\ell(u)
=
2w_\ell
\int_{\ell-a}^{a}D_f(\ell-y,y)\,dy
\ge0.
}
\]

Thus the first active cross-half prime-power channel tries to increase, not decrease, the odd-modulus energy.

## 4. The wrong-sign prime term dominates a two-bump test

The preceding sign alone does not yet prove failure, because the continuous part remains favorable. A localized test separates their scaling.

Choose distinct points `y_0,z_0 in (ell-a,a)` with

\[
y_0+z_0=\ell,
\]

and choose `y_0` generically so that no other active prime-power lag connects the finite set of reflected bump centers. At fixed `a` there are only finitely many active lags, so only finitely many resonance equations must be avoided. Let `f_\varepsilon` be the sum of two smooth bumps of width `epsilon`, with opposite signs, centered at `y_0` and `z_0`; extend it oddly to `u_\varepsilon` on `(-a,a)`.

For sufficiently small `epsilon`, the selected lag `ell` pairs the two bumps. Its one-dimensional correlation defect is

\[
P_\ell(u_\varepsilon^{\#})-P_\ell(u_\varepsilon)
=c_\ell\,\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),
\qquad c_\ell>0.
\]

All other active prime-power correlations vanish after the nonresonant support choice. By contrast, the continuous defect only sees the Cartesian product of the two opposite-sign bump supports, so

\[
Q_{\rm cont}(u_\varepsilon^{\#})-Q_{\rm cont}(u_\varepsilon)
=O(\varepsilon^2).
\]

Therefore

\[
Q_W^a(u_\varepsilon^{\#})-Q_W^a(u_\varepsilon)
=c_\ell\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)>0
\]

for all sufficiently small `epsilon`. The first Beurling--Deny criterion fails for every `a>(1/2)log2`.

The construction is robust: the failure is caused by the codimension-one translation correlation, whose overlap scales like `epsilon`, while the favorable continuous interaction scales like the two-dimensional bump area `epsilon^2`.

## 5. Relation to the live arithmetic-sign gate

`PL-294` showed that ordinary full-space semigroup positivity dies before any prime can enter, so it could not distinguish the rational-prime source from an archimedean control. The odd restriction changes that conclusion in a useful and exact way:

\[
\boxed{
\text{archimedean odd sector: positivity preserving for all }a;
\qquad
\text{first rational-prime event: exact obstruction.}
}
\]

This is source-specific arithmetic sensitivity, but it is still a **negative route result**. It does not show that `Q_W^a` becomes indefinite when `a>(1/2)log2`; failure of the modulus contraction is much weaker than failure of quadratic-form positivity. It also does not constrain the sign of the actual lowest eigenfunction once the semigroup criterion is lost.

The live RH-facing burden therefore becomes sharper. Any attempt to exploit the RH-complete odd Weil sector beyond the first prime event must use a mechanism other than standard order positivity: for example a source-specific sign law for the relevant eigenstate, a comparison that keeps the signed prime correlations rather than taking modulus, or the pointwise boundary/flux structure developed in `PL-319`--`PL-323`.

## Novelty and adversarial audit

Literature input from Suzuki (source 56) consists of the localized Weil form, its parity invariance, the explicit archimedean kernel, the prime-power translation terms, the self-adjoint realization, and the discussion of Yoshida's odd-test-function criterion. Classical Beurling--Deny theory supplies the equivalence between the modulus inequality and positivity preservation for the lower-bounded restricted form after a scalar shift.

The line-local exact derivation is the parity-paired defect identity, strict monotonicity of `F`, the identification of `(1/2)log2` as the exact endpoint, and the post-threshold two-bump construction in which a one-dimensional prime correlation dominates the two-dimensional continuous defect.

A targeted literature audit around Suzuki's localized operator, odd/parity sectors, positivity-preserving semigroups, Beurling--Deny criteria, and the first-prime event did not locate this exact threshold theorem. Recent parity-polarized/event-cell work is adjacent, so no broad originality claim is made; the finding is classified `EXACT-DERIVED` and should be read as a sharp route boundary extracted from Suzuki's formulas.

Adversarial checks:

- **Not Weil positivity.** `Q_W^a(u#)<=Q_W^a(u)` is an order/semigroup property. Its failure does not imply a negative Weil eigenvalue or a zero off the critical line.
- **Endpoint is included.** At `2a=log2` the prime translation has zero-measure overlap, while the continuous paired kernel retains the favorable sign.
- **Odd modulus is the correct cone operation.** Ordinary `|u|` leaves the odd subspace; `u#=sgn(x)|u(x)|` is exactly the modulus after identifying odd functions with `L^2(0,a)`.
- **Continuous sign is aperture-independent.** The proof uses `F(|x-y|)<F(x+y)`, not the sign of `F`; it therefore does not inherit the plastic-constant threshold from `PL-294`.
- **A prime term really dominates.** The post-threshold counterexample is localized so the selected translation contributes at order `epsilon`, the smooth/continuous cross interaction at order `epsilon^2`, and all other finitely many prime lags are avoided.
- **No hidden Euler-product continuation.** The argument uses the finite von-Mangoldt source already present in the compactly supported Weil form; it does not continue an Euler product into the critical strip.

The durable redirect is precise: **parity repairs the generic archimedean semigroup obstruction, but standard order positivity then fails exactly when rational-prime arithmetic first becomes active. The remaining RH mechanism must control signed arithmetic interaction rather than replace it by a positive cone.**
