# Calibration class and randomized stopping

This projects the explicit AF-to-Jacobi transfer in XF-204 and the function-class boundary in [[research/mind/intuition/MI-039-calibration-gluing-transfers-randomized-stopping-bounds-only-in-the-admitted-function-class|global MI-039]]. The common mechanism has different admissibility proofs and noise assumptions in the two lines.

## A fixed pair before the seed

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-575-unknown-calibration-slack-requires-unbounded-sampling-depth|AF-575]] supplies finite exact stopping on each noiseless input with unknown strictly subcritical derivative calibration. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-576-randomized-calibration-recovery-has-a-sharp-error-stopping-tail|AF-576]], equations (6)–(15), upgrades its finite-prefix obstruction to a randomized theorem: two fixed admissible inputs agree with the midpoint outside a shrinking interval near the excluded endpoint. For any capped adaptive policy, the probability of entering that interval tends to zero. The pair is selected after the policy and cap, before its seed.

[[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-204-randomized-jacobi-detection-has-a-sharp-error-stopping-tail|XF-204]], equations (7)–(17), explicitly transfers that coupling question but must preserve the queryable endpoint of a complex response. Its convex-velocity join matches both the endpoint value and the tail's value and derivative while retaining strict derivative slack. [[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-201-vanishing-jacobi-calibration-slack-has-quartic-row-cost|XF-201]], equations (6)–(11), supplies the strict speed decrease for the complete response, including the reciprocal term. [[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-203-unknown-jacobi-calibration-slack-admits-finite-certified-stopping|XF-203]], equations (8)–(12), supplies the pointwise finite certificate under one known total squared-noise budget.

In each binary experiment the capped minimax error is \(1/2\). Truncation and fair guessing give the sharp worst-case stopping tail \(1-2\alpha\) for error allowance \(\alpha<1/2\), despite finite cost at every input for an attaining policy. The worst-case input may change with the cap. These are explicit applications of [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-le-cam-two-point-testing|classical two-point testing]], not a new general testing principle.

## Polynomial calibration changes the experiment

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-577-unknown-polynomial-calibration-separates-continuous-randomness-from-finite-certification|AF-577]], equations (2)–(6), assumes a known finite family of real-analytic sources with nonpolynomial pairwise gaps and one polynomial nuisance of unknown finite, unbounded degree. Every finite deterministic transcript has another-label extension. Yet one continuously random exact validation coordinate, independent of all fitted interpolants, rejects every false analytic residual almost surely for each fixed input; degree \(d\) needs at most \(d+2\) observations, taking \(d=0\) for zero calibration. The interpolation-and-validation idea has [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-polynomial-interpolation-early-termination|classical early-termination prior art]].

AF-577, equations (6a)–(6d), now gives the sharp finite-bit return/error law. For \(m\) labels, let \(e\) be the worst-case unconditional probability of returning a wrong label. On every fixed input \(y\),
\[
e\ge(1-1/m)\Pr_y(\text{return}).
\]
A finite collection of returning coin words has a finite union of queried coordinates. One polynomial per competing label matches all of those transcripts simultaneously, before the seed is drawn. Exhaustion of the returning cylinders proves the bound without a uniform or expected resource cap. Almost-sure return therefore has minimax error \(1-1/m\), attained by blind guessing, including under a fixed query budget. The distinction from AF-576 persists: arbitrary simultaneous interpolation need not preserve a derivative bound, and smooth gluing need not preserve polynomial calibration. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/mind/intuition/MI-079-unknown-polynomial-calibration-separates-random-identification-from-certification|AF MI-079]] retains these quantifiers.

## Continuous randomness still has a fixed-budget polynomial barrier

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-578-continuous-randomness-retains-fixed-budget-polynomial-calibration-obstruction|AF-578]], equations (2)–(10), closes AF-577's fixed-cap question under bounded source derivatives through order \(N-1\) on the closed bounded window. With \(m\) labels, every cap \(N\ge1\) has minimax error \(c_m=1-1/m\), already for degree-at-most-\(N-1\) calibration with unrestricted coefficients. A smooth source gap has a uniformly bounded monomial interpolation lift, including at arbitrarily close nodes. One broad Gaussian coefficient prior then gives transcript total variation at most \(B_N(g)/(2L)\), uniformly over adaptive policies; its scale \(L\) may grow because coefficients are unbounded. This is uncertainty over fixed calibrations, not measurement noise.

For sources smooth on the closed window, truncation gives \(e+c_mq_N\ge c_m\), with worst-case unconditional wrong-return probability \(e\) and \(q_N=\sup_x\Pr_x(T>N)\). Analytic nonpolynomial gaps permit the matching per-input exact continuous-validation branch from AF-577. With known degree \(d\), the exact continuous-query threshold is \(d+1\) queries at blind-guess minimax error versus \(d+2\) for almost-sure zero error. AF-571's implicit equation verifies the prime-edit source's endpoint regularity. AF-578 alone does not cover arbitrary endpoint-singular analytic gaps, bounded coefficients, or an unbounded observation window. AF-579 verifies a separate ray extension below.

## The whole ray separates full-curve margin from finite-query recovery

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-579-unbounded-query-locations-preserve-polynomial-budget-obstruction|AF-579]], equations (5)–(13), extends AF-578 by a cap-dependent [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-polynomial-homogenization-linear-fractional-action|classical polynomial coordinate action]]. For n=N−1, subtract a known reference source, set a=x/(1−x), and multiply responses by (1−x)^n. The nuisance space of degree at most n maps bijectively onto itself. The transformed gaps must extend to C^n[0,1]; the finding verifies this using AF-571's analytic germ at zero and exponential decay of every fixed-order prime-gap derivative at infinity. Every finite ray query has an invertible exact recoding. This preserves the capped error c_m and sharp noiseless stopping law; it neither queries infinity nor preserves the unweighted noise norm.

AF-579, equations (3) and (14), separately proves a positive full-ray noise radius d(S)/4 for a fixed finite prime-edit candidate set. A bounded gap can be uniformly approximated on the ray by a polynomial only as well as by a constant, since every nonconstant polynomial is unbounded. This recovers AF-568's full-curve constant-offset radius, without its finite-query guarantee. The rational control 0 and a/(1+a)^2 has the same separation: radius 1/16, yet blind-guess error at every finite cap. The bounded-window zero-radius conclusion remains valid in its original domain. No noisy variable-stopping theorem or uniform margin across growing candidate families is asserted.

## Positive bounded error destroys polynomial finite stopping

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-580-positive-noise-destroys-finite-stopping-under-unknown-polynomial-calibration|AF-580]], equations (3)–(10), closes the positive-noise variable-stopping question left open by AF-579. For continuous sources on the ray, unrestricted finite-degree polynomial calibration and one fixed Borel error function of supremum norm at most ε>0, worst-case unconditional wrong-return probability satisfies e_ε ≥ c_m Pr_x(return) at every strict-slack input and every continuous-error input. Arbitrary discontinuous noise-saturated inputs are outside that pointwise assertion. No query cap, finite-bit restriction or expected-cost bound is imposed. Almost-sure finite return on even one noiseless input forces worst-case error at least c_m over the noisy class; blind guessing attains the bound.

[[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-weierstrass-polynomial-approximation|Classical compact-interval polynomial approximation]] supplies one polynomial per label approximating a source gap on a larger window. A smooth cutoff turns the residual into bounded error, giving exact agreement with the reference observation on K_R=[1/R,R]. For continuous reference error, approximating the gap plus that error removes the need for strict slack. Every returning execution stays in some K_R. Shared-seed coupling and exhaustion therefore give the pointwise return bound, with the competing inputs fixed before the seed at each R. For smooth sources against a noiseless reference, smooth compactly supported errors suffice; analytic errors and common derivative/support bounds are not covered. [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-randomized-integration-unsolvability-on-unrestricted-smooth-functions|Kunsch–Novak–Rudolf's smooth-integration obstruction]] is cited prior art for the general hidden-perturbation mechanism, not a substitute for this finite-label construction.

AF-579's full-ray radius d(S)/4 survives: for 0<ε<d(S)/4 the entire prime-edit observation curve still identifies its label uniquely. AF-580 proves that finite adaptive point observations cannot supply the corresponding uniformly reliable stopping rule. The rational sources j a/(1+a)^2 have the same distinction with radius 1/16. At zero noise, AF-577's independent exact continuous validation still returns on each input. These statements concern different noise allowances, not a contradiction or an ordinary-prime/RH conclusion.

Global MI-039 explicitly contrasts this with XF-203. Its n chord pairs of length h with distinct endpoints have coefficient norms ‖c‖₂=h⁻¹√(2/n) and ‖c‖₁=2/h. Xi's one total squared-noise budget permits its error bound to decrease with n; AF's fixed-function supremum bound does not. A compactly supported error need not have finite total squared energy along infinitely many queries within its support. The nuisance restrictions and source margins also differ, so changing only the noise norm does not transfer either theorem.

## A supplied degree restores finite noisy acquisition

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-581-known-polynomial-degree-restores-sharp-full-ray-adaptive-recovery|AF-581]], equations (4)–(6) and (10)–(18), separates the known-degree experiment from AF-580's unrestricted-degree class. For each supplied finite degree r, one shared polynomial in Π_r, unrestricted finite positive query locations and absolute bounded error, r+3 deterministic adaptive values attain every strict allowance below d(S)/4 for every sufficiently remote finite prime-edit candidate set. This extends [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-568-free-output-offset-costs-third-adaptive-prime-edit-probe|AF-568]] and [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-569-affine-output-drift-forces-four-adaptive-prime-edit-probes|AF-569]] beyond constant and affine drift. The full-curve radius remains d(S)/4 even at unknown degree; the new promise changes acquisition, not that global distance.

The r+1 anchors L,R,2R,…,rR reproduce the polynomial exactly. At each of the finitely many informative coordinates, their Lagrange weights tend to (1,0,…,0) as R grows with L fixed. The absolute weight sum Λ tends to one, so the contrast noise is bounded by ε(1+Λ), approaching 2ε. Taking L small removes the remaining source-gap correction. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-563-universal-sharp-one-sample-ratio-for-discrete-prime-edits|AF-563]] supplies a locator with minimum adjacent gap greater than d(S)/2 for all sufficiently remote finite sets. It leaves at most two adjacent compatible labels; one pair-specific near-peak query separates them. Reused-anchor errors may be correlated: the argument uses interval exclusions, not averaging or independence.

With only r+2 values, adaptivity equals fixed design for every finite candidate set. AF-581's zero-mass, total-variation-one signed-measure certificate controls every polynomial-annihilating contrast, including coalescing or escaping coordinates. The [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-562-multiway-prime-edit-one-sample-locality-and-two-scale-obstruction|AF-562]] four-prime families give the sharp asymptotic fraction ρ=(8e/9)log(3/2)<1 of the full margin, for each fixed r. The non-arithmetic family (0,f,3f+2g,3f+3g), f(x)=xe^(1−x), g(x)=f(3x/2), realizes the same strict gap exactly. [[research/arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-571-polynomial-drift-has-exact-small-window-probe-saturation|AF-571]] instead saturates the diminished small-window quotient margin with r+2 fixed values; its domain and denominator differ.

[[research/arithmetic_fidelity/mind/intuition/MI-080-escaping-anchors-cancel-known-degree-at-constant-noise-cost|AF MI-080]] retains this mechanism. Polynomial reproduction is the distinct-node case of [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-hermite-polynomial-interpolation|classical interpolation]]; it alone supplies neither the anchor noise bound nor the sharp adaptive budget. Fix the finite source set, degree and strict noise slack before choosing finite anchors. No uniform coordinate bound, finite-precision cost, relative-error result, endpoint recovery, randomized minimax classification or ordinary-prime/RH discriminator follows. The supplied degree prevents AF-580's unrestricted polynomial matching; it does not refute that obstruction or transfer Xi's different nuisance and noise model.

## Positive noise retains the analytic Xi obstruction

[[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-205-analytic-jacobi-calibration-retains-the-noisy-stopping-obstruction|XF-205]], equations (7)–(12), supplies a different witness within baselines holomorphic on a fixed right half-plane. For a minimizing complete response \(F\), the scalar baselines \(\pm(1-\varepsilon)F/2\) have strictly subcritical derivative norm and yield opposite-label curves differing by \(\varepsilon F\). For each fixed row cap \(N\) and **positive** total-noise allowance \(\sigma\), priors on fixed bounded-energy noise sequences give
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,\varepsilon}^{\mathcal A},P_{1,\varepsilon}^{\mathcal A})
\le\min\{1,N^{3/2}\varepsilon\|F\|_\infty/\sigma\}.
\]
The sequences are chosen independently of the policy's seed. Sending \(\varepsilon\) to zero at fixed \(N,\sigma>0\) recovers capped error \(1/2\) and stopping tail \(1-2\alpha\). The exponent is a sufficient coupling estimate, not a sharp slack-dependent rate. XF-203's finite certificate remains valid on this subclass.

This extends the unknown-slack stopping obstruction without using XF-204's nonanalytic tail join. [[research/xi_flow/mind/intuition/MI-014-endpoint-preserving-calibration-joins-force-sharp-randomized-stopping-tails|Xi MI-014]] and [[research/xi_flow/mind/intuition/MI-015-positive-noise-retains-analytic-calibration-stopping-obstruction|Xi MI-015]] retain the two different witnesses. In the restricted known-direction model \(B=cF\), \(|c|<1/2\), a single noiseless query at a nonzero source value determines the label. XF-205 alone therefore does not settle the noiseless full analytic class; XF-206 supplies a different witness below.

## Noiseless holomorphic Xi requires additional polynomial directions

[[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-206-noiseless-analytic-calibration-retains-sharp-stopping-tails|XF-206]], equations (7)–(20), closes the full-class noiseless boundary. For the minimizing complete source, XF-201's strict speed decrease supplies \(v=|F'|\), \(v(Q)=D\), and \(\kappa=\min(-v')>0\). At cap \(N\), the polynomial space spanned by \(1,s^2,\ldots,s^{2N-2}\), \(s=(r-Q)/Q\), has full evaluation rank on every at-most-\(N\) distinct real node bank, including the endpoint. Every polynomial in this space has first derivative zero at that endpoint. The coefficient region \(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}2k|a_k|\le\kappa Q^2/4\) leaves strictly subcritical derivative room when added to the two scalar baselines from XF-205, independently of their shrinking gap parameter \(\varepsilon\).

A small Gaussian coefficient prior has arbitrarily high mass inside that region. For each fixed policy, common-history conditional Gaussian continuity gives convergence of the capped transcript laws as \(\varepsilon\to0\); conditioning the prior costs at most \(\eta\) per label. The bound is \(2\eta+o_{\varepsilon\to0}(1)\), with the positive Gaussian scale fixed before taking the gap limit. There is no policy-uniform rate or uniform lower conditional variance. Each supported polynomial is fixed before the seed. No two analytic curves are asserted to agree on an interval.

The capped error is \(1/2\) and the sharp stopping tail is \(1-2\alpha\), now for every total-noise allowance \(\sigma\ge0\); XF-203 supplies the separate finite per-input certificate. This does not extend the noiseless claim to the restricted supplied scalar class, which retains its one-query test. The affine control has \(\kappa=0\) and is distinguished by two endpoint values. A supplied slack floor, derivative oracle or prescribed smaller analytic class changes the experiment.

AF-578 and XF-206 explicitly use [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-gaussian-conditioning-adaptive-linear-information|classical Gaussian conditioning under adaptive linear information]]. Global MI-039 documents their precise correspondence: AF permits broad coefficient uncertainty and a uniform smooth-gap interpolation bound; Xi requires endpoint-flat uncertainty with bounded nonconstant coefficients and a shrinking source gap, with only fixed-policy convergence. Their shared prior art alone would not establish this bridge. The findings prove their own source admissibility and testing limits; the classical linear-approximation results are not substituted for discrete-label recovery.

## Complex values still leave the endpoint derivative unobserved

[[research/xi_flow/findings/XF-207-complex-value-queries-retain-the-analytic-calibration-stopping-obstruction|XF-207]], equations (7)–(17), extends the noiseless result to arbitrary finite distinct queries in the right half-plane. XF-206's even-power space fails at reflected nodes. Instead use polynomials of degree at most N+1 with P′(0)=0 in s=(z−Q)/Q. A [[research/prior_art/incremental/PA-hermite-polynomial-interpolation|classical Hermite interpolation]] correction preserves any at-most-N complex values while enforcing that derivative condition, including when the endpoint is queried. The coefficient constraint Σ_{k=2}^{N+1} k|a_k| ≤ κQ²/4 preserves the full source's real-interval derivative budget.

A supplied compact bound ‖B‖_K ≤ M also admits this construction when M > ‖F‖_K/2; both K and M are fixed, and queries need not lie in K. A small Gaussian prior fits that positive coefficient room before the label gap shrinks. The transcript limit remains policywise 2η + o(1), with no uniform rate over unbounded or clustered query locations. Capped error 1/2 and the sharp tail 1−2α survive. XF-203's real-axis certificate remains an allowed procedure and terminates on each input.

An exact endpoint derivative, or a supplied exact Cauchy integral returning it, distinguishes the labels in one call. Finite contour samples only approximate that information; a compact analytic bound need not supply a positive label margin. A bound excluding the midpoint, a positive slack floor, or a different global analytic restriction remains outside this result. Global MI-039 explicitly compares the two domain extensions: AF uses broad coefficients and a uniform transformed interpolation bound; Xi uses bounded coefficient room and a shrinking gap. This is a source-backed comparison, not a transfer of their noise norms or source laws.

## Limits of the bridge

The AF derivative slack convention is twice the Xi convention after identifying the critical derivative magnitude. Global MI-039 explicitly compares the observation closures: bounded-window AF-577 permits whole-curve aliases under supremum-bounded errors; ray-domain AF-580 instead matches each compact window while preserving full-curve separation below AF-579's radius. XF-205 uses a total-energy constraint to obtain near-indistinguishable finite transcript laws, while XF-203 retains pointwise exact stopping. These noise conclusions are not interchangeable. Neither line supplies physical Xi access, ordinary-prime identification, or an RH implication. There is no proved classification of all analytic or quasianalytic calibration classes. The direct AF-576-to-XF-204 relation is supported by XF-204's explicit transfer passage, independently of their shared prior-art citation.
