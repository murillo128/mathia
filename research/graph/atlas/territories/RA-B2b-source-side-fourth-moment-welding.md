Mikawa pair-in-progressions dispersion remains a source-backed conditioned input, and the exact divisor-martingale structure across conductors remains real. But the obvious ways of turning those facts into the Yang welding estimate are now much more tightly constrained. Redundant common refinements still incur super-polylogarithmic cost, and opening the residue `L^2` norm raises the arithmetic object to a four-prime rectangle rather than merely improving pair-level bookkeeping.

The temporary WI-067 claim that Shao--Teräväinen directly solved the fixed-shift rectangle was withdrawn after adversarial review and is not current evidence. Its corrected successor WI-068 shows that the available finite-complexity theorem controls a free-shift average, not the booked fixed-shift slice. WI-069 and WI-070 then follow the two natural attempts to remove that slice: freeing the physical shifts produces a sparse power-index slope selector, while aggregating the changing bases produces a multivariate degree-two polynomial prime-pattern problem outside the quantitative one-variable MTW 2026 theorem and still carrying source-specific weights and anisotropic ranges.

The territory remains `open`. The needed result is now a source-faithful collective estimate for the actual weighted changing-slope/four-prime family, or a special identity that avoids the full residue norm, with sufficient quantitative control for the Yang source ledger. Existing diagonal/refinement assembly, a free-shift theorem without the slope selector, and the printed one-variable polynomial-pattern theorem do not currently close that gate. The Yang--Yang candidate remains neither certified nor refuted.
