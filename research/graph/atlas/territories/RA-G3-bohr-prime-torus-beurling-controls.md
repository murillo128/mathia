The finite-horizon audit now reaches through the formerly unresolved mean-gap prime-support bulk. Below the macroscopic scale, bounded prime clusters force unstable spectral extremes. At `T~X/log X`, the second moment, every fixed trace moment and finally the full empirical bulk law reduce, under their stated local Hardy--Littlewood hierarchy, to the corresponding Poisson sinc process rather than a new RH-sensitive phase. These are conditional routing statements and do not establish the Hardy--Littlewood inputs themselves.

The most natural von-Mangoldt weighting also fails to create a new ordinary bulk variable in this setting. On prime support it is asymptotically a deterministic macroscopic grading, and on the full prime-power shell the higher-depth indices form a vanishing-rank sector for the ordinary dimension-normalized empirical law. Exponent depth remains real pointwise information; this particular bulk statistic averages it away.

The territory remains `open`, but no longer because the ordinary critical bulk law or prime-only half-weight is unclassified. Live source-backed possibilities are concentrated in rare-sector/hard-edge and determinant statistics, growing-order moments, depth-conditioned or canonically amplified prime-power observables, target-relative/Nyman structures, and completed explicit-formula geometry whose normalization does not reduce to ordinary matrix population mass. These refinements do not justify an atlas state or mass change.
