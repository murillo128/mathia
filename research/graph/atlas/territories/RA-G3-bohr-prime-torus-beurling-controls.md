The finite-horizon and growing-lag audit now redirects a broad family of obvious escapes: fixed lags to Mirsky/Chowla/Hardy--Littlewood, smoothed collective lags to pair correlation/short-interval variance, sharp local and full-integer Gram spectra to prolate/locally-Toeplitz sampling laws, and the first determinant transition to Nyquist/Ingham density. Prime support removes the integer-band bulk phase for `T >> X/log X`, while bounded gaps leave an extreme-edge effect and the first unresolved bulk scale at the mean-gap threshold `T~X/log X`.

The territory remains `open` for the exact mean-gap-scale prime-support regime, weighted von-Mangoldt/half-weight observables, target-relative/Nyman structures, and any invariant forced by the full rational-prime norm completion that survives comparably strong generalized-prime controls. These refinements do not justify an atlas state or mass change.
