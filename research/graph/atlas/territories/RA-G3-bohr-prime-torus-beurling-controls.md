Near-Nyquist trace norm no longer supplies a raw arithmetic discriminator. Deterministic midpoint clouds attain the same continuum comparator at exact Nyquist and along every approach `r_N->1`; the prime cloud's remaining critical `S_1` excess and raw log-determinant decay are already consequences of the generic Poisson sampling bulk under the stated local Hardy--Littlewood hierarchy.

A separate local-geometry enrichment also classicalizes. Bruhat--Tits radial geometry singles out `Re(s)=1/2` as universal local temperedness, and an Ihara quotient turns the same half-axis into the graph Ramanujan bound. Abstract regular trees and Ramanujan graph families reproduce those mechanisms without rational-prime/local-field specificity, so an additional global coupling to the ordinary Riemann divisor is indispensable.

PL-108 adds a real positive bridge inside the scalar-energy sector. The energy-ball Bohr amplitude is exactly `sum_{n<=x} n^{it}`, and a new inverse theorem in the literature shows that sufficiently large coherence forces many ordinary zeta zeros near the same height. The result is not a programmable readout, but its phase and cutoff still factor through `log n`, and its analytic zero-forcing occurs near `Re(s)=1`; it therefore detects zero structure without supplying a multidimensional exponent-lattice mechanism that localizes the divisor on the critical line.

The territory remains `open`. A surviving stronger route must use information beyond the already powerful scalar-energy sum—microscopic/hard-edge inverse statistics, genuinely coordinate-sensitive exponent relations, target-relative or singular observables, completed indefinite Weil data, or another source-specific global coupling that changes zero location rather than only detecting/excluding zeros near the analytic edge.
