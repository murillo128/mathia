## Global discriminator: identify the exact resource that the matched control cannot fake

The current frontier is a classification of **source-specific coupling consumed by the destination at its own moving scale and regularity**. Recent deltas sharpen that ledger in orthogonal ways: AF-382 distinguishes relation order from local generator count; NB-166 distinguishes total variation from transport-aware analytic cancellation; FD-157 distinguishes parent multiplicity from cross-core leakage; MC-333 distinguishes low rank from the energy of a matched filter; RE-167 distinguishes a source envelope from the spatial support class that determines its capacity; VIS-256 distinguishes polynomial moment count from nonanalytic destination regularity; WI-320 distinguishes representation complexity from genuinely stronger information; WP-333 distinguishes scalar-law convergence from map-level coupling control.

For every proposed route ask: what object reaches the destination, under what norm/topology/source class and moving scale; what conserved fibre or matched geometry must actually be crossed; whether the retained tests are merely numerous or generated by a much smaller family; whether source regularity is used before cancellation is destroyed by absolute values; whether a quantitative source bound is local-shell or remote-tail; whether a joint class excludes a cheap target-matched filter in the theorem's own norm; whether moment constraints are dual to the destination's real regularity; whether enriched readouts are controlled at map level rather than only through scalar laws; and whether localization imports new information rather than recycling the same premise. The surviving theorem must expose a resource that the destination uses and a matched control cannot reproduce by completion, decoding, source-fibre-preserving rewiring, phase matching, moment matching, thin-tail martingales, universal positivity or classical recoding.
