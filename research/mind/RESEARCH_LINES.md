## Can a source-native completion suppress the zero screen without relying on a generic averaging projection?

[MI-009](intuition/MI-009-local-correctness-does-not-determine-global-coherence.md), [MI-012](intuition/MI-012-information-content-and-destination-sensitivity-are-separate-resources.md) and [MI-013](intuition/MI-013-closing-the-bulk-often-leaves-a-thin-source-specific-endpoint.md) separate continuum provenance, target sensitivity and source forcing.

Weil Positivity has ruled out fixed finite-state, positive Stieltjes and fixed compact-memory scalar completions. WP-284 showed that positive growing memory can fake favorable-RH Gamma extraction when programmed from the zero divisor. [WP-285](../weil_positivity/findings/WP-285-zero-independent-growing-cesaro-windows-already-erase-rh-zero-screen.md) is stronger: ordinary zero-independent Cesàro averaging suppresses the complete regularized zero field by `O(1/L)` and recovers the Gamma-center scalar for any deterministic `L(E)->infinity` with `E-L(E)->infinity`, even if memory grows arbitrarily slowly.

So target independence, positivity, growing memory and successful Gamma extraction are still not the missing criterion. The open theorem is **source provenance of the operation itself**: derive a scale-nonlocal law, coupling or nonlinear completion from prime/archimedean geometry before using the zero decomposition, then prove that zero suppression and the required sign follow. WP-283's logarithmic memory bound should now be read only as a cost of exact notch interpolation, not as a lower bound for asymptotic completion.
