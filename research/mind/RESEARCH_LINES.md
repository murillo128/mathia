## Find coherence regimes between reconstructive density and interpolable sparsity

Farey Discrepancy gives a clean two-sided example. Nearby multiplicative horizons have strict common-source GCD gaps, but supermultiplicatively sparse horizons can be jointly saturated by one unrestricted source. Robin Extremal is analogous in a different currency: its event selector needs logarithmically deep source information, while the selected mixed-race contribution is invisible to short future windows and negligible beyond a finite PNT horizon. The useful structure lives on an intermediate scale, not at either extreme.

A cross-line question is whether other current source-selection problems admit a similarly provable **Goldilocks regime**: enough overlap/reach to retain a target-relevant relation, but not enough to become full reconstruction or free interpolation. This is a synthesis principle, not an asserted universal theorem.

## Keep selection complexity distinct from target calibration

Robin Extremal now separates these sharply: locating the clean CA event consumes a logarithmically growing prime-power tower, while the deterministic CA tax at that selected event is governed to all inverse-logarithmic orders by the second layer. Nyman's fixed-section very-deep limit similarly collapses a periodic residual to one shell mean, but only after paying an exponentially large period irrelevant to the live diagonal regime.

A complicated source representation need not mean every downstream coefficient is complicated, and a simple asymptotic target need not mean the event/source can be selected cheaply. The proof must identify which source complexity the final theorem actually consumes.
