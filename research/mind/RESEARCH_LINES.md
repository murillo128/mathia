# Global Mathia research lines

This file holds genuinely cross-branch mathematical questions suggested by the durable Mathia intuitions. It is not a summary of the local minds, a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history. Lines should survive only while they discriminate among program-level mechanisms.

## Characterize the minimal information-preserving quotient and the correct order of operations

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-preserve-structure-before-compressing`.

For a candidate chain from arithmetic data through geometry and operators to spectral, positivity, or zeta data, determine the coarsest equivalence relation through which the desired arithmetic distinction actually factors **before** optimizing, diagonalizing, taking a determinant, or proving positivity. Full injectivity is unnecessary, but every early quotient must preserve the variable later claimed to carry the RH signal.

The decisive object is the fiber/invariance structure of each canonical transformation. Current examples include pointed circulant classicalization, path-independent Schur refinement, low-rank old/new shell defects, inner factors invisible to Gram data, the exact single-profile Gram-defect countermodels of Weil Inertia, and Hodge/information-geometric positive quotients that erase a signed Mangoldt selector. A candidate fails this line when the target distinction is already constant on one of those fibers; better optimization downstream cannot repair that loss.

## Distinguish local relational memory from exact global arithmetic placement

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-002-the-surviving-arithmetic-variable-is-relational`.

Resolved multi-gap eigenvalues show that directed relation-between-relation memory can survive local geometric spectralization. But all-composite prime-flute clones and Beurling prime-lattice controls show that relational richness alone does not imply arithmetic specificity. Determine what global normalization, target, norm assignment, or completion couples such local memory to the exact rational-prime system rather than to a large equivalence class of matched controls.

A decisive mechanism should pass a two-stage test: it must first retain the ordered local variable after natural gauge reduction, and then distinguish a control that preserves that local structure while changing the global arithmetic source. A feature that passes only the first test is a useful coordinate, not yet an RH selector.

## Make matched controls as strong as the claimed information layer

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-003-arithmetic-significance-begins-after-universal-control-subtraction`.

Do not stop at regular lattices when a proposal uses deeper structure. Use geometric all-composite clones for tail invariants, representation-matched periodic/off-lattice controls for collapsed Gram mechanisms, finite-prefix Beurling controls for exponent-lattice mechanisms, and completion/sign-matched controls when the proposed theorem is global. The mathematical goal is to identify the first control depth at which an invariant changes and verify that this depth matches the mechanism's claimed source of arithmetic information.

A canonical relative/subtracted object is especially valuable when the reference is independently forced by the geometry. But subtraction is not itself evidence: if two inputs share all data consumed by the proposed invariant, equality on them is a no-go, while survival under a strong control is only a prerequisite for further novelty and RH audits.

## Separate critical-axis generation from zero selection

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-004-critical-axis-is-not-zero-selection`.

Several branches independently generate distinguished analytic boundaries or symmetry axes, including Tate's unitary self-dual `Re(s)=1/2`, Hilbert/Schatten membership thresholds, prime-torus measure-class and Fisher boundaries, Hasse-domain summability, and universal cusp/propagation exponents. Some of these structures even recover the correct finite Weil attenuation at the boundary. Determine what additional canonical mechanism could turn such a carrier into a **selector** that eliminates off-axis zero configurations rather than merely reflecting them.

The decisive test is a theorem whose sign, totality, defect elimination, or rigidity is independent of the already-known zero divisor and genuinely uses the distinguished axis. Re-deriving the functional-equation symmetry, a convergence wall, a measure singularity, a Fisher divergence, or correct first-order Euler coefficients without such a selector does not advance the RH implication.
