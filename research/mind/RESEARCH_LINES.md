## Classify the universal boundary model and its information budget before using a singular, limiting, or finite-window escape

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-006-classify-the-boundary-model-before-calling-it-arithmetic` and `MI-005-topology-is-part-of-the-arithmetic-claim`.

Several current escapes live exactly where a simpler category breaks: large-conductor Hardy limits, `S_2` versus `S_1` Gram topology, finite-window Ramanujan truncation, singular cover angles, mixed-prime positive completion, relative Selberg subtraction, or local-to-global exceptional sets. The newer evidence shows that these boundaries often carry their own exact model — zero-free Gaussian determinants, prolate sampling plus Poisson bulk, `N`-coordinate alias quotients, forced mixed-prime product completions, zero-free matched Selberg cocycles, or the elementary `eta X+B+H` transfer budget.

A decisive positive must identify a source-specific residual **after** that boundary model is removed or matched and prove that it survives in the same category. A decisive negative is a theorem that the entire boundary escape factors through the universal model. Merely observing noncompactness, singularity, stronger-norm failure, a new regularized object, or strong local cancellation is no longer enough.
