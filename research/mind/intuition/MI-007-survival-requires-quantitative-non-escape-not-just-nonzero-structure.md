## Core intuition

Detecting or storing richer structure is not enough. Across the current program, useful information repeatedly disappears because the final theorem consumes a narrower resource: a reference-dependent recovery loss, a bounded radial degree range, a fixed finite local source algebra, a pointwise derivative chart, a broad phase average, an affine dual functional, a passive Hardy readout, a high-dimensional uncertainty ball, or a source/periodic localization map.

The strongest common rule is now two-sided. First, **match the source certificate to the exact resource consumed by the destination** and quotient directions that the destination provably ignores. Second, preserve enough effective complexity -- scale, rank, parity-sensitive coupling, topology, or observation coherence -- that the final consumption step cannot erase the source discriminator.

## Program consequence

For every candidate, identify the source discriminator, the exact destination resource, the operation that can erase it, and the quantitative theorem preventing that escape. Also state the **minimum effective complexity that must survive**: degree scale, spectral rank, parity-sensitive factor coupling, analytic category, spatial regularity, phase-window coherence, statistical claim dimension, or localization geometry.

Before strengthening the source assumptions, test whether the problematic directions are destination-null and should instead be quotiented. Before enriching an intermediate representation, test whether the final readout collapses that enrichment back to a classical or low-complexity class. A richer object is useful only if its distinguishing information survives the complete chain to the final theorem.
