## Core intuition

Detecting or storing richer structure is not enough. Across the current program, useful information repeatedly disappears because the final theorem consumes a narrower quotient, signed interaction, norm, scalarization, localization, or normalization. The strongest common rule is now five-part: declare the exact destination resource; retain a discriminator that survives that quotient; prove it can be activated and extracted under source constraints; verify that its normalized size is visible at the theorem's scale; and control the conditioning of any inverse/normalization needed to enter the destination coordinates.

## Program consequence

For every candidate, identify the source discriminator; the destination equivalence relation/norm; the operation that can erase it; the theorem that prevents cancellation or escape; the normalization at which it must remain visible; and the inverse/conditioning required to enter the final coordinates. These are separate proof obligations.

Before enriching a representation, test whether the final readout collapses it. Before celebrating a nonzero correction, test activation and visibility. Before using exact reconstruction or tiny forward error, test inverse conditioning. The useful object is **source-calibrated information that survives the exact consumption map and remains quantitatively usable there**.
