# MI-007 — Survival requires a source-calibrated quantitative margin through detection, accessibility, transfer, coercivity, and assembly

**Evidence level:** supported by the current finite-conditioning, accessibility, transfer, confluence, refinement, and mesoscopic-flow evidence

## Core intuition

Detecting the right defect is not the same as being able to use it. A carrier may see the correct source discrepancy while every coherent realization requires unbounded norm, the transfer theorem overpays the exponent, an exact sign invariant has arbitrarily small magnitude near a collision boundary, or boundary assembly consumes the local margin.

The common resource is a **source-calibrated quantitative margin that survives the entire chain from representation to the exact destination aggregate**.

## Strongest justified principle

Arithmetic Fidelity identifies accessibility non-escape. Unbudgeted finite exactness and finite provenance tests are too weak; AF-102--AF-104 show that bounded second-leg accessibility is its own category and can genuinely have infinite cost. Original-range realization remains a further gate even after bidual accessibility is finite.

Analytic Frontier identifies control-body non-escape. A scalar witness must survive the complete finite diffraction body, not only a local slope or stochastic ansatz. Renewal randomness can itself be forced to zero before the full band test, showing that candidate flexibility must remain quantitative after all target constraints are imposed.

Möbius Cancellation separates detection from transfer. Real square-free comparator enlargements do not help: global Möbius closeness plus their own power cancellation already transfers the target exponent. Complex comparators are the sharper boundary; ordinary closeness supplies only a quadratic `1/p` phase budget, whereas exponent-preserving transfer requires much stronger power-weighted control. The missing resource is cheaper signed/source-specific transport.

Prime Circle and Prime Flute show provenance/assembly non-escape. Fixed-base one-hole spectral response remains prime-blind even after full Feshbach parameterization, and prime-flute positive length accumulation is inherited by an all-composite clone. A local or absolute signal must survive the complete operator/orbit assembly and matched source class.

Weil Inertia shows coercivity non-escape in its cleanest form. The negative index counts off-line pairs exactly, yet confluence to critical-line doubles makes the corresponding negative eigenvalues and finite deficit arbitrarily small. Exact sign information needs an independently controlled source distance from the collision boundary before it yields a density theorem.

Weil Positivity and Xi Flow give two boundary-budget versions. A determinant can have the right logarithm while its canonical positive metric removes the responsible scale direction; a Xi far buffer can make remote leakage `o(1)` while near-boundary and span flux remain uncontrolled. In both cases the useful margin is what remains after the full normalized assembly, not the presence of a promising intermediate quantity.

## Program consequence

For every candidate, distinguish at least four questions: what detects the source difference; what bounded/accessibility category realizes it; what quantitative theorem transfers or coerces it toward the target; and what margin remains after the full infinite/boundary/operator assembly. All budgets must be source-forced and uniform in the relevant scale.

## Status / novelty

The component estimates are classical or persisted exact findings. The synthesis is the strengthened non-escape gate: **detectability without bounded realization, realization without cheap transfer/coercivity, or coercivity without surviving assembly is not leverage**.

## Falsification criterion

Produce a construction lacking one of these uniform budgets but still yielding a stable source-specific target theorem without new information, or derive the missing accessibility/transfer/coercivity/assembly margin from the source in a live line.
