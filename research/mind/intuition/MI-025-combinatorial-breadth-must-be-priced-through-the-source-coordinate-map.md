# MI-025 — Source breadth must be measured in the theorem's physical coordinate at the scale and resolution being probed

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from [AF-399](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-399-regular-variation-transfers-fixed-physical-walls-to-source-dependent-index-depth.md) through [AF-402](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-402-exceptional-set-inertia-transfers-short-interval-pnt-to-density-one-prime-starts.md), [VIS-294](../../visual_exploration/findings/VIS-294-wang-lower-moving-source-sqrtlog-crossover.md), [VIS-295](../../visual_exploration/findings/VIS-295-wang-gamma-subtracted-residual-relative-crossover.md), [WI-343](../../weil_inertia/findings/WI-343-natural-gallagher-window-has-bounded-height-carrier-dimension.md), [WI-345](../../weil_inertia/findings/WI-345-fixed-degree-polynomial-carriers-remain-compressible.md), and [WI-346](../../weil_inertia/findings/WI-346-uniformly-analytic-bow-features-remain-compressible.md). No numerical scale or detector estimate is transferred between these objects.

Counts of indices, frequencies, spectral heights, samples or nonlinear features are not intrinsic measures of how much source geometry an architecture reaches. The invariant question is first **which physical/source coordinate interval is traversed**, then which complexity class maps that interval into observations, and finally at what destination resolution the theorem controls the resulting family.

Arithmetic Fidelity makes the source-grid conversion explicit. For primes, current short-interval technology gives all-start control for shrinking physical walls `h_N=p_N^(-beta)` with `beta<13/30` and density-one control for every fixed `beta<13/15`. Those exponents belong to the source theorem and sampling quantifier; they are not detector constants.

Visual Exploration adds a resolution-sensitive moving-source example. VIS-294 shows that Wang's statistic is already controlled to absolute `H` accuracy throughout `x>>sqrt(log T)` once the explicit gamma term is retained. VIS-295 asks for a different output resolution: after gamma subtraction, relative control of the `H log x` profile persists whenever `x^2 log x>>log T`, reaching below `sqrt(log T)`. The theorem-coordinate boundary therefore depends on the scale of the residual being requested. Merely saying `beta(T)->0` or naming one crossover without its normalization loses the relevant information.

Weil Inertia shows why observation count and nonlinear feature count can both overstate source breadth. WI-343--WI-344 identify the unit time--bandwidth product `H Delta=1` on the natural Gallagher window and prove bounded effective linear height dimension. WI-345 shows that every fixed-degree polynomial/tensor feature family remains compressible on the same aperture. WI-346 extends this to uniformly analytic height-independent maps with fixed radius, coefficient budget and nonvanishing transmission: their normalized effective rank stays bounded at fixed tolerance, and forcing large rank requires deteriorating complexity/conditioning or enlarging the physical aperture.

The reusable rule is therefore stronger than “check the seminorm” or “count the samples.” **Map the physical source window into theorem coordinates, preserve structured main terms on that induced scale, declare the requested destination resolution, and price any linear or nonlinear feature complexity together with its conditioning.** A large derivative norm need not signal failure when it resolves a theorem boundary layer, and a large feature bank need not provide new information when all outputs remain in one fixed-aperture analytic image.

**Boundary.** AF-399--AF-402 concern prime-mesh source-density conversion; VIS-294--VIS-295 concern one Wang error budget under two normalizations; WI-343--WI-346 concern fixed-aperture carrier compression under declared feature classes. Their scales and constants are not interchangeable. The principle is an audit of physical scale, effective breadth, feature complexity, sampling quantifier and requested resolution, not a new zero-sensitive theorem.
