# MI-025 — Combinatorial breadth must be priced through the source-coordinate map

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of AF-399, VIS-270 and WI-330. Each line supplies its own exact coordinate law; no numerical scale or detector estimate is transferred between objects.

Counts of indices, heights or samples are not intrinsic measures of how much source geometry an architecture reaches. The invariant question is first **which physical/source coordinate interval is traversed**, and only then how that interval is represented by the discrete source.

Arithmetic Fidelity makes the pullback explicit. For a regularly varying source scale `a_N`, a fixed logarithmic reach `h` has successor depth asymptotic to `(exp(h/rho)-1)N`, with lower-order corrections inherited from the source-density law. The physical topology wall is fixed while its combinatorial width changes with the coordinate map.

Weil Inertia shows the converse collapse. A count-saturating family may contain many spectral heights, yet the Riemann--Siegel coordinate has derivative on the square-root scale, so an entire surviving sub-square-root bow occupies only `O(1)` source cells. More height samples therefore need not provide more arithmetic starts.

Visual Exploration supplies a support version. Fixed-order shrinking packets become exactly source-blind once their physical Fourier support falls below the first prime frequency, while a translated fixed-band packet can cancel the universal leading functional and still touch the first prime carrier. The relevant resource is support reach in the arithmetic frequency coordinate, not moment count or packet complexity by itself.

Across these mechanisms the reusable rule is: **define source reach before counting source coordinates**. A discrete breadth can be large while the source image is tiny, or linear in index even when the physical reach is fixed. Density corrections, coordinate derivatives and support thresholds are part of the resource conversion, not cosmetic normalizations.

**Boundary.** This is an accounting principle across different source maps. AF-399 concerns regularly varying index scales, WI-330 a smooth square-root source selector, and VIS-270 Fourier support. Their formulas are not interchangeable, and the principle alone supplies neither arithmetic cancellation nor a zero-sensitive destination theorem.
