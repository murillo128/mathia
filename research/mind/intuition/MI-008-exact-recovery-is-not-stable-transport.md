# MI-008 — Source restriction, exact recovery, quotient cost and stable transport are different resources

**Evidence level:** supported by Arithmetic Fidelity through [AF-315](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-315-six-point-zero-shapes-need-three-real-harmonic-coordinates.md) and Prime Flute through PF-320. No universal target modulus, arithmetic source model or theorem transferring either operator between lines is claimed.

Arithmetic Fidelity makes the source-fiber hierarchy explicit. Source restriction can shrink the admissible geometry without giving a stable failure margin: fixed saturated equal-weight prime supports can approach zero arbitrarily closely, while exact incidence differs sharply between support classes. Exact failure membership, distance from failure and non-vacuity are therefore already separate resources.

The five- and six-point zero fibers add a second separation. At five points, conditional on a hit, `(F_P(2t),F_P(3t))` recovers the complete unordered phase multiset, and after quotienting common phase the single complex invariant `kappa=F_P(2t)^3/F_P(3t)^2` is complete. Recovery is still nonuniform near the exceptional regular-pentagon geometry, and none of this decides whether the first prime hit exists.

AF-315 shows that exact recoverability and **minimal quotient representation cost** can then diverge while the raw observation family stays the same. For centered six-point unit-circle configurations, `(s_2,s_3)` again recovers the raw unordered multiset on the source-relevant stratum, but the generic shape quotient has real dimension `3`. One complex scalar is therefore topologically insufficient; the complete invariant `( |s_3|, s_2^3/s_3^2 )` needs exactly three real coordinates. Distinct rational-prime phases at a hypothetical nonzero hit automatically avoid the second- and third-harmonic singular strata, yet first incidence and stable conditioning remain open.

Thus source restriction, event non-vacuity, exact incidence, exact reconstruction, quotient dimension and conditioning can all move independently. A representation may be exactly sufficient but require more quotient coordinates as the admissible fiber grows; conversely, a compact exact invariant can remain useless for deciding whether the event ever occurs.

Prime Flute supplies the complementary operator-metric lesson. PF-319 proves that a small relative form perturbation can look arbitrarily large as a raw mixed block after congruence by an unrelated seam metric. PF-320 then shows that the actual positive-form angle is a different destination: its own diagonal energies normalize away that anisotropy, so multiplicative form closeness gives controlled intrinsic-angle perturbation even when the raw seam-normalized block is badly amplified.

The cross-line diagnostic is therefore more precise than “preserve information”: **name the admissible source class, quotient the genuine nuisance symmetry, determine the dimension/separating power of what remains, verify that the target event is non-vacuous, and measure conditioning in the metric consumed downstream.** Raw coordinate count, exact reconstruction and stability are distinct bills.

**Boundary.** AF-315 concerns centered six-point unit-circle phase geometry and a conditional distinct-prime specialization; PF-319--PF-320 concern positive local block forms and principal angles. The synthesis is destination-relative accounting, not a theorem connecting their mathematical objects.
