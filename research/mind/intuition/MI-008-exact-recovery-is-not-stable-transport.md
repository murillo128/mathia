# MI-008 — Source restriction, reachable fibers and defect-scale transport are different resources

**Evidence level:** supported by Arithmetic Fidelity through AF-301 and Prime Flute through PF-314; no universal target modulus, arithmetic source model, source-natural quotient, or theorem for the final physical prime-flute mixed response is claimed.

Arithmetic Fidelity separates acquisition from the final zero-sensitive target. AF-298 leaves the sharp ambient `N^(-1/2)` observation-leverage bill. AF-299 shows that an unrestricted nontrivial complex-linear quotient collapses exact boundary-failure distance. AF-300 then proves that an independently justified complex-affine source restriction of intrinsic dimension `r` genuinely lowers the bill to `r^(-1/2)`, while every remaining complex source direction stays essential.

AF-301 tests the first non-complex-linear escape. Positivity changes the fiber geometry from a linear nullspace to a **reachable positive cone** of discarded response rays. That can protect a fixed observation when the required correction lies outside the cone. But three discarded indices with `Q`-independent logarithms—three distinct primes suffice—produce recurrent heights where the positive rays span the entire complex plane. At those heights positivity recreates arbitrary complex correction freedom. One-sidedness alone is therefore not a durable fidelity theorem; boundedness, normalization, discreteness, multiplicative coupling, sparsity, or another restriction must bound the actual fiberwise reachable set.

Prime Flute supplies the complementary positive-form case. PF-313 says a large inverse direction is harmless only when the physical source vanishes at the exact Schur-defect scale. PF-314 then proves this automatically for a specific **jointly constrained** source class: if source, constant, and residual-high coordinates belong to one positive block form and the source coefficients are uniformly normalized in their own source energy, sequential shorting forces `J_CJ_C*<=K^2F_C`. Internal energy-normalized sources cannot independently populate a vanishing-defect direction.

The cross-line distinction is sharp. **A sign constraint on coefficients changes the shape of reachable fibers but may leave them asymptotically huge; positivity of one joint energy form can instead couple source amplitude directly to the destination defect.** What matters is not the word “positive” but whether the admissible source geometry supplies a quantitative resource bound on every target-relevant fiber.

This gives a practical proof obligation. Before claiming compression or inverse amplification harmless, state the exact admissible source class, compute or bound its fiberwise reachable correction set, and identify any joint energy inequality linking source strength to the target defect. AF-301 says positive cones without resource bounds can re-saturate; PF-314 says a common Schur geometry can provide precisely such a bound for internal sources.

**Boundary.** The AF and PF operators do not transfer. AF-301 concerns finite positive coefficient fibers and Dirichlet boundary evaluations over varying heights; PF-314 concerns residual source couplings in a finite positive block operator. Their synthesis is a distinction between uncoupled one-sidedness and jointly energy-constrained source geometry, not a shared theorem.
