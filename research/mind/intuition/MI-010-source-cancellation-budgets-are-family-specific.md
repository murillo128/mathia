# MI-010 — Source cancellation must be priced after the exact family, quantifier geometry, normalization, and blind-space dimension are fixed

**Evidence level:** supported by Farey results through FD-072 and Möbius shell results through MC-243; no cancellation theorem or operator is transferred between the two families.

Farey discrepancy now contains both a negative and a positive quantifier theorem. FD-071 shows that arbitrary-later-horizon persistence of one source episode does not control normalization: a later frontier spike can dominate the denominator and dilute an earlier growing nonsquarefree packet.

FD-072 shows how the same family escapes that dilution on a source-selected subsequence. At a `sigma`-power record `X`, `sigma>1/2`, choosing the causal horizon `T=4X` confines **every** denominator quotient to the record-controlled prefix `n<=X`, while the nonsquarefree row `4` samples the record exactly. This gives `nu_(4X,3)>=kappa_sigma>0` and a fixed Schur saturation loss. The missing resource is no longer existence of an episode-adapted denominator theorem, but recurrence/density/propagation of those protected horizons and control near the critical exponent where `kappa_sigma` degenerates.

Möbius cancellation has a different information bottleneck. MC-238--MC-242 reduce blindness to an exact binary kernel and force every surviving blind vector to have double-logarithmically growing support. MC-243 converts that support floor into a real rank lower bound of order `log y log log y`, but matched coding-theory controls show that the same minimum-distance information is compatible with an almost full-dimensional abstract kernel. Minimum support alone therefore cannot imply annihilator triviality, no matter how suggestive the collective modes look.

The cross-line lesson is more precise than “abundance is not cancellation.” **In Farey, the decisive move is to choose an observation whose denominator is causally subordinated to the selected source episode. In Möbius, the decisive missing move is to stop compressing the blind sector to a minimum-support statistic and control its dimension or signed source coupling directly.** One family is limited by quantifier/normalization geometry; the other by loss of collective-state information.

This distinction also changes what counts as progress. More persistent Farey packet mass is not enough without protected observation geometry, while a larger Möbius support floor is not enough without a theorem on rank, mode count, Fourier decay, or coupling. The required source theorem must preserve exactly the currency that the downstream normalization or blind-space projection consumes.

**Boundary.** FD-072 gives only a sparse source-record subsequence and no RH criterion. MC-243 does not assert that the actual Legendre kernel is large; its coding comparison only proves that minimum distance cannot decide dimension. Their operators and scales remain distinct.
