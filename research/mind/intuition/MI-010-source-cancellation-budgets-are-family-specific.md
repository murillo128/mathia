# MI-010 — Source cancellation must be priced on the exact selected family after representation costs are removed

**Evidence level:** supported by the proved Farey transfer geometry through FD-062 and the exact first-shell representation reductions through MC-233; no cancellation theorem is transferred between the two families.

Two Möbius-facing lines now expose complementary sides of the same methodological boundary.

In Farey discrepancy, bounded increments identify the deterministic host scale `q\asymp X/A_X`, with `A_X=|\mathcal H(X)|`. FD-062 shows that an actual all-short-interval Möbius estimate changes that scale: when `A_X(\log X)^B\ge X^\vartheta`, `\vartheta>0.55`, the available logarithmic saving can be split between penetration below the deterministic host and growth of a CRT packet. With `B+\kappa<1/3`, one gets `qA_X/X\asymp(\log X)^{-B}\to0` together with `K_X\le(\log X)^\kappa`, `K_X\to\infty`. Here a known source theorem buys a quantitatively visible geometric improvement.

In Möbius cancellation, MC-232--MC-233 remove two apparent combinatorial obstacles instead. Boolean inversion eliminates exact-incidence masks at subpower condition cost, and smooth inversion eliminates the primorial roughness mask at subpower multiplicity cost. What remains is a source-selected family of ordinary reduced-residue Möbius progression sums. At incidence scale `\alpha` and smooth-dilation scale `\beta`, support alone suffices exactly when

\[
2\alpha+\beta\ge\frac12,
\]

while below that line the componentwise sufficient target needs fixed-power gain `1/2-2\alpha-\beta`.

The cross-line lesson is concrete: **representation simplification and source cancellation spend different budgets**. Removing masks, gauges, or redundant coordinates can reveal that an apparent obstruction costs only `X^{o(1)}`, but it cannot manufacture the fixed-power cancellation still demanded by the selected arithmetic family. Conversely, when a source theorem genuinely matches the family and quantifiers, as in FD-062, even a logarithmic saving can cross a deterministic geometric barrier.

Therefore “use Möbius cancellation” is not a transferable instruction. One must state the exact family being averaged, its selection dependence, the quantifiers/uniformity required, and the exponent or logarithmic budget needed by the downstream geometry. A theorem strong in ordinary short intervals does not automatically control the source-selected progression family of MC-233, and the MC representation reductions do not improve the Farey denominator problem.

**Boundary.** This intuition compares two proved budget structures. It does not identify the MC-233 progression family with the intervals of FD-062, propose a theorem transferring between them, or imply any new Mertens/RH estimate.
