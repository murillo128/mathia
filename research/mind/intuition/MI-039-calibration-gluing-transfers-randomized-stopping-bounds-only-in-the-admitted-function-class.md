# MI-039 — Calibration testing bounds transfer through class-specific transcript mechanisms

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of [AF-575](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-575-unknown-calibration-slack-requires-unbounded-sampling-depth.md), [AF-576](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-576-randomized-calibration-recovery-has-a-sharp-error-stopping-tail.md), [AF-577](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-577-unknown-polynomial-calibration-separates-continuous-randomness-from-finite-certification.md), [AF-578](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-578-continuous-randomness-retains-fixed-budget-polynomial-calibration-obstruction.md), [AF-580](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-580-positive-noise-destroys-finite-stopping-under-unknown-polynomial-calibration.md), and [XF-203](../../xi_flow/findings/XF-203-unknown-jacobi-calibration-slack-admits-finite-certified-stopping.md)–[XF-204](../../xi_flow/findings/XF-204-randomized-jacobi-detection-has-a-sharp-error-stopping-tail.md), with the analytic boundaries in [XF-205](../../xi_flow/findings/XF-205-analytic-jacobi-calibration-retains-the-noisy-stopping-obstruction.md) and [XF-206](../../xi_flow/findings/XF-206-noiseless-analytic-calibration-retains-sharp-stopping-tails.md). The transfer identifies testing mechanisms, not the two sources or their noise geometries; it is not a new general recovery theorem or a novelty claim for Gaussian-prior methods.

Subtract a known reference source and write the binary observations as \(b\) or \(g+b\), with the nuisance seminorm strictly below \(d/2\) but no supplied slack. In AF-576, the seminorm is \(\|b^{(n)}\|_\infty\), the gap has \(g^{(n)}(0)=d>0\) and positive strictly decreasing \(g^{(n)}\), and only positive coordinates are queryable. In XF-204, it is \(\|B'\|_\infty\) on complex-valued functions, with \(g=F_{\tau_Q}\), \(d=D\), and the maximizing endpoint \(Q\) queryable. The AF slack \(d-2\|b^{(n)}\|_\infty\) equals twice the Xi convention \(D/2-\|B'\|_\infty\) after this dictionary; constants involving slack require that factor.

The reusable premise is a family of **fixed admissible pairs** \((J_u/2,g-J_u/2)\) agreeing with \(g/2\) outside a shrinking query region. AF obtains \(J_u\) from the order-\(n\) Taylor extension below \(u\). Xi requires the convex-velocity join of [local MI-014](../../xi_flow/mind/intuition/MI-014-endpoint-preserving-calibration-joins-force-sharp-randomized-stopping-tails.md), which preserves \(g(Q)\) as well as the tail values and matching derivative. Translation \(a=r-Q\) identifies the hidden regions \((0,u)\) and \((Q,Q+u)\); it does not make the Taylor and complex joins interchangeable.

For any \(N\)-query policy, let \(q_N(u)\) be the probability that its hypothetical midpoint transcript enters that hidden region. Every seed has only finitely many query positions, so \(q_N(u)\to0\). Shared-seed coupling gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{err}(x_{0,u})+\operatorname{err}(x_{1,u})}{2}
\ge\frac{1-q_N(u)}2.
\]
Choose \(u\) after the policy and cap but **before** its seed. This yields capped minimax error \(1/2\), hence the sharp uniform stopping-tail obstruction \(1-2\alpha\) for per-input error \(\alpha<1/2\), using the pointwise finite exact procedures for attainability. The hard input can depend on \(N\); pointwise finite cost is consistent with infinite worst-case expected cost. Fixing a seed and then interpolating an adversary would not establish this randomized theorem.

This gluing proof stops at the nuisance function class. A nontrivial analytic gap cannot be joined analytically to a strictly subcritical replacement while preserving equality on a tail interval. AF-577 gives a concrete different outcome, retained in [local MI-079](../../arithmetic_fidelity/mind/intuition/MI-079-unknown-polynomial-calibration-separates-random-identification-from-certification.md): for \(m\ge2\) known analytic sources with nonpolynomial pairwise gaps and an unknown finite-degree polynomial nuisance, every finite deterministic transcript has an opposite-label extension, yet one independent continuously random exact validation coordinate almost surely rejects all false interpolants at every stage. Its false residuals have null zero sets, whereas the smooth hard pairs above agree on a whole interval.

In that same noiseless polynomial class, finite-bit randomness has a stronger obstruction than any fixed query cap. A **finite collection** of returning coin words has a finite union of queried coordinates; one polynomial per label matches every transcript simultaneously. If the collection has mass \(q\), some matched label has error at least \((1-1/m)q\). Exhaustion gives \(e\ge(1-1/m)\Pr_y(\text{return})\) on every input, where \(e\) is the worst-case unconditional probability of a wrong label return. No expected-cost or uniform resource bound is assumed. Thus almost-sure return has minimax error \(1-1/m\), even with input-dependent unbounded stopping. This does not transfer to the smooth derivative-bounded class, where arbitrary simultaneous interpolation need not preserve the derivative constraint and exact pointwise finite stopping exists.

Analyticity also does not remove the **noisy** Xi obstruction. [Local MI-015](../../xi_flow/mind/intuition/MI-015-positive-noise-retains-analytic-calibration-stopping-obstruction.md) retains XF-205's holomorphic scalar baselines \(B_{0,\varepsilon}= (1-\varepsilon)F/2\), \(B_{1,\varepsilon}=-(1-\varepsilon)F/2\), whose two observations differ by \(\varepsilon F\). For each fixed cap \(N\) and positive total-noise allowance \(\sigma\), bounded-energy priors on fixed noise sequences give transcript total variation at most \(\min\{1,N^{3/2}\varepsilon\|F\|_\infty/\sigma\}\). Sending \(\varepsilon\) to zero recovers capped minimax error \(1/2\) and the same sharp tail \(1-2\alpha\), without any tail gluing. This restricted direction remains one-query identifiable when supplied and noiseless.

AF-578 and XF-206 supply a second exact correspondence: an auxiliary Gaussian prior on **one polynomial fixed before all queries** leaves positive conditional variance at every new distinct value in a capped adaptive transcript. In AF, the evaluation vector is \((1,t,\ldots,t^{N-1})\) on \([0,1]\); a smooth source gap has a uniformly bounded interpolation lift even at coalescing nodes. A coefficient prior of variance \(L^2I\) gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_i^L,P_j^L)\le B_N(F_i-F_j)/(2L),\qquad
B_N(g)=\sum_{k<N}2^k\|g^{(k)}\|_\infty/k!.
\]
Unrestricted coefficient size permits \(L\to\infty\). In Xi, the derivative constraint forbids that broad-prior repair, but the evaluation vector \((1,s^2,\ldots,s^{2N-2})\), \(s=(r-Q)/Q\), supplies a different admissible uncertainty. Its polynomial derivative vanishes at \(Q\); for the full source, \(v=|F'|\) and \(\kappa=\min(-v')>0\) allow the fixed coefficient region \(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}2k|a_k|\le\kappa Q^2/4\). Adding this polynomial to both near-critical scalar baselines preserves strict derivative admissibility. Choose a small Gaussian scale with mass at least \(1-\eta\) inside that region, then shrink the **observation gap** \(\varepsilon F\). Conditional Gaussian continuity at each common history and conditioning onto the region give \(\operatorname{TV}\le2\eta+o_{\varepsilon\to0}(1)\). Unlike AF's uniform bound, this limit is for each fixed policy and supplies no uniform rate over clustered nodes. Both use full row rank only through the cap; neither assumes a seed-dependent calibration or observation noise.

The query-domain extensions preserve that correspondence by different constructions. [AF-579](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-579-unbounded-query-locations-preserve-polynomial-budget-obstruction.md) uses, at cap \(N\), \(x=a/(1+a)\) and response multiplier \((1-x)^{N-1}\) after subtracting a known reference. This maps the ray nuisance \(\Pi_{N-1}\) bijectively onto bounded-window \(\Pi_{N-1}\). All derivatives of the prime-edit gaps decay exponentially at infinity, so the transformed gaps are smooth at \(x=1\). The prior remains broad; the experiment is exactly invertible at every finite query. [XF-207](../../xi_flow/findings/XF-207-complex-value-queries-retain-the-analytic-calibration-stopping-obstruction.md) instead uses \(\{P:\deg P\le N+1,\ P'(0)=0\}\) in \(s=(z-Q)/Q\). Hermite correction preserves arbitrary values at at most \(N\) distinct complex nodes while killing the endpoint derivative. This replaces the even powers, whose values coincide at reflected nodes. The room \(\sum_{k=2}^{N+1}k|a_k|\le\kappa Q^2/4\) still fits the real-interval derivative budget. A fixed compact bound \(\|B\|_{\mathcal K}\le M>\|F\|_{\mathcal K}/2\) also leaves a positive coefficient neighborhood. Thus unbounded real positions and complex values do not exhaust the respective nuisance directions through any finite cap. AF supplies a uniform transformed interpolation bound; Xi only needs policywise Gaussian continuity as the label gap shrinks. These are not interchangeable assumptions.

Consequently noiseless ideal continuous randomness still has capped minimax error \(c_m=1-1/m\) in AF, and noiseless holomorphic Xi has \(c_2=1/2\), including those extended domains. With \(e\) the worst-case unconditional wrong-return probability and \(q_N=\sup_x\Pr_x(T>N)\), both imply \(e+c_mq_N\ge c_m\), taking \(m=2\) in Xi. The matching exact branches differ: AF needs continuous validation with nonpolynomial analytic gaps; Xi has a deterministic short-chord certificate from strict derivative slack. These give sharp tails despite per-input finite stopping, not a common input with infinite cost. AF needs bounded-window regularity or the stated compactified-gap condition; Xi needs the complete source's speed decrease and admitted polynomial coefficient room. A known polynomial degree \(d\) instead gives AF's exact \(d+1\) versus \(d+2\) continuous-query threshold, while Xi's supplied scalar direction alone is noiselessly identifiable with one query. Thus evaluation rank, coefficient room and the shrinking-gap parameter must all be matched before transferring a testing bound.

The noise consequence depends on the observation domain. On a bounded real window, AF-577's polynomial density brings \(F_i+\Pi\) and \(F_j+\Pi\) arbitrarily close in uniform norm; opposite supremum-bounded errors alias the **whole curves**. On the ray, AF-579 gives a competing mechanism: for bounded \(g\), every nonconstant polynomial has infinite uniform error, so \(\inf_{b\in\Pi}\|g-b\|_\infty=(\sup g-\inf g)/2\). Fixed prime-edit candidates therefore have a strictly positive full-curve noise radius \(d(S)/4\), while every finite cap still has blind-guess minimax error. Full-curve norm separation alone does not provide a finite bank uniformly over the nuisance class. AF-580 strengthens this to an error/return obstruction at every positive noise allowance: for arbitrary fixed supremum-bounded errors, `e_epsilon >= c_m P_x(return)` on every strict-slack input and every continuous-error input. In particular it rules out almost-sure finite stopping on even one noiseless input with worst-case error below `c_m` over the noisy class, while retaining the positive full-ray margin. The rational pair \(0,a/(1+a)^2\), with radius \(1/16\), supplies the same non-arithmetic control. Exact compactification preserves the value experiment, not its unweighted noise norm.

Xi's scalar baselines approach a common midpoint on each fixed compact set, and its total squared-noise constraint is imposed on one sequence across all rows. XF-205 turns that proximity into near-indistinguishable **finite transcript laws**, not a full infinite-observation alias. Distinct-row contrasts have coefficient norm \(\sqrt{2/n}/h\), so XF-203 still stops exactly on each fixed positive-slack input. With complex access, a compact norm bound can make Cauchy derivative approximation quantitative, but XF-207 shows it need not separate the labels from the derivative threshold. Exact derivative or contour-integral access changes that information class. The common finite-cap conclusion thus survives both AF's positive full-ray norm margin and Xi's arbitrarily small derivative slack; it is not an equivalence between their global class distances. No physical Xi access, ordinary-prime provenance, or RH implication follows.

The new AF mechanism is not just the common finite-cap testing bound. Polynomial approximation plus a bounded-error cutoff realizes every label with **exactly the same observation on each compact window**. Returning executions are exhausted by those windows, regardless of how many continuously randomized queries they take. Smooth compactly supported errors suffice against a noiseless reference; analytic error functions or a common derivative bound are not covered. In the earlier smooth AF–Xi tail construction, by contrast, the common midpoint is excluded by the strict nuisance bound. Its capped hard pairs do not give exact-window matches around every admissible input. The exhaustion step needs an admitted reference input to yield the stronger pointwise return bound.

The noise geometries also differ concretely. XF-203's average of \(n\) distinct chord pairs of length \(h\) has coefficient vector \(c\) with
\[
\|c\|_2=\frac1h\sqrt{\frac2n},\qquad \|c\|_1=\frac2h.
\]
Xi's single total budget \(\sum_j|e_j|^2\le\sigma^2\) therefore gives error at most \(\sigma h^{-1}\sqrt{2/n}\). Combined with source loss \(a(h)=A Q^{-3/2}h\), the certificate succeeds once \(a(h)+2\sigma h^{-1}\sqrt{2/n}<\delta\), where \(\delta=D/2-\|B'\|_\infty>0\). AF's fixed-function bound \(\|\eta\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\) instead permits error \(\varepsilon\|c\|_1=2\varepsilon/h\) at a finite bank of distinct nodes, with no reduction from increasing \(n\). Smooth bounded functions can realize those finite-node signs when no derivative bound is supplied. This norm comparison is a source-backed consequence of the two experiments, not a claim that changing only the noise law transfers either theorem: their nuisance constraints and source margins differ as well. A compactly supported error need not obey any finite total squared budget along infinitely many distinct queries inside its support.
