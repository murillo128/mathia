# MI-028 — Complete measurement families can close an escape by reconstructing the classical object

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from [PC-343](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-343-full-shell-mellin-measure-kernel-is-zeta-zero-support.md) and [PL-347](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-347-endpoint-modulation-gram-weil-core.md). The completeness mechanisms are line-specific; no theorem identifies the Prime-Circle and Prime-Lattice objects.

Two recent closures have the same structural shape even though their mathematics is different.

In Prime Circle, semiprime-null scalar Mellin selectors leave an interpolation problem on the sparse zero set `{log p}` whose survivors depend on strip width and growth class. When the observation family is enlarged to **all shells**, divisor inversion forces zeros at every `{log m}`. For a finite Mellin-frequency measure, Gamma decay permits only polynomial boundary blow-up while that integer-log zero set would force essential-exponential blow-up. The transform must vanish, and Fourier uniqueness leaves only measures supported on the common zeta-zero factor (or the trivial kernel at `sigma=1`). The larger family closes the analytic escape, but what remains is exactly the classical universal factor already present in the shell formula.

In Prime Lattice, one completed endpoint state is only a classical logarithmic Riesz channel. Enlarging to **all Fourier modulations and all cross terms** genuinely adds information, but the endpoint envelope is an invertible multiplier and the modulation span is a form core for the localized Weil quadratic form. Therefore positivity of the complete Gram family is exactly positivity of the pre-existing localized Weil form, and over all apertures it is exactly Weil's RH criterion.

The shared mechanism is not “more data are bad.” It is more precise: when the added observation family is complete for the ambient analytic/form object by a classical inversion or density theorem, the apparent new rigidity can consist entirely of **reconstructing that old object**. Completeness then closes loopholes and is mathematically useful, but it does not by itself create a new source-selective law.

This gives a concrete test for intermediate proposals. Before crediting a richer family, ask whether its sufficiency is doing anything beyond one of these free closures:

- algebraic inversion upgrades the observed coordinates to a complete classical transform;
- density/form-core completion upgrades the sampled family to the entire known quadratic form.

A potentially new mechanism must live strictly before that closure while still having a source-forced sufficiency theorem: for example a sparse family not automatically complete by density, a shell subfamily whose arithmetic relations imply more than generic interpolation, or a coupling/noncommutative relation not invariant under arbitrary replacement by another complete frame.

**Boundary.** PC-343 concerns finite Mellin measures and all Prime-Circle shells; PL-347 concerns one endpoint envelope, its full modulation Gram family and the localized completed Weil form. The cross-line claim is only the reusable completeness audit above, not a transfer of either theorem to the other setting.
