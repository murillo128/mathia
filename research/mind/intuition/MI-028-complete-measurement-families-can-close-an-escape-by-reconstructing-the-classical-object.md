# MI-028 — Complete exact families can close an escape by reconstruction, annihilation or topology-dependent reopening

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from [PC-343](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-343-full-shell-mellin-measure-kernel-is-zeta-zero-support.md), [PL-347](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-347-endpoint-modulation-gram-weil-core.md), [PL-353](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-353-finite-coefficient-constraints-dual-interpolation-gate.md), [PL-354](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-354-real-dirichlet-evaluation-stack-dense-or-zero.md), [PL-355](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-355-prime-l2-boundary-zero-stacks.md), [PL-356](../../prime_lattice/findings/PL-356-boundary-zero-stacks-finite-constraint-robustness.md), and [VIS-293](../../visual_exploration/findings/VIS-293-wang-source-dirichlet-rh-half-plane.md). The closure mechanisms are line-specific; no theorem identifies these objects across lines.

Several mathematically different completeness mechanisms share one strategic danger: adding an exact family can remove ambiguity by returning to the classical target object, while exact constraints can either annihilate one source class or become highly non-rigid after changing the completion. None of these outcomes is automatically an intermediate RH mechanism.

In Prime Circle, enlarging scalar shell observations to a sufficiently complete family closes the interpolation escape but leaves the common zeta-zero factor: the enriched family has reconstructed the classical analytic object rather than generated an independent zero selector.

In Prime Lattice, complete Fourier modulations form a core for the localized Weil quadratic form, so positivity of the complete Gram family is ordinary localized Weil positivity. PL-353 adds the topological warning that many finite algebraic coefficient restrictions disappear after local Sobolev/form closure unless they are represented by continuous dual traces.

PL-354 shows the opposite algebraic extreme on finite-support Dirichlet polynomials: finite exact positive-real evaluation stacks remain in the dense-kernel regime, while an infinite distinct stack annihilates every nonzero finite polynomial. PL-355--PL-356 then show why that conclusion cannot be called topology-free. In the natural prime-supported `l2` completion, classical boundary zero-set theory produces nonzero functions vanishing on infinite real Blaschke stacks accumulating at `1/2`; the common nullspace is infinite-dimensional and survives every finite family of bounded homogeneous coefficient constraints. The same exact stack can therefore be fatal in the algebraic finite-support class and flexible in its Hilbert completion.

Visual Exploration gives an analytic-continuation version. The exact weighted prime-power source satisfies `D(s)=(log zeta)''(s)+A(s)` with `A` holomorphic on `Re(s)>1/2`. Requiring first-half-plane holomorphy after removing the elementary pole is already RH. Exact source access has not supplied a new discriminator if the decisive extension simply reinstates the target zero divisor.

The shared audit is therefore not “more data are bad.” For every enlarged exact family, ask **what theorem makes it sufficient in the declared source/destination topology**. Algebraic inversion may reconstruct a complete classical transform; density may reconstruct the whole known quadratic form; finite restrictions may vanish after closure; an infinite stack may annihilate one source class but reopen a large interpolation nullspace after completion; analytic continuation may import the target divisor itself.

A potentially new mechanism must live between these automatic closures with a source-forced sufficiency theorem that survives the intended topology: for example an infinite closed arithmetic relation with nontrivial range, a nonlinear/positivity or cross-degree constraint, a cross-aperture coupling, or another quotient whose nontriviality and destination leverage are proved after completion rather than inferred from its algebraic presentation.

**Boundary.** PC-343 concerns finite Mellin measures and complete shell data; PL-347 localized Weil form closure; PL-353 finite linear coefficient constraints; PL-354 finite-support exact evaluation stacks; PL-355--PL-356 the prime `l2` first-chaos completion and real boundary Blaschke stacks; VIS-293 one weighted source transform. The synthesis is a reusable closure/topology audit, not a transfer of any theorem between settings.
