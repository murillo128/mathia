# MI-033 — Determinant wrappers do not create new effective rank after carrier reduction

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of [MC-439](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-439-principal-minor-determinant-full-interaction-fixed-rank-collapse.md), [MC-440](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-440-path-distance-determinant-buries-gap-order-two-degrees-down.md), [MC-441](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-441-first-order-sensitive-path-coefficient-is-adjacency-endpoint-scalar.md), [PC-392](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-392-fixed-gap-log-determinants-cannot-amplify-the-thin-multi-deletion-contrast-sector.md), and [PC-393](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-393-complete-survivor-connectivity-closes-the-singular-normalized-determinant-edge.md). The determinant problems are different; the shared statement is a carrier-and-scale audit, not a transfer of formulas or constants.

A nonlinear observable can look much richer than the linear object from which it is built. Determinants are a particularly strong temptation: they mix all coordinates multiplicatively and can remain sensitive to low-rank perturbations that disappear from bulk empirical spectra. The current Möbius and prime-circle lines show that this nonlinear wrapping must still be evaluated **after** the surviving carrier has been reduced exactly and at the scale where source-specific variation first appears.

In Möbius Cancellation, principal-minor determinants pass the full Boolean-interaction gate: Möbius convolution gives `det(K-I_r)` rather than zero. But when the pair kernel has fixed separable feature rank `d`, Sylvester reduction turns the `r x r` determinant into a `d x d` determinant whose entries are additive prime-feature moments. The nonlinear statistic survives while its effective source carrier has already collapsed to fixed dimension.

MC-440 shows that clearing the rank gate is still not enough. The growing-rank path-distance kernel genuinely retains gap order, yet under common scaling its determinant has two leading homogeneous layers that depend only on the gap multiset. MC-441 then computes the first order-sensitive layer and shows that all of its permutation dependence factors through one scalar

`J(g)=1/g_1+1/g_(r-1)-(sum_i g_i)(sum_i 1/(g_i g_(i+1)))`.

So effective rank can grow while the desired global source relation remains both quantitatively subleading, at relative `O(X^(-2))`, and structurally compressed to endpoint plus nearest-neighbor data.

Prime Circle supplies the spectral counterpart. The irreducible simultaneous-Kron correction lives on a contrast sector of rank at most `k-1`. PC-392 reduces every positive-regularized determinant ratio to that carrier. PC-393 then shows why sending the regulator to zero does not create a new resource: the contrast annihilates the common constant zero mode, and complete positive survivor connectivity gives a polynomial mean-zero spectral floor. The normalized determinant correction is therefore `O((log p)^2/p)` uniformly for every `lambda>=0`, including the pseudodeterminant endpoint.

This sharpens the reusable audit. First identify the exact source-sensitive carrier. Then compute its effective rank, its internal conditioning **on that carrier**, and the first scale/feature at which the desired source relation changes the nonlinear observable. An ambient weak mode matters only if the carrier couples to it; a growing-rank determinant matters only if the desired relation reaches a sufficiently rich coefficient at a usable scale. Neither determinant nonlinearity nor a singular full-space resolvent creates information that the reduced carrier does not access.

**Boundary.** This is not a general theorem about determinants. MC-439 concerns principal-minor statistics with fixed separable rank; MC-440--MC-441 concern common-scale weighted path-distance determinants; PC-392--PC-393 concern positive complete Laplacian Kron reduction on fixed polynomial windows. Lower path coefficients, other growing-rank kernels, unnormalized exceptional determinants, individual outliers, signed operators and non-complete graphs remain separate mathematical regimes.
