# MI-014 — Cross-component coherence is proof currency only when switching is expensive at source resolution

**Evidence level:** supported by Arithmetic Fidelity, Farey Discrepancy, and Nyman--Beurling through [NB-172](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-172-small-template-entropy-suppresses-arbitrarily-rough-height-adaptation.md). The mechanisms are logically comparable but mathematically distinct.

Componentwise coercivity can fail under free switching unless the component freedom is priced, removed from the state variable, or coupled by a genuinely new compatibility equation. The current corpus contains both positive and negative examples of same-source coherence, plus a sharper adaptation lesson: path roughness and geometric repertoire are independent resources.

Arithmetic Fidelity retains and prices the component family. Fixed prime support gives a time-height modulus, while unrestricted support variation destroys every time-only gap. Bounding support height restores a uniform but degrading envelope and propagates that cost into determinant/eigenvalue slack. The component parameter matters because it changes the quantitative destination margin rather than merely labeling equivalent states.

Farey Discrepancy gives genuine nonredundant coherence across horizons. Separate horizons can admit large orientation ambiguity, and FD-164 shows that even the **entire one-horizon Farey field** leaves dense squarefree sign changes invisible. By contrast consecutive physical divisor identities can recover the source triangularly. The live resource is the amount of cross-horizon/source coherence needed to kill the one-horizon kernel without simply assuming full reconstruction.

Nyman--Beurling shows both fake coherence and two distinct prices for adaptation. Finite nested cutoff ladders can collapse to their maximal member, so simultaneous use adds no information. For height-adaptive compact samplers, NB-169 prices literal refreshes, NB-170 total capped-transport variation, and NB-171 the ordered template number via finite `p`-variation. NB-172 then removes path regularity entirely: if the whole range has small capped-transport covering number at source resolution, even infinitely rough switching among those shapes reduces to finitely many stationary exceptional sets.

The two Nyman currencies must not be conflated. **Ordered complexity** measures how the path traverses source-distinguishable states and can exploit shared mean-square bulk. **Range entropy** measures how many distinct states exist at all and ignores how often they are revisited. A path can be expensive in one and cheap in the other. Positive-density rescue has to evade whichever control is actually small; infinite variation alone does not certify rich adaptation.

The reusable test is therefore stronger than asking whether the same source appears at several components or whether an adaptive family is continuous. **Write the simultaneous feasible set, quotient redundant nesting, identify how marginal optimizers can switch, count how many source-resolution states exist, and price the order in which they are used.** Coherence is real proof currency only if joint use shrinks the feasible set or forces a destination-relevant complexity cost that matched switching cannot avoid.

This suggests four legitimate ways forward: retain a component parameter and propagate its quantitative cost; quotient out component identity by a coercive state; add a nonredundant compatibility relation; or prove ordered/range complexity tariffs for the adaptive family. A worst-case scalar envelope can still erase which component pays, so every proposed globalization should be tested against both free switching and low-entropy violent recurrence before it is promoted to an endpoint theorem.

**Boundary.** No numerical estimate transfers between these lines. The Nyman tariffs are proved for the capped Poisson sampler geometry and give density rather than pointwise exclusion. Farey's cross-horizon kernel/coherence problem has different algebraic structure, and Arithmetic Fidelity's support-height modulus is not an adaptation theorem. The shared content is the criterion for when coherence or switching restrictions add information rather than syntax.
