# MI-014 — Cross-component coherence is proof currency only when the simultaneous constraints are nonredundant

**Evidence level:** supported by Arithmetic Fidelity through [AF-327](../../arithmetic_fidelity/findings/AF-327-prime-height-time-separates-lacunary-moment-gram-boundary.md), Farey Discrepancy through [FD-122](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-122-squarefree-dilations-force-fourier-skeleton-antichain-gaps.md), and Nyman--Beurling through [NB-121](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-121-finite-nested-cutoff-coherence-collapses-to-the-maximal-section.md). The mechanisms are logically comparable but mathematically distinct.

Componentwise coercivity can fail under free switching unless the component freedom is priced, removed from the state variable, or coupled by a genuinely new compatibility equation. The current corpus now contains both positive and negative examples of “same-source coherence,” which makes the distinction testable rather than rhetorical.

Arithmetic Fidelity retains and prices the component family. Fixed prime support gives a time-height modulus, while unrestricted support variation destroys every time-only gap. Bounding maximum prime by `H` restores a uniform but degenerating source envelope, and AF-327 transfers that `H,t` control into the determinant/eigenvalue slack of a retained four-feature Gram. Support height is part of the quantitative destination margin rather than an auxiliary label.

Farey Discrepancy shows genuine nonredundant coherence. FD-117--FD-120 allow each horizon separately to approach its Möbius equality ray. FD-121 proves that one common source cannot do so at `N` and `2N`, and FD-122 extends the incompatibility to every fixed squarefree dilation. The decisive mechanism is a repeated-prime coordinate: the two horizons demand equality-ray values that cannot be realized by the same source because `mu(r) ne 0` while `mu(rp)=0`. On a finite prime cube, cheap horizons form an antichain. Sharing the source therefore adds a real constraint that is absent from either marginal problem.

Nyman--Beurling supplies the matched negative control. The natural-cell projections satisfy `Q_R Q_S=Q_R` for `R<=S`, and the Nyman spaces satisfy `V_M subseteq V_N` for `M<=N`. NB-121 proves that for every fixed finite nested ladder the simultaneous constraint set equals exactly the maximal-cutoff/maximal-section constraint set. A common source may change normalization constants, but it adds no new equation and leaves the inverse-section exponent unchanged. Here “coherence” is only a restatement of nesting.

The reusable test is therefore stronger than asking whether the same source appears at several components or scales. **Write the simultaneous feasible set and ask whether it is strictly smaller than the strongest marginal feasible set in a way that charges the destination extremizer.** If the joint constraints project from one maximal member, coherence is fake currency. If the members demand incompatible source coordinates, as in the Farey squarefree collision, the coupling is real but may still require globalization to force the designated component to pay.

This sharpens the older switching lesson. One may retain a component parameter and propagate its quantitative cost into the destination; quotient out component identity by a coercive state; or add a nonredundant compatibility relation whose violation has a destination-priced cost. In all three cases a worst-case scalar envelope can still erase which component pays, so an averaged or pairwise gap must be tested against switching before it is promoted to a pointwise endpoint theorem.

**Boundary.** No numerical potential transfers between these lines. FD-122 still has deteriorating constants and leaves nonsquarefree transitions/pointwise globalization open. NB-121 treats finite nested ladders and does not rule out widening, non-nested, tail-sensitive or actual-zero-power cross-scale laws. AF support height does not determine exact support provenance. The synthesis classifies when coherence can plausibly add information; it does not prove a common coercivity theorem.
