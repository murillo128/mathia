# MI-021 — Representation enrichment buys leverage only when source-specific coupling survives identifiability, exact reduction, closure and the destination quantifier

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of current exact results including AF-416--AF-445, FD-200--FD-226, MC-382--MC-407, NB-217--NB-237, PC-351--PC-364, PL-361--PL-385, RE-216--RE-237, VIS-302--VIS-328, WI-350--WI-363 and WP-366--WP-378. Quantitative mechanisms remain line-specific.

Across Mathia, nominal enrichment keeps failing when added coordinates do not alter the smallest source-sensitive object actually consumed downstream. Conversely, enrichment is useful only when a new relation has an exact source preimage, is identifiable on the admissible source class, survives the final quotient and closure, and remains quantitatively usable at the destination. The relevant question is not how large the representation is, but **what source-specific relation survives source restriction, acquisition, exact algebraic reduction, renormalization, completion/closure, globalization and the final topology/quantifier?**

These examples refine one reusable audit. Identify the admissible source class and effective latent dimension; determine what provenance and numerical weights survive; perform exact Fourier/lcm/Gelfand/triangular/summation-by-parts reductions; identify the finest statistic actually consumed downstream and test it for self-coupling; price localization and inverse stability separately; test moving carriers for source resonance; and classify every completion, norm algebra, commutant, bicommutant or self-adjoint closure before using its invariants. A representation deserves credit only when the source-selected relation survives all of those steps and still improves a load-bearing destination cost.
