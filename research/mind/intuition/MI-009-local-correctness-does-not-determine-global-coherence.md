# MI-009 — Positive global coherence can have a sharp index while its arithmetic scale and even inertia remain regulator-dependent

**Evidence level:** supported by explicit collision constructions and the Fock correlation/finite-part boundaries through WP-265; this is not a statement about every restricted arithmetic source.

Proper marginals do not identify a positive joint law: centered higher-order perturbations can preserve every retained lower-order marginal. The same phenomenon appears in the prime-Cauchy setting, where positive critical kernels can agree on bounded-support valuation data yet differ on higher-order prime interaction.

The Fock branch adds increasingly global versions of the same selection problem. WP-262 shows that exact critical Mangoldt diagonal data plus a bounded positive cross-prime Gram still leave locally finite graph support and edge phases programmable. WP-263 lets the source Hamiltonian choose the obvious weighted complete graph, but critical unit-diagonal normalization collapses every fixed prime-prime correlation because `sum_p1/p` diverges.

WP-264 shows that the normalized collapse hides a nontrivial finite part. Subtracting the divergent diagonal coefficient leaves `R=aa^*-2D` with exactly one positive direction, but the family

\[
R_C=aa^*-2D+CD_W
\]

shows that this Hodge-like signature does not select the finite normalization.

WP-265 strengthens the obstruction from abstract counterterm freedom to **natural regulator dependence**. Leading-only sharp-cutoff subtraction lands at `C=B_1>0` and has infinite positive index; leading-only Gibbs/Abel subtraction lands at `C=B_1-\gamma<0` and has exactly one positive direction. Both regulators are generated from the same source Hamiltonian. Reconciling them at `R_0` requires different finite constants, differing by `\gamma`.

Thus even a seemingly canonical regulator family plus global sign structure does not by itself determine the arithmetic metric. The source-selection problem has at least five distinct gates: exclude hidden higher-order directions, select one correlation geometry, keep it nontrivial at the critical boundary, fix or covariantly transform the finite renormalization, and only then match the completed Weil orientation.

A successful source identity must be derived before comparison with the target. It should either select a regulator for an independent geometric reason or supply a regulator-covariant completion whose finite and archimedean/polar pieces transform together. Choosing a finite constant because it restores index one or the desired Weil coefficient is another form of programmability.

**Boundary.** WP-265 does not show that every global completion is scheme-dependent. It shows that the current critical complete-incidence construction with leading-divergence subtraction lacks an intrinsic regulator-independent inertia and scale.
