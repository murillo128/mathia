# MI-009 — Source-native centering can fix regulator coherence while critical transport still fails analytically

**Evidence level:** supported by explicit collision constructions and the Fock complete-incidence/renormalization boundaries through WP-266; this is not a statement about every arithmetic completion.

Proper marginals do not identify a positive joint law: centered higher-order perturbations can preserve every retained lower-order marginal. The same phenomenon appears in the prime-Cauchy setting, where positive critical kernels can agree on bounded-support valuation data yet differ on higher-order prime interaction.

The Fock branch adds increasingly global versions of the same selection problem. WP-262 shows that exact critical Mangoldt diagonal data plus a bounded positive cross-prime Gram still leave locally finite graph support and edge phases programmable. WP-263 lets the source Hamiltonian choose the obvious weighted complete graph, but critical unit-diagonal normalization collapses every fixed prime-prime correlation because `sum_p1/p` diverges.

WP-264 shows that the normalized collapse hides a nontrivial finite part. WP-265 then strengthens the obstruction from abstract counterterm freedom to natural regulator dependence: leading-only sharp-cutoff and Gibbs/Abel subtraction land in different inertia classes even though both regulators are generated by the same source Hamiltonian.

WP-266 shows that this regulator mismatch is **not** the final obstruction. The simple prime Hamiltonian canonically determines its spectral-diagonal expectation `E_H`. Taking the connected part `(I-E_H)M` cancels the divergent one-body term and the regulator-dependent finite remainder exactly before the limit. Both sharp and Gibbs families converge in trace norm to

\[
K=aa^*-D,
\qquad d_p=\frac{\log p}{p^{3/2}},
\]

and every finite compression has one positive direction with strict negativity on `ker a^*`. Thus a source identity can indeed select correlation geometry and regulator-covariant finite normalization simultaneously.

But local/global coherence has another gate: **the analytic category must survive transport to the target scale**. The canonical Hamiltonian congruence `e^{H/2}` restores the critical Weil prime amplitudes at finite cutoff while preserving inertia, yet globally its rank-one coefficient vector is not in `ell^2`. The critical quadratic form is nonclosable. The canonical connected object is therefore coherent and regulator-independent only one factor `p^{-1}` below the desired half-density.

This refines the global selection problem. It is not enough to choose the right coupling, finite part, or sign class. A successful source identity must keep the construction in a valid global analytic category **at the same critical scale** and supply the missing archimedean/polar completion. Reintroducing a finite diagonal constant fixes neither nonclosability nor the absent Gamma term.

**Boundary.** WP-266 does not show that every global completion is nonclosable. It rules out the direct Hamiltonian rescaling of the canonical connected complete-incidence form and leaves boundary, cohomological, nonlinear, or otherwise genuinely global completions open.
