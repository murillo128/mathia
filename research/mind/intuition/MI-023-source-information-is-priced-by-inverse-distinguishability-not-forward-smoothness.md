# MI-023 — Source information is priced by the declared source class, acquisition topology and destination-relative discrimination geometry

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from [FD-167](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-167-full-field-l2-inversion-has-uniform-square-root-prefix-conditioning.md) through [FD-173](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-173-prime-power-annulus-hitting-exactly-classifies-sparse-midpoint-target-rigidity.md), [MC-342](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-342-forward-source-regularity-does-not-price-analog-capacity.md) through [MC-348](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-348-channel-free-edge-kl-reliability.md), and [NB-176](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-176-euler-weighted-fourier-fingerprints-certify-source-complexity.md) through [NB-182](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-182-euler-wiener-acquisition-prices-non-hilbert-phase-codes.md). No common transform theorem is claimed.

Farey Discrepancy, Möbius Cancellation and Nyman--Beurling expose complementary ways raw information counting can fail. The common question is not how many coordinates a representation has, but what **source class, target notion and acquisition topology** are declared, which source fibres the destination must distinguish, how the observation operator varies, which amplitudes/conditioning are part of the source channel, and what inverse/capacity/reliability scale makes those distinctions usable.

FD-167 gives a positive inverse theorem and FD-168 shows the same conditioning survives severe sparsification inside a fixed raw Fourier basis. FD-169 shows why that count is meaningful only because the acquisition family was fixed: with arbitrary target-aware linear measurements, the inverse can be moved into the sensor and minimum output dimension collapses to target rank.

FD-170 adds a temporal warning. One spatial coordinate is not one scalar degree of information if the horizon operator changes at every observation. Consecutive midpoint values recover the whole formal source by Dirichlet inversion, while FD-171 proves every temporal row is indispensable over the unrestricted normalized formal source class.

FD-172--FD-173 show that even this exact rank statement is source-class and target relative. Inside the bounded squarefree multiplicative cone, midpoint defects become nonnegative transformed mass repeated along each prime-power orbit. A sparse horizon set is Möbius-target-rigid **iff** its half-annuli hit every odd prime-power orbit. Consequently every fixed integer geometric ladder `{B^k}` is exact-target-rigid even though for `B>2` it leaves large multiplicative gaps. Ambient row rank and ordinary coverage are the wrong currencies; the relevant object is whether the declared source architecture forces each admissible target defect to reappear in an observed window, and at what horizon scale that happens.

MC-342 gives the opposite pathology: a scalar transcript can carry essentially `R` source bits while bounded range and benign forward regularity hide the cost in exponentially small inverse separation. MC-343 converts that hidden precision into an SNR bill. MC-344--MC-347 progressively replace fixed noisy query count by channel capacity and then accumulated discrimination divergence as the invariant reliability currency.

MC-348 removes the remaining query syntax. For an arbitrary complete transcript law `P_x`, put the natural neighboring-state graph on the reciprocal source support and charge neighboring source states by symmetrized transcript KL. Vanishing average coordinate error forces the source-averaged edge budget to be `Omega(R log(1/error))` even for one-shot jointly coded, adaptive or interactive observations. Different acquisition models matter only through how their own resources upper-bound this **destination-relative source-edge discrimination**. If the destination does not need some source directions, those directions must first be quotiented rather than charged by habit.

NB-176--NB-180 show the same declaration problem on the dual side. Genuine Euler frequency labels are nonselective if weak channels may be normalized independently; restoring actual Euler amplitudes kills the synthetic code. NB-181 then shows bounded Hilbert acquisition of those true amplitudes cannot sustain growing Hadamard-scale response at bounded norm, while the physical scalar Euler readout lies outside the Hilbert dual.

NB-182 prices that non-Hilbert escape in its natural absolute-convergence topology. After charging each Euler character by its capped-Poisson Lipschitz cost, the Euler--Wiener budget is `W(a) asymp 1/a`; normalized scalar height evaluations and Fourier--Stieltjes mixtures admit a nuclear factorization. Dense rank-`N` phase coding still requires RMS acquisition gain `Omega(sqrt N)`. Changing from Hilbert to Wiener/Dirichlet topology changes the accounting unit but does not erase the source cost. Known high-ordinate cancellation buys an explicit gain `asymp1/(aH_T)`, exposing a square crossover beyond which stronger height-specific arithmetic cancellation is required.

These examples isolate one reusable accounting rule. Before assigning an information cost, state the admissible source class, whether the task is arbitrary recovery, one-target testing or quotient recovery, the acquisition/dual family and its topology, every changing operator or horizon, source amplitudes and allowed renormalization, observation norm/noise model, downstream error tolerance and matched-control geometry. Then prove a resource that survives those declarations: an inverse modulus, orbit-hitting modulus, source-class-relative kernel bound, stable singular values, channel capacity, source-edge transcript divergence, or charged dual response that a matched control cannot reproduce under the same source weights and functional class.

Forward smoothness, raw feature count, positive noise, nominal spatial dimension, adaptivity, frequency membership and canonical probe labels are secondary descriptors. Sparse observations can be enough when source constraints repeatedly expose every target defect; arbitrary joint coding still pays if the destination needs reliable neighboring-state separation; and source-faithful non-Hilbert acquisition can remain expensive even when bounded Hilbert duality was the wrong topology.

**Boundary.** The cited results concern different Farey, finite-source and Nyman models and do not supply a universal acquisition theorem or canonical noise/dual norm. FD-173 is one-target rigidity in a strong multiplicative cone, not pairwise injectivity. MC-348 prices Hamming recovery on one reciprocal source graph, not every arithmetic destination. NB-182 prices the absolute Euler/Fourier--Stieltjes acquisition class but leaves stronger height-specific cancellation open. The synthesis is an audit rule: information budgets become mathematical only after the source fibre, destination quotient and full acquisition topology are fixed rather than optimized away.
