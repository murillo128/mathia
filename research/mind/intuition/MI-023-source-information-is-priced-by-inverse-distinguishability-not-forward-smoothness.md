# MI-023 — Source information is priced by the declared acquisition primitive and its inverse/capacity/reliability geometry

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from [FD-167](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-167-full-field-l2-inversion-has-uniform-square-root-prefix-conditioning.md), [FD-168](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-168-prefix-mobius-rows-give-coordinate-minimal-stable-partial-farey-sampling.md), [FD-169](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-169-unrestricted-mixed-measurements-collapse-to-target-rank.md), [FD-170](../../farey_discrepancy/findings/FD-170-midpoint-horizon-path-is-a-dirichlet-invertible-temporal-sensor.md), [MC-342](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-342-forward-source-regularity-does-not-price-analog-capacity.md), [MC-343](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-343-gaussian-noise-transcript-capacity.md), [MC-344](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-344-bit-flip-source-readout-capacity.md), [MC-345](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-345-adaptive-source-query-feedback-capacity.md), [MC-346](../../mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-346-adaptive-raw-bit-reliability-overhead.md), [NB-176](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-176-euler-weighted-fourier-fingerprints-certify-source-complexity.md), [NB-177](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-177-square-summable-euler-spectrum-caps-canonical-complexity-certificate.md), [NB-178](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-178-coordinatewise-phase-codes-recover-linear-source-complexity-certificates.md), and [NB-179](../../nyman_beurling/findings/NB-179-poisson-separated-dipoles-realize-maximal-coordinatewise-phase-codes-without-arithmetic.md). No common transform theorem is claimed.

Farey Discrepancy, Möbius Cancellation and Nyman--Beurling expose complementary ways raw information counting can fail. The common question is not how many coordinates a representation has, but what **acquisition or dual-access primitive** is allowed, which source fibres it identifies, how the operator itself varies across observations, and at what inverse/capacity/reliability scale those distinctions remain usable.

FD-167 gives a positive inverse theorem and FD-168 shows the same conditioning survives severe sparsification inside a fixed raw Fourier basis. FD-169 shows why that count is meaningful only because the acquisition family was fixed: with arbitrary target-aware linear measurements, the inverse can be moved into the sensor and the minimum output dimension collapses to target rank.

FD-170 adds a second warning. A single spatial coordinate is not one scalar degree of information if it is observed through a different horizon operator each time. Consecutive midpoint values have increments equal to a Dirichlet convolution with the odd-indicator, and Möbius inversion on odd divisors reconstructs every source coefficient. The temporal/operator sequence is source-complete even though the spatial sensor never moves. Any measurement budget must therefore charge the complete acquisition state, not only simultaneous output dimension.

MC-342 gives the opposite pathology: a scalar transcript can carry essentially `R` source bits while bounded range and benign forward regularity hide the cost in exponentially small inverse separation. MC-343 converts that hidden precision into an SNR bill. MC-344--MC-345 show that once the primitive is fixed to memoryless noisy coordinate queries, each actual access contributes at most the channel capacity even under feedback and adaptive stopping.

MC-346 separates capacity from reliability. Fixed nontrivial source information may cost only `Theta(R)` accesses, but if the final raw-coordinate error `bar p` must vanish then pairwise change-of-measure forces

`E[tau] = Omega_q(R log(1/bar p))`

at fixed BSC noise. Thus the relevant cost depends not only on how much mutual information reaches the transcript but also on the **resolution at which the destination needs the source to be distinguished**.

NB-176--NB-179 show the same declaration problem on the dual side. The canonical Euler feature map is source-native and jointly Hilbert-contracting, but its square-summed spectrum can be compact enough to under-certify source complexity. Coordinatewise scalar contractions enlarge the witness class and can recover a linear certificate from a Hadamard response, yet NB-179 constructs the same maximal response from source-free Poisson-width dipole packing. Enlarging the dual can remove a witness-induced ceiling while simultaneously making the witness nonselective.

These examples isolate one reusable accounting rule. Before assigning an information cost, state the admissible source class, the acquisition/dual family, every changing operator or horizon, the target quotient, the observation norm or noise model, the downstream error tolerance and the matched-control geometry. Then prove a resource that survives those declarations: inverse modulus, stable singular values, capacity per admitted access, reliability divergence, or charged dual response that cannot be reproduced by generic packing.

Forward smoothness, raw feature count, positive noise, nominal spatial dimension, adaptivity and “canonical” probe labels are secondary descriptors. Sparse coordinates can be enough when the inverse is uniformly conditioned; one spatial coordinate can be complete when the operator changes through a rich hierarchy; unrestricted mixtures can make count tautological; feedback cannot create free memoryless capacity; vanishing error can add a logarithmic reliability tariff; one jointly square-summed witness can become compact; and an unrestricted coordinatewise witness can be too expressive to distinguish arithmetic from ambient metric coding.

**Boundary.** The cited results concern different finite-source/Farey/Nyman models and do not supply a universal acquisition theorem or canonical noise/dual norm. This synthesis is an audit rule: information budgets become mathematical only after the full acquisition and resolution geometry is fixed rather than optimized away.
