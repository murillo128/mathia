# MI-012 — Information survival, admissible completion and destination sensitivity are separate resources

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of current line-local exact results, including AF-365--AF-385, FD-145/159--160, MC-316, NB-145/149/154, PC-307/313/322, PL-321--PL-323, RE-148/153/158, VIS-241/245, WI-241/307/310--311 and WP-318/322--324/336. Each mechanism remains line-specific; no estimate is transferred between objects.

A representation can retain a decisive discriminator and still be unusable because the destination consumes it through an unstable topology, a stronger target, a moving quantifier or an observation whose admissible completions remain target-mixed. Conversely, a transform that discards most source detail can remain faithful to the exact quotient the endpoint needs. The right question is not “how much information survives?” but **which source distinction survives, over which source class, after which natural completion/closure, and with what modulus at the declared destination**.

Arithmetic Fidelity separates source identification, relational arity and certificate geometry. AF-369--AF-370 show that small retained data can become exact once the admissible source class is rigid enough, yet source identification still need not select zeros. AF-371--AF-372 show that a target-faithful Schoenberg translation relation cannot in general be truncated at any fixed determinant order. AF-383--AF-385 then show how misleading raw certificate size can be: at fixed order, arbitrary placement geometry collapses first to one consecutive-row family and, under a sampled strict-interior hierarchy, all the way to solid relative-offset generators. The completion theorem is exact, but its hidden resources remain unbounded order for `PF_infinity`, infinitely many offsets, shrinking spatial scale, strict lower-order sampled signs and arithmetic provenance of those signs. Compression of presentation is not compression of every proof obligation.

Farey Discrepancy supplies the closure analogue. FD-159 shows that divisor-downward truncation is governed by average occupied primary fibre, not formal cube size. FD-160 allows arbitrary sparse holes and initially adds an incoming hole-flux term, but the destination's natural divisor closure absorbs those holes while preserving primary depth and reinstates the same exponential cut pressure. Sparsity that disappears under the relevant closure is not an independent resource; only structure that remains different after closure can alter the coercivity problem.

Prime Circle gives the compression/criticality analogue. Exact source data can be recoverable at mesh scale while the chosen destination has already become generic decoding. The first direct mixed-conductor bilinear repair classicalizes to a periodic Dirichlet `L`-bundle, and PC-313 shows that even adjoining the Fourier dual after this compression only produces the universal Hurwitz swap functional equation. PC-322 sharpens the same warning in a canonical intermediate slice: Green inversion selects `u=1`, but the resulting Hilbert matrix and `Re s=1/2` square-summability boundary are shared by every nonzero periodic matched source. A natural parameter and critical axis can therefore be destination-universal even when the source coefficients remain arithmetic.

Prime Lattice shows the converse: an exact source identity can lower the destination regularity bill. PL-322 makes the residual moment automatic by conservative endpoint-flux cancellation; PL-323 uses the source-coupled coercive equation to force `Qm->0`; PL-321 then transports the conditional moment through the moving Green kernel. The previous independent singular-trace/absolute-`L^1` requirements were sufficient-proof artifacts rather than true resources. What remains is arithmetic orientation of the transferred scalar, not more ambient endpoint regularity.

Nyman--Beurling demonstrates that changing sign or geometry can merely renormalize the bill. NB-148 cancels the universal Hadamard term with a zero-mass signed sampler but exports an equal negative lobe; NB-149 shows that Jordan/total-variation domination restores the positive budget. NB-154 shows that moving the compact sampler toward `Re s=1` multiplies useful target amplification and the worst source/adverse amplification by the same `1/a_G`; the relevant condition number is `C_G/a_G`. A geometric gain is informative only after common amplification of signal and contamination is divided out.

Robin Extremal identifies a target-specific source average rather than a stronger global norm. RE-153 rewrites the self-tangent counterexample condition as a `sqrt(2)` lower bound on a Pareto-weighted Chebyshev deficit. RE-158 further shows that exact terminal count and total Chebyshev mass still leave a quadratic internal-placement degree of freedom above the fourth-root scale. Endpoint summaries and internal destination-weighted source geometry are distinct resources.

Visual Exploration and Weil Positivity expose calibration failures in different categories. VIS-245 makes the fixed-parameter covariance of nuisance statistic `C` and confirmation statistic `bar T` exact, but a same-sample plug-in test still needs their conditional/full joint law. WP-322--WP-324 show that several finite positive kernels are universal before arithmetic selectivity enters. WP-336 adds a stability version: channel norm alone is not the resource for a growing coordinate, but the corrected quantity `||Psi-I|| sup L_-L_+` sharply controls its Kadison defect. Even the correct ambient category must be paired with the scale actually consumed by the destination.

Weil Inertia adds a representation-stability distinction. WI-307 turns a frozen essential obstruction into a finite negative-index problem. WI-310 shows that an unbounded retained positive tail can manufacture arbitrarily deep negative spectrum in the complement, so the exact split must be bounded on both sides. WI-311 goes further: even a bounded tail floor can become nearly maximally positive while carrying almost no independent simplification. Smooth frequency multiplexing makes `T` arbitrarily close to `(1/2)I`, and the exact identity `Q=D+T` simultaneously makes `D` arbitrarily close to `Q-(1/2)I`. A large positive remainder is therefore not a resource when the complement simply tracks the original unknown source by scalar subtraction.

The cross-line accounting rule is therefore: **track source supply, admissible completion/closure, quotient fidelity, relational arity, generator completeness hypotheses, anchoring, topology, moving scale, scalarization category, target conditioning, geometric amplification, sign debt, source moments, nuisance propagation, representation stability, obstruction type and final rate compatibility separately until the last implication**. A route is promising only when it identifies a resource a matched control cannot fake and proves that the destination consumes it without reconstructing, completing away, rescaling or merely restating the endpoint.

**Boundary.** This is an accounting principle, not a theorem transferring estimates between lines. The examples use different objects, norms and admissible categories; only the separation of proof obligations is shared.
