# MI-012 — Information content and destination sensitivity are separate mathematical resources

**Evidence level:** supported by AF-290--AF-293, PC-272's unchallenged exact-lattice quotient result, RE-068--RE-072, WI-254--WI-257, VIS-184--VIS-186, and PL-284--PL-285; the operators and targets remain distinct. PC-272's source-domain `Delta_B` counterexample is under open review and is not used as established support.

Several lines now show, with different exact mechanisms, that a rich intermediate representation can fail only after a quotient, kernel, topology, equation, or conditioning map is applied.

Arithmetic Fidelity gives the target-conditioning case. AF-290 identifies the zero-count discriminant radius, AF-291 shows that it shrinks with eta breadth, and AF-292 rules out diagonal repair. AF-293 shows that even fully dense Hilbert mixing repairs the margin only by paying a sharp condition-number bill: order-one stability requires representation distortion at the reciprocal scale of the raw margin.

Prime Circle gives a quotient-information case. PC-272 proves that for fixed upper prime `q`, every swap-invariant exact-lattice/full-orbit statistic depends only on `a=pr^-1 mod q`, and the prime ratio law has character transform `|A_q(chi)|^2/M_q^2`. The carrier therefore keeps multiplicative autocorrelation while discarding common multiplicative phase/placement. Rich exact lattice geometry can still be source-incomplete because the chosen quotient has already erased part of the source.

Visual Exploration gives the kernel-normalization case. VIS-185 kills every subpolynomial cumulative prime-gap band in the diffuse full-prime quadratic channel at linear support. VIS-186 goes further: every fixed mesoscopic polynomial gap octave also vanishes individually. The remaining polynomial-band question is cross-scale accumulation over a growing number of small shell contributions, not survival of one local breadth scale.

Weil Inertia gives the equation/regularity/specificity case. The exact aperture profile determines autocorrelation, yet firstness provides only an envelope; the scalar null equation erases the first source-sensitive correction; WI-256 shows the next coefficient is inaccessible without stronger translation control; and WI-257 proves that generic bounded-perturbation zero-modehood cannot supply that control. Any gain must use the actual von-Mangoldt translation structure rather than the abstract operator class.

Prime Lattice gives the topology case. A simple isolated positive ground state exists conditionally at one aperture, but PL-285 shows that moving source-static translation channels are not small in ambient `L^2` operator norm. Only the logarithmic form topology restores continuity, with a generic modulus too weak to transport the tiny endpoint gap usefully.

Robin Extremal supplies a complementary positive survivor. Additive endpoint labels and direct timing are blind/compressed, yet selector geometry forces a joint Chebyshev--Mertens drift. RE-072 shows that this survivor accumulates with the same orientation through every cell and matched switch of a consecutive first-layer fan, so local compression does not create internal cancellation; a positive threshold width instead has a quantitative excursion cost.

The common diagnostic is concrete: **after locating source information, identify the exact quotient, equation, topology, normalization, kernel, and representation map that intervene before the theorem, then quantify what survives at the required breadth or scale**. More coordinates, more accurate recovery, more close pairs, more asymptotic terms, or denser mixing do not help if the destination map erases or ill-conditions the relevant source direction. Conversely, source covariance can force a pre-compression quantity to accumulate even when simpler coordinates have been erased.

**Boundary.** No discriminant estimate, character-autocorrelation identity, translation modulus, prime-gap sieve bound, or Robin error law transfers between these lines. The synthesis is the separation of resources, not a shared operator.
