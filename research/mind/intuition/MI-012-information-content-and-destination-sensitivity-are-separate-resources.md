# MI-012 — Information survival, admissible completion and destination sensitivity are separate resources

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis of current line-local exact results, including AF-365--AF-372, FD-145, MC-316, NB-145/149/154, PC-307/313, PL-321--PL-323, RE-148/153/158, VIS-241/245, WI-241/307/310--311 and WP-318/322--324. Each mechanism remains line-specific; no estimate is transferred between objects.

A representation can retain a decisive discriminator and still be unusable because the destination consumes it through an unstable topology, a stronger target, a moving quantifier or an observation whose admissible completions remain target-mixed. Conversely, a transform that discards most source detail can remain faithful to the exact quotient the endpoint needs. The right question is not “how much information survives?” but **which source distinction survives, over which source class, and with what modulus at the declared destination**.

Arithmetic Fidelity now separates further currencies. AF-365 proves that global eventual Riesz control preserves Dirichlet principal parts above the corresponding half-plane. AF-366 shows finite-prefix fibres contain invisible pole-carrying tails. AF-367--AF-368 keep source/zero geometry mixed under strong generalized-prime counting constraints. AF-369--AF-370 show that small retained data can become exact once the admissible source class is rigid enough. AF-371--AF-372 add the certificate layer: an infinite Hankel-PSD signature can remain target-mixed, while the correct Schoenberg translation relation is target-faithful but cannot in general be truncated at any fixed determinant order. Source identification, quotient geometry and relational arity are different resources.

Prime Circle gives the compression analogue. Exact source data can be recoverable at mesh scale while the chosen destination has already become generic decoding. The first direct mixed-conductor bilinear repair then classicalizes to a periodic Dirichlet `L`-bundle. PC-313 shows that even adjoining the Fourier dual after this compression only produces the universal Hurwitz swap functional equation. Increasing state dimension after a lossy/classical scalarization is not automatically new source information when the extra components are generated by a universal transform.

Prime Lattice shows the converse: an exact source identity can lower the destination regularity bill. PL-322 makes the residual moment automatic by conservative endpoint-flux cancellation; PL-323 uses the source-coupled coercive equation to force `Qm->0`; PL-321 then transports the conditional moment through the moving Green kernel. The previous independent singular-trace/absolute-`L^1` requirements were sufficient-proof artifacts rather than true resources. What remains is arithmetic orientation of the transferred scalar, not more ambient endpoint regularity.

Nyman--Beurling demonstrates that changing sign or geometry can merely renormalize the bill. NB-148 cancels the universal Hadamard term with a zero-mass signed sampler but exports an equal negative lobe; NB-149 shows that Jordan/total-variation domination restores the positive budget. NB-154 shows that moving the compact sampler toward `Re s=1` multiplies useful target amplification and the worst source/adverse amplification by the same `1/a_G`; the relevant condition number is `C_G/a_G`. A geometric gain is informative only after common amplification of signal and contamination is divided out.

Robin Extremal identifies a target-specific source average rather than a stronger global norm. RE-153 rewrites the self-tangent counterexample condition as a `sqrt(2)` lower bound on a Pareto-weighted Chebyshev deficit. RE-158 further shows that exact terminal count and total Chebyshev mass still leave a quadratic internal-placement degree of freedom above the fourth-root scale. Endpoint summaries and internal destination-weighted source geometry are distinct resources.

Visual Exploration and Weil Positivity expose two different calibration failures. VIS-245 makes the fixed-parameter covariance of nuisance statistic `C` and confirmation statistic `bar T` exact, but a same-sample plug-in test still needs their conditional/full joint law. WP-322--WP-323 show that exact finite cross-gap scalar Pick positivity and finite confluent jets are universal resolvent Grams; WP-324 shows that changing to the direct positive-source Laplace translation kernel does not fix selectivity—the translation minors have a universal alternating sign, while the reversed Hankel orientation is universally positive. Arithmetic entries are not the same resource as arithmetic provenance of the sign.

Weil Inertia adds a representation-stability distinction. WI-307 turns a frozen essential obstruction into a finite negative-index problem. WI-310 shows that an unbounded retained positive tail can manufacture arbitrarily deep negative spectrum in the complement, so the exact split must be bounded on both sides. WI-311 goes further: even a bounded tail floor can become nearly maximally positive while carrying almost no independent simplification. Smooth frequency multiplexing makes `T` arbitrarily close to `(1/2)I`, and the exact identity `Q=D+T` simultaneously makes `D` arbitrarily close to `Q-(1/2)I`. A large positive remainder is therefore not a resource when the complement simply tracks the original unknown source by scalar subtraction.

The cross-line accounting rule is therefore: **track source supply, admissible completion, quotient fidelity, relational arity, anchoring, topology, scalarization category, target conditioning, geometric amplification, sign debt, source moments, nuisance propagation, representation stability, obstruction type and final rate compatibility separately until the last implication**. A route is promising only when it identifies a resource a matched control cannot fake and proves that the destination consumes it without reconstructing, rescaling, or merely restating the endpoint.

**Boundary.** This is an accounting principle, not a theorem transferring estimates between lines. The examples use different objects, norms and admissible categories; only the separation of proof obligations is shared.
