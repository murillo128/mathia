# MI-017 — Target-conditioned thin channels matter only if they survive closure and the required family quantifier

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from NB-147--NB-168, FD-147--FD-160, MC-319--MC-322, RE-145/RE-151--RE-158 and PL-321--PL-323. The mathematical objects and scales remain line-specific.

A recurring obstruction is quantitatively clearer: **small ambient population is not small destination influence**, but neither is syntactic sparsity automatically a surviving resource. A target may concentrate decisive mass on a thin shell, deep ancestry layer, signed lobe, exceptional height or moving stage that generic population controls underweight. The relevant questions are how much leverage the destination assigns to that set, whether the set survives the natural closure/completion of the destination geometry, and over which family of parameters the mechanism must persist.

Robin Extremal exposes a hierarchy rather than one terminal scale. RE-153 identifies the self-tangent source requirement as a Pareto-weighted average of `(t-vartheta(t))/sqrt(t)`. RE-156 finds an ordinary-prime relocation transition because both influence range and locally available supply shrink with terminal width. RE-157 removes the local-deletion cap using PNT-small generalized-prime insertion packets; RE-158 then matches terminal count and total Chebyshev mass exactly, cancels the linear placement moment and exposes quadratic placement spread. A terminal source theorem must specify which moments it controls; endpoint agreement in too few moments can still leave a thin internally placed channel with order-one destination leverage.

Farey Discrepancy supplies a closure test for sparse architecture. FD-149--FD-151 show that bounded-distortion ancestry must either reach typical squarefree factor depth or pay for a macroscopic anchor. FD-158--FD-159 then show that completed and divisor-downward auxiliary vocabularies do not dilute the exponential cut pressure. FD-160 closes the apparent non-downward-hole escape: an arbitrary sparse support pays explicit incoming hole flux, and taking the divisor closure of its depth-`r` cores internalizes that flux without losing depth, restoring an `h(2^r-1)` pressure law. Even one deep sparse core triggers the obstruction through its principal divisor ideal. Here sparsity is presentation-level unless it remains sparse under divisor closure.

Nyman--Beurling adds an orthogonal **family-quantifier** test. NB-167 leaves open arithmetic cancellation in the actual values of `zeta'/zeta`. NB-168 fixes one compact signed offset template and translates it across a dyadic height block. Montgomery--Vaughan mean-square control then shows that `q_T log T`-scale Euler rescue occupies only a density-zero set of centers whenever the one-Poisson-width mismatch satisfies `Xi_T sqrt(1+(T a_T^2)^(-1))=o(log T)`. Thus a rare phase coincidence may survive pointwise while failing as a persistent mechanism for a stationary architecture. Conversely, density-zero does not exclude a deliberately sparse sequence of selected target heights; that requires an additional robustness/counting bridge.

Möbius Cancellation gives an observation-population analogue. MC-320 shows that strong control on ambient integer intervals can coexist with maximal bias on the exact dyadic prime shell. MC-321--MC-322 then attack the correct prime-supported population and obtain a family energy bound, replacing raw character count by an `ell^2` shell-bias budget below the cubic modulus scale. A theorem becomes relevant only when it is stated on the population the destination actually consumes.

Earlier Nyman--Beurling results supply oversmoothing, conditioning and calibration versions. NB-150 shows that mesoscopic spreading fills both signed lobes with ordinary zero mass while diluting a narrow target; NB-151--NB-153 turn compact zero-mass targeting into a neighboring adverse population by completed-function conservation; NB-154 shows boundary approach amplifies signal and contamination together. NB-168 adds that even source-specific phase cancellation must be tested for persistence across height rather than inferred from isolated favorable centers.

Prime Lattice gives the complementary structural escape. PL-322--PL-323 change the destination gate itself and remove the generic moving-diagonal obstruction, so no exceptional-population theorem is needed there; the residual problem becomes the rational-prime-specific sign/first-crossing law.

The reusable diagnostic is destination-weighted, closure-aware and quantifier-aware. Identify the exact set/mode/shell/depth/sign component the target consumes; compute its leverage before judging it negligible from ambient density; identify the natural closure or completion that the destination can apply without new source information; ask whether apparent sparsity survives that operation; then state whether the mechanism must hold pointwise, on positive density, uniformly across a block, or only along a selected sparse sequence. Distinguish global source closeness from local supply and matched source moments, and normalize conditioning by any amplification acting on both signal and error.

A vanishing source fraction can matter if the destination magnifies it, sparse holes can be fake if divisor closure reinstates full pressure, and a density-zero exceptional set can still matter if the architecture is allowed to select exactly those centers. Thinness is therefore a resource only when it is both destination-visible **and compatible with the closure and family quantifiers of the claimed theorem**.

**Boundary.** The examples use unrelated measures, operators, graph depths and arithmetic families. No common exponent, closure theorem or density conclusion transfers between them. The synthesis is the logical requirement to measure source smallness, local supply, retained moments, observation breadth, conditioning, closure geometry and parameter-family quantifier in the destination's own category before declaring a thin channel harmless or a sparse architecture genuinely different.
