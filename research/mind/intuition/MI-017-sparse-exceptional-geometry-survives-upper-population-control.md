# MI-017 — Target-conditioned thin channels can carry order-one leverage while typical-population controls see almost nothing

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from NB-147--NB-154, FD-147--FD-151, MC-319--MC-322, RE-145/RE-151--RE-158 and PL-321--PL-323. The mathematical objects and scales remain line-specific.

A recurring obstruction is now quantitatively clearer: **small ambient population is not small destination influence**, and conversely a broad or ill-conditioned observation can move a target-conditioned debt without removing it. A target may concentrate decisive mass on a thin shell, deep ancestry layer, signed lobe or exceptional stage that generic population/interval controls underweight. The relevant question is not how rare the source feature is globally, but how much mass the destination assigns to it and which conditioning, localization, source moments and population cost are paid to isolate it.

Robin Extremal now exposes a hierarchy rather than one terminal scale. RE-153 identifies the self-tangent source requirement as a Pareto-weighted average of `(t-vartheta(t))/sqrt(t)`. RE-156 finds an ordinary-prime relocation transition

`epsilon_*(p)=sqrt(log U_A(p)/(sqrt(p)log p))`,

because both the influence range and the number of locally available ordinary primes shrink with terminal width. RE-157 removes the local-deletion cap using PNT-small generalized-prime insertion packets; with only cardinality matched, the surviving first placement moment pushes order-one ambiguity down to

`epsilon_PNT(p)=log U_A(p)/(sqrt(p)log p)=epsilon_*(p)^2`.

RE-158 then matches terminal count **and** total Chebyshev mass exactly. That cancels the full linear placement moment and exposes quadratic placement spread, restoring the ambiguity threshold to `epsilon_*` for this more informative observation class. The shared exponent with RE-156 has a different cause: supply scarcity in one case, moment cancellation/curvature in the other. Thus a terminal source theorem must specify which moments it controls; endpoint agreement can still leave an internally placed thin channel with order-one destination leverage.

Farey Discrepancy gives the depth/anchor analogue. FD-149 shows bounded-distortion ancestry must reach essentially typical squarefree factor depth. FD-151 makes the anchor bill explicit: if `p_T` is the directly oriented anchor mass, parent distortion obeys `h<=P_T p_T/(1-p_T)`. Keeping parent expansion bounded therefore requires positive-density direct orientation; sparse anchors merely move the information cost into the same thin arithmetic population one hoped to infer.

Möbius Cancellation gives an observation-population analogue. MC-320 shows that strong control on ambient integer intervals can coexist with maximal bias on the exact dyadic prime shell. MC-321--MC-322 then attack the correct prime-supported population and obtain a family energy bound, replacing raw character count by an `ell^2` shell-bias budget below the cubic modulus scale. A theorem becomes relevant only when it is stated on the population the destination actually consumes.

Nyman--Beurling supplies oversmoothing, conditioning and calibration versions. NB-150 proves that moving a signed two-point sampler to any growing mesoscopic horizontal scale `1<<L_G=o(G)` fills both lobes with `Theta(log G)` actual-zero mass while the narrow target is diluted. NB-151--NB-153 show that compact zero-mass sampling of a logarithmic target packet instead forces a neighboring adverse population by completed-function conservation. On a fixed compact exterior set, `C_G=||mu_G||_TV/q_G=o(log G)` leaves a fixed fraction of the adverse charge within radius `O(C_G)` and requires at least `Omega(log G/C_G)` adverse zeros.

NB-154 shows that moving the support toward `Re s=1` merely changes the correct conditioning variable. If `a_G` is the minimum horizontal gap, the useful target amplification, the absolute Euler remainder and the maximum adverse point charge all scale by `1/a_G`. The effective condition number is `D_G=C_G/a_G`; throughout `D_G=o(log G)`, logarithmic adverse charge still survives and at least `Omega(log G/D_G)` adverse zeros are required. Boundary proximity is not a free localization resource when signal and contamination are amplified together.

Prime Lattice gives the complementary structural escape. PL-322--PL-323 change the destination gate itself and remove the generic moving-diagonal obstruction, so no exceptional-population theorem is needed there; the residual problem becomes the rational-prime-specific sign/first-crossing law.

The reusable diagnostic is destination-weighted and quantifier-sensitive. Identify the exact set/mode/shell/depth/sign component the target consumes; compute its leverage before judging it negligible from ambient density; distinguish global source closeness from local source supply and matched source moments; normalize conditioning by any geometric amplification that acts on both signal and error; state whether the source theorem is average, uniform, target-conditioned or prime-restricted; and price any localization used to isolate the target. A vanishing source fraction, endpoint agreement in too few moments, an interval theorem on the wrong population, a typical-center law, a mesoscopic average that swamps a narrow packet, or boundary amplification that rescales both signal and debt has not closed the gate.

**Boundary.** The examples use unrelated measures, operators, graph depths and arithmetic families. No common exponent or estimate transfers between them. The synthesis is the logical requirement to measure source smallness, local supply, retained moments, observation breadth and conditioning in the destination's own weighting/category before declaring a thin channel harmless or a broad/ill-conditioned observation informative.
