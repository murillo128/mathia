# MI-017 — Target-conditioned thin channels can carry order-one leverage while typical-population controls see almost nothing

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from NB-147--NB-150, FD-147--FD-149, MC-319--MC-320, RE-145/RE-151--RE-154 and PL-321--PL-323. The mathematical objects and scales remain line-specific.

A recurring obstruction is now quantitatively clearer: **small ambient population is not small destination influence**, and conversely a broad averaging scale can make a narrow target disappear inside a large background. A target may concentrate its decisive mass on a thin shell, deep ancestry layer, signed lobe or exceptional stage that generic population/interval controls underweight. The relevant question is not how rare the source feature is globally, but how much mass the destination assigns to it and whether the source theorem is conditioned on the same geometry.

Robin Extremal gives the sharpest sensitivity law. RE-153 identifies the self-tangent source requirement as a Pareto-weighted average of `(t-vartheta(t))/sqrt(t)`. RE-154 then shows that moving only `sqrt(p)/log p` labels after a fixed multiplicative selector—an `asymp p^(-1/2)` fraction of a proportional prime shell—changes that normalized destination average by order one. Each label has influence `asymp log(p)/sqrt(p)`. Exact agreement below `Bp` and PNT-scale density therefore leave the target-bearing channel uncontrolled.

Farey Discrepancy gives the depth analogue. FD-147 identifies finite-`L^q` binary ancestry recovery with anchored cut exposure. FD-148 shows that one-step bounded marginals cannot expose the fixed seed without singular endpoint weight. FD-149 proves that bounded-distortion nonlocal ancestry must reach essentially the typical squarefree factor depth: positive expansion with `K=O(1)` forces `L_T >= log log T-O(sqrt(log log T))`. A relation that reaches only a vanishing low-rank population is still invisible to the macroscopic cut even when the underlying graph is connected.

Möbius Cancellation gives an observation-population analogue. MC-319 derives genuine smooth-common-modulus zero-free rigidity but only at a resolution coarser than the endpoint. MC-320 constructs an imprimitive smooth-modulus character with polylogarithmically bounded sums on every polynomial-length integer interval while retaining maximal bias on the exact dyadic prime shell. Strong control on ambient integers can therefore coexist with order-one destination bias after restriction to the source-sensitive prime subset.

Nyman--Beurling supplies both the sign and oversmoothing versions. NB-148 exports an equal negative Poisson lobe when the universal Hadamard logarithm is cancelled; NB-149 shows that Jordan domination destroys the signed gain. NB-150 then proves that moving a two-point signed sampler to any growing mesoscopic horizontal scale `1<<L_G=o(G)` makes **both** lobes carry `Theta(log G)` actual-zero mass by Riemann--von Mangoldt density, while an `O(log G)` target packet inside `o(L_G)` contributes only `O(log G/L_G)`. Here the failure is the reverse of sparse leverage: broadening the observation to gain separation dilutes the target into a universal background.

Robin's reciprocal-packet branch adds the exceptional-quantifier version: RE-151--RE-152 allow lacunary target-conditioned hiding while fixed-order typical-center statistics remain unchanged. Prime Lattice gives the complementary escape: PL-322--PL-323 change the destination gate itself and remove the generic moving-diagonal obstruction, so no exceptional-population theorem is needed there; the residual problem is arithmetic orientation.

The reusable diagnostic is therefore destination-weighted and quantifier-sensitive. Identify the exact set/mode/shell/depth/sign component the target consumes; compute its leverage before judging it negligible from ambient density; state whether the source theorem is average, uniform, target-conditioned or prime-restricted; and check whether enlarging the observation scale actually strengthens the target signal or merely admits more universal background. A vanishing source fraction, an interval theorem on the wrong population, a typical-center law, or a mesoscopic average that swamps a narrow packet has not closed the gate.

**Boundary.** The examples use unrelated measures, operators, graph depths and arithmetic families. No common exponent or estimate transfers between them. The synthesis is the logical requirement to measure source smallness and observation breadth in the destination's own weighting/conditioning category before declaring a thin channel harmless or a broad average informative.
