# MI-015 — A relaxation is exhausted until its surviving extremizer pays in the exact destination currency

**Evidence level:** supported cross-line synthesis from MC-276--MC-277, PF-333--PF-334, WI-282--WI-283, FD-119--FD-121, NB-118--NB-120, and VIS-217--VIS-219. This is a diagnostic about the current relaxed classes, not a universal impossibility theorem.

A proposed simplification is exhausted when its admissible class still contains an exact or asymptotic extremizer at the forbidden budget **in the norm or loss actually used by the endpoint**. No sharper estimate using only the retained hypotheses can then create the missing gap. The number of exact constraints is not the relevant measure: what matters is the cost of imposing them near the extremizer in the destination currency.

Möbius Cancellation makes the destination-functional test explicit. Imposing the correct blind-point value and minimizing `L^2` classification error gives exactly the Christoffel kernel, with a loss that subtracts the blind mass itself. MC-277 shows that the original scalar representation still has exact blind/nonblind margin; the relaxation is exhausted because its aggregation discards the endpoint atom, not because the representation lacks information.

Weil Inertia supplies a support-topology extremizer test. Complete prime-power screening plus nonnegativity allows the length-`log 2` packing optimizer; qualitative full span and source-specific support domination can be added with vanishing-mass repairs. A strict decrement must therefore force quantitative amplitude or operator balance that excludes those near-extremizers rather than adding more qualitative support facts.

Farey Discrepancy now shows both sides of the diagnostic. FD-119--FD-120 prove that even linearly many exact single-horizon source constraints can be imposed while preserving critical near-saturation. FD-121 then adds a genuinely expensive relation: if one common source is used at `N` and `2N`, the two excess energies satisfy a fixed positive combined lower bound. The relaxed class is therefore **no longer jointly saturated across the dyadic pair**. But this does not yet prove the pointwise endpoint, because a common source may alternate between a cheap horizon and an expensive neighbor. Once a relaxation starts charging the extremizer, the next question is whether that charge lands on the destination component or can switch elsewhere.

Nyman--Beurling shows why growing the number of exact equations is still not a cost metric. NB-118 forces strong target rescue into inverse-section retention under fixed Nyman orthogonality; NB-119 constructs perfect-target controls saturating `Theta(1/R)`. NB-120 lets the Nyman section grow with `M^6 log M << R` and the repair constant converges to a finite positive limit. Thus an increasing number of exact source equations still leaves the destination extremizer at the same inverse-section scale. What could change the conclusion is not equation count but a source law—actual zero-power structure, cross-cutoff coherence, or a faster section regime—that makes the repair itself expensive.

Prime Flute adds the necessary norm check. In operator norm, PF-333 shows the generic positive relative-square-root class already has logarithmic sensitivity, so positivity alone cannot produce a dimension-free `S_infinity` theorem. PF-334 proves the same map is dimension-free in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, which is the currency the downstream theorem consumes. A bad extremizer in the wrong norm is irrelevant; exhaustion must be demonstrated in the destination norm.

Visual Exploration gives the dual certificate version. VIS-217--VIS-218 show that normalized magnetic relaxation cannot reach Haar-RMS scale in the subcritical prime graph. VIS-219 shows that even the stronger fractional cycle-packing certificate succeeds only if it leaves residual `B-P=O(sqrt(R))`, equivalently if the relative packing deficit is `O(N_eff^-1/2)`. A positive density of frustration may sound extensive yet still be asymptotically useless in the endpoint units. The relevant question is whether the **certificate itself nearly saturates the destination budget**, not whether a lot of local structure is present.

The decisive workflow is therefore concrete: identify the relaxed class; compute or approximate its endpoint extremizers; test them in the exact destination functional; then add one source-native relation and measure the cost **where the theorem needs the gap**. If the extremizer still saturates, more constraints of subcritical cost cannot help. If the new relation creates a componentwise or averaged gap, test whether switching or redistribution can evade the designated endpoint before treating the route as closed.

**Boundary.** The mechanisms differ: target subtraction, support-topology saturation, horizon-dependent source repair, dyadic switching, inverse-section tail repair and norm-dependent frustration certificates. FD-121 does not show pointwise Farey coercivity; NB-120 does not model actual zero packets; VIS-219 does not lower-bound the exact torus optimum. The diagnostic applies only to the specific relaxed class and destination currency that have been audited.
