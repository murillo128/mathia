# MI-004 — A canonically selected critical axis is not a zero-selection theorem

**Evidence level:** supported by the stated exact dilation/Gram constructions and by the classical Hilbert mechanism isolated in [PC-322](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-322-canonical-green-slice-is-classical-hilbert-criticality.md).

The compatible punctured-plane power-map tower splits as `R x Sigma_Q`. Its radial coordinates satisfy `x_n=x_1/n`; the solenoid does not supply an additional radial constraint. On Haar `L^2(dx dmu)`, raw pullback by `(x,sigma)->(nx,D_n sigma)` has norm `n^-1/2`, so unitary normalization forces the half-density. The resulting dilation generator has continuous spectrum. Formally summing the integer pullbacks displays `zeta(1/2-it)`, but that unweighted sum is not a bounded operator and does not converge strongly even on the specified compactly supported test function ([PC-165](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-165-compatible-log-radial-refinement-is-the-classical-dilation-half-density.md)). The exponent is derived; a discrete zero-selecting operator is not.

The distinction persists when prime-local critical weights are present exactly. [WP-234](../../weil_positivity/findings/WP-234-gamma-and-bost-connes-critical-grams-are-common-versus-independent-cauchy-couplings.md) gives a continuum of positive kernels with identical `K(p^a,p^b)=p^{-|a-b|/2}` and different mixed-prime entries. For `p<q`, two such kernels have `K(p,q)=1/sqrt(pq)` and `sqrt(p/q)`. Thus the half-density does not select the global pairing either.

PC-322 adds a stronger warning because **both the intermediate parameter and the half-plane boundary are canonical**. In the cross-level Mordell--Tornheim packet, ordinary downstream Green inversion forces `u=1`. At exactly that value the additive kernel is the classical Hilbert matrix, and the associated periodic Dirichlet vectors lie in `ell^2` precisely for `Re s>1/2`. Yet the same `1/2` threshold occurs for every nonzero periodic coefficient sequence. The source-specific packet changes the vectors inside the Hilbert space; it does not move or arithmeticize the Hilbert-space boundary.

So parameter fitting is not the only failure mode. A geometry may naturally choose a distinguished coupling and land exactly on a critical `1/2` line, while that line is still a universal operator-domain transition shared by matched controls. Canonicality establishes why the parameter is there; it does not establish why zeros should live there.

The missing objects remain destination-specific: a legitimate discrete spectral realization in the dilation construction, a source-determined mixed-prime law in the Gram construction, and a zero-sensitive invariant beyond the Hilbert matrix coefficient in the Green-sliced packet. Finding the same critical exponent in these settings is not a correspondence among those objects.

**Boundary.** This synthesis does not say that every appearance of `1/2` is universal or irrelevant. It says that a critical axis becomes RH evidence only after the construction proves a source-specific zero/sign/spectral statement that matched controls with the same normalization or operator threshold cannot reproduce.
