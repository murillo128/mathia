# MI-004 — Haar half-density fixes an exponent but neither quantizes zeros nor selects a coupling

**Evidence level:** supported by the stated dilation and Gram constructions.

The compatible punctured-plane power-map tower splits as `R x Sigma_Q`. Its radial coordinates satisfy `x_n=x_1/n`; the solenoid does not supply an additional radial constraint. On Haar `L^2(dx dmu)`, raw pullback by `(x,sigma)->(nx,D_n sigma)` has norm `n^-1/2`, so unitary normalization forces the half-density. The resulting dilation generator has continuous spectrum. Formally summing the integer pullbacks displays `zeta(1/2-it)`, but that unweighted sum is not a bounded operator and does not converge strongly even on the specified compactly supported test function ([PC-165](../../prime_circle/findings/PC-165-compatible-log-radial-refinement-is-the-classical-dilation-half-density.md)). The exponent is derived; a discrete zero-selecting operator is not.

The distinction persists even when prime-local critical weights are present exactly. [WP-234](../../weil_positivity/findings/WP-234-gamma-and-bost-connes-critical-grams-are-common-versus-independent-cauchy-couplings.md) gives a continuum of positive kernels with identical `K(p^a,p^b)=p^{-|a-b|/2}` and different mixed-prime entries. For `p<q`, two such kernels have `K(p,q)=1/sqrt(pq)` and `sqrt(p/q)`. Thus the half-density does not select the global pairing either.

These are separate missing objects: a legitimate discrete spectral realization in the dilation construction, and a source-determined mixed-prime law in the Gram construction. Finding `1/2` in both is not a correspondence between those objects or evidence of zero quantization. Any bridge has to supply the operator/domain or coupling identity that is absent here, rather than cite the shared exponent.
