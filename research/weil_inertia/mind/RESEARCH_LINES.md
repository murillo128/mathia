## Do not turn a bulk pair-correlation certificate into sparse-defect exclusion by localization alone

**Linked intuition:** `MI-010-pair-correlation-slack-is-a-shared-exceptional-budget`.

WI-354 audits Wang's September 2026 short-interval pair-correlation theorem against the sparse-defect objective. For `H=T^theta` and a fixed Fourier-support width `lambda<theta`, the arithmetic evaluation has error

`O_g(H + T^lambda (log T)^2)`.

Relative to the main `H log T` scale, the support-dependent term is `T^(lambda-theta) log T`. The current interface therefore needs a strict support-versus-localization gap `lambda<theta`; fixed positive support cannot simply be carried into subpower or microscopic intervals by the same estimate.

The optimized zero-side coefficient moves in the wrong direction as the interval shrinks. Wang's simple-critical lower proportion is

`c(theta)=2-theta/2-(1/sqrt(2))cot(theta/sqrt(2))`,

which is strictly increasing in `theta`, stays below one, and becomes nonpositive below its threshold near `0.55019`. Every fixed-power interval still contains `asymp T^theta log T` zeros, so any fixed positive-proportion statement remains compatible with finitely many or zero-density off-critical defects.

The cheap bootstrap is therefore closed:

`global/bulk proportion -> same theorem on shorter windows -> individual-defect exclusion`

cannot work through the present arithmetic interface. A viable local route must improve the support-dependent prime-side error enough to decouple Fourier aperture from interval length, produce a coefficient tending to one at the right rate, or use a non-normalized/cofinal inertia mechanism in which a sparse defect has persistent signed cost.

This does not close short-interval information in general. It isolates a route-specific barrier and leaves the cofinal Bombieri-inertia direction, source-specific bow covariance, and genuinely stronger short-interval arithmetic open.

## Keep feature resolution, localization support and defect normalization as separate currencies

The current ledger now has two different localization problems. Feature geometry prices source resolution, transmission, phase-orbit complexity and covariance sign. Short-interval pair correlation prices vertical interval length, Fourier support, prime-side error and the normalization by the local zero count. Improvements in one ledger do not automatically pay the other.

A proposed bridge should state which side supplies the individual-defect sensitivity. A stronger matrix inequality after pair-correlation evaluation cannot repair a prime-side support error that already exceeds the main term. Conversely, a stronger local pair-correlation theorem is not an RH mechanism until its output ceases to tolerate sparse defects or is coupled to a separate coercivity statement that amplifies them.
