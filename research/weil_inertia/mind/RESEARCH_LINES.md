## Use the unrestricted first crossing to turn localization into a sliding autocorrelation family

**Linked intuition:** `MI-017-sliding-first-crossing-localization-produces-autocorrelation-symbols`.

WI-253 opens a parallel route at the first **global** localized crossing, where translated windows are allowed. Averaging all interval centers removes the location variable exactly and yields, for every `0<r<a_*`,

\[
\mathcal F_v(r)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\Sigma_r(z)|\widehat v(z)|^2\,dz
\ge r\lambda_r>0.
\]

Here `Sigma_r` is an explicit finite-prime plus archimedean cosine symbol and `|vhat|^2` is a nonnegative spectral density. This bypasses the reflected half-line Hankel obstruction of the odd route and converts firstness into a **continuum of source-dependent positive spectral averages** rather than one centered signed profile.

The proposed local clue `CLUE-first-global-crossing-sliding-autocorrelation-symbol` states the right next test: analyze the joint `r`-dependence, including one-sided derivative changes at `r=(log n)/2`, and determine whether any compactly supported zero mode can satisfy the whole family together with the exact null equation. WI-253 proves only necessity; no incompatibility, pointwise symbol sign, or RH consequence is established.

## Treat global signed growth, finite-radius reserve, parity, radial flux, sliding autocorrelation, and cone positivity as separate gates

Global subexponential discrepancy control is already RH. Uniform prime-symbol/all-test bounds are too expensive. The odd first crossing produces a signed radial source problem and loses generic semigroup positivity; the unrestricted first crossing gains translation freedom and a nonnegative spectral density but still needs a genuinely zeta-specific incompatibility across the sliding symbol family.

Neither representation should be promoted beyond its evidence.
