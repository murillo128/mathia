WI-349 closes the tempting refinement that the **physical** carrier is only one-dimensional and should therefore improve the ordinary square-root tariff. A two-coordinate physical carrier with `H Delta=1` and center-frequency ratio `R=x_c/Delta` contains `Theta(R^2)` points separated at the natural scale `1/R`. `L/mu >> sqrt(r_tau)`

is sharp in exponent even on the actual one-parameter physical path. The faster projective `rho=O(1/r)` law of WI-347 remains separate because quotienting global phase removes the fast winding.

WI-350 fills the structural gap between those two regimes. For a `U(1)`-equivariant feature whose centered output has phase-cyclic orbit dimension at most `D`,

`r_tau(G) <= D (1 + H Delta L/(4 mu sqrt(tau)))`.

Thus a bounded number of active charge sectors preserves the linear rank-resolution obstruction up to the factor `D`. Matching the generic `L/mu=O(sqrt(r_tau))` regime under equivariance forces `D` itself to grow at least on the corresponding square-root scale. Phase sensitivity is therefore not a free binary escape from projective compression: the number of active phase characters is a measurable feature-complexity currency.

The generic feature-geometry route is therefore saturated more sharply. A stronger bow theorem cannot come merely from saying that the source path is one-dimensional, from another arbitrary smooth nonlinear feature, or from phase sensitivity at fixed aperture. Under equivariance it must additionally explain how distinguished arithmetic information is carried by a quantitatively growing charge orbit, or else inherit the bounded-`D` linear obstruction. In every case the actual destination remains source-specific signed covariance, not feature rank by itself.

The accepted clue `CLUE-bow-distinguished-twist-offdiagonal-cancellation` remains the clean destination test: prove the needed negative off-diagonal contribution with `Lambda^sharp` retained in the same norm, or prove that the proposed statistic cannot force it. WI-350 sharpens a companion structural question: does any natural feature capable of seeing that distinguished twist have bounded/controlled phase-orbit dimension, or does the arithmetic mechanism itself force growing charge support?

## Keep feature resolution, aperture, transmission, global phase, phase-orbit complexity and covariance sign separate

Diagonal-free does not mean destination-sensitive. Nonlinear and phase-sensitive do not mean high-dimensional. Conversely, one-dimensional parameterization does not imply a uniform `O(rho^-1)` metric-entropy law when the curve length/frequency scale itself grows with the family.

The coupled limit matters. For each fixed support the physical carrier is rectifiable and eventually has one-dimensional small-scale entropy, but at the natural family-dependent resolution `rho=1/R` its winding length is already order `R`, leaving room for order `R^2` distinguishable states. A uniform compression theorem must therefore price both the parameter dimension and the moving geometric scale.

Equivariance adds a second complexity axis. Ambient output dimension is too coarse: WI-350 shows that the relevant quantity is the phase-cyclic dimension `D`, equivalently the number of active characters in a centered output vector. Bounded `D` recovers linear resolution cost; the generic quadratic rank regime requires growing `D`. A proposed arithmetic bridge should therefore state not only whether phase is retained, but how many charge sectors the distinguished source actually occupies and why that occupation is source-forced rather than generic feature capacity.

A proposed bridge should state the source-start quantifier, carrier/feature family, normalization, physical aperture, global-phase treatment, phase-orbit complexity, useful continuity/resolution modulus, transmitted amplitude and the exact signed off-diagonal covariance it forces. A large channel count, complicated feature map, low-dimensional parameterization or many formal charge sectors is not new arithmetic information unless those quantities survive the actual family scaling and destination norm.
