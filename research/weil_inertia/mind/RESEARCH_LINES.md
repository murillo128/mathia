# Weil-inertia research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Weil-Inertia evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Separate collective RH reserve from defect-sensitive arithmetic information

WI-387--WI-393 locate the compactness and square-root-prime boundaries for fixed-width Weil trials. Escaping logarithmic spectral tightness can create a source-exact positive RH floor, but the same dilute carrier may remain blind to sparse off-line defects. The crossover occurs when the shifted prime polynomial has absolute size comparable with the local zero-density reserve, near `e^(A/2)~log R`. Beyond that scale a useful converse needs signed prime cancellation, stronger per-defect amplification, or collective defect aggregation rather than only an RH-side density floor.

WI-397--WI-398 show that passing from a scalar Fourier observable to a matrix-valued one does not remove this cost by dimension alone. Jutila-type horizontal Fourier control leaves a `log^2 T` source-budget barrier: a positive-density scalar separation requires that budget, and a matrix inertia change requires either the same order of operator total variation or a reference spectral margin collapsing at the corresponding rate. Nominal matrix rank is not a free amplification resource.

## Use the Widder hierarchy only with jointly shrinking mesh, margin and a genuinely coercive height statistic

WI-399--WI-405 depend on a recent non-peer-reviewed Rafik preprint and should be read with that evidence boundary explicit. Conditional on the fixed-height prime/zero root-rate interface audited there, the high-order Widder statistic is individually sensitive to horizontal zero displacement, while the complete clipped root profile only recodes maximal strip width.

WI-401 gives the exact endpoint sampling geometry: an off-critical zero of horizontal depth `d` is visible above the neutral threshold on a height interval of radius exactly `d`, so a sample set with covering radius `h` cannot force better than `d<=h`. WI-402 extends this to a certified root-rate bound `L_P(T)<=1+epsilon`. The exact scalar law is

`d^2 <= epsilon gamma^2 + (1+epsilon) h^2`.

Amplitude slack and sampling slack therefore consume the same squared horizontal-resolution budget. Even continuous height sampling leaves `d<=sqrt(epsilon) gamma`; every fixed positive `epsilon` becomes vacuous at sufficiently high ordinate. To resolve a fixed horizontal scale `a` near height `gamma`, the scalar route requires both `h<a` and endpoint excess on the order of at most `a^2/gamma^2`, up to the exact combined law.

WI-403 closes geometric self-bootstrap through that same scalar profile. For any admissible zero-geometry set `A`, forming its sharp pointwise Widder envelope and then inverting those scalar bounds is a closure operator: `A` is contained in its image, and a second pass changes nothing. Geometry can improve collars and finite-order positivity, but it cannot contract itself without a prime bound strictly below the geometry-implied envelope or information not factored through that scalar maximum.

WI-404 closes the simplest cross-height averaging repair. For one fixed-depth defect at ordinate `gamma`, the weighted finite-`p` positive-excess moment

`I_(p,a)(gamma,d)=int_0^infinity T^a (r_(gamma,d)(T)-1)_+^p dT`

has the sharp asymptotic `C_p d^(2p+1) gamma^(a-2p)`. Thus `a=2p` is the exact polynomial height-weight threshold: every subcritical weight `a<2p`, including unweighted and logarithmic-height averaging, assigns vanishing cost to a fixed-depth defect as `gamma->infinity`. An exponentially sparse family of fixed-depth abstract defects can even have finite total unweighted positive excess. At the critical weight the height loss disappears for fixed `d`, but the cost remains proportional to `d^(2p+1)` and still degenerates for shallow defects.

WI-405 closes the naive signed-Mellin escape. In the absolutely convergent Mellin half-plane, pure-power signed Widder averages reduce to classical Voros superzeta data rather than creating a new population or interference carrier. The locally attractive critical signed weight gives an exact defect-scale charge, but the corresponding global zero sum lies outside absolute convergence. Analytically continuing the superzeta produces a renormalized global object, not a termwise positive or defect-preserving decomposition, so the finite local charge cannot simply be summed.

The live Widder question is therefore more specific than “retain signed information.” A viable scalar route still needs a critical/supercritical height weight together with prime-side control strong enough to pay it. A genuinely signed route must instead prove a **zeta-specific renormalization/cancellation identity** at the critical scale and show that, after the global baseline is removed, a defect-sensitive term survives with the required sign or coercivity. Analytic continuation by itself is not that theorem. Cross-height phase coupling, zero-population/correlation data or a prime estimate strictly below the geometry-implied scalar envelope remain structurally different possibilities. Any proposal whose effective one-defect positive height weight is `o(gamma^(2p))` cannot have height-uniform finite-`p` scalar coercivity at this interface.

## Keep budget, margin, defect response and representation dimension distinct

A candidate inertia mechanism must state whether it changes the source budget, the reference spectral margin, individual defect response, collective zero density, height-sampling resolution, endpoint-amplitude precision, cross-height weight, summability/renormalization law, or genuinely adds independent information. Enlarging a scalar observable to a matrix, converting strip width into an invertible root profile, sampling on a finer mesh, certifying that profile with fixed positive slack, feeding a geometric profile through its own sharp scalar envelope, taking a subcritical nonnegative finite-`p` average, or analytically continuing a signed Mellin family changes representation, aggregation or globalization without eliminating the surviving information budget.
