# Weil-inertia research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Weil-Inertia evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Keep source regularity separate from regularity of the canonical defect measure

WI-417--WI-422 identify sharp coercive source-space phenomena for translated-Blaschke probes. With an independently fixed planar density cap, one-target cost has the expected shallow-depth blow-up and critically separated target families converge to a Poisson-capacity problem. Generic TV/Carleson control is too weak because target-adaptive atoms remain cheap. For the bare translated-Blaschke response, ordinary global `L^p` control is bounded exactly for `1<p<2`, with a Lorentz endpoint and different behavior once a finite-TV budget is added.

WI-423 shows why these useful auxiliary spaces cannot simply be imposed on the canonical exact zeta source. The Riesz identity `-Delta U = 2 pi mu_off` makes that source the atomic counting measure of off-critical zeros; exact absolute continuity of the same source would already force the defect measure to vanish. A nontrivial route must therefore generate an auxiliary probe/coefficient source independently and prove an exact or quantitatively controlled coupling from its potential back to the canonical defect measure.

The live question is to derive such an auxiliary source and a target-independent concentration-and-tail budget from accessible prime-side, boundary or explicit-formula data. The norm must be fixed without inspecting hypothetical zero locations and must control the actual Poisson-capacity geometry consumed by the destination.

## Approach the boundary with a source-controlled probe family; transport alone cannot eliminate its own defect signal

WI-424 identifies the boundary logarithmic derivative of the Kunik Blaschke factor as a canonical positive Poisson defect counter. WI-425 shows that any fixed interior safe line is cubically blind to sufficiently shallow defects. WI-426 gives the same blind-or-singular obstruction for positive multiscale `L^2` line energy, and WI-427 closes every finite-cost positive diagonal `L^p` portfolio below the local pole threshold.

WI-428 closes the two most immediate nonpositive escapes on a fixed safe strip. For the one-defect response, the exact cross-scale kernel is `K_d(a,b)=16 pi d^2 / [(a+b)((a+b)^2-4d^2)]`. Any signed or complex cross-scale charge with finite depth-independent weighted variation produces only `O(d^2)` response against the canonical `2 pi/d` boundary energy. A fixed-safe-strip quadratic form therefore needs operator norm of order at least `d^(-3)` to retain a constant fraction of a shallow defect.

WI-429 identifies a different category in which no operator singularity is needed. On a line `Re(s)=1/2+a`, the norm-one Hardy projection `P_+` keeps exactly zeros with depth `d_j>a` and annihilates shallower ones. Its energy has an exact positive pair formula and, for one defect, already retains at least half of the canonical boundary energy as `a downarrow0`. Every fixed `a>0` is nevertheless blind to all defects shallower than `a`, so a complete detector needs a predetermined family `a_n downarrow0`.

WI-430 extracts a global coercive consequence. For zeros with depth greater than `a` and height bounded by `T`, the projected energy `E_a(T)` pays a uniform first-defect quantum `pi/(1/2-a)`. The formal boundary criterion `liminf_(a downarrow0) E_a<=2pi` is equivalent to RH, so an unconditional upper bound at that scale would already contain the missing zero-exclusion theorem rather than provide a cheap consequence of existing source control.

WI-431 closes the bare safe-line projection shortcut. For `F(s)=zeta'(s)/zeta(s)+1/(s-1)`, all absolutely convergent prime frequencies on `Re(s)>1` are negative, as is the pole-cancelling Cauchy term, hence the global positive Hardy projection is exactly zero there. WI-432 shows that adiabatic localization does not repair this: broad smooth windows drive the positive safe-line prime leakage rapidly to zero because of the spectral gap below frequency zero, even though the commutator operator itself has scale-invariant norm.

WI-433 gives the exact horizontal bookkeeping. For a compact height window `chi`, the positive-frequency transform `H_sigma` of `chi F(sigma+it)` obeys a distributional transport equation whose integration from a safe line `sigma_0>1` to an interior line `sigma` decomposes `H_sigma` into damped safe-line leakage, one window-edge forcing integral, and explicit exponentially transported residues of right-half zeros crossed during continuation. WI-434 then shows that the raw principal-pole divergence in that edge is not a genuine obstruction: the exact transport semigroup cancels the crossing-depth singularity, and the transported principal-pole contribution of all crossed zeros is `o(1)` for adiabatic windows using only classical local zero counts.

WI-435 corrects the inference that remained after WI-434. Even granting `o(1)` control of the **entire** transported edge term, together with vanishing safe-line leakage, does not force the residue signal to vanish. The transport identity then says only that the interior Hardy component converges to the negative of that residue signal. A finite Kunik Blaschke block realizes this exactly: its safe-line projection tends to zero, its complete transported edge term tends to zero, while a zero of depth `delta>0` leaves interior defect energy `pi m^2/delta`. Thus the WI-430 first-defect quantum is compatible with vanishing source and edge errors; small edge forcing makes the interior Hardy field a better representation of the defect rather than an upper bound for it.

The live theorem must therefore add information not already contained in horizontal transport. Adequate targets include an independently justified subquantum upper bound for a predetermined localized interior `P_+(chi_T F_sigma)` family, a mixed positive/negative-frequency or source functional whose coercivity cannot be saturated by the finite-Blaschke model, or a boundary/explicit-formula observable that bounds the interior Hardy norm without importing reciprocal distances to the same right-half zeros. Edge estimates can remain auxiliary, but controlling the regular remainder left by WI-434 is not a defect-to-zero bootstrap by itself.

The adversarial test is now exact: every proposed continuation argument must be run against the finite Kunik Blaschke block of WI-435. If the proposal allows vanishing safe-line leakage and vanishing edge transport while the interior Hardy component remains the nonzero residue field, it has supplied no independent source information. Standard bounds for `zeta'/zeta` inside the strip remain especially suspect when they encode reciprocal zero distances; target-adaptive localization and fixed-safe-strip signed processing remain excluded by WI-428--WI-432. A viable source inequality must distinguish the full zeta object from the finite-Blaschke countermodel for a reason fixed independently of the hypothetical defects.
