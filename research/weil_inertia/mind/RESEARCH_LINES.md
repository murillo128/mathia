WI-350 identifies the finite phase-complexity variable. For a `U(1)`-equivariant feature whose centered output has phase-cyclic orbit dimension at most `D`,

`r_tau(G) <= D (1 + H Delta L/(4 mu sqrt(tau)))`.

A bounded number of active charge sectors therefore preserves the linear rank-resolution obstruction up to `D`; approaching the generic `L/mu=O(sqrt(r_tau))` regime requires `D` to grow.

WI-351 shows that replacing finite charge support by infinitely many tiny sectors does not remove the obstruction. If the feature has controlled charge-Sobolev moment `B_s`, truncating the charge tail below destination resolution reduces the problem to WI-350. In the generic sharp square-root regime, effective rank `r_tau` then forces

`B_s/mu = Omega(r_tau^(s/2))`

up to fixed threshold/geometric constants.

WI-352 identifies the exact dependency at first angular order. On the phase-saturated normalized carrier, `U(1)` equivariance plus the same `L`-Lipschitz hypothesis already implies

`B_1<=L`, and therefore `B_s<=L` for every `0<s<=1`.

Substituting this automatic bound into WI-351 reproduces the generic square-root sensitivity tariff rather than a new independent obstruction. The threshold is sharp: ordinary Lipschitz equivariance does not imply `B_(1+epsilon)<infinity` for any `epsilon>0`. Thus bookkeeping `B_1` as a second feature-complexity resource cannot strengthen the bow bound; any stronger charge-tail route must prove genuinely additional higher angular regularity or another source-specific invariant.

The generic feature-geometry route is now saturated more sharply. A stronger bow theorem cannot come merely from another smooth nonlinear feature, phase sensitivity, a one-dimensional source parameter, boundedly many charge sectors, a controlled infinite charge tail, or first-order charge regularity already implied by Lipschitz equivariance. It must explain why distinguished arithmetic information forces higher-order charge regularity/complexity, use a different non-equivariant or source-specific mechanism, or directly prove the required signed covariance. In every case the destination remains source-specific signed covariance, not feature rank.

The accepted clue `CLUE-bow-distinguished-twist-offdiagonal-cancellation` remains the clean destination test: prove the needed negative off-diagonal contribution with `Lambda^sharp` retained in the same norm, or prove that the proposed statistic cannot force it.

## Keep feature resolution, aperture, transmission, global phase, phase-orbit complexity, charge-tail mass and covariance sign separate

Diagonal-free does not mean destination-sensitive. Nonlinear and phase-sensitive do not mean high-dimensional. Conversely, one-dimensional parameterization does not imply a uniform `O(rho^-1)` entropy law when the curve length/frequency scale grows with the family.

Equivariance introduces a hierarchy of complexity currencies, but WI-352 shows that they are not automatically independent. Finite phase-cyclic dimension `D` measures hard charge support. WI-351 adds weighted charge-tail mass `B_s` as the soft analogue. At `s=1`, however, Lipschitz equivariance already pays that moment through `L`; only genuinely stronger angular information beyond one derivative can become an additional resource. Ambient output dimension, literal support cardinality, formal phase sensitivity and first-order charge mass can therefore all overstate independent usable source information.

A proposed bridge should state the source-start quantifier, carrier/feature family, normalization, physical aperture, global-phase treatment, hard or soft phase-orbit complexity, which complexity bounds are genuinely independent of the continuity constant, useful continuity/resolution modulus, transmitted amplitude and the exact signed off-diagonal covariance it forces. A large channel count, complicated feature map, low-dimensional parameterization or infinitely many tiny charge sectors is not new arithmetic information unless those quantities survive the actual family scaling and destination norm.
