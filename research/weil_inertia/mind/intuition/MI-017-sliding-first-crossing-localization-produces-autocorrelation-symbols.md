# MI-017 — Suzuki first-crossing null modes obey a PSD source Laplacian, but prime-lag overlap remains unforced

**Evidence level:** exact under the stated hypotheses through WI-269. WI-267 proves prime-translation support domination for arbitrary nonzero localized Weil null vectors; WI-268 proves failure of ordinary positivity preservation; WI-269 derives a source-specific positive-semidefinite multiplier form and an uncancelled nodal compensation law at a hypothetical first unrestricted crossing. No theorem yet forces a nonzero prime-lag autocorrelation or proves RH.

WI-264--WI-265 show that finite source activation and abstract first-crossing positivity can be screened. WI-266 uses Suzuki's actual equation to exclude the endpoint-bump extremizer, and WI-267 upgrades this to an exact support theorem: every point outside the essential support must be reached by an active prime-power translation of the support.

WI-268 independently closes the standard route to sign coherence. The unrestricted off-diagonal archimedean cross kernel changes sign at `h_0=log rho`, so ordinary Beurling--Deny/Perron--Frobenius positivity cannot make the relevant first-crossing mode one-signed.

WI-269 supplies a different, source-specific order structure. For a hypothetical first unrestricted zero mode `v`, every bounded real Lipschitz multiplier `chi` satisfies `q_a(chi v)>=0`, and the exact ground-state transform writes this as the same signed source `prime atoms + w(h)dh` applied to squared multiplier increments weighted by `v(x+h)v(x)`. Polarization makes these multiplier energies a positive-semidefinite bilinear form; every finite partition of unity therefore produces a PSD signed-Laplacian matrix with constants in its kernel.

The nodal split is stronger than scalar autocorrelation. Writing `A_-(h)` for the absolute opposite-sign overlap, WI-269 shows that opposite-sign overlap at every active prime-power lag and at short archimedean lags where `w(h)>0` must be financed by long-range archimedean overlap where `w(h)<0`. Because `A_-` is unsigned inside its nodal sector, this tariff cannot disappear through cancellation between positive and negative overlap regions.

This is genuine progress from activity to a family of overlap constraints, but not yet the desired prime-lag lower bound. A multi-component mode could in principle satisfy the PSD/nodal tariff using long-range archimedean compensation while all scalar prime autocorrelations remain screened. The next theorem must combine the multiplier PSD family, support domination and first-crossing geometry strongly enough to exclude such compensation or exhibit it as a decisive negative control.

**Boundary.** PSD of the signed source Laplacian does not imply a Markov sign pattern, one-signedness of `v`, or nonzero `C_v(log n)`. The nodal tariff constrains where sign changes can be financed; it does not by itself identify an active prime lag with coherent signed overlap.
