# MI-014 — Weil-inertia coercivity must charge actual source mass, and fixed compact detectors have an uncertainty floor

**Evidence level:** exact Gallagher reduction, fixed-band rank sharpness, remote-screen source tariffs, and compact-detector localization boundaries through WI-223

## Core intuition

The Weil-inertia bow has passed the limit of free exponential-screen rank arguments. A fixed power-scale band forces positive-density channel complexity, but that tariff is attained by relaxed screens, and arbitrary remote Fourier tails remain complete when coefficient/source costs are ignored.

The newer results restore a real source price: pushing a cancelling screen far from the bow forces rapidly growing weighted coefficient mass. Compact detectors can turn that tariff into strong spatial localization of dyadic zero-source mass, but classical uncertainty prevents the fixed compact-detector architecture from reaching the bow scale itself. The remaining theorem is therefore a **source-budget conversion after localization**, not another free-screen approximation theorem.

## Strongest justified principle

WI-218 forces `Omega(M)` channels across a fixed band, WI-219 gives a matching `Theta(M)` relaxed companion, and WI-220 proves that qualitative support separation alone is noncoercive because remote exponential tails remain complete on a proper interval.

WI-221 adds the missing coefficient currency. If every screen frequency is at natural distance `R_M->infinity`, cancellation of the post-box bow on one fixed neighborhood forces weighted coefficient mass larger than every fixed power of `R_M`; for actual mirror-pair channels this becomes a radial-weighted zeta-source tariff of order `M R_M^N` for every fixed `N`.

WI-222 uses compact Gevrey detectors to localize order-`M` dyadic weighted source to physical ordinate radius `M(log T)^epsilon` for every fixed `epsilon>0`. WI-223 improves the loss to arbitrary fixed iterated-log hierarchies using non-quasianalytic Denjoy--Carleman cutoffs.

The same WI-223 argument identifies the architectural ceiling. Ingham's uncertainty theorem forbids a nonzero compactly supported detector with genuine exponential Fourier decay. Hence fixed compact detector plus total radial-mass tail suppression cannot uniformly localize to `O(M)` physical radius and cannot reach the smaller original bow scale `M/log T`.

## Program consequence

Stop treating remoteness as a free geometric parameter: it now has a proved weighted source cost. The next result should turn the localized radial-weighted population into an incompatible local zero-count, multiplicity, coefficient, or radial-depth budget, or replace the fixed-detector architecture by a source-specific covariance theorem, coupled observables, or controlled `T`-dependent detectors.

Any new localization claim should keep separate the natural-frequency radius, physical ordinate radius, radial weight, total weighted mass, and raw zero count.

## Counterevidence / boundary

WI-221--WI-223 do not contradict the local Riemann--von Mangoldt count. They force weighted source mass, not necessarily many distinct zeros, and retain a dyadic-screen hypothesis. The Ingham obstruction closes only the fixed nonzero compact-detector + total-tail-estimate architecture; adaptive/coupled or source-specific methods remain open.

## Epistemic status

**Exact source-weighted remoteness tariff with progressively sharp compact-detector localization and an exact uncertainty boundary; the final conversion to an impossible zeta-zero source budget remains open.**

## Falsification criterion

Produce a super-bow remote screen with polynomially bounded weighted source cost contradicting WI-221, or a fixed nonzero compact detector with the exponential Fourier decay excluded by WI-223's uncertainty input. A positive continuation should convert the localized weighted source into a source-admissibility contradiction or change the observation architecture.
