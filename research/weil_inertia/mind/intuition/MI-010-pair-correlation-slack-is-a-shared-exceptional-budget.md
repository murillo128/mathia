# MI-010 — Pair-correlation slack and localization are bulk budgets, not individual-defect certificates

**Evidence level:** exact finite Lamzouri-model identities through WI-170, strengthened by the literature-backed short-interval interface audit in [WI-354](../../findings/WI-354-short-interval-pair-correlation-has-a-support-localization-barrier.md).

A scalar pair-correlation deficit can charge several qualitatively different exceptional populations without distinguishing them. In the Lamzouri finite model the same surplus funds both off-real mass and multiplicity excess, so combining marginal percentage bounds does not create an independent bootstrap.

WI-170 gives the exact decomposition

`Q - N = O + 2M + R`, with `R>=0`,

where `O` is the non-real population counted with multiplicity and `M=N-D` is total multiplicity excess. The coefficient `2` on `M` is exactly saturated by a real double. The coefficient `1` on `O` is asymptotically saturated by a simple conjugate pair approaching the real axis. The two exceptional mechanisms can therefore spend the same scalar resource at their sharp leading costs.

WI-354 shows that **vertical localization does not by itself turn this bulk resource into an individual certificate**. In Wang's short-interval extension, for `H=T^theta` and Fourier support `lambda<theta`, the pair-correlation evaluation has normalized support error

`T^(lambda-theta) log T`.

Thus the existing proof loses fixed positive Fourier aperture as the power interval shrinks. At the same time the optimized simple-critical proportion

`c(theta)=2-theta/2-(1/sqrt(2))cot(theta/sqrt(2))`

strictly decreases when `theta` decreases and remains below one. Since every fixed-power interval contains `asymp T^theta log T` zeros, such a normalized proportion is compatible with any finite or zero-density off-critical set.

The reusable principle is therefore two-stage. First ask what exceptional mechanisms share the scalar pair-correlation budget. Then ask whether localization changes the **normalization and information aperture** enough to make one defect visible. Shorter intervals are not automatically stronger: if the arithmetic theorem simultaneously loses Fourier support while its output is still normalized by a macroscopically growing local zero count, sparse defects remain invisible.

A genuine escape needs new information at one of those stages. It may couple off-line depth, multiplicity, local correlations or another signed observable to an independently evaluated budget; improve the prime-side short-interval error enough to retain useful support at microscopic scale; produce a coefficient tending to one fast enough; or use a non-normalized/cofinal inertia mechanism that gives each defect persistent cost.

**Boundary.** WI-354 is a route-specific statement about Wang's current theorem and displayed error, not an impossibility theorem for short-interval pair correlation. It does not say support `lambda>=theta` is mathematically forbidden. The exact inertia count still records distinct off-line pairs, but neither inertia nor the present normalized pair-correlation budgets supply quantitative exclusion of a sparse defect without an additional coercive bridge.
