# MI-021 — Complete screening promotes support avoidance to prime-deleted spectral criticality

**Evidence level:** exact for the one-signed completely screened first-crossing branch by [WI-284](../../findings/WI-284-complete-screening-forces-a-prime-deleted-critical-support.md), building on the support-packing boundary WI-281--WI-283 and the prime-deleted form of WI-240. No sign theorem, incomplete-screening estimate or RH consequence is claimed.

WI-281--WI-283 show that complete one-signed screening forces prime-log-distance avoidance, but every purely support-topological relaxation still saturates the sharp packing budget `K_pack`. Tiny-amplitude satellites can radically alter support closure and domination while barely changing the scalar endpoint functional. Another support-cover inequality cannot distinguish actual null modes.

WI-284 recovers information that the relaxation discarded. If a nonnegative first-crossing null mode `v` screens every active prime-power autocorrelation and `E={v>0}`, then the screening disjointness is inherited by **every** pair of functions supported in `E`. Consequently every von Mangoldt translation term vanishes identically on the supported form-domain subspace `D_E`.

At first crossing the full localized Weil form is nonnegative and `v` is a null vector. Since the prime part vanishes on `D_E`, the exact prime-deleted gamma-plus-pole form satisfies

`q_arch^a|_(D_E) >= 0`,  `q_arch^a(v)=0`.

Thus complete screening does not merely choose a distance-avoiding support: it chooses a support on which the prime-deleted form has spectral bottom **exactly zero**. This is an infinite-dimensional variational constraint, not a scalar support statistic, and it survives the small-amplitude saturation mechanism of WI-283.

The direct contradiction target is now support-sensitive: prove that every first-crossing-compatible prime-log-distance-avoiding support either carries a negative prime-deleted direction or has strictly positive bottom. Either conclusion contradicts the required zero ground state. A quantitative negative gap would also create a route toward incomplete screening.

The reusable distinction is **support topology versus operator-domain inheritance**. Vanishing of one signed autocorrelation can, under one-signedness, delete an operator term on an entire support subspace; firstness can then turn that deletion into a spectral constraint that no scalar packing relaxation records.

**Boundary.** The full-space negative index of the prime-deleted form does not imply negativity on every sparse supported subspace. One-signedness and complete screening are essential, and the sign-changing or incompletely screened branches remain separate.
