## 1. The finite window sees every low divisor marginal and only one high-divisor moment

Kluyver's classical formula is

\[
c_m(h)=\sum_{d\mid(m,h)}d\mu(m/d).
\tag{10}
\]

For a nonzero sampled lag `1<=|h|<N`, insert (10) into (1) and interchange the finite sums:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_\omega(h)
&:=\sum_m\omega_mc_m(h)\\
&=\sum_{d\mid h}d
\sum_{m:d\mid m}\mu(m/d)\omega_m\\
&=\boxed{\sum_{d\mid h}dF_\omega(d)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Every divisor of such an `h` is strictly below `N`. Therefore **no** `F_omega(d)` with `d>=N` appears in an off-diagonal entry of the `N x N` Toeplitz matrix.

At lag zero, `c_m(0)=phi(m)` and the classical identity

\[
\varphi(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}d\mu(m/d)
\tag{12}
\]

gives instead

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_\omega(0)
&=\sum_m\varphi(m)\omega_m\\
&=\sum_{d\ge1}dF_\omega(d)\\
&=\boxed{
\sum_{d<N}dF_\omega(d)+T_N(\omega)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Equations (11)--(13) identify the exact information loss caused by the finite window. It is more specific than saying that long periods become overcomplete: the coordinates `F(1),...,F(N-1)` survive **separately**, while the infinite/super-window tail is collapsed to the one weighted scalar `T_N` on the diagonal.

No asymptotic estimate, large sieve, or positivity argument enters this reduction.

## 2. The WI-079 marginal gates are necessary and sufficient

\[
\boxed{

=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)R_\omega(e).
}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus every `F_omega(d)=0` for `d<N`. Equation (13) and `R_omega(0)=0` then imply `T_N(omega)=0`.

Conversely, if all low marginals and the tail moment vanish, (11) kills every nonzero sampled lag and (13) kills the diagonal. The Toeplitz matrix is therefore zero. This proves (4).

The statement also gives the exact finite-cutoff dimension. Fix any `M>=N` and restrict to coefficient vectors `(omega_1,...,omega_M)`. WI-084 proves that `B_1^(N),...,B_N^(N)` are linearly independent, so the map

\[

## 4. A single long-period block aliases through its large divisors only

Take `omega_m=1` for one modulus `m>N` and zero otherwise. Then

\[
F(d)=
\begin{cases}
\mu(m/d),&d\mid m,\\
0,&d\nmid m.
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
\]

The tail coordinate is therefore

\[
T_N(m)=
\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d\ge N}}d\mu(m/d).
\tag{22}
\]

Substituting (21)--(22) into (6) first gives

\[
\widetilde\omega_j
=
\sum_{\substack{d<N\\j\mid d\\d\mid m}}\mu(m/d)
+
\frac{\mathbf 1_{j\mid N}}N T_N(m).
\tag{23}
\]

Because `j<=N<m`, the complete divisor-Möbius sum over `j|d|m` vanishes whenever `j|m`, and is empty otherwise:

\[
\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\j\mid d}}\mu(m/d)=0.
\tag{24}

Subtracting the omitted large divisors yields exactly (9).

Two useful consequences are immediate.

Second, from (9),

\[

Hence if `m<=C N` for fixed `C`, a single long-period block aliases to a subwindow coefficient vector with at most divisor-function complexity and coefficients `O_C(tau(m))=m^{o(1)}`. ## 5. The divisor identity gives a recursive alias formula and sharper examples than the prime collapse

The classical identity

\[
\sum_{d\mid m}c_d(h)=m\,\mathbf1_{m\mid h}
\tag{27}
\]

has an exact finite-section consequence. If `m>N`, then for every sampled difference `|h|<N`, the right side of (27) is `m` on the diagonal and zero off it. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d\mid m}B_d^{(N)}=mI_N.
}
\tag{28}
\]

Applying the same identity at `m=N` gives

\[
I_N=\frac1N\sum_{e\mid N}B_e^{(N)}.
\tag{29}
\]

Combining (28)--(29), every super-window block satisfies the exact recursion

\[
\boxed{
B_m^{(N)}
=
\frac mN\sum_{e\mid N}B_e^{(N)}
-
\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d<m}}B_d^{(N)}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

Every modulus on the second sum is smaller than `m`, so repeated application terminates in the WI-084 basis. If

\[
N<m\le2N,
\]

then every proper divisor of `m` is at most `m/2<=N`, and (30) is already a one-step subwindow alias.

WI-083's prime identity is the simplest special case. For prime `p>N`, the only proper divisor is `1`, `B_1=J_N`, and (30) gives

\[
B_p^{(N)}=pI_N-J_N.
\tag{31}
\]

There are even more degenerate universal examples. If `p>N` is prime, then every nonzero sampled lag is not divisible by `p`; from the prime-square Ramanujan values or from (28) with divisors `1,p,p^2`,

\[
\boxed{
B_{p^2}^{(N)}=\varphi(p^2)I_N=p(p-1)I_N.
}
\tag{32}
\]

Hence two distinct primes `p,q>N` give the exact two-block cancellation

\[
\boxed{
\varphi(q^2)B_{p^2}^{(N)}
-
\varphi(p^2)B_{q^2}^{(N)}=0.
}
\tag{33}
\]

This is a sparser universal relation than WI-083's three-large-prime witness. It is **not** asserted to occur in the Yang source coefficient law; it only reinforces the interface conclusion that once super-window moduli are allowed, universal scalar finite-section geometry by itself cannot force residual signature or norm.

## 8. Boundary conditions and falsification

1. **Finite coefficient support.** The sums in (1)--(3) are finite here. Infinite Ramanujan expansions need convergence hypotheses and are not covered by this finding.
2. **Consecutive sample window.** Translation of `N` consecutive samples is harmless because only differences appear. Sparse/nonconsecutive sampling changes the lag set and therefore the quotient.
3. **Scalar modulus blocks only.** The alias map identifies information after reduction to `sum omega_m B_m^(N)`. It need not preserve source labels, reduced directions, or the full Yang four-prime covariance.
4. **The quotient is not an inertia formula.** Knowing `tilde omega` exactly determines the matrix, but a nonzero alias can still have small rank or strongly unbalanced signature. WI-082 controls one undercomplete regime; no universal inertia lower bound for arbitrary aliases is asserted here.
5. **Original coefficients can be huge inside the kernel.** Equation (19) controls the canonical alias coefficients, not the super-window source coefficients individually. WI-083 and (33) show why no such universal inverse for the original coefficients can exist.
6. **Single-block divisor sparsity does not survive arbitrary aggregation automatically.** Equations (25)--(26) describe one `B_m`; a source sum over many long moduli can produce a dense alias vector and requires source-specific cancellation estimates.
7. **The prime-square relation is an interface witness, not source evidence.** The Yang effective scalar moduli/weights may forbid prime squares of the required size or their signs. Equation (33) only falsifies universal residual-sign claims.
8. **No new zeta theorem is claimed.** The unconditional simple-critical bound remains unchanged. This result redirects one candidate route toward exact source quotient coordinates or back to labelled/multidimensional covariance information.
