# WI-147 — a universal compact-spectrum Lamzouri-form scalar bound forces Fourier positivity

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT`. WI-145 showed that a universal Lamzouri-form scalar inequality cannot use a genuine eventually negative outer Fourier tail, because a single non-real conjugate pair forces the scalar kernel to have the opposite hyperbolic sign. WI-146 then showed that this two-point obstruction is not enough to force Fourier positivity: a signed spectral profile can hide a fixed negative intermediate band behind an arbitrarily small positive repair placed farther out, and a smoothed compactly supported version still passes every one- and two-element necessary test.

The full universal finite inequality is much more rigid. Let `phi` be real, even, continuous, and compactly supported, normalize `int phi = 1`, and define the entire scalar kernel

\[
H(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(t)e^{-2\pi i zt}\,dt.
\tag{1}
\]

Assume that for **every** nonempty finite conjugation-invariant multiset `Z`, with `N=|Z|` and `s(Z)` the number of simple real elements,

\[
\boxed{
 s(\mathcal Z)\ge 2N-\sum_{z,w\in\mathcal Z}H(z-w).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Then necessarily

\[
\boxed{\phi(t)\ge0\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb R.}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus, inside the natural continuous compact-spectrum class, **universality of the scalar zero-side inequality itself forces the Fourier-positive/Bochner cone**, even if no Hilbert or Gram derivation is assumed. In particular, the smooth compactly supported signed repair constructed in WI-146 cannot satisfy the full inequality (2): multi-pair configurations detect what every two-point test misses.

No zeta-zero proportion changes here. The result closes the sign-indefinite **compact-spectrum scalar** escape left after WI-145/WI-146; it does not close noncompact oscillatory spectra, matrix/joint observables, explicit horizontal correction terms, or source-specific inequalities valid only for actual zeta-zero configurations.

## 1. Primary-source interface

The finite model is Proposition 2.1 of Youness Lamzouri, *A new proof that more than 2/3 of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line*, arXiv:2609.02882v1 (2 September 2026). For a real even compactly supported window `eta`, normalized by `widehat(eta^2)(0)=1`, Lamzouri puts

\[
K(\xi)=\widehat{\eta^2}(\xi)
\]

and proves for every finite conjugation-invariant multiset

\[
 s(\mathcal Z)
 \ge
 2|\mathcal Z|-\sum_{z,w\in\mathcal Z}K(z-w)^2.
\tag{4}
\]

The scalar replacement studied in WI-143--WI-146 is obtained by writing `H=K^2` in (4) and asking which other normalized entire translation-invariant kernels could possibly satisfy the same universal inequality. Lamzouri's actual kernel has Fourier profile

\[
\phi=\eta^2*\eta^2\ge0,
\]

so it lies in the class allowed by (3). The issue is whether a new proof, unrelated to positive Hilbert geometry, could support a compactly supported **signed** `phi` and thereby import the favorable tail-sign mechanism used under RH by Chirre--Gonçalves--de Laat. The theorem above says no.

## 2. Every conjugation-invariant multiset gives a signed spectral quadratic form

For a finite conjugation-invariant multiset `Z` define

\[
S_{\mathcal Z}(t):=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}e^{-2\pi i t z}.
\tag{5}
\]

Because `Z` is invariant under complex conjugation and `t` is real,

\[
\sum_{w\in\mathcal Z}e^{2\pi i t w}
=\overline{S_{\mathcal Z}(t)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Substituting (1) into the scalar pair sum and exchanging the finite sums with the compact integral gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
Q_H(\mathcal Z)
:=\sum_{z,w\in\mathcal Z}H(z-w)
=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(t)|S_{\mathcal Z}(t)|^2\,dt.
}
\tag{7}
\]

This is not yet Bochner positivity: (2) tests only the special coefficient-one exponential sums generated by conjugation-invariant multisets, not arbitrary complex coefficient vectors. WI-146 exploits exactly that apparent weakness at cardinality at most two. The point of the construction below is that large multisets supply a tunable family of such coefficient-one sums that localizes (7) at any prescribed nonzero spectral radius.

For a multiset with no real points, `s(Z)=0`, so (2) requires

\[
\boxed{Q_H(\mathcal Z)\ge2|\mathcal Z|>0.}
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore it is enough to construct one conjugation-invariant non-real multiset with `Q_H(Z)<0` from any negative value of `phi`.

## 3. Binomial conjugate-pair probes form a spectral microscope

Assume for contradiction that `phi(a)<0` for some `a>0`. If the only displayed negative point were `0`, continuity and evenness would give a nearby nonzero negative point, so no generality is lost. Choose `S>0` with

\[
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset[-S,S]
\]

and choose any `R>S`. Put

\[
\theta_a:=\frac{\pi a}{2R}\in(0,\pi/2).
\tag{9}
\]

For each integer `m>=1`, define

\[
b_m:=\frac{m}{4R}\tan\theta_a
\tag{10}
\]

and real centers

\[
x_k:=\frac{k}{2R},\qquad 0\le k\le m,
\tag{11}
\]

where the center `x_k` is taken with multiplicity `binom(m,k)`. Let `Z_m` contain, with those multiplicities, the conjugate pair

\[
x_k+ib_m,\qquad x_k-ib_m.
\tag{12}
\]

This is a finite conjugation-invariant multiset with no real elements and

\[
N_m=2\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk=2^{m+1}.
\tag{13}
\]

Its horizontal exponential polynomial is exactly binomial:

\[
P_m(t)
:=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk e^{-2\pi i t x_k}
=\left(1+e^{-\pi i t/R}\right)^m.
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently

\[
S_{\mathcal Z_m}(t)
=2\cosh(2\pi b_m t)P_m(t),
\tag{15}
\]

and, because `|t|<=S<R`,

\[
|P_m(t)|^2
=\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}\right)^m.
\tag{16}
\]

Substitution into (7) yields

\[
\boxed{
Q_H(\mathcal Z_m)
=4\int_{-S}^{S}
\phi(t)\cosh^2(2\pi b_m t)
\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}\right)^m dt.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus the multiset does not test `phi` uniformly. Its horizontal binomial multiplicities create a spectral taper, while the common imaginary depth creates an opposing hyperbolic amplification. The ratio in (10) is chosen so that the two effects balance at exactly `t=a`.

## 4. The induced exponential weight has a unique strict maximum at the negative point

For `0<=t<=S`, define

\[
F(t)
:=
\frac{\pi t}{R}\tan\theta_a
+\log\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}\right).
\tag{18}
\]

Since `4 pi b_m t = m (pi t/R) tan(theta_a)`, we have the exact factorization

\[
\cosh^2(2\pi b_m t)
\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}\right)^m
=
\frac14 e^{mF(t)}
\left(1+e^{-4\pi b_m t}\right)^2.
\tag{19}
\]

The final factor lies between `1` and `4`. The exponential rate is therefore controlled entirely by `F`. Differentiation gives

\[
F'(t)
=\frac{\pi}{R}
\left(
\tan\theta_a-	an\frac{\pi t}{2R}
\right),
\tag{20}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
F''(t)
=-\frac{\pi^2}{2R^2}
\sec^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}<0.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Hence `F` is strictly concave on `[0,S]` and has its **unique global maximum at `t=a`**.

By continuity of `phi`, choose a closed interval `I` around `a`, contained in `(0,S)`, and `eta>0` such that

\[
\phi(t)\le-\eta\qquad(t\in I).
\tag{22}
\]

Strict maximality gives a gap from `a` to the complement of `I`. More explicitly, choose a smaller closed interval `J` around `a` and `delta>0` such that

\[
\inf_{t\in J}F(t)\ge F(a)-\delta,
\qquad
\sup_{t\in[0,S]\setminus I}F(t)
\le F(a)-3\delta.
\tag{23}
\]

The part of (17) over `I\setminus J` is non-positive and may be discarded in an upper bound. On `J`, the factor in parentheses in (19) is at least `1`; outside `I` it is at most `4`. Therefore, for constants `c,C>0` independent of `m`, the positive-half integral in (17) satisfies

\[
\int_0^S\phi(t)\cosh^2(2\pi b_m t)
\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi t}{2R}\right)^m dt
\le
-c\,e^{m(F(a)-\delta)}
+C\,e^{m(F(a)-3\delta)}.
\tag{24}
\]

The right-hand side is negative for all sufficiently large `m`. Evenness of `phi` and of the weight doubles the same sign on the negative half-axis, so

\[
\boxed{Q_H(\mathcal Z_m)<0}
\tag{25}
\]

for all sufficiently large `m`.

But `Z_m` has no real points, so the universal inequality (8) demands

\[
Q_H(\mathcal Z_m)\ge2N_m=2^{m+2}>0,
\tag{26}
\]

a contradiction. Thus no negative point `a` exists and (3) follows.

The proof uses no zeta asymptotic, no pair correlation, no positivity assumption on `H`, and no Hilbert representation. The only structural hypotheses are the universal finite inequality (2), conjugation symmetry of its allowed multisets, and a continuous compactly supported Fourier profile.

## 5. WI-146's remote repair passes two points but necessarily fails many points

WI-146 constructs a signed atomic profile

\[
\mu=(1+A-B)\delta_0
-\frac A2(\delta_a+\delta_{-a})
+\frac B2(\delta_R+\delta_{-R}),
\qquad
B=A(a/R)^2,
\tag{27}
\]

which satisfies the complete two-point conditions

\[
H(x)\ge0,
\qquad
H(iy)\ge1.
\tag{28}
\]

It then convolves `mu` with a small nonnegative smooth autocorrelation bump `rho` to obtain a real-even `C_c^infinity` density `phi=mu*rho` with genuine negative bands near `+-a` and positive repair bands near `+-R`, while preserving (28).

The theorem above applies directly to this **smoothed** profile: because `phi<0` on an open interval and has compact support, some binomial conjugate-pair multiset `Z_m` makes its full pair sum negative. Therefore the smooth repair cannot satisfy (2), regardless of how small the remote positive mass `B` is.

The original atomic model is even easier to falsify. For the single remote radius `R`, take two centers `0` and `1/(2R)` and put a conjugate pair of common depth `b` above and below each center. The horizontal factor is

\[
P(t)=1+e^{-\pi i t/R}.
\]

It vanishes exactly at `t=R` while remaining nonzero at `t=a<R`, so the positive remote atom contributes exactly zero and the negative `a`-atom grows like `-cosh^2(2 pi a b)`. Hence its pair sum tends to `-infinity` as `b->infinity`. More generally, finitely many farther positive atoms can be annihilated simultaneously by the product of the corresponding factors. The continuous proof above is the robust version: instead of exact pointwise annihilation, repeated binomial factors create an exponentially sharp spectral maximum at the negative band.

Thus WI-146 remains correct but its research disposition sharpens decisively:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two-point admissibility}\not\Rightarrow\text{Fourier positivity},
\quad
\text{but full compact-spectrum scalar universality}\Rightarrow\text{Fourier positivity}.
}
\tag{29}
\]

## 6. Relation to Bochner, CGdL, and prior art

Classical Bochner--Khinchin theory says that a continuous translation-invariant kernel on the real line is positive definite if and only if it is the Fourier transform of a finite positive measure. That theorem assumes positivity of all finite quadratic forms with arbitrary complex coefficients. Equation (2) is a different and apparently weaker condition: it supplies only the coefficient-one sums coming from finite conjugation-invariant multisets, together with the population term `2N-s(Z)`.

The present deduction does not reprove Bochner in general. It shows that, for **continuous compactly supported spectral densities**, the special complex multisets available in Lamzouri's universal zero-side problem are already rich enough to force the Bochner sign. The binomial/common-depth family (12) is the load-bearing extra structure. The closest general prior-art surfaces located in the audit were classical Bochner theory, Fourier--Stieltjes positive-type representations, and Bernstein/Widder exponential-convexity theory for bilateral Laplace transforms. None of those was used as a black-box implication from (2), and the literature search did not locate this exact conjugation-invariant unit-coefficient localization statement. No priority claim is made.

Chirre--Gonçalves--de Laat, *Pair Correlation Estimates for the Zeros of the Zeta Function via Semidefinite Programming*, Advances in Mathematics 361 (2020), 106926, remain the comparison target. Under RH their SDP can exploit a favorable signed Fourier profile because every zero gap is real. WI-143 and WI-144 showed that the known Lamzouri positive-Hilbert constructions cannot synthesize that tail sign; WI-145 showed that an uncompensated negative outer tail has the wrong sign already on one conjugate pair; WI-146 showed that a remote positive repair evades every two-point test. The current theorem closes the compact-spectrum version of that last escape at the **full universal scalar** level.

The public follow-up repositories and the current `weil_inertia` corpus were searched for scalar signed-kernel repairs, conjugate-pair tests, compact-spectrum Fourier positivity, and multi-point cancellation after Lamzouri's proposition. No stored result with the implication (3) was located. This is a novelty boundary for the line, not a statement of mathematical priority.

## 7. Boundaries and surviving routes

Several hypotheses are load-bearing and should not be silently generalized.

First, the theorem is proved for a **fixed continuous compactly supported Fourier profile**. It does not cover a noncompact profile with alternating positive and negative mass arbitrarily far out. The actual polynomial-Gaussian `A_LP` profiles used by CGdL have noncompact Fourier-side support; when their outer sign is genuinely negative, WI-145 already rules out the direct universal scalar transplant. A different noncompact profile with compensating positive mass at arbitrarily large radii requires a separate uniform analysis.

Second, the theorem assumes (2) for **every** finite conjugation-invariant multiset. A source-specific inequality proved only for actual zeta-zero configurations could exclude the binomial probes and is outside the result. Such a restriction would itself require new arithmetic information and cannot be inferred from Lamzouri's universal proposition.

Third, the result concerns one scalar translation-invariant pair kernel. It does not cover a joint multi-profile inequality, a sign-indefinite matrix statistic, a nonlinear incidence constraint, or a scalar inequality with an explicit correction term charging horizontal depth/negative inertia. These are precisely the kinds of additional structure left open by WI-137--WI-146.

Finally, (3) does not improve the current certified simple-critical proportion. It is a route closure: **if a proposed unconditional improvement keeps Lamzouri's universal scalar form and keeps the spectral profile compact, then allowing signed Fourier mass buys no new feasible kernels at all.** Any compact-spectrum candidate can be replaced conceptually by its now-forced nonnegative profile and is back inside the Fourier-positive side of the problem.

## Research consequence

The cheap scalar escape from the CGdL/Lamzouri mismatch is now closed substantially beyond WI-145. It is not enough to add a farther positive repair to make a compact signed profile survive off-line conjugate pairs: large binomial families of conjugate pairs act as a spectral microscope and expose every continuous negative band.

The live scalar question is therefore restricted to genuinely **noncompact** spectra with sign changes that survive all such localization probes, or to zeta-specific restrictions that invalidate universal multiset testing. More plausibly, a successful unconditional improvement using CGdL-like tail freedom will have to leave the single-scalar interface altogether and retain an additional horizontal/inertia charge or a genuinely joint matrix observable. This is a structural redirection, not a new numerical bound.
