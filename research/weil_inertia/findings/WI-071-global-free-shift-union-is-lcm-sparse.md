# WI-071 — aggregating all Yang slope lines produces an lcm-sparse free-shift set

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECTION`. This finding does **not** certify the Yang--Yang one-sided fourth-moment candidate and does not change Mathia's current unconditional simple-critical proportion. It closes one specific escape left open by WI-069 and complements WI-070: aggregating the whole changing reduced-base family before passing to the coefficient-free physical shifts does **not** make the Yang source a positive-density free-shift region. After the exact change

\[
(h_1,h_2)=(rk,qk),\qquad (r,q)=1,
\]

the union of all primitive slope lines occurring in the source lies inside a least-common-multiple sublevel set

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{lcm}(|h_1|,|h_2|)\ll X.}
\]

Even if one grants **all** coprime integer slopes, rather than only the prime/prime-power reduced bases actually present in the Yang ledger, that set has only

\[
\boxed{O(X(\log X)^2)}
\]

points in an ambient shift square of area `asymp X^2`. Thus its density is `O((log X)^2/X)`. A coefficient-free prime-pattern theorem giving only a fixed logarithmic relative error on a genuine two-dimensional free-shift box cannot be restricted to this globally aggregated source by density or by termwise ambient control: one would need a power-scale localization gain, an estimate normalized directly to the lcm-sparse support, or arithmetic cancellation in the selector/source weights.

This is not a no-go theorem for the Yang route. It leaves alive the base-aggregated multivariate polynomial representation of WI-070, a direct theorem on the lcm-sparse weighted shift set, and source-specific dispersion/orthogonality. The point is narrower: **the global union of slope lines does remain power-sparse**, so the cheap hope explicitly left open in WI-069 — that merely aggregating many changing slopes might fill enough of the free-shift box for an existing ambient theorem — is false.

## 3. The full all-integer envelope has only `O(X log^2 X)` points

For a fixed positive integer `n`, the number of ordered pairs with least common multiple exactly `n` is

\[
F(n):=\#\{(a,b)\in\mathbf N^2:\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=n\}.
\tag{14}
\]

If

\[
n=\prod_p p^{e_p},
\]

then for each prime `p` the exponent pair `(i,j)` must satisfy `max(i,j)=e_p`. There are

\[
(e_p+1)^2-e_p^2=2e_p+1
\]

choices, so

\[
\boxed{F(n)=\prod_{p^{e}\parallel n}(2e+1)=d(n^2).}
\tag{15}
\]

This lcm-pair count is classical; no novelty is claimed for (15). An elementary upper bound is enough for the source question. For every `e>=0`,

\[
2e+1\le {e+2\choose2},
\tag{16}
\]

hence multiplicatively

\[
d(n^2)\le d_3(n),
\tag{17}
\]

where `d_3(n)` counts ordered factorizations `abc=n`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal L(Z)|
&=\sum_{n\le Z}d(n^2)\\
&\le\sum_{n\le Z}d_3(n)\\
&=\#\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbf N^3:abc\le Z\}\\
&\ll Z(\log Z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

The last bound follows directly by summing `floor(Z/(ab))` over `a,b`. Equivalently, the classical Dirichlet series of `d(n^2)` is

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{d(n^2)}{n^s}
=\prod_p\frac{1+p^{-s}}{(1-p^{-s})^2}
=\frac{\zeta(s)^3}{\zeta(2s)},
\tag{19}
\]

so standard average-order theory gives the sharper leading scale `asymp Z(log Z)^2`; that sharper asymptotic is not needed for the barrier.

Since (10) also implies `|h_1|,|h_2|<=CX`, the complete source support in physical shift coordinates is contained, up to the four sign choices, in a square of area `asymp X^2` but has cardinality at most

\[
\boxed{O(X(\log X)^2).}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus even the **maximally enlarged all-integer slope union** has ambient density

\[
\boxed{O\!\left(\frac{(\log X)^2}{X}\right).}
\tag{21}
\]

Restricting primitive directions to the prime/prime-power bases actually carrying the Yang Mertens weight can only decrease this support cardinality.

## 4. Dyadic form of the same conservation law

The global lcm count can also be seen directly in the source scaling. Take a dyadic reduced-base block

\[
r\asymp R,\qquad q\asymp Q,
\tag{22}
\]

and grant all coprime integer slopes in it. From (7), the shift length is at most

\[
K\ll \frac{X}{RQ}.
\tag{23}
\]

There are `O(RQ)` slopes and `O(K)` points on each primitive slope, with no collisions between distinct triples `(r,q,k)` because (13) recovers the triple. Hence the union contains at most

\[
O(RQK)=O(X)
\tag{24}
\]

shift pairs.

A coefficient-free free-shift rectangle large enough to contain that block has side lengths

\[
O(RK)\quad\text{and}\quad O(QK),
\]

and therefore area

\[
O(RQK^2).
\tag{25}
\]

The source density inside that natural block is consequently at most

\[
\boxed{O(1/K)=O(RQ/X).}
\tag{26}
\]

On every fixed long-shift interior `RQ<=X^(1-delta)`, this is `O(X^{-delta})`. Thus aggregation over **all** changing slopes gains a factor `RQ` relative to the single-slope index of WI-069, but it does not remove the power sparsity while the booked shift range is genuinely long.

Near the boundary `RQasymp X`, `K` becomes short and (26) need not be small. That is exactly the boundary regime already separated in WI-046/WI-052; the present finding does not convert small raw Mertens mass there into a covariance bound and makes no claim that the boundary is harmless.

## 5. Consequence for coefficient-free prime-pattern black boxes

WI-068 identifies the attractive bounded-coefficient system obtained when both shifts are genuinely free:

\[
(m,\ m-h_1,\ n,\ n-h_2).
\tag{27}
\]

Established finite-complexity prime-pattern theorems apply to genuine summation boxes/convex bodies (with their own coefficient, modulus and local-factor hypotheses). Suppose, optimistically, that a consumer supplied an ambient estimate over a two-dimensional shift rectangle with relative error

\[
\varepsilon_X=(\log X)^{-A}
\tag{28}
\]

for arbitrarily large but fixed `A`. Equations (21) or, on a dyadic long-shift block, (26), show that merely restricting such an ambient estimate to the aggregated Yang support by density would require a relative localization gain of order at least

\[
X/(\log X)^2
\quad\text{globally, or}\quad
K
\quad\text{on the block.}
\tag{29}
\]

No fixed logarithmic saving absorbs either power-scale loss when `K>=X^delta`.

This is the global counterpart of WI-069's single-line calculation. At fixed slope the selector had index `rqK`; after summing every slope in a dyadic block the index improves to `K`, and after summing all scales the support is the classical lcm-sparse set (12). The geometric obstruction weakens, but it does **not** disappear.

The claim is deliberately about a **black-box ambient-to-source restriction**. It does not say that the true prime error on the lcm set is large. A theorem normalized directly to (12), a square-function estimate across its arithmetic components, or cancellation from the actual Mertens/singular-series weight would use information absent from an ambient relative-error statement and is outside this no-go.
