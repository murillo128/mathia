# WI-132 — normalized odd Schur stability converts off-line square depth into Lamzouri slack

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + BOOTSTRAP-INTERFACE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT`. WI-126 identifies Lamzouri's discarded horizontal remainder as an exact charge for the anti-invariant vectors of off-line conjugate pairs, while WI-130 shows that the unordered raw Vandermonde spectrum is not the right stability object: raw exponential columns can become macroscopically near-null for harmless translation-confluent reasons even when the actual `g/h` quotient stays transverse. The exact normalization below isolates the orientation-sensitive invariant that controls the horizontal remainder. After dividing each odd vector by its horizontal depth, the resulting column is precisely a first exponential divided difference. The smallest eigenvalue of its Schur-complement Gram matrix gives a direct square-depth charge

\[
\boxed{
 n\ge 2N-Q+4a\sum_{z\in Z_+}m_z(\operatorname{Im}z)^2.
}
\tag{A}
\]

Consequently, if the Lamzouri baseline is asymptotically sharp while a macroscopic amount of off-line **square depth** remains, then the normalized odd Schur complement must develop a near-null direction. This is a rigid characterization of one possible exceptional near-extremizer and a concrete defect-to-zero interface. It does not improve the unconditional zeta percentage by itself: no uniform lower bound for `a` is presently available for the actual zeta family, and square-depth mass may vanish if the exceptional zeros approach the critical line or if the complement is instead carried by critical-line multiplicity/proof slack.

## 1. Exact interface from Lamzouri's finite inequality

Use the notation reconstructed in WI-126 from Youness Lamzouri, *A new proof that more than 2/3 of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line*, arXiv:2609.02882v1 (2 September 2026). For a finite conjugation-invariant multiset `Z`, let

\[
f_z(u)=\eta(u)e^{-2\pi iuz},\qquad
 g_z=\frac{f_z+f_{\bar z}}2,\qquad
 h_z=\frac{f_z-f_{\bar z}}{2i}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `V` be Lamzouri's retained space generated by all real-point vectors and all conjugation-even vectors `g_z`. Choose one representative

\[
z_j=x_j+i y_j,\qquad y_j>0,
\]

from each distinct non-real conjugate pair, with multiplicity `m_j`. WI-126 proves

\[
 n\ge 2N-Q
 +4\sum_jm_j\operatorname{dist}(h_{z_j},V)^2.
\tag{2}
\]

The finite exponential family consisting of the distinct real vectors together with all `g_{z_j}` and `h_{z_j}` is linearly independent. In particular the odd directions are linearly independent modulo `V`.

## 2. Divide out the only unavoidable near-line collapse

Define the normalized odd vectors

\[
u_j:=\frac{h_{z_j}}{y_j}.
\tag{3}
\]

This normalization is not ad hoc. Since `z_j-\bar z_j=2iy_j` and `f_{z_j}-f_{\bar z_j}=2ih_{z_j}`,

\[
\boxed{
 u_j
 =\frac{f_{z_j}-f_{\bar z_j}}{z_j-\bar z_j}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus `u_j` is exactly the first divided difference of the Lamzouri exponential column across the conjugate pair. When the pair approaches the real axis,

\[
 u_j(u)
 \longrightarrow
 -2\pi i\,u\,\eta(u)e^{-2\pi iux_j},
\tag{5}
\]

so the normalized direction has a finite confluent limit even though `h_{z_j}\to0`. This separates the trivial loss caused solely by horizontal depth from genuine screening of the odd direction by the retained even/real span.

Let `U:\mathbb C^k\to L^2` be the synthesis operator

\[
Uc=\sum_{j=1}^k c_j u_j
\]

and define the orientation-sensitive quotient Gram matrix

\[
\boxed{
 S:=U^*(I-P_V)U.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Because the `u_j` are nonzero rescalings of the independent `h_{z_j}` and no nontrivial odd combination lies in `V`, `S` is positive definite for every finite configuration. Put

\[
a:=\lambda_{\min}(S)>0.
\tag{7}
\]

Its diagonal entries have the direct geometric meaning

\[
S_{jj}=\operatorname{dist}(u_j,V)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

## 3. Exact square-depth lower bound

Because `h_{z_j}=y_j u_j`, equations (7)--(8) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_jm_j\operatorname{dist}(h_{z_j},V)^2
&=\sum_jm_jy_j^2 S_{jj}\\
&\ge a\sum_jm_jy_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Inserting this into (2) proves (A):

\[
\boxed{
 n\ge 2N-Q+4aD_2,
 \qquad
 D_2:=\sum_jm_jy_j^2.
}
\tag{10}
\]

No approximation, asymptotic passage, spacing hypothesis, or new prime-side moment is used in (10). The only ingredients beyond Lamzouri's published finite setup are the exact WI-126 remainder and elementary Hilbert-space projection algebra.

A useful count version follows immediately. If every off-line representative in a selected population satisfies `y_j>=y_0>0`, then

\[
D_2\ge y_0^2\sum_jm_j,
\]

so a lower bound `a>=a_0>0` charges that population by at least

\[
4a_0y_0^2\sum_jm_j.
\tag{11}
\]

This is precisely the distinction that a real double does not satisfy: critical-line multiplicity contributes no positive horizontal depth and remains compatible with the zero-cost equality pattern already identified in WI-126.

## 4. Near-equality forces collapse of the correct quotient, not the raw Vandermonde

Equation (10) has an immediate stability converse. Suppose a sequence of finite configurations has

\[
 n-(2N-Q)=o(N)
\tag{12}
\]

while its off-line square-depth mass obeys

\[
D_2\ge\delta N
\qquad(\delta>0).
\tag{13}
\]

Then

\[
4aD_2\le n-(2N-Q)
\]

forces

\[
\boxed{a\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{14}
\]

Equivalently, there are unit coefficient vectors `c` for which

\[
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(\sum_jc_ju_j,V\right)^2
=c^*Sc\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore a near-extremizer carrying macroscopic off-line square depth must create a genuinely **anti-invariant grouped near-null direction modulo the complete retained space**. This is stronger and better aligned than merely requiring small singular values of the raw reciprocal-node Vandermonde.

The distinction is load-bearing. WI-130 constructs positive-density raw Vandermonde tail collapse caused by nearby same-sign centers while the Lamzouri `g/h` quotient remains uniformly transverse. Such a family can make unordered raw singular values tiny without making `a` tiny. Equation (14) shows that actual horizontal screening has to collapse the normalized odd Schur complement itself.

## 5. Full Riesz stability is a sufficient, but deliberately stronger, condition

The quotient constant `a` is the minimal condition needed for (10). A conventional Riesz lower bound for the full conjugation-adapted family is sufficient. Choose any linearly independent synthesis matrix `B` for the retained space `V` and consider

\[
W=[B\ U].
\]

If

\[
W^*W\succeq A I,
\tag{16}
\]

then for every coefficient vector `c`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
c^*Sc
&=\inf_b\|Bb+Uc\|^2\\
&\ge\inf_b A(\|b\|^2+\|c\|^2)
\ge A\|c\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{S\succeq AI,\qquad a\ge A.}
\tag{18}
\]

This is the Schur-complement/shorted-Gram formulation of the same statement. It explains exactly what kind of nonharmonic-Fourier theorem would be strong enough to feed the Lamzouri remainder: a lower Riesz bound for the **normalized conjugation-adapted** family automatically yields an off-line square-depth charge.

The converse need not hold. The even/real block can itself be poorly conditioned while the odd quotient remains transverse, so proving full Riesz stability may be unnecessarily expensive. For the defect-to-zero problem, `S` is the sharper target.

## 6. Exponential divided differences are the correct classical neighboring theory

Avdonin--Ivanov, *Exponential Riesz bases of subspaces and divided differences* (Algebra i Analiz 13:3 (2001), English translation St. Petersburg Math. J. 13:3 (2002), arXiv:math/0103160), develop exactly the classical normalization needed when exponential frequencies collide. Their generalized divided differences remain well behaved in confluent limits, and their Riesz-basis theory is formulated for relatively uniformly discrete frequency sets decomposed into bounded close-frequency groups. Avdonin--Moran, *Ingham-type inequalities and Riesz bases of divided differences* (2001), supplies complementary quantitative grouped-basis results. These are established classical results; no novelty is claimed for divided differences, Riesz sequences, Schur complements, or principal-angle language.

For one conjugate pair, the Lamzouri coordinates are connected to the ordinary divided-difference coordinates by an explicit triangular change of basis. From

\[
f_z=f_{\bar z}+2iyu
\]

we get

\[
g=f_{\bar z}+iyu.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus, with columns ordered as `[f_{\bar z},u]`, the change to `[g,u]` is

\[
M_y=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
iy&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]

If `|y|<=B`, then `M_y` and its inverse are uniformly bounded; for example

\[
\sigma_{\min}(M_y)^2
\ge\frac1{\|M_y^{-1}\|_F^2}
=\frac1{2+y^2}
\ge\frac1{2+B^2}.
\tag{21}
\]

So any genuinely uniform divided-difference Riesz lower bound for the underlying grouped system transfers to the Lamzouri `(g,u)` coordinates with only a bounded-depth constant loss. This is the positive reason the grouped theory, rather than real-projection or raw-column conditioning, is the appropriate literature surface after WI-130--WI-131.

## 7. Why the classical theorem does not black-box close the zeta problem

The preceding correspondence is an interface, not a hidden proof of uniform zeta transversality. Several hypotheses remain load-bearing.

First, Avdonin--Ivanov's complete Riesz-basis description is for unweighted exponential divided differences and includes global density/generating-function conditions. Lamzouri's columns carry the common window `eta`, which tapers at the support boundary in the Montgomery--Taylor optimization. Multiplication by such a window is not globally boundedly invertible, so an unweighted Riesz theorem cannot simply be quoted to obtain (16). On a fixed subinterval where `|eta|` is bounded below one can transfer a lower bound from the restricted unweighted system, but proving the needed critical-density lower estimate for the actual zero frequencies remains a separate theorem.

Second, bounded horizontal depth is required for a uniform version of the triangular estimate (21). In the zeta scaling, no unconditional input audited here supplies a uniform bounded strip containing every off-line zero. Deep zeros and near-line zeros therefore have to be separated quantitatively rather than hidden in one global Riesz constant.

Third, relative uniform discreteness/group size and the relevant complete-interpolation condition are not automatic for zeta zeros. Multiplicity and arbitrarily small vertical gaps are part of the exceptional population being studied. Grouped divided differences are designed to regularize finite clusters, but a source-compatible theorem must still control the cluster size/density and the resulting quotient lower bound.

Finally, WI-131 gives an exact conjugate-lattice counterexample to the blanket claim that bounded-strip complex exponential Riesz stability is equivalent to Riesz stability of the real projections. Duplicating real projections can coexist with a perfectly stable complex conjugate-pair system. Therefore real-projection spacing is not a legitimate shortcut to (18); the normalized grouped orientation must be preserved.
