# WI-243 — Subexponential growth of one dyadic odd autocorrelation discrepancy is equivalent to RH

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`.

WI-241 isolates the signed von Mangoldt discrepancy as the load-bearing source term in the odd localized Weil form, and WI-242 shows that absolute Prime Number Theorem error envelopes destroy the cancellation needed to control it. A particularly simple odd autocorrelation makes the remaining difficulty exact: its source discrepancy is a dyadic finite difference of the weighted Chebyshev/Riesz mean studied by Suzuki.

For this one profile, even a very weak large-radius estimate is already target-equivalent. If `Delta(a)` denotes the discrepancy below and

\[
\tau_\Delta:=\limsup_{a\to\infty}\frac{1}{a}\log\bigl(1+|\Delta(a)|\bigr),
\tag{1}
\]

then every nontrivial zero `rho=beta+i gamma` of `zeta` satisfies

\[
\boxed{\left|\beta-\frac12\right|\le \frac{\tau_\Delta}{2}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}
\iff
\tau_\Delta=0
\iff
|\Delta(a)|=\exp(o(a))
}
\tag{3}
\]

where the last condition means equivalently that for every `epsilon>0`, `|Delta(a)|=O_epsilon(e^{epsilon a})`. In particular, boundedness, polynomial growth in `a`, or any other subexponential bound for this single discrepancy would already prove RH. Under RH one has the sharper limit

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(a)\longrightarrow
2-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(\frac12\right)
=\frac{M(0)}2
=-0.6860917096\ldots,
}
\tag{4}
\]

with `M(0)` the mean-model multiplier from WI-241/WI-242.

This is a barrier, not a proof of RH. It shows that replacing the failed absolute-envelope route by a generic theorem asserting merely subexponential growth of the signed slow-source discrepancy is not an easier intermediate objective: for this concrete autocorrelation that statement already contains RH. The useful remaining source-side leverage must exploit additional finite-radius/first-crossing structure, or establish a genuinely weaker directional statement rather than global large-`a` growth control.

## 1. A closure-level odd profile with an exact two-scale autocorrelation

Consider the normalized odd `L^2` profile

\[
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\operatorname{sgn}(x)\mathbf 1_{(-1,1)}(x),
\qquad \|\phi\|_2=1.
\tag{5}
\]

For `u>=0`, its real autocorrelation

\[
C(u):=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(t+u)\phi(t)\,dt
\tag{6}
\]

is elementary:

\[
C(u)=
\begin{cases}
1-\frac32u,&0\le u\le1,\\[2mm]
\frac12u-1,&1\le u\le2,\\[2mm]
0,&u\ge2.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{C(u)=2(1-u)_+-\frac12(2-u)_+.}
\tag{8}
\]

The profile is deliberately only a closure-level `L^2` test and is not claimed here to belong to Suzuki's smooth compactly supported core. The theorem below is an arithmetic statement about the explicit discrepancy generated by its autocorrelation; no operator-domain assertion for (5) is needed.

Define

\[
\Delta(a)
:=
\sum_{n\le e^{2a}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 C\!\left(\frac{\log n}{a}\right)
-
\int_1^{e^{2a}}x^{-1/2}
 C\!\left(\frac{\log x}{a}\right)dx.
\tag{9}
\]

This is exactly the WI-241 signed source discrepancy for the autocorrelation (7), written independently of any smooth-core approximation.

## 2. Exact reduction to Suzuki's weighted Chebyshev remainder

Let

\[
W(X):=\sum_{n\le X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\log\frac{X}{n},
\qquad
F(X):=W(X)-4\sqrt X.
\tag{10}
\]

There is no endpoint convention issue in `W`: if `X` is a prime power, the endpoint summand vanishes because `log(X/X)=0`.

From (8), the discrete term in (9) is exactly

\[
\frac{2}{a}W(e^a)-\frac{1}{2a}W(e^{2a}).
\tag{11}
\]

The matching continuum integral uses

\[
\int_1^X x^{-1/2}\log\frac{X}{x}\,dx
=4\sqrt X-2\log X-4.
\tag{12}
\]

Substituting (12) at `X=e^a` and `X=e^{2a}` into the same hinge decomposition cancels the square-root main terms in (11) and gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(a)
=2+\frac6a
+\frac{2F(e^a)}a
-\frac{F(e^{2a})}{2a}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Set

\[
H(a):=\frac{F(e^a)}{a}.
\tag{14}
\]

Then the entire source observable collapses to the dyadic relation

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(a)=2+\frac6a+2H(a)-H(2a).
}
\tag{15}
\]

This identity is exact. No asymptotic prime theorem, RH assumption, or zero-density input enters it.

## 3. A growth exponent for the discrepancy bounds horizontal zero depth

Let `tau_Delta` be (1), and choose any fixed `kappa>tau_Delta`. By definition, after increasing `A` if necessary,

\[
|\Delta(a)|\ll_\kappa e^{\kappa a}
\qquad(a\ge A).
\tag{16}
\]

Equation (15) therefore gives

\[
H(2a)=2H(a)+E(a),
\qquad
E(a)\ll_\kappa e^{\kappa a}.
\tag{17}
\]

For any large `t`, write

\[
t=2^n b,
\qquad A\le b<2A.
\tag{18}
\]

Iteration of (17) yields

\[
H(t)
=2^nH(b)
+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^{n-1-j}E(2^j b).
\tag{19}
\]

The compact base interval makes `H(b)` bounded. The last summand in (19) is `O_kappa(e^{kappa t/2})`; all earlier summands have exponent at most `kappa t/4` and only polynomial prefactors in `t`. Hence

\[
\boxed{H(t)\ll_\kappa e^{\kappa t/2}.}
\tag{20}
\]

Using (14), with `x=e^t`,

\[
\boxed{
F(x)\ll_\kappa (\log x)x^{\kappa/2}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Now take the Mellin transform. For `Re w>1/2`, absolute convergence permits termwise integration and gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^\infty F(x)x^{-w-1}\,dx
&=
-\frac1{w^2}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(w+\frac12\right)
-\frac4{w-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

The apparent pole at `w=1/2` cancels: the pole of `zeta` at `s=1` gives

\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(w+\frac12\right)
\sim\frac1{w-1/2},
\qquad
\frac1{w^2}\sim4.
\tag{23}
\]

By (21), the left-hand side of (22) converges locally uniformly and is holomorphic throughout

\[
\Re w>\frac\kappa2.
\tag{24}
\]

Therefore the meromorphic continuation of the right-hand side has no pole there. A nontrivial zero `rho=beta+i gamma` with

\[
\beta>\frac12+\frac\kappa2
\tag{25}
\]

would create a nonremovable pole of `zeta'/zeta` at

\[
w=\rho-\frac12,
\tag{26}
\]

where neither `w^{-2}` nor the elementary term in (22) can cancel it. Thus no such zero exists. Since `kappa>tau_Delta` was arbitrary,

\[
\beta\le\frac12+\frac{\tau_\Delta}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]

The functional equation supplies the reflected zero `1-\bar\rho`, so (27) also gives the lower inequality and proves (2).

This argument does not require a rightmost off-line zero. It excludes each hypothetical zero directly through the holomorphic half-plane forced by the source-growth estimate.

## 4. Subexponential growth is exactly RH

If

\[
\tau_\Delta=0,
\tag{28}
\]

then (2) forces every nontrivial zero onto `Re s=1/2`, hence RH.

Conversely, Masatoshi Suzuki's Theorem 1 in **On variants of Chebyshev's conjecture** proves under RH that

\[
\frac{F(x)}{\log x}
\longrightarrow
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(\frac12\right).
\tag{29}
\]

With `x=e^a`, this is

\[
H(a)\longrightarrow
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(\frac12\right).
\tag{30}
\]

Substituting (30) into the exact dyadic identity (15) proves

\[
\Delta(a)\longrightarrow
2-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\left(\frac12\right),
\tag{31}
\]

which is bounded and therefore has `tau_Delta=0`. WI-242 already identifies the same constant with `M(0)/2`, giving (4).

A useful strengthening of (3) is immediate: **any fixed polynomial bound**

\[
|\Delta(a)|\ll (1+a)^B
\tag{32}
\]

for any finite `B` would prove RH, as would boundedness or any estimate `exp(o(a))`. This explains why the `O(a^3)` scale that WI-242 obtains by inserting the usual RH pointwise PNT bound and taking absolute values is not an innocuous target for an unconditional proof: despite being very weak numerically, a cancellation-preserving bound of that size for the actual signed discrepancy would already force RH.

## 5. Relation to the source-side Weil program

WI-241 showed that after exact pole/main-density cancellation the odd localized Weil form is

\[
Q_W^a(v)=Q_\gamma(v)+R(v)-2\Delta_\Lambda^a(v).
\tag{33}
\]

WI-242 then ruled out absolute-envelope control of `Delta_Lambda` at the slow-dilate scale. Equations (15)--(32) sharpen the diagnosis in a different direction. For one explicit odd autocorrelation, a generic statement of the form

\[
\text{``the signed discrepancy grows only polynomially/subexponentially in the support radius''}
\tag{34}
\]

is already an RH criterion.

Thus a large-radius source proof cannot expect to close the program by first proving a seemingly modest uniform or polynomial estimate for this discrepancy and only later extracting RH. The hard zero-free information is already encoded in that estimate.

This redirects the live source branch toward information not collapsed by (34): for example the finite **first-crossing** geometry of WI-238, the fact that all smaller support radii remain positive before a hypothetical zero mode appears, or another one-sided/local relation that is genuinely weaker than controlling the global exponential growth rate of (9). The finding supplies a quantitative falsification tariff for such proposals: a theorem implying `tau_Delta=0` for (7) has already crossed the RH boundary.

## 7. Stress tests and boundaries

1. **No smooth-core claim.** The sign profile (5) is discontinuous. The result uses only its explicit `L^2` autocorrelation in the arithmetic discrepancy. A later use inside Suzuki's operator domain would need a separate approximation/domain argument.
2. **The condition is global in large radius.** Equation (3) requires a bound for all sufficiently large `a` (equivalently the limsup growth exponent), not merely a sparse subsequence, an average over `a`, or isolated radii.
3. **This does not prove RH.** It classifies a natural source-side target as RH-equivalent and quantifies how any exponential growth allowance translates into a horizontal zero strip.
4. **No claim for every odd profile.** The equivalence is proved for the specific autocorrelation (7). Other profiles can have different scale relations or lose the exact dyadic recurrence.
5. **The factor `1/2` in (2) is structural.** A discrepancy bound `exp(kappa a)` enters (17) one scale below `H(2a)`, producing `F(x)=O(x^{kappa/2}\log x)` and therefore a zero-free half-plane beginning at `1/2+kappa/2`.
6. **The pole at `s=1` is not mistaken for a zero obstruction.** Equation (23) removes exactly the only elementary pole of (22) in the relevant region; every nontrivial off-line zero produces a distinct logarithmic-derivative pole.

## 8. Consequence

The source-side program now has a sharper boundary. WI-242 says that absolute PNT error bounds are too coarse to control the required signed discrepancy; WI-243 says that a cancellation-preserving global theorem making even this one dyadic discrepancy merely subexponential would already prove RH. The next source-side step should therefore exploit the additional finite-radius information present at Suzuki's first crossing rather than seek a generic large-support magnitude estimate whose successful conclusion is already equivalent to the final target.
