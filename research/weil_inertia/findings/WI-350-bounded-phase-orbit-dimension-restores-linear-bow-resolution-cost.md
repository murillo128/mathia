# WI-350 — bounded phase-orbit dimension restores linear bow resolution cost

**Status:** `established`

**Claim type:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-MECHANISM + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + NON-RH`

**One-line statement:** For a height-independent Lipschitz bow feature that is \(U(1)\)-equivariant and whose output vector has uniformly bounded phase-cyclic orbit dimension \(D\), the normalized Gram effective rank grows at most linearly in the resolution ratio \(L/\mu\):
\[
r_\tau(G)\le D\left(1+\frac{H\Delta L}{4\mu\sqrt\tau}\right).
\]
Thus bounded phase complexity cannot realize the generic quadratic rank-versus-resolution scaling of `WI-348`/`WI-349`; matching that regime under equivariance forces \(D\) itself to grow.

## Why this matters

`WI-347` showed that a single projective/fixed-charge sector pays a linear rank-resolution cost, whereas `WI-348` gave only the generic quadratic covering obstruction and `WI-349` showed that the latter is sharp on the physical bow carrier. Those results left a gap: an equivariant feature may mix several \(U(1)\) charges, so it is neither a single-charge feature nor an arbitrary non-equivariant feature.

The theorem below fills that gap. The relevant complexity is not ambient output dimension but the dimension of the phase orbit generated by each centered output vector. A uniformly bounded number of active charge sectors preserves the linear obstruction, up to the factor \(D\). Consequently the generic \(r\asymp (L/\mu)^2\) behavior can coexist with equivariance only if the phase-orbit complexity grows with the target rank.

This is a structural restriction on possible bow witnesses. It does **not** establish the distinguished off-diagonal cancellation requested by `CLUE-bow-distinguished-twist-offdiagonal-cancellation`, and it does not improve the certified simple-critical-zero proportion by itself.

## Setup

Use the normalized physical bow carrier \(w_U\) over a height interval \(I\) of length \(H\). Let the logarithmic source support be centered at \(x_c\) and have width \(\Delta\). Define the centered carrier
\[
z_U=e^{-iUx_c}w_U.
\]
The physical bow geometry used in the preceding findings gives
\[
\|z_U-z_V\|\le \frac{\Delta}{2}|U-V|.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(F\) be a height-independent map from the phase-saturated source domain into a Hilbert space \(\mathcal K\). Assume:

1. **\(U(1)\)-equivariance.** There is a unitary representation \(R:S^1\to U(\mathcal K)\) such that
   \[
   F(e^{i\phi}x)=R(e^{i\phi})F(x).
   \tag{2}
   \]
2. **Lipschitz control.** For all relevant source vectors,
   \[
   \|F(x)-F(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|.
   \tag{3}
   \]
3. **Transmission floor.** For all relevant source vectors,
   \[
   \|F(x)\|\ge \mu>0.
   \tag{4}
   \]
4. **Uniform phase-cyclic dimension.** Define
   \[
   D:=\sup_{U\in I}\dim\operatorname{span}\{R(\zeta)F(z_U):\zeta\in S^1\}<\infty.
   \tag{5}
   \]

For sampled heights \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\), put
\[
h_i=\frac{F(w_{U_i})}{\|F(w_{U_i})\|},
\qquad
G=(\langle h_i,h_j\rangle)_{i,j=1}^m.
\]
Write the eigenvalues of \(G\) as \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_m\ge0\), and define the effective rank at tail tolerance \(\tau\in(0,1)\) by
\[
r_\tau(G):=\min\left\{q:\sum_{j>q}\lambda_j(G)\le \tau m\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

## Theorem

Under (1)--(5), for every \(\tau\in(0,1)\) and \(L>0\),
\[
\boxed{
 r_\tau(G)
 \le
 D\left(1+\frac{H\Delta L}{4\mu\sqrt\tau}\right).
}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, whenever \(r_\tau(G)>D\),
\[
\boxed{
 \frac{L}{\mu}
 \ge
 \frac{4\sqrt\tau}{H\Delta}
 \left(\frac{r_\tau(G)}D-1\right).
}
\tag{8}
\]
For fixed \(H\Delta\) and bounded \(D\), extensive effective rank therefore costs \(L/\mu=\Omega(r_\tau)\), not merely the generic \(\Omega(\sqrt{r_\tau})\) resolution cost from `WI-348`.

## Proof

Fix a scale \(\rho>0\). Partition the height interval into
\[
N\le 1+\frac{H\Delta}{4\rho}
\tag{9}
\]
cells of length at most \(4\rho/\Delta\), and choose a center \(V_k\) in each cell. If \(U\) belongs to the \(k\)-th cell, then \(|U-V_k|\le2\rho/\Delta\), so (1) gives
\[
\|z_U-z_{V_k}\|\le\rho.
\tag{10}
\]

For every cell center define its cyclic output subspace
\[
C_k:=\operatorname{span}\{R(\zeta)F(z_{V_k}):\zeta\in S^1\}.
\]
By (5), \(\dim C_k\le D\). Let
\[
W:=C_1+\cdots+C_N,
\qquad
\dim W\le DN.
\tag{11}
\]

Since \(w_U=e^{iUx_c}z_U\), equivariance gives
\[
F(w_U)=R(e^{iUx_c})F(z_U).
\]
For \(U\) in the cell centered at \(V_k\), the vector \(R(e^{iUx_c})F(z_{V_k})\) lies in \(C_k\subseteq W\). By unitarity, (3), and (10),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(F(w_U),W)
\le
\|R(e^{iUx_c})(F(z_U)-F(z_{V_k}))\|
\le L\rho.
\tag{12}
\]
After normalization and use of the floor (4),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(h_U,W)\le \frac{L\rho}{\mu}.
\tag{13}
\]

Let \(P_W\) be orthogonal projection onto \(W\). The matrix whose columns are \(P_Wh_i\) has rank at most \(DN\), and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\|h_i-P_Wh_i\|^2
\le
m\left(\frac{L\rho}{\mu}\right)^2.
\tag{14}
\]
By the Eckart--Young low-rank approximation principle, equivalently the variational characterization of the singular-value tail, the squared singular values of the column matrix are the eigenvalues of its Gram matrix \(G\). Hence
\[
\sum_{j>DN}\lambda_j(G)
\le
m\left(\frac{L\rho}{\mu}\right)^2.
\tag{15}
\]
Choose
\[
\rho=\frac{\mu\sqrt\tau}{L}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then the right side of (15) is \(\tau m\), so \(r_\tau(G)\le DN\). Substituting (9) and (16) proves (7), and rearranging proves (8).

## Edge cases

If \(L=0\), the centered output \(F(z_U)\) is constant on the connected carrier. Equivariance then puts every physical output \(F(w_U)\) in one cyclic subspace, so \(r_\tau(G)\le D\).

If \(H\Delta=0\), there is no centered bow variation to resolve: again all outputs lie in one cyclic subspace of dimension at most \(D\). The assumption \(\mu>0\) is essential because the statement concerns normalized output vectors; without a transmission floor the normalization can amplify arbitrarily small raw differences.

## Complexity corollary

Suppose \(H\Delta\le1\) and an equivariant feature attempts to realize the generic `WI-349` scaling at effective rank \(r=r_\tau(G)\), namely
\[
\frac L\mu\le C\sqrt r.
\tag{17}
\]
Then (7) implies
\[
r\le D\left(1+\frac{C\sqrt r}{4\sqrt\tau}\right),
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
D\ge
\frac{r}{1+C\sqrt r/(4\sqrt\tau)}
\sim
\frac{4\sqrt\tau}{C}\sqrt r.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus an equivariant construction can approach the generic quadratic phase-saturated covering law only by increasing its phase-orbit dimension. Bounded charge complexity is incompatible with that asymptotic regime.

## Peter--Weyl / charge interpretation

For \(S^1\), the irreducible unitary representations are the one-dimensional integer characters \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^n\). Decompose a vector \(v\in\mathcal K\) into its character components \(v=\sum_n v_n\). Its cyclic span
\[
\operatorname{span}\{R(\zeta)v:\zeta\in S^1\}
\]
is exactly the span of the nonzero vectors \(v_n\). Consequently its dimension is the number of distinct active charges in \(v\); multiplicity of a character in the ambient representation does not increase the orbit dimension of a single vector.

This converts (5) into a concrete feature-complexity condition: \(D\) bounds the number of charge sectors simultaneously active along the centered bow carrier. The statement is a direct application of the standard compact-abelian representation decomposition; no new representation-theoretic theorem is claimed here.

## Relation to nearby findings

- `WI-345` showed that fixed-degree polynomial/tensor lifts have only finitely many relevant \(U(1)\) charge sectors. Such architectures therefore fall naturally into a bounded-\(D\) regime and inherit the linear obstruction above.
- `WI-347` is recovered at \(D=1\): a pure fixed-charge/projective orbit is one-dimensional after centering, so the linear rank-resolution cost survives with no phase-complexity factor.
- `WI-348` treated arbitrary height-independent uniformly continuous phase-sensitive features and obtained the generic two-dimensional source-cylinder covering law, hence the weaker \(L/\mu=\Omega(\sqrt r)\) requirement.
- `WI-349` proved that this generic quadratic law is sharp on the physical bow carrier. `WI-350` explains how that sharpness can coexist with equivariance: the number of active phase sectors must itself grow, at least at the scale forced by (18) if the generic \(\sqrt r\) resolution law is retained.

Together these findings give an interpolation rather than a dichotomy: one charge gives the strongest linear obstruction, boundedly many charges preserve it up to \(D\), while unrestricted phase complexity can return to the generic quadratic covering regime.

## What this does not prove

The theorem does not cover arbitrary non-equivariant features; the `WI-349` sharpness construction is allowed to evade it. It also does not obstruct architectures in which \(D\) grows sufficiently rapidly, the transmission floor \(\mu\) collapses, \(L/\mu\) diverges, \(H\Delta\) grows, or the feature map explicitly depends on height.

Most importantly for the canonical research mandate, bounded phase-orbit dimension alone says nothing about the sign of the distinguished \(\Lambda-\Lambda^\sharp\) covariance. It neither proves the accepted off-diagonal cancellation clue nor separates multiple critical-line zeros from off-line blocks. It is a no-go/interpolation statement about one possible witness architecture, not an RH argument and not a new zero-proportion theorem.

## Evidence state

**Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-MECHANISM + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + NON-RH`.

The bow metric estimate (1) is inherited from the established physical-carrier analysis in `WI-348`--`WI-349`. The new step is the finite-cyclic-dimension cover: center the carrier, cover only the height coordinate, and absorb the otherwise free global phase motion into a \(D\)-dimensional cyclic subspace per height cell. The spectral-tail conclusion is then the classical optimal low-rank approximation principle.

## Novelty and claim boundary

The genuinely new content relative to this research line is the interpolation parameter \(D\): phase equivariance by itself does not force the single-charge obstruction, but finite phase-orbit complexity does. This closes the structural gap between `WI-347` and the unrestricted `WI-348`/`WI-349` regime.

The theorem should be falsified only by violating one of its explicit hypotheses or the bow metric estimate; it should not be read as a statement about all conceivable Weil-form features. In particular, infinite or rank-growing charge support remains a live escape route.

For the accepted distinguished-twist clue, the useful next question is now sharper: determine whether any naturally arising feature or covariance functional capable of distinguishing \(\Lambda\) from \(\Lambda^\sharp\) has **bounded or quantitatively controlled phase-orbit dimension** on the physical bow carrier.
