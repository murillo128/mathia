# WI-134 — depth-weighted Schur rearrangement forces a macroscopic near-null sector before off-line screening can be sharp

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + LITERATURE+DERIVED + STRUCTURAL-RIGIDITY + BOOTSTRAP-INTERFACE + PRIOR-ART-REDIRECT`. WI-132 identifies Lamzouri's normalized odd Schur complement and obtains a square-depth charge from its single smallest eigenvalue. WI-133 then gives a decisive counterexample to using that scalar minimum as the bootstrap invariant: a harmless endpoint-concentration mode can make `lambda_min -> 0` while the actual horizontal remainder remains extensive. The exact refinement below keeps the **entire Schur spectrum** and the depth weights instead of collapsing them to one minimum eigenvalue.

For the off-real conjugate-pair representatives, repeat the normalized odd column once for each multiplicity occurrence and let

\[
\widetilde S=\widetilde U^*(I-P_V)\widetilde U\succeq0.
\]

If its eigenvalues are

\[
0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_K
\]

and the repeated normalized horizontal depths are `y_1,...,y_K>0`, then Lamzouri's exact horizontal charge

\[
H:=\sum_jm_j\operatorname{dist}(h_{z_j},V)^2
\]

obeys the rearrangement inequality

\[
\boxed{
H\ge\sum_{i=1}^K s_i\,w_i^\downarrow,
\qquad w_i=y_i^2,
}
\tag{A}
\]

where `w_i^downarrow` denotes the depth squares in decreasing order. Consequently, after truncating depths at any fixed `A>0`, if

\[
r_a:=\#\{i:s_i<a\},
\qquad
D_{2,A}:=\sum_{i=1}^K\min(y_i^2,A^2),
\]

then for every `a>0`

\[
\boxed{
H\ge a\bigl(D_{2,A}-A^2r_a\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{B}
\]

Thus a Lamzouri near-extremizer with `H=o(N)` and `D_{2,A}\ge\delta N` must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\liminf\frac{r_a}{N}\ge\frac{\delta}{A^2}
\quad\text{for every fixed }a>0.
}
\tag{C}
\]

This is the nonperiodic Schur-spectrum analogue of WI-128's periodic Vandermonde-tail condition. A single collapsing eigenvalue is not enough. If a density-scale amount of bounded, nonvanishing off-line square depth is genuinely screened, then a **macroscopic low-energy sector** of the normalized odd quotient must collapse toward zero. The only cheap alternatives are horizontal-depth collapse toward the critical line, multiplicity-generated duplicate modes, or slack carried elsewhere in Lamzouri's decomposition. No unconditional zeta-zero percentage changes in this finding.

## 1. Exact interface from WI-126 and WI-132

Lamzouri's finite Proposition 2.1 is applied to a conjugation-invariant multiset `Z`. For one representative

\[
z_j=x_j+iy_j,\qquad y_j>0,
\]

of each distinct non-real conjugate pair, with multiplicity `m_j`, define

\[
f_z(u)=\eta(u)e^{-2\pi iuz},
\qquad
g_z=\frac{f_z+f_{\bar z}}2,
\qquad
h_z=\frac{f_z-f_{\bar z}}{2i}.
\]

Let `V` be the retained space generated by the real-point vectors and all conjugation-even `g_z` vectors. WI-126 reconstructs the discarded nonnegative remainders in Lamzouri's Hilbert-space proof and proves

\[
 n\ge2N-Q+4H,
\qquad
H:=\sum_jm_j\operatorname{dist}(h_{z_j},V)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

WI-132 divides out the unavoidable collapse of an off-line pair as it approaches the real axis:

\[
u_j:=\frac{h_{z_j}}{y_j}
=\frac{f_{z_j}-f_{\bar z_j}}{z_j-\bar z_j}.
\tag{2}
\]

Writing `Ue_j=u_j`, its normalized odd quotient Gram matrix is

\[
S=U^*(I-P_V)U\succ0
\tag{3}
\]

for every finite collection of distinct off-real pairs. In particular,

\[
\operatorname{dist}(h_{z_j},V)^2
=y_j^2S_{jj}.
\tag{4}
\]

The scalar estimate in WI-132 replaced every diagonal `S_jj` by `lambda_min(S)`. WI-133 proves that this loses too much: `lambda_min(S)` can vanish for endpoint-taper reasons even when the sum in (1) remains extensive. The purpose here is to retain the spectral distribution of `S` and the actual depth weights.

Primary source for the finite Hilbert inequality and zeta scaling: Youness Lamzouri, *A new proof that more than 2/3 of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line*, arXiv:2609.02882v1 (2 September 2026), especially Proposition 2.1 and Section 3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882.

## 2. Expand multiplicities before taking the spectrum

The multiplicities in (1) are weights, whereas the matrix `S` in (3) has one coordinate for each **distinct** conjugate pair. For a count-scale spectral statement it is cleaner to expand those weights first.

Let

\[
K:=\sum_jm_j
\tag{5}
\]

be the number of off-real conjugate-pair occurrences, so the corresponding individual off-line zeros contribute `2K` labels to `N`. Form a synthesis operator `\widetilde U:C^K->L^2` by repeating the column `u_j` exactly `m_j` times, and put

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde S:=\widetilde U^*(I-P_V)\widetilde U\succeq0.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Repeat `y_j` along the same `m_j` coordinates and let

\[
Y:=\operatorname{diag}(y_1,\ldots,y_K)
\tag{7}
\]

in this expanded indexing. The diagonal of `\widetilde S` repeats `S_{jj}` with the same multiplicities, hence (4) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
H=\operatorname{tr}(Y^2\widetilde S).
}
\tag{8}
\]

This expansion also records multiplicity honestly. If `m_j>1`, the repeated columns create `m_j-1` exact zero directions in `\widetilde S`. Those are not geometric screening modes; they are the spectral footprint of off-line multiplicity. Critical-line multiplicity is different again: it creates no off-real `h` coordinate at all and remains compatible with the zero-cost real-double equality pattern of WI-126.

For a simple off-line population all `m_j=1`, so `\widetilde S=S` and there are no duplication zero modes.

## 3. Exact depth--spectrum rearrangement inequality

Let

\[
0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_K
\tag{9}
\]

be the eigenvalues of `\widetilde S`, and let

\[
w_1^\downarrow\ge\cdots\ge w_K^\downarrow\ge0
\tag{10}
\]

be the decreasing rearrangement of the depth squares `y_i^2`. The Hermitian trace rearrangement inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(Y^2\widetilde S)
\ge
\sum_{i=1}^K w_i^\downarrow s_i.
}
\tag{11}
\]

For completeness, no black box is needed. Diagonalize

\[
\widetilde S=U\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_K)U^*.
\]

Then its diagonal in the depth-coordinate basis is

\[
d_j=\sum_i|U_{ji}|^2s_i.
\]

The matrix `P_{ji}=|U_{ji}|^2` is doubly stochastic. By Birkhoff's theorem it is a convex combination of permutation matrices, and the elementary rearrangement inequality says that the minimum of `w^T P s` occurs when the decreasing weights are paired with the increasing eigenvalues. This proves (11).

This is classical matrix analysis, not a new inequality. It is the positive-semidefinite lower-side companion of von Neumann's trace inequality; see L. Mirsky, *A trace inequality of John von Neumann*, Monatshefte fuer Mathematik 79 (1975), 303--306, DOI `10.1007/BF01647331`. The line-specific deduction is its application to the exact depth-weighted Lamzouri Schur charge (8).

Equation (11) already gives the sharp worst-case orientation statement: if the horizontal charge is small, the deepest pair occurrences must be aligned, in the majorization sense, with the smallest Schur eigenvalues. It is not enough for the spectrum merely to possess one accidental low mode.

## 4. A threshold form that counts the required near-null sector

Fix a depth cutoff `A>0` and set

\[
W_A:=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\min(y_i^2,A^2)\bigr),
\qquad
D_{2,A}:=\operatorname{tr}W_A.
\tag{12}
\]

Because `Y^2-W_A` is diagonal nonnegative and `\widetilde S` is PSD,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(Y^2\widetilde S)
\ge\operatorname{tr}(W_A\widetilde S).
\tag{13}
\]

For `a>0`, let

\[
r_a:=\#\{i:s_i<a\}.
\tag{14}
\]

Apply (11) to `W_A` and `\widetilde S`. Every eigenvalue after the first `r_a` is at least `a`, while every eigenvalue of `W_A` is at most `A^2`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
H
&\ge\sum_{i=1}^K w_{A,i}^\downarrow s_i\\
&\ge a\sum_{i=r_a+1}^K w_{A,i}^\downarrow\\
&\ge a\bigl(D_{2,A}-A^2r_a\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Together with nonnegativity this yields the advertised exact bound

\[
\boxed{
H\ge a\bigl(D_{2,A}-A^2r_a\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Inserting (15) into WI-126 gives the finite strengthening

\[
\boxed{
 n\ge2N-Q
 +4a\bigl(D_{2,A}-A^2r_a\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{16}
\]

This is a direct bootstrap interface. Any source-compatible theorem controlling both a truncated off-line square-depth mass and the number of Schur eigenvalues below a fixed threshold immediately produces a quantitative additive improvement over the Lamzouri pair-correlation baseline.

## 5. Near-sharpness forces positive-density spectral collapse

Suppose a sequence of finite configurations is near-sharp for Lamzouri's baseline in the sense

\[
 n-(2N-Q)=o(N).
\tag{17}
\]

Since the exact WI-126 remainder decomposition is nonnegative and contains at least `4H`, equation (17) forces

\[
H=o(N).
\tag{18}
\]

Assume that for some fixed `A,delta>0`

\[
D_{2,A}\ge\delta N.
\tag{19}
\]

Then (15) implies, for every fixed `a>0`,

\[
a\bigl(\delta N-A^2r_a\bigr)_+=o(N),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{r_a}{N}
\ge\frac{\delta}{A^2}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus a density-scale amount of bounded square depth cannot be screened by one, finitely many, or `o(N)` small Schur eigenvalues. A **positive-density** spectral sector must enter every fixed neighborhood of zero.

There is a particularly transparent equal-depth case. Suppose `K=pN+o(N)` simple off-line pair occurrences all have the same fixed normalized depth `b>0`. Taking `A=b` gives `D_{2,A}=Kb^2`, hence (20) yields

\[
\frac{r_a}{N}\ge p-o(1).
\]

Since `r_a<=K`, this is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\frac{r_a}{K}\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{for every fixed }a>0.
}
\tag{21}
\]

So near-sharpness would require essentially the **entire** normalized odd Schur spectrum of that off-line population to collapse to zero. WI-133's endpoint-taper example does not do this: it only proves a small minimum eigenvalue, while WI-127 supplies an extensive diagonal trace and hence an extensive horizontal charge. This is exactly why the WI-133 false positive disappears when the spectral distribution is retained.

## 6. Zeta-scale dichotomy after zero-density localization

Lamzouri applies Proposition 2.1 to

\[
\mathcal Z_T
=\left\{i\left(\rho-\frac12\right)\frac{\log T}{2\pi}:0<\gamma\le T\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]

Thus the normalized horizontal depth in this finding is

\[
y_\rho
=\left|\beta-\frac12\right|\frac{\log T}{2\pi}.
\tag{23}
\]

WI-029 audits the published Selberg--Jutila near-line zero-density theorem and proves that any positive-density off-line population has, after discarding an arbitrarily small density tail, a **bounded** normalized horizontal depth. In the present normalization, this says that if off-line zeros occur with positive density, then a positive-density set of off-line pair occurrences lies below some fixed `A` in (23).

Combine that localization with (20). Along a Lamzouri near-extremizing sequence carrying positive-density off-line mass, one of the following must happen:

1. **normalized-depth collapse:** for every fixed `b>0`, the density of off-line pair occurrences with `y_rho>=b` tends to zero, so the bounded-depth off-line population itself approaches the critical line on the `1/log T` scale; or
2. **macroscopic Schur collapse:** there exist fixed `0<b<=A` and `q>0` such that at least `qN` pair occurrences satisfy `b<=y_rho<=A`; then `D_{2,A}>=qb^2N`, and for every fixed `a>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf\frac{r_a}{N}
\ge q\frac{b^2}{A^2}>0.
}
\tag{24}
\]

This dichotomy is qualitative but source-rigid. Scalar zero density alone cannot rule out the first branch, and WI-029 already shows that it does not control the vertical geometry needed to rule out screening. The new point is that the second branch now has an **arbitrary-configuration spectral signature**, not only the periodic Vandermonde signature of WI-128.

Critical-line multiples remain a third, separate exceptional population: they are absent from `\widetilde S` and can still realize Lamzouri's real-double zero-cost pattern. Pure proof slack is also separate: if (17) fails because `R_B`, `R_U`, or `R_M` stays extensive, then no horizontal-screening conclusion is needed because the baseline already has positive slack from another source.

## 7. Relation to WI-128 and WI-133

WI-128 proves a positive-density bottom-singular-value collapse for **periodic density-one cells** by fiberizing the full `g/h` system and applying Eckart--Young--Mirsky to the reciprocal-node Vandermonde. It is stronger than (20) in that structured periodic model because it expresses the obstruction in raw reciprocal-node geometry, but it depends on periodicity, bounded depth and the fiber factorization.

Equation (20) removes the periodicity assumption completely. Its price is that it speaks directly about the quotient object `\widetilde S` rather than reducing that object to an externally checkable Vandermonde matrix. In this sense the two findings identify the same rigidity at different layers:

\[
\text{near-sharp horizontal screening}
\Longrightarrow
\text{macroscopic normalized-odd Schur near-null sector},
\]

and, in WI-128's periodic setting, the latter is forced by a macroscopic reciprocal-Vandermonde near-null sector.

WI-133 shows why the word **macroscopic** is load-bearing. A smooth compactly supported taper inevitably permits collective coefficient modes with vanishing minimum energy on the conjugate lattice. Equation (20) says that those isolated taper modes are irrelevant to density-scale screening unless their low-energy sector grows large enough to absorb the actual truncated square-depth mass.

## 9. Falsification controls and next source-side target

The algebraic statement can be falsified at three concrete points: (i) the multiplicity expansion could fail to preserve the diagonal horizontal charge; (ii) the trace rearrangement direction could be reversed; or (iii) truncating `Y^2` could fail to preserve the lower bound because `Y^2` and `\widetilde S` do not commute. None occurs. Equation (8) is a diagonal trace identity; (11) follows from a doubly-stochastic matrix and the scalar rearrangement inequality; and (13) follows directly from

\[
\operatorname{tr}((Y^2-W_A)\widetilde S)
=\sum_i(y_i^2-\min(y_i^2,A^2))\widetilde S_{ii}\ge0.
\]

The remaining difficulty is source-side, not matrix-side. A numerical or arithmetic improvement now needs a theorem showing that the actual zeta `\widetilde S_T` cannot devote the positive-density spectral mass demanded by (20) to arbitrarily small eigenvalues while a bounded-away-from-zero off-line depth population persists. Candidate inputs include a lower spectral quantile for the normalized divided-difference family, a localized frame inequality losing only `o(N)` modes, or a zeta-specific correlation/spacing theorem that prevents a macroscopic anti-invariant quotient from becoming near-null.

Any such theorem would plug into (16) quantitatively. Conversely, an explicit source-compatible zeta model with positive-density bounded nonzero depth and a positive-density `\widetilde S` near-null sector would be a decisive negative result for this bootstrap route.

**Research implication.** WI-133 kills the global `lambda_min` bootstrap but not the Schur-spectrum program. The correct necessary object is now exact: density-scale bounded off-line square depth can be hidden from Lamzouri's horizontal remainder only by a density-scale low-energy sector of the normalized odd quotient, unless that off-line population itself collapses horizontally toward the critical line or is absorbed by multiplicity/proof-slack branches.
