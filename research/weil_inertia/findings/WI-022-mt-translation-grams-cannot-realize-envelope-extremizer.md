# WI-022 — Montgomery--Taylor translation Grams cannot realize the high-branch envelope extremizer at the WI-011 scale

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + STRUCTURAL-CONSTRAINT`. This resolves the realizability question left explicit in WI-020: in the energy strip actually relevant to the WI-011 four-point assembly, the sharp abstract trace--energy extremizer is not merely unlikely but impossible for a Gram matrix generated by ordered translates of the Montgomery--Taylor kernel. The obstruction is elementary and quantitative at the entry level. No new prime-side information, moment, support, or computer-assisted certificate is used.

## 1. Statement

Let

\[
m=438,
\qquad
E_c:=\frac{m}{m-1}=\frac{438}{437},
\qquad
A:=\frac{20097}{20000},
\]

and let

\[
G(y)_{ij}=k_{\rm MT}(y_i-y_j),
\qquad
y_1<\cdots<y_m,
\]

be a limiting Montgomery--Taylor Gram block of `m` distinct simple critical zeros in the normalization used by WI-009--WI-021. Put

\[
E(G)=\operatorname{tr}(G-I)^2,
\qquad
D(G)=\operatorname{tr}\Psi(G),
\]

with the same defect profile and envelope `Phi_m` as in WI-020.

Then

\[
\boxed{
E_c<E(G)\le A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D(G)>\Phi_m(E(G)).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus no actual 438-point Montgomery--Taylor translation Gram in the WI-011 high-energy strip attains the sharp abstract spectral envelope.

More strongly, if an abstract unit-diagonal real Gram matrix at an energy `E in (E_c,A]` attains `D=Phi_m(E)`, then its off-diagonal entries all have a common modulus

\[
q=\frac{1}{m-1}\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m}E},
\qquad
\frac1{437}<q<\frac1{400}.
\tag{2}
\]

For the Montgomery--Taylor kernel, no ordered set of more than `172` real points can have

\[
|k_{\rm MT}(y_i-y_j)|=q
\quad(i\ne j)
\tag{3}
\]

for any `q` in the interval in (2). In particular `m=438` is excluded by a large packing margin.

For the exact 438-point problem there is also an explicit entrywise separation: for every such `q` and every ordered 438-point configuration, at least one off-diagonal pair satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\bigl||k_{\rm MT}(y_i-y_j)|-q\bigr|>\frac1{805}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Hence the Frobenius distance from the Montgomery--Taylor translation-Gram class to the relevant unit-diagonal equality matrices is at least `sqrt(2)/805` at the entry level.

## 2. WI-020 equality forces signed equicorrelation, not merely a one-spike spectrum

WI-020 proves that for

\[
E>E_c
\]

equality in the abstract finite-dimensional problem has spectrum

\[
\lambda_1=1+r,
\qquad
\lambda_2=\cdots=\lambda_m
=1-\frac{r}{m-1},
\qquad
r=\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m}E}>1.
\tag{5}
\]

Let

\[
a=1+r,
\qquad
b=1-\frac{r}{m-1}.
\]

Because the top eigenvalue is simple, every real symmetric matrix with this spectrum has the form

\[
G=bI+(a-b)uu^T
\tag{6}
\]

for a real unit vector `u`. The unit-diagonal condition now removes the remaining freedom. From

\[
1=G_{ii}=b+(a-b)u_i^2
\]

and

\[
1-b=\frac{r}{m-1},
\qquad
a-b=\frac{mr}{m-1},
\]

one obtains exactly

\[
u_i^2=\frac1m
\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus `u_i=s_i/sqrt(m)` with `s_i in {+1,-1}`, and for `i != j`

\[
\boxed{
G_{ij}=\frac{r}{m-1}s_i s_j.
}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular every off-diagonal entry has the same modulus `q=r/(m-1)`. The signs are not arbitrary: their triangle products are positive because they factor as `s_i s_j`.

This sharpens the WI-020 equality description in the direction needed for realizability. The abstract envelope extremizer is a signed equicorrelation Gram, so an actual translation kernel can attain it only if one level set of `|k_MT|` contains **every pairwise difference** of the ordered point set.

For `m=438` and `E<=A`, (2) follows immediately. The lower bound is `r>1`. For the upper bound, `r^2=((m-1)/m)E<A`, while

\[
A=\frac{20097}{20000}
<\left(\frac{437}{400}\right)^2,
\]

so `r<437/400` and hence `q<1/400`.

## 3. The Montgomery--Taylor kernel is too large on `[0,1]`

For the Montgomery--Taylor window

\[
v(t)=\cos(\sqrt2\,t),
\qquad |t|\le\frac12,
\]

the normalized overlap kernel is

\[
k_{\rm MT}(x)
=
\frac{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(t)\cos(2\pi xt)\,dt}
{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(t)\,dt}.
\tag{9}
\]

The weight `v` is strictly positive on this interval. For `0<x<1`, differentiation under the integral gives

\[
k_{\rm MT}'(x)
=-\frac{4\pi}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v}
\int_0^{1/2}t\,v(t)\sin(2\pi xt)\,dt<0,
\tag{10}
\]

because `0<2 pi x t<pi`. Hence `k_MT` is strictly decreasing on `[0,1]`.

At the endpoint the explicit Fourier integral gives

\[
k_{\rm MT}(1)=\frac1{2\pi^2-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Using the classical bound `pi<22/7`,

\[
2\pi^2-1<\frac{919}{49}<20,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
k_{\rm MT}(x)\ge k_{\rm MT}(1)>\frac1{20}
\qquad(0\le x\le1).}
\tag{12}
\]

Since every equality modulus in (2) satisfies `q<1/400`, no pairwise distance in a putative equality configuration can be at most `1`.

Therefore every consecutive gap must satisfy

\[
\boxed{y_{i+1}-y_i>1.}
\tag{13}
\]

## 4. The same kernel is too small sufficiently far out

The Fourier integral in (9) also has the exact closed form

\[
\boxed{
k_{\rm MT}(x)
=
\frac{
2\cos(\pi x)
-2\pi\sqrt2\,x\cot(1/\sqrt2)\sin(\pi x)
}{
2-4\pi^2x^2
},}
\tag{14}
\]

with the removable singularity interpreted by continuity.

For a coarse but fully explicit tail bound, note

\[
\sqrt2<\frac32,
\qquad
\cot(1/\sqrt2)<\frac32.
\tag{15}
\]

The second inequality follows from `tan u>u` on `(0,pi/2)` and `1/sqrt(2)>2/3`. Together with `pi<22/7` and `pi>3`, (14) gives for `x>=2`

\[
|k_{\rm MT}(x)|
<
\frac{2+(99/7)x}{36x^2-2}.
\tag{16}
\]

For every `x>=172`, the right side is strictly below `1/437`. Indeed, after clearing denominators the required inequality is

\[
252x^2-43263x-6132>0,
\tag{17}
\]

whose left side equals `7800` at `x=172` and is strictly increasing thereafter. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|k_{\rm MT}(x)|<\frac1{437}
\qquad(x\ge172).}
\tag{18}
\]

A putative equality configuration with `q>1/437` consequently cannot contain any pair at distance at least `172`.

Combining (13) and (18), an ordered `M`-point equality configuration would have

\[
M-1<y_M-y_1<172,
\]

so necessarily

\[
\boxed{M\le172.}
\tag{19}
\]

This proves (1) for `m=438`.

## 5. A quantitative entrywise separation for 438 points

For the fixed 438-point problem one can avoid the intermediate `172` cutoff and obtain a useful robust mismatch.

If some consecutive gap is at most `1`, then by (12) and `q<1/400`,

\[
\bigl||k_{\rm MT}(g)|-q\bigr|
>\frac1{20}-\frac1{400}
=\frac{19}{400}.
\tag{20}
\]

Otherwise all `437` consecutive gaps exceed `1`, so the endpoint span satisfies

\[
y_{438}-y_1>437.
\]

For `x>=437`, the same elementary bounds used above can be weakened to the convenient form

\[
|k_{\rm MT}(x)|
<\frac{16}{35x}
\le\frac{16}{35\cdot437}.
\tag{21}
\]

Since `q>1/437`, the endpoint pair then has

\[
\bigl||k_{\rm MT}(y_{438}-y_1)|-q\bigr|
>
\frac1{437}-\frac{16}{35\cdot437}
=\boxed{\frac1{805}}.
\tag{22}
\]

The smaller of the two margins (20) and (22) is `1/805`, proving (4). Because a real symmetric Gram contains both entries `(i,j)` and `(j,i)`, the Frobenius distance to any signed-equality matrix with the target modulus is strictly greater than `sqrt(2)/805`.

The point of (22) is not the particular constant. It shows that the obstruction is not merely an exact symbolic incompatibility: at the WI-011 scale, the translation-kernel geometry is separated by an explicit entry-level distance from every abstract high-branch equality Gram.

## 6. Near-extremizers are uniformly separated away from the kink

The exact nonattainment also gives a qualitative stability consequence which is useful for future refinements.

Fix any

\[
\varepsilon>0,
\qquad
E_c+\varepsilon\le A.
\]

Then there exists a constant

\[
\eta(\varepsilon)>0
\]

such that every 438-point Montgomery--Taylor translation Gram with

\[
E_c+\varepsilon\le E(G)\le A
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D(G)\ge\Phi_{438}(E(G))+\eta(\varepsilon).
}
\tag{23}
\]

This existence statement is exact, although the present argument does not optimize an explicit value of `eta`.

To see it, suppose instead that a sequence had defect gaps tending to zero. The matrices themselves lie in a compact bounded subset of the finite-dimensional Hermitian space, so after passing to a subsequence their energies converge to some `E_* in [E_c+epsilon,A]` and their spectra converge. Continuity plus WI-020 forces the limiting spectrum to be the unique high-branch equality spectrum at `E_*`.

The unit diagonal then forces the limiting matrix to have the signed-equicorrelation form (8), with modulus `q_* in (1/437,1/400)`. Since every entry of the sequence is `k_MT(y_i-y_j)` and `k_MT(x)->0` as `|x|->infinity`, convergence to a nonzero common modulus prevents any pairwise distance from escaping to infinity. Convergence to a modulus below `1/400` also prevents point collisions because `k_MT(0)=1`. After translating `y_1=0`, the point configurations therefore have a convergent subsequence. Its limit would be an actual Montgomery--Taylor translation Gram attaining the equality matrix, contradicting Sections 3--4.

The exclusion is **not uniform down to the kink** `E=E_c`. WI-020 already shows why: the low-energy branch has `D=E` identically, and the high-branch rigidity degenerates as `E downarrow E_c`. Any quantitative use of (23) must therefore retain either a fixed distance from the kink or another variable, such as the four-point pressure used in WI-021.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The matrix geometry behind (8) is classical in spirit: constant off-diagonal modulus is the standard equiangular-frame/equiangular-lines condition, and Gram-matrix sign/phase descriptions are a routine part of that literature. The sharp spectral envelope itself is WI-020, while the Montgomery--Taylor kernel and its Gram interpretation come from the Alpöge--Furman/stability sources already recorded in `SOURCES.md`.

Targeted searches around equiangular Gram matrices, translation-invariant positive-definite kernels, shift-generated frames, and constant-modulus Fourier-kernel level sets did not locate a theorem asserting this particular packing obstruction for the Montgomery--Taylor kernel or the WI-011 energy strip. Absence of a search hit is not a priority claim.

No external equiangular-frame theorem is load-bearing here. The new deduction is the direct composition

\[
\text{WI-020 equality spectrum}
\Longrightarrow
\text{signed equicorrelation by unit diagonal}
\Longrightarrow
\text{one MT level set must contain all pair differences}
\Longrightarrow
\text{short-distance/long-distance packing contradiction}.
\]

## 8. Boundaries and falsification tests

- The result concerns the **simple-critical Montgomery--Taylor Gram block**. It does not constrain the exceptional indefinite block `Q'` and does not break the doubles/off-line screening equivalence of WI-005--WI-007.
- It does not by itself provide a new explicit global percentage beyond WI-021. Equation (23) gives existence of away-from-kink slack, not an optimized rational `eta` suitable for the final zero-count ledger.
- The upper energy cutoff `A` is used only to put the equality modulus safely below `1/400`. Other energy intervals can be treated by the same method with different level-set bounds.
- Distinctness of the points is natural for simple zeros. Allowing coincident normalized points cannot create equality here: a collision gives an off-diagonal entry `k_MT(0)=1`, already incompatible with `q<1/400`.
- A direct falsification test is finite: exhibit `438` ordered real points and an `E in (438/437,A]` whose MT Gram attains `D=Phi_438(E)`. Sections 2--4 show that this would require all 437 consecutive gaps to exceed `1` while the endpoint distance is below `172`, an immediate contradiction.

## 9. Consequence for `weil_inertia`

WI-020 left open whether the one-spike/flat-complement envelope extremizer might actually be realizable by the ordered Montgomery--Taylor Gram geometry. The answer at the WI-011 scale is now negative.

The abstract spectral conversion therefore has two distinct facts which should not be conflated:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_m(E)\text{ is sharp over all unit-diagonal PSD Grams,}
}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{438}(E)\text{ is unattainable by MT translation Grams for }438/437<E\le A.
}
\]

This confirms, independently of the span-pressure argument of WI-021, that **realizability geometry contains genuine support-one slack beyond the scalar trace--energy envelope**. The remaining task is quantitative rather than existential: convert the explicit entrywise separation, or a sharper ordered-kernel analogue of it, into a useful lower bound for `D-Phi(E)` that survives as the energy approaches the kink and can be assembled globally. That is a more specific target than further sharpening the already-optimal scalar envelope.
