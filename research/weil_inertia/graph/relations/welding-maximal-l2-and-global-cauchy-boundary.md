# Welding boundary: conditioned pair input exists, but every black-box cross-conductor splice exposes a stronger source problem

[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-041-mrt-endpoint-stability-gives-maximal-interval-l2|WI-041]]--[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-060-retained-w-spectrum-wiener-norm-forces-square-function-control|WI-060]] remove moving-endpoint loss, the invalid across-family Cauchy consumer, fixed-polylog coefficient regimes, pure quotient modes and ordinary marginal MRT as the missing conditioned interface. They also show that the `W`-local pair main is `L^2`-concentrated at subpolynomial conductors while fixed-polylog truncation and Wiener/triangle assembly are insufficient.

The review lifecycle for [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-061-mikawa-pair-ap-dispersion-is-the-conditioned-input|WI-061]] and [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-062-lossless-conductor-truncation-forces-superpolylog-diagonal-hilbert-cost|WI-062]] has converged with the canonical findings retained; no current review sidecar is used as evidence here. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-063-lossless-spectral-sparsification-forces-superpolylog-diagonal-cost|WI-063]] and [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-064-mikawa-errors-form-exact-divisor-martingale-but-one-refinement-still-costs-superpolylog|WI-064]] independently establish the lossless diagonal/refinement obstruction and expose exact divisor-martingale structure across conductors.

The first attempted repair remains too expensive even with arbitrary redundancy. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-065-redundant-common-refinements-still-have-superpolylog-mikawa-cost|WI-065]] proves a weighted-frame lower bound showing that splitting each conductor mode among every available common refinement can save at most one logarithm over the diagonal conductor cost; asymptotically lossless retention is still super-polylogarithmic under the Mikawa residue-max square-function budget.

Trying to replace the residue maximum by a residue `L^2` norm changes the arithmetic object rather than only improving bookkeeping. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-066-residue-summed-pair-dispersion-is-a-four-prime-rectangle-problem|WI-066]] opens that norm exactly into the four-prime rectangle `(n,n+h,n+r,n+r+h)` with the conditioning modulus dividing `r`. The subsequently withdrawn WI-067 is not current evidence. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-068-shao-teravainen-only-opens-shift-averaged-rectangle|WI-068]] is its corrected successor and shows that Shao--Teräväinen's finite-complexity theorem controls the free-shift average, not a prescribed fixed-shift slice.

The actual Yang `k`-average does not remove the source lock for free. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-069-free-shift-lift-is-a-power-index-slope-slice|WI-069]] shows that promoting the two physical shifts to independent variables removes the large coefficients from the prime forms but turns the source into a sparse slope slice whose exact selector has power-sized complexity. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-070-base-family-aggregation-crosses-multivariate-polynomial-pattern-gap|WI-070]] then promotes the changing reduced bases themselves and obtains a fixed-coefficient multivariate degree-two polynomial system, but the closest quantitative MTW 2026 theorem is one-parameter on the polynomial side and does not supply the required multivariate, anisotropic, source-weighted asymptotic.

The Yang--Yang candidate remains neither certified nor refuted. The source-backed frontier is now an exact theorem/interface question rather than a generic request for more averaging: obtain a source-faithful collective estimate for the changing-slope, weighted, anisotropic four-prime family (or find a special identity that avoids that full norm) with enough quantitative uniformity to survive the Yang ledger, local-main subtraction, collisions and moving regions. Reusing diagonal Hilbert cost, one common refinement, a free-shift theorem without the slope selector, or a one-variable polynomial theorem after promoting all base variables no longer closes the splice.
