# Welding boundary: positive scalar interfaces fail, signed finite-window structure survives

[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-041-mrt-endpoint-stability-gives-maximal-interval-l2|WI-041]]--[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-070-base-family-aggregation-crosses-multivariate-polynomial-pattern-gap|WI-070]] establish the earlier source-backed boundary. Conditioned pair dispersion exists, but fixed-polylog truncation, Wiener/triangle assembly, lossless diagonal sparsification, common refinements, free-shift averaging and one-variable polynomial-pattern technology do not by themselves deliver the exact Yang weighted changing-slope four-prime splice.

The newest source-agnostic scalar exits are also constrained. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-071-global-free-shift-union-is-lcm-sparse|WI-071]]--[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-073-cartesian-pruning-cannot-densify-long-shift-lcm-support|WI-073]] show that the free-shift physical support is LCM-sparse and remains expensive in fixed finite `L^p` or Cartesian interfaces. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-075-ambient-lcm-projection-needs-weighted-support-theorem|WI-075]]--[[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-078-positive-weight-scalar-energy-remains-maximal-under-mass-preserving-pruning|WI-078]] then correct the scalar-support picture and show that the effective positive scalar LCM projection is not sparse enough to help: its unweighted and source-positive additive energies retain maximal exponent even after mass-preserving pruning.

The actual signed scalar object is more informative. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-079-signed-scalar-large-sieve-needs-ramanujan-operator-cancellation|WI-079]] identifies an exact Ramanujan-sum Toeplitz operator and shows that the standard positive sparse-moduli large-sieve interface cannot exploit the Yang signs. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-080-common-period-ramanujan-projectors-localize-signed-cancellation-to-boundary|WI-080]] shows distinct modulus blocks are orthogonal projectors on a complete common period, so any cross-modulus cancellation is a finite-window boundary effect. [[research/weil_inertia/findings/WI-081-pairwise-lcm-boundary-rank-controls-finite-window-ramanujan-leakage|WI-081]] quantifies that leakage by pairwise LCM boundary rank and proves substantial sharpness: pairwise rank saving is exact in the small-boundary regime and generically disappears for sufficiently separated blocks.

No current review sidecar is needed to support this relation; only surviving canonical findings are used. The territory remains open for three source-sensitive interfaces already isolated by current synthesis: a direct weighted rectangle/incidence theorem on the non-Cartesian source, a **centered signed finite-window Ramanujan operator** estimate using many-modulus coherence beyond positive scalar energy or pairwise rank, or the exact base-aggregated multivariate polynomial representation with its anisotropic boxes and Yang weights. The candidate remains neither certified nor refuted.
