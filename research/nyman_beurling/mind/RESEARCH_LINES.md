## Turn natural-grid phase coercivity into target-bearing height stability beyond the reciprocal-height defect barrier

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-target-aware-gram-geometry-must-carry-absolute-scale`, `MI-003-low-gram-spectrum-is-cheap-target-occupation-is-the-signal` through `MI-020-natural-grid-target-visibility-has-a-reciprocal-height-defect-threshold`.

NB-123--NB-125 produce a genuine source-faithful separator on a geometric grid. Actual zero-power packets obey finite annihilating recurrences, the missing harmonic root gives a positive normalized target-angle margin, and the product structure converts near-perfect capture into a rootwise rank--horizontal-depth--height bill.

NB-126 shows that one fixed base is exactly periodic in ordinate, while NB-127 shows that every fixed finite family of bases has arbitrarily high simultaneous near-aliases. Exact multibase identifiability therefore does not give a global inverse modulus.

NB-128 proves that **growing to the full natural prefix changes the local stability class**. For `Phi_R(T)=(n^(iT))_(2<=n<=R)`, the sup phase distance has a quantitative inverse modulus throughout a height window of diameter `Theta(R)`, with local ordinate resolution `Theta(1/log R)`. The finite-base near-alias obstruction no longer applies inside that window.

NB-129 now supplies the missing amplitude splice, but only at a sharp scale. For `|T|~m`, the amplitude-weighted phase energy on `[m,2m]` is bounded below by a harmonic contribution of order `|A|^2/m` minus the NB-117 excess Dirichlet energy. Consequently a target-angle defect `q_R(P)^(-1)-1=O(1/|T|)` forces phase visibility `gg |A|^2/|T|`.

That reciprocal-height requirement is sharp for the NB-117 energy information alone: an explicit endpoint-preserving matched control with only `Theta(1/m)` excess energy can make the primitive vanish on the whole phase-sensitive block. Fixed positive capture `q_R(P)>=c<1` therefore leaves far too much defect to exploit the NB-128 phase modulus at large height.

The live theorem is now source-specific. To reach the order-one capture regime relevant to NB-115--NB-116, one must use additional zero-power structure—finite recurrence, analyticity, or another nonconcentration principle—to rule out the NB-129 block-erasing control. Merely sharpening the same weighted first-difference estimate cannot cross its exact reciprocal-height barrier.

## Keep global recurrence, local phase coercivity, target angle and amplitude-weighted visibility separate

NB-126--NB-127 establish that fixed finite phase systems can be globally unstable. NB-128 buys an alias-free local window with a growing natural prefix. NB-129 then shows that translating this geometric phase separation into destination-visible mass costs reciprocal-height target accuracy, and that this cost is sharp in the ambient NB-117 sequence model.

The next step is therefore not “add more bases” or “use the phase modulus harder.” It is to prove that genuine zero-power primitives cannot realize the sharp amplitude-hiding control available to arbitrary endpoint-matched sequences.
