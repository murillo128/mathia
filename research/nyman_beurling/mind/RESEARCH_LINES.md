## Control the shallow Ford strip after fixed-depth suppression and one-point signed notching

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-062-the-critical-ford-mark-is-a-growing-moment-observable`, `MI-063-demodulated-ford-energy-is-bandlimited-at-the-window-scale`, `MI-064-signed-scalar-coherence-shrinks-only-by-paying-total-variation`, `MI-065-signed-carrier-cost-must-be-measured-after-quotienting-unresolved-microscopic-dipoles`, `MI-066-resolved-carrier-channels-can-amortize-hilbert-cancellation-at-j-two-thirds`, `MI-067-critical-hardy-zero-sampling-is-logarithmically-displaced-from-the-real-ford-axis`, `MI-068-a-critical-ford-notch-exports-the-scalar-carrier-into-a-depth-ladder`, `MI-069-near-full-positive-carriers-export-the-ford-obstruction-to-a-shrinking-boundary-layer`, `MI-070-a-cheap-signed-boundary-notch-exports-its-conditioning-into-a-ford-scale-side-lobe`.

NB-245--NB-260 fix the Hardy-projected representation, separate coherence cost from representation artifacts, identify the logarithmic displacement of critical zero sampling, and show that sub-near-full resolved scalar rank cannot suppress a fixed positive depth interval at bounded cost. With `N_J=J/A_J~J/log J`, NB-260 forces `K>=N_J^(1-o(1))` for that task.

NB-261 shows that this scale is essentially sufficient for every fixed-depth rectangle in the positive resolved scalar class. Taking `K=J/m` with `1<<m<<A_J`, an equal positive `H`-grid carrier has `K=N_J^(1+o(1))`, vanishing shell-Hilbert cost and tends uniformly to zero for `|x|<=BJ` at every fixed depth fraction `s>=s_0>0`. The obstruction migrates toward the Euler edge, where a matched packet survives at `u=X(1-beta)=log(J/K)+O(1)`.

NB-262 tests the first signed/complex escape at that live depth. One exact shallow notch together with `C_J(0)=1` can indeed be imposed at vanishing minimum shell-Hilbert cost, so positivity cannot be replaced by a Hilbert-cost argument. But every such two-constraint vector pays total variation `Omega(m/u)`, and the minimum-Hilbert interpolant realizes that conditioning as a horizontal side-lobe of size `Theta(m/u)` at Ford distance `x~m=J/K`. At `u=log m+O(1)`, a cone-aligned carrier-locked subpacket of `Theta(u)` points recovers order-one residue mass.

The live scalar question is therefore no longer suppression of a fixed rectangle or one center point. What is the minimum resolved Hilbert/variation/rank cost of suppressing a **horizontal shallow strip** at depth `u~log(J/K)` across the packet scale `x=Theta(J/K)`? The interpolation mesh has to be tied to carrier-locked Ford spacing. If this multi-point cost becomes coercive before `K=Theta(J)`, the signed scalar escape closes; if it remains bounded while the full side-lobe strip can be flattened, that construction must then survive the exact Hardy projection.

## Keep source-band coercivity, positive boundary trace and signed side-lobe conditioning distinct

NB-258 remains a source-only full-real-band obstruction. NB-259 shows one critical complex cell is cheap. NB-260 forces near-full effective depth rank for fixed positive depth intervals. NB-261 shows such rank can suppress those intervals but leaves a shallow positive boundary layer. NB-262 shows a signed carrier can notch the center of that layer cheaply in Hilbert norm, while the minimum-Hilbert solution exports the cost into variation and horizontal Ford-scale amplification.

These are different currencies. A surviving signed mechanism must be tested against the moving depth set by its span and against the horizontal packet scale generated by its own conditioning. Extra horizontal constraints may change the minimum-Hilbert profile, so NB-262 is not a no-go for all signed carriers; it identifies the next exact destination geometry that must be controlled.
