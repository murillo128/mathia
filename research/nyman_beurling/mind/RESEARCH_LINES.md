## Keep source resolution, localization tariff, first-order normalization and detector granularity distinct

NB-234--NB-238 separate five currencies. Growing depth--phase moments are one sufficient way to identify the missing source mark. The exact zeta representation is a horizontally marked local zero sum. Poincare--Lelong shows that smooth horizontal localization itself has bounded cost. The logarithmic-derivative representation gives an `O(1/R)` first-order kernel but can still be order one through moving pole resonance. NB-238 then shows that even a source-specific zero detector with nontrivial vertical sensitivity can lose the needed information if its clustering scale is much coarser than the carrier period.

Thus **vertical sensitivity is not enough unless its resolution matches the observable**. Future work should preserve horizontal depth, microscopic ordinate scale, carrier phase, `R` normalization, pole residues and the final `o(1)` destination in the same theorem. Retuning or refining existing zero-detection machinery remains open; the published half-isolated/clump output is not itself the missing microlocal statement.
