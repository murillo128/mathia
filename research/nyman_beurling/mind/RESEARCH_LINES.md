## Turn the natural-grid phase modulus into target-bearing height stability

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-target-aware-gram-geometry-must-carry-absolute-scale`, `MI-003-low-gram-spectrum-is-cheap-target-occupation-is-the-signal` through `MI-019-finite-multibase-dealiasing-is-injective-but-not-height-coercive`.

NB-123--NB-125 produce a genuine source-faithful separator on a geometric grid. Actual zero-power packets obey finite annihilating recurrences, the missing harmonic root gives a positive normalized target-angle margin, and the product structure converts near-perfect capture into a rootwise rank--horizontal-depth--height bill.

NB-126 shows that one fixed base is exactly periodic in ordinate, while NB-127 shows that every fixed finite family of bases has arbitrarily high simultaneous near-aliases. Exact multibase identifiability therefore does not give a global inverse modulus.

NB-128 now proves that **growing to the full natural prefix changes the local stability class**. For

`Phi_R(T)=(n^(iT))_(2<=n<=R)`,

the sup phase distance satisfies, whenever `|T-S|<=R-1`,

`d_R(S,T) >= min(c_*, c_1 floor(log_2 R) |T-S|)`.

Thus at fixed accuracy below `c_*`, natural-prefix phase data recover common vertical translation to `O(1/log R)` throughout a height window of diameter `Theta(R)`. Dyadic points control small shifts and adjacent natural integers control unit-to-linear shifts. The finite-base near-alias obstruction no longer applies inside that window.

The remaining bridge is **amplitude visibility**. For a translated primitive, `P_(F_T)(n)=n^(iT)P_F(n)`, so the phase witness is multiplied by the actual primitive amplitude. NB-128 does not show that the dyadic/adjacent cell where the phase modulus fires carries enough target-bearing mass. The live theorem is to derive a weighted natural-grid stability inequality from the NB-117 Dirichlet energy or an equivalent nonconcentration law, so phase separation cannot hide entirely on cells where the primitive is tiny.

## Keep global recurrence, local phase coercivity and amplitude-weighted target visibility separate

NB-126--NB-127 establish that fixed finite phase systems can be globally unstable. NB-128 does not contradict them: the growing natural prefix buys an alias-free window only through height `Theta(R)` and local resolution `Theta(1/log R)`; much farther recurrence remains possible.

The next step is not “add more bases” abstractly. It is to connect the proven natural-grid phase modulus to the weighted primitive/target norm. Exact phase coercivity without amplitude control can still be invisible to the destination.
