NB-181 closes the bounded-Hilbert repair. Retaining the true amplitudes inside the natural feature Hilbert space gives a nuclear bound forcing RMS Hilbert acquisition norm `Omega(sqrt N)` for an `N x N` Hadamard-scale response. The physical scalar Euler readout is not a counterexample because its phase vector is not `ell^2`; its convergence uses absolute Dirichlet summability.

NB-182 now prices that exact non-Hilbert escape. After charging each Euler character by its capped-Poisson Lipschitz cost, the absolute Euler exposure

`W(a)=sum Lambda(n)n^(-1-a) max{2,a log n}`

satisfies `W(a) asymp 1/a`. Dense rank-`N` phase coding still requires `G=Omega(sqrt N)`: the Wiener/Dirichlet topology changes the currency but does not make coherent phase acquisition free.

The known high-ordinate zeta bound supplies gain `kappa_T asymp 1/(a H_T)`, where `H_T=min{1/a,log T/log log T}`. Matching the full generic packing dimension `N asymp 1/a` at fixed-fraction strength requires `a H_T^2=O(1)`, hence in the one-line-capped regime `a lesssim (log log T/log T)^2`. Above that square crossover a successful full-rank code needs height-specific arithmetic cancellation stronger than the current uniform bound, and that extra gain must be measured rather than hidden in the acquisition choice.

The post-NB-182 target is therefore precise: either prove a stronger **height-selective source cancellation theorem** that yields enough charged Wiener gain on a suitably rich set of heights, or prove that no such source-selective gain can beat the matched Poisson-packing control at the dimension required by the Nyman target. Merely changing from bounded Hilbert duality to the genuine scalar Euler functional no longer leaves an unpriced escape.

## Keep target conditioning and arithmetic-source persistence separate

NB-168--NB-182 are source-side persistence/resource theorems. NB-177 shows one canonical square-summed witness can be too compact; NB-178--NB-180 show why enlarging and renormalizing the dual can manufacture nonselective generic codes; NB-181 prices true amplitudes in bounded Hilbert geometry; NB-182 prices the actual non-Hilbert Euler scalar family in Wiener/Fourier--Stieltjes geometry.

None of these results turns a density obstruction into pointwise exclusion at a selected sparse sequence, removes the ultra-thin regime, constructs a source-selective arithmetic phase code at bounded charged acquisition cost, or by itself connects surviving source response back to global Nyman approximation. The next certificate must therefore exploit arithmetic cancellation that survives a source-faithful acquisition budget and a matched-control comparison, rather than obtaining apparent complexity by changing or renormalizing the witness class.
