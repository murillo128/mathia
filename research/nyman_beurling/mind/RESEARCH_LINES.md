## Keep aggregate Ford coverage separate from Hardy coercivity

NB-264--NB-272 show that large aggregate Ford response can coexist with a vanishing positive Hardy quadratic signal. Resolved shell bands can carry order-one point residue while their projected `H^2` energy leaks with rank, and positive band-diagonal repairs need rank-growing strength to restore a uniform anchored floor. Target pairings change the projection defect and anchor mismatch but do not secretly strengthen the source-source Gram. The live destination-side fork remains: either the canonical target quantities themselves stay order one in the moving regime, or a new exact source component supplies the missing rank-growing Gram strength.

## Price first-free boundary data through future sample-null innovations

NB-276--NB-283 move the source-native divisor route away from universal zero sampling. The Ford current is a singular Mellin derivative of the canonical Möbius Nyman source, but finite differentiated sources are entire and cannot carry the interior divisor current across a zero. Moving the pole into a legal dual functional reduces all source-independent point and multiplicity-jet data to the finite Burnol/Blaschke defect. The first source-dependent jet can be interpolated with sparse finite support, so support rank alone is not an obstruction.

NB-284--NB-286 then isolate the genuine finite-section penalty. For a fixed zero packet the first-free synthesis cost converges to the full-space cost; any persistent obstruction must be a **moving-packet** phenomenon. The finite-section excess splits into representer-tail and sample-repair terms and telescopes through target-aligned rank-one leverage increments. Generic Gram ill-conditioning is irrelevant unless the new source direction is simultaneously large in the inverse sample metric and aligned with the prescribed first-free data.

NB-287 gives the intrinsic source-space form of the same quantity. After the first surjective section, each genuinely new Dirichlet direction has one canonical neutralized innovation in the exact sampling nullspace; these innovations are orthonormal and complete that nullspace. The finite-section excess is exactly the Parseval tail of the current minimum-norm interpolant on the **future sample-null innovations**. The two factors previously described as leverage and target alignment are therefore one squared source-space angle after sample neutralization.

The decisive moving-packet question is now concrete: for zero packets `Z_X` and natural sections `M_X`, does the minimum-norm first-free interpolant retain a nonvanishing amount of Parseval mass in future neutralized Dirichlet innovations, or does that tail vanish? A lower bound would provide a genuine finite arithmetic-section obstruction; an upper bound would close the first-free repair route and force movement to higher/global data or a nonlinear boundary mechanism. Neither fixed-packet conditioning, universal zero geometry, support count nor determinant growth can substitute for this target-aware null-innovation tail.
