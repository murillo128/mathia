NB-261--NB-263 move the scalar problem to the shallow depth `u~log(J/K)` and show a real quantifier split: one cheapest notch exports a Ford-scale side-lobe, but every positive-depth strip chosen before the eventual rank can be flattened at vanishing resolved Hilbert cost if conditioning and later rank are free.

NB-264 locates the opposite side of that split. With bounded resolved `ell^2` cost, the Euler anchor is exactly reconstructible from carrier-locked shallow samples, forcing aggregate response `Omega(m)` inside every fixed-power rank-coupled radius `B_K=K^delta`. Hence uniform suppression at bounded cost cannot cover polynomial horizontal scale in `K`; any successful bounded-cost suppressing sequence must have `B_K=K^(o(1))`.

The live scalar question is now the **subpolynomial rank-coupled transition**. How fast can `B_K` grow while bounded Hilbert cost, acceptable total variation/conditioning and exact Euler normalization still allow suppression at the live depth? Can the reconstruction tail be quantified sharply enough to locate the threshold, and does the resulting carrier-locked obstruction survive the exact Hardy projection rather than only matched compatibility controls?

## Keep preassigned strips, rank-coupled coverage, variation and actual-zero claims distinct

NB-263 and NB-264 are complementary, not contradictory. The former chooses the strip before rank and can export conditioning; the latter ties coverage to eventual rank and assumes bounded resolved Hilbert cost. Their gap is subpolynomial.

A future theorem must therefore state the order of quantifiers and the bounded resource. Matched carrier-locked packets remain adversarial controls, not assertions about actual zero locations. Total variation, reconstruction conditioning and the final Hardy/Nyman distance remain separate from the resolved `ell^2` obstruction.
