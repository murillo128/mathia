# MI-033 — Finite polynomial-height Gamma balancing preserves target charge but cannot select it

**Evidence level:** proved for the explicit three-height construction by [NB-157](../../findings/NB-157-polynomial-depth-gamma-balancing-cancels-macroscopic-completed-cost.md)--[NB-158](../../findings/NB-158-polynomial-depth-gamma-balancing-keeps-target-charge-but-imports-a-routine-adverse-reservoir.md), and extended to every fixed finite polynomially separated zero-mass atomic sampler with bounded target conditioning by [NB-159](../../findings/NB-159-finite-polynomial-height-zero-mass-samplers-force-a-routine-adverse-reservoir.md).

The leading macroscopic completed-Gamma cost is a signed log-height moment, so finite signed combinations of polynomially separated sampling heights can cancel that background while keeping a nondegenerate target lobe near height `G`. NB-158 showed this explicitly for three heights with bounded total variation.

NB-159 shows that adding finitely many heights or cancelling finitely many further Gamma/Stirling moments does not repair selection. For any fixed number of polynomial scales `G^theta_j`, zero total mass and a positive, well-conditioned top atom force at least one lower negative coefficient of size comparable to the target coefficient. Polynomial separation makes that atom dominate a fixed-width lobe around its own height, while the explicit Riemann--von Mangoldt remainder supplies `Omega(log G)` actual zeta zeros there. The sampler therefore exports `Omega(c_top log G)` adverse charge into a routine zero reservoir.

The obstruction is finite-dimensional and structural. It does not depend on which finite list of universal moments is cancelled: zero mass supplies the sign debt, fixed polynomial separation isolates a negative lobe, and ordinary zero density populates it. **Finite moment cancellation can make a sampler balanced and target-conditioned without making it selective.**

The surviving signed route must spend a genuinely different resource: let the number of heights grow, let scale separations collapse into overlapping or continuous vertical geometry, move the adverse scale below every fixed positive power, accept unbounded conditioning together with useful prime-side cancellation, or couple the sampler to a source-specific Nyman/von-Mangoldt observable before the zero side separates into signed lobes. These are changes of architecture, not higher-order versions of the same finite polynomial balancing scheme.

**Boundary.** The theorem does not classify growing-cardinality or non-atomic samplers, collapsing exponents, subpolynomial lower scales, or source-coupled signed prime cancellation. It is a method boundary, not a zero-spacing theorem or RH estimate.
