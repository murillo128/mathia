# MI-086 — Adjacent cancellation forces near-quadratic uniform primitive complexity

**Evidence level:** supported by the upper/lower primitive estimates in NB-315 and [NB-316](../../findings/NB-316-adjacent-cancellation-forces-near-quadratic-centered-shell-primitives.md).

The centered-shell primitive problem can be summarized by the amplification

`Pi_N = sup_(u != 0) ||G_u||_infinity / ||u||_2`,

where `G_u` is the primitive generated by the centered shell combination. NB-315 gives the general upper bound `Pi_N <= C N^(5/2)`. NB-316 supplies a concrete adjacent-cancellation witness that nearly reaches quadratic growth from below.

For

`u_N = g_N - ((N-1)/N) g_(N-1)`,

the centered primitive reaches `floor(N^2/4)/(2N)`, while the source norm is at most of order `sqrt(log N)/N`. Consequently

`Pi_N >= c N^2/sqrt(log N)`.

Thus the large primitive amplification is not merely an artifact of a loose global estimate. Even the most local adjacent-shell cancellation can convert a very small centered source vector into a primitive whose uniform size is almost quadratic in `N`. No uniform primitive theorem with polynomial exponent strictly below `2` can hold for this class.

The remaining gap is correspondingly narrow in exponent: the presently justified window lies between `N^2/sqrt(log N)` and `N^(5/2)`. This makes near-quadratic primitive complexity a structural constraint on any argument that tries to control the translated Nyman--Beurling geometry solely through a uniform primitive norm. Improving the upper bound may still matter, but it cannot change that basic scale by a full power of `N`.

**Boundary.** This is a method-side amplification statement. It does not give a matching lower bound for the Nyman--Beurling approximation error, the mixing operator, or the target distance, and it does not identify the sharp exponent inside the remaining `2` to `5/2` window.
