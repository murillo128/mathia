# MI-003 — Low Gram spectrum is cheap; target occupation is the signal

**Evidence level:** exact canonical Rayleigh bound plus universal target-poor controls through NB-026

## Core intuition

Ill-conditioning of the Nyman--Beurling Gram matrix is not intrinsically arithmetic. The dictionary itself contains many cheap adjacent-cancellation directions with low Rayleigh quotient and almost no coupling to the target.

What can carry arithmetic meaning is not a small eigenvalue but the placement of the **canonical target-selected coefficient vector** inside or outside those universal low-energy sectors.

## Strongest justified principle

NB-024 shows that if the canonical approximation is good, its normalized coefficient vector has Rayleigh quotient at most the inverse logarithmic scale. That looks promising in isolation. NB-025 constructs a universal adjacent-generator mode with an even smaller low-energy scale but negligible target loading. NB-026 amplifies the control to a macroscopic-dimensional target-poor low-Rayleigh sector.

Therefore spectral abundance cannot certify the desired approximation. A useful theorem must be target-aware and distinguish the canonical vector from low modes generated by local dictionary redundancy.

## Program consequence

Replace eigenvalue counting by projection geometry. Measure the canonical coefficient vector's mass, angle, or spectral distribution relative to the target-poor universal sector, and demand an absolute target-coupling statement in the same normalization as the Nyman distance.

## Counterevidence / boundary

The constructed universal sector need not exhaust all low-energy directions, and the canonical vector may still have a distinguished component not captured by adjacent cancellations. The findings do not prove that no spectral method can work; they specify what a nontrivial spectral method must measure.

## Epistemic status

**Exact matched control showing that low Gram energy is generically abundant and target-poor, while good approximation selects a particular low-Rayleigh vector; their relative geometry remains open.**

## Falsification criterion

Show that the universal adjacent sector necessarily carries the same target loading as the canonical vector at the relevant scale, or prove that a target-independent spectral statistic already determines the Nyman distance despite NB-025--NB-026.
