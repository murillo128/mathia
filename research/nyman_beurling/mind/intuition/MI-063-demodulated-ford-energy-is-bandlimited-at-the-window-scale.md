# MI-063 — Ford-scale observability is one hard box, but the logarithmic-derivative field must be paired before squaring

**Evidence level:** exact harmonic/source-specific synthesis from [NB-246](../../findings/NB-246-demodulation-bandlimits-ford-pair-energy-to-the-window-scale.md), [NB-247](../../findings/NB-247-bellotti-population-turns-hard-ford-window-energy-into-pointwise-control.md), and the method boundary [NB-248](../../findings/NB-248-squaring-the-ford-edge-logarithmic-derivative-hits-the-pole-critical-l2-endpoint.md), interpreted against NB-230, NB-237, NB-243 and NB-245. The required local mean is sufficient, not known for zeta zeros.

Freeze the Ford parameters at a selected center and demodulate the common carrier. NB-246 shows that the moving-center current has bandwidth `O(H)` while its zero-pair expansion retains the fine phase `e^{iX(gamma_j-gamma_k)}` and horizontal depth marks. NB-247 adds Bellotti population regularity: the demodulated current is uniformly Lipschitz on the scaled center variable, so a large selected value persists across a fixed fraction of a Ford window.

Consequently one literal hard-window energy `I_A(t_0)=H int_{t_0-A/H}^{t_0+A/H}|R_{t_0}(s)|^2 ds` is enough: a uniform `o(1)` bound implies uniform pointwise `o(1)` control. The matched coherent packet still has nonvanishing hard-box energy, so this reformulation does not average away the adversary.

NB-248 closes the most direct first-order proof of that hard-window theorem. In the Ford-scaled two-dimensional edge coordinates, each zero gives a Cauchy pole in `R^{-1} zeta'/zeta`. The pole is locally `L^p` exactly for `p<2`; at `p=2` it has a logarithmic divergence. With a cutoff `epsilon`, a simple zero contributes `asymp J^{-1} log(1/epsilon)`, and a carrier-compatible packet of `Theta(J)` zeros leaves an order-one logarithmic coefficient. Fixed subcritical `p<2` also sums to an order-one absolute population budget.

Thus the operation order is load-bearing: **pair the divisor first, then square the finite current**. Squaring or taking absolute values of the local logarithmic-derivative field before the signed area pairing destroys the cancellation interface and hits the pole-critical endpoint. The remaining hard-window theorem must retain that signed pairing or pass directly to the bilinear/two-copy depth-marked carrier energy.

**Boundary.** NB-248 does not disprove the hard-window second moment. It rules out a black-box local `L^2` estimate for the first-order logarithmic-derivative field and shows why subcritical Hölder does not produce the missing decay.
