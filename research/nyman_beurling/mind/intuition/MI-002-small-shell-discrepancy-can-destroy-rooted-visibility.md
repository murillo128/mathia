# MI-002 — Small shell discrepancy compresses the target across all deeper rooted dilations

**Evidence level:** exact shell identities and Burnol-scale consequence through NB-023

## Core intuition

Shell discrepancy plays two opposite roles. It can look like improved mixing, but once the rooted normal equation is imposed, making that discrepancy small forces the residual to become invisible when projected back into the old section. The newer identities show that this is an all-depth phenomenon, not merely a finite rooted-band effect.

## Strongest justified principle

NB-019 ties effective shell mixing to the actual target-selected coefficient budget. NB-020 gives that budget a Möbius-sized lower core. The corrected NB-021 shows that sublinear discrepancy drives the rooted mixed scalar to zero for every deeper dilation covered by the construction. NB-022 makes the compression quantitative through shell Fourier inversion and Möbius inversion: the high-index section gain is bounded directly by the discrepancy.

NB-023 then reverses the lesson. Combining this compression mechanism with Burnol's distance scale forces the canonical discrepancy to become almost linear on the relevant approximation scale. Thus the system cannot simultaneously enjoy strong approximation and cheap shell mixing.

## Program consequence

Treat near-linear discrepancy as positive source information to be exploited, not merely as a nuisance lower bound. Look for an identity turning that forced roughness into target-visible multiplicative energy or a contradiction with the canonical coefficient geometry.

## Counterevidence / boundary

A large discrepancy does not by itself prove approximation or RH. The argument only says that a successful approximant cannot live in the small-discrepancy compression regime. Unrooted or differently organized observations may also use the same coefficients in another way.

## Epistemic status

**Exact all-depth rooted compression under small discrepancy, plus a strong lower bound forcing near-linear discrepancy for good canonical approximation; the conversion of that roughness into a positive theorem remains open.**

## Falsification criterion

Produce good canonical approximants with discrepancy inside the ruled-out sublinear regime under the same hypotheses, or show that the shell inversion bound does not control the rooted section as stated.
