# NB-048 — a dilated smaller-section projection approximates the unique weighted-tail repair

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + RECURSIVE-TARGET-REPAIR + SMALLER-SECTION-TARGET-ORACLE + EXACT-DISTANCE-LOSS-IDENTITY + N-UNIFORM-POLYNOMIAL-CONDITIONING + SQUARE-ROOT-ORACLE-REDUCTION + METHOD-BOUNDARY`. `NB-039` shows that the weighted nuisance tail contains a dilated copy of a smaller canonical Nyman section, while `NB-040` shows that exact zero-loss repair of the weighted skeleton requires the unique target-selected line generated by the full weighted-tail projection. These two facts combine more strongly than was previously recorded: dilating the **smaller best approximant itself** gives an explicit vector in the weighted tail whose distance from the unique repair vector is exactly the slack in the recursive `NB-039` target-loss bound.

Use the canonical finite section

\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}(g_2,\ldots,g_N),
\qquad
p_N=P_{V_N}e,
\qquad
d_N=\|e-p_N\|,
\tag{1}
\]

and, for integers `2<=M<N`, the weighted tail and retained skeleton

\[
\widehat W_{M,N}
=\operatorname{span}\bigl(ng_n-(n+1)g_{n+1}:M\le n<N\bigr),
\tag{2}
\]

\[
\widehat K_{M,N}
=V_N\cap\widehat W_{M,N}^{\perp},
\qquad
\widehat\kappa_{M,N}
=\operatorname{dist}(e,\widehat K_{M,N}).
\tag{3}
\]

Put

\[
K=\left\lfloor\frac NM\right\rfloor,
\qquad
v_K=P_{V_K}e,
\tag{4}
\]

and let `A_M` be the Bagchi dilation isometry used in `NB-039`, satisfying

\[
A_Mg_j
=\sqrt M\left(g_{Mj}-j^{-1}g_M\right),
\qquad
A_Me=\sqrt M\,\mathbf1_{(0,1/M]}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the recursively supplied tail vector

\[
\boxed{
u_{M,N}:=\frac1{\sqrt M}A_Mv_K.}
\tag{6}
\]

`NB-039` gives `A_MV_K\subseteq\widehat W_{M,N}`, hence `u_{M,N}\in\widehat W_{M,N}`. If

\[
w_{M,N}:=P_{\widehat W_{M,N}}e,
\qquad
L_{M,N}:=\|w_{M,N}\|^2,
\tag{7}
\]

is the unique exact repair vector from `NB-040`, then

\[
\boxed{
\|w_{M,N}-u_{M,N}\|^2
=
L_{M,N}-\frac{1-d_K^2}{M}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
\|u_{M,N}\|^2
=\langle e,u_{M,N}\rangle
=\frac{1-d_K^2}{M},
}
\tag{9}
\]

so `u_{M,N}` is itself exactly the orthogonal projection of the target onto its one-dimensional span.

Consequently the `M`-dimensional recursively repaired skeleton

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde K_{M,N}
:=
\widehat K_{M,N}\oplus\operatorname{span}(u_{M,N})
}
\tag{10}
\]

has squared target distance

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde d_{M,N}^2
:=\operatorname{dist}(e,\widetilde K_{M,N})^2
=d_N^2+\|w_{M,N}-u_{M,N}\|^2.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde d_{M,N}^2-d_N^2
=
L_{M,N}-\frac{1-d_K^2}{M},
\qquad
0\le
\widetilde d_{M,N}^2-d_N^2
\le\frac{d_K^2}{M}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus the exact target-selected repair of `NB-040` can be replaced by a repair obtained from the best projection in the **smaller section `V_K`**, with a completely explicit additive loss. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d_K^2}{M d_N^2}\longrightarrow0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\widetilde d_{M,N}^2}{d_N^2}\longrightarrow1.
}
\tag{13}
\]

The ratio in (13) is the same source-adaptive scale that appeared in `NB-039` only as a visibility condition for one upper-section defect. Here it controls an actual target-distance approximation.

## 1. The recursive loss sandwich is a Pythagorean repair identity

Let

\[
e_T:=\mathbf1_{(0,1/M]},
\qquad e_E:=e-e_T.
\tag{14}
\]

Every vector in `\widehat W_{M,N}` vanishes on `(1/M,1]`, so

\[
w_{M,N}=P_{\widehat W_{M,N}}e_T.
\tag{15}
\]

`NB-039` already constructs `u_{M,N}` and proves

\[
\|e_T-u_{M,N}\|
=\frac{d_K}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{16}
\]

Because `e_T-w_{M,N}` is orthogonal to the whole weighted tail while both `u_{M,N}` and `w_{M,N}` belong to that tail,

\[
\|e_T-u_{M,N}\|^2
=
\|e_T-w_{M,N}\|^2
+
\|w_{M,N}-u_{M,N}\|^2.
\tag{17}
\]

The first term is

\[
\|e_T-w_{M,N}\|^2
=
\|e_T\|^2-L_{M,N}
=
\frac1M-L_{M,N}.
\tag{18}
\]

Substituting (16) and (18) into (17) proves (8). Thus the lower bound from `NB-039`,

\[
L_{M,N}\ge\frac{1-d_K^2}{M},
\tag{19}
\]

has a geometric remainder: its slack is **exactly** the squared error made by replacing the full weighted-tail target projection with the dilated smaller-section projection.

The normalization (9) is equally exact. Since `A_M` is an isometry and `v_K=P_{V_K}e`,

\[
\|u_{M,N}\|^2
=
\frac1M\|v_K\|^2
=
\frac{1-d_K^2}{M}.
\tag{20}
\]

Also `u_{M,N}` is supported on `(0,1/M]`, and `e_T=M^{-1/2}A_Me`, so

\[
\langle e,u_{M,N}\rangle
=
\langle e_T,u_{M,N}\rangle
=
\frac1M\langle e,v_K\rangle
=
\frac1M\|v_K\|^2.
\tag{21}
\]

This proves (9) and therefore

\[
P_{\operatorname{span}(u_{M,N})}e=u_{M,N}.
\tag{22}
\]

No asymptotic estimate, Möbius cancellation, or zero-free information is used in (8)--(22).

## 2. The approximate repair preserves the conditioning order of the exact repair

Since `\widehat K_{M,N}\perp\widehat W_{M,N}` and `u_{M,N}\in\widehat W_{M,N}`, the sum in (10) is orthogonal. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde d_{M,N}^2
&=
1-\|P_{\widehat K_{M,N}}e\|^2-\|u_{M,N}\|^2\\
&=
\widehat\kappa_{M,N}^2-\frac{1-d_K^2}{M}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

`NB-039` gives

\[
\widehat\kappa_{M,N}^2
=d_N^2+L_{M,N},
\tag{24}
\]

so (23) proves the exact identity in (11)--(12). The upper bound in (12) follows from `L_{M,N}\le1/M`.

For `K>=2`, normalize the recursive repair by

\[
\widetilde b_{M,N}
:=
\frac{u_{M,N}}{\|u_{M,N}\|^2}.
\tag{25}
\]

Then

\[
\langle e,\widetilde b_{M,N}\rangle=1,
\qquad
\|\widetilde b_{M,N}\|^2
=
\frac{M}{1-d_K^2}.
\tag{26}
\]

Monotonicity of `d_K` and the exact value `1-d_2^2=\log2` used in `NB-039` imply

\[
\boxed{
M\le
\|\widetilde b_{M,N}\|^2
\le\frac{M}{\log2}.
}
\tag{27}
\]

The `NB-034` retained basis `(\widehat k_2,\ldots,\widehat k_M)` is orthogonal to the whole weighted tail, hence to `\widetilde b_{M,N}`. Its Gram matrix obeys

\[
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat H_{M,N})
\ge\frac1{14M^2H_M^2},
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(\widehat H_{M,N})
\le2(H_M-1).
\tag{28}
\]

Therefore the block basis

\[
(\widehat k_2,\ldots,\widehat k_M,\widetilde b_{M,N})
\tag{29}
\]

has

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cond}_2
\le
14M^2H_M^2
\max\left\{2(H_M-1),\frac{M}{\log2}\right\}
=O(M^3(\log M)^2),
}
\tag{30}
\]

the same asymptotic conditioning order as the exact target-oracular repair in `NB-040`. Replacing the full repair vector by the recursively supplied one therefore costs target accuracy, not an additional conditioning instability.

## 3. A square-root auxiliary section is unconditionally enough for relative target-distance recovery

The tradeoff becomes concrete by balancing the retained dimension and the auxiliary section. Let

\[
M_N=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor,
\qquad
K_N=\left\lfloor\frac{N}{M_N}\right\rfloor.
\tag{31}
\]

Then `M_N,K_N=Theta(sqrt(N))`, and (12) gives the unconditional additive estimate

\[
0\le
\widetilde d_{M_N,N}^2-d_N^2
\le
\frac{d_{K_N}^2}{M_N}
\le
\frac1{M_N}
=O(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{32}
\]

Burnol's unconditional lower bound says

\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}d_N^2\log N
\ge\mathfrak B_\zeta>0.
\tag{33}
\]

Hence for every fixed `0<eta<\mathfrak B_\zeta`, all sufficiently large `N` satisfy `d_N^2>=eta/log N`, and therefore

\[
0\le
\frac{\widetilde d_{M_N,N}^2}{d_N^2}-1
\le
\frac{\log N}{\eta M_N}
=O_\eta\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\sqrt N}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus a weighted skeleton of dimension `Theta(sqrt(N))` can recover the full finite target distance multiplicatively while its **single repair direction** is supplied by an exact best projection in another section of only `Theta(sqrt(N))` generators. The exact `NB-040` repair needed `P_{\widehat W_{M,N}}e`, which is tied to the full `N`-section target geometry; the recursive replacement lowers the target-oracle section size from `N` to `K=floor(N/M)` at a quantitatively controlled price.

More generally, (13) permits highly asymmetric choices whenever the Nyman distances at `N` and `floor(N/M)` are comparable enough. This is stronger than the unconditional balanced corollary, but it remains a conditional statement about the ratio of two finite distances, not a new estimate for either distance.

## 4. Adversarial boundary

This result does **not** make the weighted skeleton fully source-constructible. The vector `u_{M,N}` still contains the smaller target projection `P_{V_K}e`; the target oracle has been reduced recursively, not eliminated. Moreover the retained vectors

\[
\widehat k_j=(I-P_{\widehat W_{M,N}})g_j
\tag{35}
\]

still contain the weighted-tail projector. Nothing here proves that the entire reduced Gram matrix or the full skeleton can be formed with `Theta(sqrt(N))` arithmetic work. The result is a reduction in the size of the **target-selected repair oracle**, not a computational-complexity theorem.

Nor does (34) give an upper bound on `d_N`, prove `d_N\to0`, or imply RH. Burnol is used only to compare the explicit additive repair loss with the already known unconditional lower scale of the finite distance. The exact identities (8)--(12) themselves are purely finite Hilbert geometry plus the classical dilation relation.

The recursive replacement need not equal the true repair. Equality holds exactly when

\[
L_{M,N}=\frac{1-d_K^2}{M},
\tag{36}
\]

that is, when the specific embedded copy `A_MV_K` already captures the full weighted-tail target projection. `NB-039` proves only the inclusion `A_MV_K\subseteq\widehat W_{M,N}`, not equality, so no self-similar decomposition of the entire weighted tail is being asserted.

Finally, (13) does not by itself justify iterating the construction down through several scales. Each recursive level would introduce its own approximation error and retained-space geometry; an iteration theorem requires separate error propagation and compatibility estimates.

## 5. Prior art and research consequence

The integer-dilation semigroup and identity (5) are classical Nyman--Beurling/Báez--Duarte machinery, already anchored through Báez-Duarte and Bagchi in `SOURCES.md`. The orthogonal-projection argument in (17)--(23) is elementary Hilbert geometry. No novelty is claimed for either ingredient separately.

A targeted literature audit covered the Nyman--Beurling dilation/semigroup formulation, finite-section projection criteria, and recent multiscale Gram-compressibility work. The accessible statements did not expose the specific replacement

\[
P_{\widehat W_{M,N}}e
\quad\leadsto\quad
M^{-1/2}A_M P_{V_{\lfloor N/M\rfloor}}e
\tag{37}
\]

with the exact error identity (8), the repaired-distance formula (11), or the square-root target-oracle reduction (34). Search absence is not a priority claim. No new external theorem is load-bearing, so `SOURCES.md` needs no additional dependency.

The accepted clue `CLUE-weighted-skeleton-target-data-without-full-section-oracle` is therefore narrowed but not resolved. `NB-040` made the exact one-dimensional repair look irreducibly tied to the full target projection. The present result shows that the repair line has a controlled **recursive surrogate** coming from a smaller Nyman section, and that an auxiliary section of square-root size already suffices for unconditional relative distance fidelity. What remains unresolved is whether the smaller projection and the retained weighted-skeleton geometry can themselves be replaced by genuinely source-only data without reconstructing the finite projection problem at another scale.
