# NB-096 — canonical deflation cancels raw Cauchy crowding volume

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + ACTUAL-NYMAN-SOURCE + FINITE-DISTINCT-OFF-CRITICAL-DIVISOR + GROWING-DEPTH + CAUCHY-VOLUME-CANCELLATION + SHARP-LEVERAGE-DETERMINANT`.

`NB-095` proves that a fixed finite set of pairwise distinct right-half zeta zeros contributes the additive growing-depth exponent

\[
2A_F=2\sum_{\rho\in F}\left(\Re\rho-\frac12\right)
\]

to the finite Nyman correlation-volume repair. Its proof leaves the finite-set constant hidden inside two pieces: the Cauchy/model-space Gram of the raw zero states and the triangular change of coordinates generated by successive canonical Blaschke deflation. Those pieces are not independent. Their determinants cancel **exactly**.

For the notation of `NB-095`, let

\[
\lambda_r=\rho_r-\frac12,
\qquad
a_r=\Re\lambda_r>0,
\qquad
A_F=\sum_{r=1}^d a_r,
\]

and let

\[
G_F(\infty)_{rs}
=\frac{2\sqrt{a_ra_s}}{\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s}}
\]

be the half-line Gram of the normalized exponential states. Let `T_F` be the exact triangular matrix from `NB-095` satisfying

\[
S_R=U_RT_F,
\qquad
(T_F)_{kk}=B_{k-1}(\lambda_k)^{-1},
\]

where

\[
B_{k-1}=\prod_{r<k}b_r,
\qquad
b_r(z)=\frac{z-\lambda_r}{z+\overline{\lambda_r}}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
|\det T_F|^2\det G_F(\infty)=1.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
\delta_{rs}
:=
\left|\frac{\lambda_r-\lambda_s}
{\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s}}\right|
\qquad(r<s),
\]

then

\[
\det G_F(\infty)
=\prod_{r<s}\delta_{rs}^2,
\qquad
|\det T_F|^2
=\prod_{r<s}\delta_{rs}^{-2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the apparent determinant penalty produced when zero states crowd in the raw Cauchy Gram is exactly the determinant gain introduced by the canonical sequential deflation coordinates. Pairwise crowding can still damage **spectral conditioning**, but it does not impose any residual multiplicative loss on the limiting interacting-state volume.

## 1. Exact Cauchy determinant of the raw zero states

The matrix `G_F(infinity)` is the Gram matrix on `L^2(0,infinity)` of

\[
f_r(t)=\sqrt{2a_r}\,e^{-\lambda_rt}.
\]

Each `f_r` has norm one. The Cauchy determinant identity applied to

\[
C_{rs}=\frac1{\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s}}
\]

gives

\[
\det C
=
\frac{
\prod_{r<s}(\lambda_s-\lambda_r)
\prod_{r<s}(\overline{\lambda_s}-\overline{\lambda_r})
}{
\prod_{r,s}(\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s})
}.
\]

Multiplying row `r` and column `r` by `sqrt(2a_r)` and separating the diagonal terms

\[
\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_r}=2a_r
\]

therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\det G_F(\infty)
=
\prod_{r<s}
\frac{|\lambda_r-\lambda_s|^2}
{|\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s}|^2}
=
\prod_{r<s}\delta_{rs}^2.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This formula makes the raw conditioning issue visible at volume level: if two selected zeros approach one another, the raw Gram determinant tends to zero through the corresponding pseudo-hyperbolic separation factor.

## 2. The canonical triangular deflation contributes the reciprocal product

`NB-095` derives the exact finite-state interaction law

\[
S_R=U_RT_F
\]

with `T_F` triangular and diagonal

\[
(T_F)_{kk}=B_{k-1}(\lambda_k)^{-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
|\det T_F|^2
=
\prod_{k=1}^d |B_{k-1}(\lambda_k)|^{-2}.
\]

For every `r<k`,

\[
|b_r(\lambda_k)|
=
\left|\frac{\lambda_k-\lambda_r}
{\lambda_k+\overline{\lambda_r}}\right|
=\delta_{rk}.
\]

Every unordered pair occurs exactly once, so

\[
\boxed{
|\det T_F|^2
=
\prod_{r<k}\delta_{rk}^{-2}
=
\det G_F(\infty)^{-1}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Combining `(3)` and `(4)` proves `(1)`. Although `T_F` itself depends on the chosen deflation order, the modulus of its determinant does not. The cancellation is therefore intrinsic to the selected finite divisor, not an artifact of how its factors are enumerated.

This identity is deliberately only a **volume statement**. It does not imply

\[
T_F^*G_F(\infty)T_F=I,
\]

and no such matrix identity is used here. A matrix may have determinant one while being arbitrarily ill-conditioned. The distinction matters for any attempt to let `|F|` grow.

## 3. The interacting leverage determinant has a canonical leading coefficient

Retain the notation of `NB-095`. For `R=mq`,

\[
H_{m,mq}=D_qC_{m,q}D_q^*,
\qquad
D_q=\operatorname{diag}(q^{\lambda_1},\ldots,q^{\lambda_d}),
\]

and

\[
C_{m,q}=G_F(q)+O_F(m^{-1})
\]

entrywise, uniformly for `q>=2`, where

\[
G_F(q)_{rs}
=
2\sqrt{a_ra_s}
\frac{1-q^{-(\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s})}}
{\lambda_r+\overline{\lambda_s}}.
\]

Let

\[
M_{m,q}:=S_R^*A_{m,R}^{-1}S_R
=T_F^*H_{m,R}T_F.
\]

Taking determinants and using `(1)` gives the sharper identity

\[
\boxed{
q^{-2A_F}\det M_{m,q}
=
\frac{\det C_{m,q}}{\det G_F(\infty)}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Since `F` is fixed, determinant is Lipschitz on the bounded matrix family involved in `NB-095`, so its uniform entrywise estimate implies

\[
\boxed{
q^{-2A_F}\det M_{m,q}
=
\frac{\det G_F(q)}{\det G_F(\infty)}
+O_F(m^{-1}).
}
\tag{6}
\]

The ratio in `(6)` has a direct finite-window interpretation. `G_F(q)` is the Gram of the same exponentials restricted to `[0,log q]`; hence

\[
0<G_F(q)\preceq G_F(\infty),
\qquad
G_F(q)\uparrow G_F(\infty)
\quad(q\to\infty).
\]

Therefore

\[
0<
\frac{\det G_F(q)}{\det G_F(\infty)}
\le1,
\qquad
\frac{\det G_F(q)}{\det G_F(\infty)}\to1.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed finite distinct `F`,

\[
\boxed{
q^{-2A_F}\det M_{m,q}\longrightarrow1
}
\tag{8}
\]

whenever `m->infinity` and `q->infinity`.

The matrix determinant lemma in `NB-095` gives

\[
\frac{\det K_{m,mq}^{[F]}}{\det A_{m,mq}}
=
\det(I+M_{m,q}).
\]

Its already-proved lower spectral bound

\[
\lambda_{\min}(M_{m,q})\gg_F q^{2a_{\min}},
\qquad
a_{\min}=\min_r a_r>0,
\]

shows that `det(I+M)/det M -> 1` as `q->infinity`. Thus `(8)` upgrades the unnormalized `asymp_F` law of `NB-095` to

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det K_{m,mq}^{[F]}}
     {\det A_{m,mq}}
=
q^{2A_F}(1+o_F(1))
}
\tag{9}
\]

in the joint regime `m->infinity`, `q->infinity`.

This sharp coefficient is not asserted for the **diagonally normalized correlation determinant**. The coordinate-normalization bill of `NB-095` is uniformly `O_F(1)` but need not tend to zero, so the normalized law remains

\[
\log\frac{\det\mathcal R_{m,mq}^{[F]}}
          {\det\widetilde{\mathcal R}_{m,mq}}
=2A_F\log q+O_F(1).
\]

## 4. Stress tests and boundary of the conclusion

The cancellation survives arbitrary reordering of a fixed distinct zero set because both sides of `(2)` are products over unordered pairs. It also has the correct one-zero limit: when `d=1`, both products are empty, `G_F(infinity)=[1]`, and `|det T_F|=1`.

Near collision is the decisive control. If `lambda_r-lambda_s -> 0`, then `det G_F(infinity)->0` and `|det T_F|->infinity` at reciprocal rates, so `(1)` remains exact. This **does not** make the collision harmless: the operator norms and smallest singular values can still deteriorate without cancellation. Thus `(1)` rules out only a determinant-volume obstruction, not the spectral-conditioning obstruction already identified in `NB-095`.

The argument also does not cover repeated factors at exact confluence. `NB-095` used distinct simple poles in its triangular reduction. A genuine multiple zero requires a confluent/Jordan version of the state system; taking a singular distinct-zero limit is not a proof of that case.

Finally, this remains generator geometry. The line-level target-awareness control and `NB-001` still apply: neither `(1)` nor `(9)` converts volume repair into a lower bound for the Nyman target distance.

The line-specific consequence is sharper than the boundary stated at the end of `NB-095`. **Raw Cauchy determinant crowding is not itself a growing-family volume tax in the canonical deflation coordinates.** Any extension from fixed `F` to a growing divisor must fail, if it fails, through a stronger resource: spectral conditioning rather than determinant alone, finite-window loss as dimension grows, lack of uniformity in the `O_F(m^-1)` discretization, accumulation of diagonal-normalization cost, or genuine confluent/multiple-zero structure. This removes one natural explanation for the hidden `F`-dependence without pretending that the remaining growing-family problem is solved.
