## Claim

`NB-291` shows that the exact raw Dirichlet zero-sample table and the complete scalar canonical pivot profile can be grafted onto an arbitrary-lethargy Hilbert control. Hence neither the samples nor the scalar consequences of `NB-290` distinguish the true Nyman system. What the graft does not preserve is the common isometric dilation geometry that produces those scalars.

For the actual Blaschke-deflated first-free source spaces, that missing geometry has an exact finite-dimensional shadow. Fix a finite packet of distinct zeros

\[
Z=\{\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_N\}\subset\{\Re s>1/2\}
\]

with their actual multiplicities absorbed into the finite packet Blaschke factor `B_Z`, and let

\[
V_{r,Z}\subset \mathcal H_Z^\infty,
\qquad
L_Zh=(h(\rho_1),\ldots,h(\rho_N)),
\qquad
K_{r,Z}=L_ZP_{r,Z}L_Z^*
\]

be the quotient-source sections, first-free sampling map, and finite sampling Grams of `NB-284`--`NB-287`. Let

\[
(S_mh)(s)=m^{1/2-s}h(s),
\qquad
D_Z(m)=\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(m^{1/2-\rho_1},\ldots,m^{1/2-\rho_N}\bigr).
\]

Then

\[
L_ZS_m=D_Z(m)L_Z
\tag{1}
\]

and the canonical inclusion is

\[
S_mV_{n-1,Z}\subseteq V_{mn-1,Z}.
\tag{2}
\]

Define the **multiplicative quotient band**

\[
W_{m,n,Z}
:=V_{mn-1,Z}\ominus S_mV_{n-1,Z}.
\tag{3}
\]

Its first-free sampling Gram is

\[
\Gamma_{m,n,Z}
:=L_ZP_{W_{m,n,Z}}L_Z^*.
\tag{4}
\]

The band Gram is not an abstract extra object: it is exactly the cross-scale defect

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{m,n,Z}
=
K_{mn-1,Z}
-
D_Z(m)K_{n-1,Z}D_Z(m)^*
\succeq0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Moreover these bands and their Grams obey exact semigroup cocycles. For `k,m,n>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
W_{km,n,Z}
=
W_{k,mn,Z}
\oplus
S_kW_{m,n,Z},
}
\tag{6}
\]

orthogonally, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{km,n,Z}
=
\Gamma_{k,mn,Z}
+
D_Z(k)\Gamma_{m,n,Z}D_Z(k)^*.
}
\tag{7}
\]

This is vector/matrix structure that the scalar graft of `NB-291` does not encode.

There is also an exact coupling to the normalized canonical innovations. Let

\[
\psi_{n,Z}\in V_{n,Z}\ominus V_{n-1,Z},
\qquad \|\psi_{n,Z}\|=1,
\qquad
v_{n,Z}:=L_Z\psi_{n,Z}.
\]

Blaschke deflation is inner and hence isometric, so the canonical retention coefficient from `NB-290` is unchanged by the packet:

\[
c_{m,n}
=
\frac{\sqrt m\,\sigma_{mn}}{\sigma_n}
\in(0,1].
\]

The old-section part of the dilated innovation is

\[
\boxed{
b_{m,n,Z}
:=S_m\psi_{n,Z}-c_{m,n}\psi_{mn,Z}
\in W_{m,n,Z},
}
\tag{8}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\|b_{m,n,Z}\|^2=1-c_{m,n}^2.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Its first-free sample defect is exactly

\[
\boxed{
d_{m,n,Z}
:=L_Zb_{m,n,Z}
=
D_Z(m)v_{n,Z}-c_{m,n}v_{mn,Z}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Since `b_(m,n,Z)` is one realization of `d_(m,n,Z)` inside the quotient band, the minimum-norm repair in that band has squared cost

\[
d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}.
\]

More strongly, the dilation loss has the exact orthogonal decomposition

\[
\boxed{
1-c_{m,n}^2
=
d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}
+
\|z_{m,n,Z}\|^2,
}
\tag{11}
\]

where

\[
z_{m,n,Z}\in W_{m,n,Z}\cap\ker L_Z.
\]

Thus the scalar loss `1-c^2` from `NB-290` splits canonically into a **first-free visible repair charge** and a **first-free sample-null band charge**. This is the first exact source/sample coupling after the negative controls `NB-288`--`NB-291`. It still does not force either charge to be large, nor does it by itself control the target-aware Parseval tail of `NB-287`.

## 1. The Blaschke-deflated sections inherit the canonical dilation exactly

Write the canonical Hardy Nyman atoms as

\[
G_j(s)
=
\left(j^{-s}-j^{-1}\right)\frac{\zeta(s)}s.
\tag{12}
\]

For the packet `Z`, every `G_j` is divisible by the finite packet Blaschke factor `B_Z`. Put

\[
H_{j,Z}:=\frac{G_j}{B_Z}.
\tag{13}
\]

Multiplication by `B_Z` is an isometry on `H^2`, and it maps the quotient section generated by `H_(2,Z),...,H_(r,Z)` onto the canonical section generated by `G_2,...,G_r`. Consequently the quotient sections have exactly the canonical Gram geometry and the same one-step pivot norms `sigma_r`.

The inner multiplier

\[
S_mh=m^{1/2-s}h
\]

commutes with multiplication by `B_Z`. Hence the covariance from `NB-002` descends unchanged:

\[
\boxed{
S_mH_{j,Z}
=
\sqrt m\left(H_{mj,Z}-\frac1jH_{m,Z}\right).
}
\tag{14}
\]

For `j<n`, both indices `m` and `mj` are strictly below `mn`, so `(14)` gives `(2)`.

At the selected zeros, `S_m` is diagonal under point evaluation:

\[
(S_mh)(\rho_j)
=m^{1/2-\rho_j}h(\rho_j).
\]

This proves `(1)`. Notice that the diagonal here contains the Hardy normalization `m^(1/2-rho_j)`. It is distinct from the raw reduced Dirichlet-sample matrix `diag(m^(-rho_j))` appearing in `NB-289`; the two describe different levels of the same source construction.

Because multiplication by `B_Z` is isometric and commutes with `S_m`, the normalized innovation in the quotient is simply the Blaschke-deflated canonical innovation. Therefore the exact quotient transport of `NB-290` also descends:

\[
P_{V_{mn-1,Z}^{\perp}}S_m\psi_{n,Z}
=
c_{m,n}\psi_{mn,Z}.
\tag{15}
\]

No packet-dependent pivot renormalization appears.

## 2. Multiplicative quotient bands are orthogonal semigroup increments

The inclusions

\[
S_{km}V_{n-1,Z}
\subseteq
S_kV_{mn-1,Z}
\subseteq
V_{kmn-1,Z}
\tag{16}
\]

form a nested chain. Splitting the orthogonal difference between the first and last spaces at the middle space gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{kmn-1,Z}\ominus S_{km}V_{n-1,Z}
&=
\bigl(V_{kmn-1,Z}\ominus S_kV_{mn-1,Z}\bigr)\\
&\quad\oplus
\bigl(S_kV_{mn-1,Z}\ominus S_{km}V_{n-1,Z}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The first summand is `W_(k,mn,Z)`. Since `S_k` is an isometry and `S_(km)=S_kS_m`, the second is exactly `S_kW_(m,n,Z)`. This proves `(6)`.

For one band, nestedness gives the projection identity

\[
P_{W_{m,n,Z}}
=
P_{mn-1,Z}
-
S_mP_{n-1,Z}S_m^*.
\tag{18}
\]

Apply `L_Z` on the left and `L_Z^*` on the right. Equation `(1)` and its adjoint imply

\[
L_ZS_mP_{n-1,Z}S_m^*L_Z^*
=
D_Z(m)K_{n-1,Z}D_Z(m)^*.
\tag{19}
\]

Equations `(18)`--`(19)` prove `(5)`. Positivity is not an additional estimate: `Gamma_(m,n,Z)` is literally the Gram `L_ZP_WL_Z^*` of an orthogonal band.

Applying `(4)` to the orthogonal decomposition `(6)` gives `(7)` directly. Equivalently, it follows by adding and subtracting `D_Z(k)K_(mn-1,Z)D_Z(k)^*` and using

\[
D_Z(km)=D_Z(k)D_Z(m).
\]

Thus the sampled cross-scale defect is a positive matrix-valued cocycle, not merely the scalar retention cocycle of `NB-290`.

## 3. The normalized innovation defect lives in exactly one quotient band

Equation `(15)` gives

\[
S_m\psi_{n,Z}
=
c_{m,n}\psi_{mn,Z}+b_{m,n,Z},
\]

where `b_(m,n,Z)` belongs to `V_(mn-1,Z)`. Since `psi_(n,Z)` is orthogonal to `V_(n-1,Z)` and `S_m` is isometric,

\[
S_m\psi_{n,Z}\perp S_mV_{n-1,Z}.
\]

Also `psi_(mn,Z)` is orthogonal to all of `V_(mn-1,Z)`, hence in particular to `S_mV_(n-1,Z)`. Subtracting the two terms shows

\[
b_{m,n,Z}\perp S_mV_{n-1,Z}.
\]

Therefore `b_(m,n,Z)` lies in `W_(m,n,Z)`, proving `(8)`. The two terms in `(8)` are orthogonal and `S_m psi_(n,Z)` has unit norm, so Pythagoras gives `(9)`.

Applying `L_Z` and using `(1)` gives `(10)`.

The vector defects themselves satisfy a stronger cocycle than their norms. Since `S_kS_m=S_(km)` and the scalar coefficients obey the `NB-290` law

\[
c_{km,n}=c_{k,mn}c_{m,n},
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
b_{km,n,Z}
=
c_{m,n}b_{k,mn,Z}
+
S_kb_{m,n,Z}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

The two terms on the right are orthogonal. Indeed, `b_(k,mn,Z)` is orthogonal to `S_kV_(mn-1,Z)`, while `S_kb_(m,n,Z)` belongs to that space. Hence

\[
1-c_{km,n}^2
=
(1-c_{m,n}^2)
+c_{m,n}^2(1-c_{k,mn}^2),
\tag{21}
\]

now as the norm identity of an actual orthogonal vector decomposition rather than only an algebraic consequence of the scalar cocycle.

Sampling `(20)` gives the compatible vector relation

\[
\boxed{
d_{km,n,Z}
=
c_{m,n}d_{k,mn,Z}
+
D_Z(k)d_{m,n,Z}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Equations `(20)` and `(22)` are precisely the kind of common source/sample operator geometry that the old-section triangular graft of `NB-291` leaves unconstrained.

## 4. Dilation loss splits exactly into visible repair and sample-null energy

Restrict the sampling map to one quotient band,

\[
A_{m,n,Z}:=L_Z|_{W_{m,n,Z}}.
\]

Then

\[
A_{m,n,Z}A_{m,n,Z}^*
=
\Gamma_{m,n,Z}.
\tag{23}
\]

By `(8)` and `(10)`, `d_(m,n,Z)` lies in the range of `A_(m,n,Z)`. The minimum-norm vector in the band with those samples is therefore

\[
s_{m,n,Z}
:=
A_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}
=
P_{W_{m,n,Z}}L_Z^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}.
\tag{24}
\]

Standard Moore--Penrose geometry gives

\[
\|s_{m,n,Z}\|^2
=
d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z},
\tag{25}
\]

and `s_(m,n,Z)` is orthogonal to `W_(m,n,Z) cap ker L_Z`. Since `b_(m,n,Z)` is another feasible vector with the same samples, there is a unique orthogonal split

\[
b_{m,n,Z}=s_{m,n,Z}+z_{m,n,Z},
\qquad
z_{m,n,Z}\in W_{m,n,Z}\cap\ker L_Z.
\tag{26}
\]

Combining `(9)`, `(25)`, and `(26)` proves `(11)`. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
0
\le
 d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}
\le
1-c_{m,n}^2.
}
\tag{27}
\]

The ratio

\[
\eta_{m,n,Z}
:=
\frac{d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}}
{1-c_{m,n}^2}
\in[0,1]
\tag{28}
\]

when `c_(m,n)<1` is a natural **first-free visibility fraction of the canonical dilation loss**. Unlike `sigma_n`, `c_(m,n)`, or the raw Dirichlet sample table, it simultaneously uses the actual source dilation, the normalized innovation samples, and the metric of the cross-scale band in which the dilation defect is forced to live.

The complementary charge `1-eta` is not missing energy. Equation `(26)` identifies it exactly as a sample-null vector in the same multiplicative band. After any surjective first-free cutoff, such vectors belong to the finite interpolation nullspace and can be expanded in the neutralized-null basis of `NB-287`. What is not automatic is that their expansion lies in the particular future tail relevant to a moving cutoff, or that it has nontrivial correlation with the minimum-norm target source.

## 5. Stress tests and research disposition

The identity `(11)` does **not** manufacture a lower bound on `eta`. Generic Hilbert geometry permits both extremes. If the sampler annihilates the particular band defect `b_(m,n,Z)` while remaining onto through other directions, then `d_(m,n,Z)=0` and all of `1-c^2` sits in the sample-null term. At the other extreme, if the defect direction has no sample-null component inside its band, then `z_(m,n,Z)=0` and the full dilation loss is paid by the visible repair charge. Therefore no positive visibility fraction follows from semigroup isometry, nestedness, and finite-dimensional sampling alone.

Nor does the positive matrix cocycle `(7)` itself force a Nyman-distance rate. In one sample dimension a slowly increasing sequence of sampling Grams can satisfy the order relation in `(5)` simply because `|m^(1/2-rho)|<1` for an interior zero. The new information is the **joint** requirement that the actual innovation defect `(10)` be realized inside the same positive band Gram with the energy budget `(11)`.

This leaves a sharper continuation of the frontier after `NB-291`. A useful arithmetic estimate must show that, in the moving Ford regime, either

\[
d_{m,n,Z}^*\Gamma_{m,n,Z}^+d_{m,n,Z}
\]

is quantitatively visible in a target-relevant sample direction, or that the complementary sample-null vector `z_(m,n,Z)` has controlled projection onto the future neutralized innovations that form the `NB-287` Parseval tail. Another scalar bound on `sigma_n`, `c_(m,n)`, or `K_n` separately would not use the coupling isolated here.

The accepted clue `CLUE-visible-semigroup-defect-controls-section-enrichment` is therefore not resolved. This finding supplies an exact first-free sampling analogue of its missing visibility split, but not the residual-specific lower bound needed to turn visibility into a distance decrement.

### Prior-art audit

The multiplicative inner semigroup and the covariance of the Nyman atoms are classical; Bagchi's 2006 survey remains the line's source anchor for those facts. A fresh search also found the current Hardy-space orthogonality work of Calderaro--Manzur--Noor--Santos, Ehm's 2024 work on Nyman--Beurling Gram matrices, and Carvill's 2025 Mellin-smoothed multiplicative-ladder Gram decay. These are adjacent to the present ingredients, but the surfaced statements do not provide the quotient-band identity `(5)`, the positive matrix cocycle `(7)`, or the exact visible/null innovation split `(11)`.

No external theorem is load-bearing for the derivation: `(5)`--`(11)` follow from the already established line-local dilation covariance, Blaschke inner isometry, orthogonal projections, and finite-dimensional Moore--Penrose geometry. `SOURCES.md` therefore requires no change.
