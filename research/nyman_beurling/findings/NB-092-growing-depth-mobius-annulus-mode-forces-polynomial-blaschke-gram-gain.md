## 1. Möbius inversion synthesizes an exact flat visible annulus

`NB-091` writes the raw integer-cell coefficients as

\[
a_j(n)
=
 j\left\lfloor\frac nj\right\rfloor
-(j+1)\left\lfloor\frac n{j+1}\right\rfloor
\tag{14}
\]

and, for a coefficient vector `(c_j)_(m<=j<R)`, defines

\[
F_n:=\sum_{j=m}^{n}c_j a_j(n).
\tag{15}
\]

With

\[
b_m=c_m,
\qquad
b_k=c_k-c_{k-1}\quad(k>m),
\tag{16}
\]

the telescoping identity there gives

\[
F_n
=\sum_{k=m}^{n}k b_k\left\lfloor\frac nk\right\rfloor.
\tag{17}
\]

For the coefficients `(2)`, `b_k=0` unless `k=mr`. At those multiples,

\[
b_{mr}
=
\frac1m\left(S_r-S_{r-1}\right)
=
\frac{\mu(r)}{mr},
\tag{18}
\]

including `r=1` through `b_m=c_m=1/m`. Thus

\[
F_n
=
\sum_{r\le n/m}
\mu(r)
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{mr}\right\rfloor.
\tag{19}
\]

Put `N=floor(n/m)`. Writing `n=mN+s` with `0<=s<m` shows exactly that

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{mr}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lfloor\frac Nr\right\rfloor.
\tag{20}
\]

The classical Möbius divisor identity then gives

\[
F_n
=
\sum_{r\le N}\mu(r)\left\lfloor\frac Nr\right\rfloor
=
\sum_{k\le N}\sum_{r\mid k}\mu(r)
=1.
\tag{21}
\]

The raw innovations vanish before their own support origins, so the combination is zero before `log m`. On every integer cell `m<=n<mq`, `(21)` makes its value exactly `e^(-t/2)`. This proves `(4)`.

The visible energy is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}

&=

\frac1m-\frac1{mq}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

For the coefficient norm, `floor(j/m)=r` on exactly the `m` indices

\[
mr\le j<m(r+1),
\qquad1\le r\le q-1.
\]

Hence

\[
\|c\|_2^2
=

 m\left|\frac{S_r}{m}\right|^2
=
\frac{Q_q}{m},
\tag{23}
\]

which proves `(5)` and the Rayleigh bound `(6)`.

This makes the deterioration of the fixed-`q` inverse constant from `NB-091` explicit. It is not caused by a generic long interval: the exact direction is the Möbius inverse of the constant integer-cell profile.

## 2. The weighted Möbius energy detects the zero frontier

Extend

\[
S(x):=\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\mu(n)}n
\tag{24}
\]

as the usual right-continuous step function. Since `S(x)=S_r` on `[r,r+1)`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_q=\int_1^q|S(x)|^2\,dx.
}
\tag{25}
\]

For `Re z>0`, absolute convergence permits termwise integration:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^\infty S(x)x^{-z-1}\,dx
&=
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mu(n)}n
\int_n^\infty x^{-z-1}\,dx\\
&=
\frac1z
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{z+1}}\\
&=
\boxed{\frac1{z\zeta(z+1)}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Suppose for some `alpha>=0` that

\[
Q(X):=\int_1^X|S(x)|^2\,dx=O(X^\alpha).
\tag{27}
\]

On the dyadic shell `[2^k,2^(k+1)]`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives for real `sigma`

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{2^k}^{2^{k+1}}
|S(x)|x^{-\sigma-1}\,dx
&\le
Q(2^{k+1})^{1/2}
\left(
\int_{2^k}^{2^{k+1}}x^{-2\sigma-2}\,dx
\right)^{1/2}\\
&\ll
2^{-k(\sigma+(1-\alpha)/2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

Thus the Mellin integral in `(26)` converges locally uniformly, and hence holomorphically, throughout

\[
\Re z>\frac{\alpha-1}{2}.
\tag{29}
\]

By analytic continuation `(26)` would then make `1/[z zeta(z+1)]` holomorphic there. If `rho=beta+i gamma` is a nontrivial zero with `beta>1/2`, then `z=rho-1` is a genuine pole. Therefore `(27)` is impossible whenever

\[
\alpha<2\beta-1.
\tag{30}
\]

Equivalently, for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
Q(X)\ne O_\epsilon\left(X^{2\beta-1-\epsilon}\right).
\tag{31}
\]

Taking zeros with real parts approaching `Theta` proves `(12)`. In particular, if false RH holds, `Q_q` cannot remain bounded. Equation `(6)` then shows that the raw growing-depth band necessarily loses every uniform spectral floor.

This is a one-way zero-frontier implication, not a new equivalence claim. It is the spectral form needed here: the same weighted Möbius partial sums forced by the exact inverse of the raw integer-cell flag quantify the obstruction to extending the fixed-depth Gram conditioning of `NB-091` uniformly in `q`.

## 3. One off-critical Blaschke zero polynomially amplifies the same annulus

Fix an off-critical zero `rho` as in `(7)`. In the shifted half-plane coordinate `z=s-1/2`, its Blaschke factor is

\[
b_\lambda(z)=\frac{z-\lambda}{z+\overline\lambda}.
\tag{32}
\]

`NB-088`--`NB-089` derive the exact causal finite-prefix inverse

\[
(T_\lambda f)(u)
=
f(u)
+2a\int_0^u e^{\lambda(u-v)}f(v)\,dv.
\tag{33}
\]

Translate the annulus `(4)` by its support origin `log m`. On

\[
0\le u< L:=\log q
\]

its raw profile is

\[
f_m(u)=m^{-1/2}e^{-u/2}.
\tag{34}
\]

Substitution into `(33)` is elementary:

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_\lambda f_m(u)
&=
\frac1{\sqrt m}
\left[
 e^{-u/2}
+
\frac{2a}{\lambda+1/2}
\left(e^{\lambda u}-e^{-u/2}\right)
\right]\\
&=
\frac1{\sqrt m}
\left[
(1-C_\rho)e^{-u/2}
+C_\rho e^{\lambda u}
\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]

where

\[
C_\rho
:=\frac{2a}{\lambda+1/2}
=\frac{2a}{\rho}.
\tag{36}
\]

Because `0<a<1/2`, the mixed integral between `e^{-u/2}` and `e^(lambda u)` stays bounded as `L->infinity`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T_\lambda f_m\|_{L^2(0,\log q)}^2
&=
\frac1m
\left[
|C_\rho|^2
\frac{q^{2a}-1}{2a}
+O_\rho(1)
\right]\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac1m
\left[
\frac{2a}{|\rho|^2}q^{2a}
+O_\rho(1)
\right].
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]

Now factor the full Burnol inner function as

\[
B=b_\lambda B'.
\tag{38}
\]

For the coefficient combination `V_c=sum c_jD_j`, its raw version is `\widetilde V_c=BV_c`. Dividing only by `b_lambda` gives

\[
V_c^{(\rho)}
:=\frac{\widetilde V_c}{b_\lambda}
=B'V_c.
\tag{39}
\]

Multiplication by the remaining inner factor `B'` is a causal isometry. Its compression to the visible prefix is a contraction, so

\[
\|J_{mq}V_c\|
\ge
\|J_{mq}V_c^{(\rho)}\|.
\tag{40}
\]

The right side is exactly the finite-prefix norm computed in `(37)`, while `(22)` gives the raw denominator. Consequently

\[
\frac{c^*K_{m,mq}c}
     {c^*\widetilde K_{m,mq}c}
\ge
\frac{
\frac{2a}{|\rho|^2}q^{2a}+O_\rho(1)
}{1-1/q},
\tag{41}
\]

which proves `(8)`.

The gain is therefore present for **one fixed zero**. It does not require summing Blaschke factors, a zero-density theorem, or nonsummable horizontal mass. This is the first place in the current terminal-Gram route where a single off-critical zero becomes polynomially visible simply by letting the relative horizon grow.

## 5. What this does and does not settle

The matched exponents in `(12)` and `(47)` sharpen the growing-depth frontier substantially. The `q`-dependence discarded by the fixed-depth notation in `NB-091` contains two genuine arithmetic effects: a weighted-Möbius raw near-kernel and a Blaschke inverse gain. Both turn on at the exponent `2 beta-1` associated with an off-critical zero.

However, `NB-086` lower-bounds radial Szegő charge by a **diagonally normalized correlation determinant**, whereas `(9)`--`(47)` concern the generalized determinant of the unnormalized pair `(K,\widetilde K)`. Writing

\[
\mathcal E_{m,q}^{\rm corr}
:=
-\log\det\mathcal R_{m,mq}
+
\log\det\widetilde{\mathcal R}_{m,mq},
\tag{48}
\]

one has exactly

\[
\mathcal E_{m,q}^{\rm corr}
=
\sum_{j=m}^{mq-1}
\log\frac{(K_{m,mq})_{jj}}
          {(\widetilde K_{m,mq})_{jj}}
-
\log\frac{\det K_{m,mq}}
          {\det\widetilde K_{m,mq}}.
\tag{49}
\]

Thus diagonal energy inflation can in principle absorb the determinant gain. Nothing above proves that `(48)` is positive, divergent, or even source-discriminating. Nor does the result by itself produce a Nyman distance bound.

The decisive next theorem is correspondingly precise: determine whether the polynomial gain `(47)` leaves an unavoidable residue after diagonal normalization, or package the generalized pair `(K,\widetilde K)` into an inter-scale certificate whose target/radial relevance does not quotient out this annulus mode. Repeating another fixed-ratio determinant estimate cannot answer that question.
