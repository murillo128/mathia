# NB-021 — a rooted normal equation forces all deep sublinear-discrepancy mixing to become invisible

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + SOURCE-NORMAL-EQUATION + INTRINSIC-DISCREPANCY-BARRIER + ALL-DEPTH-TAIL-INVISIBILITY + UNROOTED-TRANSPORT-OBSTRUCTION + NEGATIVE/METHOD-BOUNDARY`. `NB-019` proves an intrinsic discrepancy mixing estimate for **every** dilation depth, while `NB-017` identifies visible section enrichment with the future multiplicative field generated by those dilations. The original form of this finding used the old `g_2` normal equation only inside the rooted range `2m<=N` and therefore stated invisibility only on an intermediate rooted band. That restriction is unnecessary for the mixing conclusion.

One rooted normal equation at the single scale `L_N=floor(N/2)` already pins the global shell mean `mu_N`. Once that scalar is small, the all-depth `NB-019` estimate propagates the conclusion to **every deeper dilation**, including all channels whose source index is beyond the old section and hence all genuinely unrooted channels of `NB-017`.

Let

\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}\{g_2,\ldots,g_N\},\qquad
r_N=e-P_Ne,\qquad d_N=\|r_N\|,
\tag{1}
\]

and let `mu_N` be the arithmetic mean of the periodic harmonic-shell sequence of `r_N`. Write

\[
\Delta_N:=\Delta(r_N)
=\sup_{A\le B}\left|
\sum_{n=A}^{B}(r_{N,n}-\mu_N)
\right|
\tag{2}
\]

for the intrinsic consecutive shell discrepancy from `NB-019`, and put

\[
Q_m:=\sqrt m\,A_m^*,\qquad
D_m:=A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I.
\tag{3}
\]

For `N>=4` set

\[
L_N:=\left\lfloor\frac N2\right\rfloor.
\tag{4}
\]

Then the source normal equation gives

\[
\boxed{
|\mu_N|\le\frac{2\Delta_N}{L_N}
\le\frac{4\Delta_N}{N-1}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

More importantly, for **every** integer `K>=2`, define

\[
\varepsilon_N(K)
:=\frac{2\Delta_N}{L_N}+\frac{2\Delta_N}{K}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{m\ge K}\|Q_mr_N\|_{\infty}
\le\varepsilon_N(K).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the old-section anchor at one rooted scale controls the whole future tail; no condition `2m<=N` remains in (7).

For `M>=K`, write

\[
\mathcal E_{N;K,M}
:=\sum_{m=K}^{M}\|D_mr_N\|^2,
\qquad
\mathcal J_{N;K,M}
:=\sum_{m=K}^{M}\|P_ND_mr_N\|^2,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
H_{K,M}:=\sum_{m=K}^{M}\frac1m.
\tag{9}
\]

Then the tail satisfies the finite inequalities

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{N;K,M}
\le \varepsilon_N(K)^2 H_{K,M},
}
\tag{10}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(d_N-\varepsilon_N(K)\bigr)_+^2H_{K,M}
\le
\mathcal E_{N;K,M}
\le
\bigl(d_N+\varepsilon_N(K)\bigr)^2H_{K,M}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently, whenever `varepsilon_N(K)<d_N`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal J_{N;K,M}}
     {\mathcal E_{N;K,M}}
\le
\left(
\frac{\varepsilon_N(K)}{d_N-\varepsilon_N(K)}
\right)^2
\qquad(M\ge K).
}
\tag{12}
\]

The bound is uniform in the terminal depth `M`. It therefore covers arbitrary unrooted horizons rather than merely the rooted band used in the earlier formulation.

Assume now, along an unbounded sequence of section sizes, that

\[
\Delta_N=o(N),
\tag{13}
\]

and choose

\[
K_N:=1+\left\lceil\sqrt{N(1+\Delta_N)}\right\rceil.
\tag{14}
\]

Then `K_N=o(N)` and

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_N(K_N)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\sup_{m\ge K_N}\|Q_mr_N\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{15}
\]

If in addition the limiting Nyman distance is positive,

\[
d_\infty:=\lim_{N\to\infty}d_N>0,
\tag{16}
\]

then (12) gives the all-depth tail collapse

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{M\ge K_N}
\frac{\mathcal J_{N;K_N,M}}
     {\mathcal E_{N;K_N,M}}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{17}
\]

In particular every genuinely unrooted channel of the `NB-017` multiplicative graph lies in this asymptotically invisible tail, because such a channel has source `m>N` while `K_N=o(N)`. Under the sublinear-discrepancy alternative, passing beyond the old section cannot restore a positive visible fraction merely by adding deeper multiplicative transport.

The remaining possible visibility is therefore localized before the mixing threshold `K_N`, where the discrepancy estimate has not yet made `Q_mr_N` small. This is a materially sharper boundary than the previous rooted-band statement: **the normal equation is needed only to pin the scalar mean, not to keep every later edge rooted.**

## 1. A single rooted scale pins the global shell mean

`NB-017` gives the future target-pairing field

\[
q_k=\langle r_N,g_k\rangle,
\qquad
s_k=kq_k,
\tag{18}
\]

and the exact dilation-pairing identity

\[
\boxed{
\langle Q_mr_N,g_j\rangle
=\frac{s_{mj}-s_m}{j}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

The projection normal equations give `s_k=0` for every `k<=N`. Hence for every `m` with `2m<=N`, taking `j=2` yields

\[
\langle Q_mr_N,g_2\rangle=0.
\tag{20}
\]

On the other hand, the intrinsic mixing estimate of `NB-019` is valid for every `m>=2`:

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_mr_N-\mu_Ne\|_{\infty}
\le\frac{2\Delta_N}{m}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

In Bagchi's real-space model `g_2` is nonnegative and

\[
\langle e,g_2\rangle
=\|g_2\|_1
=\frac{\log2}{2}>0.
\tag{22}
\]

Pairing (21) with `g_2` at any rooted scale gives

\[
|\mu_N|\,\langle e,g_2\rangle
\le
\frac{2\Delta_N}{m}\|g_2\|_1,
\]

hence

\[
|\mu_N|\le\frac{2\Delta_N}{m}.
\tag{23}
\]

Taking the single admissible choice `m=L_N` proves (5). The crucial logical point is that `mu_N` is one scalar attached to the whole periodic residual. Once (5) is known, it remains true when later dilations leave the rooted range.

No coefficient estimate, Gram condition number, lcm period, zero estimate, or closure hypothesis enters this step.

## 2. The rooted anchor propagates to every deeper dilation

For arbitrary `m>=K`, combine (5) with the all-depth estimate (21):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Q_mr_N\|_\infty
&\le
|\mu_N|+
\|Q_mr_N-\mu_Ne\|_\infty\\
&\le
\frac{2\Delta_N}{L_N}
+
\frac{2\Delta_N}{m}\\
&\le
\varepsilon_N(K).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

This proves (7). In particular the conclusion does not stop at `m=N/2`, `m=N`, or any other finite multiple of the old section.

Under (13), the choice (14) satisfies

\[
\frac{\Delta_N}{K_N}
\le
\frac{\Delta_N}{\sqrt{N(1+\Delta_N)}}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_N}{N}}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{25}
\]

while (5) gives `Delta_N/L_N->0`. Hence (15).

More generally, the special square-root choice is not essential. Any sequence `K_N` satisfying

\[
\Delta_N=o(K_N)
\tag{26}
\]

already gives `varepsilon_N(K_N)->0` under (13). Requiring also `K_N=o(N)` merely exhibits a nonempty transition between the discrepancy scale and the old-section scale.

This corrects the earlier boundary of the finding. The normal-equation hypothesis `2m<=N` is load-bearing only in (23), where one admissible rooted dilation pins `mu_N`; it is **not** a hypothesis of the future mixing estimate (24).

## 3. Full semigroup defect persists while every deep visible fraction collapses

Because `P_Nr_N=0`,

\[
\sqrt m\,P_ND_mr_N=P_NQ_mr_N.
\tag{27}
\]

Equation (7) and contractivity of `P_N` give, for every `m>=K`,

\[
\|P_ND_mr_N\|
\le
\frac{\varepsilon_N(K)}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{28}
\]

Summing squares proves (10).

For the full defect, the exact identity is

\[
\sqrt m\,D_mr_N=Q_mr_N-r_N.
\tag{29}
\]

The reverse and ordinary triangle inequalities combined with (7) yield

\[
\bigl(d_N-\varepsilon_N(K)\bigr)_+
\le
\|Q_mr_N-r_N\|
\le
 d_N+\varepsilon_N(K).
\tag{30}
\]

After squaring, dividing by `m`, and summing, this is (11). Equations (10)--(11) immediately give (12).

Under (13)--(16), `d_N` stays bounded below while `varepsilon_N(K_N)->0`, so (17) follows without any restriction on how `M` grows. The result is stronger than saying that an averaged block has small visible energy: it says that **each individual tail channel has vanishing visible/full ratio uniformly**, and therefore so does every finite collection of those channels.

There is still a distinction between relative and absolute visibility. At fixed `N`, if `mu_N` is nonzero, an infinite harmonic tail can accumulate unbounded visible energy even when the coefficient is small. Equation (17) is an `N->infinity` ratio statement under the positive-distance alternative; it does not claim that the infinite tail sum is finite for each fixed section.

The previous rooted-band conclusion remains as a useful absolute statement. Since `K_N<=L_N` eventually under (13), equations (24) and the sharper decomposition `Q_mr_N=mu_Ne+O(Delta_N/m)` recover

\[
\sum_{m=K_N}^{L_N}\|P_ND_mr_N\|^2\longrightarrow0,
\tag{31}
\]

while under (16)

\[
\sum_{m=K_N}^{L_N}\|D_mr_N\|^2
=\bigl(d_N^2+o(1)\bigr)
\sum_{m=K_N}^{L_N}\frac1m
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{32}
\]

Thus the old result is contained in the all-depth theorem rather than replaced by a different mechanism.

## 4. Sufficiently deep total visibility is also dominated by the invisible tail

Let the full statistics from `NB-015` be

\[
\mathcal E_M(r_N)=\sum_{m=2}^{M}\|D_mr_N\|^2,
\qquad
\mathcal J_{N,M}=\sum_{m=2}^{M}\|P_ND_mr_N\|^2,
\tag{33}
\]

and `rho_(N,M)=mathcal J_(N,M)/mathcal E_M(r_N)` when the denominator is nonzero.

The early part has the crude universal bound

\[
\sum_{m=2}^{K-1}\|P_ND_mr_N\|^2
\le
\sum_{m=2}^{K-1}\|D_mr_N\|^2
\le4K,
\tag{34}
\]

because `A_m^*` is a contraction and `d_N<=1`. Combining (10), (11), and (34) gives, whenever `varepsilon_N(K)<d_N`,

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{N,M}
\le
\frac{4K}
{(d_N-\varepsilon_N(K))^2H_{K,M}}
+
\left(
\frac{\varepsilon_N(K)}{d_N-\varepsilon_N(K)}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{35}
\]

Therefore under (13)--(16), if a terminal depth `M_N>=K_N` is chosen so that

\[
\frac{H_{K_N,M_N}}{K_N}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{36}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{N,M_N}\longrightarrow0.

\tag{37}
\]

This is not an obstruction inside every diagonal corridor. Condition (36) requires an extremely deep terminal horizon because `H_(K,M)` grows only logarithmically in `M`; in particular it need not be compatible with the `sqrt(M)<=N` path used in the closure criterion of `NB-015`. Its role is different: going arbitrarily deep cannot manufacture a source-visible fraction after the tail has mixed. The only place a positive total fraction can remain is the pre-mixing block.

Equation (40) is only a coordinate description of the same phenomenon, not a substitute for the Hilbert-space estimate.
