# NB-003 — finite semigroup target probes have no quantitative gap

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + EXACT-DERIVED + TARGET-CHARACTER-RIGIDITY + APPROXIMATE-SPECTRAL-ALIASING + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`. `NB-002` shows that the canonical Nyman semigroup remembers absolute multiplicative scale through the fixed target even when a finite generator block has identical Gram geometry. This suggests a stronger target-aware discriminator: the real-space target `1` is an adjoint eigenvector of every integer semigroup map with character `m^{-1/2}`. Algebraically, only two multiplicatively independent scales already identify that target direction. Quantitatively, however, **every fixed finite collection of such semigroup probes has zero stability gap** inside the canonical discrete Nyman ambient space.

More precisely, let `\mathcal M` be Bagchi's discrete closed subspace of `L^2((0,1])`, consisting of functions constant on the harmonic intervals

\[
I_n=\left(\frac1{n+1},\frac1n\right],\qquad n\ge1,
\]

and let `A_m` be the restriction to `\mathcal M` of the standard Nyman semigroup isometry

\[
(T_mf)(x)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt m\,f(mx),&0<x\le1/m,\\
0,&1/m<x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]

The target `e(x)=1` has norm one and satisfies

\[
A_m^*e=m^{-1/2}e.
\]

The exact joint eigenspace for the two scales `m=2,3` is

\[
\boxed{
\ker(A_2^*-2^{-1/2}I)\cap
\ker(A_3^*-3^{-1/2}I)
=\mathbb C e.
}
\]

Nevertheless, for every finite set `S` of integer scales containing `2` and `3`, there are unit vectors `w_j\in\mathcal M\cap e^\perp` such that

\[
\boxed{
\max_{m\in S}
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)w_j\|\longrightarrow0.
}
\]

Thus exact target identification does **not** yield a quantitative target certificate. There is no modulus tending to zero that can bound distance to the target direction from finitely many adjoint-semigroup covariance defects. The obstruction is an explicit family of projected Mellin characters whose vertical frequencies can alias any fixed finite set of logarithmic scales by simultaneous Diophantine approximation.

This does not estimate the Nyman distance `d_N` or the target-aware scalar `q_N=1-d_N^2`. Its durable consequence is narrower: a proposed replacement of the explicit target pairings by finitely many semigroup-eigenvalue tests cannot supply the uniform target control demanded by `NB-001`. Any positive semigroup route must use a scale set or bandwidth that grows with the section, together with a quantitative anti-aliasing theorem, or retain the target pairings directly.

## 1. The target is the unique exact two-scale adjoint character

First work in the full space `L^2((0,1])`. Direct substitution in the inner product gives the adjoint

\[
\boxed{
(T_m^*h)(y)=m^{-1/2}h(y/m),
\qquad 0<y\le1.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle T_mf,h\rangle
&=\int_0^{1/m}\sqrt m\,f(mx)\overline{h(x)}\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1 f(y)\overline{m^{-1/2}h(y/m)}\,dy.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the constant target satisfies

\[
T_m^*e=m^{-1/2}e.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose conversely that `h\in L^2((0,1])` obeys the target character at scales two and three:

\[
T_2^*h=2^{-1/2}h,
\qquad
T_3^*h=3^{-1/2}h.
\tag{3}
\]

By (1),

\[
h(y/2)=h(y),
\qquad
h(y/3)=h(y)
\quad\text{for a.e. }y\in(0,1].
\tag{4}
\]

Put `H(t)=h(e^{-t})` for `t\ge0`. Then

\[
H(t+\log2)=H(t),
\qquad
H(t+\log3)=H(t)
\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{5}
\]

Extend `H` to the real line using the first period. The second equality then also extends to all real `t`. Since `\log2/\log3` is irrational, the additive subgroup generated by `\log2` and `\log3` is dense in `\mathbb R`. A measurable locally square-integrable function invariant under this dense translation subgroup is constant almost everywhere: translations are continuous in `L^2_{\rm loc}`, so invariance passes from the dense subgroup to every real translation. Therefore `h` is constant almost everywhere.

Thus

\[
\ker(T_2^*-2^{-1/2}I)\cap
\ker(T_3^*-3^{-1/2}I)
=\mathbb C e.
\tag{6}
\]

Bagchi's discrete space `\mathcal M` is invariant under every `T_m`, so the same uniqueness holds after restriction to `A_m=T_m|_{\mathcal M}`. This is a genuinely target-sensitive statement: unlike the repeated Gram blocks in `NB-002`, the adjoint character records how the global target transforms under absolute multiplicative scale.

## 2. Projected Mellin characters are exact joint eigenvectors for shifted characters

Let `P_{\mathcal M}` be orthogonal projection onto `\mathcal M`, and for real `\tau` define the unit-modulus Mellin character

\[
h_\tau(x)=x^{i\tau},
\qquad \|h_\tau\|_2=1.
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (1) gives the exact full-space eigenrelation

\[
T_m^*h_\tau
=m^{-1/2-i\tau}h_\tau.
\tag{8}
\]

Set

\[
v_\tau:=P_{\mathcal M}h_\tau.
\tag{9}
\]

The projection does not destroy this covariance. For `f\in\mathcal M`, invariance of `\mathcal M` under `T_m` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle f,A_m^*P_{\mathcal M}h_\tau\rangle
&=\langle T_mf,P_{\mathcal M}h_\tau\rangle\\
&=\langle T_mf,h_\tau\rangle\\
&=\langle f,T_m^*h_\tau\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
A_m^*v_\tau
=m^{-1/2-i\tau}v_\tau
\qquad(m\ge1).
}
\tag{10}
\]

So the canonical discrete ambient space already contains a continuous family of exact joint eigencharacters, indexed by vertical Mellin frequency. The target corresponds to `\tau=0`; finite scale probes must distinguish that character from frequencies for which the phases `m^{-i\tau}` are close to one.

The vectors `v_\tau` are nonzero. Since `e\in\mathcal M`, projection preserves their target pairing:

\[
\boxed{
\langle v_\tau,e\rangle
=\langle h_\tau,e\rangle
=\int_0^1x^{i\tau}\,dx
=\frac1{1+i\tau}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

To quantify their norm, note that `P_{\mathcal M}h_\tau` is the conditional average of `x^{i\tau}` on each harmonic interval. Fix `|\tau|\ge1` and put

\[
N_\tau=\lceil2|\tau|\rceil.
\]

For `n\ge N_\tau`, the phase variation across `I_n` is at most

\[
|\tau|\log\frac{n+1}{n}
\le\frac{|\tau|}{n}
\le\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]

All values therefore lie in an arc of angular width at most `1/2`, so the modulus of their normalized average is at least `\cos(1/4)`. Since

\[
\sum_{n\ge N}|I_n|=\frac1N,
\]

we obtain the explicit lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\|v_\tau\|_2^2
\ge
\frac{\cos^2(1/4)}{\lceil2|\tau|\rceil}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

After normalization `u_\tau=v_\tau/\|v_\tau\|`, equations (11)--(13) imply

\[
|\langle u_\tau,e\rangle|
\le
\frac{\sqrt{\lceil2|\tau|\rceil}}
     {\cos(1/4)\sqrt{1+\tau^2}}
=O(|\tau|^{-1/2}).
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}(u_\tau,\mathbb Ce)\longrightarrow1
\qquad(|\tau|\to\infty).
}
\tag{15}
\]

This rules out a hidden compactness escape: the approximate target characters below can be chosen to remain asymptotically maximally far from the target direction.

## 3. Two-scale exact rigidity has zero quantitative gap

From (10), the defect of `u_\tau` from the target character at scale `m` is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)u_\tau\|
=m^{-1/2}|m^{-i\tau}-1|.
}
\tag{16}
\]

For the two scales `2,3`, take continued-fraction convergents `p_j/q_j` to

\[
\alpha=\frac{\log3}{\log2}
\]

and set

\[
\tau_j=\frac{2\pi q_j}{\log2}.
\tag{17}
\]

Then

\[
2^{-i\tau_j}=1
\tag{18}
\]

exactly, while

\[
3^{-i\tau_j}
=\exp\left(-2\pi i q_j\frac{\log3}{\log2}\right)
\longrightarrow1
\tag{19}
\]

because `q_j\alpha-p_j\to0`. Thus

\[
\max_{m\in\{2,3\}}
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)u_{\tau_j}\|
\to0,
\tag{20}
\]

whereas (15) gives distance to the exact joint eigenspace tending to one.

The statement can be made literally on the target-orthogonal complement. Put

\[
c_\tau=\langle u_\tau,e\rangle,
\qquad
w_\tau=
\frac{u_\tau-c_\tau e}{\sqrt{1-|c_\tau|^2}}.
\tag{21}
\]

For large `|\tau|`, this is well defined, has norm one, and lies in `e^\perp`. Since `(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)e=0`,

\[
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)w_\tau\|
=
\frac{m^{-1/2}|m^{-i\tau}-1|}
     {\sqrt{1-|c_\tau|^2}}.
\tag{22}
\]

Combining (14), (17)--(19), and (22) proves

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\substack{w\in\mathcal M\cap e^\perp\\\|w\|=1}}
\left(
\|(A_2^*-2^{-1/2}I)w\|^2
+\|(A_3^*-3^{-1/2}I)w\|^2
\right)=0.
}
\tag{23}
\]

So the target eigencharacter is algebraically unique but is **not spectrally separated** from the target-orthogonal part of the canonical discrete space.

## 4. Every fixed finite scale family has the same aliasing obstruction

The phenomenon is not special to the pair `2,3`. Let

\[
S=\{m_1,\ldots,m_r\}
\]

be any fixed finite set of positive integer scales. By simultaneous Diophantine approximation, there is an unbounded sequence `\tau_j` such that

\[
\boxed{
m_\ell^{-i\tau_j}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{for every }1\le\ell\le r.}
\tag{24}
\]

For example, apply Dirichlet's simultaneous approximation theorem to the finite vector

\[
\left(\frac{\log m_1}{2\pi},\ldots,
      \frac{\log m_r}{2\pi}\right).
\]

Equations (16) and (24) then give

\[
\max_{m\in S}
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)u_{\tau_j}\|
\to0,
\tag{25}
\]

while (15) again gives distance from `\mathbb Ce` tending to one. If `S` contains `2,3`, the exact common target eigenspace is still only `\mathbb Ce` by (6), so this is a direct failure of quantitative stability around an exactly identifiable target.

Equivalently, there is no function `\omega_S(\varepsilon)\to0` as `\varepsilon\downarrow0` for which every unit `u\in\mathcal M` satisfies

\[
\operatorname{dist}(u,\mathbb Ce)
\le
\omega_S\left(
\max_{m\in S}
\|(A_m^*-m^{-1/2}I)u\|
\right)
\tag{26}
\]

when `S` contains `2,3`. The sequence above makes the argument of `\omega_S` tend to zero while the left side tends to one.

Under the Mellin transform, the unprojected characters `x^{i\tau}` correspond to the vertical reproducing-kernel family

\[
\int_0^1x^{s-1}x^{i\tau}\,dx
=\frac1{s+i\tau}.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus the obstruction is not an arbitrary synthetic Hilbert-space deformation. It is the ordinary vertical Mellin character already intrinsic to the Nyman transform, projected into Bagchi's canonical discrete ambient space.

## 5. Consequence for target-aware finite-section certificates

`NB-001` identifies the exact finite target data `(G_N,b_N)` and shows that Gram geometry alone is non-identifying. `NB-002` then shows internally that the Nyman semigroup copies a Gram block while scaling its fixed-target mass. The present result closes the most direct attempt to use that same semigroup covariance as a finite replacement for explicit target alignment.

The character equations

\[
A_m^*u\approx m^{-1/2}u
\]

at finitely many fixed scales do not yield a stable target selector, even though exact equality at scales two and three does. Vertical frequencies can alias every finite logarithmic sampling set. This is analogous to finite-frequency sampling without a bandwidth assumption: injectivity at the exact solution does not supply a quantitative inverse on an unbounded frequency class.

A semigroup-based positive continuation therefore needs an additional ingredient absent from the fixed-scale test. Natural possibilities are:

- a scale family whose logarithmic sampling bandwidth grows with the finite Nyman section, together with an explicit anti-aliasing estimate on the admitted Mellin frequencies;
- an independent theorem restricting the vertical-frequency content of the actual residual or near-optimal coefficient family;
- direct retention of the canonical target pairings `b_k=-\log k/k` and control of their mass in the small Gram modes;
- a normed primal/dual certificate whose target value is evaluated after, rather than inferred from, semigroup covariance.

These are distinct proof obligations. The present obstruction does not say that a growing-scale semigroup test fails, and it does not show that the actual Nyman residuals realize the aliasing sequence. It says that **finite fixed-scale covariance alone cannot provide the uniform modulus** required to make exact target-character rigidity quantitatively useful.

## 6. Prior art, audit, and evidence boundary

The load-bearing Nyman input is classical and already anchored in `research/nyman_beurling/SOURCES.md`: Bhaskar Bagchi, *On Nyman, Beurling and Baez-Duarte's Hilbert space reformulation of the Riemann hypothesis*, Proc. Indian Acad. Sci. Math. Sci. 116 (2006), 137--146; arXiv:math/0607733; DOI `10.1007/BF02829783`. Bagchi defines the discrete harmonic-cell space, the isometric semigroup `T_m`, and its covariance on the Báez-Duarte generators. Equation (1) is the elementary adjoint of that same isometry.

The remaining ingredients are standard: conditional expectation onto a partition, Mellin characters, measurable rigidity under two incommensurable periods, continued fractions, and simultaneous Diophantine approximation. A targeted literature search found current work on Hilbert/subspace geometry of Nyman--Beurling spaces, including Jongho Yang, *A Friedrichs angle between the Nyman-Beurling spaces and the Riemann hypothesis*, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 560 (2026), 130494, DOI `10.1016/j.jmaa.2026.130494`, but did not expose a theorem matching the specific semigroup-character stability question above. This is only a neighboring prior-art boundary, **not a claim of novelty**.

Several audit boundaries matter. First, (23) concerns the canonical ambient discrete space `\mathcal M`, not the finite generator span `V_N`; no assertion is made that each aliasing vector belongs to a prescribed finite section. Second, the lack of a fixed-scale stability gap does not imply the target-aware scalar `q_N` is uncontrolled by other arithmetic information. Third, simultaneous approximation in (24) exploits unbounded Mellin frequency; any theorem that independently bounds the relevant frequency content may evade the obstruction. Fourth, exact two-scale uniqueness is measure-theoretic and qualitative; using it as a numerical or asymptotic certificate without a bandwidth hypothesis is precisely the invalid inference ruled out here.

No new approximation rate, zero-free region, unconditional improvement to the Nyman--Beurling criterion, or RH implication follows. The result is an exact information/stability boundary: the global target is the unique exact two-scale semigroup character, but that character has zero quantitative separation under every fixed finite family of semigroup probes.
