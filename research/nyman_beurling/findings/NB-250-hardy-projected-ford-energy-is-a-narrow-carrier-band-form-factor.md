# NB-250 — Hardy-projected Ford energy is a narrow carrier-band form factor

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUADRATIC-FREQUENCY-REDUCTION + HARDY-PROJECTED-CURRENT + CARRIER-BAND-FORM-FACTOR + MATCHED-CONTROL + METHOD-BOUNDARY + NB-246/NB-249-REFINEMENT + SOURCE-ONLY + FRESH-PRIOR-ART-AUDIT`.

`NB-246` shows that after freezing the source parameters and demodulating the common carrier, the moving-center Ford current has bandwidth `O(H)`. `NB-249` then identifies the legal first-order operation before squaring: positive Mellin frequencies Hardy-project each Cauchy pole to the finite marked zero coefficient. The live problem is therefore the hard `1/H` second moment of that projected finite current.

There is an exact frequency-side form of this target. For a finite local truncation of the projected current, write the moving center as

\[
z:=H(t-t_0).
\]

The current factors as

\[
\boxed{
G_{J,L}(z)=\int_a^b \phi(u)\,A_{J,L}(u)e^{-iuz}\,du,
}
\]

where `u` is the original Mellin-shell coordinate and

\[
\boxed{
A_{J,L}(u)
=
\frac{e^{-r}}J
\sum_{\rho}
 m(\rho)e^{-d_\rho}\chi(d_\rho)\psi_L(\tau_\rho)
 e^{-u\eta_\rho}
 e^{i(YJ+u)\tau_\rho}.
}
\]

Since `tau_rho=H(gamma-t_0)` and `YJ=X/H`, the last phase is exactly

\[
 e^{i(X+uH)(\gamma-t_0)}.
\]

Thus the hard Ford second moment is a positive quadratic form in a **depth-marked zero exponential sum sampled across the physical carrier band**

\[
\boxed{
X+H[a,b],
}
\]

whose total width is only `Theta(H)=Theta(X/J)`.

This gives a useful positive reduction and a sharp negative boundary at the same time. Plancherel shows that an `o(1)` carrier-band `L^2` form-factor estimate is sufficient for the hard Ford box. But the coherent `NB-231` packet keeps this entire band energy bounded below: over a band of width `H`, adjacent carrier-cell zeros separated by `Theta(1/X)` acquire only `O(1/J)` relative phase sweep. Generic frequency averaging therefore cannot decorrelate the packet. A bandwidth comparable to `X`, not `H=X/Theta(J)`, would be needed before ordinary separated-frequency orthogonality could resolve carrier-cell spacing.

So `NB-246`'s favorable center bandlimit does **not** itself provide an averaging gain after `NB-249`. The remaining theorem can be stated as a narrow-band, depth-marked local form-factor estimate, but any proof must use zeta-specific cancellation inside that band rather than the shell bandwidth alone.

## 1. Start from the legally projected finite current

Use the Ford notation stabilized in `NB-230`--`NB-249`:

\[
Q:=\log(10+|t_0|),
\qquad
R:=Q^{2/3}(\log Q)^{1/3},
\qquad
Y:=\frac XR\asymp1,
\qquad
J:=\frac RH\to\infty,
\tag{1}
\]

and let

\[
F(w)=\int_a^b\phi(u)e^{iuw}\,du,
\qquad
0<a<b<1,
\qquad
\phi\in C_c^\infty((0,1)).
\tag{2}
\]

For a zero `rho=beta+i gamma`, retain

\[
d_\rho:=X(1-\beta)-\log J,
\qquad
\tau_\rho:=H(\gamma-t_0),
\qquad
\eta_\rho:=\frac{\log J+d_\rho+r}{YJ}.
\tag{3}
\]

`NB-249` proves that the positive-frequency Hardy projection of the first-order edge field gives the finite coefficient

\[
\frac{e^{-r-d_\rho}}J
m(\rho)\chi(d_\rho)
 e^{iYJ\tau_\rho}
 F(\tau_\rho+i\eta_\rho).
\tag{4}
\]

To keep every Fourier manipulation literal, insert a fixed smooth ordinate truncation

\[
\psi_L\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R),
\qquad
\psi_L(\tau)=1\quad (|\tau|\le L),
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\psi_L\subset[-2L,2L].
\tag{5}
\]

Only finitely many zeros occur in this support at fixed `t_0`. When the observation center moves to

\[
t=t_0+\frac zH,
\tag{6}
\]

the corresponding truncated projected current is

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{J,L}(z)
:=\frac{e^{-r}}J
\sum_\rho &m(\rho)e^{-d_\rho}\chi(d_\rho)\psi_L(\tau_\rho)
 e^{iYJ\tau_\rho}\\
&\times F(\tau_\rho-z+i\eta_\rho).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The common phase generated by moving `t` at the large carrier `X` has already been demodulated, exactly as in `NB-246`; it does not affect `|G_{J,L}|`.

The truncation in (5) is bookkeeping, not a new source assumption. `NB-247` already supplies the Bellotti-weighted population estimate needed to control Schwartz tails of the original `F`-current uniformly on every fixed hard box `|z|<=A`. Thus one may first derive all identities below for fixed `L`, then let `L` grow after the Ford limit. This order matters because the frequency amplitude obtained below need not be absolutely summable if the truncation is removed before the shell transform is reassembled.

## 2. The center Fourier transform is a zero form factor near frequency `X`

Insert (2) into (7):

\[
F(\tau_\rho-z+i\eta_\rho)
=
\int_a^b
\phi(u)e^{-u\eta_\rho}
 e^{iu\tau_\rho}e^{-iuz}\,du.
\tag{8}
\]

Because the zero sum is finite, interchange sum and integral. Then

\[
\boxed{
G_{J,L}(z)
=
\int_a^b
\phi(u)A_{J,L}(u)e^{-iuz}\,du,
}
\tag{9}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
A_{J,L}(u)
:=
\frac{e^{-r}}J
\sum_\rho
m(\rho)e^{-d_\rho}\chi(d_\rho)\psi_L(\tau_\rho)
 e^{-u\eta_\rho}
 e^{i(YJ+u)\tau_\rho}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Now use

\[
YJ=\frac XH,
\qquad
\tau_\rho=H(\gamma-t_0).
\tag{11}
\]

The phase in (10) becomes

\[
\boxed{
(YJ+u)\tau_\rho
=(X+uH)(\gamma-t_0).
}
\tag{12}
\]

Hence `A_{J,L}(u)` is not an abstract Fourier coefficient. It is the actual depth-marked zero exponential sum evaluated at the physical carrier frequency

\[
\xi=X+uH.
\tag{13}
\]

As `u` ranges through the fixed shell support `[a,b]`, the entire frequency interval is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{X,H}
=[X+aH,X+bH],
\qquad
|\mathcal B_{X,H}|=(b-a)H.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Since `H=R/J` and `X=YR`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|\mathcal B_{X,H}|}{X}
=
\frac{b-a}{YJ}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is the quadratic frequency-side meaning of `NB-246`'s center bandlimit. The moving-center variable does not give access to a broad interval of carrier frequencies; it probes only a relative `Theta(1/J)` neighborhood of the selected carrier `X`.

## 3. Hard Ford energy is an exact positive carrier-band quadratic form

For a fixed hard-box radius `A>0`, define

\[
I_{A,L}
:=
\int_{-A}^{A}|G_{J,L}(z)|^2\,dz.
\tag{16}
\]

From (9), direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
I_{A,L}
=
\int_a^b\int_a^b
\phi(u)\overline{\phi(v)}
A_{J,L}(u)\overline{A_{J,L}(v)}
K_A(u-v)\,du\,dv,
}
\tag{17}
\]

where

\[
K_A(q)
:=
\int_{-A}^{A}e^{-iqz}\,dz
=
\begin{cases}
2\sin(Aq)/q,&q\ne0,\\
2A,&q=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
\]

The form in (17) is positive semidefinite because it is exactly the physical hard-box norm (16). No absolute values have been inserted into the zero sum and the carrier phase remains intact.

Plancherel on the full `z`-line gives the stronger but simpler sufficient estimate

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|G_{J,L}(z)|^2\,dz
=
2\pi\int_a^b
|\phi(u)|^2|A_{J,L}(u)|^2\,du.
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
I_{A,L}
\le
2\pi\int_a^b
|\phi(u)|^2|A_{J,L}(u)|^2\,du.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Together with the uniform Schwartz-tail passage from the truncated current to the full current and `NB-247`'s hard-box-to-pointwise implication, (20) yields a clean sufficient theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}
\limsup_{Q\to\infty}
\sup_{t_0}
\int_a^b
|\phi(u)|^2|A_{J,L,t_0}(u)|^2\,du
=0
}
\tag{21}
\]

forces the selected Ford current to be `o(1)` uniformly in the same regime.

Condition (21) is deliberately labeled **sufficient**, not equivalent. A hard-box norm can be smaller than the global `z`-norm, so replacing (17) by the diagonal Plancherel majorant (20) may discard useful cancellation. The exact object remains (17); (21) is an analytically convenient carrier-band target.

## 4. Expanding the band norm recovers a depth-marked local pair form factor

Expanding the right side of (20) shows exactly what information a theorem proving (21) must contain. Put

\[
c_\rho
:=
m(\rho)e^{-d_\rho}\chi(d_\rho)\psi_L(\tau_\rho).
\tag{22}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_a^b |\phi(u)|^2|A_{J,L}(u)|^2\,du\\
&\quad=
\frac{e^{-2r}}{J^2}
\sum_{\rho,\rho'}
 c_\rho\overline{c_{\rho'}}
 e^{iYJ(\tau_\rho-\tau_{\rho'})}
 \mathcal K_{\rho,\rho'}
 (\tau_\rho-\tau_{\rho'}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{\rho,\rho'}(v)
:=
\int_a^b
|\phi(u)|^2
 e^{-u(\eta_\rho+\eta_{\rho'})}
 e^{iuv}\,du.
}
\tag{24}
\]

The factor `mathcal K` is smooth in the normalized ordinate difference `v`; its natural localization scale is `|v|=Theta(1)`, corresponding to physical difference `Theta(1/H)`. Inside that envelope, however, the unresolved phase is still

\[
 e^{iYJ(\tau_\rho-\tau_{\rho'})}
=
e^{iX(\gamma-\gamma')}.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus (23) is precisely a **depth-marked, Ford-local, carrier-resolved form factor**. The vertical envelope is on the shell width `1/H`, while the oscillatory pair phase resolves the finer carrier scale `1/X`. This separates the two scales without confusing them.

The diagonal `rho=rho'` is still harmless. The Bellotti population control used in `NB-243` gives `sum |c_rho|^2=O(J)` in the critical window, so after the `J^-2` normalization its contribution is `O(1/J)`. The live content of (23) is again the off-diagonal coherence.

## 5. The natural shell band is `J`-fold too narrow to decorrelate the matched packet

The representation above might suggest that averaging `A_{J,L}(u)` over the shell band automatically creates a large-sieve gain. The `NB-231` matched packet gives a sharp counterexample.

Take a common bounded Ford depth `d_*`, multiplicity one, `chi(d_*)=1`, and

\[
M:=\lfloor\delta J\rfloor,
\qquad
\tau_j:=\frac{2\pi qj}{YJ},
\qquad
0\le j<M,
\tag{26}
\]

for fixed positive integer `q` and sufficiently small fixed `delta>0`. Then

\[
 e^{iYJ\tau_j}=1
\tag{27}
\]

for every point, and

\[
\eta_*:=\frac{\log J+d_*+r}{YJ}=o(1).
\tag{28}
\]

For a truncation containing the packet, its contribution to (10) is

\[
A_J^{\rm pkt}(u)
=
\frac{e^{-r-d_*}}J
 e^{-u\eta_*}
\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}
 e^{2\pi iquj/(YJ)}.
\tag{29}
\]

Choose `delta` so small that, uniformly for `u in [a,b]` and `Y>=Y_->0`,

\[
\frac{2\pi q b\delta}{Y_-}<\frac\pi4.
\tag{30}
\]

All summands in (29) then lie in one angular sector of width less than `pi/4`. Consequently

\[
\left|
\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}
 e^{2\pi iquj/(YJ)}
\right|
\ge
M\cos(\pi/4),
\tag{31}
\]

and therefore, uniformly on `[a,b]`,

\[
\liminf_{J\to\infty}
|A_J^{\rm pkt}(u)|
\ge
\frac{\delta}{\sqrt2}e^{-r-d_*}>0.
\tag{32}
\]

If `phi` is nonzero, (32) implies

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{J\to\infty}
\int_a^b
|\phi(u)|^2
|A_J^{\rm pkt}(u)|^2\,du
>0.
}
\tag{33}
\]

Thus the entire carrier band in (14), not merely the single central frequency `X`, remains coherent on the dangerous packet.

The scale reason is transparent in physical variables. Adjacent packet ordinates have separation

\[
\Delta\gamma
\asymp
\frac1X.
\tag{34}
\]

Across the full natural shell band, the relative phase sweep of two adjacent points is only

\[
|\mathcal B_{X,H}|\,\Delta\gamma
\asymp
\frac HX
\asymp
\frac1J.
\tag{35}
\]

Ordinary separated-frequency orthogonality becomes available only once the averaging interval has width comparable to the inverse spacing,

\[
W\,\Delta\gamma\gtrsim1,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
W\gtrsim X.
\tag{36}
\]

The actual Mellin shell provides only `W=Theta(H)=Theta(X/J)`. It is short by the diverging factor

\[
\boxed{
\frac XH=YJ.
}
\tag{37}
\]

Equation (33) is the matched-control proof of this boundary; (36) is the classical separated-frequency interpretation. Therefore no argument based only on the existence of the shell-frequency average can force (21) from population or spacing information already compatible with `NB-231`.

## 6. Prior-art audit and what this changes

The harmonic ingredients in (9)--(20) are classical Fourier inversion and Plancherel. The separated-frequency scale behind (36) is the standard large-sieve/Hilbert-inequality geometry; Montgomery and Vaughan's *Hilbert's Inequality* (J. London Math. Soc. (2) 8 (1974), 73--82, DOI `10.1112/jlms/s2-8.1.73`) is already recorded in `SOURCES.md` as a durable source for that geometry. No novelty is claimed for those general tools.

A fresh literature check also revisited Biao Wang's September 2026 short-interval pair-correlation theorem, arXiv:2609.07918. As audited in `NB-240`, its zero intervals have power length `T^theta` and its fixed Montgomery tests live at the mean-spacing normalization. It does not provide the depth-marked carrier-band estimate (21) in a shrinking `1/H` selected-height window. No newer source found in this pass supplies such a theorem.

The line-local content here is the exact combination of `NB-249`'s legally projected finite zero current with `NB-246`'s moving-center Fourier variable. It identifies the Fourier amplitude of the required hard second moment as the marked zero form factor on `X+H supp(phi)` and then proves by the `NB-231` packet that this natural band is too narrow to manufacture decorrelation by itself.

Several boundaries are essential. First, (9)--(20) are written for a finite smooth ordinate truncation; the full-current statement uses the existing Schwartz-tail population control and the stated order of limits. Second, (21) is sufficient rather than necessary. Third, the matched packet is an adversarial control compatible with the source information audited earlier; it is not a claim that the actual zeta zeros realize that geometry. Fourth, this remains a source-side statement and does not by itself close the bridge to the Nyman--Beurling distance.

The practical correction to the search target is therefore narrow. After Hardy projection, a future proof may attack either the exact hard-box quadratic form (17) or the stronger carrier-band norm (21). But it should not expect the `O(H)` shell bandwidth to provide the missing cancellation automatically. The required gain must come from genuinely zeta-specific depth-marked off-diagonal structure inside a frequency band that is still `Theta(J)` times too narrow for generic carrier-cell orthogonality.
