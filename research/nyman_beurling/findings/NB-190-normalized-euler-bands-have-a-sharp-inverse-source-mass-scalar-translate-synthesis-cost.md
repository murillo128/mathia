# NB-190 — Normalized Euler bands have a sharp inverse-source-mass scalar-translate synthesis cost

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Status:** `DERIVED + SOURCE-ACQUISITION + FOURIER-STIELTJES + BAND-SYNTHESIS + SHARP-INVERSE-MASS-TARIFF + NB-189-REFINEMENT`.

`NB-189` identifies independent coefficient-side access to `K asymp 1/a` normalized Euler--Wiener bands as the new resource that makes a full Fourier code possible on Poisson-packed matched controls. It leaves the right next question explicit: can those normalized bands themselves be synthesized from the genuine scalar vertical-translate family of `NB-182` at an affordable acquisition cost?

There is a sharp answer in the natural coefficient topology that uniformly controls every capped-Poisson template. If a normalized probe is concentrated on a subset carrying Euler--Wiener source mass `Theta`, then approximating that probe by a Fourier--Stieltjes mixture of scalar Euler translates costs at least order `1/Theta` in total variation. For the balanced bands of `NB-189`, `Theta asymp a asymp 1/K`, so **each band costs `Theta(K)` scalar-translate total variation**. A smooth Fourier cutoff attains the same order for every fixed positive accuracy, so this inverse-mass tariff is sharp within the unrestricted Fourier--Stieltjes class.

This exposes a genuine extra currency hidden by the coefficient-side routing of `NB-189`. The representation-level density/fanout theorem `NB-188` charges only `sqrt K` RMS source-density gain for the band code, while deriving the individual normalized bands from one scalar Euler observable charges `K` per output coordinate. Equivalently, the independent band oracle hides an additional factor `sqrt K` beyond the sharp representation tariff.

The coefficient topology is not arbitrary. An `ell^1` coefficient error `epsilon` changes the response on **every** template of capped-transport radius `X` by at most `X epsilon`. Hence synthesizing all `K` bands to a fixed error below the `NB-189` Fourier-code constant preserves `Theta(K^(3/2))` nuclear response. Thus scalar Euler translates can recover the band code, but only after paying the linear-in-`K` band-synthesis tariff.

There is also a high-ordinate consequence under the uniform source-safe normalization of `NB-182`. A scalar sample normalized by the one-line bound carries only the gain

\[
\kappa_T\asymp \frac{1}{aH_T},
\qquad
H_T:=\min\left\{a^{-1},\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}\right\}.
\]

Since an `NB-189` band has source mass `Theta(a)`, any mixture of these uniformly normalized high-ordinate samples that approximates one normalized band must have total variation `Omega(H_T)`. Bounded-TV high-ordinate scalar mixing therefore cannot supply even one of the independent normalized band coordinates when `H_T -> infinity`.

## Setup

Use the normalized Euler--Wiener decomposition of `NB-182`. Put

\[
\lambda_n:=\log n,
\qquad
M_a(\lambda):=\max\{2,a\lambda\},
\]

\[
\phi_{n,a}(u)
:=
\frac{e^{-iu\lambda_n}}{M_a(\lambda_n)},
\qquad
\alpha_{n,a}
:=
\frac{\Lambda(n)n^{-1-a}M_a(\lambda_n)}{\mathcal W(a)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Then

\[
\alpha_{n,a}\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge2}\alpha_{n,a}=1,
\tag{2}
\]

and every `phi_(n,a)` is unit contractive for

\[
d_a(u,v)=\min\left\{1,\frac{|u-v|}{a}\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

For a finite complex Borel measure `mu` on the height variable, the normalized scalar Euler translate mixture is

\[
\Psi_{a,\mu}(u)
=
\sum_{n\ge2}
\alpha_{n,a}
\widehat\mu(\lambda_n)
\phi_{n,a}(u),
\tag{4}
\]

where

\[
\widehat\mu(\lambda)
:=
\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-it\lambda}\,d\mu(t).
\tag{5}
\]

The elementary Fourier--Stieltjes bound is

\[
|\widehat\mu(\lambda)|\le\|\mu\|_{TV}.
\tag{6}
\]

Now let `S` be any set of Euler channels with normalized source mass

\[
\Theta_S
:=
\sum_{n\in S}\alpha_{n,a}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The corresponding normalized positive source projection is

\[
F_S(u)
:=
\frac1{\Theta_S}
\sum_{n\in S}
\alpha_{n,a}\phi_{n,a}(u).
\tag{8}
\]

Its coefficient row has absolute Wiener mass one.

Define the coefficient synthesis error

\[
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
:=
\sum_{n\ge2}
\alpha_{n,a}
\left|
\widehat\mu(\lambda_n)
-
\frac{\mathbf 1_S(n)}{\Theta_S}
\right|.
\tag{9}
\]

This is exactly the `ell^1` distance between the coefficient row generated by the scalar translate mixture (4) and the normalized source projection (8).

## Claim 1: normalized source concentration costs inverse source mass

For every finite complex measure `mu` and every `0<=epsilon<1`,

\[
\mathcal E_S(\mu)\le\epsilon
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{
\|\mu\|_{TV}
\ge
\frac{1-\epsilon}{\Theta_S}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

More generally, let `beta=(beta_n)` be any coefficient row supported in `S` with

\[
\sum_{n\in S}|\beta_n|=1.
\tag{11}
\]

If

\[
\sum_n
\left|
\alpha_{n,a}\widehat\mu(\lambda_n)-\beta_n
\right|
\le\epsilon,
\tag{12}
\]

then the same lower bound (10) holds.

### Derivation

From (12), restricted to `S`,

\[
\sum_{n\in S}
\alpha_{n,a}
|\widehat\mu(\lambda_n)|
\ge
\sum_{n\in S}|\beta_n|-\epsilon
\ge
1-\epsilon.
\tag{13}
\]

On the other hand, (6) gives

\[
\sum_{n\in S}
\alpha_{n,a}
|\widehat\mu(\lambda_n)|
\le
\Theta_S\|\mu\|_{TV}.
\tag{14}
\]

Equations (13)--(14) prove (10).

The mechanism is deliberately elementary. A scalar vertical mixture can rephase all source channels through one Fourier--Stieltjes multiplier, but the multiplier amplitude is globally bounded by the measure total variation. Renormalizing source mass `Theta_S` to unit coefficient mass therefore forces amplitude at least `1/Theta_S` somewhere on that source subset.

## Claim 2: the NB-189 band tariff is `Theta(K)` and is sharp at fixed accuracy

Use the `NB-189` bands

\[
S_{k,a}
=
\{n:\ L_k\le\log n<L_k+\Delta\},
\qquad
0\le k<K,
\tag{15}
\]

where

\[
L_k=\frac{r_0}{a}+k\Delta,
\qquad
K\asymp a^{-1},
\qquad
0<r_0<r_1<2,
\tag{16}
\]

and let

\[
\theta_{k,a}
:=
\sum_{n\in S_{k,a}}\alpha_{n,a}.
\tag{17}
\]

`NB-189` proves uniformly in `k` that

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{k,a}\asymp a\asymp K^{-1}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Its band probe is exactly

\[
F_{k,a}(u)
=
\frac1{\theta_{k,a}}
\sum_{n\in S_{k,a}}
\alpha_{n,a}\phi_{n,a}(u).
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore Claim 1 immediately gives, for every fixed `0<=epsilon<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{S_{k,a}}(\mu)\le\epsilon
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|\mu\|_{TV}
\gg_{\epsilon,r_0,r_1,\Delta}
K.
}
\tag{20}
\]

For every **fixed positive** error `epsilon`, this order is attainable.

Choose `eta>0` small compared with `Delta`, and choose a smooth cutoff

\[
\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R),
\qquad
0\le\chi\le1,
\tag{21}
\]

such that

\[
\chi(x)=1
\quad(0\le x\le\Delta),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{supp}\chi
\subset[-\eta,\Delta+\eta].
\tag{22}
\]

Let `g` be its inverse Fourier transform in the convention

\[
\chi(\lambda)
=
\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-it\lambda}g(t)\,dt.
\tag{23}
\]

Because `chi` is smooth and compactly supported, `g` is Schwartz and

\[
C_{\chi}:=\|g\|_1<\infty.
\tag{24}
\]

Define the absolutely continuous measure

\[
d\mu_{k,a}(t)
:=
\frac{e^{iL_kt}}{\theta_{k,a}}
 g(t)\,dt.
\tag{25}
\]

Then

\[
\widehat\mu_{k,a}(\lambda)
=
\frac{\chi(\lambda-L_k)}{\theta_{k,a}},
\tag{26}
\]

and hence the scalar translate mixture matches the target coefficients **exactly on the whole band** `S_(k,a)`.

The only coefficient error lies in the two logarithmic collars

\[
[L_k-\eta,L_k)
\cup
[L_k+\Delta,L_k+\Delta+\eta].
\tag{27}
\]

For small `a` these collars remain in the `a log n<2` regime. The same PNT calculation used in `NB-189` gives their total normalized Euler--Wiener mass as

\[
O(a\eta)
\tag{28}
\]

uniformly in `k`, while `theta_(k,a) asymp a Delta`. Consequently

\[
\mathcal E_{S_{k,a}}(\mu_{k,a})
\ll_{r_0,r_1,\Delta}
\frac{\eta}{\Delta}.
\tag{29}
\]

Given any fixed `epsilon>0`, choose `eta=eta(epsilon,Delta)>0` so that the right side of (29) is at most `epsilon`. Then

\[
\|\mu_{k,a}\|_{TV}
=
\frac{C_\chi}{\theta_{k,a}}
\ll_{\epsilon,r_0,r_1,\Delta}
K.
\tag{30}
\]

Combining (20) and (30), for every fixed `0<epsilon<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\mathcal E_{S_{k,a}}(\mu)\le\epsilon}
\|\mu\|_{TV}
\asymp_{\epsilon,r_0,r_1,\Delta}
K
\asymp
\frac1a,
}
\tag{31}
\]

uniformly over the `NB-189` bands.

No exact-synthesis upper bound is claimed at `epsilon=0`; the sharp statement (31) is a fixed-positive-accuracy theorem.

## Claim 3: synthesizing all bands costs RMS gain `Theta(K)`

\[
\mathcal E_{S_{k,a}}(\mu_k)\le\epsilon
\qquad(0\le k<K)
\tag{32}

for a fixed `0<epsilon<1`. Put

\[
Q_{\rm band}

\qquad
G_{\rm band}
:=
\frac{Q_{\rm band}}{\sqrt K}.

Then (31) gives

\[
\boxed{

G_{\rm band}\asymp K,

\tag{34}

where the upper bound is realized by the independent smooth-cutoff measures (25).

This is strictly more expensive than the representation tariff isolated in `NB-188`. There the same disjoint balanced band routing has

\tag{35}

Thus

\[
\boxed{

\tag{36}

The missing factor `sqrt K` is exactly the price of manufacturing `K` independently normalized coefficient-side spectral projections from one scalar translate family rather than declaring those projections available as primitive observables.

## Claim 4: coefficient synthesis preserves the NB-189 nuclear code

The coefficient norm above controls every capped-Poisson response uniformly. Let `nu` be any zero-mass template with

\mathsf D_a(\nu)\le qX.
\tag{37}
\]

\[
\boxed{

\left|
\int (G-F)\,d\nu
\right|
\le X\epsilon.

\tag{38}

Now use the `K` Poisson-packed templates from `NB-189`. Let `A` be their response matrix to the exact band probes and `A^(mu)` the response to scalar-mixture approximants satisfying (32). Entrywise,

\[
|A^{(\mu)}_{ik}-A_{ik}|
\le X\epsilon.
\tag{39}
\]

Hence

\[
\|A^{(\mu)}-A\|_F
\le X\epsilon K,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|A^{(\mu)}-A\|_*
\le
X\epsilon K^{3/2}.
}
\tag{40}

`NB-189` gives

\[
\|A\|_*
\ge
\left(c_0-o(1)\right)XK^{3/2},

c_0:=\frac{\operatorname{Si}(\pi)}{2\pi}>0.
\tag{41}

Thus every fixed

\[

\tag{42}

\left(c_0-\epsilon-o(1)\right)

So the `Theta(K)` scalar-synthesis upper construction is not merely a coefficient approximation with no geometric consequence. At sufficiently small fixed accuracy it actually transports the full `NB-189` growing nuclear certificate into the scalar Fourier--Stieltjes translate class.

The converse is deliberately **not** claimed: another scalar multiplier family might reproduce a large response matrix on the chosen templates without approximating the band coefficients in `ell^1`. Claim 4 shows that coefficient-faithful synthesis is sufficient for preserving the code, not necessary.

## Claim 5: uniform high-ordinate source normalization still charges `Omega(H_T)` per band

`NB-182` defines the source-safe high-ordinate normalization

\[
H_T
:=
\min\left\{a^{-1},\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}\right\},
\tag{44}
\]

and, up to a constant depending only on the fixed support radius,

\[
\kappa_T
:=
\frac{\mathcal W(a)}{H_T}
\asymp
\frac1{aH_T}.
\tag{45}
\]

A unit source-normalized scalar height sample therefore has normalized Euler coefficients

\[
\kappa_T\alpha_{n,a}e^{-it\lambda_n}.
\tag{46}
\]

Let `nu` be a finite complex measure supported on the high-ordinate block where this uniform normalization is valid. Its mixture has coefficient multiplier `kappa_T hat nu(lambda_n)`. If it approximates one `NB-189` band in the same coefficient norm with error at most `epsilon<1`, Claim 1 applied after the gain `kappa_T` gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\nu\|_{TV}
\ge
\frac{1-\epsilon}
{\kappa_T\theta_{k,a}}
\asymp
H_T.
}
\tag{47}
\]

Here we used

\[
\theta_{k,a}\asymp a,
\qquad
\mathcal W(a)\asymp a^{-1}.
\tag{48}
\]

Therefore, whenever `H_T -> infinity`, no bounded-total-variation mixture of the uniformly normalized scalar high-ordinate source can synthesize even one independently normalized `NB-189` band to fixed coefficient accuracy.

This does not contradict `NB-183`--`NB-184`. Those findings show that selected heights can have substantially smaller **local** capped-Euler norm than the uniform `H_T` bound. Renormalizing by such height-specific norms can provide more acquisition gain. Equation (47) prices the uniform source-safe architecture only; exploiting better local norms is an additional arithmetic resource and remains an escape.

## What this resolves after NB-189

`NB-189` showed that positive source mass, true Euler amplitudes, bounded fanout and the sharp `sqrt K` source-density tariff are still insufficient to distinguish arithmetic from a Poisson-packed matched control if coefficient-side spectral bands are available independently.

The present result shows that those independent bands are **not free consequences of the scalar Euler observable**. Within the exact Fourier--Stieltjes translate class of `NB-182`, a balanced normalized band has a sharp fixed-accuracy synthesis norm `Theta(K)`. Consequently the `NB-189` coefficient-side architecture hides a stronger acquisition resource than its internal Wiener row norm suggests.

At the same time, the result is not a new no-go for phase codes as such. The smooth-cutoff construction proves that scalar translates really can manufacture the `NB-189` band probes, and therefore their DFT-scale response, if linear `K`-scale total variation per coordinate is allowed. The frontier has moved from

> can scalar Euler data generate the bands?

to the quantitative question

> can the actual Nyman source obtain the required code without paying this `K`-scale global synthesis norm, for example through height-local arithmetic cancellation or through a different non-band scalar multiplier family?

A second unresolved issue is acquisition locality. The upper construction (25) is an unrestricted Fourier--Stieltjes measure on the height variable. It does not keep the acquisition measure inside one high block `[T,2T]`. Requiring both high-ordinate localization and an approximately flat normalized spectral band is an additional time--frequency extrapolation problem and may cost more than (31). No such stronger uncertainty bound is claimed here.

## Self-review

The strongest possible overclaim would be to say that **every** scalar-translate phase code costs `Theta(K)` per coordinate. That is not proved. Equation (31) prices faithful synthesis of the normalized `NB-189` spectral bands in a coefficient norm strong enough to control every capped-Poisson template. A different scalar Fourier--Stieltjes mask may agree on the realized response matrix while having very different coefficients.

A second overclaim would be to identify `Theta(K)` with computational complexity. It is a total-variation norm in the source acquisition representation. It measures signed/complex linear amplification of scalar vertical samples, not wall-clock cost, sample count, or numerical precision.

The fixed-accuracy upper bound uses smooth collars around each logarithmic band. As `epsilon -> 0`, the cutoff constant `C_chi` may grow; no uniform `epsilon -> 0` asymptotic and no exact finite-TV band projector are asserted.

The high-ordinate bound (47) uses the uniform one-line source normalization from `NB-182`. It does not exclude exceptional or density-one subsets with better local normalization, nor does it address the extra cost of forcing the synthesis measure itself to remain inside one high block.

Finally, none of the band-synthesis statements proves that the actual realized Nyman template range has the Poisson-packed geometry used by `NB-189`. Source acquisition and destination conditioning remain separate, exactly as required by the current line frontier.
