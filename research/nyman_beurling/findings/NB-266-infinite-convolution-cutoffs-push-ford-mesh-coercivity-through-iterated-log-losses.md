# NB-266 - Infinite-convolution cutoffs push Ford mesh coercivity through iterated-log losses

## Result

NB-265 localized the exact Euler-anchor reconstruction to a Ford mesh radius

\[
B_K=O_\epsilon((\log K)^{1+\epsilon})
\]

by replacing a generic smooth cutoff with a compact Gevrey cutoff. Its explicit next question was whether a finer nonquasianalytic cutoff can approach the logarithmic endpoint without changing the exact reconstruction identity.

The answer is yes, up to any fixed finite hierarchy of iterated-log losses.

Fix an integer \(r\ge 1\) and \(\epsilon>0\). Define, for sufficiently large \(t\),

\[
L_{r,\epsilon}(t)
:=
\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\log^{(j)}t\right)
\bigl(\log^{(r)}t\bigr)^{1+\epsilon},
\]

where \(\log^{(j)}\) denotes the \(j\)-fold iterated logarithm and the empty product is one. There exists a compact \(C^\infty\) plateau cutoff \(\chi_{r,\epsilon}\), equal to one on \([0,1]\), whose Fourier-Laplace transform obeys on every fixed horizontal strip

\[
|\widehat\chi_{r,\epsilon}(\xi+i\eta)|
\le
C_A
\exp\!\left(
-c_A\frac{|\xi|}{L_{r,\epsilon}(|\xi|)}
\right),
\qquad |\eta|\le A.
\]

Substituting this cutoff into the exact NB-265 reconstruction gives the following coercive radius:

\[
\boxed{
B_K
=
C_{r,\epsilon}\,
\log K\,L_{r,\epsilon}(\log K).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
B_K
\asymp
\log K
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{r}\log^{(j)}K\right)
\bigl(\log^{(r+1)}K\bigr)^{1+\epsilon}.
\]

For example,

\[
\begin{aligned}
r=1:&\quad
B_K\asymp
\log K\,(\log\log K)^{1+\epsilon},\\
r=2:&\quad
B_K\asymp
\log K\,\log\log K\,
(\log\log\log K)^{1+\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every fixed \(\delta>0\), this satisfies

\[
B_K=o((\log K)^{1+\delta}).
\]

Thus the unknown region left by NB-265 is not the whole interval between logarithmic and polylogarithmic radius. Bounded resolved carrier cost forces enough response to reconstruct the Euler anchor already within a logarithmic radius multiplied by an arbitrarily deep fixed iterated-log loss.

The pure \(O(\log K)\) endpoint remains open.

## 1. Exact reconstruction input

Keep the NB-265 setup unchanged:

\[
J=mK,
\qquad
m\to\infty,
\qquad
K\to\infty,
\]

and

\[
C_J(w)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}c_j e^{ijw/J},
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}c_j=1,
\qquad
\|c\|_2\le M.
\]

At the live shallow depth

\[
u=\log m+d_*,
\]

write

\[
\alpha=\frac{u}{YJ},
\qquad
\Delta_m=\frac{2\pi q}{Ym},
\]

and sample on the carrier-locked mesh

\[
w_n
=
\frac{2\pi q n}{Y}+i\frac{u}{Y}.
\]

Then

\[
D_n:=C_J(w_n)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}
c_j e^{ijn\Delta_m}e^{-\alpha j}.
\]

As in NB-265, choose a compact plateau \(\chi\) with \(\chi=1\) on \([0,1]\), set

\[
g_\alpha(t)=\chi(t)e^{\alpha Kt},
\]

periodize it on the mesh period, and denote its Fourier coefficients by \(d_n^{(\alpha)}\). The Fourier series evaluated at \(j/K\) cancels the depth factor exactly, so

\[
\boxed{
1=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}d_n^{(\alpha)}D_n.
}
\]

Nothing in the present finding changes that identity. The only change from NB-265 is the auxiliary plateau used to localize its dual coefficients.

Because \(u=O(\log m)\) while \(J=mK\), the imaginary Fourier shift generated by \(e^{\alpha Kt}\) is

\[
\alpha K=\frac{u}{Ym}=o(1).
\]

Hence it is enough to construct a plateau whose Fourier transform has the desired decay uniformly on fixed horizontal strips.

## 2. A compact cutoff with near-exponential iterated-log decay

Fix \(r\) and \(\epsilon\). For large integer \(j\), define

\[
a_j
=
\frac{a_0}{jL_{r,\epsilon}(j)}.
\]

The final iterated logarithm occurs with exponent \(1+\epsilon\), so the integral test gives

\[
\sum_j a_j<\infty.
\]

Choose \(a_0>0\) so that

\[
\sigma:=\sum_j a_j<\frac14.
\]

Let \(\mu_j\) be the uniform probability measure on

\[
[-a_j/2,a_j/2]
\]

and let

\[
\mu=*_{j\ge j_0}\mu_j.
\]

Since the support diameters are summable, the infinite convolution exists as a compactly supported probability measure with

\[
\operatorname{supp}\mu
\subset[-\sigma/2,\sigma/2].
\]

Its Fourier-Laplace transform factors as

\[
\widehat\mu(z)
=
\prod_{j\ge j_0}
\operatorname{sinc}\!\left(\frac{a_jz}{2}\right).
\]

The product gives the required decay directly.

### Strip-decay estimate

Fix \(A>0\), write \(z=x+i\eta\), and assume \(|\eta|\le A\). For each factor,

\[
\left|
\operatorname{sinc}\!\left(\frac{a_jz}{2}\right)
\right|
\le
\exp(Aa_j/2)
\min\!\left(1,\frac{C}{a_j|x|}\right)
\]

once \(|x|\) is large; changing \(C\) absorbs the bounded transition range. The product of the exponential strip factors is harmless because

\[
\prod_j e^{Aa_j/2}
=e^{A\sigma/2}.
\]

Set

\[
N
=\left\lfloor
c\frac{|x|}{L_{r,\epsilon}(|x|)}
\right\rfloor
\]

with a sufficiently small fixed \(c>0\). For this finite iterated-log function,

\[
L_{r,\epsilon}(N)
\sim
L_{r,\epsilon}(|x|),
\]

because \(N=|x|^{1-o(1)}\). Consequently, for every \(j\in[N/2,N]\),

\[
a_j|x|
\asymp
\frac{|x|}{N L_{r,\epsilon}(N)}
\asymp
\frac1c.
\]

Taking \(c\) small enough forces each of these \(\asymp N\) factors below a common constant \(\rho<1\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat\mu(x+i\eta)|
\le
C_A
\exp\!\left(
-c_A\frac{|x|}{L_{r,\epsilon}(|x|)}
\right),
\qquad |\eta|\le A.
}
\]

This decay dominates every inverse power of \(|x|\). Fourier inversion therefore implies that \(\mu\) has a compactly supported \(C^\infty\) density, say \(\psi\).

Now define

\[
\chi
=
\mathbf 1_{[-\sigma/2,\;1+\sigma/2]}*\psi.
\]

Then \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\),

\[
0\le\chi\le1,
\qquad
\chi(t)=1
\quad(0\le t\le1),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{supp}\chi
\subset[-\sigma,1+\sigma].
\]

The extra interval factor costs only a polynomial Fourier factor, so after weakening the constants,

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat\chi(x+i\eta)|
\le
C_A
\exp\!\left(
-c_A\frac{|x|}{L_{r,\epsilon}(|x|)}
\right)
}
\]

uniformly for \(|\eta|\le A\).

This is the only new analytic input.

## 3. Reconstruction coefficients and their tail

For large \(m\), the fixed support of \(g_\alpha\) fits inside one period because the period is \(\asymp m\). Its Fourier coefficients therefore satisfy, up to the fixed Fourier normalization,

\[
d_n^{(\alpha)}
=
\frac1{L_m}
\widehat g_\alpha(n\Delta_m),
\qquad
L_m\asymp m.
\]

The depth tilt is a bounded imaginary shift of \(\widehat\chi\), so the strip estimate gives

\[
\boxed{
|d_n^{(\alpha)}|
\le
\frac{C}{m}
\exp\!\left(
-c\frac{|n\Delta_m|}
{L_{r,\epsilon}(|n\Delta_m|)}
\right).
}
\]

Let

\[
\Phi(t)=\frac{t}{L_{r,\epsilon}(t)}.
\]

For large \(t\), \(\Phi\) is increasing and

\[
\Phi'(t)\asymp\frac1{L_{r,\epsilon}(t)}.
\]

Since the mesh spacing is \(\Delta_m\asymp1/m\), the prefactor \(1/m\) in \(d_n\) cancels the lattice density. Comparing the tail to an integral yields

\[
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|>B}|d_n^{(\alpha)}|
\ll
L_{r,\epsilon}(B)
\exp(-c\Phi(B)).
\]

The slowly varying prefactor can be absorbed by weakening \(c\), giving

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|>B}|d_n^{(\alpha)}|
\le
C
\exp\!\left(
-c'\frac{B}{L_{r,\epsilon}(B)}
\right).
}
\]

Now choose

\[
B_K
=C_0\log K\,L_{r,\epsilon}(\log K).
\]

Finite iterated logarithms are slowly varying strongly enough that

\[
L_{r,\epsilon}(B_K)
\sim
L_{r,\epsilon}(\log K).
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{B_K}{L_{r,\epsilon}(B_K)}
\sim C_0\log K.
\]

Choosing \(C_0\) sufficiently large gives

\[
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|>B_K}|d_n^{(\alpha)}|
=o(K^{-1/2}).
\]

The crude carrier bound

\[
|D_n|\le\|c\|_2\sqrt K\le M\sqrt K
\]

therefore shows that the omitted part of the exact reconstruction is \(o(1)\):

\[
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|>B_K}d_n^{(\alpha)}D_n=o(1).
\]

Thus

\[
\left|
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|\le B_K}
d_n^{(\alpha)}D_n
\right|
\ge1-o(1).
\]

## 4. Coercive response inside the iterated-log radius

The total coefficient mass remains uniformly bounded,

\[
\sum_n|d_n^{(\alpha)}|=O(1).
\]

Consequently the truncated identity forces a pointwise response

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|n\Delta_m|\le B_K}|D_n|
\ge c>0.
}
\]

More importantly for the Ford residue, the coefficientwise bound

\[
|d_n^{(\alpha)}|\le\frac{C}{m}
\]

turns the same identity into an aggregate lower bound:

\[
1-o(1)
\le
\frac{C}{m}
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|\le B_K}|D_n|.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|n\Delta_m|\le B_K}|D_n|
\gg m.
}
\]

This is the quantity needed by the matched Ford compatibility control. The improvement is therefore not merely a better location for one exceptional mesh value; the full \(\Omega(m)\) response needed to compensate the live damping remains localized inside the smaller radius.

## 5. Return to physical Ford coordinates

The physical Ford coordinate attached to the mesh is

\[
x_n=m n\Delta_m.
\]

Thus

\[
|n\Delta_m|\le B_K
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|x_n|\ll mB_K.
\]

Because \(B_K=K^{o(1)}=o(K)\),

\[
\frac{mB_K}{J}
=
\frac{B_K}{K}
\to0.
\]

Therefore the entire reconstructed region remains in the same local shell patch as NB-265, where

\[
F\!\left(
\frac{x_n}{J}+i\frac{u}{YJ}
\right)
=F(0)+o(1)
\]

uniformly.

The number of carrier-locked sites in the region is

\[
O(mB_K)=o(J),
\]

so the population ledger used in NB-264 and NB-265 is unchanged.

Partition the values \(D_n\) into a fixed number of angular cones. One cone retains \(\Omega(m)\) of the aggregate modulus. Place the same matched compatibility packet on those carrier-locked sites,

\[
\gamma_n
=t_0+\frac{2\pi qn}{X},
\qquad
X(1-\beta_n)=\log m+d_*.
\]

The large carrier phase is exactly locked and

\[
e^{-X(1-\beta_n)}
=\frac{e^{-d_*}}m.
\]

After cone selection, the localized response therefore contributes

\[
|\mathcal R_S|
\gg
\frac1m
\sum_{n\in S}|D_n|
\gg1.
\]

As before, this is a matched compatibility control: it shows that the audited source-side information and zero-density ledger do not exclude an order-one residue. It does **not** assert that the actual zeros of \(\zeta\) occupy the constructed sites.

## 6. What changed relative to NB-265

NB-265 used a fixed Gevrey order \(s=1+\epsilon\), giving Fourier decay

\[
\exp(-c|\xi|^{1/s})
\]

and reconstruction radius

\[
(\log K)^s.
\]

The present construction uses summable convolution widths just beyond the nonquasianalytic threshold. This replaces the power loss by a slowly varying loss:

\[
\exp\!\left(-c\frac{|\xi|}{L_{r,\epsilon}(|\xi|)}\right),
\]

which in turn gives

\[
B_K
\asymp
\log K\,L_{r,\epsilon}(\log K).
\]

Because \(r\) may be any **fixed** positive integer, the loss can be pushed through an arbitrarily deep fixed iterated-log hierarchy. However, \(r\) and \(\epsilon\) must be fixed before \(K\to\infty\). This finding does not justify a diagonal choice \(r=r(K)\).

The endpoint \(B_K=O(\log K)\) is also not proved. In this explicit convolution family, removing the final \((\log^{(r)}j)^{\epsilon}\) summability surplus makes

\[
\sum_j\frac1{jL(j)}
\]

diverge, so compact support is lost. That is an obstruction to this construction, not a theorem that every possible dual reconstruction must lose more than a logarithm.

## 7. Representation audit

The source representation is unchanged from NB-265. The raw scalar carrier remains

\[
C_J(w)=\sum c_j e^{ijw/J},
\]

with Euler normalization \(C_J(0)=1\) and bounded resolved coefficient norm \(\|c\|_2\le M\). The live evaluation remains the depth-tilted Ford mesh \(w_n=2\pi qn/Y+i u/Y\), with \(u=\log m+d_*\).

The new object \(\chi_{r,\epsilon}\) is **not** a source, a zero statistic, or an approximation to \(\zeta\). It is an auxiliary dual cutoff used only to reconstruct the invariant anchor \(C_J(0)=1\) from the sampled values \(D_n\). Convolving compact probability kernels changes the localization profile of that dual representation but preserves the exact plateau value on \([0,1]\), so the anchor identity remains exact.

The scale conversion is also unchanged:

\[
\text{dual radius }B_K
\quad\mapsto\quad
\text{physical Ford radius }mB_K.
\]

The useful truth preserved is the exact Euler normalization and the aggregate \(\Omega(m)\) response forced by it. What is deliberately discarded is any claim about the phases or locations of actual zeta zeros. The final packet remains adversarial compatibility data only.

## 8. Prior-art audit

The generic harmonic-analysis mechanism is not new. Classical Ingham-type nonquasianalytic Fourier-decay theory studies how compact support can coexist with decay arbitrarily close to exponential, subject to an integrability threshold. A. E. Ingham's *A Note on Fourier Transforms* appeared in the *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* 9 (1934), 29-32. Tamer Tlas later gave explicit compact smooth bumps with controlled subexponential Fourier decay in *Journal of Approximation Theory* 278 (2022), article 105742.

Neither source supplies the Mathia-specific bridge used here: the exact Euler-anchor identity on the rank-coupled Ford mesh, the \(J=mK\) scaling, the live depth \(u=\log m+O(1)\), or the conversion of dual localization into an \(\Omega(m)\) matched Ford residue. The proof above constructs the required cutoff directly, so no external theorem is load-bearing and `SOURCES.md` does not need a new dependency.

## 9. Owner-side adversarial review

The main failure modes are quantifier and representation errors.

First, \(r\) and \(\epsilon\) are fixed. Allowing either to vary with \(K\) would require uniform constants in the convolution decay and is not justified here. The conclusion is therefore "arbitrarily deep fixed iterated-log loss", not a single cutoff beating every such loss simultaneously.

Second, the complex depth tilt is harmless only because \(\alpha K=u/(Ym)=o(1)\). The strip estimate was checked before inserting the cutoff into the reconstruction; real-axis Fourier decay alone would not be sufficient.

Third, the lattice tail keeps its normalization. The Fourier coefficient carries \(1/L_m\asymp1/m\), while the mesh has density \(\asymp m\); these factors cancel. Dropping either would incorrectly introduce an additional factor of \(m\) into the localization radius.

Fourth, the construction does not prove a lower bound for the actual Nyman-Beurling distance. It only strengthens the source-side compatibility obstruction already isolated by NB-264 and NB-265. A zeta-specific theorem could still prevent the matched packet from occurring.

Finally, the inability of this convolution family to reach pure exponential Fourier decay does not establish that \(O(\log K)\) coercivity is impossible. It marks the next genuine boundary: either sharpen the dual reconstruction to the logarithmic endpoint by a different near-quasianalytic mechanism, or produce a bounded-cost carrier/annihilator whose response really stays hidden throughout every radius of the form above.

## Consequence

The NB-265 frontier can be narrowed from

\[
O_\epsilon((\log K)^{1+\epsilon})
\]

to

\[
\boxed{
O_{r,\epsilon}\!\left(
\log K
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{r}\log^{(j)}K\right)
(\log^{(r+1)}K)^{1+\epsilon}
\right)
}
\]

for every fixed \(r\ge1\) and \(\epsilon>0\).

So another fixed Gevrey exponent is no longer the useful next experiment. The unresolved scale is now genuinely near-logarithmic: the pure \(O(\log K)\) endpoint, or a construction that survives all fixed iterated-log localization losses without paying unbounded resolved carrier cost.
