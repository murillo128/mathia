## Derivation

Let `B` be the `N`-row Hilbert-valued matrix whose `i`th row is `R_a(nu_i)`. Equation (4)--(5) gives

\[
\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=
\sum_{i=1}^N\|R_a(\nu_i)\|_{\mathcal H_a}^2
\le
NX^2.
\tag{14}
\]

Let `H` be the `K`-row acquisition operator whose `k`th row is `h_k`. Its Hilbert--Schmidt norm is exactly

\[
\|H\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=Q.
\tag{15}
\]

The response factors as

\[
A=BH^*.
\tag{16}
\]

For finite truncations this is an ordinary matrix product, and the Schatten--Holder inequality gives

\[
\|BH^*\|_*
\le
\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|H\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]

Passing through finite coordinate truncations if necessary yields the same inequality in the Hilbert-space formulation. Using (14)--(15) proves (8).

If `||h_k||<=1`, Cauchy--Schwarz and (4) show

\[
|\ell_k^{(h)}(\sigma)|
\le
\frac{\mathsf D_a(\sigma)}q,
\]

so `NB-178` applies. Divide the positive part in its certificate by `sqrt(NK)` and insert (8):

\[
\frac{\|A\|_*}{\sqrt{NK}}
\le XG,
\qquad
\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{NKr}}{\sqrt{NK}}
=\epsilon\sqrt r.
\]

Squaring proves the first inequality in (9), while `G<=1` proves the second. Equations (10)--(11) follow immediately.

For (13), condition (12) gives

\[
\|A\|_*
\ge
\alpha N^{3/2}.
\]

At `N=K`, (8) gives

\[
\|A\|_*
\le
XNG.
\]

Comparing the two inequalities yields (13).

The argument is deliberately insensitive to which Euler coordinates are mixed. Prime coordinates, prime-power towers, broad Dirichlet bands and arbitrary finite or square-summable linear mixtures all obey the same tariff as long as the probes factor through the canonical actual-amplitude Hilbert channel with the declared acquisition norm.
