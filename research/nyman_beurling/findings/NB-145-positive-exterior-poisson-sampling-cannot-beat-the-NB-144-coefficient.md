# NB-145 — positive exterior Poisson sampling cannot beat the NB-144 coefficient

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + SOURCE-SPECIFIC + METHOD-OPTIMALITY + POSITIVE-POISSON-CONE + MULTIPOINT-NO-GAIN + FIXED-INTERIOR-BARRIER + NEAR-ONE-SHARP`.

`NB-144` obtains the local occupancy bound

\[
d_G\le \left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)\log G
\]

for a right-half zero packet centered at `beta_G=1-epsilon_G`, by evaluating the positive zero Poisson kernel once at `1+delta_G+i t_G` and controlling `zeta'/zeta` through the absolutely convergent Euler product. The natural next question is whether sampling the exterior half-plane at many points, or taking positive weighted averages of those samples, can improve the coefficient and force `o(log G)` occupancy at a fixed interior `epsilon`.

It cannot. The coefficient in `NB-144` is the exact asymptotic optimum of the entire **positive exterior Poisson cone** generated by that argument.

Let

\[
0<\epsilon_G\le \frac12,
\qquad
\beta_G:=1-\epsilon_G,
\qquad
L:=\log G,
\tag{1}
\]

and let `t_G in [G,2G]`. Suppose a packet `C_G` consists of `d_G` actual zeta zeros, counted with multiplicity, satisfying

\[
|\beta-\beta_G|\le h_G,
\qquad
|\gamma-t_G|\le h_G,
\tag{2}
\]

with

\[
h_G=o(\epsilon_G).
\tag{3}
\]

For every packet zero `rho=beta+i gamma`, include its functional-equation partner

\[
\widetilde\rho=1-\overline\rho=(1-\beta)+i\gamma.
\tag{4}
\]

Consider an arbitrary nonzero finite positive measure `mu_G` on exterior sample points

\[
s=1+\delta+i v,
\qquad
0<\delta\le1,
\qquad
\frac G2\le |v|\le 3G.
\tag{5}
\]

Write

\[
W_G:=\mu_G(\Omega_G)
\tag{6}
\]

and define the integrated pair charge

\[
Q_G(\rho;\mu_G)
:=
\int_{\Omega_G}
\left[
\frac{1+\delta-\beta}
{(1+\delta-\beta)^2+(v-\gamma)^2}
+
\frac{\delta+\beta}
{(\delta+\beta)^2+(v-\gamma)^2}
\right]d\mu_G.
\tag{7}
\]

The best lower charge that this positive sampler can certify uniformly over the packet is

\[
q_G(\mu_G):=
\inf_{\rho\in C_G}Q_G(\rho;\mu_G).
\tag{8}
\]

If one now uses only the same two ingredients as `NB-144` — positivity of every zero contribution and the pointwise Euler-product majorant

\[
\left|\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1+\delta+i v)\right|
\le \frac1\delta+O(1),
\tag{9}
\]

— then every such positive superposition yields the certificate

\[
\boxed{
 d_G
 \le
 \frac{
 \frac12 L W_G
 +\displaystyle\int \delta^{-1}\,d\mu_G
 +O(W_G)
 }
 {q_G(\mu_G)}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

The key point is that the denominator in `(10)` has a universal ceiling. Since `x/(x^2+y^2)<=1/x` for `x>0`, every pair in `(2)` satisfies pointwise

\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\frac{1+\delta-\beta}
{(1+\delta-\beta)^2+(v-\gamma)^2}
+
\frac{\delta+\beta}
{(\delta+\beta)^2+(v-\gamma)^2}
\\[3pt]
&\qquad\le
\frac1{\epsilon_G-h_G}
+
\frac1{1-\epsilon_G-h_G}
=
\frac{1+o(1)}{\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
q_G(\mu_G)
\le
\frac{1+o(1)}{\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)}W_G.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Substituting `(12)` into the *best possible right-hand side furnished by the proof template* `(10)` gives the method floor

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive-Poisson certificate}
\ \ge\ 
\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)
\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)L.
}
\tag{13}
\]

In particular, when `epsilon_G=epsilon` is fixed in `(0,1/2]`, **no number of exterior samples, no adaptive placement of them, and no positive weighting of them can improve the leading coefficient**

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}2
}
\tag{14}
\]

obtained by `NB-144`. The one-point proof is already asymptotically optimal in this whole positive class.

The same statement is sharp in the moving near-one regime. If

\[
\epsilon_G L\to\infty,
\qquad
h_G=o(\epsilon_G),
\tag{15}
\]

choose the one-point measure at

\[
v=t_G,
\qquad
\delta_G:=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_G}{L}}.
\tag{16}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\delta_G}{\epsilon_G}
=\frac1{\sqrt{\epsilon_G L}}\to0,
\qquad
\frac{\delta_G^{-1}}L
=\frac1{\sqrt{\epsilon_G L}}\to0,
\tag{17}
\]

and `(7)` is uniformly

\[
Q_G(\rho;\delta_G,t_G)
=
\frac{1+o(1)}{\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)}.
\tag{18}
\]

Thus `(10)` attains

\[
\boxed{
 d_G
 \le
 \left(\frac12+o(1)\right)
 \epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)L,
}
\tag{19}
\]

exactly the `NB-144` bound. Equations `(13)` and `(19)` therefore identify its coefficient as a minimax value for this proof architecture, not an artifact of using only one sample.

## 1. Positive superposition gives exactly one weighted budget

For `s=1+delta+i v` with `(5)`, the Hadamard logarithmic-derivative identity used in `NB-144` gives

\[
\sum_\rho
\frac{1+\delta-\beta}
{(1+\delta-\beta)^2+(v-\gamma)^2}
=
\frac12\log|v|
+
\Re\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1+\delta+i v)
+O(1).
\tag{20}
\]

All zero terms on the left are nonnegative. Integrating `(20)` against `mu_G`, retaining only each packet zero and its reflected partner, and using `(9)` gives

\[
d_G q_G(\mu_G)
\le
\frac12\int\log|v|\,d\mu_G
+
\int\delta^{-1}\,d\mu_G
+O(W_G).
\tag{21}
\]

Because all sample heights in `(5)` are comparable with `G`,

\[
\int\log|v|\,d\mu_G
=L W_G+O(W_G),
\tag{22}
\]

which proves `(10)`.

Nothing in this step assumes discrete sampling. Continuous averages, arbitrarily many sample points and `G`-dependent adaptive positive weights are all included.

## 2. A reflected pair has a hard charge ceiling

For one right-half packet zero, put

\[
x_1:=1+\delta-\beta,
\qquad
x_2:=\delta+\beta.
\tag{23}
\]

From `(2)`--`(3)`,

\[
x_1\ge\epsilon_G-h_G,
\qquad
x_2\ge1-\epsilon_G-h_G.
\tag{24}
\]

For every real `y`,

\[
0<\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}\le\frac1x.
\tag{25}
\]

Applying `(25)` to both members of the functional-equation pair gives `(11)`. Equality is approached only by observing vertically on top of the packet and sending `delta` to zero relative to `epsilon_G`. Therefore adding more positive samples can only average charges that are individually no larger than the boundary charge

\[
\frac1{\epsilon_G}
+
\frac1{1-\epsilon_G}
=
\frac1{\epsilon_G(1-\epsilon_G)}.
\tag{26}
\]

This is the geometric reason the coefficient in `NB-144` cannot be improved inside the positive cone.

## 3. The Euler-product remainder and the charge ceiling are simultaneously sharp

To approach `(26)` one must take `delta_G=o(epsilon_G)`. To keep the absolute Euler-product remainder in `(9)` negligible against `L`, one also needs

\[
\delta_G^{-1}=o(L).
\tag{27}
\]

A scale satisfying both exists exactly under the natural compatibility condition

\[
\epsilon_G L\to\infty.
\tag{28}
\]

The choice `(16)` balances the two requirements. It is precisely the scale already used in `NB-144`. Thus the earlier proof did not merely make a convenient choice of exterior point: it chose an asymptotically optimal point for the full positive-sampling problem.

For fixed `epsilon`, `(28)` is automatic and one may take `delta_G=L^{-1/2}`. For `epsilon_G->0`, `(28)` is the same condition under which `NB-144` obtains its moving-edge estimate, and the Vinogradov--Korobov zero-free region guarantees it for actual zeros at height `G`.

## 4. Consequence for the research frontier

`NB-144` left open whether a stronger multi-point exterior argument might improve the fixed-interior `O(log G)` local occupancy law. `NB-145` closes that route **for every argument that preserves zero-side positivity and controls the prime side only by the pointwise absolute Euler-product bound**.

At fixed `epsilon>0`, the positive Poisson cone has an unavoidable `Theta(log G)` floor. To get `o(log G)` one must therefore introduce information that is absent from this cone, for example:

- a signed or oscillatory combination of exterior identities, together with enough control to handle the zero terms that then lose positivity;
- cancellation or correlation on the prime side beyond `|zeta'/zeta|<=-zeta'/zeta(sigma)`;
- a different local source law, such as a second-moment or pair-correlation input;
- or a direct Nyman argument showing that the remaining `Theta(log G)` packets are target-bearing even though they are not excluded as zero configurations.

The distinction is important: `(13)` is **not** a lower bound asserting that actual zeta possesses such crowded cells. It is a sharp limitation of the `NB-144` positive-certification architecture. The actual source may satisfy a much stronger local occupancy theorem; proving it requires genuinely new arithmetic information rather than more positive sampling of the same logarithmic-derivative identity.

## Limitations

The method boundary is deliberately narrow and falsifiable. It does not cover signed weights, nonlinear combinations of several logarithmic-derivative samples, mean-square information, correlations between prime-side values, or any argument that controls the contribution of the non-packet zeros after positivity is abandoned. It also does not prove existence of fixed-interior logarithmic-rank packets for the actual zeta divisor.

What it does rule out is the most immediate proposed refinement of `NB-144`: **more exterior sample points with positive weights cannot improve the fixed-interior coefficient at all.** The next source-specific step must spend a genuinely different arithmetic resource.
