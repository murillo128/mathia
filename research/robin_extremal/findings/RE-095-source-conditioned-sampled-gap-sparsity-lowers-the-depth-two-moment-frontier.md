# RE-095 — source-conditioned sampled-gap sparsity lowers the depth-two moment frontier

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CONDITIONAL-SAMPLED-HOST-SPARSITY + MIXED-FIRST/SECOND-LAYER-CHAMBERS + STADLMANN-SECOND-MOMENT + SOURCE-CONDITIONED-FRONTIER + 173/200-BREAKING-TARGET + AMBIENT-EXCEPTIONAL-SET-NONIMPROVEMENT + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`.

`RE-094` identifies the exact limitation of using an **ambient** short-interval exceptional set on the residual mixed first/depth-two cells: if a PNT exceptional set at length `H=X^vartheta` has measure exponent `m(vartheta)`, the resulting false-RH frontier is

\[
\Theta>\max\!\left(\frac12+\vartheta,\,m(\vartheta)\right).
\]

That leaves open the source-conditioned route highlighted by `RE-093`: perhaps one does not need to control all bad starts near scale `X`; one only needs to control the prime-indexed CA sample points

\[
x_q:=\eta_1^{-1}(\eta_2(q))
=\frac{q^2}{2}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac1{\log q}\right)\right),
\qquad q\asymp X^{1/2}.
\]

This finding gives the exact exponent law for such a sampled theorem after combining it with the prime-gap second moment already used in `RE-091`. The gain is sharper than the ambient max law: **any polynomial saving in the number of bad prime hosts below the trivial square-root count lowers the `173/200` moment frontier.** It also proves that this gain cannot be manufactured by simply repackaging the same ambient exceptional-set estimate; a genuine improvement must exploit the prime-indexed/deformed source condition or stronger selector information.

Fix a compact false-RH fan interval

\[
J=[b_0,b_1]\Subset(1-\Theta,1/2)
\]

and a bounded-ratio physical shell `X<=Y_C<2X`. Let `\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)` be the residual mixed cells of `RE-094`: one adjacent support boundary has a first-layer atom, while the opposite boundary has a depth-`2` atom generated by an ordinary prime `q` and no first-layer atom. Put

\[
H=X^{\vartheta},\qquad 0<\vartheta<\frac12.
\]

For fixed harmless shell constants `0<c<C`, define the two-sided bad-host set

\[
\mathcal B_{\vartheta}(X)
:=
\left\{
q\in\mathbb P:\ cX^{1/2}\le q\le CX^{1/2},\
(x_q-H,x_q)\cap\mathbb P=\varnothing
\text{ or }
(x_q,x_q+H)\cap\mathbb P=\varnothing
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose a source-conditioned theorem gives, for some exponent `kappa(vartheta)<=1/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\mathcal B_{\vartheta}(X)
\ll X^{\kappa(\vartheta)+o(1)}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Then the mixed-cell threshold mass satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{C\in\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)}|I_C|
\ll_J
X^{b_1+\vartheta-1/2+o(1)}
+
X^{b_1-77/200+\kappa(\vartheta)/2+o(1)}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently the mixed first/depth-two channel has vanishing threshold measure whenever

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta<\frac12-b_1,
\qquad
b_1<\frac{77}{200}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}2.
}
\tag{4}
\]

At the false-RH exponent level this becomes the sampled-host frontier

\[
\boxed{
\Theta>
\max\!\left(
\frac12+\vartheta,\,
\frac{123}{200}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}2
\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, the trivial host exponent `kappa=1/2` reproduces exactly

\[
\frac{123}{200}+\frac14
=\frac{173}{200}.
\tag{6}
\]

If instead

\[
\kappa(\vartheta)=\frac12-\delta
\qquad(\delta>0),
\]

then the second-moment barrier drops by exactly `delta/2`:

\[
\frac{123}{200}+\frac{\kappa}2
=
\frac{173}{200}-\frac\delta2.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus to improve the present `173/200` frontier it is enough to prove, for **any**

\[
\vartheta<\frac{73}{200},
\]

a polynomial saving

\[
\boxed{
\#\mathcal B_{\vartheta}(X)
\ll X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}
}
\tag{8}
\]

for some fixed `delta>0`. No PNT asymptotic at the sample points is required; mere scarcity of prime-free one-sided intervals already suffices.

## 1. Small chambers retain the deterministic square-root-host cost

For `C in \mathcal M_{1/2}(X)`, write

\[
\Delta_C:=\xi_+(C)-\xi_-(C).
\]

As in `RE-094`, the depth-`2` endpoint is generated by a prime `q\asymp X^{1/2}`. A physical event coordinate borders at most two open support chambers, so

\[
\#\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)\ll X^{1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{9}
\]

The exact CA capacity conversion from `RE-040`, in the form already used in `RE-091` and `RE-094`, is

\[
|I_C|
\ll_J
\frac{\Delta_C X^{b_1-1}}{\log X}.
\tag{10}
\]

For cells with `Delta_C<4H`, equations (9)--(10) give

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{C\in\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)\\\Delta_C<4H}}
|I_C|
\ll_J
X^{b_1+\vartheta-1/2+o(1)}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

This is the first term of (3). It is unchanged from `RE-094`: a source-conditioned theorem only helps on chambers large enough to expose a genuinely bad sampled host.

## 2. Every large mixed chamber produces a bad prime-indexed sample

Pull the open physical chamber back through the first-layer event map,

\[
Q_C:=\eta_1^{-1}\bigl((\xi_-(C),\xi_+(C))\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

`RE-091` gives

\[
Q_C\cap\mathbb P=\varnothing,
\qquad
|Q_C|=(1+o_J(1))\Delta_C.
\tag{13}
\]

For a residual mixed cell the depth-`2` boundary contains no first-layer atom. Therefore its inverse under `eta_1` is precisely the analytic sample point `x_q`; in particular `x_q` is one endpoint of `Q_C`. The orientation may be left or right, which is why (1) includes both one-sided intervals.

If

\[
\Delta_C\ge4H,
\tag{14}
\]

then for sufficiently large `X`, (13) implies that the appropriate one-sided interval of length `H` adjacent to `x_q` is contained in `Q_C`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_C\ge4H
\Longrightarrow
q\in\mathcal B_{\vartheta}(X).
}
\tag{15}
\]

A fixed depth-`2` event can border at most two support chambers. Under (2), the number of large residual cells is therefore

\[
\boxed{
N_X^{\rm large}
\ll X^{\kappa(\vartheta)+o(1)}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

This is where source conditioning enters. The trivial prime-host count gives `kappa=1/2`; any theorem that makes bad prime hosts polynomially sparser creates a genuine new resource.

## 3. Stadlmann converts bad-host count into total physical chamber mass

Distinct exposed cells have disjoint physical chambers, and monotonicity of `eta_1^{-1}` makes the prime-free intervals `Q_C` pairwise disjoint. Each `Q_C` lies inside a consecutive ordinary-prime gap

\[
G_n=(p_n,p_{n+1}),
\qquad d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n,
\]

with `p_n\ll X`. Several `Q_C` may lie in one prime gap; this causes no loss. If `\mathcal G_X` is the set of distinct containing gaps used by the large mixed cells, then

\[
\sum_{C\ {m large}}|Q_C|
\le
\sum_{G_n\in\mathcal G_X}d_n,
\qquad
\#\mathcal G_X\le N_X^{\rm large}.
\tag{17}
\]

The load-bearing external input is still exactly the Stadlmann theorem already anchored by `RE-091`,

\[
\sum_{p_n\le x}d_n^2
\ll_\varepsilon x^{123/100+\varepsilon}.
\tag{18}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz, (13), and (16)--(18) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{C\ {m large}}\Delta_C
&\ll_J
(\#\mathcal G_X)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{p_n\ll X}d_n^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&\ll_{J,\varepsilon}
X^{\kappa(\vartheta)/2+123/200+\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Applying (10),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{C\ {m large}}|I_C|
\ll_{J,\varepsilon}
X^{b_1-77/200+\kappa(\vartheta)/2+\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Together with (11), this proves (3). Choosing `epsilon` below the strict margin in (4) and summing dyadic shells gives vanishing threshold measure.

The exponent law is therefore transparent. A host-count saving `delta` is worth exactly `delta/2` in the false-RH frontier because it enters through Cauchy--Schwarz against the fixed second-moment exponent `123/100`.

## 4. Ambient exceptional mass cannot manufacture the needed host saving more efficiently than RE-094

There is a tempting but redundant way to try to obtain (2): start from the ambient PNT exceptional set of `RE-094` and simply count how many sample points `x_q` can lie inside it. The exact sample spacing from `RE-093` shows why this does not create a new theorem.

Suppose an ambient short-interval result at length `H=X^vartheta` gives exceptional-start measure

\[
|E(X,H)|\ll X^{m(\vartheta)+o(1)}.
\tag{21}
\]

A bad host from (1) supplies, after shrinking constants harmlessly, an interval of `\gg H` nearby start points whose `H`-interval is prime-free and hence PNT-exceptional. Meanwhile `RE-093` gives, for distinct integer hosts,

\[
x_{q'}-x_q\gg X^{1/2}
\tag{22}
\]

and `H=o(X^{1/2})`. Therefore the exceptional-start packets attached to distinct bad hosts are disjoint. Consequently

\[
\#\mathcal B_{\vartheta}(X)
\ll
\min\!\left(
X^{1/2+o(1)},
X^{m(\vartheta)-\vartheta+o(1)}
\right).
\tag{23}
\]

Thus ambient information can certify at best the exponent

\[
\kappa_{\rm amb}(\vartheta)
\le
\min\!\left(\frac12,\,m(\vartheta)-\vartheta\right).
\tag{24}
\]

But (24) gives **no new frontier beyond the direct RE-094 packing argument**. There are two cases.

If

\[
m(\vartheta)-\vartheta\ge\frac12,
\]

then (23) does not beat the trivial square-root host exponent, so the second-moment term in (5) remains exactly `173/200`.

If instead

\[
m(\vartheta)-\vartheta<\frac12,
\]

then

\[
m(\vartheta)<\frac12+\vartheta.
\tag{25}
\]

But the direct max law of `RE-094` is already

\[
\max\!\left(\frac12+\vartheta,m(\vartheta)\right)
=
\frac12+\vartheta.
\tag{26}
\]

Every sampled-host argument still pays the same small-chamber term `1/2+vartheta`, so the detour through (23) and the second moment cannot lower (26).

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{ambient exceptional measure}
\to
\text{sampled-host count}
\to
\text{gap second moment}
\quad\text{never improves the direct RE-094 ambient splice.}
}
\tag{27}
\]

This is an information-class statement about this particular conversion, not a claim that sampled prime hosts behave like arbitrary starts. Its point is exactly the opposite: **to gain below `173/200`, the estimate (8) must use information not already contained in an ambient one-variable exceptional-set measure.**

## 5. Prior-art audit: the missing theorem is genuinely sparse and source-conditioned

A targeted literature search checked the two nearest short-interval regimes.

Danilo Bazzanella, *Prime Numbers in Intervals Starting at a Fixed Power of the Integers*, J. Aust. Math. Soc. **87** (2009), 83--99, DOI `10.1017/S1446788709000020`, studies almost-all intervals `[n^alpha,n^alpha+H]`. This is structurally much closer than an ambient theorem because the starts already lie on a sparse power sequence. However, its main theorem is an almost-all statement over integer `n`; it does not provide the required fixed polynomial saving for the **prime-indexed**, deformed samples `x_q=eta_1^{-1}(eta_2(q))`, nor does it condition on `q` being prime.

The current manuscript of Runbo Li, *Primes in Almost All Short Intervals III* (2026), proves that `[n-n^(1/24+epsilon),n]` contains primes for all but `O(X(log X)^(-B))` integers `n in [X,2X]`. The interval exponent is far shorter than anything needed here, but the exceptional set is still `X^(1+o(1))` in ambient power-law terms. Such a theorem alone can contain every `X^(1/2+o(1))` CA depth-two sample and therefore gives no estimate of the form (8).

A search for short-interval or prime-gap estimates explicitly conditioned on `q` prime near `q^2`, prime squares, and sparse quadratic prime-indexed starts found no theorem that supplies (8) for the deformed CA sample points. This is a prior-art boundary, not a priority claim.

No new external theorem is load-bearing here. Stadlmann's second moment is already recorded in `SOURCES.md` through `RE-091`; Bazzanella and Li are comparative prior art only. `SOURCES.md` therefore requires no change.

## 6. Research consequence

`RE-094` left two broad-looking possibilities: improve the ambient exceptional exponent, or attack the prime-indexed sample directly. Equation (27) separates them cleanly. An ambient exceptional-set theorem should continue to be evaluated by the direct `RE-094` max law; there is no hidden gain from first converting it to a host count and then applying the gap second moment.

The genuinely new target is (8). It is substantially weaker than a PNT asymptotic at every sample: one only needs a fixed polynomial saving over the trivial number of prime hosts whose anchored one-sided interval is completely prime-free. If such a theorem gives `kappa=1/2-delta`, its value in the Robin fan is immediately quantified by (7), while the interval-length cost remains the deterministic barrier `1/2+vartheta`.

Thus the durable depth-two frontier after `RE-091`--`RE-095` is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{beat }173/200
\iff
\text{find some }\vartheta<73/200
\text{ with a genuinely source-conditioned bad-host saving }\kappa(\vartheta)<1/2,
}
\]

within this host-count + Stadlmann-moment information architecture. Rightmost-maximizer constraints, standard/reciprocal prime-race state, or another selector-specific law could of course bypass this architecture entirely.
