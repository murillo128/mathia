# RE-166 — Lambert-W inversion localizes the KV-capacity transition

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + SHARP-CAPACITY-INVERSION + MOVING-KV-HORIZON + O(1)-V-TRANSITION-WIDTH + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`.

**Parent findings:** RE-153, RE-164, RE-165.

`RE-165` identifies

\[
\mathfrak K_b(p,T)
=
\sqrt p\log p\,
\frac{e^{-bV(T)}}{V(T)},
\qquad
V(T):=\frac{(\log T)^{3/5}}{(\log\log T)^{1/5}},
\tag{1}
\]

as the sharp generalized-source capacity generated by a quantitative Korobov--Vinogradov PNT envelope. It also shows that a fixed canonical horizon

\[
\log T=B(\log p)^{5/3}(\log\log p)^{1/3}
\tag{2}
\]

has a strict leading-order threshold `b kappa_B=1/2`, but that nominal equality lies on the ambiguous side because the slowly varying correction to `V(T)` has the wrong sign for rigidity.

The remaining inversion can be done exactly. The capacity-one contour has a closed Lambert-`W` center, and every fixed nonzero capacity contour lies only `O(1)` away from it in the `V` coordinate. Thus the qualitative transition between `mathfrak K_b -> 0` and nonvanishing generalized-source ambiguity is concentrated in an **additive `O(1)` layer of `V(T)`**, much thinner than the fixed-`B` scale resolved in `RE-165`.

This is an inversion statement about the quantitative-PNT information boundary. It does not by itself constrain the ordinary-prime/CA selector and therefore does not resolve `CLUE-clean-peak-selector-height-coupling`.

## 1. Exact Lambert-W center

Put

\[
L:=\log p.
\tag{3}
\]

Define `v_*(p)` by normalizing the capacity to one:

\[
\sqrt p\,L\,\frac{e^{-bv_*}}{v_*}=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) is equivalent to

\[
(bv_*)e^{bv_*}=bL\sqrt p.
\tag{5}
\]

Hence, on the principal real branch,

\[
\boxed{
 v_*(p)
 =
 \frac1b W_0\!\left(bL\sqrt p\right).
}
\tag{6}
\]

The capacity-one terminal horizon `T_*(p)` is the unique sufficiently large solution of

\[
V(T_*)=v_*.
\tag{7}
\]

This inverse is also explicit. Write

\[
y:=\log T,
\qquad
x:=\log y.
\tag{8}
\]

Since

\[
V(T)^5=\frac{y^3}{\log y}=\frac{e^{3x}}x,
\tag{9}
\]

solving `V(T)=v` gives

\[
x e^{-3x}=v^{-5}.
\tag{10}
\]

The large-`T` solution uses the `W_{-1}` branch:

\[
\boxed{
 \log T_*(p)
 =
 \exp\!\left[
 -\frac13
 W_{-1}\!\left(-3v_*(p)^{-5}\right)
 \right].
}
\tag{11}
\]

Equations (6) and (11) give the exact moving center of the `RE-165` capacity boundary, up to the normalization choice `mathfrak K_b=1`.

That normalization is not a claim that one is a universal physical threshold: the upper and generalized-insertion lower bounds in `RE-164`--`RE-165` carry fixed constants. Its value is that **all fixed positive capacity contours have the same asymptotic center to additive `O(1)` accuracy in `V`**, as the next section shows.

## 2. The transition has additive O(1) width in V

For an arbitrary terminal horizon `T`, set

\[
\Delta_p:=V(T)-v_*(p).
\tag{12}
\]

Using (4) directly in (1) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak K_b(p,T)
=
\frac{e^{-b\Delta_p}}
{1+\Delta_p/v_*(p)}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

No asymptotic expansion of `V` is used in (13). Since `v_*(p) -> infinity`, the three regimes are immediate:

\[
\Delta_p\to+\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak K_b(p,T)\to0,
\tag{14}
\]

\[
\Delta_p\to c\in\mathbb R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak K_b(p,T)\to e^{-bc},
\tag{15}
\]

and, whenever `V(T)` remains positive,

\[
\Delta_p\to-\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak K_b(p,T)\to\infty.
\tag{16}
\]

Combining this with `RE-164` and the generalized insertion sharpness of `RE-165` yields the information-theoretic phase portrait:

\[
\boxed{
V(T)-v_*(p)\to+\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{quantitative-PNT rigidity},
}
\tag{17}
\]

whereas every bounded `Delta_p` subsequence leaves the generalized-source capacity nonvanishing, and `Delta_p -> -infinity` gives unbounded raw capacity. Thus the transition is not spread across an `o(log p)` uncertainty in `V`; it is localized to an **additive constant scale**.

More generally, fixing any capacity level `q in (0,infinity)` and solving `mathfrak K_b=q` gives

\[
V_q(p)=v_*(p)-\frac1b\log q+o(1).
\tag{18}
\]

Hence two fixed capacity contours differ by

\[
V_{q_1}(p)-V_{q_2}(p)
=
\frac1b\log\frac{q_2}{q_1}+o(1).
\tag{19}
\]

This is the precise sense in which the `V`-transition has `O(1)` width.

## 3. The moving center inside the canonical terminal scale

The principal-branch expansion of (6) is especially simple. Since

\[
bL\sqrt p=bL e^{L/2},
\tag{20}
\]

the standard large-argument expansion of `W_0` gives

\[
\boxed{
 v_*(p)
 =
 \frac{L}{2b}
 +
 \frac{\log(2b)}b
 +
 O\!\left(\frac{\log L}{L}\right).
}
\tag{21}
\]

The apparent `log L` correction cancels between `log z` and `log log z`. Therefore the capacity center is already determined to additive constant precision by the second term in (21), exactly the precision relevant to (13).

To compare with the fixed-`B` scale of `RE-165`, put

\[
R:=\log L,
\qquad
S:=\log R,
\tag{22}
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
 B_0
 :=
 \left(\frac53\right)^{1/3}(2b)^{-5/3}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

This is the leading coefficient satisfying

\[
b B_0^{3/5}\left(\frac35\right)^{1/5}=\frac12.
\tag{24}
\]

Using the `RE-165` expansion

\[
V(U_B)
=
\kappa_B L
\left[
1-
\frac{S+3\log B}{25R}
+O\!\left(\frac{S^2+1}{R^2}\right)
\right],
\tag{25}
\]

and matching it to (21), the exact center satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\log T_*(p)
=
B_0 L^{5/3}R^{1/3}
\left[
1+
\frac{S+3\log B_0}{15R}
+O\!\left(\frac{S^2+1}{R^2}\right)
\right].
}
\tag{26}
\]

Equation (26) explains the endpoint phenomenon of `RE-165`. The fixed nominally critical horizon `B=B_0` omits a relative displacement of order

\[
\frac{\log\log\log p}{\log\log p},
\tag{27}
\]

so it sits asymptotically below the true moving center by far more than the actual transition width. Its capacity therefore diverges even though the leading exponent says `b kappa_{B_0}=1/2`.

## 4. In log-height coordinates the boundary layer is extremely thin

Let `y=log T`. Differentiating

\[
V(e^y)=y^{3/5}(\log y)^{-1/5}
\tag{28}
\]

gives

\[
\frac{dV}{dy}
=
\frac{V}{y}
\left(
\frac35-
\frac1{5\log y}
\right).
\tag{29}
\]

At the center, equations (21), (23), and (26) imply

\[
\frac{dy}{dV}\Big|_{T_*}
\sim
\frac53\frac{\log T_*}{v_*}
\sim
\frac{10bB_0}{3}
L^{2/3}R^{1/3}.
\tag{30}
\]

Consequently an additive `O(1)` displacement in the capacity coordinate `V` corresponds to

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(\log T)
=
O\!\left(L^{2/3}R^{1/3}\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]

If one continues to parameterize the horizon by

\[
B(p):=
\frac{\log T}{L^{5/3}R^{1/3}},
\tag{32}
\]

then the whole constant-capacity boundary layer has width

\[
\boxed{
\Delta B
=O\!\left(\frac1L\right).
}
\tag{33}
\]

This is substantially thinner than the slowly moving center correction in (26), which is of order `S/R`. The fixed-`B` dichotomy of `RE-165` therefore sees only the coarse two-phase picture; the actual quantitative-PNT capacity transition occurs inside a narrow moving layer around `B_*(p)`.

## 5. Research consequence

`RE-165` established that the quantitative PNT remainder has enough information to rigidify the generalized source only after its propagated Robin capacity tends to zero. The present inversion makes the required terminal horizon explicit and removes the remaining ambiguity about the equality scale.

There are now three distinct scales rather than a single nominal threshold. First, `B_0` in (23) is only the **leading fixed-coefficient threshold**. Second, the moving center `T_*(p)` in (11)/(26) includes the slowly varying displacement that decides which side of equality a fixed canonical horizon actually occupies. Third, the genuine transition layer around that moving center has only `O(1)` width in `V`, equivalently `O(L^{2/3}R^{1/3})` width in `log T` or `O(1/L)` width in `B`.

This sharpening does not supply the missing arithmetic coupling between clean CA peaks, their selector index, and the source fidelity available at the corresponding height. It therefore leaves `CLUE-clean-peak-selector-height-coupling` open. What it does provide is a precise target for such a coupling: to force source rigidity from a KV-strength PNT estimate, the arithmetic selector must place the effective terminal horizon **above the moving Lambert-W center by an unbounded amount in the `V` coordinate**. Merely reaching the leading equality `b kappa_B=1/2` is insufficient.
