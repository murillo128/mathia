## 1. Integerizing one exact first-layer support pair

The local real-label construction of `RE-053` is structurally stable under an `O(1)` perturbation of either boundary label, provided the neighboring state is re-solved from the exact CA transition rather than left fixed.

Take one of the large three-state controls of `RE-053` and write its middle state as

\[
(Y,R),
\qquad
R=e^h-1=c_0Y^{-b_0},
\tag{13}
\]

so that `A_(b_0)=c_0` exactly. Put

\[
F(Y,R):=\log\log Y+\log(1+R).
\tag{14}
\]

Let `q_-^*<q_+^*` be the two real boundary labels of that control. Their adjacent amplitude breakpoints are exactly `b_0` and `b_1`, and

\[
q_\pm^*=Y+O_J(RY\log Y),
\tag{15}
\]

while

\[
q_+^*-q_-^*
=
\bigl(c_0(b_1-b_0)+o(1)\bigr)
Y^{1-b_0}\log Y.
\tag{16}
\]

We now keep the middle state fixed and vary one boundary label `q` near its real reference value.

For a proposed **left** label `q`, put

\[
d=\log q,
\qquad
Y_-(q)=Y-d,
\tag{17}
\]

and define `R_-(q)` by the exact transition equation

\[
F(Y,R)-F(Y_-(q),R_-(q))
=\log(1+q^{-1}).
\tag{18}
\]

This is explicit:

\[
\boxed{
1+R_-(q)
=
\frac{\log Y\,(1+R)}
{(1+q^{-1})\log(Y-\log q)}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

For a proposed **right** label, put

\[
Y_+(q)=Y+\log q
\tag{20}
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
1+R_+(q)
=
\frac{(1+q^{-1})\log Y\,(1+R)}
{\log(Y+\log q)}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Equations (19) and (21) make both adjacent transitions satisfy (9) **exactly for the newly chosen labels**. For `q=q_-^*` or `q=q_+^*` they recover the original neighboring state.

The amplitude tie exponents generated by these exact neighbors are

\[
\beta_-(q)
:=
\frac{\log(R_-(q)/R)}
{\log(Y/(Y-\log q))},
\tag{22}
\]

and

\[
\beta_+(q)
:=
\frac{\log(R/R_+(q))}
{\log((Y+\log q)/Y)}.
\tag{23}
\]

Hence

\[
A_{\beta_-(q)}(Y_-(q),R_-(q))
=A_{\beta_-(q)}(Y,R),
\tag{24}
\]

and similarly on the right. Direct expansion of (19)--(23), uniformly for

\[
q=Y+O_J(RY\log Y),
\tag{25}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\beta_\pm}{dq}
=
\frac{1+o_J(1)}{RY\log Y}>0.
}
\tag{26}
\]

One way to see the scale without hiding the cancellation is to expand the two factors in (19):

\[
\frac{\log Y}{\log(Y-\log q)}
=1+\frac{\log q}{Y\log Y}+O\!\left(\frac{(\log Y)^2}{Y^2}\right),
\tag{27}
\]

whereas

\[
(1+q^{-1})^{-1}
=1-q^{-1}+O(Y^{-2}).
\tag{28}
\]

The leading `1/Y` terms cancel when `q\asymp Y`; the remaining variation on moving `q` is of order `(q-Y)/Y^2`. Dividing by `R` and then by the intrinsic relative jump `log q/Y` yields

\[
\beta(q)
=
\frac{q-Y}{RY\log Y}+o_J(1)
\tag{29}
\]

at the relevant scale, and differentiating gives (26). The right transition has the same leading derivative. Since `R=c_0Y^{-b_0}` and `b_0<1/2`, one unit of label motion changes a breakpoint by only

\[
O_J\!\left(
\frac{Y^{b_0-1}}{\log Y}
\right)=o(1).
\tag{30}
\]

Now take the integer labels

\[
\boxed{
q_-:=\lfloor q_-^*\rfloor,
\qquad
q_+:=\lceil q_+^*\rceil.
}
\tag{31}
\]

If a reference label is already integral it is left unchanged. By (26), for all sufficiently large controls,

\[
\beta_-(q_-)\le b_0,
\qquad
\beta_+(q_+)\ge b_1.
\tag{32}
\]

Therefore the fixed middle state beats its left neighbor throughout `b>=b_0` and its right neighbor throughout `b<=b_1`. Explicitly,

\[
\log\frac{A_b(Y,R)}{A_b(Y_-,R_-)}
=(b-\beta_-(q_-))
\log\frac{Y}{Y_-},
\tag{33}
\]

and

\[
\log\frac{A_b(Y_+,R_+)}{A_b(Y,R)}
=(b-\beta_+(q_+))
\log\frac{Y_+}{Y}.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus the middle state is exposed on all of `J`, with at most a guard tie at an endpoint.

The support statement also survives **exactly**, not merely by continuity. At either actual tie exponent, the same strict-concavity argument used in `RE-040` and `RE-053` gives

\[
\frac{\log(1+q_-^{-1})}{\log q_-}
>
 g(Y)-\frac{b\,a}{Y}
>
\frac{\log(1+q_+^{-1})}{\log q_+}
\qquad(b\in J),
\tag{35}
\]

where

\[
a=\frac{R}{1+R}.
\tag{36}
\]

Indeed the left secant dominates the tangent at `beta_-` and hence at every larger `b`, while the right secant lies below the tangent at `beta_+` and hence at every smaller `b`. Since `g` and the first-layer event map are monotone, the selector `Z_b` defined by

\[
g(Z_b)=g(Y)-\frac{ba}{Y}
\tag{37}
\]

lies strictly in the integer-label chamber (10) for every interior `b in J`.

Finally, (31) changes the real boundary gap by at most two. Because the scale in (16) tends to infinity,

\[
q_+-q_-
=
q_+^*-q_-^*+O(1),
\tag{38}
\]

which proves the first equality in (12). The first-layer event map satisfies `eta_1(q)=q+1/2+O(1/log q)`, so the selector-space chamber changes by only `O(1)` under the integerization as well.

## 2. The all-layer avoidance argument is stable under integerization

`RE-055` did not obtain all-layer avoidance from randomness. It chose each large trial scale away from the finitely many events generated by previously protected small labels, then deleted only the finitely many macroscopic inverse chambers generated by later labels. Both steps tolerate the `O(1)` endpoint displacement in Section 1.

At a trial scale `T`, the support width is

\[
W_T
=
\bigl(c_0(b_1-b_0)+o(1)\bigr)
T^{1-b_0}\log T,
\tag{39}
\]

and `W_T->infinity`. In the protected-label stage of `RE-055`, replace every forbidden neighborhood of an old event by one whose radius is enlarged by a fixed constant, say `3`. The total forbidden measure changes by only `O(B_X)` while the original bound is

\[
O_J\!\left(
B_X X^{1-b_0}(\log X)^2
\right)=o(X).
\tag{40}
\]

Hence a safe trial scale still exists. The integerized chamber is contained in that fixed fattening of the real-label chamber.

For the finitely many macroscopic layers, delete every remaining candidate integer label `q` whose event satisfies

\[
\eta_j(q)\in\mathcal H.
\tag{41}
\]

For each fixed `j`, the inverse interval has the same Jacobian scale as in `RE-054`--`RE-055`:

\[
q=(jT)^{1/j}(1+o_j(1))
\tag{42}
\]

and

\[
|\eta_j^{-1}(\mathcal H)|
=
\left(
 c_0(b_1-b_0)j^{-(j-1)/j}+o_j(1)
\right)
T^{1/j-b_0}\log T.
\tag{43}
\]

Intersecting this real inverse interval with the integer lattice can only reduce the number of candidate labels to its length plus `O(1)`. For sufficiently deep layers, the same cutoff argument as in `RE-055` shows that labels above the protected base range cannot generate an event in `H`; no infinite deletion is required. Thus after the finite deletion step the exact all-layer statement (11) holds.

The two integer boundary labels are not removed. Their first-layer events are the chamber endpoints, while their higher-layer events lie at much larger coordinates. Consequently they remain consecutive boundary events in the complete retained layer-event staircase.

The key exponent is unchanged. At the local scale

\[
x_{T,j}:=(jT)^{1/j},
\tag{44}
\]

the deleted inverse interval obeys

\[
\frac{|\eta_j^{-1}(\mathcal H)|+O(1)}{x_{T,j}^\theta}
\ll_{J,j}
T^{(1-\theta)/j-b_0}\log T
+T^{-\theta/j}.
\tag{45}
\]

Because `b_0>1-theta`, both terms tend to zero for every fixed `j`. As in `RE-055`, the first layer is the worst one.

## 3. One PNT-dense integer label set supports infinitely many controls

The global embedding can start from an integer rather than real mesh. For sufficiently large `n`, put

\[
r_n:=\lfloor n\log n\rfloor.
\tag{46}
\]

The sequence is eventually strictly increasing and elementary inversion gives

\[
\#\{n:r_n\le x\}
\sim\frac{x}{\log x},
\tag{47}
\]

while

\[
r_{n+1}-r_n=O(\log r_n).
\tag{48}
\]

Choose the successive block scales exactly as in the diagonal construction of `RE-055`, with affected base ranges disjoint and escaping sufficiently rapidly. At each stage:

1. choose a trial scale whose fixed fattening avoids all protected old events;
2. integerize its two exact first-layer boundaries by Section 1;
3. delete baseline integer labels whose events fall in the finitely many relevant inverse chambers;
4. insert or retain the two integer boundary labels.

The result is a set `Q subset N`; no real labels remain.

At a fixed-layer hole, the number of deleted baseline labels is

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{|\eta_j^{-1}(\mathcal H)|}{\log x_{T,j}}+1
\right).
\tag{49}
\]

Relative to the baseline count `x/(log x)`, this tends to zero because (43) gives

\[
\frac{|\eta_j^{-1}(\mathcal H)|}{x_{T,j}}
=O_{J,j}(T^{-b_0}\log T)=o(1).
\tag{50}
\]

Taking the stage scales sufficiently separated makes the cumulative perturbation `o(x/log x)` uniformly through successive affected ranges, proving (3).

Outside the modified ranges, consecutive gaps remain logarithmic. Across a deleted inverse chamber, a new gap is bounded by the chamber length, two neighboring mesh gaps, and an `O(1)` integer-rounding term. Equation (45) therefore yields

\[
q_{n+1}-q_n=o(q_n^\theta)
\tag{51}
\]

on every affected scale. The stagewise diagonal choice makes the little-`o` uniform along the final sequence, proving (4).

Hence one single increasing integer label set drives all layers and supports infinitely many exact one-cell controls. The construction is not a different integer set for each threshold, layer, or block.

## 4. What integrality does and does not buy

The strengthened control now matches all of the following simultaneously:

- the exact adaptive one-cell amplitude geometry on the fixed compact interval `J`;
- exact first-layer CA intrinsic-mass, abundance-gain, support-slope, and event formulas at both boundaries;
- **integer** boundary labels and an entirely integer global label source;
- one common source driving every exact layer map `eta_j`;
- complete all-layer event-free support chambers;
- PNT-scale label density;
- and the same global `o(x^theta)` consecutive-label gap ceiling.

Therefore an argument which uses only the fact that the CA event bases lie on the integer lattice cannot distinguish the physical ordinary-prime fan from this control.

What is still absent is specifically **prime arithmetic**. The set `Q` need not consist of irreducibles in `N`; it may contain composites, common factors, and multiplicative relations. The construction imposes no congruence restrictions, no unique factorization for the semigroup generated by `Q`, no ordinary Euler product, no identity tying the synthetic intrinsic state to the global accumulated layer mass, and no ordinary standard/reciprocal prime-race law.

This distinction matters in relation to classical Beurling-prime rigidity. Beurling generalized primes are normally allowed to be arbitrary real labels. Lagarias proved a strong classification when the **generated generalized-integer system itself** has the Delone property and is contained in the ordinary integers: that setting forces unique factorization and, up to finitely many exceptions, ordinary primes. The present control does not satisfy or claim those hypotheses. Integral labels alone do not make the generalized-integer multiset Delone; composite labels can create repeated products and multiplicities. Thus that classical rigidity does not invalidate the construction, but it clarifies what extra multiplicative structure would constitute genuinely new input.

## 5. Stress tests, prior-art boundary, and research consequence

The integerization is not a density-only approximation. Equations (19) and (21) re-solve the neighboring state after rounding, so the first-layer mass and slope formulas remain exact. Equations (33)--(35) then preserve the whole threshold cell rather than merely keeping one selected parameter nearby. The `O(1)` rounding displacement is negligible compared with the diverging chamber width, but exact local identities do not rely on that negligibility.

Nor is the global claim obtained by rounding the final real sequence of `RE-055` indiscriminately. Such rounding could create collisions and would not preserve the local transition equations. The construction instead begins with a separated integer mesh and performs each local integerization before the deletion surgery. Distinctness, exact boundary formulas, PNT density, and the gap ceiling are therefore checked in the same final system.

The prior-art search covered Beurling generalized-prime perturbations, integer/Delone generalized-integer systems, prime-like subsets of the integers, current CA prime-layer work, and Robin counterexample-transition literature. Classical Beurling theory allows real prime labels and contains much stronger analytic constructions than the elementary mesh used here. Lagarias's 1999 Delone classification is the relevant boundary for when integrality of the **whole multiplicative system** becomes rigid. The search did not locate a prior construction matching this particular adaptive one-cell CA geometry with an all-layer event-free integer label source. No external theorem is load-bearing in the proof above, so `SOURCES.md` is unchanged.

The consequence for the live near-critical route is negative but useful. `RE-055` left three broad escape currencies: global accumulated layer mass, integer-prime arithmetic, and joint analytic race/Euler-product information. The first phrase can now be sharpened:

\[
\boxed{
\text{integer lattice information alone is not enough.}
}
\tag{52}
\]

Any successful `integer-prime` continuation must spend a property that fails for the composite-contaminated set `Q`: primality/irreducibility, multiplicative independence or unique factorization, congruence structure, the global accumulated CA identity, or an analytic relation generated by the ordinary Euler product. Adding another integer-valued gap or layer-placement constraint without such a property stays inside the matched control class.
