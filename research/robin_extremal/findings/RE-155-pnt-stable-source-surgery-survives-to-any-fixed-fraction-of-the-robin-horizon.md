# RE-155 — PNT-stable source surgery survives to any fixed fraction of the Robin horizon

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + TERMINAL-ANNULUS MATCHED-SYNTHETIC-SOURCE-OBSTRUCTION + PNT-STABLE + SELECTOR-HORIZON-BARRIER + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-153` turns every regular self-tangent Robin counterexample into a lower bound on a canonical Pareto-weighted Chebyshev-deficit average, while `RE-154` shows that matching the ordinary source through any fixed multiple `Bp` does not determine that average: `Theta(sqrt(p)/log p)` post-selector relocations can move it by order one. The fixed-`B` qualifier in `RE-154` is not the real boundary. Equal-cardinality relocation cancels the upper-endpoint term in the exact Robin-kernel influence, so the same obstruction can be pushed all the way into any fixed terminal fraction of the `RE-022` horizon `U_A(p)` while the perturbation remains invisible at PNT scale.

Retain the `RE-153` regular self-tangent sequence, write

\[

=

\[
J_p=[p+p^\alpha,U],

h(t)=\frac{-\log(1-t^{-1})}{\log t},

\mathcal A_p(Q)

Fix constants

\[
0<c<c+\delta<d<d+\delta<1,

There exist increasing real prime-like controls `Q_p^+` and `Q_p^-`, each obtained from the ordinary primes `P` by exactly `m_p` deletions and `m_p` insertions, such that every modification lies in

(cU,(d+\delta)U],

After the modified shell has been passed, the counting discrepancy is exactly zero and the residual Chebyshev discrepancy is only `O(m_p)=o(U)`.

Since `c<1` is arbitrary, for every fixed `epsilon>0` the controls can agree exactly with the ordinary source, and with the generalized CA event history generated by that source, through `(1-epsilon)U_A(p)` and still move the `RE-153` source average by an arbitrary fixed amount while preserving PNT-scale density. Thus merely replacing the fixed multiplier `B` of `RE-154` by an unbounded propagation radius does not close the source barrier. At the matched-control information level, even propagation through every fixed fraction of the entire `RE-022` horizon is insufficient.

## 1. The exact influence has a stronger relocation law near the upper endpoint

For one added label `q<U`, `RE-154` derived the exact positive influence

\[
\Lambda_p(q)
=
\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{Z_p}
\log q\,
\bigl(h(q)-h(U)\bigr),
\tag{12}
\]

so adding `q` decreases `A_p` by `Lambda_p(q)` and deleting it increases `A_p` by the same amount.

For a single label at a fixed fraction of `U`, the truncation term `h(U)` is no longer negligible. The useful object is instead the influence **difference** of an equal-cardinality relocation. If

\[
q=yU+O(1),
\qquad
s=zU+O(1),
\qquad
0<y<z<1
\tag{13}
\]

with `y,z` fixed, then (12) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_p(q)-\Lambda_p(s)
&=
\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{Z_p}
\Bigl[
-\log(1-q^{-1})
+\log(1-s^{-1})
+\log(s/q)h(U)
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The first two terms satisfy

\[
-\log(1-q^{-1})
+\log(1-s^{-1})
=
\frac1U\left(\frac1y-\frac1z\right)
+O(U^{-2}),
\tag{15}
\]

while

\[
\log(s/q)h(U)
=O_{y,z}\!\left(\frac1{U\log U}\right).
\tag{16}
\]

Since `Z_p=2+o(1)`, equations (14)--(16) yield the terminal relocation law

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_p(yU+O(1))
-
\Lambda_p(zU+O(1))
=
\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{2U}
\left(\frac1y-\frac1z\right)
+o_{y,z}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{U}\right).
}
\tag{17}
\]

This is the point missed by reading `RE-154` label-by-label. Near `U`, the endpoint subtraction changes the individual influence at leading order, but it disappears from an equal-cardinality relocation to first order. The leverage therefore remains `Theta(sqrt(p) log(p)/U)` per relocated label throughout every fixed fractional subannulus below `U`.

## 2. Terminal positive and negative surgeries

For `Q_p^+`, delete any `m_p` ordinary primes

\[
r_i\in(cU,(c+\delta)U]
\qquad(1\le i\le m_p)
\tag{18}
\]

and insert `m_p` distinct non-prime real labels

\[
s_i\in(dU,dU+1].
\tag{19}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal A_p(Q_p^+)-\mathcal A_p(P)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{m_p}
\bigl(\Lambda_p(r_i)-\Lambda_p(s_i)\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]

The uniform version of (17) on the fixed compact fractional shells gives

\[
\Lambda_p(r_i)-\Lambda_p(s_i)
\ge
\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{2U}
\left(
\frac1{c+\delta}-\frac1d
\right)
+o\!\left(\frac{\sqrt p\log p}{U}\right),
\tag{21}
\]

and (5) proves (8).

For `Q_p^-`, reverse the geometry: insert `m_p` distinct non-prime real labels in `(cU,cU+1]` and delete any `m_p` ordinary primes in `[dU,(d+\delta)U]`. Then

\[
\mathcal A_p(Q_p^-)-\mathcal A_p(P)
=
-\sum_{i=1}^{m_p}
\bigl(\Lambda_p(q_i)-\Lambda_p(s_i)\bigr),
\tag{22}
\]

and (17), now with the early labels asymptotic to `cU` and all deleted late labels at least `dU`, proves (9).

As in `RE-154`, increasing the fixed amplitude `a` realizes any prescribed fixed source displacement in either direction. The difference is geometric: the surgery can now be confined to the terminal part of the full `RE-022` annulus rather than to a fixed-multiple shell above `p`.

## 3. There are enough ordinary primes and the perturbation is PNT-small

The construction uses the ordinary PNT only in fixed proportional intervals at scale `U`. The number of primes in each deletion shell in (18) and (22) is `asymp U/log U`. On the other hand, from (1),

\[
\frac{m_p}{U/\log U}
\asymp_a
\frac{\log U}{\sqrt p\log p}
=
A\,
\frac{(\log p)^{2/3}(\log\log p)^{1/3}}{\sqrt p}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]

Hence there are more than `m_p` deletion primes in both fixed fractional shells for all sufficiently large `p`.

At any intermediate point, at most `m_p` uncompensated labels contribute to the counting discrepancy, proving (10). Their logarithmic weights are `log U+O(1)`, so

\[
|\vartheta_{Q_p^\pm}(x)-\vartheta(x)|
\ll
m_p\log U
=
U\,
O_a\!\left(
\frac{\log U}{\sqrt p\log p}
\right)
=o(U),
\tag{24}
\]

which proves (11) at the affected scale. Once both equal-cardinality modifications have occurred, the leading `log U` pieces cancel: every paired logarithmic difference is `O_{c,d,\delta}(1)`, hence the residual discrepancy is `O(m_p)`.

Thus these are much larger absolute modifications than in `RE-154`, but they are still an asymptotically vanishing fraction of the available labels and an `o(1)` relative perturbation of both prime-counting and Chebyshev main terms at scale `U`.

## 4. Exact selector matching can extend through almost the whole fixed-fraction horizon

Every changed label lies strictly above `cU`, so the controls equal the ordinary prime set exactly below that point. The generalized CA event map used in `RE-055` and `RE-154` has the same one-sided property: every event generated by a sufficiently large label `q` lies to the right of `q`. Therefore the generalized CA event staircase of `Q_p^+`, `Q_p^-`, and the ordinary source is also identical below `cU`.

Now fix any `epsilon>0` and set `c=1-epsilon`. Constants `delta,d` satisfying (4) still exist. The preceding construction therefore produces matched controls with identical ordinary-prime and generalized-event data through

\[
(1-\epsilon)U_A(p),
\tag{25}
\]

yet with an order-one difference in the normalized source coordinate. The terminal fraction `epsilon` can be chosen arbitrarily small, provided it is fixed before `p` tends to infinity.

This strictly strengthens the fixed-cutoff warning of `RE-154`. In particular, every fixed polynomial horizon `p^(1+kappa)` is far below `cU_A(p)` and is automatically covered. More importantly, even knowing the exact source through an arbitrarily large **fixed fraction of the full finite annulus that carries the `RE-022` mixed-race height** does not determine the target average if the remaining information is only PNT-scale density.

## 5. What this rules out, and what remains open

The result is an information obstruction. It does **not** claim that the ordinary primes admit these relocations, that the controls are genuine Robin counterexamples, or that they arise from globally consistent ordinary CA states. The controls are `p`-dependent finite real-label perturbations designed to test which information is actually used by a prospective selector-to-height theorem.

What is ruled out is a broad strategy class: exact CA/source propagation from the selected frontier prime all the way to `cU_A(p)` for some fixed `c<1`, followed only by PNT-density control on the remaining terminal annulus, cannot force the `RE-153` average below `sqrt(2)` with a fixed margin. The terminal source can still be changed by order one without violating any of those inputs.

The theorem deliberately stops short of a shrinking terminal layer. It does not exclude propagation to `(1-o(1))U_A(p)`: then a fixed proportional relocation shell no longer fits after the cutoff, and ordinary PNT alone does not guarantee enough primes in an arbitrarily shrinking relative interval. Nor does it address a sharp signed square-root estimate, zero-driven explicit-formula constraint, correlation law, or exact ordinary-prime/global-CA identity. Any of those can distinguish the controls and remains a legitimate route.

The source-side frontier is therefore narrower than after `RE-154`: simply making the propagation radius tend to infinity is not enough, and neither is reaching any fixed fraction of the canonical horizon. A successful source theorem must either control a terminal layer whose relative width tends to zero, or use arithmetic information strong enough to forbid the coherent terminal relocation itself.

## 6. Prior-art boundary

The flexibility of generalized-prime systems under long initial matching and small perturbations is classical in the Beurling setting. A targeted audit included normalization constructions in which generalized primes agree with the rational primes through an arbitrarily prescribed finite bound, and modern perturbative Beurling-prime constructions. Those results support the general warning that finite initial matching is weak, but they do not supply (17), the `U/(sqrt(p) log p)` terminal relocation count, or the splice to the self-tangent Robin source coordinate.

No external theorem is load-bearing here beyond the ordinary PNT already used throughout this line. The new step is the exact cancellation structure in the internally derived influence formula (12), followed by the explicit matched-source construction at the `RE-022` terminal scale. No `SOURCES.md` update is required.

## Research consequence

`RE-153` identified the canonical source observable; `RE-154` showed that a fixed multiplicative continuation beyond the CA selector does not control it. The present result removes the natural repair of merely letting that continuation grow. At PNT information level, the source can remain perfectly matched through `(1-epsilon)U_A(p)` for every fixed `epsilon>0` and still be moved by order one inside the remaining terminal fraction.

The next useful source theorem should therefore target the genuinely narrow residual interface: either prove ordinary-prime/CA control reaching a relative `o(1)` terminal layer, quantify how much shrinking-room propagation is actually needed using stronger short-interval input, or bypass propagation entirely with a signed global identity whose value is stable under the terminal relocation tested above.
