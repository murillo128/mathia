## 1. The depth-2 incidence samples the first layer at a sparse quadratic lattice

For fixed layers, `RE-054` gives

\[
\eta_1'(x)=1+O((\log x)^{-1}),
\qquad
\eta_2'(q)=q\left(1+O((\log q)^{-1})\right),
\tag{18}
\]

and

\[
\eta_1(x)=x(1+O((\log x)^{-1})),
\qquad
\eta_2(q)=\frac{q^2}{2}(1+O((\log q)^{-1})).
\tag{19}
\]

Applying the inverse-function theorem to (9) proves (10). In particular, for distinct integer labels `q<q'` in one dyadic host shell,

\[
\boxed{
x_{q'}-x_q\gg Q(q'-q)\ge Q.}
\tag{20}
\]

By (4), `D_X=o(Q)` for every fixed `b>0`. Therefore the desired first-layer holes around distinct sampled points are pairwise disjoint. This is stronger than the average separation `Q\log Q`: the integer lattice alone gives enough deterministic room to install the full matched family.

The same calculation explains why the host-layer gap is cheap. A physical displacement `D_X` corresponds under `eta_2^{-1}` to only `D_X/Q=o(1)` in the base coordinate. The apparent two-gap condition from `RE-086` is therefore extremely asymmetric at depth `2`: the first-layer side pays the entire macroscopic gap cost, while the host side needs less than one lattice unit.

## 2. PNT-dense integer surgery realizes the exact incidence

Start from the integer PNT mesh used in `RE-056`, for example

\[
r_n=\lfloor n\log n\rfloor
\tag{21}
\]

for sufficiently large `n`. It has counting function `x/log x(1+o(1))` and local gaps `O(log x)`.

Choose the host labels `q` from this same mesh in `[Q,2Q]`. Around each sampled point `x_q`, choose an integer

\[
p_q=x_q-D_X+O(1)
\tag{22}
\]

and retain or insert `p_q` as a source label. Delete every first-layer source label strictly between `p_q` and `x_q`. Equations (20) and `D_X=o(Q)` make these surgeries disjoint. The number of removed first-layer labels is

\[
\ll
N_X\frac{D_X}{\log X}
\asymp
X^{1-b},
\tag{23}
\]

while the ambient number of labels up to `X` is `X/log X`; hence

\[
\frac{X^{1-b}}{X/\log X}=X^{-b}\log X\to0.
\tag{24}
\]

The insertions contribute only `O(N_X)=O(\sqrt X/\log X)`. Repeating the surgery on sufficiently separated shells therefore preserves

\[
\boxed{\Pi_{\mathcal Q}(x)\sim x/\log x.}
\tag{25}
\]

The labels `q` are untouched because they live at scale `Q`, far below the first-layer surgery at scale `X`. Thus the depth-2 endpoint and the first-layer hole are generated by one and the same source set.

It remains to ensure that a chamber in (7) contains no event from layer `j>=3`. Before the first-layer surgery, the number of depth-at-least-three event occurrences in a physical shell of size `X` is, by the same elementary layer count used in `RE-004` and `RE-091`,

Finally, deleting or inserting labels at scale `X` cannot create a new layer `j>=2` event back in the same physical shell, because their higher events lie at scales `\gg X^2`.

Hence (5)--(8) follow. The control therefore satisfies the exact adjacency geometry that one obtains from an actual mixed first-layer/depth-2 support chamber.

The unmodified mesh contributes only polylogarithmic first-layer gaps. The surgery creates `N_X(1+o(1))` disjoint first-layer gaps of size `D_X(1+o(1))`. Their added square mass is exactly (12). Therefore a source satisfying the numerical second-moment budget

## 5. What the control does and does not rule out

The construction does **not** show that ordinary primes can realize these chambers. The labels may be composite, and no Euler product, Chebyshev/Mertens race, exact cumulative CA height, or global rightmost-maximizer fan is imposed. Those omissions are now the point.

What it does rule out is a broad but tempting next step after `RE-092`: one cannot improve `173/200` merely by observing that the first-layer gap must contain the precise analytic point

\[
x_q=\eta_1^{-1}(\eta_2(q))
\tag{31}
\]

with `q` drawn from the same PNT-dense integer source, or by additionally requiring the correct one-sided orientation and no intervening generalized CA event. All of those constraints fit inside the same second-moment budget.

The genuinely missing theorem must distinguish **ordinary primes at the sampled quadratic-scale points** from this integer PNT mesh, or must bring in selection information absent from a bare event staircase. Examples include a sampled-gap theorem for the actual points `x_q` with `q` prime, a correlation of those gaps with the standard/reciprocal prime races, or a rightmost-maximizer compatibility law that forbids most of the event-adjacent pairs even though the pairs exist geometrically.

## 6. Prior-art boundary and research consequence

A targeted search found two nearby bodies of work but no theorem that closes this sampled-incidence gap. Bazzanella proves strong *almost-all* prime results for intervals starting at fixed powers of integers, including square-scale families; however an `o(Q)` exceptional set for `Q` polynomial sample points is not automatically `o(Q/log Q)`, so it may still contain every prime-indexed host relevant here. Maier--Rassias and related work go in the opposite direction, constructing large ordinary-prime gaps that contain fixed powers of primes; that confirms that the mere presence of a prime-power landmark inside a prime gap is not itself contradictory. Neither result supplies the conditioned upper bound on gaps around the specific deformed points `eta_1^{-1}(eta_2(q))` with `q` prime that the present frontier would need.

No new external theorem is load-bearing in this finding. Stadlmann's `1.23+epsilon` exponent is already an anchored dependency through `RE-091`; the new result is a synthetic information-class control. `SOURCES.md` therefore needs no change.
