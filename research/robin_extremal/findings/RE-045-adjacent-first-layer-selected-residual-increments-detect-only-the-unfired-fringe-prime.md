# RE-045 — adjacent first-layer selected residual increments detect only the unfired fringe prime

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + FIRST-LAYER-EVENT-QUANTIZATION + SELECTED-RESIDUAL-FINITE-DIFFERENCE + BINARY-FRINGE-PRIME-LAW + GENERATING-PRIME-CANCELLATION + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-042` identifies the individual-prime jump of the translated nonlinear prime-race residual at scale `log p / sqrt(p)`. `RE-043`--`RE-044` then show that, at an adjacent first-layer threshold switch of the common false-RH fan, the two tied adaptive selectors lie on opposite sides of the generating prime and that their whole logarithmic bracket contains at most one additional ordinary prime in the final half-unit fringe.

Comparing the **two selected residuals themselves** gives a sharper and partly negative common-source law: the large first-order contribution of the generating prime `p` cancels once its own CA first-layer event has fired. The only surviving contribution at the individual-prime scale is a possible next prime whose ordinary prime-race jump has occurred at the upper selector but whose CA first-layer event has not yet occurred.

Assume RH is false, fix

\[
J=[b_0,b_1]\Subset(1-\Theta,1/2),
\]

and work on the sufficiently late common positive CA counterexample blocks of `RE-040`. Fix `b\in J` and suppose two adjacent CA states

\[
C_-<C_+,
\qquad
C_+=pC_-,
\]

are tied maximizers of the adaptive amplitude `A_b`, with their unique intervening CA transition the ordinary first-layer atom generated by the prime `p`. Write

\[
Y_\pm:=\log C_\pm,
\qquad
L_p:=\log p,
\qquad
Y_+-Y_-=L_p,
\]

and let `Z_-<\eta_p<Z_+` be the two adaptive tangent parameters of `RE-043`. Let `p^+` denote the next ordinary prime after `p`, and define

\[
\chi_p:=\mathbf 1_{\{p^+\le Z_+\}}.
\tag{1}
\]

By `RE-044`, if `\chi_p=1` then necessarily

\[
Z_+-\frac12<p^+\le Z_+<\eta_{p^+},
\tag{2}
\]

and `p^+` is the only ordinary prime in the bracket besides `p`.

Use exactly the translated residual of `RE-042`,

\[
\mathcal R_b(Z)
:=
V(Z)
-
\sqrt Z\log Z\,
\Psi_{b,\log Z}\!\left(
\frac{U(Z)}{\sqrt Z\log Z}
\right)
-
\sqrt2\,\frac{2b-1}{b}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then, uniformly for `b\in J` along any sequence of such switches escaping to infinity,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_b(Z_+)-\mathcal R_b(Z_-)
=
\chi_p\,\frac{\log p}{b\sqrt p}
+o_J\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p}\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b\sqrt p}{\log p}
\bigl(\mathcal R_b(Z_+)-\mathcal R_b(Z_-)\bigr)
-\chi_p
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the selected-residual increment has an asymptotically **binary first-prime-scale normalization**: it tends to `0` if the next ordinary prime has not yet been crossed, and to `1` after multiplication by `b sqrt(p)/log p` if that next prime has crossed the ordinary race but its own CA event is still pending.

The consequence for the original one-prime strategy is important. `RE-042` gives a positive residual jump

\[
\mathcal R_b(p^+_{\rm right})-
\mathcal R_b(p^-_{\rm left})
\sim\frac{\log p}{b\sqrt p}
\]

when one crosses the ordinary prime `p` at a fixed external coordinate. Equation (4) shows that this quantum is **not present** in the finite difference between the two physical adaptive selectors tied by the corresponding CA event. At that comparison the ordinary-prime jump and the CA-source displacement have already reconciled to first order. The local mismatch is instead the unmatched upper-fringe prime in (2).

## 1. The selected residual has an exact source-separated normal form

For one selected pair `(C,Z)` put

\[
\ell:=\log Z,
\qquad
N(Z):=\sqrt Z\,\ell,
\]

\[
q_0:=\frac{Z-Y}{Z\ell},
\qquad
q:=\frac{Z-\vartheta(Z)}{Z\ell},
\qquad
P(Z):=\Phi_{\ge2}(Z).
\tag{6}
\]

The exact identities reconstructed in `RE-036`--`RE-037` are

\[
E(Z)=\Psi_{b,\ell}(q_0)+T(C)+\delta_\ell(Z),
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
q-q_0
=
\frac{P(Z)-\delta_\vartheta(Z)}{Z\ell}.
\tag{8}
\]

Here `T(C)` is the finite-exponent Euler-product tail. The first-layer endpoint corrections have a concrete common-source meaning. `\delta_\vartheta(Z)` is the `\log r` mass of an ordinary prime `r\le Z` whose first-layer CA event `\eta_r` has not yet fired, and `\delta_\ell(Z)` is the matching ordinary Euler-product contribution `-\log(1-r^{-1})`; both are zero if no such prime exists. The first-layer event shift `0<\eta_r-r<1/2` for large `r` makes that exceptional prime unique whenever it exists.

Since `P(Z)=O(\sqrt Z)` and `\delta_\vartheta(Z)=O(\ell)`, Taylor expansion of the exact nonlinear function around `q_0`, using the uniform `\Psi''_{b,\ell}=O_J(\ell)` estimate of `RE-036`, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_b(Z)
={}&-
\sqrt2\,\frac{2b-1}{b}
+N(Z)T(C)
+N(Z)\delta_\ell(Z)\\
&-
\Psi'_{b,\ell}(q_0)
\frac{P(Z)-\delta_\vartheta(Z)}{\sqrt Z}
+O_J(Z^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed the quadratic Taylor remainder contributes

\[
N(Z)\,O_J\!\left(
\ell\left(\frac{P-\delta_\vartheta}{Z\ell}\right)^2
\right)
=O_J(Z^{-1/2}).
\tag{10}
\]

Equation (9) is the useful scale-separated form: the translated second-layer constant is fixed, the genuine higher-layer mass enters through `P`, and the only first-layer mismatch is the paired endpoint datum `(\delta_\vartheta,\delta_\ell)`.

## 2. Across an adjacent first-layer switch, all common CA terms are frozen or too smooth

Because `C_-` and `C_+` are adjacent and their unique intervening event is the first-layer atom `p`, there is no higher-layer CA event between the two tangent parameters. Therefore

\[
\boxed{P(Z_+)=P(Z_-)=:P,}
\qquad
P=O(\sqrt p).
\tag{11}
\]

The exponent vectors differ only by introducing `p` with exponent one. Hence the tail changes exactly by

\[
\boxed{
T(C_+)-T(C_-)
=-\log(1-p^{-2})
=O(p^{-2}).
}
\tag{12}
\]

`RE-043` supplies

\[
Z_+-Z_-=(1+o_J(1))L_p,
\qquad
Z_\pm\sim p.
\tag{13}
\]

Since `N'(Z)=O(L_p/\sqrt p)` on this interval and `T(C_-)=O((\sqrt p L_p)^{-1})` by `RE-036`, equations (12)--(13) give

\[
\boxed{
N(Z_+)T(C_+)-N(Z_-)T(C_-)
=O_J(L_p/p)
=o_J(L_p/\sqrt p).
}
\tag{14}
\]

The coefficient of the common higher-layer mass is equally stable, but this needs one small check because `\Psi` itself depends on `\ell=\log Z`. Put

\[
x:=\ell q_0=\frac{Z-Y}{Z},
\qquad
u:=\ell^{-1},
\qquad
s:=-\log(1-x).
\tag{15}
\]

From the explicit `RE-036` definition of `\Psi`, if

\[
A=\frac{\nu}{b}
\left(x+\frac{\nu s}{1-\nu s}\right),
\]

then direct differentiation gives

\[
\Psi'_{b,\ell}(q_0)
=
\frac{1}{b(1-A)}
\left[
1+\frac{\nu}{(1-x)(1-\nu s)^2}
\right]
-
\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\nu s)}.
\tag{16}
\]

For `b\in J`, the right side is analytic in `(x,\nu)` on one fixed neighborhood of `(0,0)`. In particular

\[
\Psi'_{b,\ell}(q_0)
=\frac{1-b}{b}+o_J(1).
\tag{17}
\]

More importantly, the two endpoints are much closer than this pointwise limit alone records. If `D_\pm:=Z_\pm-Y_\pm`, then (13) and `Y_+-Y_-=L_p` imply

\[
D_+-D_-=o_J(L_p).
\tag{18}
\]

Also `D_\pm/Z_\pm=o_J(1)` by the uniform small-height regime of `RE-040`. Therefore

\[
x_+-x_-=o_J(L_p/p),
\qquad
\nu_+-\nu_-=O_J((pL_p)^{-1}),
\tag{19}
\]

and analyticity of (16) yields

\[
\Psi'_{b,\ell_+}(q_{0,+})
-
\Psi'_{b,\ell_-}(q_{0,-})
=o_J(L_p/p).
\tag{20}
\]

Combining (11), (13), and (20),

\[
\boxed{
P\left[
\frac{\Psi'_{b,\ell_+}(q_{0,+})}{\sqrt{Z_+}}
-
\frac{\Psi'_{b,\ell_-}(q_{0,-})}{\sqrt{Z_-}}
\right]
=o_J(L_p/\sqrt p).
}
\tag{21}
\]

Thus neither the finite-exponent tail nor the common higher-layer mass can contribute at the leading individual-prime scale in the selected finite difference.

## 3. The lower endpoint is matched; the upper endpoint has exactly one possible unmatched prime

At `Z_-`, `RE-044` gives

\[
\eta_{p^-}<Z_-<p,
\tag{22}
\]

where `p^-` is the previous ordinary prime. Since first-layer event coordinates are ordered with their primes, every ordinary prime at most `Z_-` has already fired its first-layer CA event. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta_\vartheta(Z_-)=\delta_\ell(Z_-)=0.
}
\tag{23}
\]

At `Z_+`, the generating event has fired because `\eta_p<Z_+`. `RE-044` also proves that no ordinary prime lies in `(p,Z_+-1/2]`, and that at most one prime may lie in the final half-unit `(Z_+-1/2,Z_+]`. Therefore the only possible unmatched first-layer source is the next prime `p^+`. With `\chi_p` from (1),

\[
\boxed{
\delta_\vartheta(Z_+)=\chi_p\log p^+,
\qquad
\delta_\ell(Z_+)
=
\chi_p\bigl[-\log(1-(p^+)^{-1})\bigr].
}
\tag{24}
\]

If `\chi_p=1`, equation (2) and `Z_+\sim p` imply

\[
p^+\sim p,
\qquad
\log p^+\sim\log p.
\tag{25}
\]

The two endpoint terms in (9) now have the same sign. From (17), (24), and (25),

\[
\Psi'_{b,\ell_+}(q_{0,+})
\frac{\delta_\vartheta(Z_+)}{\sqrt{Z_+}}
=
\chi_p
\left(\frac{1-b}{b}+o_J(1)\right)
\frac{L_p}{\sqrt p},
\tag{26}
\]

while

\[
N(Z_+)\delta_\ell(Z_+)
=
\chi_p\left(1+o_J(1)\right)
\frac{L_p}{\sqrt p}.
\tag{27}
\]

Their coefficients add to

\[
1+\frac{1-b}{b}=\frac1b.
\tag{28}
\]

Subtracting the two instances of (9), using (14), (21), (23), and the `O(p^{-1/2})` Taylor errors, proves (4)--(5).

## 4. Why the generating prime disappears from the selected finite difference

The cancellation in (4) is not a cancellation of the ordinary prime-race jump itself. `RE-042` remains exact: at the fixed coordinate `p`, crossing the prime changes the translated residual by

\[
\frac{\log p}{b\sqrt p}(1+o_J(1)).
\tag{29}
\]

What changes here is the comparison. The lower selected state lies before the ordinary jump at `p`, while the upper selected state lies after **both** the ordinary jump and the delayed first-layer event `\eta_p`. In the exact source-separated identity (9), a first-layer prime contributes at leading order only while its ordinary race contribution and CA event contribution are unmatched. Once both have occurred, it leaves no endpoint correction; the remaining change in `T(C)` is only `O(p^{-2})`.

Thus the half-unit prime/event staggering of `RE-042` creates a transient residual quantum between `p` and `\eta_p`, but the two tied adaptive endpoints of `RE-043` do not sample that transient interval. They sample the matched state before the prime and the matched state after its event. This is why the generating prime does not survive in (4).

The optional `p^+` behaves differently. If it lies below `Z_+`, its ordinary prime-race jump has already occurred but its first-layer event is forced by chamber adjacency to satisfy `\eta_{p^+}>Z_+`. It is therefore exactly one unmatched source atom, and it produces the full coefficient `1/b` in (4).

This also turns the upper-gap geometry of `RE-044` into a residual diagnostic. If `\chi_p=1`, then (2) and the `RE-043` expansion for `Z_+` force

\[
p^+-p
=\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)\log p.
\tag{30}
\]

So the nonzero binary quantum occurs only when the next-prime gap asymptotically saturates the half-log lower scale forced by `RE-044`, up to the terminal half-unit event lag.

## 5. Stress tests and the resulting obstruction

Equation (4) does **not** prove RH and does not show that adjacent first-layer switches occur infinitely often. It applies only when the common false-RH fan actually exposes a tie between adjacent CA states whose central transition is one first-layer prime atom. Tied multi-atom transitions and higher-layer central transitions remain outside this statement.

It also shows that one seemingly natural strengthening of the current route would still be insufficient. Suppose a future argument sharpened the uniform `RE-040` calibration on this subfamily to

\[
\mathcal R_b(Z_\pm)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p}\right).
\tag{31}
\]

Then (4) would force `\chi_p=0` eventually, so

\[
p^+>Z_+.
\tag{32}
\]

That would strengthen the source-selected upper prime-gap condition, but **the generating prime `p` itself would still create no contradiction**: its first-order residual quantum is gone by the time the upper adaptive selector is reached. A local proof must therefore either sample the transient interval `(p,\eta_p)` by some additional selector, force an incompatible pattern of fringe indicators across many switches, or aggregate deeper/common-source data that does not cancel after each atom is matched.

Conversely, `\chi_p=1` is perfectly compatible with the current `o(1)` theorem because `L_p/\sqrt p\to0`. The binary law sharpens the resolution target but does not manufacture a contradiction from the existing accuracy.

The synthetic-support controls of `RE-039`--`RE-041` also remain relevant. They can reproduce selector geometry and support capacity, but they have no forced analog of the paired endpoint corrections (24) generated by one ordinary prime before its CA event. Equation (4) therefore uses genuine common-source quantization even though its immediate strategic conclusion is negative.

## 6. Prior-art boundary and research consequence

The first-layer CA threshold and event ordering are classical Alaoglu--Erdos structure; the exact prime-layer selector/workload framework is already bounded by Mantovanelli in `SOURCES.md`. Zimov is the nearby source for consecutive CA quotient analysis, while Bhattacharya--Martin--Simpson and Kalyabin bound the standard/reciprocal prime-race and Mertens/Gronwall sides. A targeted search also found current work on global/smoothed Robin defects and explicit windows for hypothetical CA counterexamples, but no source deriving the adaptive selected-residual finite difference (4) or the cancellation of the generating first-layer prime against its own event.

No new external theorem is load-bearing here: the proof uses only the exact local identities already persisted in `RE-036`--`RE-044` plus elementary Taylor/analytic estimates. `SOURCES.md` therefore needs no new dependency.

The frontier is narrower than after `RE-044`. The first-layer common-source information does survive the square-root normalization, but at adjacent adaptive endpoints it records **unmatched event state**, not the generating prime itself. The next local obstruction should therefore target the pattern of these binary fringe states, an additional selector inside the half-unit prime/event lag, or a multi-atom quantity whose contributions do not telescope when each ordinary prime is eventually matched by its CA event.
