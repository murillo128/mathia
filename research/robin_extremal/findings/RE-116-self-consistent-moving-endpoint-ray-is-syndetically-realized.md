# RE-116 — self-consistent moving endpoint ray is syndetically realized

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + FINITE-ZERO-EDGE + EDGE-NORMALIZED-ADAPTIVE-SELECTOR + MOVING-THRESHOLD + DIAGONAL-RAY + UNIFORM-ALMOST-PERIODICITY + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`.

Assume the finite attained off-critical edge regime of `RE-113`--`RE-115`,

\[
\frac12<\Theta:=\sup_{\zeta(\rho)=0}\Re\rho<1,
\qquad
c:=1-\Theta\in(0,1/2),
\tag{1}
\]

and retain the actual edge-face profiles

\[
F(u)=F_\Theta(u)
=
\sum_{\Re\rho=\Theta}\frac{e^{i\Im(\rho)u}}{\rho},
\qquad
G(u)=G_\Theta(u)
=
\sum_{\Re\rho=\Theta}\frac{e^{i\Im(\rho)u}}{1-\rho}.
\tag{2}
\]

`RE-115` shows that the edge-normalized adaptive selector carries the moving exponent

\[
b_Y=c+\frac1{\log Y},
\qquad Y=\log C,
\tag{3}
\]

and proves uniform syndetic realizability of the corresponding mixed ray when the exponent is frozen in a compact interval above `c`. There is, however, a genuine logical gap between

- for every fixed `b`, finding a phase `u` that realizes the `b`-ray, and
- finding a phase `u` that realizes the ray with the **same phase-dependent exponent** `b(u)=c+1/u`.

Uniformity in a parameter does not by itself solve this diagonal fixed-point problem.

In the actual edge face the diagonal problem collapses exactly to a weighted critical-point equation. Put

\[
J(u):=F(u)+G(u).
\tag{4}
\]

Then the coefficient identity already used in `RE-115` gives

\[
\boxed{J'(u)=cJ(u)-F(u).}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently, for every `u>0`, if

\[
b(u):=c+\frac1u,
\tag{6}
\]

then the mixed-ray residual satisfies the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
K_{b(u)}(u)
:=b(u)G(u)-(1-b(u))F(u)
=J'(u)+\frac{J(u)}u
=\frac{(uJ(u))'}u.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Hence the self-consistent moving endpoint ray is realized exactly at the weighted critical points

\[
\boxed{(uJ(u))'=0.}
\tag{8}
\]

Moreover, these positive weighted critical points are uniformly syndetic. There exist constants

\[
U_0>0,
\qquad L<\infty,
\qquad m_0>0,
\tag{9}
\]

such that every interval `[U,U+L]` with `U>=U_0` contains a point `u_*` for which

\[
\boxed{
(u_*J(u_*))'=0,
\qquad
J(u_*)\ge m_0.
}
\tag{10}
\]

At every such point, for all sufficiently large `u_*`,

\[
\boxed{
F(u_*)=b(u_*)J(u_*),
\qquad
G(u_*)=(1-b(u_*))J(u_*),
}
\tag{11}
\]

with

\[
c<b(u_*)<\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus even the exact logarithmically drifting exponent generated by the edge-normalized CA variational problem is spectrally compatible with the actual zeta edge face on a relatively dense set. The `1/\log Y` drift does not create an endpoint spectral obstruction.

## 1. The stable-filter coordinate linearizes the moving ray

For a fixed exponent `b`, `RE-115` uses the ray residual

\[
K_b(u):=bG(u)-(1-b)F(u).
\tag{13}
\]

Since `J=F+G`,

\[
K_b=b(J-F)-(1-b)F=bJ-F.
\tag{14}
\]

From (5),

\[
F=cJ-J',
\tag{15}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{K_b=J'+(b-c)J.}
\tag{16}
\]

This is the stable first-order filter already exposed by `RE-115`. What changes here is that the selector does not ask for one externally chosen `b`. Its exact exponent at phase

\[
u=\log Y
\tag{17}
\]

is

\[
b_Y=c+\frac1{\log Y}=c+\frac1u.
\tag{18}
\]

Substituting (18) into (16) gives (7):

\[
K_{b(u)}(u)=J'(u)+\frac{J(u)}u.
\tag{19}
\]

The apparent two-coordinate diagonal problem therefore becomes one scalar equation. Multiplying by `u` gives

\[
uK_{b(u)}(u)=uJ'(u)+J(u)=(uJ(u))'.
\tag{20}
\]

There is no approximation and no replacement of `1/u` by a constant. This is exactly the moving exponent generated by the logarithmic factor in the block functional of `RE-115`.

If `K_{b(u)}(u)=0`, then (14) gives

\[
F(u)=b(u)J(u).
\tag{21}
\]

Since `G=J-F`,

\[
G(u)=(1-b(u))J(u).
\tag{22}
\]

Thus a positive zero of `(uJ)'` is exactly a positive point of the self-consistent moving ray, with ray amplitude

\[
q=J(u)>0.
\tag{23}
\]

Finally, because `c<1/2`,

\[
b(u)=c+\frac1u<\frac12
\tag{24}
\]

for every sufficiently large `u`. Hence the diagonal points eventually lie in the same admissible exponent chamber as the adaptive CA selectors.

## 2. Positive diagonal zeros occur with bounded gaps

The issue is not existence of isolated zeros of (19), but whether the slowly moving correction `J(u)/u` could destroy the syndetic ray recurrence obtained for frozen exponents.

It cannot.

`RE-115` proves, from the nontrivial actual edge spectrum and uniform almost periodicity of the pair `(J,J')`, the existence of a compact positive transversality cell. Concretely there are

\[
u_-<u_+,
\qquad
m>0,
\qquad
\alpha>0,
\tag{25}
\]

such that

\[
J(u)\ge m
\quad (u_-\le u\le u_+),
\tag{26}
\]

and

\[
J'(u_-)\ge\alpha,
\qquad
J'(u_+)\le-\alpha.
\tag{27}
\]

The same argument provides uniform almost periods for the vector `(J,J')`: for every sufficiently small `\varepsilon>0`, the set of shifts `\tau` satisfying

\[
\sup_u|J(u+\tau)-J(u)|<\varepsilon,
\qquad
\sup_u|J'(u+\tau)-J'(u)|<\varepsilon
\tag{28}
\]

is relatively dense.

Choose

\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{m/2,\alpha/4\}.
\tag{29}
\]

Then every such translated cell satisfies

\[
J(u+\tau)\ge m/2
\quad (u_-\le u\le u_+),
\tag{30}
\]

and

\[
J'(u_-+\tau)\ge3\alpha/4,
\qquad
J'(u_++\tau)\le-3\alpha/4.
\tag{31}
\]

Because `J` is bounded, let

\[
M:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}|J(u)|<\infty.
\tag{32}
\]

For every translated cell sufficiently far to the right,

\[
\frac{M}{u_-+\tau}<\frac\alpha4.
\tag{33}
\]

Define

\[
Q(u):=J'(u)+\frac{J(u)}u.
\tag{34}
\]

At the two ends of a late translated cell, (31)--(33) imply

\[
Q(u_-+\tau)>\frac\alpha2>0,
\qquad
Q(u_++\tau)<-\frac\alpha2<0.
\tag{35}
\]

By continuity there is

\[
u_*\in(u_-+\tau,u_++\tau)
\tag{36}
\]

with

\[
Q(u_*)=0.
\tag{37}
\]

Equation (30) simultaneously gives

\[
J(u_*)\ge m/2.
\tag{38}
\]

Thus `u_*` is a positive diagonal ray point with a uniform amplitude floor. Since the admissible shifts `\tau` are relatively dense and the cell width `u_+-u_-` is fixed, the resulting points `u_*` are also relatively dense. This proves (9)--(10) with, for instance, `m_0=m/2` and a syndeticity length enlarged by the fixed cell width.

The important point is that the perturbation created by the moving exponent is only

\[
\frac{J(u)}u=O(1/u),
\tag{39}
\]

whereas the transversality cell has fixed derivative margin `\alpha`. Once the cell is translated far enough right, the diagonal correction is too small to remove its sign change.

## 3. This closes a real diagonal loophole in `RE-115`

The uniform-in-`b` theorem of `RE-115` says, schematically, that for every fixed

\[
b\in[c,c+\delta]
\tag{40}
\]

the equation `K_b(u)=0` has positive solutions with bounded gaps, uniformly over `b`.

That statement alone does **not** imply that

\[
K_{c+1/u}(u)=0
\tag{41}
\]

has any solution. A family of nonempty uniformly syndetic zero sets indexed by `b` need not intersect the diagonal `b=c+1/u` at all. Treating uniformity as a fixed-point theorem would therefore be invalid.

Equation (7) removes that ambiguity rather than estimating it. The diagonal is not found by intersecting a two-parameter incidence set with a moving graph; it is the zero set of the single scalar function

\[
Q(u)=\frac{(uJ(u))'}u.
\tag{42}
\]

The translated positive transversality cells then force exact diagonal intersections directly.

Consequently the edge-normalized selector cannot be obstructed merely because its exponent slides toward the endpoint at rate `1/\log Y`. Any future finite-edge obstruction must use information absent from this edge-face compatibility problem: arithmetic placement of actual CA selectors, lower-order/non-edge spectral terms at the required resolution, inter-block recovery geometry, or another source-specific constraint.

## 4. Representation audit and matched control

The representation kill test is especially useful here. The edge face may be written in two coordinates `(F,G)`, but (5) shows that it is generated by the single stable-filter state `J` and its derivative:

\[
F=cJ-J',
\qquad
G=(1-c)J+J'.
\tag{43}
\]

For the moving endpoint exponent, the residual itself collapses to the weighted derivative (42). Introducing additional coordinates built only from the same edge pair would not create a new obstruction; the diagonal condition already lives in a one-dimensional differential representation.

A matched single-frequency control confirms the geometry. Take

\[
J(u)=A\cos(\gamma u+\phi),
\qquad A\ne0.
\tag{44}
\]

Then

\[
(uJ(u))'
=A\bigl(\cos(\gamma u+\phi)-\gamma u\sin(\gamma u+\phi)\bigr).
\tag{45}
\]

For large `u`, there is one zero close to each ordinary critical point with positive cosine, and these positive zeros have bounded gaps. Thus the theorem is not using a hidden multi-frequency phenomenon. The `1/u` drift merely shifts the ordinary critical phases by `O(1/u)` in this control model.

This control also marks the limit of the result. Syndetic diagonal realizability is a compatibility theorem, not a contradiction. A one-frequency edge model already realizes it, so no argument based only on the existence, density, or positivity of these diagonal points can distinguish the actual zeta source.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

Bohr almost periodicity, relatively dense almost periods, and recurrence of uniformly almost-periodic finite/trigonometric packets are classical. `RE-114`--`RE-115` already imported exactly the edge-face recurrence needed here, so this finding introduces no new external theorem and no new load-bearing source anchor.

The line-specific content is narrower:

1. the exact selector drift `b_Y=c+1/\log Y` from `RE-115` is imposed **at the same phase** `u=\log Y`;
2. the actual zeta edge-face identity `J'=cJ-F` converts that diagonal ray equation exactly into `(uJ)'=0`;
3. the positive transversality cell already proved for the actual edge face survives the `O(1/u)` weighted-derivative perturbation, giving positive diagonal solutions with a uniform amplitude floor and bounded gaps.

The prior-art audit found the surrounding almost-periodic recurrence to be classical, but no equivalent Robin/CA statement coupling the exact edge-normalized moving selector to this weighted critical-point equation. Novelty should therefore be understood as this Mathia-specific reduction and obstruction closure, not as a new theorem about almost-periodic functions in general.

## 6. Boundary conditions and falsification tests

The claim is conditional on the same finite attained edge regime as `RE-113`--`RE-115`. In particular it uses the actual edge profiles and the uniform almost-periodicity/transversality package already established there. It does not address the branch `\Theta=1`, nor does it turn a non-attained supremum into an edge profile.

The theorem is also purely spectral. It does **not** prove that actual CA block maximizers occur at the phases `u_*`, and it does not replace the arithmetic placement problem. The edge face supplies compatible phases; the CA sequence still has to hit them with the required lower-order accuracy.

There are three decisive audit tests:

- **diagonal algebra:** expand `K_{c+1/u}=bG-(1-b)F` using `J=F+G` and `J'=cJ-F`; any remainder would invalidate the exact reduction;
- **positivity:** the IVT root must be taken inside a translated interval on which `J` stays uniformly positive, not merely between two positive endpoints separated by an uncontrolled excursion;
- **syndeticity:** the shifts used to reproduce the transversality cell must be common almost periods of both `J` and `J'`; recurrence of `J` alone does not preserve the endpoint derivative signs.

All three conditions are satisfied by the `RE-115` edge-face package and the argument above.
