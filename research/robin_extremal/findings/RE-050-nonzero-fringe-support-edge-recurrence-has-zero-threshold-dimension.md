# RE-050 — nonzero fringe support-edge recurrence has zero threshold dimension

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + THRESHOLD-SUPPORT-QUANTIZATION + SUMMABLE-EDGE-CAPACITY + HAUSDORFF-ZERO-RECURRENCE + COUNTEREXAMPLE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-048` shows that a nonzero fringe switch enters its new CA state in the final `O(1/log p)` fraction of that state's genuine support interval, while `RE-049` forces the outgoing event onto the fixed base-two ladder and makes the host coordinates multiplicatively lacunary. Combining the two facts gives an aggregate threshold-space restriction that neither result alone records: the remaining right-support slacks are not merely small individually; their lengths are summable, all positive Hausdorff costs are summable, and the set of thresholds recurrently lying in those exceptional edge layers has Hausdorff dimension zero.

## 1. The support slack has an exact source-separated identity

At the selected state,

\[
g(Z)=g(Y)-\frac{\beta a}{Y},
\qquad
g(x)=\frac1{x\log x}.
\tag{19}
\]

The outgoing supporting slope is

\[
\varepsilon_+(C)=g(\xi),
\tag{20}
\]

so the upper endpoint of the genuine CA support interval is

\[
\kappa_C^+
=\frac{Y\bigl(g(Y)-g(\xi)\bigr)}a.
\tag{21}
\]

Subtracting the selected threshold gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_C^+-\beta
=\frac{Y\bigl(g(Z)-g(\xi)\bigr)}a.
}
\tag{22}
\]

This quantity is a **support slack**, not necessarily the width of the exposed fan cell. A competing amplitude line may terminate the actual cell before `kappa_C^+`; equation (22) measures how much of the state's physical CA supporting interval remains available after the nonzero-fringe breakpoint.

By the mean-value theorem there is `zeta in (Z,xi)` with

\[
g(Z)-g(\xi)=|g'(\zeta)|(\xi-Z).
\tag{23}
\]

`RE-048`--`RE-049` give

\[
Z\sim\xi\sim Y\sim p,
\tag{24}
\]

uniformly on the compact threshold interval. Since

\[
|g'(x)|
=\frac{\log x+1}{x^2(\log x)^2},
\tag{25}
\]

we obtain

\[
Y|g'(\zeta)|
=\frac{1+o_J(1)}{Y\log Y}.
\tag{26}
\]

Substituting into (22),

\[
\kappa_C^+-\beta
=(1+o_J(1))
\frac{\xi-Z}{aY\log Y}.
\tag{27}
\]

The amplitude normalization now cancels exactly. Put

\[
R:=e^h-1=cY^{-\beta}.
\tag{28}
\]

Then

\[
a=1-e^{-h}=\frac{R}{1+R},
\qquad
\frac{cY^{-\beta}}a=1+R.
\tag{29}
\]

The uniform small-height regime of `RE-040` gives `R->0`. Multiplying (27) by `cY^(1-beta)log Y` and using (29) yields (7):

\[
cY^{1-\beta}\log Y
(\kappa_C^+-\beta)
=(1+o_J(1))(1+R)(\xi-Z)
=\xi-Z+o_J(1).
\tag{30}
\]

This is stronger than the one-sided upper scale in `RE-048`: after normalization by the **actual adaptive amplitude** `c`, the threshold slack is asymptotically the physical event-coordinate clearance itself.

## 2. The base-two quotient supplies a nonvanishing lower quantum

`RE-048` alone gives only

\[
0<\xi-Z<\frac12,
\tag{31}
\]

which is enough for an upper bound but allows the normalized slack to collapse to zero. `RE-049` supplies the missing lower scale. Amplitude maximality and barrier curvature give

\[
\xi-Z
\ge
\left(\frac12-o_J(1)\right)\Delta,
\qquad
\Delta=\log(D/C),
\tag{32}

where `D` is the immediate next CA state. The same half-unit upper clearance then forces

\[
D/C=2,

\[
\frac{\log2}{2}-o_J(1)

Combining (30) and (34) proves the two-sided quantization (9). In particular the right-support slack is not merely `O(Y^(b_1-1)/log Y)`; after the intrinsic factor `cY^(1-beta)log Y` it stays between two positive universal constants.

For the uniform upper bound, the same-block inheritance from `RE-040` gives

\[
c=A_\beta(C)\ge c_0,
\tag{35}
\]

while `beta<=b_1`. Equation (10) therefore gives (11).

## 3. Base-two host lacunarity makes every Hausdorff cost summable

`RE-049` proves that distinct nonzero-fringe switches inject into distinct deep base-two layers. Their outgoing event coordinates are consequently multiplicatively lacunary as in (12). Since `Y_n/xi_n->1`, for every `r<2` the late host coordinates satisfy (13).

Put

\[
\alpha:=1-b_1>\frac12.
\tag{36}
\]

The function

\[
f(x)=\frac{x^{-\alpha}}{\log x}
\tag{37}
\]

is eventually decreasing. Equations (11) and (13) therefore give, after discarding finitely many hosts,

\[
\kappa_{C_n}^+-\beta_n
\ll_J
\frac{Y_n^{-\alpha}}{\log Y_n}
\ll_J r^{-\alpha n}.
\tag{38}
\]

The geometric majorant proves (14). If the first late host with `Y>=X` has coordinate `P`, then `P>=X`, `f(P)<=f(X)`, and the same geometric tail proves (15).

For any fixed `s>0`, raising (38) to the power `s` still leaves a geometric series,

\[
\sum_n
(\kappa_{C_n}^+-\beta_n)^s
\ll_{J,s}
\sum_n r^{-\alpha s n}<\infty,
\tag{39}
\]

which is (16). The strength here comes from the fixed-base reduction of `RE-049`: generic higher-layer CA events have only a much weaker aggregate count, while one base-two ladder supplies exponential separation in the host index.

## 4. The recurrent exceptional threshold set has Hausdorff dimension zero

The interval `E_p` in (17) has diameter at most

\[
|E_p|\le\kappa_C^+-\beta.
\tag{40}
\]

For every `s>0`, equation (16) gives

\[
\sum_{\chi_p=1}|E_p|^s<\infty.
\tag{41}
\]

For each tail of the host sequence, the corresponding intervals cover the limsup set

\[
\mathcal E_\infty
:=
\bigcap_{N\ge1}
\bigcup_{n\ge N}E_{p_n}.
\tag{42}
\]

The `s`-dimensional Hausdorff content of that cover tends to zero by (41). Hence

\[
\mathcal H^s(\mathcal E_\infty)=0
\qquad\text{for every }s>0,
\tag{43}
\]

and therefore

\[
\dim_H\mathcal E_\infty=0.
\tag{44}
\]

The weaker first Borel--Cantelli conclusion also follows from (14): the limsup set has Lebesgue measure zero. The Hausdorff conclusion is sharper and is still purely deterministic; no independence, equidistribution, randomness, or probabilistic model for primes is used.

## 5. Stress tests and exact boundary of the claim

The result does **not** show that nonzero fringe bits occur, nor that they must recur for a positive-measure set of thresholds. If all sufficiently late first-layer switches have `chi_p=0`, every statement here is vacuous. If nonzero bits do occur infinitely often, (18) says only that their associated right-support neighborhoods cannot recur on a positive-dimensional threshold set.

The exceptional intervals are physical **support** intervals, not guaranteed fan-occupancy intervals. A state may stop maximizing before its support edge because another amplitude line overtakes it. Thus (18) must not be read as a theorem about the measure of all exposed fan cells. It is a stronger statement in one direction and a weaker one in another: the interval is defined by genuine CA support geometry, but membership does not imply that the fan actually selects that state throughout the interval.

Nor does Hausdorff dimension zero contradict false RH. The current common-fan theory requires a maximizer for every `b in J`, but it does not require the nonzero-fringe mechanism to carry a positive-dimensional subset of `J`. The fan may use `chi_p=0` first-layer transitions, skipped CA states, other exceptional transitions, or a null set of support-edge events. A closing argument would need an independent theorem forcing a positive-dimensional or otherwise nonsummable amount of the false-RH common-source signal through these nonzero-fringe edge layers.

The proof also does not assume the Alaoglu--Erdos conjecture that consecutive CA quotients are prime. The incoming switch is assumed to be the same unique first-layer atom used by `RE-043`--`RE-049`; the outgoing quotient `2` is already a theorem of `RE-049` under `chi_p=1`.

No external theorem beyond the line's existing source anchors is load-bearing, so `SOURCES.md` does not require a new dependency.

The resulting threshold-space capacity conclusion is not a new theorem about arbitrary convex envelopes or arbitrary lacunary intervals.

## Consequence

The live interruption problem has acquired an aggregate threshold-space boundary. Nonzero fringe bits cannot merely be described as rare physical hosts: their entire post-switch physical support slacks have finite total length, finite `s`-cost for every positive `s`, and zero-dimensional recurrent limsup in the common threshold interval.

This rules out one more possible accumulation mechanism. Any contradiction to false RH based on the exceptional edge channel must now prove that the common-source arithmetic forces more threshold capacity than this base-two channel can supply—for example positive-dimensional recurrence, a nonsummable support demand, or a separate signal attached to `chi_p=0` and the skipped/exceptional transitions. Without such a lower-demand theorem, zero-dimensional fringe recurrence remains compatible with the current false-RH fan.
