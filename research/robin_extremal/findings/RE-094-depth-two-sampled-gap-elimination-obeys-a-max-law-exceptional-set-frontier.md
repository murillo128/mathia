## 1. Small mixed chambers are paid for by the square-root host count

Work after the `RE-091` reduction, and fix a bounded-ratio physical shell

\[
X\le Y_C<2X.
\]

A residual cell in `\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)` has a depth-`2` support boundary generated by some ordinary prime `q`. The fixed-layer event asymptotic from `RE-054` gives

\[
\eta_2(q)=\frac{q^2}{2}(1+o(1)),
\]

so every such host satisfies `q asymp sqrt(X)`. A physical event coordinate borders at most two open support chambers. Hence the prime number theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\#\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)
\ll \frac{X^{1/2}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Let

\[
\Delta_C:=\xi_+(C)-\xi_-(C)
\]

be the full physical support-chamber width. The exact support-capacity law of `RE-040`, in the form used by `RE-090` and `RE-091`, gives uniformly

\[
\boxed{
|I_C|
\ll_J
\frac{\Delta_C X^{b_1-1}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Set `H=X^vartheta`. For cells with

\[
\Delta_C<4H,
\]

(8)--(9) immediately give

\[
\sum_{\substack{C\in\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)\\\Delta_C<4H}}
|I_C|
\ll_J
X^{b_1+\vartheta-1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{10}
\]

This part vanishes exactly when

\[
\vartheta<\frac12-b_1.
\tag{11}
\]

The first term in (2) is therefore not an analytic-number-theory loss. It is the deterministic price of having `X^(1/2+o(1))` possible second-layer hosts.

## 2. Large mixed chambers inject disjoint mass into the PNT exceptional set

Now suppose

\[
\Delta_C\ge4H.
\]

Pull the full support chamber back through the first-layer event map:

\[
Q_C:=\eta_1^{-1}\bigl((\xi_-(C),\xi_+(C))\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

Exactly as in `RE-091`, `Q_C` contains no ordinary prime and

\[
|Q_C|=(1+o_J(1))\Delta_C.
\tag{13}
\]

Distinct exposed cells have disjoint open support chambers, and `eta_1` is increasing, so the intervals `Q_C` are pairwise disjoint. For every sufficiently large `X`, each large `Q_C=(u_C,v_C)` therefore contains a subinterval of start points of length `gg Delta_C` such that

\[
[x,x+H]\subset Q_C.
\tag{14}
\]

There are no ordinary primes in the interval on the right. Because `vartheta<1/2`, prime powers cannot fake a PNT main term on such an interval. At scale `X`, consecutive squares are separated by `asymp sqrt(X)>H`; for each exponent `k>=2` there are only `O(1)` `k`-th powers in an `H`-interval, there are only `O(log X)` possible exponents, and every von Mangoldt weight is `O(log X)`. Hence

\[
\sum_{x<n\le x+H}\Lambda(n)
=O((\log X)^2)
=o(H).
\tag{15}
\]

Thus every start point in (14) belongs to the fixed-`delta` PNT exceptional set once `X` is large. Since the start-point intervals inherited from distinct `Q_C` remain disjoint, (1), (13), and (14) imply

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{C\in\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)\\\Delta_C\ge4H}}
\Delta_C
\ll
X^{m(\vartheta)+o(1)}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Applying the CA capacity conversion (9) once more gives

\[
\sum_{\substack{C\in\mathcal M_{1/2}(X)\\\Delta_C\ge4H}}
|I_C|
\ll_J
X^{b_1-1+m(\vartheta)+o(1)},
\tag{17}
\]

which is the second term of (2). This part vanishes precisely when

\[
m(\vartheta)<1-b_1.
\tag{18}
\]

The argument uses exceptional-set **measure**, not independence of sampled gaps and not an injective assignment of cells to consecutive-prime gaps. The disjoint first-layer inverse chambers themselves supply the required measure packing.

## 3. The two costs combine into the max-law

Conditions (11) and (18) say that one can choose `b_1` only if

\[
1-\Theta<b_1<
\min\!\left(
\frac12-\vartheta,
1-m(\vartheta)
\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Such a compact fan interval exists exactly when

\[
1-\Theta<\frac12-\vartheta
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
1-\Theta<1-m(\vartheta),
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\Theta>rac12+\vartheta,
\qquad

\tag{20}

This proves (4)--(5).

The law clarifies why the interval-length exponent by itself is not the relevant currency. Making `vartheta` tiny reduces the deterministic small-chamber term, but unless the exceptional set has a **polynomially saving measure exponent** below `Theta`, the large chambers can still carry all the threshold mass. Conversely, an excellent exceptional-measure exponent at a large `vartheta` is useless once `1/2+vartheta` exceeds the desired false-RH frontier.

At the existing depth-two second-moment endpoint

\Theta_*:=\frac{173}{200},

\[
\vartheta<\Theta_*-\frac12

\[
m(\vartheta)<\Theta_*.
\]

This is (6). It is the exact target an ambient short-interval exceptional-set theorem must cross before it can outperform `RE-091`--`RE-093` on the mixed `1/2` channel.

## 4. Current Gafni--Tao majorants miss the target even after truncating at `Theta`

Gafni and Tao, *On the number of exceptional intervals to the prime number theorem in short intervals*, Essential Number Theory **5** (2026), 221--241, DOI `10.2140/ent.2026.5.221`, define the exceptional exponent `mu(vartheta)` and prove a general zero-density bound. Their refined Theorem 1.3 uses

\[
\mu_{2,\sigma}(\vartheta)
=(1-\vartheta)(1-\sigma)A(\sigma)+2\sigma-1
\tag{21}
\]

and

\[
\mu_{4,\sigma}(\vartheta)
=(1-\vartheta)(1-\sigma)A^*(\sigma)+4\sigma-3,
\tag{22}
\]

where `A` is the zero-density exponent and `A*` the zero-additive-energy exponent. Their Tables 1--2 record the current unconditional upper majorants used to turn the theorem into numerical exceptional-set estimates.

There is an important false-RH refinement to stress-test before comparing with (6). If the actual rightmost zero exponent is `Theta`, then no zeros occur for `sigma>Theta`, so one may restrict the relevant zero-density supremum to `sigma<=Theta`. This does improve the generic unconditional envelope, but it still does not cross the present endpoint.

Indeed take

\[
\sigma_0=\frac{17}{20}=0.85.
\]

For every

\[
\frac{17}{20}<\Theta\le\frac{173}{200}

The zero-density admissibility threshold is

which is smaller than `30/17` throughout the interval above. Thus `sigma_0` is present in the computable majorant supremum. Substitution into (21)--(22) gives

This is not a lower bound for the true `mu(vartheta)`. The actual zero set could have much less density or additive energy than the available majorants permit. The obstruction is epistemic and methodological: the currently proved general zero-density inputs leave enough room that the exceptional-set theorem cannot supply the power saving demanded by (20).

## 5. Very short "almost all" prime intervals do not address the missing exponent

A complementary stress test comes from sieve theorems with dramatically shorter interval lengths. Runbo Li's 2025 working paper *Primes in almost all short intervals II* proves that, for arbitrary fixed large `B`, all but

\[
O\!\left(X(\log X)^{-B}\right)
\]

integers `n in [X,2X]` have primes in intervals of length `n^(1/22+epsilon)`; the paper's final remark reports a further `1/24+epsilon` version. These interval exponents are vastly smaller than `73/200`.

For the present problem, however, the exceptional-count exponent is still `1` on the polynomial scale:

\[
X(\log X)^{-B}=X^{1+o(1)}.
\tag{27}
\]

In the notation of (1), this behaves as `m=1`, and therefore cannot satisfy

\[
m<1-b_1
\]

for any positive `b_1`. Equivalently, a family of prime-free chambers of length `D=X^d` could still hide inside that logarithmically sparse exceptional set with count as large as

\[
X^{1-d}(\log X)^{-B};
\]

at the `RE-093` scale `d=73/200`, this is `X^(127/200+o(1))`, still much larger than the `X^(1/2+o(1))` depth-two host population.

Thus "almost all intervals contain primes" and "the bad set has sufficiently small power-law mass for the CA fan" are materially different statements. The latter is what (2)--(5) require.

Li's result is a non-peer-reviewed working paper and is used here only as a stress-test boundary, not as a load-bearing dependency. The same conceptual warning applies to older almost-all fixed-power results: a density-zero exceptional family may still contain every prime-indexed quadratic host unless its size is quantitatively below the sparse host/capacity scale.

## 6. Boundary of the result and next useful theorem

Equation (2) does **not** prove that `173/200` is an intrinsic barrier. It proves that an ambient exceptional-set splice based only on

\[
\text{depth-2 host count}
+
\text{first-layer chamber length}
+
\text{one-variable short-interval exceptional measure}
\]

must beat the max-law (5). A theorem conditioned directly on

\[
x_q=\eta_1^{-1}(\eta_2(q)),
\qquad q\ \text{prime},
\]

need not pay the ambient exceptional measure at all. Likewise, a rightmost-maximizer constraint could discard most depth-two hosts before any short-interval estimate is applied.

The finding also does not use the standard/reciprocal prime-race values attached to a selected cell. `RE-093` deliberately left those out of its PNT-dense integer control. A correlation theorem saying that large chamber-facing gaps at the sampled `x_q` are incompatible with the CA-selected Chebyshev/Mertens race state could evade (5), because it would no longer be a one-variable exceptional-set theorem.

The prior-art audit therefore changes the target rather than merely reporting that a new short-interval theorem exists. Gafni--Tao supplies exactly the right language for measuring the missing resource, and current sieve work shows that interval length alone can be made extremely small; neither presently supplies enough **power-saving exceptional mass at the quadratic prime-indexed sample** to improve the `173/200` frontier.

No new external theorem is load-bearing for the exact CA reduction (2)--(5): it is a conditional transfer statement valid for any exceptional exponent `m(vartheta)`. Gafni--Tao and Li are used only to calibrate the present technology boundary, so `SOURCES.md` does not need a new durable dependency.

The durable next target is now quantitative and falsifiable: either prove a general exceptional-set estimate crossing

\[
\boxed{
\exists\,\vartheta<\frac{73}{200}
\quad\text{with}\quad
m(\vartheta)<\frac{173}{200},
}
\]

or bypass ambient exceptional measure by proving a stronger theorem specifically for the prime-indexed CA samples `x_q`, preferably with the rightmost-maximizer or mixed prime-race state retained. Merely shortening the interval in an "almost all" theorem, without a power-saving exceptional exponent, cannot move the current frontier.
