# RE-048 — nonzero fringe bits pin adaptive switches to the right edge of CA support

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + FIRST-LAYER-EVENT-QUANTIZATION + CA-SUPPORT-EDGE-SATURATION + THRESHOLD-ASYMMETRY + COUNTEREXAMPLE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-047` shows that a nonzero upper-fringe bit forces the threshold cell entered at an adjacent first-layer fan switch to have vanishingly little room on its right. The same geometry yields a stronger statement that no longer depends on where the fan next switches or on whether the monitored compact interval ends first: **the switch itself is asymptotically pinned to the right edge of the genuine CA supporting-slope interval of the entered state.**

Assume RH is false, fix

\[
J=[b_0,b_1]\Subset(1-\Theta,1/2),
\]

and work on one sufficiently late common positive CA counterexample block from `RE-040`. Let `beta` be an interior breakpoint at which the rightmost adaptive fan makes a unique adjacent first-layer switch

\[
C_-<C_+,
\qquad
C_+=pC_-,
\tag{1}
\]

through the ordinary prime `p`. Put

\[
C:=C_+,
\qquad
Y:=\log C,
\qquad
h:=\log G(C)-\gamma>0,
\qquad
a:=1-e^{-h},
\tag{2}
\]

and let

\[
t:=g(Y)-\frac{\beta a}{Y}=g(Z),
\qquad
g(x)=\frac1{x\log x},
\tag{3}
\]

where `Z=Z_C(beta)` is the upper adaptive tangent selector. Let `epsilon_-(C)>epsilon_+(C)` be the genuine CA supporting slopes on the transitions immediately before and after `C`. As in `RE-040`, define the full physical threshold-support interval

\[
K_C=(\kappa_C^-,\kappa_C^+),
\tag{4}
\]

with

\[
\kappa_C^-:=\frac{Y\bigl(g(Y)-\varepsilon_-(C)\bigr)}a,
\qquad
\kappa_C^+:=\frac{Y\bigl(g(Y)-\varepsilon_+(C)\bigr)}a.
\tag{5}
\]

`RE-040` gives `beta in K_C` strictly. Let `p^+` be the next ordinary prime and retain the fringe bit

\[
\chi_p:=\mathbf 1_{\{p^+\le Z\}}.
\tag{6}
\]

If

\[
\boxed{\chi_p=1,}
\tag{7}
\]

then, uniformly along such switches escaping to infinity,

\[
\boxed{
\beta-\kappa_C^-
=\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)\frac1{ap},
}
\tag{8}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
0<\kappa_C^+-\beta
\le
\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)
\frac1{ap\log p}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
|K_C|
=\kappa_C^+-\kappa_C^-
=\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)\frac1{ap},
}
\tag{10}
\]

and the breakpoint occupies the final vanishing fraction of the entire physical support interval:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_C^+-\beta}{|K_C|}
=O_J\!\left(\frac1{\log p}\right),
\qquad
\frac{\beta-\kappa_C^-}{|K_C|}
=1-O_J\!\left(\frac1{\log p}\right).
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus `chi_p=1` does more than force the exposed fan cell to end quickly. The fan enters `C` only after the adaptive tangent has already traversed all but an `O(1/log p)` fraction of `C`'s **actual CA support chamber in threshold coordinates**. This support-edge saturation survives even if the fan terminates at `b_1`, skips many later CA states, or encounters a higher-layer/tied event before the next first-layer transition.

## 1. The fringe condition leaves less than half a unit before the physical chamber ends

Because the transition into `C` is the unique first-layer atom generated by `p`, its left supporting slope is exactly

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_-(C)=g(\eta_p),}
\tag{12}
\]

where `eta_p` is the first-layer CA event coordinate. Let `xi_C` be the next CA event coordinate after `eta_p`. Then

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_+(C)=g(\xi_C).}
\tag{13}
\]

This remains true if the next transition is higher-layer or tied: `xi_C` is simply the common event coordinate defining the right support slope.

Since `Z` is a genuine regular selector for `C`,

\[
\eta_p<Z<\xi_C.
\tag{14}
\]

The first-layer event of the next prime `p^+` is one future CA event, so by definition of the next event

\[
\xi_C\le\eta_{p^+}.
\tag{15}
\]

If `chi_p=1`, then `p^+<=Z`. The exact first-layer staggering from `RE-042`--`RE-044` gives, for sufficiently large primes,

\[
p^+<\eta_{p^+}<p^++\frac12.
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (14)--(16),

\[
\boxed{
0<\xi_C-Z
\le\eta_{p^+}-Z
<\frac12.
}
\tag{17}
\]

On the other side, `RE-043` gives the much larger distance from the entering event to the upper selector,

\[
\boxed{
Z-\eta_p
=\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)\log p.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Hence the selector at the fan switch is already asymptotically at the terminal end of the physical chamber:

\[
\frac{\xi_C-Z}{\xi_C-\eta_p}
=O_J\!\left(\frac1{\log p}\right).
\tag{19}
\]

This is a physical event-coordinate statement, independent of the next fan breakpoint. An intervening higher-layer or tied event only decreases the numerator in (19).

## 2. Physical chamber asymmetry becomes supporting-slope asymmetry

The relevant event coordinates all satisfy

\[
\eta_p\sim Z\sim\xi_C\sim Y\sim p.
\tag{20}
\]

Indeed `RE-043` gives `Z~Y~p`; under `chi_p=1`, `p^+<=Z=p+O_J(log p)` also gives `p^+~p`, and (15)--(16) then place `xi_C` on the same scale.

The derivative

\[
|g'(x)|
=\frac{\log x+1}{x^2(\log x)^2}
\tag{21}
\]

therefore satisfies uniformly throughout `[eta_p,xi_C]`

\[
|g'(x)|
=\frac{1+o_J(1)}{p^2\log p}.
\tag{22}
\]

Apply the mean-value theorem first between `eta_p` and `Z`. From (12), (18), and (22),

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_-(C)-t
=g(\eta_p)-g(Z)
=\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)\frac1{p^2}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Apply it again between `Z` and `xi_C`. Equations (13), (17), and (22) give

\[
\boxed{
0<t-\varepsilon_+(C)
\le
\left(\frac12+o_J(1)\right)
\frac1{p^2\log p}.
}
\tag{24}
\]

Thus the adaptive supporting slope at a nonzero-fringe switch is not centered inside the physical CA slope interval. It lies within a `1/log p` relative boundary layer of the **right** slope:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{t-\varepsilon_+(C)}
{\varepsilon_-(C)-t}
=O_J\!\left(\frac1{\log p}\right).
}
\tag{25}
\]

The factor `log p` is source-specific. It comes from the coexistence of the half-log tangent displacement `Z-eta_p` and the sub-half-unit unfired-prime window `xi_C-Z`; an abstract affine fan has no reason to produce this scale separation.

## 3. Converting slope slack to threshold-support slack proves the edge pinning

Equation (3) can be solved exactly for `beta`:

\[
\beta=\frac{Y(g(Y)-t)}a.
\tag{26}
\]

Subtracting the two endpoints (5) gives the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
\beta-\kappa_C^-
=\frac{Y\bigl(\varepsilon_-(C)-t\bigr)}a,
}
\tag{27}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_C^+-\beta
=\frac{Y\bigl(t-\varepsilon_+(C)\bigr)}a.
}
\tag{28}
\]

Since `Y/p->1`, substituting (23)--(24) into (27)--(28) proves (8)--(9). Equation (10) follows because the right-hand slack is smaller than the left-hand slack by a factor `O(1/log p)`, and (11) follows after division by the total width.

The quantitative lower amplitude inherited in `RE-040`,

\[
a\gg_J p^{-b_1},
\tag{29}
\]

also recovers the absolute right-edge scale

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_C^+-\beta
\ll_J\frac{p^{b_1-1}}{\log p}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

Since the exposed fan cell to the right of `beta` is contained in `K_C`, (30) implies the cell-thinning estimate of `RE-047`. The converse is not true: a short fan cell could be caused by a competing amplitude line well before the physical support boundary. Equations (8)--(11) show that a nonzero fringe bit pins the **physical support boundary itself**, so the strengthening is independent of how or when the next fan state becomes exposed.

## 4. Stress tests and boundary of the result

No abundance theorem for nonzero fringe bits is proved. If all sufficiently late adjacent first-layer switches have `chi_p=0`, the support-edge condition is silent. Likewise, the theorem does not show that the right support boundary is itself a first-layer event. If a higher-layer or tied event lies before `eta_(p^+)`, then `xi_C` is earlier and the right-edge pinning becomes only stronger.

The argument does not assume that consecutive CA quotients are always prime. Only the entering transition in (1) is required to be a unique first-layer atom. The right transition may be arbitrary, and the fan may skip it entirely. This avoids the open Alaoglu--Erdos consecutive-quotient issue.

Nor is (11) a contradiction from capacity alone. `RE-041` shows that abstract support staircases can realize arbitrary small threshold cells and the `RE-040` capacity inequality. What is new here is the **relative placement inside the physical support interval** forced by the same next ordinary prime whose unfired first-layer event bounds the chamber. A synthetic control that is allowed to assign arbitrary support slopes can imitate the numerical asymmetry; it becomes a faithful control only if it also reproduces the prime/event common-source constraint that creates (17).

The result uses no improvement to the `o(1)` translated prime-race remainder. In particular it is compatible with `RE-045`'s conclusion that the residual quantum itself remains below the current race-error floor. The gain is geometric: the binary mismatch is converted into a `1/log p` support-edge alignment that lives entirely in exact CA selector/event data.

## 5. Prior-art boundary

Alaoglu--Erdos supplies the classical CA supporting-slope and prime-exponent threshold structure already anchored in `SOURCES.md`. Mantovanelli's August 2026 prime-layer workload framework is the closest current source for expressing the same CA thresholds as ordered event coordinates, and Zimov studies neighboring consecutive-CA transition constraints. A targeted current search also finds recent Robin-defect/window work on CA counterexamples, but no result coupling an adaptive Robin-amplitude breakpoint to the **relative position inside its full CA support interval** through an unfired next-prime event.

No new external theorem is load-bearing. Equations (8)--(11) are obtained by combining the exact support interval of `RE-040`, the half-log tangent placement of `RE-043`, and the next-prime event isolation of `RE-044`--`RE-047`. `SOURCES.md` therefore requires no new dependency.

## Consequence

`RE-047` says that after a nonzero fringe bit the fan cannot keep the entered state for more than a collapsing threshold interval. `RE-048` identifies why at the level of the physical CA geometry: **the state is entered when its adaptive tangent is already within the final `O(1/log p)` fraction of its genuine supporting chamber.**

Any aggregate obstruction can therefore target support-edge saturation rather than merely count short exposed cells. To close this route one would need source-specific information showing that false-RH amplitude maxima cannot repeatedly enter genuine CA chambers so late—for example a lower bound on the distance of exposed secants from the outgoing support slope, a restriction on how often prime-power support intervals can be sampled in their final `1/log p` fraction, or a common-source relation between this support-edge alignment and the translated prime-race coordinate.
