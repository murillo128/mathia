**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + HIGHER-LAYER-EVENT-QUANTIZATION + NONCANCELLING-RESIDUAL-CHARGE + FRINGE-INTEGER-LAW + CROSS-CELL-SOURCE-COHERENCE + COUNTEREXAMPLE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`.

`RE-045` shows a striking cancellation at an adjacent **first-layer** switch of the compact false-RH fan: once the generating prime has crossed both its ordinary-prime location and its delayed first-layer CA event, its leading residual quantum disappears, leaving only a possible unmatched upper-fringe prime. `RE-059` then shows that the adjusted standard and reciprocal first-layer sources are frozen inside each fixed CA chamber. The natural cross-cell question is whether every genuine CA event is similarly invisible after it has fired.

It is not. A single fixed higher-layer event leaves an unavoidable signed quantum in the same translated nonlinear residual. The possible first-layer fringe corrections at the two selected endpoints occur at the physical event scale and therefore contribute **integer multiples of the layer depth** after normalization. For every fixed depth `j>=2`, those multiples cannot cancel the unit higher-layer charge.

In particular

## 1. The tied-selector centering argument does not depend on the layer

Let

\[
c:=A_b(C_-)=A_b(C_+)>0,
\qquad
\tau(x):=\log(1+cx^{-b}),
\qquad
H(x):=\log\log x+\tau(x).
\tag{12}
\]

Exactly as in `RE-043`, the tie gives

\[
\log\frac{\sigma(C_\pm)}{C_\pm}
=\gamma+H(Y_\pm).
\tag{13}
\]

The single layer-`j` transition changes `log(sigma(C)/C)` by `lambda_j(p)` while changing `Y=log C` by `L_p`. Hence its CA supporting slope is simultaneously the secant of `H` and the physical event slope:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H(Y_+)-H(Y_-)}{Y_+-Y_-}
=
\frac{\lambda_j(p)}{L_p}
=g(\eta).
}
\tag{14}
\]

The adaptive tangent equation is

\[
g(Z_\pm)=H'(Y_\pm).
\tag{15}
\]

The uniform small-height regime of `RE-040` gives

\[
\frac{\tau''(x)}{|g'(x)|}=o_J(1)
\tag{16}
\]

through every neighboring CA interval on the late blocks, so `H` is strictly concave. Therefore

\[
H'(Y_-)>g(\eta)>H'(Y_+),
\tag{17}
\]

and the monotonicity of `g` proves (3).

It remains to justify the half-gap asymptotic without assuming the relevant scales in advance. `RE-040` gives `Z_\pm/Y_\pm->1` uniformly. From (3), `Y_-<Z_-<eta<Z_+=(1+o_J(1))Y_+`. Combining this with the fixed-layer asymptotic (6) shows

\[
p^j=O_j(Y_+),
\qquad
L_p=O_j(\log Y_+)=o(Y_+).
\tag{18}
\]

Since `Y_+-Y_-=L_p`, it follows that

\[
Y_-\sim Y_+\sim\eta\sim Z_\pm.
\tag{19}
\]

Across an interval of length `L_p=o(Y)`, the logarithmic derivative of `|g'|` is `O(1/Y)`, so

\[
-H''(t)
=(1+o_{J,j}(1))|g'(\eta)|
\tag{20}
\]

uniformly for `Y_-<=t<=Y_+`. The exact secant-minus-tangent integral identities then give

\[
H'(Y_-)-g(\eta)
=\left(\frac12+o_{J,j}(1)\right)|g'(\eta)|L_p,
\tag{21}
\]

\[
g(\eta)-H'(Y_+)
=\left(\frac12+o_{J,j}(1)\right)|g'(\eta)|L_p.
\tag{22}
\]

Applying the mean-value theorem to `g` between `Z_-` and `eta`, and between `eta` and `Z_+`, proves (4). Thus the factor `1/2` in `RE-043` is not a first-layer artifact; it is the universal first-order secant/tangent geometry of any single fixed-layer CA event exposed as an adjacent adaptive tie.

## 2. A higher-layer event changes the two CA-side terms in a locked ratio

The source-separated normal form from `RE-045` is valid for every selected pair `(C,Z)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_b(Z)
={}&-
\sqrt2\,\frac{2b-1}{b}
+N(Z)T(C)
+N(Z)\delta_\ell(Z)\\
&-
\Psi'_{b,\ell}(q_0)
\frac{P(Z)-\delta_\vartheta(Z)}{\sqrt Z}
+O_J(Z^{-1/2}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

where

\[
N(Z)=\sqrt Z\log Z,
\qquad
P(Z)=\Phi_{\ge2}(Z),
\qquad
q_0=\frac{Z-Y}{Z\log Z}.
\tag{24}
\]

For the present single higher-layer transition, the higher-layer event mass jumps exactly by

\[
\boxed{P(Z_+)-P(Z_-)=L_p.}
\tag{25}
\]

The finite-exponent Euler-product tail changes by the same physical layer increment with the opposite sign. Before the event, the exponent of `p` is `j-1`; afterwards it is `j`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T(C_+)-T(C_-)
=-\lambda_j(p).
}
\tag{26}
\]

By the event definition (2), this is the exact relation

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_j(p)
=\frac{L_p}{\eta\log\eta}.}
\tag{27}
\]

The common parts of `P` and `T` vary too smoothly to enter at the scale `L_p/sqrt(eta)`. Indeed, `RE-036` gives

\[
P(Z_-)=O(\sqrt\eta),
\qquad
T(C_-)=O\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt\eta\log\eta}\right).
\tag{28}
\]

Equation (4) gives `Z_+-Z_-=O(L_p)`, while (1) gives `Y_+-Y_-=L_p`. Hence, for `D_\pm=Z_\pm-Y_\pm`,

\[
D_+-D_-=o_{J,j}(L_p).
\tag{29}
\]

Using the explicit analytic derivative formula for `Psi` recorded in `RE-045`, (29) and `D_\pm/Z_\pm=o_J(1)` imply

\[
\Psi'_{b,\log Z_+}(q_{0,+})
-
\Psi'_{b,\log Z_-}(q_{0,-})
=o_{J,j}(L_p/\eta),
\tag{30}
\]

and each coefficient satisfies

\[
\Psi'_{b,\log Z_\pm}(q_{0,\pm})
=\frac{1-b}{b}+o_J(1).
\tag{31}
\]

Together with the smoothness of `N(Z)` and `Z^{-1/2}`, equations (28)--(31) show that all common `P` and `T` contributions to the finite difference are

\[
o_{J,j}(L_p/\sqrt\eta).
\tag{32}
\]

The two actual event jumps are different. From (26)--(27),

\[
N(Z_+)\,[T(C_+)-T(C_-)]
=-\left(1+o_{J,j}(1)\right)
\frac{L_p}{\sqrt\eta}.
\tag{33}
\]

From (25) and (31),

\[
-\Psi'_{b,\log Z_+}(q_{0,+})
\frac{P(Z_+)-P(Z_-)}{\sqrt{Z_+}}
=-\left(\frac{1-b}{b}+o_J(1)\right)
\frac{L_p}{\sqrt\eta}.
\tag{34}
\]

Their coefficients add rather than cancel:

\[
-1-\frac{1-b}{b}=-\frac1b.
\tag{35}
\]

Thus, before accounting for possible first-layer endpoint mismatch,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta\mathcal R_b
=-\frac1b\frac{L_p}{\sqrt\eta}
+o_{J,j}(L_p/\sqrt\eta).
}
\tag{36}
\]

This is the central higher-layer charge.

## 3. First-layer fringe corrections contribute exactly `j` units asymptotically

For any selected pair, a nonzero endpoint correction means that an ordinary prime `r` has entered the standard and reciprocal prime sums while its delayed first-layer CA event has not yet fired:

\[
r\le Z<\eta_1(r).
\tag{37}
\]

The first-layer event lag is eventually less than `1/2`, so such a prime is unique and satisfies

\[
Z-\frac12<r\le Z.
\tag{38}
\]

This applies independently at `Z_-` and `Z_+`; define `chi_\pm` and `r_\pm` by (8). From (5)--(6) and (38), whenever the corresponding bit is nonzero,

\[
r_\pm\sim\eta\sim\frac{p^j}{j},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log r_\pm}{L_p}\longrightarrow j.}
\tag{39}
\]

For one unmatched prime, the two paired terms in (23) have the same sign. Using

\[
-\log(1-r^{-1})=r^{-1}+O(r^{-2}),
\tag{40}
\]

(31), and `r\sim Z\sim eta`, we obtain

\[
N(Z)\delta_\ell(Z)
=\left(1+o_{J,j}(1)\right)
\frac{\log r}{\sqrt\eta},
\tag{41}
\]

\[
\Psi'_{b,\log Z}(q_0)
\frac{\delta_\vartheta(Z)}{\sqrt Z}
=\left(\frac{1-b}{b}+o_J(1)\right)
\frac{\log r}{\sqrt\eta}.
\tag{42}
\]

Therefore one unmatched endpoint atom contributes

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac1b+o_J(1)\right)
\frac{\log r}{\sqrt\eta}
=
\left(\frac{j}{b}+o_{J,j}(1)\right)
\frac{L_p}{\sqrt\eta}.
}
\tag{43}
\]

The upper correction enters the finite difference with positive sign and the lower correction with negative sign. Adding (43) to the central charge (36) proves (9)--(10). The `O_J(Z^{-1/2})` Taylor remainder in (23) is harmless because `L_p->infinity`.

There is a useful arithmetic reason the cancellation is impossible. At layer `j`, the generating event carries logarithmic mass `log p`. An unmatched **ordinary** prime at the same physical coordinate has size `r\asymp p^j`, hence logarithmic mass asymptotic to `j log p`. The normalized source charges are therefore congruent to `-1` modulo `j`:

\[
-1+j(\chi_+-\chi_-).
\tag{44}
\]

For `j>=2` this can never vanish. The noncancellation is not a sign assumption or a prime-gap conjecture; it is the mismatch between one layer-`j` event quantum and the first-layer mass quantum available at the same physical scale.

## 4. Contrast with the first layer and consequence for the live cross-cell problem

The result pinpoints why `RE-045` behaves differently. At a first-layer transition, the generating prime `p` itself crosses the ordinary source before its CA event. By the time the upper adaptive selector is reached, both occurrences have been matched, so the generating atom leaves no leading endpoint charge. Only the next ordinary fringe prime can remain unmatched.

For `j>=2`, the ordinary first-layer contribution of `p` was absorbed much earlier, near physical scale `p`, whereas the layer-`j` event fires near `p^j/j`. There is no same-scale ordinary jump of mass `log p` available to reconcile it. The CA-side changes `Delta P=log p` and `Delta T=-lambda_j(p)` therefore survive as the unit charge in (36). The only available first-layer endpoint atoms have mass asymptotic to `j log p`, producing the discrete three-value law (10).

This gives a concrete cross-cell discriminator after the within-cell information compression of `RE-059`. If one could improve the adaptive residual calibration on this subfamily to

\[
\mathcal R_b(Z_\pm)
=o_{J,j}\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\sqrt{\eta_j(p)}}\right),
\tag{45}
\]

then (11) would exclude **every** sufficiently large adjacent single layer-`j` switch exposed as a tied fan breakpoint. Unlike the first-layer case, there is no fringe-bit choice that evades such a refinement.

The current calibration is nowhere near (45): `log p/sqrt(eta_j(p))` tends to zero like `log p/p^(j/2)`, while the existing uniform translated residual control is only `o(1)` at the relevant stage. Thus (45) is a resolution target, not an achieved contradiction.

Nor does this result force the false-RH fan to expose such switches. `RE-004` shows that higher-layer transitions have zero count density among CA transition coordinates, and an exposed convex-envelope switch can skip intervening CA states rather than tie two adjacent ones. The theorem therefore does not prove RH, does not establish infinitely many higher-layer breakpoints, and does not replace the need for global cross-cell coherence. What it does establish is that **higher-layer events are not source-invisible once one compares neighboring selected chambers**: they carry a quantized nonzero residual signature that the first-layer matched transition lacks.

## 5. Stress tests and prior-art boundary

Several hypotheses are load-bearing. The depth `j` is fixed while the switch escapes to infinity; no uniform claim is made for `j` growing with `p`. The central transition must be a single layer-`j` atom; tied multi-atom CA transitions can carry several masses and require a separate charge calculation. The two states must be adjacent CA states and tied adaptive maximizers, because the secant identity (14) is what centers the physical event between the two adaptive tangents. The result does not apply to a fan edge that skips intermediate CA states.

The two fringe indicators are not assumed independent, and no assertion is made that all three values in (10) occur. The theorem only needs that each endpoint has at most one unmatched first-layer prime and that such a prime has logarithmic mass asymptotic to `j log p`. Even the most favorable possible correlation between the two bits cannot make the coefficient vanish.

The underlying CA layer thresholds and prime-exponent increments are classical Alaoglu--Erdos structure. Mantovanelli's August 2026 preprint is the closest current prior art for placing all prime-power layers on the logarithmic-size event axis and for exact workload bookkeeping. A targeted search of that framework and neighboring Robin/CA literature found no statement of the adaptive tied-selector centering for arbitrary fixed layers, nor the translated-residual charge law (9)--(10). No priority claim is made: the durable delta here is the Mathia-specific splice between the existing adaptive fan residual and the classical higher-layer event atom.

No new external theorem is load-bearing. The proof uses the exact adaptive barrier and residual identities already persisted in `RE-036`, `RE-040`, and `RE-045`, the fixed-layer event asymptotics and common-source map of `RE-054`, and the elementary first-layer endpoint lag already used in this line. `SOURCES.md` therefore requires no change.

The decisive audit test is direct: for a fixed `j>=2`, reconstruct a sequence of adjacent single layer-`j` tied fan switches, compute the exact source-separated terms in (23), and normalize their finite differences by `b sqrt(eta_j(p))/log p`. Any accumulation value outside `{-j-1,-1,j-1}`, or a zero accumulation value, would falsify the claim. A multi-atom tie, a skipped CA transition, or growing layer depth is outside the theorem rather than a counterexample.
