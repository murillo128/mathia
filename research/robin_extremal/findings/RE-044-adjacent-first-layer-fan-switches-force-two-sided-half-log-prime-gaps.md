# RE-044 — adjacent first-layer fan switches force two-sided half-log prime gaps

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + FIRST-LAYER-EVENT-QUANTIZATION + ORDINARY-PRIME-ISOLATION + TWO-SIDED-HALF-LOG-GAP + COUNTEREXAMPLE-NARROWING + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-043` proves that whenever a compact false-RH threshold fan switches between two adjacent CA states through one ordinary first-layer prime atom `p`, the two tied adaptive tangent parameters straddle the generating prime in a window of asymptotic width `log p`. Its conservative boundary note allowed that this logarithmic window might contain other ordinary primes. The first-layer event quantization from `RE-042`, combined with the fact that both tangent parameters lie inside the two **adjacent CA chambers**, removes almost all of that ambiguity.

Under the hypotheses of `RE-043`, let

\[
C_-<C_+,
\qquad
C_+=pC_-,
\qquad
Y_+-Y_-=\log p,
\]

be adjacent CA states tied as maximizers of the same adaptive amplitude `A_b` for some `b` in the compact interval `J`, and let `Z_-<\eta_p<Z_+` be their adaptive tangent parameters. Let `p^-<p<p^+` be the consecutive ordinary primes surrounding `p`. Then, for all sufficiently large such switches,

\[
\boxed{\eta_{p^-}<Z_-<p<\eta_p<Z_+<\eta_{p^+}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
p-p^-
\ge
\left(\frac12-o_J(1)\right)\log p,
\qquad
p^+-p
\ge
\left(\frac12-o_J(1)\right)\log p.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus every adjacent first-layer threshold switch exposed by the false-RH fan is generated by a prime having **both** neighboring ordinary-prime gaps at least asymptotically one half of the local mean-gap scale `log p`.

There is a sharper local isolation statement. For all sufficiently large switches,

\[
\boxed{
\pi(p^-)-\pi(Z_-)=0,
\qquad
\pi(Z_+-1/2)-\pi(p)=0,
}
\tag{3}
\]

with the evident interpretation that there are no ordinary primes in `[Z_-,p)` and none in `(p,Z_+-1/2]`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\#\{q\text{ prime}:Z_-<q\le Z_+\}\le2,

\tag{4}

and the only possible extra prime besides `p` must be the next prime `p^+`, lying in the terminal half-unit interval

\[
Z_+-\frac12<p^+\le Z_+.
\tag{5}
\]

The logarithmic selector bracket of `RE-043` is therefore not a generic short interval containing an uncontrolled number of prime jumps. On the adjacent first-layer subfamily it contains the generating prime and at most one endpoint-fringe prime. This removes the short-interval prime-counting ambiguity from the local common-source comparison; what remains is the finer residual-resolution problem already isolated by `RE-042`, together with the global problem that the compact fan has not yet been shown to contain infinitely many adjacent first-layer switches.

## 1. First-layer event coordinates are strictly ordered with the primes

For a prime `r`, the first-layer CA event coordinate is defined by

\[
g(\eta_r)
=
\frac{\log(1+r^{-1})}{\log r},
\qquad
g(x)=\frac1{x\log x}.
\tag{6}
\]

The function

\[
r\longmapsto
\frac{\log(1+r^{-1})}{\log r}
\]

is strictly decreasing for `r>1`: its numerator is strictly decreasing and positive while its denominator is strictly increasing and positive. Since `g` is strictly decreasing, the event coordinate `\eta_r` is strictly increasing with `r`.

`RE-042` gives the sharper large-prime location

\[
\boxed{
r<\eta_r<r+\frac12.}
\tag{7}
\]

No prime-distribution theorem is used here; (7) is the elementary inversion of the exact first-layer CA threshold.

## 2. Adjacency forces the neighboring first-layer events outside the tangent bracket

The crucial input beyond the numerical location (7) is CA chamber adjacency. By `RE-040`, each adaptive tangent parameter is a genuine regular CA selector. At the tied switch of `RE-043`, `C_-` and `C_+` are adjacent CA states and their common boundary is the single first-layer event `\eta_p`. Therefore `Z_-` lies in the open CA chamber immediately before `\eta_p`, while `Z_+` lies in the open CA chamber immediately after it.

There can be no CA event coordinate in either open interval

\[
(Z_-,\eta_p)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
(\eta_p,Z_+).
\tag{8}
\]

Because first-layer event coordinates are among the CA event coordinates and are strictly ordered with their primes, the previous prime's event must satisfy

\[
\eta_{p^-}<Z_-,
\tag{9}
\]

and the next prime's event must satisfy

\[
\eta_{p^+}>Z_+.
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (9)--(10) with the placement theorem of `RE-043` gives exactly (1).

This argument remains valid even if a neighboring chamber boundary is a higher-layer or tied event: such a boundary can only push the nearest first-layer event farther away. The hypothesis that the central transition itself is one single first-layer atom is the only place where the event at the tie must be exactly `\eta_p`.

## 3. The two-sided ordinary-prime gap follows at the half-log scale

No average prime-gap theorem is being inserted.

## 4. The whole logarithmic bracket contains at most one extra prime

Equation (9) already implies that there is no ordinary prime in `[Z_-,p)`, because `p^-` is the largest prime below `p` and `p^-<Z_-`.

Now suppose that a prime `q>p` satisfies

\[
q\le Z_+-\frac12.
\tag{15}
\]

By (7),

\[
\eta_q<q+\frac12\le Z_+.
\tag{16}
\]

Since `q>p`, strict ordering of first-layer events gives `\eta_q>\eta_p`. Thus (16) places a CA event in `(\eta_p,Z_+]`, contradicting (8). Therefore no such `q` exists, proving (3).

Any additional prime in the full selector bracket must consequently lie in `(Z_+-1/2,Z_+]`. This interval has length `1/2`, so it contains at most one integer and therefore at most one prime. That proves (4)--(5).

This sharpens an explicit caveat in `RE-043`: nearby primes cannot populate the logarithmic bracket at their natural short-interval multiplicity. The CA chamber condition itself has already evacuated the whole bracket except for the generating prime and a possible upper endpoint-fringe prime whose own first-layer event has not yet fired.

## 5. What this buys, and what it does not

The local common-source problem after `RE-043` had two apparent complications: the nonlinear residual is only known to `o(1)` rather than below the individual-prime scale `log p/sqrt(p)`, and a logarithmic bracket might contain several unrelated prime jumps. Equations (3)--(5) remove the second complication on every adjacent first-layer switch. A future local comparison needs to account for only the generating prime, plus at most one next-prime endpoint correction.

This is still not a contradiction to false RH. Prime gaps of size comparable with `log p` are entirely compatible with known prime-distribution theory, and the result does **not** show that the threshold fan has infinitely many adjacent first-layer switches. Nor does it improve the uniform `RE-040` residual rate. In particular, (2) cannot be combined with a global theorem saying that some primes have small gaps; the needed statement would have to apply to the source-selected switch primes themselves.

The main strategic change is therefore narrower but durable: a one-switch argument no longer needs a generic theorem for the number of primes in a `log p` interval. Its remaining arithmetic currency is genuinely the sub-`o(1)` race resolution or a theorem forcing enough physical first-layer switches to aggregate.
