# RE-237 — Co-phased single-center cascades have exact multiplicative Wiener cost

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CONTINUUM-VERTICAL-EULER + OFF-CENTER-POLYNOMIAL-PREDICTION + MULTI-CENTER-SERIAL-COMPOSITION + WIENER-NORM-REPRESENTATION-OBSTRUCTION + SOURCE-MAPPING + PRIOR-ART-AUDIT`.

**Parent findings:** RE-216, RE-218, RE-220.

The single-center boundary map is now narrow enough that the natural next architectural move is to use several centers. The simplest such move is serial: construct several normalized single-center predictors and multiply them, equivalently convolving their delay measures.

For centers on one common complex ray, that serial architecture has **no Wiener-norm discount at all**. The coefficient phase identity behind RE-220 survives convolution exactly.

For `r=1,...,J`, let

\[
a_r=\rho_r e^{i\varphi},
\qquad
\rho_r>0,
\qquad
m_r\ge1,
\qquad
N_r\ge0,
\tag{1}
\]

with one common phase `phi`, and define

\[
Q_r(z)
:=
z^{m_r}a_r^{-m_r}
\sum_{k=0}^{N_r}
\binom{m_r+k-1}{k}
\left(1-\frac{z}{a_r}\right)^k,
\qquad
F_r(z):=\frac{Q_r(z)}{Q_r(1)},
\tag{2}
\]

assuming `Q_r(1) != 0`. Put

\[
G(z):=\prod_{r=1}^{J}F_r(z).
\tag{3}
\]

Then, for every finite `J`,

\[
\boxed{
\|G\|_A
=
\prod_{r=1}^{J}\|F_r\|_A.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Moreover the exact support interval of `G` is

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\sum_r m_r,\;
\sum_r(m_r+N_r)
\right].
}
\tag{5}
\]

Hence, writing

\[
M:=\sum_{r=1}^{J}m_r,
\tag{6}
\]

the normalized Wiener rate on any common arc half-width `alpha>0` obeys the exact convex-average identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log\|G\|_A}{M\alpha}
=
\sum_{r=1}^{J}
\frac{m_r}{M}
\frac{\log\|F_r\|_A}{m_r\alpha}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Therefore a serial cascade of individually successful co-phased single-center predictors can never improve on the best constituent Wiener rate. In particular, cascading or mixing positive-real single-center predictors cannot lower the `5.996390` rate supplied by the best constituent merely through serial composition. Repeating the same construction preserves its normalized rate exactly.

This is not a universal obstruction to multi-center prediction. It rules out the most direct **same-ray serial** realization. Any genuine Wiener saving from several centers must introduce coefficient-phase disagreement, additive/minimax interference, a product mechanism whose factors are not individually predictors, or a different representation altogether.

## 1. Every single-center factor carries one phase character

RE-220 gives the exact coefficient identity before the final monomial shift. Expanding (2), the coefficient at degree `m_r+j` is

\[
[z^{m_r+j}]F_r
=
\frac{(-1)^j a_r^{-m_r-j}}{Q_r(1)}
\sum_{k=j}^{N_r}
\binom{m_r+k-1}{k}
\binom{k}{j},
\qquad
0\le j\le N_r.
\tag{8}
\]

Define

\[
B_{r,j}
:=
\rho_r^{-j}
\sum_{k=j}^{N_r}
\binom{m_r+k-1}{k}
\binom{k}{j}
>0
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
\gamma_r:=\frac{a_r^{-m_r}}{Q_r(1)},
\qquad
\omega:=-e^{-i\varphi}.
\tag{10}
\]

Because all centers have the same argument,

\[
\boxed{
[z^{m_r+j}]F_r
=
\gamma_r\,\omega^j\,B_{r,j}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus each factor has an arbitrary global phase `gamma_r`, but its relative coefficient phases are generated by the **same one-dimensional character** `omega^j`. For positive-real centers this reduces to the alternating sign pattern `(-1)^j`.

Equation (11) is stronger than a triangle-inequality estimate. It is the exact coefficient vector. In particular, the final DC normalization changes only `gamma_r`; it cannot disturb the relative phase character.

## 2. Convolution preserves the character and eliminates all internal cancellation

Let

\[
M=\sum_r m_r.
\]

The coefficient of `z^{M+j}` in the product (3) is the finite convolution

\[
[z^{M+j}]G
=
\sum_{\substack{j_1+\cdots+j_J=j\\0\le j_r\le N_r}}
\prod_{r=1}^{J}
\gamma_r\omega^{j_r}B_{r,j_r}.
\tag{12}
\]

Since `j_1+...+j_J=j`, every summand in (12) has exactly the same phase. Hence

\[
[z^{M+j}]G
=
\left(\prod_r\gamma_r\right)
\omega^j
C_j,
\tag{13}
\]

where

\[
C_j:=
\sum_{\substack{j_1+\cdots+j_J=j\\0\le j_r\le N_r}}
\prod_r B_{r,j_r}
>0
\qquad
(0\le j\le\sum_rN_r).
\tag{14}
\]

There is therefore **zero cancellation** among the coefficient paths produced by multiplying the factors. Taking absolute values and summing,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|G\|_A
&=
\left|\prod_r\gamma_r\right|
\sum_j C_j\\
&=
\left|\prod_r\gamma_r\right|
\prod_r\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N_r}B_{r,j}\right)\\
&=
\prod_r\|F_r\|_A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

This proves (4). Positivity of every `B_{r,j}` also shows that every relative degree from zero through `sum N_r` occurs, which proves the support statement (5).

The usual Wiener-algebra inequality only gives

\[
\|F_1\cdots F_J\|_A
\le
\prod_r\|F_r\|_A.
\tag{16}
\]

Here the exact negative-binomial phase character forces equality. The serial architecture therefore cannot exploit the only place where the generic Banach-algebra estimate could have become cheaper: cancellation between convolution paths landing at the same output degree.

## 3. The source-domain map makes the serial cost statement exact

Associate to each factor the finite delay measure

\[
\mu_r
=
\sum_{j=0}^{N_r}
[z^{m_r+j}]F_r\,
\delta_{m_r+j}.
\tag{17}
\]

Polynomial multiplication is exactly convolution of these measures:

\[
\mu
=
\mu_1*\cdots*\mu_J,
\qquad
\widehat{\mu}
=
\prod_r\widehat{\mu_r}.
\tag{18}
\]

The minimum delays add to `M`, the maximum delays add to `M+sum N_r`, and (15) becomes the exact total-variation law

\[
\boxed{
\|\mu_1*\cdots*\mu_J\|_{TV}
=
\prod_r\|\mu_r\|_{TV}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus the representation statement is not an artifact of polynomial notation: the physical delay convolution itself receives no TV discount.

Prediction also composes in the expected way. If on the common observed arc

\[
\varepsilon_r
:=
\sup_{|\theta|\le\alpha}
|F_r(e^{-i\theta})-1|,
\tag{20}
\]

then

\[
\sup_{|\theta|\le\alpha}
|G(e^{-i\theta})-1|
\le
\prod_r(1+\varepsilon_r)-1
\le
\exp\left(\sum_r\varepsilon_r\right)-1.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence a fixed finite cascade remains a predictor whenever each constituent error tends to zero. The same is true for a growing number of stages whenever

\[
\sum_r\varepsilon_r\to0.
\tag{22}
\]

For every such factorwise-valid cascade, taking logs in (4) and dividing by `M alpha` gives (7). The cascade rate is exactly a weighted average of the constituent rates, so

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log\|G\|_A}{M\alpha}
\ge
\min_r
\frac{\log\|F_r\|_A}{m_r\alpha}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

No choice of positive-real radii, truncation depths, or stage weights can turn serial composition of already valid stages into a lower-rate predictor.

## 4. What this removes from the multi-center search

RE-220 left several centers as a possible exit from the single-center obstruction. Equations (4)--(23) show that "several centers" is not by itself new geometry.

If the proposed construction consists of a chain of positive-real centers, or more generally centers on one common complex ray, and every stage is itself tuned as a predictor, then the stages merely add their log-Wiener costs and minimum delays. The normalized result lies in the convex hull of the stage rates. A cascade of two `5.996390`-rate stages still has rate `5.996390`; mixing one such stage with a more expensive stage only makes the result worse.

The useful architectural split is therefore sharper:

\[
\boxed{
\text{same-ray serial multi-center}
\quad\text{has no Wiener discount.}
}
\tag{24}
\]

A multi-center attempt intended to improve the live constructive constant should instead create **cross-center phase cancellation at the final coefficient level**. The natural candidates are parallel/additive or minimax combinations, or serial factors with genuinely different phase characters. Those architectures are not reducible to (15).

This finding does not raise the universal separated-prediction lower bound. The global interval remains

\[
1\le C_{\rm sep}\le5.996390.
\tag{25}
\]

It is a representation obstruction that prunes one proposed route toward the upper endpoint.

## 5. Falsification boundary and prior-art audit

The common-ray hypothesis is load-bearing. If two factors have different center phases, the contribution to an output degree contains factors

\[
e^{-i(j_1\varphi_1+\cdots+j_J\varphi_J)},
\tag{26}
\]

which depend on the partition of `j`. Convolution paths can then cancel and the Wiener inequality can be strict. This is not merely formal. For the tiny unnormalized example `m=N=1`, centers `a_1=2` and `a_2=-2` give

\[
Q_1(z)=z-\frac14z^2,
\qquad
Q_2(z)=-z-\frac14z^2.
\tag{27}
\]

Their middle product coefficient cancels exactly:

\[
Q_1(z)Q_2(z)
=
-z^2+\frac1{16}z^4.
\tag{28}
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{\|Q_1Q_2\|_A}
{\|Q_1\|_A\|Q_2\|_A}
=
\frac{17}{25}
<1.
\tag{29}
\]

The same ratio is unchanged by nonzero scalar DC normalizations. This example is not asserted to satisfy the predictor contraction geometry; it is a falsification control showing that the algebraic equality really depends on phase coherence. Different-phase serial designs remain open.

A second scope boundary is factorwise prediction. Equation (4) holds whether or not the factors predict, but corollary (23) only says that a cascade of **individually successful** stages cannot beat its best stage. It does not rule out a deliberately coupled product in which no individual factor approximates one while the product does. Such a mechanism would need a separate approximation argument.

A fresh prior-art search recovered the standard Wiener-algebra fact that absolutely summable Fourier coefficients form a Banach algebra and satisfy the submultiplicative norm bound (16). Equality for convolution of nonnegative or globally dephased coefficient sequences is elementary. The architecture-specific input here is the exact RE-220 coefficient phase law and its consequence for serial negative-binomial centers. No external theorem is load-bearing, so no `SOURCES.md` update is required.

Private numerical checks over random positive-real factors verified (4) to floating-point precision, while factors with different phases showed strict submultiplicativity, consistent with the exact derivation. The persisted result depends only on equations (8)--(15).

## Research consequence

The first multi-center architecture worth testing is **not** a cascade of positive-real single-center predictors. Such a cascade cannot create coefficient cancellation and has exactly multiplicative TV cost, so its normalized Wiener exponent is only a weighted average of costs already available.

The next decision-relevant branch is therefore parallel/minimax multi-center synthesis, or a genuinely mixed-phase construction where several centers contribute to the **same final coefficient before absolute values are taken**. That is the first place where multiple centers can provide a Wiener economy unavailable to the exhausted single-center geometry.
