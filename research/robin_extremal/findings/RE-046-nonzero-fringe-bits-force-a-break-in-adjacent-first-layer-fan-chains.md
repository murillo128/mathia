# RE-046 — nonzero fringe bits force a break in adjacent first-layer fan chains

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE-RH-FAILURE + SAME-BLOCK-FAN-KINEMATICS + FIRST-LAYER-EVENT-QUANTIZATION + FRINGE-BIT-EXCLUSION + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + PRIOR-ART-BOUNDED`. `RE-045` shows that at one adjacent first-layer breakpoint of the compact false-RH threshold fan, the selected-residual increment retains only one possible first-prime-scale datum: the binary fringe bit

\[
\chi_p=\mathbf 1_{\{p^+\le Z_+\}},
\]

which records whether the next ordinary prime has crossed the upper adaptive selector before its own CA first-layer event fires. A natural continuation is to try to accumulate these bits over many adjacent first-layer switches. The fan itself prevents that on every uninterrupted chain of such switches.

Assume RH is false, fix

\[
J=[b_0,b_1]\Subset(1-\Theta,1/2),
\]

and work on one sufficiently late common positive CA counterexample block from `RE-040`. Let

\[
\beta<\beta'
\]

be two successive interior breakpoints of the rightmost adaptive fan such that one CA state `C_1` is exposed throughout the open threshold cell `(\beta,\beta')`. Suppose both endpoint switches are **unique adjacent first-layer transitions**:

\[
C_0<C_1<C_2,
\qquad
C_1=pC_0,
\qquad
C_2=qC_1,
\tag{1}
\]

where the transition at `\beta` is the first-layer atom generated by the ordinary prime `p`, and the transition at `\beta'` is again a single first-layer atom generated by `q`.

Then necessarily

\[
\boxed{q=p^+}
\tag{2}
\]

and, more importantly, the fringe bit of the first switch vanishes:

\[
\boxed{\chi_p=0.}
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, if `Z_+(p;\beta)` denotes the upper adaptive selector at the first switch,

\[
\boxed{Z_+(p;\beta)<p^+.}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus a nonzero `RE-045` fringe quantum cannot be followed, through the same exposed intermediate state, by its immediate adjacent first-layer successor switch. Any switch with `\chi_p=1` forces a **break in the clean adjacent-first-layer fan chain** before the fan can expose the `p^+` first-layer transition: the next exposed breakpoint must skip CA states, encounter a higher-layer or tied/multi-atom transition, or the relevant fan segment must terminate at the boundary of `J`.

Consequently, on any finite chain of successive fan breakpoints

\[
C_0\xrightarrow{p_1}C_1
\xrightarrow{p_2}C_2
\xrightarrow{p_3}\cdots
\xrightarrow{p_m}C_m
\tag{5}
\]

in which every arrow is a unique adjacent ordinary first-layer CA transition and every intermediate `C_i` owns a nonempty threshold cell, one has

\[
\boxed{\chi_{p_i}=0\qquad(1\le i<m).}
\tag{6}
\]

Only the terminal arrow can carry an unconstrained fringe bit from this local argument. Therefore the binary signal isolated in `RE-045` cannot build up along a long clean first-layer chain. Any persistent aggregate of nonzero fringe quanta must be supported by the **interruptions** of that chain rather than by its ordinary interior links.

## 1. The adaptive selector of one fixed exposed state is strictly increasing in the threshold exponent

Fix the intermediate state `C_1` and write

\[
Y_1:=\log C_1,
\qquad
h_1:=\log G(C_1)-\gamma>0,
\qquad
a_1:=1-e^{-h_1}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

`RE-040` gives, for every threshold `b` for which `C_1` is a maximizer, its unique regular adaptive tangent parameter `Z_1(b)` through the exact equation

\[
\boxed{
g(Z_1(b))
=g(Y_1)-\frac{b a_1}{Y_1},
\qquad
g(x)=\frac1{x\log x}.}
\tag{8}
\]

There is no asymptotic approximation in (8). Since `g'(x)<0`, implicit differentiation gives

\[
g'(Z_1(b))Z_1'(b)=-\frac{a_1}{Y_1},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
Z_1'(b)
=\frac{a_1}{Y_1|g'(Z_1(b))|}>0.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{Z_1(\beta)<Z_1(\beta').}
\tag{10}
\]

At the first breakpoint, `C_1` is the upper tied state, so the selector in `RE-043`--`RE-045` is exactly

\[
Z_1(\beta)=Z_+(p;\beta).
\tag{11}
\]

At the second breakpoint the same `C_1` is the lower tied state, hence

\[
Z_1(\beta')=Z_-(q;\beta').
\tag{12}
\]

The strict inequality (10) is therefore a kinematic constraint linking two otherwise separate applications of the local switch theorem.

## 2. Two consecutive adjacent first-layer exits must use consecutive ordinary primes

The first-layer CA event coordinate of a prime `r` is

\[
g(\eta_r)=\frac{\log(1+r^{-1})}{\log r}.
\tag{13}
\]

`RE-044` proves that `r\mapsto\eta_r` is strictly increasing. After the first transition in (1), the first-layer event `\eta_p` has fired. If the next adjacent CA transition out of `C_1` is again a unique first-layer event generated by `q`, then no CA event lies between the two adjacent states.

If `q>p^+`, strict ordering would place the first-layer event `\eta_{p^+}` strictly between `\eta_p` and `\eta_q`, contradicting adjacency. A prime `q\le p` cannot be a new first-layer activation after `p` has fired. Thus the only possible label is

\[
q=p^+,
\]

which proves (2). Notice that this does not assume the open Alaoglu--Erdos conjecture that every pair of consecutive CA integers has prime quotient. It is conditional only on the two transitions explicitly assumed here to be single first-layer atoms; higher-layer and tied transitions are retained as possible interruptions.

## 3. The next switch forces the previous upper selector to remain below the next prime

Apply `RE-043` to the second switch, now generated by `q=p^+`. Its lower adaptive selector lies before the generating ordinary-prime jump:

\[
\boxed{Z_-(p^+;\beta')<p^+.}
\tag{14}
\]

Equations (10)--(12) give

\[
Z_+(p;\beta)
=Z_1(\beta)
<Z_1(\beta')
=Z_-(p^+;\beta').
\tag{15}
\]

Combining (14) and (15),

\[
\boxed{Z_+(p;\beta)<p^+,}
\tag{16}
\]

which is exactly `\chi_p=0` by the definition in `RE-045`. This proves (3)--(4).

The contradiction formulation is especially transparent. If `\chi_p=1`, then

\[
p^+\le Z_1(\beta).
\]

Strict selector monotonicity would imply

\[
p^+<Z_1(\beta'),
\]

whereas the next adjacent first-layer switch through `p^+` requires `Z_1(\beta')<p^+`. Hence the two local configurations cannot occur consecutively around the same exposed state.

Iterating the same argument over the intermediate states in (5) proves (6).

## 4. Stress tests and boundary of the obstruction

This result does **not** prove that nonzero fringe bits are absent from the false-RH fan. It says where they cannot live. A bit `\chi_p=1` is compatible with all current findings provided the fan does not next expose the immediate adjacent first-layer `p^+` transition through the same state. The fan may jump over one or more CA states, encounter a higher-layer event, pass through a tied/multi-atom boundary, or reach an endpoint of the threshold interval before such a switch is exposed.

The argument also does not prove that the fan contains a long adjacent first-layer chain. No density or abundance theorem for these switches is supplied. Therefore (6) is not yet an aggregate contradiction: it redirects the aggregate problem from ordinary first-layer interiors to the frequency and structure of **exceptional interruptions**.

No residual-rate improvement is used. In particular, the unspecified `o(1)` in the uniform nonlinear race law of `RE-040` and the `o(log p/sqrt p)` remainder in `RE-045` play no role. The exclusion is exact once the fan, regular selector equation, and two adjacent first-layer hypotheses are present. This is why it survives even though the individual residual quantum is below the current global race-error floor.

The result is source-specific only through the combined prime/event dictionary. The monotonicity (9) is elementary convex-envelope/tangent kinematics and can be copied by a synthetic fan. What cannot be ignored is that the possible upper-fringe atom of the first switch is **the same next prime** whose ordered first-layer CA event would generate the next clean transition. The theorem says these two roles cannot both be realized consecutively by the physical fan.

## Consequence

`RE-045` reduced the first-prime-scale selected residual to a binary unmatched-fringe datum. The present result shows that this datum is not freely repeatable: **a nonzero fringe bit certifies an interruption of the clean adjacent-first-layer fan dynamics.** Conversely, every interior link of an uninterrupted first-layer chain has zero fringe bit.

This materially changes the natural aggregation target. Summing the `RE-045` quanta over ordinary adjacent first-layer switches cannot accumulate a contradiction unless one simultaneously controls the exceptional transitions that separate the nonzero bits. The next source-specific question is therefore whether a false-RH counterexample fan can contain enough higher-layer, tied, or nonadjacent interruptions to host all required fringe mismatches, or whether the genuine CA event-capacity/prime-power structure bounds those interruptions strongly enough to force an inconsistency.
