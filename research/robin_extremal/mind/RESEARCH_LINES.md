RE-112--RE-119 reduce the attained finite-edge branch to almost-periodic edge profiles and show that the regular CA staircase samples the continuous leading profile at leading edge resolution. RE-120 proves that under a horizontally isolated attained edge every fixed algebraic correction remains inside the stable-filter closure of that same profile.

RE-121 removes horizontal isolation as the structural gate. Keeping all subedge zeros gives a damped correction controlled by the weighted near-edge mass `W(t)`, whose positive Laplace envelope satisfies `W(t)=O(t^A)` iff `L(u)=O(u^-A)`. RE-122 converts that mass condition into a zero-geometry law: an algebraically visible anomaly of order `nu^-A` forces a subedge zero into horizontal width `O(log nu/nu)` at ordinate `O(nu^A log nu)`. Thus a fixed-order anomaly requires polynomial-height approach to the attained edge.

RE-123 removes a separate phase-placement escape. The complete signed subedge packet has reciprocal-square zero weights. Truncating it at any polynomial ordinate height gives only polylogarithmic phase speed, while the omitted high-frequency tail is algebraically negligible. The regular-CA phase mesh is exponentially finer, `O(e^(-u/2)u^(3/2))`, so for every fixed algebraic order it samples the **full infinite signed packet** superalgebraically well. If the continuum packet has an excursion of size `u^-A`, actual regular CA states realize the same signed excursion once the edge expansion is taken beyond order `A`.

RE-124 localizes the remaining internal cancellation around any individually strong subedge zero. A band-limited matched filter can use a phase window of length `U^(1-eta)` because every algebraically visible zero has horizontal damping only `O(log U/U)`. For every fixed `eta>0`, a coefficient of size at least `U^-A` therefore either forces a continuum packet excursion of the same algebraic size, hence a regular-CA residual excursion by RE-123, or it must be accompanied by comparable subedge coefficient mass inside an ordinate window of width `U^(-1+eta)`. Thus an invisible strong atom requires near-reciprocal `U^(-1+o(1))` ordinate clustering. The separate escape is a genuinely diffuse packet in which no individual zero carries the target algebraic scale.

RE-125 removes the sub-reciprocal core of that cluster as a cancellation mechanism. For `sigma_0<=beta<Theta<1`, the positive-ordinate leading coefficients `1/[rho(1-rho)]` lie in a fixed sector strictly inside the open right half-plane, and the fixed-order `1/U` corrections preserve that sector. After target demodulation, every neighbor with `|gamma-gamma_0|<=c_*/U` therefore has a uniformly positive real projection and reinforces the target; exact multiplicity and `o(1/U)` micro-clusters cannot hide it. Combining this phase-sector fact with the RE-124 filter shows that a hidden strong atom needs comparable coefficient mass in the annulus `c_*/U<|gamma-gamma_0|<=U^(-1+eta)` for every fixed `eta>0`. The remaining strong-atom obstruction is genuine reciprocal-scale phase rotation, while the diffuse-cloud branch remains separate.

RE-126 converts that annular mass condition into an actual pairwise zero constraint. A strong target already has `gamma_0<<U^(A/2)`, while the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula puts only `O(log U)` zeros, counted with multiplicity, in any unit ordinate window at that height. Therefore, if the RE-125 packet remains small and the annulus carries the required comparable mass, one annular companion must satisfy `|a_1(U)|>>|a_0(U)|/log U`. It lies in the same `O(log U/U)` horizontal edge layer, has distinct ordinate with `c_*/U<|gamma_1-gamma_0|<=U^(-1+eta)`, and is algebraically strong up to an arbitrary `U^-epsilon` loss. Along any unbounded hidden-atom sequence, `|gamma_1-gamma_0| log gamma_0 -> 0`; the strong-atom escape therefore requires an actual vanishing-normalized-spacing near-edge pair, not a huge local cloud of individually negligible coefficients.

Therefore the CA staircase itself is no longer a phase-selection bottleneck at any fixed algebraic scale, and ordinary distant-frequency cancellation, sub-reciprocal same-sector clustering, or locally diffuse annular mass is not enough to hide an individually strong near-edge mode. The surviving finite-edge problem is now threefold: establish or rule out the necessary polynomial-height approach; determine whether algebraic boundary mass can be forced into individually strong atoms rather than a genuinely diffuse global cloud; and, in the strong-atom regime, control near-edge zero doublets with vanishing normalized ordinate spacing and the phase relation needed for cancellation.

If the edge is not attained, RE-113's separate obstruction remains. The `Theta=1` branch also remains separate.

## Keep stable-profile universality, boundary-mass rate, height approach, signed packet geometry and arithmetic placement distinct

RE-119 shows that the regular CA mesh is already finer than every algebraic correction scale for the leading profile. RE-120--RE-122 show that algebraic visibility of subedge information requires polynomial-height approach. RE-123 upgrades the placement statement from a leading finite packet to the complete signed subedge packet: regular CA phases resolve it to superalgebraic accuracy at every fixed order. RE-124 then shows that a single algebraically strong subedge mode cannot be canceled by spectrally distant modes: invisibility requires comparable mass in ordinate clusters of width `U^(-1+eta)` for every fixed `eta>0`, or else the relevant algebraic mass must be diffuse across individually weaker modes. RE-125 sharpens the strong-atom alternative further: the coefficient phase sector makes every neighbor within `c_*/U` reinforce, so the necessary competing mass must occupy the reciprocal annulus `c_*/U<|gamma-gamma_0|<=U^(-1+eta)` rather than an arbitrarily tight micro-cluster. RE-126 then uses the local zero count to show that this annular mass cannot itself be arbitrarily diffuse: a hidden target forces a distinct companion with coefficient at least a logarithmic fraction of the target and normalized ordinate gap tending to zero.

The remaining selector cannot therefore be “continuum phases are favorable but CA states miss them,” nor can a strong atom be hidden by generic distant-frequency interference, exact multiplicity, sub-reciprocal clustering, or a very large population of locally tiny annular modes. It must come from algebraically strong near-edge zero pairs with vanishing normalized ordinate gaps and suitable phase rotation, diffuse many-mode cancellation with no strong atom at all, a source coordinate outside the finite-edge packet, or another mechanism not encoded by the current stable-filter/boundary-spectrum representation.
