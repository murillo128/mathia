## Resolve the surviving fringe-prime/common-source signal below the current race error floor

**Linked intuition:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps`.

RE-017--RE-042 reduce hypothetical Robin failure to an adaptive same-block selector fan coupled to a translated standard/reciprocal prime-race relation. The fan, capacity budget, and even an abstract event staircase are reproducible by synthetic controls; the first genuinely physical datum is the common prime-power quantization of CA events and race jumps.

RE-043--RE-044 solve much of the selector-placement problem for adjacent first-layer switches. The two tied adaptive selectors straddle the generating prime, their displacements are asymptotically half-logarithmic, and the whole selector bracket contains at most one additional ordinary prime in the upper fringe. This converts a vague placement issue into a tightly quantized local geometry.

RE-045 then removes the most tempting local contradiction. When the two **physical selected residuals** are compared, the generating prime's `log p/sqrt(p)` contribution cancels to first order after its own CA event has fired. The normalized finite difference is asymptotically binary and detects only whether the next ordinary prime has crossed the upper selector while its CA event is still pending.

The live theorem is therefore not merely to sharpen the uniform error below the generating-prime jump. It must resolve or aggregate the **unfired fringe-prime mismatch**, prove that its occurrence/absence across the relevant adjacent switches has a source-specific bias, or identify a more global quantized statistic in which the generating-prime cancellation does not erase the arithmetic signal. Local selector geometry alone is now substantially exhausted.
