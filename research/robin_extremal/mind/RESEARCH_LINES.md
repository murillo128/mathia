## Keep boundary-mass rate, individual atom strength, local zero count, pair spacing and diffuse cancellation distinct

RE-123 eliminates CA phase placement as a fixed-algebraic-scale bottleneck. RE-124--RE-125 show that an individually strong atom cannot be hidden by distant modes, exact multiplicity or an arbitrarily tight same-sector cluster. RE-126 adds the local zero-count budget: the annular mass cannot be diluted among arbitrarily many tiny modes, so a hidden strong atom forces at least one logarithmically comparable companion whose normalized ordinate spacing tends to zero.

This does not make zero spacing itself sufficient. The companion must also lie in the same near-edge horizontal layer and participate with the right signed phase geometry. Conversely, a diffuse packet evades the individual-atom premise entirely. The remaining selector must therefore control either **strong near-edge pair geometry plus signed phase**, or **diffuse many-mode boundary mass**, rather than generic crowding or CA sampling.
