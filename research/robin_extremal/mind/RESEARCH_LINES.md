## Exclude self-tangent half-mass event gaps on the colossally abundant ladder

**Linked intuition:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps`.

RE-001 removes the continuous/discrete ambiguity from the first pilot. A local Robin maximum on the colossally abundant ladder is a regular self-tangent state for the intrinsic parameter `1/(log n log log n)`, and if RH is false there are infinitely many such counterexample peaks. The prime-layer event representation gives their transition exactly, with no free rounding remainder.

RE-002 converts local maximality into exact secant-clearance inequalities for the adjacent CA transition coordinates. RE-003 makes their transparent form uniform: consecutive CA logarithmic jumps are `O(log X)`, so every sufficiently large self-tangent counterexample peak must sit in an event gap wide enough to absorb asymptotically half of the total logarithmic mass of its two boundary transitions.

The live theorem is now local and discrete: prove that sufficiently large regular self-tangent returns cannot satisfy this **event-spacing versus boundary-mass** inequality, or isolate exactly which higher prime-power/semiprime transition patterns remain capable of doing so.

## Separate first-layer prime gaps from the full event process

When both boundary transitions are first-layer prime insertions, RE-003 turns the gate into an ordinary prime-gap restriction of logarithmic size. This excludes an explicit class of local geometries but does not control higher prime-power events or semiprime CA jumps.

A complete continuation must work on the full prime-layer event measure or prove that counterexample peaks eventually have a restricted boundary type. Importing a prime-gap theorem without that bridge is insufficient.
