# Robin-extremal research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable robin-extremal intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Find a selector-sensitive source invariant between one boundary charge and coefficient-by-coefficient Euler recovery

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-044-honest-multiplicity-prices-early-repair-by-deletion-support`.

RE-153--RE-168 identify the Pareto-weighted Chebyshev-deficit destination and quantify how much post-selector Robin motion remains possible under quantitative Korobov--Vinogradov source fidelity. The intrinsic decay length `ell_KV(T)=log T/V(T)` gives the spatial capacity scale: a bounded-multiplicative or subpower region can still carry order-one normalized displacement while enough future capacity remains.

RE-169 identifies Mertens normalization as an exact conservation law for the same Robin kernel. RE-170--RE-171 show that exact global charge does not localize it over flexible generalized sources. RE-172 adds finite ordinary-prime rigidity, but RE-173 proves that this is compactness rigidity: an infinite `{0,1,2}` delete/duplicate tail on ordinary prime labels can match `Gamma=gamma` exactly, stay below every fixed KV envelope and still create an order-one transient Robin excursion.

RE-174 closes the opposite extreme: an unbounded exact high-temperature Euler profile identifies every bounded multiplicity perturbation, but at exponentially bad Robin-scale conditioning. RE-175--RE-177 show that the first right-boundary Euler slope already forces and spatially prices sign changes in delayed repairs. RE-178 then shows that adding any subcritical scalar jet family `j=o(log p/log log p)` only buys logarithmic leverage and cannot improve the leading polynomial distance-versus-sign-complexity exponent.

RE-179 replaces the remaining raw sign-phase count by its intrinsic location-sensitive currency. With exact Mertens value and first Euler slope, ordinary-prime repair beyond `lambda p`, and the KV envelope, define the negative reset starts `z_j` and

`R_b^-(Y)=sum_j exp(-b V(z_j))`.

If `N_p^-(Y)` is the amount of forced negative centered repair already paid below `Y`, then

`(delta_(lambda,p) d_p-N_p^-(Y))_+ <<_b R_b^-(Y)+ell_KV(Y) exp(-bV(Y))`.

Hence, while less than half of the Robin-scale debt has been paid early,

`sqrt(p) log p [R_b^-(Y)+ell_KV(Y) exp(-bV(Y))] >> 1`.

Infinite sign count is therefore not an escape by itself. Very remote alternations have exponentially diminishing value in the source metric; a countably infinite tail may still have finite or negligible reset budget. Conversely, if the weighted remote budget is too small, a fixed fraction of the required negative repair must already occur near the selector. RE-177's raw `M exp(-bV(Y))` law is the finite-phase corollary of this sharper weighted statement.

RE-180 restores the honest multiplicity constraint `m_Q(q)>=0` that the relaxed signed-capacity model suppresses. Since a negative coefficient is then exactly one deleted ordinary prime, while the centered two-jet kernel satisfies `K_D(q)=O(1/p)` throughout the repair region, the forced negative debt requires at least `Omega(sqrt(p)/log p)` distinct repair deletions in total. More locally,

`N_del(Y)/p + R_b^-(Y) + ell_KV(Y) exp(-bV(Y)) >> d_p`.

Thus the early branch of RE-179 is no longer cost-free: avoiding the remote reset budget requires a second deletion set on the same cardinality scale as the original selector packet.

RE-181 shows that this discrete tariff is **attainable**, not a closure mechanism. It constructs ordinary-prime-supported sources with multiplicities in `{0,1,2}` that agree with the baseline below the selector scale, match the Mertens boundary value and first slope exactly, remain under every fixed KV envelope, and still create a transient Robin-scale displacement. The construction pays the RE-180 cost with a selector-scale second deletion packet and then repairs the two boundary coordinates through geometrically separated delete/duplicate shells.

The live matched-control boundary has therefore moved. Cardinality of early deletions, bounded honest multiplicity, exact two-jet matching and KV fidelity are jointly insufficient. A stronger theorem must use **where** the repair primes are allowed to sit relative to genuine CA/Robin selector geometry, a joint moment/integrality invariant that the shell construction cannot match, or another source constraint coupling the selector packet to its compensators. Merely demanding the RE-180 deletion count or another shallow scalar jet no longer advances the boundary.

This still does not make any Euler boundary jet a free invariant of the original Robin problem. RE-175--RE-181 state what additional source coordinates force, how their repair cost is conditioned, and now that the first nontrivial support tariff can actually be paid. Any closure must explain why the decisive placement/joint constraint follows from genuine arithmetic input rather than being an extra hypothesis.

## Control one-sided vertical reciprocal escape without overstating the symmetric obstruction

RE-144--RE-152 put the zero-side packet into reciprocal-scale language and show that one-sided vertical phase geometry can hide a scale-maximal target at lacunary stages while fixed-order global local-correlation statistics remain unchanged. A closure theorem on this branch still needs target-conditioned exceptional geometry rather than typical-center averages.

## Keep source capacity, exact identification, jet depth and usable conditioning distinct

Quantitative PNT/KV fidelity, exact Mertens normalization, ordinary-prime support, integer multiplicity, finite/growing Euler boundary jets, the full high-temperature Euler germ, weighted reset complexity and the zero-side packet obstruction answer different questions. RE-173 shows strong coarse fidelity plus one exact boundary charge is non-rigid; RE-174 shows the full exact Euler hierarchy is rigid but ill-conditioned; RE-175--RE-178 price delayed compensation and expose the saturation of shallow scalar jets; RE-179 identifies where sign resets occur as the right continuous oscillation currency; RE-180 adds the complementary discrete tariff forced by honest nonnegative prime multiplicities; RE-181 proves that this tariff can still be realized while preserving exact two-jet data and KV fidelity.

Future matched controls must therefore state **what source ambiguity survives, where its sign resets occur, how much KV-weighted reset budget they carry, how many actual prime deletions are consumed, how those deletions are geometrically placed relative to the selector, which joint source moments are genuinely available, and at what selector-normalized precision/placement cost** before source rigidity is credited as Robin leverage.
