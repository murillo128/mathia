RE-120 proves that under a horizontally isolated attained edge every fixed algebraic correction remains inside the stable-filter closure of that same profile.

RE-121 removes horizontal isolation as the structural gate. Keeping all subedge zeros gives a damped correction controlled by the weighted near-edge mass `W(t)`, whose positive Laplace envelope satisfies `W(t)=O(t^A)` iff `L(u)=O(u^-A)`. Thus a fixed-order anomaly requires polynomial-height approach to the attained edge.

RE-123 removes a separate phase-placement escape. The complete signed subedge packet has reciprocal-square zero weights. Truncating it at any polynomial ordinate height gives only polylogarithmic phase speed, while the omitted high-frequency tail is algebraically negligible. The regular-CA phase mesh is exponentially finer, `O(e^(-u/2)u^(3/2))`, so for every fixed algebraic order it samples the **full infinite signed packet** superalgebraically well. If the continuum packet has an excursion of size `u^-A`, actual regular CA states realize the same signed excursion once the edge expansion is taken beyond order `A`.

Therefore the CA staircase itself is no longer a phase-selection bottleneck at any fixed algebraic scale. Polynomial-height near-edge zeros may still fail to matter because their weighted signed contributions cancel **internally**, but a favorable continuum phase cannot be systematically missed by arithmetic placement. The surviving finite-edge problem is now twofold: establish or rule out the necessary polynomial-height approach, and, if it occurs, control the signed correlation/cancellation geometry of that near-edge zero packet.
