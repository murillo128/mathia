## Find a selector-sensitive source invariant beyond every fixed Euler-jet depth

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-045-every-fixed-euler-jet-depth-can-be-matched-by-bounded-honest-prime-repairs`.

RE-153--RE-168 identify the Pareto-weighted Chebyshev-deficit destination and quantify how much post-selector Robin motion remains possible under quantitative Korobov--Vinogradov source fidelity. The intrinsic decay length `ell_KV(T)=log T/V(T)` gives the spatial capacity scale: a bounded-multiplicative or subpower region can still carry order-one normalized displacement while enough future capacity remains.

RE-169 identifies Mertens normalization as an exact conservation law for the same Robin kernel. RE-170--RE-171 show that exact global charge does not localize it over flexible generalized sources. RE-172 adds finite ordinary-prime rigidity, but RE-173 proves that this is compactness rigidity: an infinite `{0,1,2}` delete/duplicate tail on ordinary prime labels can match `Gamma=gamma` exactly, stay below every fixed KV envelope and still create an order-one transient Robin excursion.

RE-174 closes the opposite extreme: an unbounded exact high-temperature Euler profile identifies every bounded multiplicity perturbation, but at exponentially bad Robin-scale conditioning. RE-175--RE-177 show that the first right-boundary Euler slope already forces and spatially prices sign changes in delayed repairs. RE-178 then shows that adding any subcritical scalar jet family `j=o(log p/log log p)` only buys logarithmic leverage and cannot improve the leading polynomial distance-versus-sign-complexity exponent.

RE-179 replaces raw sign-phase count by its intrinsic location-sensitive currency. With exact Mertens value and first Euler slope, ordinary-prime repair beyond `lambda p`, and the KV envelope, the weighted negative reset budget must remain large until enough centered debt has been paid. RE-180 restores honest nonnegative multiplicity and proves that avoiding remote reset budget requires `Omega(sqrt(p)/log p)` actual prime deletions. RE-181 then shows this discrete tariff is attainable with multiplicities in `{0,1,2}` while matching the first two boundary coordinates exactly.

RE-182 closes the fixed-depth scalar-jet escape. For every fixed `K`, an ordinary-prime-supported `{0,1,2}` source can match **all** Euler derivatives through order `K` exactly at `s=1`, remain inside every fixed KV envelope, and still preserve the selector-scale transient Robin excursion. The construction recenters the raw jets at each repair scale, turning powers of `log x` into a uniformly conditioned fixed Vandermonde response on multiplicative shells; geometrically separated prime-shell quantization then drives every residual jet to zero with absolutely convergent corrections.

The first repair generation still contains `Theta_K(sqrt(p)/log p)` deletions and the same order of duplications, so increasing any fixed jet depth does not raise the polynomial source tariff that RE-180 already exposed. The live matched-control boundary is therefore beyond **every fixed finite scalar boundary jet**. A stronger theorem must use source information whose effective dimension grows with selector scale, a genuinely joint/nonlocal integrality or placement invariant that the shell interpolation cannot match, or another arithmetic constraint coupling the selector packet to its compensators.

This still does not make the full Euler germ a free invariant of the original Robin problem. RE-174 shows full exact identification is rigid but badly conditioned. The useful frontier is to find an intermediate source coordinate that is both genuinely implied by the arithmetic source and selective at Robin scale, rather than merely adding finitely many scalar derivatives that a bounded honest prime repair can interpolate.

## Keep source capacity, exact identification, jet depth and usable conditioning distinct

Quantitative PNT/KV fidelity, exact Mertens normalization, ordinary-prime support, integer multiplicity, finite/growing Euler boundary jets, the full high-temperature Euler germ, weighted reset complexity and the zero-side packet obstruction answer different questions. RE-173 shows strong coarse fidelity plus one exact boundary charge is non-rigid; RE-174 shows the full exact Euler hierarchy is rigid but ill-conditioned; RE-175--RE-178 price delayed compensation and expose the saturation of shallow scalar jets; RE-179 identifies where sign resets occur as the right continuous oscillation currency; RE-180 adds the complementary discrete tariff forced by honest nonnegative prime multiplicities; RE-181 proves that tariff can be paid while preserving exact two-jet data; RE-182 extends the matched repair to every fixed jet depth.

Future matched controls must therefore state **what source ambiguity survives, how jet depth scales with the selector, where sign resets occur, how much KV-weighted reset budget they carry, how many actual prime deletions are consumed, how those deletions are geometrically placed relative to the selector, which joint source moments are genuinely available, and at what selector-normalized precision/placement cost** before source rigidity is credited as Robin leverage.
