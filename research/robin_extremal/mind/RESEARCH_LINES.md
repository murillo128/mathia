## Control one-sided near-left cancellation at bounded reciprocal scale or with source-specific phase rigidity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-028-arbitrarily-slow-scaled-escape-defeats-target-anchored-turan`.

RE-112--RE-138 reduce the attained finite-edge branch to a strong atom plus reciprocal-scale cancellation geometry. RE-139--RE-142 show that rightmost-anchored Turán visibility can lose an unusable transfer factor even under strong upper source envelopes. RE-143 removes that transfer by anchoring at the original algebraically strong atom and applying Turán's First Main Theorem only to zeros on or to its right.

The apparent remaining hope was a shrinking one-sided near-left slab. RE-144 closes the naive version of that hope. A matched simple spectrum can have a strong target with **exactly one** retained mode on or to its right, negligible far-left and high-height tails, and yet hide the target on a fixed-relative observation window using only a near-left cluster whose scaled radius `W(U)` tends to infinity as slowly as prescribed. The cluster may lie inside `o(log U/U)` width. With local cardinality `B`, scaled radius only `O(log B)` already supports polynomial suppression.

Thus rightward mode count, global one-level sparsity and the mere fact that the left neighborhood shrinks are not the missing source resource. The positive theorem must force a **uniformly bounded reciprocal-scale radius**, a source-specific bound on local cardinality as that radius grows, or phase/sign rigidity that forbids the product cancellation mechanism for actual zeta zeros. RE-135 supplies visibility for fixed scaled radius; RE-144 shows that any unbounded radius, however slow, is a genuine matched-control escape.

## Keep target anchoring, rightward complexity, near-left scaled radius, local cardinality and high-height tail separate

Target anchoring removes the rightmost-transfer loss, but it does not make the remaining left packet harmless. RE-144 makes the currencies explicit: the rightward count can be one; the far-left packet can be negligible; the high-height tail can be negligible; the obstruction can still be carried by a growing near-left cluster. A useful theorem must therefore state which source law controls the cluster's scaled radius/cardinality/phase geometry rather than fold everything into “zero density.”
