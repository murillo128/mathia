RE-112--RE-119 reduce the attained finite-edge branch to almost-periodic edge profiles and show that the regular CA staircase samples the continuous leading profile at leading edge resolution. RE-120 proves that under a horizontally isolated attained edge every fixed algebraic correction remains inside the stable-filter closure of that same profile.

RE-121 removes horizontal isolation as the structural gate. Keeping all subedge zeros gives a damped correction controlled by the weighted near-edge mass `W(t)`, whose positive Laplace envelope satisfies `W(t)=O(t^A)` iff `L(u)=O(u^-A)`. Superpolynomial boundary-mass flatness therefore preserves every fixed algebraic edge-only correction even without a horizontal gap.

RE-122 now converts that abstract mass condition into a geometric zero-approach law. Let `H(t)` be the least ordinate of a subedge zero within horizontal distance `t` of the attained edge. Riemann--von Mangoldt gives

`W(t) << log(e H(t))/H(t)`.

If `kappa_H=liminf_(t->0) log H(t)/log(1/t)`, then every fixed order-`K` residual is `o(nu^-A)` for `A<min(kappa_H,K+1)`. In particular, superpolynomial height approach (`kappa_H=infinity`) makes every fixed algebraic CA-height correction edge-determined. Conversely, an actual residual of order `nu^-A` forces a subedge zero into horizontal width `O(log nu/nu)` at ordinate `O(nu^A log nu)`, hence `kappa_H<=A`.

The live finite-edge source is therefore no longer merely boundary-mass flatness. A fixed-order anomaly requires **polynomial-height approach to the edge**, and even that supplies only positive mass: the signed phases must still combine without cancelling. A closing theorem can prove sufficiently slow horizontal approach as a function of height, or, if polynomial-height approach occurs, control the signed oscillatory packet strongly enough to determine its contribution to the Robin/CA destination.

If the edge is not attained, RE-113's separate obstruction remains. The `Theta=1` branch also remains separate.

## Keep stable-profile universality, boundary-mass rate, height approach and signed spectral selection distinct

RE-119 shows that the regular CA mesh is already finer than every algebraic correction scale. RE-120--RE-121 show that horizontal isolation is only one way to make subedge mass invisible. RE-122 adds the concrete geometry: algebraic visibility requires subedge zeros to approach the finite edge at polynomial ordinate cost. This is still only a necessary positive-envelope resource; useful signed phase remains a separate gate.

A genuinely new finite-edge selector must therefore come from polynomially visible near-edge mass together with useful signed phase structure, a source coordinate outside this stable-filter/boundary-envelope closure, or another mechanism not encoded by the current finite-edge zero packet.
