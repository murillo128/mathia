## Find a selector-sensitive source invariant beyond subcritical scalar Euler interpolation

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-047-growing-scalar-euler-depth-below-the-kv-shell-scale-remains-non-rigid`.

RE-153--RE-168 identify the Pareto-weighted Chebyshev-deficit destination and quantify how much post-selector Robin motion remains possible under quantitative Korobov--Vinogradov source fidelity. The intrinsic decay length `ell_KV(T)=log T/V(T)` gives the spatial capacity scale: a bounded-multiplicative or subpower region can still carry order-one normalized displacement while enough future capacity remains.

RE-169 identifies Mertens normalization as an exact conservation law for the same Robin kernel. RE-170--RE-171 show that exact global charge does not localize it over flexible generalized sources. RE-172 adds finite ordinary-prime rigidity, but RE-173 proves that this is compactness rigidity: an infinite `{0,1,2}` delete/duplicate tail on ordinary prime labels can match `Gamma=gamma` exactly, stay below every fixed KV envelope and still create an order-one transient Robin excursion.

RE-174 closes the opposite extreme: an unbounded exact high-temperature Euler profile identifies every bounded multiplicity perturbation, but at exponentially bad Robin-scale conditioning. RE-175--RE-177 show that the first right-boundary Euler slope already forces and spatially prices sign changes in delayed repairs. RE-178 then shows that adding any subcritical scalar jet family `j=o(log p/log log p)` only buys logarithmic leverage and cannot improve the leading polynomial distance-versus-sign-complexity exponent.

RE-179 replaces raw sign-phase count by its intrinsic location-sensitive currency. With exact Mertens value and first Euler slope, ordinary-prime repair beyond `lambda p`, and the KV envelope, the weighted negative reset budget must remain large until enough centered debt has been paid. RE-180 restores honest nonnegative multiplicity and proves that avoiding remote reset budget requires `Omega(sqrt(p)/log p)` actual prime deletions. RE-181 then shows this discrete tariff is attainable with multiplicities in `{0,1,2}` while matching the first two boundary coordinates exactly.

RE-182 closes the fixed-depth scalar-jet escape. For every fixed `K`, an ordinary-prime-supported `{0,1,2}` source can match **all** Euler derivatives through order `K` exactly at `s=1`, remain inside every fixed KV envelope, and still preserve the selector-scale transient Robin excursion. The construction recenters the raw jets at each repair scale, turning powers of `log x` into a uniformly conditioned fixed Vandermonde response on multiplicative shells; geometrically separated prime-shell quantization then drives every residual jet to zero with absolutely convergent corrections.

RE-183 closes the corresponding separated-temperature escape. For every fixed finite panel `1=sigma_0<sigma_1<...<sigma_m`, one can again use an ordinary-prime-supported `{0,1,2}` repair to match the Mertens coordinate and every Euler value at the selected temperatures exactly, remain inside every fixed KV envelope and retain the transient Robin excursion. The finite shell response is a Vandermonde-type matrix in `2^(1-sigma_i)`, so replacing derivatives at one boundary point by finitely many separated scalar temperatures does not create rigidity.

RE-184 shows that the fixed-depth qualifier is not the true boundary. With `V(p)=(log p)^(3/5)/(log log p)^(1/5)`, the same honest ordinary-prime source class can match every boundary derivative through any growing depth satisfying `K(p) log(K(p)+2)=o(V(p))`, remain below every fixed KV envelope and retain the order-one transient Robin ambiguity. Its monomial-shell construction has interpolation overhead `exp(O(K log K))`.

RE-185 shows that the extra `log K` is not source rigidity but coordinate conditioning. After normalizing each logarithmic repair interval and using a Chebyshev-type interpolation basis, the shell-response inverse costs only `exp(O(K))`. There is an absolute `c_*>0` such that every depth `K<=c_*V(p)` can still be matched exactly by an ordinary-prime-supported `{0,1,2}` control with the same KV fidelity and order-one transient Robin excursion.

RE-186 shows that even the `Theta(V(p))` boundary is architectural rather than source-rigid. Keep the Chebyshev interpolation window at fixed logarithmic width, advance successive repair baselines by the microstep `h_p=exp(-Theta(V(p)))`, and perturb the `K+1` Chebyshev nodes onto distinct residue tracks modulo `h_p`. Since the actual PNT shell width is quadratically smaller than the chosen microstep, all prime shells from all generations remain globally disjoint; because the normalized Chebyshev translation per generation is only `O(h_p)`, its exact transport cost is polynomial rather than `exp(Theta(K))`. The construction therefore matches every boundary derivative through every depth `K log log p=o(log p)` while retaining ordinary-prime support, `{0,1,2}` multiplicity, every fixed KV envelope and the same order-one transient Robin excursion.

The live scalar boundary is now the **critical crossover itself**,
`K_cap(p) asymp log p/log log p`. Every asymptotically subcritical truncation `K=o(K_cap)` is constructively non-rigid in the honest bounded-multiplicity prime source class. The remaining question is whether a fixed positive fraction of `K_cap`, the full critical scale, or a joint/nonlocal source invariant finally defeats shellwise interpolation. On the constructive side the new obstruction is the exact prime-power Euler remainder `exp(-log p+O(K log log p))`, not KV shell spacing or moving-frame conditioning.

This still does not make the full Euler germ a free invariant of the original Robin problem. RE-174 shows full exact identification is rigid but badly conditioned. The useful frontier is to find an intermediate source coordinate that is both genuinely implied by the arithmetic source and selective at Robin scale, rather than replacing one scalar family by a larger but still interpolable one.

## Keep source capacity, exact identification, scalar-family dimension and usable conditioning distinct

Quantitative PNT/KV fidelity, exact Mertens normalization, ordinary-prime support, integer multiplicity, finite/growing Euler boundary jets, finite temperature panels, the full high-temperature Euler germ, weighted reset complexity and the zero-side packet obstruction answer different questions. RE-173 shows strong coarse fidelity plus one exact boundary charge is non-rigid; RE-174 shows the full exact Euler hierarchy is rigid but ill-conditioned; RE-175--RE-178 price delayed compensation and expose the saturation of shallow scalar jets; RE-179 identifies where sign resets occur as the right continuous oscillation currency; RE-180 adds the complementary discrete tariff forced by honest nonnegative prime multiplicities; RE-181 proves that tariff can be paid while preserving exact two-jet data; RE-182 extends the matched repair to every fixed jet depth; RE-183 extends non-rigidity to every fixed finite separated-temperature panel; RE-184 extends exact boundary-jet matching to a substantial growing depth; RE-185 removes the interpolation-coordinate log loss and reaches `K=Theta(V(p))`; and RE-186 removes the coarse-generation transport loss and reaches every `K=o(log p/log log p)`.

Future matched controls must therefore state **what source ambiguity survives, how scalar-family dimension scales with the selector, whether the proposed depth reaches the critical `log p/log log p` scale rather than merely growing, where sign resets occur, how much KV-weighted reset budget they carry, how many actual prime deletions are consumed, how those deletions are geometrically placed relative to the selector, which joint source moments are genuinely available, and at what selector-normalized precision/placement cost** before source rigidity is credited as Robin leverage.
