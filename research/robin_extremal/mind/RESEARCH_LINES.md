## Resolve the scalar Euler window between repairability and log-squared saturation

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-048-high-euler-boundary-jets-become-a-net-multiplicity-channel-beyond-log-squared-depth`.

The matched-control program has progressively removed apparent scalar rigidity thresholds. Ordinary-prime `{0,1,2}` controls can preserve every fixed KV source envelope and an order-one transient Robin excursion while matching exact Mertens/Euler data at fixed depth, growing depth and separated temperatures. RE-188 optimizes the deep Euler-jet repair to a linear-width Chebyshev window and keeps non-rigidity throughout

`K=o((log p)^(5/3)(loglog p)^(1/3))`.

RE-189 supplies a qualitatively different upper-scale obstruction. For a prime factor with `L=log q`, the factorial-normalized `j`th boundary derivative equals a universal `1` plus nonzero pole modes of size roughly `exp(-c j/L^2)`. Once `j` reaches the log-squared scale, primes in a fixed multiplicative selector window become exponentially indistinguishable to these normalized jets. A finite packet is seen primarily through net multiplicity; zero-net-multiplicity packets have exponentially small normalized response.

RE-190 closes a separate near-boundary loophole. For a packet supported on a fixed multiplicative selector shell `q~p`, the entire exact Euler profile sampled at `s=1+tau/log p` with `|tau|=o(log p)` becomes, after the trivial `e^tau` normalization, operator-norm close to the single reciprocal-mass functional `sum c_q/q`. Thus arbitrarily dense sampling in any `o(1)` neighborhood of `s=1` is asymptotically rank one at the natural weighted source scale. More nearby scalar values do not restore robust location information that finite jets failed to supply.

Scalar depth is therefore not an indefinitely increasing information resource, and neither is arbitrarily dense local spectral sampling. The current open derivative region remains the **intermediate window** between the constructive repair range and the onset of `j~(log p)^2` saturation. A useful theorem could close that gap by extending matched repair, prove a genuine conditioning barrier before saturation, or show that recovering the exponentially small prime-location modes beyond saturation costs an unaffordable selector-normalized resource.

A genuinely different scalar test must now leave the shrinking-boundary regime: for example, exact spectral probes a constant distance from `1`, or probes coupled across several logarithmically separated prime scales. More importantly, the combined evidence strengthens the case for a joint/nonlocal source invariant tied to the Robin destination. Below saturation, scalar constraints remain repairable over a large range; above saturation, high jets approach a common count mode; and throughout shrinking spectral neighborhoods, exact values approach a common reciprocal-mass mode. Simply asking for more local scalar data is no longer a credible monotone route to selector rigidity.

## Keep source fidelity, exact identification and usable conditioning distinct

Finite ordinary-prime rigidity, full exact Euler-germ identification, fixed/growing jet matching, KV fidelity, weighted reset complexity, repair width, high-order jet saturation, shrinking-window spectral collapse and transient Robin displacement answer different questions. RE-188 shows a representation cost can disappear under better conditioning; RE-189 shows a failure mode in which a high-order observable approaches a low-information common mode; RE-190 shows that even a continuum of exact nearby values can have asymptotically rank-one conditioning on selector-scale support.

Future proposals should therefore state the surviving source ambiguity, exact invariant family, optimized repair width, source-fidelity cost, integer-multiplicity constraints, normalization, inverse conditioning, spectral/source diameter and selector-normalized destination effect. A scalar family should be credited as Robin leverage only after matched repair, high-depth common-mode collapse and shrinking-window low-rank collapse are ruled out at the scale actually used.
