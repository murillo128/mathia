## Resolve the vertical Euler continuum between constructive hiding and the sharpened source-capacity boundary

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-054-continuum-vertical-hiding-pays-an-exponential-signed-prefix-tariff`.

The scalar Euler program separates positive-real compression from vertical-phase resolution. RE-196--RE-203 show that the positive real ray can be nearly blind and finite vertical panels can still be aliased. RE-204 moves to a whole vertical interval and proves that separated hiding has an exponential signed-prefix cost. RE-205--RE-208 construct ordinary-prime repairs in a small explicit range while respecting fixed KV envelopes. RE-216 improves that constructive side without changing the arithmetic realization: an off-center binomial predictor cuts the proven coefficient-variation rate from `12 log 2=8.3177...` to at most `6.081`, lifting the explicit range at `H=log 8` from `c<0.0289079...` to `c<0.0395410...`.

RE-209--RE-213 establish the unit exponential converse and show that KV fidelity closes the exact half-log endpoint and a thicker subendpoint corridor. RE-214 then isolates a source-independent layer: bounded ordinary-prime edit multiplicity alone implies `R<<sqrt(Q)`, so finite carrier capacity already forbids every fixed height above `HT=(1/2)log Q` up to a logarithmic boundary layer.

RE-215 sharpens that layer. Replacing the squared Kaiser test by the endpoint-zero continuous Kaiser--Bessel window cancels the nonintegrable first tail and gives

`R >>_H (1-epsilon) e^(HT)/(HT log(HT))`.

Combining this with bounded prime capacity yields

`HT_Q <= (1/2)log Q + log log Q + log log log Q + O_H(1)`.

The leading threshold `1/(2H)` is unchanged, but the source-independent boundary layer is now only one `log log Q` plus `log log log Q`, rather than the `2 log log Q` allowance of RE-214. KV remains genuinely stronger at the exact endpoint because it forces a deficit of order `omega(V(Q))` below it.

RE-217 isolates the remaining **spectral** constant. The same RE-215 test implies that every finite or measure-valued separated predictor satisfying `sup_(|t|<=T)|1-mu_hat(t)|<=epsilon<1` must pay

`||mu||_TV >>_H (1-epsilon) e^(HT)/(HT log(HT))`.

Thus any logarithmic-height predictor with coefficient variation `Q^(kappa+o(1))` necessarily has `kappa>=Hc`. Defining `C_sep` as the optimal leading Wiener-norm exponent for fixed-corridor separated prediction gives the current rigorous interval

`1 <= C_sep <= 6.081`,

where the upper endpoint is RE-216. This is the clean leading-order bottleneck: RE-208 already realizes every regular discrete predictor with strict `kappa<1/2`, so a predictor with `C_sep=1` would make the existing arithmetic interface reach every fixed `c<1/(2H)`, exactly the leading bounded-prime capacity boundary. Progress should therefore target the pure prediction problem—construct a fixed-corridor causal predictor with Wiener exponent substantially below `6.081`, or prove a spectral lower bound strictly above one. Classical Chebyshev/Newton band-limited predictors are relevant prior art, but qualitative convergence is not enough; their uniform-error Wiener norm at depth `HT` is the missing quantity.

## Keep exact identification, stable inversion, source capacity and fidelity distinct

RE-174 gives exact source identification from rich Euler data, but the later results show why injectivity is not the selector-scale statement. Positive-real data can be nearly singular; finite vertical panels can be aliased; and a continuum interval forces an exponentially large signed-prefix primitive.

RE-216 adds another resource distinction on the constructive side: **spectral prediction norm is not the same currency as prime-placement capacity**. The old `12 log 2` coefficient tariff was not arithmetic; moving the analytic expansion center already reduces it to `6.081` while reusing the same ordinary-prime realization. RE-217 sharpens that distinction into a quantitative sandwich: separated prediction itself costs at least unit Wiener exponent, the current explicit predictor costs at most `6.081`, and the prime realization accepts every strict coefficient exponent below `1/2`. RE-215 separately shows that the half-logarithmic leading ceiling is a carrier-cardinality obstruction, window optimization determines only a subexponential boundary layer, and KV discrepancy is a finer source-fidelity input still required at and below the exact endpoint.

The full ledger includes spectral interval width, logarithmic support gap, predictor/Wiener norm, total reciprocal variation, signed prefix primitive, prime-count capacity, Chebyshev discrepancy, window-tail norm, moment-cancellation order, integer multiplicities and KV source fidelity. They remain independent until an exact theorem relates them.
