RE-085 removes every regular bounded fixed-order mixed-event statistic as a leading escape on stretched same-block fans. Higher-layer logarithmic event mass is `o(1)`, so the complete normalized event measure converges in total variation to its endpoint-determined first layer.

RE-086 now opens one singular channel far enough to expose its arithmetic price. If a fixed-layer higher event is actually an adjacent support boundary, its inverse-layer support chamber is prime-free and anchored at that event's generating base prime. The neighboring chamber-facing prime gap obeys the layer-independent normalized law

`G_C(p,j)/(p log p) >= (1-o(1)) a |I_C|`.

Tied support boundaries force simultaneous versions of this condition at power-related prime scales. A rare higher-layer event can therefore remain structurally important only by being selected at a location carrying enough ordinary-prime-gap capacity.

The live theorem is to show that these **anchored gap/event resonances** are too sparse or carry too little total threshold capacity to support the long false-RH fan, or to identify a countermechanism that keeps them viable. If that channel survives, the remaining singular possibilities are skipped-span conditioning, tied-state admissibility beyond endpoint geometry, and discontinuous rightmost-maximizer selection. Regular event moments and generic higher-layer mass estimates are no longer the place to spend proof effort.

## Keep event mass, boundary selection and anchored arithmetic cost separate

RE-085 proves vanishing higher-layer normalized mass, not vanishing structural effect. RE-086 shows the first concrete way structural effect can survive: selection as a support boundary converts a mass-negligible event into an anchored prime-gap requirement. The requirement is necessary, not yet rare.

For fixed layer depth, normalized threshold capacity costs the same `G/(p log p)` currency at every layer. Deep layers, non-boundary skipped events and nonlocal state-selection rules remain outside that theorem and must be treated separately rather than folded into the anchored-gap estimate.
