`G(U,T) exp(-Lambda_kappa N_T) M_0(U)`.

RE-143 changes the anchoring rather than improving that transfer. Normalize at the original algebraically strong atom and apply Turán's First Main Theorem only to the retained zeros on or to its right. The power-sum roots then satisfy `|z|>=1`, the Robin coefficient sector makes the zeroth sum coherent, and one obtains

`sup |S_0^+| >= 2 c_Theta exp(-Lambda_kappa^(1) N_+) M_0(U)`

inside the physical observation window with **no rightmost gain factor**. The cost depends only on the rightward mode count.

The remaining finite-core obstruction is now explicit. Zeros left of the target can cancel the rightward witness, but if `M_0(U)>=cU^-A`, every zero farther than `H log U/U` to the left contributes only an arbitrarily small algebraic remainder for large fixed `H`. Hence, up to algebraic accuracy, the unresolved cancellation lives in the one-sided slab

`beta_0-H log U/U < beta < beta_0`.

The live theorem is therefore relational and local: control the signed Robin mass, occupancy or phase cancellation in this shrinking near-left slab around a strong atom, while separately paying the rightward Turán complexity and high-height tail. A theorem forcing enough one-sided emptiness, sign imbalance or noncancellation here could close the packet without any rightmost transfer estimate. Another global upper zero-density exponent that does not constrain this target-relative slab is not the needed resource.

## Keep target anchoring, rightward complexity, near-left cancellation and high-height tail as separate currencies

RE-130 prices the size of an extracted atom. RE-139 makes every finite rightmost-anchored packet visible without horizontal-spread penalty.
