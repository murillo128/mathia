## Resolve the vertical Euler continuum between constructive hiding and the now-sharp positive-window coercive boundary

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-robin-failure-requires-self-tangent-half-mass-event-gaps` through `MI-054-continuum-vertical-hiding-pays-an-exponential-signed-prefix-tariff`.

The scalar Euler program now separates positive-real compression from vertical-phase resolution. RE-196--RE-199 show that the full positive real ray can remain exponentially almost blind at selector scale even when a fixed positive fraction of `log p` boundary jets are matched. RE-200 restores linear Euclidean dimension with vertical complex samples on one shell, but RE-201--RE-203 show that finite panels remain globally vulnerable to multiplicative shell aliases: arbitrary finite vertical Euler panels can be aliased by multi-shell polynomial synthesis.

RE-204 moves from panels to a whole interval. Hiding a local selector-scale Euler atom from `|t|<=T` by repairs separated by a logarithmic gap `H` has an exponential spectral-gap cost in reciprocal variation. RE-205--RE-208 then show that ordinary primes can pay a substantial part of that continuum cost while preserving every fixed Korobov--Vinogradov source envelope. With `T_Q=c log Q` and `H=log 8`, the current constructive range is

`0<c<1/(24 H log 2)=0.0289079...`.

RE-209 supplies the source-faithful converse inside the same separated ordinary-prime architecture. Integrating the spectral-gap test one level further forces a large exponentially weighted cumulative primitive, which is exactly a signed prime-count prefix after arithmetic pullback. RE-210--RE-212 progressively optimize the positive compact test, culminating in Kaiser--Bessel rates approaching the unit exponent from below.

RE-213 closes both gaps that RE-212 deliberately left open. The Kaiser tail estimates are uniform when the parameter grows with `A=HT`; choosing `beta=sqrt(A log A)` gives

`log R >= A-O(sqrt(A log A))`.

For `T_Q=O(log Q)`, this loss is `o(V(Q))`, where `V(Q)=(log Q)^(3/5)/(log log Q)^(1/5)`. Therefore preserving every fixed KV envelope requires the sharp near-endpoint condition

`((1/2)log Q-H T_Q)/V(Q) -> +infinity`.

In particular, a fixed logarithmic window `T_Q=c log Q` requires **strictly** `c<1/(2H)`; equality is now excluded. At `H=log 8`, the separated ordinary-prime `{0,1,2}` architecture cannot hide a selector-scale packet for any

`c>=1/(2 log 8)=0.2404491734...`.

RE-213 also solves the positive compact-window extremal subproblem. If `w` is any nonnegative probability density supported on `[-1,1]`, its transform satisfies `M(a)>=sech(a)`, hence the tail functional

`Gamma(w)=sup_a[-log M(a)]/a`

obeys `Gamma(w)<=1`. Combined with the Kaiser lower construction, `sup_w Gamma(w)=1`. The unit exponential rate is therefore the exact ceiling of this positive compact-window representation, not merely a limit reached by one convenient family.

The remaining gap is no longer a window-shaping question. Explicit hiding still exists only below `0.0289079...`, while positive compact-test coercivity rules out every fixed `c>=0.2404491734...` and even quantifies the required subendpoint KV margin. Progress must either improve the constructive source architecture by controlling signed weighted prefixes more efficiently, prove a stronger coercive theorem that is not limited to positive compact tests, or leave the fixed-gap separated-repair architecture entirely.

## Keep exact identification, stable inversion and continuum source cost distinct

RE-174 still gives exact source identification from sufficiently rich Euler data. The newer results show why exact injectivity is not the relevant selector-scale statement. Positive-real data can be nearly singular; finite vertical panels can be aliased; and even a continuum vertical interval admits ordinary-prime near-nulls up to positive logarithmic height. Conversely, beyond the sharp positive-window threshold the interval forces a source prefix excursion that violates KV fidelity.

The resource ledger must therefore include spectral interval width, logarithmic support gap, total reciprocal variation, signed prefix primitive, Chebyshev discrepancy, prime-placement capacity, moment-cancellation order, integer multiplicities and KV source fidelity. RE-213 adds a distinction between the **optimal rate of one proof representation** and the broader source problem: `sup_w Gamma(w)=1` closes the positive compact-window extremal question, but does not prove that all separated-source coercive mechanisms are capped at one.
