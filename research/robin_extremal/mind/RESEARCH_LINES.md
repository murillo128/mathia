RE-076--RE-085 reconstruct the late selector geometry and remove bounded fixed-order mixed-event mass as a leading escape. RE-086 turns selection of a fixed-layer event as an adjacent support boundary into an anchored ordinary-prime-gap cost, while RE-087 shows that summing marginal one-sided gap capacity over all candidate primes is too generous to contradict the CA amplitude.

RE-088 removes sufficiently deep selected boundaries: under a positive off-RH exponent gap, some finite depth cutoff makes all deeper layers threshold-negligible. Its ambient counting mechanism cannot eliminate depth `2`.

The challenged RE-089 formulation has been withdrawn and replaced by RE-090. The corrected theorem uses Järviniemi's `0.57+epsilon` dyadic bound for the total length of square-root-sized consecutive-prime gaps. If both adjacent support boundaries contain the same fixed higher depth `j>=2`, their event-free chamber pulls back to such a large ordinary-prime gap, and CA support capacity converts the aggregate gap-mass bound into vanishing threshold measure whenever `Theta>0.57`.

Combining RE-088 with RE-090 and the currently anchored all-large short-interval exponent `0.52` gives an explicit finite reduction. Outside vanishing threshold measure, every sufficiently late selected cell either touches a first-layer atom on at least one boundary or has two disjoint nonempty depth sets contained in `{2,...,19}`. For untied boundaries the residual higher-layer alternative is `2<=j_-!=j_+<20`.

The live theorem must therefore exploit **joint low-depth selection compatibility**: first-layer contact, mixed-depth resonances, tied power-related events with disjoint depth sets, simultaneous boundary constraints, skipped spans, or rightmost-maximizer dynamics. Neither marginal gap counting, deeper-layer asymptotics, nor same-depth higher-layer scarcity addresses that surviving carrier.

## Keep event mass, selected-boundary depth, same-depth chamber scarcity and mixed-depth compatibility separate

RE-085 says high layers carry vanishing normalized event mass; RE-088 says sufficiently deep selected boundaries occupy vanishing threshold measure; RE-090 says fixed same-depth higher-layer chambers also occupy vanishing threshold measure above `Theta>0.57`. None of these proves that mixed-depth low-layer chambers or first-layer-touching cells are rare.

The remaining obstruction is finite-depth and relational. The next estimate must couple the two selected boundaries rather than sum candidate capacity one side or one layer at a time. The `0.57` threshold is the current large-gap-mass frontier, not an intrinsic barrier of the CA mechanism.
