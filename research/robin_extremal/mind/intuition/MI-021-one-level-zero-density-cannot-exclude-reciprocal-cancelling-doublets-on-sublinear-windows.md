# MI-021 — One-level zero density cannot exclude reciprocal cancelling doublets on sublinear windows

**Evidence level:** exact formal matched-control obstruction from [RE-132](../../findings/RE-132-arbitrarily-sparse-simple-subedge-spectra-hide-dominant-atoms-on-every-sublinear-window.md), complementing the atom extraction of RE-130 and the forced-companion geometry of RE-129--RE-131

RE-130 shows that stronger off-critical zero-density estimates sharpen the conversion from total algebraic packet mass to a dominant algebraically strong atom. RE-132 identifies the boundary of that resource: after the atom has been extracted, **global one-level counting strength alone does not exclude the local cancellation geometry needed to hide it**.

A formal simple spectrum can be made extremely lacunary, with off-critical count only `O(log log T)`, while containing globally dominant equal-amplitude doublets separated by exactly `pi/U` and horizontally locked. With the actual finite-order coefficient kernel, those doublets cancel uniformly on every observation window `H=o(U)`. The construction obeys functional-equation symmetry and is far sparser than any polynomial zero-density upper bound requires.

The obstruction is therefore local rather than global. Better estimates for `N(sigma,T)` can make the forced atom larger but cannot, by themselves, prevent a reciprocal-scale companion with the cancelling phase. The useful next input must constrain local spacing/phase geometry of **actual** off-critical zeta zeros, derive the fixed-relative-window visibility used by RE-131, or impose an arithmetic zero-detection relation absent from the matched spectrum.

The window scale remains decisive. The `pi/U` control ceases to hide on a fixed relative window, so RE-132 does not contradict RE-131 and does not realize an actual zeta-zero configuration. It only closes the implication “arbitrarily strong one-level zero-density upper bounds eliminate the sublinear-window hiding geometry.”
