# MI-001 — Robin failure requires deep event selection, an intermediate mixed-race excursion, and increasingly sparse event-ladder coincidences

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed CA selection, finite-annulus localization, all-orders tax calibration, and envelope-to-layer Diophantine narrowing through RE-019

## Core intuition

A hypothetical clean Robin counterexample has several distinct nonlocal requirements. The CA mechanism must first select a self-tangent event using a prime-power tower whose depth grows with scale. Once selected, the decisive mixed prime-race excursion is forced into a broad finite intermediate annulus rather than a tiny local window or an arbitrarily remote tail.

The newest evidence adds a third constraint: if the adjacent CA event gap sits inside a small ordinary-prime envelope, the boundary events are forced onto progressively deeper prime-power ladders, and a bounded envelope can recur only through increasingly sparse near-collisions between fixed prime bases.

## Strongest justified principle

RE-001--RE-013 tie clean counterexamples to self-tangent CA boundary events, reduce the sign to a mixed standard/reciprocal prime race, close local-jet freedom, and show that the selector cannot be resolved at its natural scale from any fixed layer depth. RE-014--RE-015 localize any first-order threshold excursion between a short-window invisibility range and a finite PNT-controlled tail. RE-016 shows that the deterministic CA tax is simpler than the selector: all inverse-logarithmic orders are governed by the second prime-power layer.

RE-017 places every adjacent CA gap inside its consecutive-prime envelope and proves a gap-to-depth ladder. If the ordinary gap is `g`, boundary prime bases satisfy `log r<2g+O(1)` while their layer depths are at least roughly `log X/(2g)`. In particular `g=o(log X)` forces all boundary layers to infinity and all boundary prime bases to `X^{o(1)}`.

RE-018 sharpens fixed `g<=G`: only finitely many boundary prime bases are possible, and any infinite escape has a subsequence formed by two fixed distinct prime-power event ladders at bounded additive coordinate distance. RE-019 proves those near-collisions require logarithmically growing jumps in at least one layer index, improving the bounded-envelope host count to `O_G(log Z/log log Z)`.

## Program consequence

Exploit the **selector-plus-annulus-plus-ladder** structure. In the bounded-envelope regime, seek a Diophantine separation theorem strong enough to exclude the fixed-base ladder near-collisions altogether or show that their selected mixed-race contribution is negligible. In growing envelopes, quantify how the weakening depth bound trades against available prime-gap and annulus information.

Do not infer Robin merely from sparsity of candidate events; the final step still has to control the mixed race at those selected events.

## Counterevidence / boundary

The near-collision results are necessary conditions inside the clean regular chamber and do not rule out an infinite sparse exceptional sequence. The bounded-envelope theorem says nothing uniform when `g` grows. The finite-annulus localization and tax expansion also remain distinct from a sign theorem for the selected race.

## Epistemic status

**Exact separation of selection, calibration and race scales, with a new gap-to-layer rigidity that makes bounded-envelope hypothetical failures Diophantine and logarithmically sparse; Robin's inequality is not proved.**

## Falsification criterion

Find a clean self-tangent peak violating the event-gap/depth formulas, construct dense bounded-envelope near-collisions contrary to RE-019, or show a first-order threshold contribution outside the RE-014--RE-015 annulus. A positive continuation should convert the fixed-ladder sparsity or growing-envelope tradeoff into control of the selected mixed race.
