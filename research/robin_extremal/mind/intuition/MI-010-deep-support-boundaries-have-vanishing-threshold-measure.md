# MI-010 — Deep support-boundary layers have vanishing threshold measure; the low-layer chamber remains

**Evidence level:** exact under the off-RH exponent-gap hypotheses of RE-088. The result removes sufficiently deep adjacent support-boundary layers from positive threshold measure but leaves every fixed low layer, especially `j=2`, unresolved.

RE-086 turns selection of a CA layer-`j` event as an adjacent support boundary into an ordinary-prime-gap cost. RE-087 shows that the total marginal gap pool is too abundant to force a contradiction when selection compatibility is ignored. RE-088 instead weights the selected cells by depth.

If `delta=1-b_1-theta>0`, the individual fan-cell width decays like `Y^(-delta)/log Y`, while the number of CA events at depth at least `r` grows only like `T^(1/r)` up to logarithms. Therefore, whenever `delta>1/r`, the total threshold measure of cells touching depth-`>=r` selected boundaries tends to zero. Under a hypothetical exponent gap `Theta>theta`, some finite `r` always satisfies that inequality.

The consequence is a genuine finite-depth reduction: arbitrarily deep support-boundary resonances cannot carry positive threshold measure. But the mechanism has a hard low-layer limit. Since `delta<1/2`, it can never eliminate the second layer by itself.

The remaining theorem should therefore analyze the finite low-layer compatibility problem, not improve the deep-layer count. Tied power-related events, simultaneous left/right boundary constraints and rightmost-maximizer dynamics are now structurally more relevant than the tail of the CA hierarchy.

**Boundary.** Vanishing threshold measure does not mean deep events never occur, and it does not show that low layers are rare. The argument is conditional on the exponent-gap setup and is not an RH proof.
