# MI-019 — Strong finite-edge atoms can only hide through reciprocal-scale annular phase competition

**Evidence level:** exact consequence of [RE-125](../../findings/RE-125-invisible-algebraic-subedge-atoms-require-a-reciprocal-scale-ordinate-annulus.md), building on the full-packet phase transfer of RE-123 and matched-filter localization of RE-124. No theorem on actual zeta-zero crowding, diffuse packets, the nonattained edge or the `Theta=1` branch is claimed.

RE-124 shows that an algebraically strong subedge atom can remain invisible only if comparable coefficient mass is spectrally localized within `U^(-1+eta)` of its ordinate. RE-125 identifies a source-specific lower edge for that cancellation window.

For positive-ordinate finite-edge zeros with `sigma_0<=beta<Theta<1`, the leading Robin/CA coefficient

`1/[rho(1-rho)]`

lies in a fixed angular sector strictly inside the open right half-plane, uniformly in the ordinate. Fixed-order `1/U` corrections preserve that sector. After demodulating at a target ordinate `gamma_0`, every neighboring atom with

`|gamma-gamma_0| <= c_*/U`

therefore has a uniformly positive real projection. Exact multiplicity and tighter `o(1/U)` micro-clusters **reinforce** the target; they cannot cancel it.

Combining this sector geometry with the RE-124 matched filter leaves a genuine annulus as the only strong-atom cancellation region:

`c_*/U < |gamma-gamma_0| <= U^(-1+eta)`.

The outer radius is set by spectral localization; the inner radius is set by the phase rotation needed to leave the common half-plane sector. A two-frequency control with spacing `Theta(1/U)` shows that this reciprocal lower scale is order-sharp for the mechanism: once the phase difference reaches a constant multiple of `1/U`, cancellation over the long observation window can become power-small.

This is a useful distinction between **frequency proximity** and **cancellation-capable proximity**. A cluster may be arbitrarily dense yet still be ineffective if source-specific coefficient phases keep it in one cone. The relevant source geometry is not “many nearby zeros” but enough mass at the scale where relative phase can actually rotate across the separating half-plane.

The surviving finite-edge alternatives are correspondingly narrow. Either algebraic boundary mass is diffuse so that no individual atom reaches the target scale, or a strong atom is accompanied by comparable mass in the reciprocal annulus with the correct signed phase geometry. The regular CA mesh is already fine enough to realize any surviving continuum excursion at fixed algebraic order.

**Boundary.** RE-125 is a necessary geometry for hiding a strong atom, not an existence or exclusion theorem for reciprocal-scale zero clusters. It does not control the aggregate signed mass inside the annulus and does not address a diffuse cloud of individually subcritical atoms. The next source theorem must price those two remaining possibilities rather than count arbitrarily close zeros.
