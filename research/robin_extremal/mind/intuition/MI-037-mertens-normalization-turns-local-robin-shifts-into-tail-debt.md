# MI-037 — Mertens normalization is a conservation law; finite prime support is rigid, but noncompact prime compensation survives

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed synthesis from [RE-153](../../findings/RE-153-self-tangent-robin-height-is-a-pareto-weighted-chebyshev-deficit-average.md), [RE-164](../../findings/RE-164-korobov-vinogradov-fidelity-controls-robin-source-ambiguity.md) through [RE-173](../../findings/RE-173-infinite-prime-supported-exact-mertens-compensation-survives-kv-fidelity.md). Classical Mertens normalization, unique factorization and prime distribution are external mathematics; the Robin-kernel debt/capacity consequences are line-specific.

RE-169 identifies the global invariant. With `h(t)=-log(1-t^(-1))/log t`, the difference of source Mertens constants is exactly the full future Robin-kernel weighted discrepancy. Once `Gamma` is fixed, an accumulated post-selector Robin displacement is a debt that must be repaid later. The KV envelope bounds repayment capacity but does not, by itself, specify where the opposite-sign charge sits.

RE-170--RE-171 prove that scalar precision cannot supply localization over flexible generalized sources: one can create an order-one transient excursion and later tune the final Mertens mismatch to `Gamma(Q)=gamma` exactly.

RE-172 adds an arithmetic support gate. For a **finite perturbation on ordinary primes with rational multiplicity changes**, exact Mertens matching forces every coefficient change to vanish. After clearing denominators and exponentiating, the largest changed prime is a private numerator divisor, so a nontrivial finite Euler-factor relation cannot equal one.

RE-173 shows exactly where that rigidity ends. Remove finitely many ordinary primes just beyond the selector, partially repay their Mertens debt with later duplicated primes, then use an infinite geometrically escaping prime tail to fill the remaining positive debt by an absolutely convergent subsum. The final source uses only ordinary prime labels and multiplicities `{0,1,2}`, satisfies `Gamma(Q)=gamma` exactly, differs from the ordinary Chebyshev source by only `O(sqrt(p)+(log x)^2)` and therefore lies inside every prescribed fixed KV envelope, yet has already produced an order-one normalized Robin excursion before compensation begins.

The durable distinction is therefore **finite algebraic injectivity versus noncompact spatial localization**. Exact Mertens normalization plus prime support and integer multiplicity is rigid only when the perturbation has a largest modified prime. An infinite tail removes that private-prime argument while keeping the global conservation law and very strong PNT fidelity.

This closes the natural hope that RE-172 would extend to all KV-controlled prime-supported integer sources. The next source-faithful discriminator must use information the delete/duplicate tail does not preserve. The obvious remaining arithmetic gate is multiplicity **exactly one** at every ordinary prime, but invoking that condition is useful only if it yields a selector-sensitive theorem rather than tautologically specifying the source. Another possibility is a nonlocal Euler-product/zero relation that couples the selected prefix to its future tail strongly enough to forbid delayed compensation.

**Boundary.** RE-173 is a synthetic matched source, not the actual primes: some primes are deleted and others duplicated. It does not show that multiplicity-one prime sources are ambiguous, nor produce a Robin counterexample. Its role is to prove that prime labels, integer multiplicity, exact Mertens normalization and arbitrarily strong fixed KV relative fidelity still leave the transient Robin coordinate underdetermined once noncompact compensation is admitted.
