# MI-009 — The long-fan front survives every bounded fixed-order event-mass observable; the obstruction must be singular cross-layer assembly

**Evidence level:** supported by RE-080--RE-085 under the stated long same-block fan normalization. RE-082 is an exact/asymptotic negative control for the common-amplitude selector law; RE-083--RE-084 give exact endpoint first-layer reconstruction; RE-085 gives total-variation and fixed-order event-mass collapse. None constructs a genuine false-RH CA fan.

RE-080 shows that a long same-block fan forces complementary power fronts in the positive transport measure. RE-081 removes the obvious discrete explanation by proving that higher-layer prime-power atoms are negligible in the two leading Lyapunov transport moments. RE-082 then shows that the remaining smooth common-amplitude selector law itself already realizes the required front.

RE-083--RE-084 close first-layer-only assembly escapes. Endpoint selected-prime data reconstruct the full first-layer birth set and exact threshold timeline, and at late scale that timeline is uniformly an ordinary-prime configuration shifted by one half. Its direct Robin timing tail is summable. Thus neither endpoint support mismatch nor a regular functional of the first-layer timing geometry supplies independent leading information.

RE-085 now upgrades higher-layer smallness from selected transport moments to the **complete physical event measure**. Give every crossed CA event `(p,j)` mass `log p` at its exact threshold and normalize by the total log-state growth `L_01`. On a stretched fan, the higher-layer mass fraction

`epsilon_01 = P_01/L_01 << Y_1^(-1/2) (log Y_1)^(3/2)`

tends to zero. The normalized full event measure converges in total variation to its first-layer part, which is endpoint-determined by RE-083--RE-084. Consequently every bounded one-event observable and every bounded fixed-order mass-polynomial statistic has the same asymptotic value after all higher-layer events are deleted.

The remaining information is therefore not merely “nonlinear” but **singular with respect to normalized event log-mass**. A sparse higher-layer event can still split a chamber, change an exponent vector, create a tie, alter adjacency, or change the exposed CA state even though its normalized mass vanishes. Such state-selection observables are discontinuous under deletion of a small amount of event mass, so RE-085 does not suppress them. Conversely, any bounded fixed-order statistic of mixed event locations is now a matched control and cannot carry the needed order-one cross-layer signal.

This sharpens the proof-allocation rule. Smooth density shape, ordinary-prime birth support, first-layer timing, and every regular bounded finite-order statistic of the mixed physical event distribution are not the obstruction. A viable contradiction must exploit adjacency, exceptional-event conditioning, supporting-slope/chamber changes, tied spans, rightmost-maximizer selection, or another singular state/barrier mechanism whose sensitivity can remain order one when higher-layer event mass tends to zero.

**Boundary.** The collapse is for long stretched same-block fans, log-mass normalization, bounded observables, and fixed sampling order. It does not control bounded-dilation segments, event counts or Lebesgue density, statistics conditioned on rare higher-layer events, adjacency, extrema, or orders growing like the reciprocal higher-layer mass fraction. Higher layers have vanishing normalized mass, not vanishing structural effect.
