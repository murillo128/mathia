# MI-009 — Higher-layer mass is asymptotically invisible, but a selected support boundary pays an anchored ordinary-prime-gap cost

**Evidence level:** supported by RE-080--RE-086 under the stated late long-fan and fixed-layer hypotheses. RE-085 gives total-variation collapse of bounded fixed-order event-mass observables; RE-086 gives an exact anchored inverse-chamber law and fixed-layer asymptotic gap cost. No sparsity theorem for the resulting resonances or global Robin contradiction is proved.

RE-080--RE-082 show that the smooth common-amplitude selector law can already realize the complementary power front. RE-083--RE-084 then reconstruct the complete first-layer birth geometry from selected endpoints and show that its direct Robin timing tail is summable.

RE-085 removes regular cross-layer statistics as a leading escape. After weighting each physical CA event `(p,j)` by `log p`, the normalized higher-layer mass tends to zero on stretched same-block fans. Every bounded one-event observable and bounded fixed-order mass-polynomial statistic therefore converges to its endpoint-determined first-layer value.

RE-086 identifies a genuinely singular channel that survives this collapse. If a fixed-layer event `eta_j(p)` is actually an adjacent support boundary of an exposed threshold cell, the inverse layer chamber is prime-free **and anchored at the generating base prime `p`**. Its chamber-facing neighboring prime gap must satisfy

`G_C(p,j)/(p log p) >= (1-o(1)) a |I_C|`.

The layer index cancels at leading order: inverse-layer expansion exactly offsets power compression. Thus every fixed layer pays the same normalized ordinary-prime-gap currency for a unit of threshold-cell capacity. If several layer events are tied at one support boundary, their base primes at power-related scales must satisfy the same normalized gap lower threshold simultaneously.

This is the distinction left open by RE-085. Higher layers can be negligible in every regular mass statistic yet remain order-one relevant when **selection turns one rare event into a boundary condition**. The relevant quantity is then not event mass but a source-anchored neighboring-gap resonance at the selected prime.

The next theorem should attack that resonance class directly: bound the frequency or total threshold capacity of anchored gap/event coincidences along the rightmost-maximizer fan, or show that tied multi-scale resonances cannot carry the required long-fan geometry. If that fails, the remaining singular channels are skipped-span conditioning, tied-state admissibility beyond endpoint geometry, and discontinuous rightmost-maximizer selection.

**Boundary.** The normalized law is uniform only for fixed layer depth. It does not control `j` growing with scale, higher-layer events that affect a skipped span without becoming adjacent support boundaries, or nonlocal state changes not represented by an endpoint event. No probabilistic independence of prime gaps is assumed; tied resonances are deterministic necessary conditions, not rarity statements.
