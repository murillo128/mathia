# MI-009 — Higher-layer mass can vanish while selected boundaries remain singular, but marginal prime-gap capacity is abundant

**Evidence level:** supported by RE-080--RE-087 under the stated late long-fan and fixed-layer hypotheses. RE-085 gives total-variation collapse of bounded fixed-order event-mass observables; RE-086 gives the anchored inverse-chamber gap law; RE-087 proves that the corresponding marginal dyadic capacity diverges after the CA small-height conversion. No sparsity theorem for the actually selected resonance set or global Robin contradiction is proved.

RE-080--RE-084 show that the smooth common-amplitude selector law can realize the complementary power front and that first-layer timing is reconstructible from selected endpoints. RE-085 then removes regular cross-layer statistics as a leading escape: normalized higher-layer event mass tends to zero, so bounded fixed-order event observables become asymptotically first-layer.

RE-086 identifies how a mass-negligible higher-layer event can still matter. If a fixed-layer event `eta_j(p)` becomes an adjacent support boundary, the inverse layer chamber is prime-free and anchored at its generating base prime. The chamber-facing ordinary gap must satisfy `G_C(p,j)/(p log p) >= (1-o(1))a(C)|I_C|`. Tied boundaries require simultaneous versions at power-related prime scales.

RE-087 shows that this local price cannot be summed naively into a global scarcity theorem. Consecutive prime gaps carry `Theta(1/log P)` normalized mass in every dyadic base-prime shell, while the uniform CA small-height law gives `a(C)log P -> 0` at fixed layer. Dividing the marginal gap budget by an admissible amplitude envelope therefore yields threshold-width capacity tending to infinity.

The resulting distinction is decisive: **selection can turn a negligible event into an order-one boundary condition, but a necessary boundary inequality can still be globally weak when evaluated over all possible locations rather than the locations the dynamics actually selects.** The missing rigidity must live in the selection rule, joint resonance geometry or nonlocal adjacency structure, not in marginal first-moment prime-gap scarcity.

The next theorem should therefore constrain the rightmost-maximizer-selected subset, prove incompatibility of tied power-related gap demands, or exploit skipped-span/discontinuous state-selection structure. Replacing the selected set by all primes and summing RE-086 cannot close the channel.

**Boundary.** RE-087 is fixed-layer and deliberately ignores whether the adaptive fan can realize the abstract capacity allocation. It says nothing about growing `j`, exact ties, correlations between the two chamber-facing gaps, skipped events or endpoint compatibility. Those are the remaining places where genuine scarcity can enter.
