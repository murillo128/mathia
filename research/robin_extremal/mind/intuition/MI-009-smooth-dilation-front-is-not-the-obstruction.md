# MI-009 — The long-fan front survives smooth dilation, endpoint first-layer conservation, and linear timing tax; the obstruction must be nonlinear CA assembly

**Evidence level:** supported by RE-080--RE-083 under the stated long same-block fan normalization. RE-082 is an exact/asymptotic negative control for the common-amplitude selector law; RE-083 is an exact mixed-segment support identity plus timing estimate. Neither constructs a genuine false-RH CA fan.

RE-080 shows that a long same-block fan forces complementary power fronts in the positive transport measure. RE-081 removes the obvious discrete explanation by proving that higher-layer prime-power atoms and first-layer births are negligible at leading transport order. RE-082 then shows that the remaining smooth common-amplitude selector law itself already produces the required front: its density is asymptotically `s^(-b) log s`, with upper-endpoint-dominated mass, lower-endpoint-dominated Robin moment and crossover exponent `1-b`.

RE-083 tests whether ordinary-prime support conservation obstructs assembling that smooth law once arbitrary higher-layer events are interleaved. It does not. At every selected endpoint,

`theta(Z)-chi log r = log rad(C)`,

so unique factorization upgrades the adjusted Chebyshev identity to an exact prime-set identity. Between any two selected states, the ordinary primes crossed by the selector equal the first-layer births in the genuine CA staircase up to the two possible endpoint fringe primes. Higher-layer events cancel before this support comparison because they change `Y` and `P_C` equally.

The first-layer delay also fails as a direct linear obstruction. Its flat area is macroscopically large—on a stretched fan it is `(1/2+o(1))Y_1`—but against the exact Robin derivative kernel its whole contribution is only `O(1/Z_0)`, negligible relative to the long-fan reciprocal transport. Thus the first layer can dominate support growth while still contribute no unmatched endpoint source and only a summable direct timing tax.

The remaining information is **nonlinear assembly geometry**. First-layer and higher-layer event positions still determine chamber boundaries, exponent vectors, tied spans, supporting slopes and the order in which smooth selector arcs can be spliced. A successful contradiction must use those joint placement/order constraints or another functional that does not collapse to the endpoint support multiset plus linear timing cost.

This sharpens the proof-allocation rule. Smooth density shape is not the obstruction; lack of ordinary-prime births is not the obstruction; endpoint additive mismatch is not the obstruction; and the raw first-layer delay cannot win after Robin weighting. The useful arithmetic must live in how genuine CA states can or cannot concatenate the locally admissible pieces.

**Boundary.** RE-083 does not say first-layer event locations are irrelevant, nor does it eliminate higher layers from the assembly problem. It eliminates only endpoint additive first-layer mismatch and the direct linear delay workload as standalone contradictions.
