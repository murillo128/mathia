# MI-004 — Higher-layer CA switches carry a quantized residual charge

**Evidence level:** supported by exact adjacent-switch asymptotics in RE-060; global frequency and forcing of such switches remain open.

Inside a fixed CA support chamber, RE-059 shows that the endpoint-corrected standard and reciprocal prime sources are frozen. RE-060 shows where genuinely new source information can enter: a higher-layer boundary event.

For two adjacent tied selected states separated by one layer-`j` event, `j>=2`, generated by a prime `p` at event coordinate `eta=eta_j(p)`, the translated residual satisfies

\[
\frac{b\sqrt\eta}{\log p}
\bigl(\mathcal R_b(Z_+)-\mathcal R_b(Z_-)\bigr)
\longrightarrow
-1+j(\chi_+-\chi_-)
\in\{-j-1,-1,j-1\}.
\]

The limiting set excludes zero. The central `-1` is the higher-layer event charge. Any unmatched first-layer fringe prime at either endpoint lives at the physical scale `eta asymp p^j`, so its logarithm contributes `j` units after the same normalization. Those `j`-sized endpoint corrections cannot cancel the unit higher-layer charge.

This sharply contrasts RE-045. At a first-layer switch, ordinary-prime entry and its delayed first-layer CA event reconcile and the leading charge can disappear. RE-060 shows that this cancellation is a first-layer feature, not a generic law of CA transitions.

The reusable distinction is now concrete: a continuum of parameters inside one cell is only a reparameterization of frozen source data, while the **type of cross-cell event** can carry a discrete arithmetic observable. Higher-layer switches provide a source-sensitive signed coordinate whose value is quantized by the layer depth.

**Boundary.** RE-060 assumes an adjacent adaptive tie whose unique intervening transition is one fixed-`j` event along the established late false-RH CA blocks. It does not prove that enough such switches occur, that their signs align, or that their charges accumulate into a global contradiction.
