# MI-020 — Macroscopic hiding forces a horizontally locked reciprocal-scale zero doublet

**Evidence level:** exact finite-edge implication from [RE-131](../../findings/RE-131-macroscopic-hiding-forces-polylogarithmic-reciprocal-scale-horizontally-locked-companions.md)

On an attained finite off-critical edge, suppose a dominant packet atom has algebraic strength `M_K(U)>=cU^-A` and the full residual is hidden not just at one point but throughout a fixed relative window `|u-U|<=kappa U`. RE-131 shows that such hiding forces a distinct zero companion with, for every fixed `D>2`,

`c_*/U < |gamma_1-gamma_0| <= C_D (log U)^D/U`,

amplitude at least `c M_K(U)/log U`, and genuinely negative target-demodulated projection. The coefficient ratio then forces

`|beta_1-beta_0| << (log log U)/U`.

The obstruction is therefore two-dimensional. Macroscopic cancellation of one algebraically visible atom requires a nearby zero whose ordinate is reciprocal-scale up to polylogarithms, whose real part is locked even more tightly, and whose phase is arranged to cancel the dominant mode. A nearby ordinate alone is not enough.

The stronger spacing is paid for by the stronger observation hypothesis. RE-129 uses a sublinear window and gives a wider inverse-window scale; RE-131 uses a macroscopic window to localize Fourier frequency to `U^-1` up to polylogarithms. The exponent condition `D>2` comes from the present proof budget—one logarithm from local zero count and one from coefficient drift—and is not claimed intrinsic.

The remaining attained-edge problem is now external zero geometry: rule out infinitely many dominant near-edge atoms accompanied by doublets satisfying all three constraints simultaneously. RE-131 does not prove such doublets impossible, does not make the companion consecutive, and does not address the nonattained edge or `Theta=1` branches.
