# MI-060 — Center drift must cross the local phase threshold and then pay the macroscopic/Wiener cost

**Evidence level:** exact synthesis from [RE-229](../../findings/RE-229-bounded-complex-single-center-vanishing-arcs-have-divergent-wiener-rate.md), [RE-230](../../findings/RE-230-unbounded-complex-single-centers-force-corridor-blowup.md), [RE-231](../../findings/RE-231-large-center-widening-corridors-have-a-unit-microscopic-threshold.md), [RE-232](../../findings/RE-232-supercritical-large-centers-have-a-sharp-macroscopic-radius-and-a-six-plus-wiener-floor.md), [RE-233](../../findings/RE-233-the-large-center-transition-arc-has-a-logarithmic-over-m-depth-layer.md), and [RE-234](../../findings/RE-234-finite-ray-complex-large-centers-retain-the-six-plus-wiener-floor.md).

The remaining single-center loophole after the fixed-parameter analysis was to let the center and corridor drift with scale. RE-229 closes bounded-modulus complex drift in the relevant contracting regime, while RE-230 shows that an unbounded center inside a fixed corridor becomes too weak to cancel the raw delay on the microscopic scale.

RE-231 identifies the right dimensionless coordinate, `kappa_m=N_m/(m(a_m-1))`. If `kappa_m->kappa` with `Re(kappa)<1`, the normalized predictor converges on compact `u=m theta` windows to a residual delay. For positive-real centers, finite `kappa>=1` can flatten that compact microscopic profile. The coefficient-one boundary is therefore the local phase transition, not merely a heuristic corridor size.

RE-232 shows that crossing this local threshold is only the first gate. For positive-real large centers and finite `kappa>1`, global fixed-arc prediction has the sharp macroscopic radius `alpha_*(kappa)=arccos((1+log kappa)/kappa)`, but the same family pays Wiener rate `kappa+1+log kappa`, with normalized rate strictly above `6`. RE-233 then resolves the equality arc in a fast-center subfamily: the success/failure boundary has a `log(m)/m` detuning layer, but this finer tuning does not improve the six-plus cost.

RE-234 shows that a finite complex ray cannot evade that verdict. Write `a_m=rho_m e^(i phi_m)`, assume `rho_m->infinity`, `phi_m->phi`, and `N_m/(m rho_m)->tau in (0,infinity)`. Successful prediction on a fixed symmetric contracting arc forces `c=cos(phi)>0`, effective radial load `x=tau c>1`, and `x cos(alpha)>=1+log x`. The Wiener norm, however, still pays the full `tau`: its exponential rate is at least `tau+1+log x>=x+1+log x`. Hence the same one-variable bound gives normalized cost greater than `6`.

The key geometric meaning is that complex phase can reduce the **radial predictive component** from `tau` to `x=tau cos(phi)`, but it does not reduce the coefficient budget by the same amount. After the admissible arc is expressed through `x`, one still has `tau>=x`; any destructive phase cancellation in the DC normalizer can only make the normalized Wiener norm larger. The positive-real ray is therefore already the cheapest finite-ray large-center model.

The reusable audit has four currencies: local normal-form threshold, effective radial load, macroscopic destination region, and global coefficient/Wiener cost. A parameter drift is useful only if it changes the destination-visible normal form and the resulting predictor remains globally competitive. Solving the microscopic phase problem, tuning the equality layer, or tilting a complex center does not create a new extremal architecture when the cost remains above the known finite-center construction.

**Boundary.** RE-234 assumes a finite positive ray load `N_m/(m|a_m|)->tau`, a fixed symmetric arc and eventual contraction; it does not cover `tau->infinity`, scale-dependent arcs, multi-center/minimax constructions, rational predictors or other bases. RE-233's equality-layer criterion has its own fast-center hypothesis. These results do not improve the universal separated lower bound and do not imply RH; they close only the finite-ray single-center large-center escape.
