# Classical zeta zero-free region

## What it is

A family of proved regions excluding zeta zeros near the line Re(s)=1. The retained Mathia source describes the mechanism as converting upper bounds for zeta into a forbidden region and distinguishes the classical inverse-log boundary from the stronger Vinogradov–Korobov-scale shape.

## Relation to RH / Mathia research

Zero-free regions give unconditional partial control of zeros and support quantitative prime-number-theorem error bounds. They are a classical-method baseline for any proposed Mathia route that claims only near-one exclusion.

## Known scope and limits

These regions are strictly weaker than RH and do not locate every nontrivial zero on the critical line. Exact constants and formula-sensitive boundaries are not reconstructed from OCR-damaged passages. qwen records only verified components in an external Lean project, not a complete formalization of every historical refinement.
