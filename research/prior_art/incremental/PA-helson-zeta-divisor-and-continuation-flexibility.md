# Helson zeta divisor and continuation flexibility

## What it is

Helson zeta functions arise from completely multiplicative unimodular characters, equivalently independent phase choices on the primes. Classical and modern results show that this common prime-phase/Bohr framework supports radically different analytic behavior: Helson's random-character side is almost surely analytic and zero-free in `Re(s)>1/2`; Bochkov and Romanov construct essentially arbitrary prescribed zeros and poles in a substantial substrip; and Andersson's 2024 Mittag-Leffler theorem extends prescribed meromorphic divisors throughout `Re(s)<1` and allows arbitrary connected maximal continuation domains containing `Re(s)>1` for suitable zero-free Helson zeta functions.

## Relation to RH / Mathia research

[[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-003-ambient-prime-torus-zero-flexibility]] uses this theorem family as the decisive control against a torus-only RH mechanism. The same infinite prime character torus, coordinate labels, `log p` frequencies and generic Euler-product structure do not rigidly force either the Riemann zero set or even one continuation domain.

## Known scope and limits

These are existence/flexibility results for specially chosen multiplicative characters; they do not say that a typical character realizes arbitrary behavior and they do not determine the untwisted Riemann zeta function. Prescribed zero and pole sets remain subject to the ordinary discreteness requirements for meromorphic divisors. The Mathia-specific conclusion is the obstruction obtained by comparing these flexible twists with the distinguished untwisted character.
