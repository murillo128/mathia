# Randomized-zeta critical GMC boundary

## What it is

Saksman and Webb prove a functional limit theorem for random vertical shifts of zeta on the critical line. The shifted function converges in law to a nontrivial random generalized function that factors into a regular random smooth factor and a complex Gaussian multiplicative-chaos distribution. Their work gives a rigorous probabilistic boundary object generated by the prime-frequency structure at `Re(s)=1/2`.

## Relation to RH / Mathia research

[[research/prime_lattice/findings/PL-006-random-bohr-boundary-gmc]] identifies this as the established version of a natural renormalized Bohr-boundary idea. The finding also records the crucial regularity obstruction: the standard random boundary is distribution-valued, so treating it as an ordinary function with a pointwise zero set is not justified.

## Known scope and limits

This is a statement about random vertical shifts and randomized/statistical limits, not a deterministic analytic-continuation formula for one fixed zeta function and not a Hilbert–Pólya construction. Statistical critical-line structure does not by itself determine individual Riemann zeros.
