# Shift-clone comparison through the remaining global nonlocal-value gate

[[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-118-shift-clone-pants-are-arc-lipschitz-close|PF-118]]--[[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-155-local-heat-invariant-tower-is-area-only|PF-155]] advance the exact prime/all-composite shift-clone comparison from local geometric closeness to compact relative resolvent, sharp local `L^1` interface currencies and a squared-resolvent trace-class route. They also show that changing resolvent power, `det_2`, residue or local heat data does not create an independent source-specific value.

A relative orbit route now crosses the absolute obstruction at distribution level. [[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-156-matched-short-orbit-packets-cancel-relative-trace-divergence|PF-156]] uses the canonical marked pairing to show that full repeated short-orbit Selberg packet differences are absolutely summable against admissible test functions, including heat tests. This is a genuine relative extension beyond the nowhere-locally-finite absolute orbital measure, though not yet a full relative trace formula.

The corresponding local value is nevertheless strongly constrained. [[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-157-matched-source-short-selberg-cocycle-is-zero-free|PF-157]] gives a holomorphic zero-free matched short-core factor on `Re s>0`. [[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-158-full-canonical-separator-selberg-cocycle-has-sharp-one-quarter-abscissa|PF-158]] extends the conclusion to every canonical consecutive-block separator, with a sharp natural convergence boundary `Re s>1/4`; the entire critical line lies inside that zero-free half-plane.

The territory therefore still needs both a global comparison and a genuinely nonlocal value theorem, but the source of that value cannot be the matched short-core or canonical-separator Selberg factors themselves. Primitive word classes outside the canonical separator family, full scattering/spectral-shift data, resonances or another marked inverse invariant remain outside these closures.
