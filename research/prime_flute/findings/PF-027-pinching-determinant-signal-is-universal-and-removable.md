# PF-027 — pinching determinant signal is universal and canonically removable

**Status:** NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION, with an exact prime-flute specialization of standard finite-volume degeneration theory.

## Claim

The very large response of a spectral determinant or Selberg zeta function to the prime-flute short separating geodesics is real, but it is the universal hyperbolic pinching singularity. After removing the canonical local pinching factors, the remaining finite-volume spectral data converge to those of the pinched limit surface. Therefore raw determinant/zeta blow-up from isolated prime clusters is not a prime-specific spectral mechanism.

This finding concerns finite-volume approximants or finite prime-islands to which the standard degeneration theorems apply. It does **not** assert that a global determinant already exists for the full infinite prime-flute; PF-020 is precisely an obstruction to the ordinary global Selberg product.

## 1. Exact prime-flute pinching variable

PF-004 gives, for the separating class determined by four ordered endpoints `a < b < c < d`, the exact cross-ratio

```text
chi = ((c-b)(d-a))/((b-a)(d-c)) > 0
```

and exact translation length

```text
L = 4 asinh(sqrt(chi)).
```

Hence a prime-gap configuration with `chi -> 0` gives a genuine hyperbolic pinching degeneration `L -> 0`. PF-005 and PF-007 supply arithmetic mechanisms producing such sequences.

For small `chi`,

```text
L = 4 sqrt(chi) + O(chi^(3/2)).
```

Equivalently, when the endpoint intervals are

```text
X = b-a,
Y = c-b,
Z = d-c,
```

PF-004 gives

```text
chi = Y(X+Y+Z)/(XZ).
```

Thus the pinching variable is an intrinsic multi-gap cross-ratio, not an arbitrarily chosen prime statistic.

## 2. Standard determinant degeneration theorem

Lundelius' finite-volume degeneration theorem states that if a finite-volume hyperbolic surface degenerates by pinching simple closed geodesics of lengths `L_j -> 0`, then the relative hyperbolic determinant has leading asymptotic

```text
-log |det Delta_hyp(C_tau, C_0)|
    = sum_j pi^2/(3 L_j)
      + O(-log L_min)
      + O(1).
```

References:

- R. Lundelius, *Asymptotics of the determinant of the Laplacian on hyperbolic surfaces of finite volume*, Duke Math. J. 71 (1993), 211–242, DOI `10.1215/S0012-7094-93-07109-8`.
- J. Jorgenson and R. Lundelius, *Convergence theorems for relative spectral functions on hyperbolic Riemann surfaces of finite volume*, Duke Math. J. 80 (1995), 785–819.

Substituting the exact prime-flute identity gives, for each pinching PF-004 class,

```text
pi^2/(3 L)
  = pi^2 / (12 asinh(sqrt(chi)))
  = pi^2/(12 sqrt(chi)) + O(sqrt(chi)).
```

Therefore an extreme prime-gap configuration can indeed produce an exponentially small determinant, with

```text
log |det| = -pi^2/(12 sqrt(chi)) + lower-order terms
```

for a single pinching channel, modulo the standard relative-determinant normalization and the logarithmic error in the degeneration theorem.

This looks at first like a strong spectral amplification of the prime gaps. It is not a new mechanism: the coefficient and singular dependence are the universal collar/pinching contribution once the geodesic length is known.

## 3. Selberg zeta makes the removal explicit

For a finite-volume degenerating family, let the local Selberg factor of the pinching classes be

```text
Z_pinch(s)
  = product_{gamma pinching}
      product_{k >= 0}
        (1 - exp(-(s+k) L_gamma)).
```

The degeneration results of Wolpert, Jorgenson–Lundelius and subsequent refinements show that after dividing by these local factors the remaining Selberg zeta converges to the Selberg zeta of the finite-volume pinched limit. A convenient modern statement is that, in dimensions two and three, the normalized zeta converges on compact subsets of the half-plane to the right of the critical line; in dimension two,

```text
Z_{M_j}(s) / Z_pinch,j(s) -> Z_{M_infinity}(s)
```

for `Re(s) > 1/2` under the finite-volume degeneration hypotheses.

Reference:

- M. Avdispahić, J. Jorgenson, L. Smajlović et al., *Asymptotic Behavior of the Selberg Zeta Functions for Degenerating Families of Hyperbolic Manifolds*, Commun. Math. Phys. 310 (2012), 217–236, DOI `10.1007/s00220-011-1408-5`, together with the earlier two-dimensional Jorgenson–Lundelius/Wolpert results cited there.

Thus the entire singular contribution generated by `chi -> 0` is canonically identifiable as the standard local pinching factor.

## 4. Decisive negative consequence

Reject the branch

```text
extreme prime gaps
  -> short PF-004 geodesic
  -> determinant collapse / Selberg-zeta singularity
  -> new prime-specific spectral law.
```

The first two arrows are genuine and exact. The third is also genuine. The final interpretation is false: the determinant/zeta singularity is the same universal one produced by pinching any simple closed geodesic on a finite-volume hyperbolic surface.

In particular, neither

```text
-log det ~ const/L
```

nor, after substituting the prime cross-ratio,

```text
-log det ~ pi^2/(12 sqrt(chi))
```

should be advertised as evidence of a new Riemann/prime spectral mechanism. It is a known degeneration law written in the prime-flute coordinate.

## 5. What survives after renormalization

What is not removed by the pinching factor is the spectral data of the limiting punctured surface `S_H` associated with the finite prime pattern. This is the same object that appears in PF-025/right-limit analysis.

Therefore a potentially prime-specific determinant question must be of the form

```text
H -> S_H -> renormalized determinant / Z_{S_H}(s)
```

and must compare two or more surfaces of the **same topological type**. The singular dependence on the neck lengths has to be removed first.

The determinant on the moduli space of punctured surfaces is known to depend nontrivially on the moduli (Takhtajan–Zograf and related work), so such a comparison is not vacuous. But without a theorem or computation distinguishing specific recurrent prime patterns, this is only a remaining test, not a positive finding.

## 6. Why recent large-volume determinant results do not rescue a global determinant

Recent work on normalized spectral determinants for sequences of compact hyperbolic surfaces of growing volume assumes, among other conditions, uniform discreteness and non-accumulation of bounded geodesics. The prime-flute violates exactly these hypotheses because PF-005/PF-020 give primitive lengths accumulating at zero and infinitely many primitive geodesics below every positive length threshold.

Thus those large-volume determinant asymptotics do not provide a canonical global determinant for the prime-flute.

Reference:

- J. Jorgenson, L. Smajlović, P. Spilioti, *Asymptotics of the spectral determinant of the weighted Laplacian for a sequence of compact Riemann surfaces of infinitely growing volume*, arXiv:2607.26779 (2026).

## 7. Interior/exterior duality

Nothing here changes PF-017. The inversion exchanging the interior and exterior copies preserves the exact orthogonal-circle configuration but is not an internal isometry of the prime-flute quotient. Applying the same finite-volume pinching analysis to the exterior copy simply reproduces the same local degeneration law; it does not create a second determinant channel.

## Research consequence

The natural determinant branch is now sharply constrained:

```text
raw pinching determinant/zeta        -> rejected as universal;
ordinary global Selberg determinant -> obstructed by PF-020;
renormalized finite-island data      -> still meaningful, but must distinguish moduli at fixed topology.
```

The next legitimate determinant test is therefore not to look for divergence at `chi -> 0`, but to compare the **finite renormalized remainder** for distinct recurrent prime patterns after all universal pinching factors are removed.
