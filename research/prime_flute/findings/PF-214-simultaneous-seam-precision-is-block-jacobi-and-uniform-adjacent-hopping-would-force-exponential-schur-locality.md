# PF-214 — simultaneous seam precision is block Jacobi and uniform adjacent hopping would force exponential Schur locality

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + TOPOLOGICAL-DOMAIN-DECOMPOSITION + CONDITIONAL-DECAY + NEGATIVE/BOUNDARY`. PF-213 reduces the smooth decomposition-cuff route to quantitative off-diagonal locality of the simultaneous energy-normalized Schur factor

\[
D=\Lambda_Q^{1/2}(\Lambda_Q+\Lambda_E)^{-1}\Lambda_Q^{1/2}.
\]

One part of that gate is in fact forced by the exact zero-twist flute topology. If all PF-205 natural-width seam strips are cut simultaneously, the strip side `Q` is a disjoint union of two-sided cuff strips, while the complement `E` is a chain of one-cusp pant cores. Consequently `\Lambda_Q` is module diagonal and `\Lambda_E` is nearest-neighbor in the physical cuff-module decomposition. After interior-energy normalization,

\[
K:=\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}\Lambda_E\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}\ge0,
\qquad
D=(I+K)^{-1},
\]

and `K` is an exact block-Jacobi precision: `Q_mKQ_n=0` for `|m-n|>1` at form level. Thus the actual PF problem does **not** require discovering an arbitrary long-range precision operator; long-range mixing can arise only by inverting this local one-dimensional boundary precision.

There is also an exact sufficient local gate. If the normalized nearest-neighbor blocks extend boundedly and

\[
J:=\sup_n\|Q_{n+1}KQ_n\|<\infty,
\]

then a direct Combes--Thomas conjugation gives exponential module decay

\[
\|Q_mDQ_n\|\le 2e^{-\alpha|m-n|},
\qquad
\alpha=\log\!\left(1+\frac1{4J}\right)
\]

for `J>0` (and `D` is block diagonal when `J=0`). This exponential envelope satisfies PF-213's square-root summability condition and therefore closes the smooth-interface reassembly gate. Importantly, no uniform upper bound on the diagonal blocks of `K` is needed for this implication.

Bandedness and positivity alone are nevertheless insufficient. The scalar nearest-neighbor family `K_t=t(2I-S-S^*)`, `t>0`, has

\[
(I+K_t)^{-1}_{0k}
=\frac{r_t^{|k|}}{\sqrt{1+4t}},
\qquad
r_t=\frac{1+2t-\sqrt{1+4t}}{2t},
\]

and taking `t=k^2` gives a lower bound `c/k` for the distance-`k` matrix element. Hence no uniform exponentially decaying envelope follows from `K\ge0` plus nearest-neighbor support alone, and the resulting universal envelope does not satisfy PF-213's required summability. The remaining PF question is therefore sharply local and quantitative: control the **adjacent normalized pant-to-strip hopping**, or prove another weighted inverse-decay estimate exploiting its actual scale. The separate PF-183 true-short-collar splice remains open.

## Claim

Use the canonical distinguished decomposition cuffs `C_n` and PF-205 natural-width signed Fermi strips

\[
Q_n=\{|x|<w_n\},
\qquad
w_n\asymp d_n,
\]

chosen pairwise disjoint after a finite head. Let

\[
Q=\bigsqcup_n Q_n,
\qquad
E=X\setminus\overline Q,
\]

with the harmless convention that finite-head pieces are absorbed into one finite block. Each `Q_n` has two boundary circles, denoted `\Sigma_n^-` and `\Sigma_n^+`, and let the module boundary-energy space be

\[
\mathcal B_n=\mathcal H(\Sigma_n^-)\oplus\mathcal H(\Sigma_n^+),
\qquad
\mathcal B=\bigoplus_n\mathcal B_n.
\]

At the positive shifted operator `L=\Delta+1`, let `\Lambda_Q` and `\Lambda_E` be the positive Dirichlet-to-Neumann forms on the two sides of the simultaneous cut, in the PF-211 boundary-energy rigging. Then:

1. `\Lambda_Q=\bigoplus_n\Lambda_{Q_n}` is block diagonal on `\mathcal B`.
2. The complement `E` is the disjoint union of one-cusp pant cores `E_n` between consecutive seam strips. Each `E_n` meets only `\Sigma_n^+` and `\Sigma_{n+1}^-`. Therefore
   \[
   \Lambda_E=\bigoplus_n\Lambda_{E_n}
   \]
   before regrouping by cuff modules, and after regrouping
   \[
   \boxed{Q_m\Lambda_EQ_n=0\quad\text{for }|m-n|>1}
   \tag{1}
   \]
   in the quadratic-form sense.
3. Since `\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}` is itself module diagonal, the normalized exterior precision
   \[
   K=\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}\Lambda_E\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}
   \tag{2}
   \]

   \[
   \boxed{Q_mKQ_n=0\quad\text{for }|m-n|>1.}
   \tag{3}
   \]
4. The PF-211 normalized Schur factor is exactly
   \[
   \boxed{D=(I+K)^{-1}.}
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Thus every nonlocal block of `D` is generated by inversion of a local block-Jacobi precision, not by a nonlocal exterior coupling inserted at the form level.

The same statements apply separately to the source and target metrics used in PF-211--PF-213.

Now assume, in addition, that the neighboring form blocks in (3) extend to bounded operators on the normalized module spaces and satisfy

\[
\boxed{J:=\sup_n\|Q_{n+1}KQ_n\|<\infty.}
\tag{5}
\]

Then for every `\alpha>0` satisfying

\[
2J(e^\alpha-1)<1,
\tag{6}

one has

\[
\boxed{

## 1. Why the simultaneous exterior precision is exactly nearest-neighbor

PF-182 records the geometric fact needed for the support statement: every distinguished cuff is shared by exactly two adjacent one-cusp pants, and the zero-twist flute is assembled as their chain. PF-205 replaces each shared cuff by a two-sided strip contained inside its embedded collar. Removing all those mutually disjoint strips separates the flute along every chain interface.

The remaining component between `Q_n` and `Q_{n+1}` is precisely the interior of the corresponding one-cusp pant with thin collar pieces removed at its two finite cuffs. Its cusp remains part of the same component but creates no connection to any other module. Hence a Dirichlet datum on `E_n` has only two finite interface slots: the right side of module `n` and the left side of module `n+1`.

At `\Delta+1`, the Dirichlet energy on each such component is coercive. If `\lambda_{E_n}` denotes its local DtN quadratic form, the simultaneous exterior form is simply

\[
\lambda_E(\varphi)
=
\sum_n
\lambda_{E_n}
\bigl(\varphi_n^+,\varphi_{n+1}^-\bigr),
\tag{11}
\]

on the natural finite-energy form domain. Equation (11) proves (1) directly: no summand contains boundary variables whose module indices differ by more than one. This is a support identity, not a decay estimate and not an approximation.

Likewise the interior side is the disjoint union of the strips `Q_n`, so

\[
\lambda_Q(\varphi)
=
\sum_n\lambda_{Q_n}(\varphi_n^-,\varphi_n^+).
\tag{12}
\]

Functional calculus of the block-diagonal positive form `\Lambda_Q` preserves each `\mathcal B_n`. Conjugating (11) by `\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}` therefore cannot create longer-range blocks, proving (3). Finally the algebraic form identity

\[
\Lambda_Q+\Lambda_E
=
\Lambda_Q^{1/2}(I+K)\Lambda_Q^{1/2}
\tag{13}
\]

gives (4).

This isolates where globality actually enters: **only the inverse in (4)**. The exterior DtN form itself is local in module index because the physical complement has been disconnected into pant cores by the simultaneous cut.

## 2. A bounded neighboring block gives exponential inverse locality

The required decay estimate has a short self-contained proof that does not need a uniform bound on the diagonal of `K`.

Write the block-Jacobi form schematically as

\[
K=K_{\rm diag}+B+B^*,
\tag{14}
\]

where `B` is the one-step block shift with coefficient

\[
B_n=Q_{n+1}KQ_n,
\qquad
\|B\|=\sup_n\|B_n\|\le J.
\tag{15}
\]

Fix two modules `n,m`, say `m>n`. For a large truncation radius choose a bounded positive diagonal weight `W` which equals `e^{\alpha j}` between the relevant modules, is constant outside a large finite window, and satisfies

\[
|\log W_{j+1}-\log W_j|\le\alpha.
\tag{16}
\]

The diagonal part of `K` commutes with `W`. Only the one-step terms change, and (15)--(16) give

\[
\boxed{
\|WKW^{-1}-K\|
\le2J(e^\alpha-1)=:\delta_\alpha.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Because `K\ge0`,

\[
\|(I+K)^{-1}\|\le1.
\tag{18}
\]

If `\delta_\alpha<1`, then

\[
W(I+K)W^{-1}
=(I+K)+E_\alpha,
\qquad
\|E_\alpha\|\le\delta_\alpha,
\tag{19}
\]

and the resolvent identity/Neumann bound gives

\[
\left\|
\bigl(W(I+K)W^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}
\right\|
\le\frac1{1-\delta_\alpha}.
\tag{20}
\]

On the `(m,n)` block the weight contributes exactly `e^{\alpha(m-n)}` before the outer truncation is sent away. Hence

\[
e^{\alpha(m-n)}\|Q_m(I+K)^{-1}Q_n\|
\le\frac1{1-\delta_\alpha},
\tag{21}
\]

which is (7). Reversing the weight handles `m<n`.

This is the block-Jacobi version of the standard Combes--Thomas/exponential-weight argument. The useful feature for PF is its hypothesis: it asks only for bounded **adjacent normalized hopping**. Large or unbounded positive diagonal exterior energies are not themselves an obstruction.

## 3. Nearest-neighbor support alone does not give a uniform decay rate

PF-213's noncompactness model used an arbitrary positive projection and therefore did not distinguish a genuinely local precision from unrestricted mixing. The following scalar test shows why (3) is progress but not yet the desired endpoint theorem.

On `\ell^2(\mathbb Z)` let `S` be the bilateral shift and

\[
K_t=t(2I-S-S^*),
\qquad t>0.
\tag{22}
\]

Then `K_t\ge0` and is strictly nearest-neighbor. Fourier inversion gives the standard Green kernel

\[
\boxed{
\langle e_k,(I+K_t)^{-1}e_0\rangle
=
\frac{r_t^{|k|}}{\sqrt{1+4t}},
\qquad
r_t=
\frac{1+2t-\sqrt{1+4t}}{2t}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

For integer `k\ge2`, take `t=k^2`. Rationalizing the numerator gives

\[
1-r_{k^2}
=
\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+4k^2}+1}
\le\frac1k.
\tag{24}
\]

Therefore

\[
r_{k^2}^{k}
\ge(1-1/k)^k
\ge\frac14,
\tag{25}
\]

while

\[
\sqrt{1+4k^2}\le3k.
\tag{26}
\]

Combining (23)--(26),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t>0}
\left|
\langle e_k,(I+K_t)^{-1}e_0\rangle
\right|
\ge\frac1{12k}.
}
\tag{27}
\]

Thus any envelope valid uniformly over positive nearest-neighbor precisions must allow at least `c/k` along this family. Such an envelope cannot satisfy PF-213's condition, since

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}
\sqrt{k\cdot(c/k)}=\infty.
\tag{28}
\]

The obstruction is quantitative: the hopping norm of `K_t` is `t`, so its correlation length grows with `t`. This example does **not** claim that the PF pant precision has this behavior. It prevents the topology statement (3) from being silently upgraded to the decay statement needed by PF-213.

## 4. The remaining PF gate is local energy comparison, not global topology

Equations (3)--(10) replace PF-213's broad instruction “prove locality of the simultaneous Schur complement” by a more concrete hierarchy.

The strongest simple target is

\[
\boxed{
\sup_n
\|Q_{n+1}
\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}\Lambda_E\Lambda_Q^{-1/2}
Q_n\|<\infty.
}
\tag{29}
\]

Because only one pant core contributes to each neighboring pair, (29) is a **one-pant estimate repeated uniformly along the tail**. It can be attacked by comparing the local pant-core DtN cross form with the two adjacent PF-205 strip energies. No global inversion or infinite weak-ideal sum is needed until after that local estimate has been proved.

There is an important low-mode warning. On a `2w_n`-thin PF strip, boundary data which are nearly equal on the two sides have interior positive-shift energy on the small `w_nL_n` scale, whereas one-sided or antisymmetric data can live on the much larger `L_n/w_n` scale. Consequently one cannot infer (29) merely from a generic trace theorem or from the fact that every local pant DtN form is positive. The comparison must respect the actual two-sided module energy and its low/high splitting.

A failure of (29) would not by itself kill the smooth route. One could still prove a scale-dependent hopping estimate and feed it into a weighted Combes--Thomas argument, or bypass blockwise decay with another weighted ideal factorization that reproduces PF-213's offset counts. What PF-214 removes is the need to search for an unexplained source of far-off-diagonal precision: the only remaining source of long-range `D` is propagation through a nearest-neighbor chain whose local coefficients can now be studied explicitly.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The underlying operator facts are classical. Nonoverlapping domain decomposition and Steklov--Poincare/Schur-complement formulations assemble an interface operator from local subdomain contributions; see, for example, Andrea Toselli and Olof Widlund, *Domain Decomposition Methods — Algorithms and Theory*, Springer Series in Computational Mathematics 34 (2005), DOI `10.1007/b137868`. Exponential off-diagonal decay of inverses and analytic functions of banded positive operators goes back at least to Demko--Moss--Smith and has broad matrix/block extensions; PF-213 already records the 1984 Demko--Moss--Smith source. Michele Benzi and Paola Boito, *Decay properties for functions of matrices over C*-algebras*, Linear Algebra and its Applications **456** (2014), 174--198, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2013.11.027`, is particularly close in spirit to operator-valued/block entries.

No novelty is claimed for domain decomposition, DtN additivity, block-tridiagonal Schur complements, the exponential-weight/Combes--Thomas argument, or the discrete-Laplacian Green kernel. Equations (17)--(21) are included so that the exact hypothesis used here is auditable without importing a finite-dimensional condition-number theorem.

The project-specific deduction is narrower: **the canonical PF-205 simultaneous seam cut makes the actual normalized exterior precision nearest-neighbor in physical cuff-module index, and therefore a uniform bound on one adjacent normalized pant block is already sufficient to trigger PF-213's complete weak-endpoint reassembly.** The scalar family (22)--(28) simultaneously records why nearest-neighbor topology by itself is not enough.

## 6. Falsification and boundary checks

A later adversary can test PF-214 at the following points.

1. The simultaneous cut must use the actual distinguished decomposition cuffs and the two-sided PF-205 strips. If extra interfaces are introduced inside pant cores, the module adjacency statement must be recomputed rather than assumed.
2. Module `n` means the **pair** of boundary circles of `Q_n`. Regrouping by some diagonalized boundary basis is not allowed when asserting (3).
3. The infinite exterior DtN form must be the positive-shift finite-energy direct sum of the disconnected pant-core forms. A hidden global boundary condition at the infinite end could change the operator domain and must be justified if introduced.
4. Equation (5) is not a consequence of (3), positivity, or contractivity. The discrete-Laplacian family (22)--(28) is an explicit falsifier of that shortcut.
5. Any proof of (5) must use the normalized `\Lambda_Q` energy, not an unnormalized `L^2` boundary norm. The thin-strip symmetric sector can have a very different scale from the antisymmetric/high sector.
6. The exponential estimate concerns `D=(I+K)^{-1}` at the fixed positive spectral point used by PF-211. Threshold or on-spectrum scattering parameters need separate analysis.
7. Finite-head irregularities only add a finite block and do not affect PF-213's tail summability, but they cannot be used to hide a tail loss in (5).
8. Nothing here closes PF-204's unsmoothed cotangent-transport endpoint, the PF-183 true-short-collar splice, wave operators, determinants/resonances, prime/clone separation, or any RH implication.

## Consequences for the research line

PF-214 now removes one layer of global ambiguity: **the precision underneath that Schur factor is already an exact nearest-neighbor block-Jacobi object because of the flute's pant-chain topology.**

The next smooth-route calculation should therefore be local. Estimate the normalized DtN cross block of one pant core against the two adjacent PF-205 strip energies, uniformly in the exact prime-flute tail parameters. A uniform bound proves exponential `D` locality by (7)--(10) and closes the decomposition-interface reassembly gate. If the bound grows, determine its exact arithmetic/geometric scale and test whether a weighted inverse-decay argument still meets PF-213.
