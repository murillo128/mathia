## 1. PF-235's nonlinear-looking footpoint map is an exact additive flow

The compactification (1) satisfies

\[
\sinh\tau=-\cot\theta.
\tag{19}
\]

Equation (2) therefore gives

\[
\sinh T_\rho(\tau(\theta))
=e^{-c}\sinh\tau(\theta)
=-e^{-c}\cot\theta.
\tag{20}
\]

If `F_c` is defined by (4), then `\sinh\tau(F_c)=-\cot F_c`, proving (5). Formula (6) follows by differentiation.

Even before compactification, the maps form an exact group:

\[
\sinh T_c(T_d(\tau))
=e^{-c}\sinh T_d(\tau)
=e^{-(c+d)}\sinh\tau,
\tag{21}
\]

hence `T_c\circ T_d=T_{c+d}` and likewise for `F_c`. Differentiating (5) at `c=0` gives (7). Thus PF-235's `O(w^2)` coordinate defect is not an arbitrary near-identity diffeomorphism; it is one point on a fixed one-parameter flow with time `c=\frac12\log(1+w^2)`.

## 2. The Hilbert-space transport is a half-density flow with a parity-local generator

Equation (8) is unitary because the factor `F_c'^{1/2}` is exactly the square root of the change-of-variable Jacobian. The group law for `F_c` gives the group law for `V_c`.

The stronger nearest-neighbor statement (11) follows directly from the classical Gegenbauer recurrences. If

\[
\phi_n(\theta)=\sin^\alpha\theta\,C_n^{(\alpha)}(\cos\theta),
\tag{23}
\]

then, with `x=\cos\theta`,

\[

=\sin^\alpha\theta
\left[-x(1-x^2)\frac d{dx}

Combining the standard derivative/three-term identities gives cancellation of the diagonal term and

\[

After the PF-247 normalization this is exactly (11)--(12). In particular `\gamma_n\sim n/4`: the infinitesimal transport is a parity Jacobi-type first-order flow, not an arbitrary dense spectral rotation.

## 3. The commutator with the PF-247 transverse operator is only first-order relative to `A`

For a general first-order half-density generator `v\partial_\theta+v'/2`, direct differentiation gives

\[
[-\partial_\theta^2,G]
=-2v'\partial_\theta^2
-2v''\partial_\theta

\tag{26}

Here

\[
v=\sin\theta\cos\theta,
\qquad
v'=\cos2\theta,
\qquad
v''=-4\sin\theta\cos\theta,
\qquad
v'''=-4\cos2\theta.
\tag{27}

For the singular potential `W=\csc^2\theta`,

\[
[W,G]=-vW'.
\tag{28}

The apparently singular zeroth-order terms cancel:

\[
-2v'W-vW'-\frac12v'''=4\cos^2\theta.
\tag{29}
\]

Substituting `-\partial_\theta^2=A-W` proves (13).

PF-247 gives `A\ge\alpha^2`. For `f\in\operatorname{Dom}A`,

\[
\|f\|\le\alpha^{-2}\|Af\|,
\qquad
\|f'\|^2
\le\langle f,Af\rangle
\le\alpha^{-2}\|Af\|^2.

## 8. Falsification and boundary checks

1. **Not the full seam square root.** Equation (17) controls the PF-235 coordinate/density transport, not `q_w^{\rm hyp,+}(M)^{1/2}`. PF-245's analytic-strip obstruction remains untouched.
2. **Not the full polar factor.** `U_w` in PF-254 also contains orientation coming from the fixed-axis seam relative to the corridor operator. PF-255 removes only the width-dependent hypercycle transport from the list of possible catastrophic components.
3. **`A` modes, not yet `K_a` modes.** The two-derivative bound is exact for `K_0=A^{1/2}`. PF-233's form comparison with `T_a` is insufficient by itself to transfer spectral off-diagonal locality.
4. **Complete-lift statement only.** Nothing here performs the finite one-cusp-pant completion or the physical `P/H` projection/reassembly.
5. **No contradiction with PF-244.** PF-244 permits arbitrary nonlocal positive seams. `V_c` is a very special smooth geometric flow and therefore has much stronger spectral locality than the abstract countermodel.
6. **No contradiction with PF-245.** A coordinate pullback can be Sobolev-local even though the intrinsic seam square-root multiplier has a branch point at distance approaching `1`.
7. **No RH claim.** The result is a local operator-transport theorem. It neither produces a relative determinant/scattering invariant nor proves any statement about zeta zeros.
