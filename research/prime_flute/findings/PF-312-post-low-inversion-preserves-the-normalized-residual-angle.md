# PF-312 — post-low inversion preserves the normalized residual angle

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-CANONICAL-CORRELATION-DUALITY + POSITIVE/ROUTE-NARROWING`.

[[research/prime_flute/findings/PF-311-low-band-screening-bounds-the-first-residual-high-conversion|PF-311]] leaves the post-low constant/high block

\[
\mathcal B=
\begin{pmatrix}
S_L&E_{LH}\\
E_{LH}^*&R_H
\end{pmatrix}>0,
\qquad
T_H=S_L^{-1/2}E_{LH}R_H^{-1/2},
\]

and points out that the mixed inverse contains the apparently dangerous factor

\[
(I-T_HT_H^*)^{-1}T_H.
\]

That factor really can blow up as a singular value of `T_H` approaches one. However, the blow-up has a sharper exact structure: **block inversion does not create a new or larger normalized constant/high angle.** Once the inverse is normalized by its own diagonal Green blocks, its cross operator has exactly the same singular values as `T_H` (with only a sign and blockwise unitary changes of coordinates).

In the energy coordinates of PF-311 the statement is even exact without unitary ambiguity. Put

\[
D_C=(I-T_HT_H^*)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_H=(I-T_H^*T_H)^{1/2}.
\]

Then

\[
Q:=
\begin{pmatrix}
I&T_H\\
T_H^*&I
\end{pmatrix}>0
\]

has inverse blocks

\[
G_C=D_C^{-2},
\qquad
G_H=D_H^{-2},
\qquad
G_{CH}=-D_C^{-2}T_H=-T_HD_H^{-2}.
\]

The normalized mixed Green operator therefore satisfies

\[
\boxed{
G_C^{-1/2}G_{CH}G_H^{-1/2}=-T_H.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the raw singular amplification

\[
\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma^2}
\]

on a singular direction with `T_H`-singular value `\sigma` is exactly accompanied by diagonal Green amplification

\[
\frac1{1-\sigma^2}
\]

on both sides. After whitening by those diagonal Green energies, the remaining cross correlation is again just `\sigma`.

This changes the interpretation of the PF-311 frontier. The remaining source/reassembly problem is **not** to control a second angle generated by inversion. The angle is already `T_H`. What remains is to determine whether the actual physical source and target reassembly couple strongly to the diagonal Green strengths carried by the near-unit `T_H` directions. Equivalently, the mixed response factors exactly into

\[
\boxed{
\text{left diagonal Green strength}
\times
\text{the same post-low angle}
\times
\text{right diagonal Green strength}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

That is precisely the currency targeted by the accepted source-weighted-return clue: a return-potential estimate can now be interpreted as controlling a diagonal Green/source factor rather than having to defeat an additional mixed inverse singularity.

## Claim

Work in any finite canonical PF-205 section after the retained physical-low variables have been shorted as in PF-311. Let

\[
\mathcal B=
\begin{pmatrix}
S&E\\
E^*&R
\end{pmatrix}>0
\tag{3}
\]

on `\mathcal C\oplus\mathcal H`, where in the prime-flute application

\[
S=S_L,
\qquad E=E_{LH},
\qquad R=R_H.
\]

Define

\[
T:=S^{-1/2}ER^{-1/2},
\qquad
D_C:=(I-TT^*)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_H:=(I-T^*T)^{1/2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then `\|T\|<1`, so both defect operators are strictly positive. Let

\[
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
I&T\\
T^*&I
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal B=(S^{1/2}\oplus R^{1/2})Q(S^{1/2}\oplus R^{1/2}).
\tag{5}
\]

Write

\[
Q^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
G_C&G_{CH}\\
G_{HC}&G_H
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then:

1. the diagonal and mixed Green blocks are
   \[
   \boxed{
   G_C=D_C^{-2},
   \qquad
   G_H=D_H^{-2},
   \qquad
   G_{CH}=-D_C^{-2}T=-TD_H^{-2};
   }
   \tag{7}
   \]
2. the inverse-normalized cross operator is exactly
   \[
   \boxed{
   G_C^{-1/2}G_{CH}G_H^{-1/2}=-T;
   }
   \tag{8}
   \]
3. hence the complete singular-value list of the normalized mixed inverse equals that of the original post-low angle:
   \[
   \boxed{
   s_j\!\left(G_C^{-1/2}G_{CH}G_H^{-1/2}\right)=s_j(T)
   }
   \tag{9}
   \]
   for every `j`;
4. if `\mathcal G=\mathcal B^{-1}` and `\mathcal G_C,\mathcal G_{CH},\mathcal G_H` are its blocks in the original constant/high coordinates, then there are unitaries `U_C,U_H` on the finite section such that
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal G_C^{-1/2}\mathcal G_{CH}\mathcal G_H^{-1/2}
   =-U_C^*TU_H;
   }
   \tag{10}
   \]
   and therefore the same singular-value identity (9) holds for the actual diagonal-normalized Green blocks;
5. for arbitrary external source/reassembly maps `J_C:X\to\mathcal C` and `J_H:Y\to\mathcal H`, the mixed response has the exact strength-angle-strength factorization
   \[
   \boxed{
   J_C^*\mathcal G_{CH}J_H
   =
   (\mathcal G_C^{1/2}J_C)^*\,
   \Gamma_G\,
   (\mathcal G_H^{1/2}J_H),
   }
   \tag{11}
   \]
   where
   \[
   \Gamma_G:=\mathcal G_C^{-1/2}\mathcal G_{CH}\mathcal G_H^{-1/2}
   \]
   is a contraction with exactly the singular values of `T`.

Consequently no estimate on the raw mixed inverse can be interpreted correctly without separating the two diagonal Green-strength factors from the unchanged normalized angle.

## 1. The defect identity removes the apparent extra inverse angle

From `\|T\|<1`, the standard block inverse gives

\[
Q^{-1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(I-TT^*)^{-1}
&-(I-TT^*)^{-1}T\\
-(I-T^*T)^{-1}T^*
&(I-T^*T)^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]

This is (7). The load-bearing relation is the contraction defect intertwining identity

\[
\boxed{
D_CT=TD_H.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed

\[
(I-TT^*)T=T(I-T^*T),
\]

and continuous functional calculus gives (13). Since the finite section has `\|T\|<1`, both defect operators are invertible and

\[
D_C^{-1}TD_H=T.
\tag{14}
\]

Using (7),

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_C^{-1/2}G_{CH}G_H^{-1/2}
&=D_C\,(-D_C^{-2}T)\,D_H\\
&=-D_C^{-1}TD_H\\
&=-T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

This proves (8)--(9). The factor `(I-TT^*)^{-1}` in the raw mixed inverse is therefore not a second conversion angle. It is the same defect denominator already present in the diagonal Green blocks.

On a singular pair `Tv=\sigma u`, `T^*u=\sigma v`, equations (7) reduce to

\[
G_Cu=\frac1{1-\sigma^2}u,
\qquad
G_Hv=\frac1{1-\sigma^2}v,
\qquad
G_{CH}v=-\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma^2}u.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus the raw mixed response diverges exactly as the geometric mean of the two diagonal Green responses times the unchanged normalized correlation `\sigma`.

## 2. Returning to the physical constant/high coordinates changes only the singular vectors

Equation (5) gives

\[
\mathcal G:=\mathcal B^{-1}
=(S^{-1/2}\oplus R^{-1/2})Q^{-1}(S^{-1/2}\oplus R^{-1/2}).
\tag{17}
\]

Define

\[
X:=D_C^{-1}S^{-1/2},
\qquad
Y:=D_H^{-1}R^{-1/2}.
\tag{18}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal G_C=X^*X,
\qquad
\mathcal G_H=Y^*Y,
\tag{19}
\]

while (13) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_{CH}=X^*(-T)Y.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Because the finite section is strictly positive, `X` and `Y` are invertible. Their polar decompositions may therefore be written

\[
X=U_C\mathcal G_C^{1/2},
\qquad
Y=U_H\mathcal G_H^{1/2}
\tag{21}
\]

with unitaries `U_C,U_H`. Substituting (21) into (20) yields

\[
\mathcal G_{CH}
=
\mathcal G_C^{1/2}
\bigl(-U_C^*TU_H\bigr)
\mathcal G_H^{1/2},
\tag{22}
\]

which proves (10)--(11).

This is the operator-valued analogue of the scalar fact that for

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&\tau\\\tau&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
=
\frac1{1-\tau^2}
\begin{pmatrix}1&-\tau\\-\tau&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

the raw off-diagonal entry diverges as `|\tau|\uparrow1`, while normalizing it by the two inverse diagonal entries returns exactly `-\tau`.

## 3. Consequence for PF-311: inversion amplifies strength, not angle

PF-311 correctly warns that a small residual direct high-conversion energy does not control the later mixed Green response. The present identity identifies exactly what is missing.

For the post-low prime-flute section, let

\[
\mathcal G=(\mathcal A/\mathcal L_\kappa)^{-1}.
\]

Then the canonical correlation singular values of its constant/high Green blocks are exactly those of

\[
T_H=S_L^{-1/2}E_{LH}R_H^{-1/2}.
\tag{23}
\]

Hence:

- a near-unit `T_H` direction remains a near-unit normalized Green correlation direction, but inversion does not make that angle closer to one;
- the potentially large factor in PF-311 equation (13) is completely accounted for by the diagonal Green strengths `\mathcal G_C^{1/2}` and `\mathcal G_H^{1/2}`;
- controlling the final physical mixed response may therefore be split into two independent currencies: diagonal source/reassembly strength and the already-defined post-low angle;
- a proof that the actual source or target avoids the large diagonal Green-strength directions can close the corresponding amplification mechanism even when `\|T_H\|` approaches one;
- conversely, showing only that the raw residual block `E_{LH}` is small cannot close the route, because `\mathcal G_C` or `\mathcal G_H` may diverge on the same singular channels.

This is a stronger decomposition of the “source/reassembly overlap” language at the end of PF-311. The next calculation need not invent a new mixed inverse statistic. It should extract the actual source and target maps in (11) and estimate

\[
\mathcal G_C^{1/2}J_C,
\qquad
\mathcal G_H^{1/2}J_H,
\tag{24}
\]

on the canonical tail, while `\Gamma_G` carries no singular spectrum beyond the already-known `T_H` angle.

The accepted clue `CLUE-source-weighted-return-potential-controls-mixed-response` is naturally aligned with this separation. Its proposed `q`-weighted occupation estimate should be tested as a possible bound on one of the diagonal Green/source factors in (24). The present theorem does **not** prove that identification; it says exactly what such a return-potential estimate would have to control to neutralize the inverse amplification.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

The abstract algebra is classical. Whitening a positive two-block matrix and reading singular values of the normalized cross block is the standard canonical-correlation construction. Canonical correlations are invariant under invertible changes of coordinates within each block, and inverse-covariance formulations of canonical/partial correlations are classical statistical matrix theory; see, for example, C. R. Rao, *A lemma on G-inverse of a matrix and computation of correlation coefficients in the singular case*, Communications in Statistics — Theory and Methods **10** (1981), 1--10, DOI `10.1080/03610928108828015`, for the inverse/generalized-inverse correlation viewpoint.

The defect identity (13) is also standard contraction theory. A concise direct reference is P. L. Robinson, *Julia operators and Halmos dilations*, arXiv:1803.09329, where the Julia-operator calculation yields

\[
(I-TT^*)^{1/2}T=T(I-T^*T)^{1/2}.
\]

No novelty is claimed for block inversion, canonical correlations, defect operators, or (13). A targeted literature search found exactly this surrounding classical theory rather than a prime-flute-specific theorem.

The durable Mathia-specific deduction is the placement of that classical duality at the precise PF-311 post-low screening frontier. It proves that the feared downstream inverse does **not** introduce an additional normalized angle obstruction: all extra growth is diagonal Green strength around the same `T_H` singular geometry. That narrows the next prime-flute theorem from a vague “control inverse amplification/source overlap” to the explicit source-weighted diagonal factors (24), with the accepted return-potential clue providing one concrete candidate mechanism.

## Boundaries and falsification

1. **Finite-section theorem.** The proof is exact on every finite canonical Galerkin section used by PF-311. Passing (10)--(11) to an infinite limit requires the relevant closed-form/inverse domains and source maps to converge; this finding does not assume that automatically.
2. **No angle gap.** The theorem does not prove `\|T_H\|\le1-\varepsilon`, `\rho_H(x^{(M)})\to0`, compactness, or any singular-value decay for `T_H`.
3. **Diagonal strength may still diverge.** Equality of normalized angles does not make the raw mixed inverse small. Near-unit singular values make the diagonal Green blocks large by exactly the same defect denominator.
4. **Source/reassembly still unresolved.** Equation (11) is useful only after the actual physical `J_C,J_H` are derived. It does not assert that the current `q`-weighted return-potential clue already controls either factor.
5. **No cancellation theorem.** Signed cancellation may improve a mixed response beyond the absolute strength-angle-strength bound; nothing here rules that in or out.
6. **No weak-trace/RH conclusion.** The result is a structural narrowing of the post-low Green problem, not a proof of the PF-281 weak-trace endpoint, scattering/determinant control, zeta-zero identification, the critical line, or RH.

A decisive internal audit is immediate: choose arbitrary finite positive `S,R`, any strict contraction `T`, form

\[
\mathcal B=(S^{1/2}\oplus R^{1/2})
\begin{pmatrix}I&T\\T^*&I\end{pmatrix}
(S^{1/2}\oplus R^{1/2}),
\]

invert it numerically or symbolically, and compare the singular values of

\[
\mathcal G_C^{-1/2}\mathcal G_{CH}\mathcal G_H^{-1/2}
\]

with those of `T`. They must agree exactly up to numerical error. A counterexample in a positive finite section would refute (10) and expose an algebraic error in the polar-factor argument.
