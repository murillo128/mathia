# MI-002 — Repetition changes a tangent pole into global essential spectrum

**Evidence level:** supported by the persisted localization and spectral findings.

An isolated finite tangent can have `lambda_H=s_H(1-s_H) in (0,1/4)` as a finite-rank resolvent/scattering pole. Recurrent escaping copies yield Weyl sequences on the infinite flute, so the same value belongs to its essential spectrum. For the ladder `lambda_j -> 0`, the physical roots `s_j=(1+sqrt(1-4lambda_j))/2 -> 1` make `Delta-s_j(1-s_j)` non-Fredholm. Thus the ordinary global `L^2` realization cannot obtain a near-one divisor by simply taking the union of finite-tangent poles. Compact operator corrections do not remove this obstruction ([PF-092](../../findings/PF-092-subquarter-essential-spectrum-blocks-standard-near-one-meromorphic-fredholm-resolvent.md)).

A different issue arises before globalization: an endpoint spectral measure/Weyl function retains the weights and order of a finite Jacobi path, whereas unmarked eigenvalues need not determine it. The useful data are the marked measure, not a scalar determinant ([PF-049](../../findings/PF-049-canonical-endpoint-spectral-measure-recovers-the-relative-gap-path.md), [PF-050](../../findings/PF-050-local-spectral-measures-of-the-infinite-flute-converge-to-prime-tangent-jacobi-data.md)). The explicit determinant cancellation is recorded in [MI-001](MI-001-relative-multigap-geometry-carries-the-spectral-signal.md).

These statements concern the ordinary `L^2` Fredholm picture and the specified compressions. They neither prohibit relative/weighted operator constructions nor provide a full localization-at-infinity theorem for the collapsing surface. A proposed alternative must specify its realization and what information survives there.
