# XF-125 — sublinear proper-harmonic heat histories remain blind to asymptotically full collision density

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` + `DYNAMIC-CROSS-SCALE` + `HISTORY-NONIDENTIFIABILITY` + `COLLISION-SATURATION`. XF-124 left one important escape route open. Its balanced roots-of-unity controls agree exactly on an arbitrary sublinear collection of high-mode **snapshots**, but an isolated high mode is not dynamically closed: under the exact XF-087 power-sum heat law its derivative sees lower pairs whose indices add to the target. It was therefore still possible that observing the full heat history of a few scattered high harmonics, rather than only their values at one slice, would expose the hidden collision geometry.

That generic repair also fails below the terminal degree. Let `N -> infinity` through even integers and let

\[
S_N\subset\{1,\ldots,N-1\},
\qquad
s_N:=|S_N|=o(N).
\tag{1}
\]

Then for every sufficiently large `N` there are two degree-`N` periodic unit-circle divisors `F_N` and `G_N` such that

- a fraction `1-o(1)` of all root slots of `F_N` belong to isolated simple double collisions at the target slice;
- every root of `G_N` is simple at that slice;
- and, under the exact collision-safe periodic heat flow of XF-087,

\[
\boxed{
P_m(F_N(t))=P_m(G_N(t))=0
\qquad
(m\in S_N)
}
\tag{2}
\]

for the **entire coefficient-level heat trajectory**, not merely at the target time and not merely to finite jet order.

The collision fraction can be made quantitative. With the explicit choice

\[
\varepsilon_N
:=
\left(\frac{s_N+1}{N}\right)^{1/3},
\tag{3}
\]

one may arrange

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\#\{\text{root slots of }F_N\text{ in double collisions}\}}{N}
\ge 1-2\varepsilon_N
=1-o(1).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the dynamic cross-scale escape left by XF-124 is much narrower than it first appeared. A sublinear family of individual proper-harmonic histories can remain **identically zero through heat** while almost every root is at a transition collision. The first unavoidable resonance in this construction is the terminal mode `m=N`, not a generic scattered high mode below it.

This is still an adversarial periodic matched-control statement, not a source-specific theorem for Xi's actual degree-scale high moments. It rules out a universal collision-coercivity claim based only on `o(N)` individual proper-harmonic histories.

## 1. Leave a vanishing simple filler and vary the collision-crystal order

Let `s=s_N` and define `epsilon=epsilon_N` by `(3)`. Since `s=o(N)`,

\[
\varepsilon\to0,
\qquad
\varepsilon N\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{s}{\varepsilon^2N}\to0.
\tag{5}
\]

Consider integers `d` with the same parity as `N` in the interval

\[
\varepsilon N\le d\le2\varepsilon N.
\tag{6}
\]

There are `Theta(epsilon N)` such candidates. For each one put

\[
R:=\frac{N-d}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]

For large `N`, `R>N/3`. Define the additive frequency semigroup

\[
\Gamma_{R,d}
:=
\{aR+bd:\ a,b\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0},\ a+b\ge1\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The point of the small filler degree `d` is that the total degree identity is

\[
2R+d=N,
\tag{9}
\]

while the semigroup generated by `R` and `d` can still avoid an arbitrary sublinear set of frequencies strictly below `N`.

Fix one `m<N`. If `m in Gamma_{R,d}`, then

\[
m=aR+bd
=\frac{aN}{2}+\left(b-\frac a2\right)d.
\tag{10}
\]

Because `R>N/3`, necessarily `a in {0,1,2}`. Each case excludes only `O(1/epsilon)` candidates `d` from `(6)`.

If `a=0`, then `m=bd` with `b>=1`. Since `d>=epsilon N` and `m<N`, there are fewer than `1/epsilon` possible values of `b`, and each determines at most one `d=m/b`.

If `a=1`, then

\[
2m-N=(2b-1)d.
\tag{11}
\]

Again `d>=epsilon N` and `|2m-N|<N`, so `|2b-1|<1/epsilon`; there are only `O(1/epsilon)` possible `b`, each determining at most one candidate `d`.

If `a=2`, then

\[
m=N+(b-1)d.
\tag{12}
\]

The condition `m<N` forces `b=0`, so at most the single value `d=N-m` is excluded. The case `b=1` is exactly the terminal resonance `m=N` and is deliberately outside `(1)`.

Therefore one sampled harmonic forbids at most `C/epsilon` candidates, for an absolute constant `C`, and the whole sampled set forbids at most

\[
O\!\left(\frac{s}{\varepsilon}\right)
\tag{13}
\]

values of `d`. The candidate pool has size `Theta(epsilon N)`, while by `(5)`

\[
\frac{s/\varepsilon}{\varepsilon N}
=
\frac{s}{\varepsilon^2N}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]

Hence for every sufficiently large `N` there is a parity-compatible `d` in `(6)` such that

\[
\boxed{
S_N\cap\Gamma_{R,d}=\varnothing.
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is the dynamic replacement for the divisor-avoidance step of XF-124. Snapshot invisibility required the sampled frequencies to miss two cyclic divisors. Full-history invisibility requires them to miss the entire additive cascade generated by the active cyclic orders. Because one generator is `~N/2` and the other occupies only a vanishing degree fraction, that larger avoidance problem still has enough entropy for every `o(N)` query set below `N`.

## 2. Two degree-`N` divisors have radically different collision geometry but the same sparse support semigroup

For a unit complex number `u`, retain the XF-124 cyclic factor

\[
Q_{r,u}(w):=w^r-u.
\tag{16}
\]

Its `r` unit-circle roots form a rotated regular `r`-gon and have raw power sums

\[
P_m(Q_{r,u})
=
\begin{cases}
r\,u^{m/r},&r\mid m,\\
0,&r\nmid m.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]

Choose unit phases `u,u_1,u_2,v` generically so that every distinct cyclic factor below has disjoint root support, with `u_1 != u_2`. Define

\[
\boxed{
F_N(w)
:=Q_{R,u}(w)^2Q_{d,v}(w)
}
\tag{18}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
G_N(w)
:=Q_{R,u_1}(w)Q_{R,u_2}(w)Q_{d,v}(w).
}
\tag{19}
\]

Both have degree `2R+d=N`. In `F_N`, the `R` roots of `Q_{R,u}` are all isolated double roots, while the `d` filler roots are simple. Thus the fraction of root slots in collisions is

\[
\frac{2R}{N}
=1-\frac dN
\ge1-2\varepsilon,
\tag{20}
\]

which gives `(4)`. In `G_N`, the two rotated `R`-gons and the common `d`-gon are pairwise disjoint, so all `N` roots are simple.

Equation `(17)` shows that at the target slice the nonzero proper power sums of **both** configurations are supported only on multiples of `R` or multiples of `d`. In particular their initial Fourier support lies inside `Gamma_{R,d}`:

\[
P_m(F_N(0))=P_m(G_N(0))=0
\qquad(m\notin\Gamma_{R,d}).
\tag{21}
\]

Unlike XF-124, we do not need the two controls to cancel different nonzero values on the sampled modes. After `(15)`, every sampled coordinate starts exactly at zero for each control separately.

## 3. XF-087 makes additive-semigroup support invariant for the entire heat trajectory

The exact periodic power-sum heat equation of XF-087 is, for `1<=m<=N`,

\[
\boxed{
P_m'
=-a\,m(N-m)P_m
-a\,m\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}P_iP_{m-i},
}
\tag{22}
\]

where `a=Delta^2` in the line's normalization. This identity is coefficient-level, so it remains valid through collisions and through intervals in which roots leave the real/unit-circle divisor.

Equation `(22)` has a simple support consequence that is load-bearing here. Suppose the initial proper-mode support lies in an additive semigroup `Gamma`. Then it stays in `Gamma` for the complete triangular trajectory.

Indeed, argue upward in `m`. Let `m notin Gamma`. The initial value is zero. In every convolution term `P_i P_{m-i}`, if both factors could be nonzero then by the induction hypothesis both indices would lie in `Gamma`; additivity would force

\[
m=i+(m-i)\in\Gamma,
\tag{23}
\]

contrary to the choice of `m`. Hence every nonlinear term in `(22)` vanishes, and the remaining scalar equation is

\[
P_m'=-a\,m(N-m)P_m,
\qquad
P_m(0)=0.
\tag{24}
\]

Therefore `P_m(t)=0` identically. Applying this induction to `(21)` gives

\[
\boxed{
P_m(F_N(t))=P_m(G_N(t))=0
\qquad
(m\notin\Gamma_{R,d})
}
\tag{25}
\]

for every finite heat time in the exact coefficient trajectory. Combining `(15)` and `(25)` proves `(2)`.

This is stronger than matching any prescribed finite time jet. Every derivative of every queried coordinate agrees automatically because the entire analytic coordinate trajectory is the zero function. The lower-mode mixing highlighted as the open escape in XF-124 is present, but it can only generate frequencies in the additive closure of the initial cyclic supports. The sampled modes were chosen to lie outside that closure.

The mechanism is the discrete one-sided counterpart of the familiar spectral cascade in quadratic Burgers dynamics: nonlinear interactions generate sum frequencies. The new point is adversarial rather than descriptive — the generator orders can be selected after the sparse observation set is known so that the complete cascade misses all queried proper harmonics.

## 4. The same histories cannot decide whether the target slice is a transition boundary

At `t=0`, every root of `F_N` and `G_N` lies on the unit circle, hence on the real periodic divisor. The generic phase choice keeps the `R` double sites of `F_N` isolated from the filler.

For `F_N`, the collision-safe local discriminant law used throughout XF-002, XF-099 and XF-120--XF-123 makes every isolated double root leave the real divisor immediately on the backward side of the target slice. Forward periodic backward-heat evolution preserves real-rootedness from the all-real target state. In the normalization with target time zero,

\[
\Lambda_{\rm per}(F_N)=0.
\tag{26}
\]

For `G_N`, all roots are simple at the target slice. The real-simple root ODE therefore has a real solution on a nonzero two-sided time neighborhood of `0`; equivalently, the implicit-function continuation of the simple periodic roots stays real for sufficiently small negative time. Forward real-zero preservation then gives

\[
\Lambda_{\rm per}(G_N)<0.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus the controls have different transition status at the observed slice while every sampled history in `(1)` is exactly the same zero trajectory. An observer may query values at arbitrarily many times, all time derivatives, Laplace transforms of an individual queried history, or any other functional depending only on those selected coordinate trajectories; nothing changes because the selected trajectories themselves vanish identically.

This directly strengthens the slice-level conclusion of XF-124. Sparse dynamic cross-scale coupling does not generically rescue collision detection merely by replacing high-mode snapshots with high-mode histories.

## 5. Deterministic adaptive history queries still require more than a sublinear proper-mode budget

The construction also survives deterministic adaptivity. Consider a deterministic procedure that may select its next harmonic after seeing the **complete heat histories** of all previous queried harmonics, with every query restricted to `1<=m<N`, and suppose it makes at most `s_N=o(N)` distinct queries.

Run the procedure conceptually on the transcript in which every returned history is the identically zero function. This fixes a queried set `S_N` of size at most `s_N`. Perform the avoidance construction of Sections 1--2 for that set. Equation `(25)` then shows that both `F_N` and `G_N` realize the assumed zero transcript exactly. Consequently the adaptive procedure follows the same query path on both inputs and produces the same output, although one input has collision fraction `1-o(1)` and is exactly at its periodic transition while the other is simple and lies inside a real-rooted interval.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{deterministic }o(N)\text{ adaptive queries of complete proper-harmonic histories}
\text{ do not universally detect macroscopic collision geometry.}
}
\tag{28}
\]

This is a coordinate-query statement. It does not apply to an observable that combines linearly many harmonics before measurement, nor to source-specific analytic data that imposes relations unavailable to these cyclic controls.

## 6. Stress tests and exact boundary

### 6.1 The terminal mode is a genuine resonance of this construction

The restriction `m<N` is essential. The degree relation `(9)` gives

\[
N=2R+d\in\Gamma_{R,d}
\tag{29}
\]

for every admissible `d`. In the counting proof this is exactly the `a=2,b=1` case of `(12)`, which is independent of the chosen generator order. Therefore the present semigroup-avoidance mechanism cannot make the `N`th power-sum history identically invisible.

This identifies a much sharper positive escape than the generic one left by XF-124. A top-degree history can aggregate nonlinear interactions from the whole lower spectrum and is not defeated by simply moving the cyclic generator. Whether that particular global coordinate, or a small number of comparably global functionals, carries useful **Xi-specific** transition information is open here.

### 6.2 Consecutive full degree data still reconstruct the divisor

There is no conflict with Newton identities. The first `N` consecutive power sums determine the degree-`N` root multiset. The theorem concerns a sublinear family of individual histories among the `N-1` proper harmonics. Its point is that adding time as a continuum of observations does not automatically compensate for sparse frequency coverage.

### 6.3 The filler is necessary for asymptotically full collisions and query entropy simultaneously

A pure double crystal of degree `N` has fixed symmetry order `N/2`, so an observer could query that mode directly. The simple filler lets the collision order `R=(N-d)/2` move through `Theta(epsilon N)` possibilities while consuming only an `O(epsilon)` fraction of the root budget. The condition `s/(epsilon^2 N)->0` is exactly the balance between the size of that order family and the `O(1/epsilon)` semigroup resonances excluded by each query.

The explicit cubic-root choice `(3)` is only convenient. More generally, any `epsilon_N -> 0` satisfying

\[
\varepsilon_NN\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{s_N}{\varepsilon_N^2N}\to0
\tag{30}
\]

gives collision fraction at least `1-2 epsilon_N` by the same proof.

### 6.4 This is not yet an Xi high-mode matched-control theorem

The common histories in `(2)` are zero. XF-119--XF-123 match the actual Xi source only in the natural q-scale low prefix; no theorem currently realizes arbitrary prescribed Xi histories near the degree scale while retaining the semigroup sparsity used here. Therefore XF-125 does **not** say that Xi's true sparse high-mode histories are useless. It says that their usefulness would have to come from Xi-specific values or cross-frequency constraints, not from a generic theorem that full high-mode heat histories must reveal collision density.

### 6.5 The argument uses the full nonlinear heat equation, not a linearized proxy

The support invariance `(25)` is exact for the quadratic XF-087 ODE. There is no truncation in time, no small-amplitude assumption, and no neglect of lower-pair forcing. The forcing is precisely what generates the additive semigroup, and the counting argument avoids that whole semigroup rather than only the original cyclic frequencies.

Those sources delimit the ingredients but do not supply the present matched-control statement. A directed search did not identify a prior result combining arbitrary sparse proper-harmonic **history** queries, additive-cascade avoidance, asymptotically full collision density and transition/nontransition unit-circle divisors. No external theorem is load-bearing, so `SOURCES.md` does not need a new durable dependency.

The frontier after XF-125 is therefore more specific than after XF-124. Merely observing `o(N)` scattered high modes **for all heat times** is still not a generic collision detector below the terminal degree. A positive route must use at least one ingredient absent from this adversary: source-specific Xi high-mode values strong enough to break the cyclic semigroup construction, a genuinely global/top-degree observable such as the `m=N` history, an aggregate that couples a linear-density set of harmonics at once, or entire-function/arithmetic geometry not reducible to coordinate histories.
