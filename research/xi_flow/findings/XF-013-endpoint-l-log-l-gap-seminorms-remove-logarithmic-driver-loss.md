# XF-013 — endpoint `L log L` gap seminorms remove the near-`L^1` logarithmic driver loss

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` + `LITERATURE+DERIVED` + `BOUNDARY/REDIRECT`. XF-011 showed that total variation has the right `h^2` visibility but does not strongly control the Cauchy driver, while XF-012 recovered strong control by taking `p -> 1+` at the cost of a sharp `Theta(log log T)` Hilbert-transform constant. That logarithmic loss is a feature of the pure `L^p` route, not an endpoint obstruction for all local adjacent-gap observables.

The classical Zygmund endpoint inequality for the periodic conjugate/Hilbert transform gives a different bridge:

\[
\mathcal H:L\log L(\mathbb T)\longrightarrow L^1(\mathbb T)
\]

boundedly. Combining it with the exact Markov-convolution structure of the linearized Xi lattice produces an adjacent-gap **Orlicz Lyapunov** whose raw size is already `Theta(h^2)` and whose normalized limit controls `|| |D|U||_1` with an `h`-independent constant. The price is not a `log log T` operator blowup; it is an entropy-like sensitivity to concentration in the gap derivative distribution.

This does not upper-bound `Lambda`: the Lyapunov is still a universal feature of the arithmetic-lattice linearization and still presupposes an ordered real-gap regime. It does, however, materially change the information-interface target left by XF-012.

## 1. Claim

Use the periodic linearized gap flow from XF-011,

\[
u_j'=L_hu_j,
\qquad
L_h=\frac{2}{h^2}\sum_{m\ne0}\frac{T_m-I}{m^2},
\tag{1}
\]

and let

\[
D:=T_1-I.
\]

On the normalized periodic index set define the Young function

\[
\Phi(s):=s\log(e+s),\qquad s\ge0,
\tag{2}
\]

and the discrete Luxemburg norm

\[
\|x\|_{\Phi,N}
:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:
\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
\Phi\!\left(\frac{|x_j|}{\lambda}\right)
\le1\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|Du(t)\|_{\Phi,N}
\le
\|Du(0)\|_{\Phi,N}
\qquad(t\ge0).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the adjacent-gap `L log L` Luxemburg seminorm is an **exact Lyapunov quantity** for the finite periodic linearized lattice flow.

There is also a raw modular version. With the mesoscopic normalization `delta=h^2`, set

\[
\mathcal E_{\Phi,h}(u)
:=h^2\frac1N\sum_j
\Phi\!\left(\frac{|u_{j+1}-u_j|}{h^2}\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Then the same semigroup satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\Phi,h}(u(t))
\le
\mathcal E_{\Phi,h}(u(0)).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Now impose the XF-008 mesoscopic scaling

\[
\delta=h^2=\frac{2\pi}{N},
\qquad
u_j=U(\delta j),
\tag{7}
\]

for a smooth periodic nonconstant profile `U`. If `||.||_Phi` denotes the continuum Luxemburg norm on the normalized circle for the same Young function, then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|Du\|_{\Phi,N}}{h^2}
\longrightarrow
\|U'\|_{\Phi},
}
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal E_{\Phi,h}(u)}{h^2}
\longrightarrow
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Phi(|U'(X)|)\,dX.
}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular both local observables have raw scale `Theta(h^2)` for a fixed nonconstant profile.

Finally, the classical Zygmund inequality for the periodic conjugate function gives an absolute constant `C_Phi` such that

\[
\|\mathcal H f\|_{L^1}
\le C_\Phi\|f\|_{L\log L},
\tag{10}
\]

where the standard `L log L` norm is equivalent to the Luxemburg norm generated by (2). Since

\[
|D_X|U=\mathcal H U',
\tag{11}
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\||D_X|U\|_{L^1}
\le C\|U'\|_{\Phi}
=C\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\|Du\|_{\Phi,N}}{h^2},
}
\tag{12}
\]

with `C` independent of `h`. Therefore the endpoint Orlicz bridge controls the `L^1` Cauchy driver **without** the `Theta(log(1/h))` constant forced on any pure near-endpoint `L^p` scheme that retains a fixed fraction of the `h^2` raw moment.

At Xi height, `h_T \asymp 1/\log T`, so the raw Orlicz statistic remains

\[
\boxed{
\|Du\|_{\Phi,N},\ \mathcal E_{\Phi,h_T}(u)
=\Theta(\log^{-2}T)
}
\tag{13}
\]

for a fixed nonconstant mesoscopic profile, with no additional `log log T` factor in the operator norm.

## 2. Exact discrete Orlicz contraction

XF-011 writes the semigroup as convolution with a nonnegative mass-one periodic kernel,

\[
e^{tL_h}x=K_t*x,
\qquad K_t\ge0,
\qquad \sum_m K_t(m)=1,
\tag{14}
\]

and translation invariance gives

\[
Du(t)=K_t*Du(0).
\tag{15}
\]

Fix `lambda>0`. Convexity and monotonicity of `Phi`, followed by Jensen, give pointwise

\[
\Phi\!\left(
\frac{|(K_t*x)_j|}{\lambda}
\right)
\le
\Phi\!\left(
\sum_mK_t(m)\frac{|x_{j-m}|}{\lambda}
\right)
\le
\sum_mK_t(m)
\Phi\!\left(\frac{|x_{j-m}|}{\lambda}\right).
\tag{16}
\]

Averaging over `j` preserves the right-hand modular exactly because translations preserve normalized counting measure. Hence every `lambda` admissible for `x` in (3) is also admissible for `K_t*x`, proving (4).

Taking `lambda=h^2` inside the same Jensen estimate gives (6) directly. More generally, **every convex adjacent-difference modular** is contractive under this linearized Markov semigroup. The specific role of `Phi(s)=s log(e+s)` is the endpoint harmonic-analysis bridge (10), not the contraction itself.

## 3. Mesoscopic scaling

With `delta=h^2`, smoothness gives uniformly in the lattice index

\[
\frac{u_{j+1}-u_j}{h^2}
=
\frac{U(X_j+\delta)-U(X_j)}{\delta}
=U'(X_j)+O(\delta).
\tag{17}
\]

For each fixed `lambda>0`, continuity of `Phi` on bounded sets and periodic Riemann summation imply

\[
\frac1N\sum_j
\Phi\!\left(
\frac{|u_{j+1}-u_j|}{h^2\lambda}
\right)
\longrightarrow
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\Phi\!\left(
\frac{|U'(X)|}{\lambda}
\right)dX.
\tag{18}
\]

For nonzero `U'`, the continuum modular is continuous and strictly decreasing in `lambda`, so the defining threshold in the Luxemburg norm converges. Homogeneity of the Luxemburg norm then yields (8). Equation (9) is the same Riemann-sum limit with `lambda=1`.

This is a genuine endpoint power-scale statement. Unlike XF-012, no exponent depends on `h`, and no factor `h^{2(p_h-1)}` must be balanced against an endpoint-diverging `L^p` operator constant.

## 4. Why the entropy bump fixes the XF-011 counterexample

The Poisson family from XF-011 pinpoints what total variation forgets. Let

\[
P_r(X)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos X+r^2},
\qquad
Q_r=\mathcal H(P_r-1),
\qquad 0<r<1.
\tag{19}
\]

Then

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int|P_r-1|\le2,
\tag{20}
\]

while XF-011 computed

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int|Q_r|
=\frac2\pi\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{21}
\]

So `L^1` derivative mass alone is blind to increasingly concentrated spikes. The `L log L` bump is designed to see exactly this concentration. In fact the Poisson kernel has the exact entropy identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}P_r(X)\log P_r(X)\,dX
=-\log(1-r^2).
}
\tag{22}
\]

To verify it, write

\[
\log P_r
=\log(1-r^2)-2\log|e^{iX}-r|.
\]

The measure `P_r(X)dX/(2\pi)` is harmonic measure at `r`, and the harmonic boundary function `log|1-rz|` has Poisson value `log(1-r^2)` at `z=r`. Substitution gives (22).

Thus the entropy cost diverges on the same logarithmic concentration scale as the conjugate-kernel `L^1` norm. The endpoint Orlicz statistic does not contradict the `L^1` obstruction; it adds precisely the concentration information that total variation omitted.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

The endpoint theorem (10) is classical Zygmund theory for conjugate functions on the circle. Tat-Leung Yee and Kwok-Pun Ho, **Zygmund inequality of the conjugate function on Morrey-Zygmund spaces**, *Demonstratio Mathematica* 52:1 (2019), 97–104, DOI `10.1515/dema-2019-0011`, state the classical normalized-circle inequality explicitly before extending it to Morrey-Zygmund spaces. The underlying result belongs to the classical conjugate-function theory developed by Zygmund.

No novelty is claimed for Orlicz/Luxemburg norms, `L log L`, the Zygmund inequality, Jensen contraction under Markov convolution, Poisson-kernel entropy, or the identity `|D|=\mathcal H\partial_X`. A targeted search did not identify a de Bruijn–Newman formulation combining this endpoint Orlicz bridge with the adjacent-gap lattice scaling; absence from that search is not used as novelty evidence.

The Mathia-specific durable result is the exact combination of already-established Xi-flow structure: the adjacent-difference process in XF-011 is a Markov convolution, the fixed-time mesoscopic coordinate in XF-008 has `delta=h^2`, and the endpoint `L log L -> L^1` theorem turns the resulting `Theta(h^2)` Orlicz Lyapunov into uniform control of the continuum Cauchy driver. This shows that XF-012's `Theta(log log T)` degradation is **not** an unavoidable endpoint price once the observable class is enlarged beyond pure powers.

## 6. Falsification controls and remaining boundary

This result belongs to the **linearized arithmetic-lattice model**. The Markov kernel in (14) is universal for that linearization, so the Orlicz Lyapunov is shared by matched controls and is not an Xi-specific selector. It cannot by itself imply an upper bound on `Lambda`.

The observable is also more demanding statistically than a single algebraic moment. The normalization by `h^2` places the full adjacent-difference distribution inside a convex entropy bump. An upstream theorem must therefore control concentration/tails at the mesoscopic derivative scale, not merely a fixed `p`th moment. In particular, pair correlation alone need not determine this Orlicz functional.

The ordered-gap problem remains unchanged. Under a hypothetical positive `Lambda`, one cannot assume consecutive real zeros at a time below the real-rootedness threshold. Any arithmetic input must be attached at a time where the relevant zeros are already real or be reformulated as a configuration-level statistic meaningful with nonreal zeros.

Finally, no nonlinear monotonicity is claimed for the full zero ODE. Extending (4) or (6) beyond the linearized regime would require a separate estimate; the convex Markov proof does not survive automatically when the jump rates themselves depend nonlinearly on the evolving gap configuration.

## 7. Consequence for `xi_flow`

The endpoint frontier is now sharper than the fixed-`p`/variable-`p` dichotomy. There are three distinct information interfaces:

- total variation has raw `h^2` scale but misses concentration and therefore fails to control the Cauchy driver strongly;
- pure `L^{p_h}` moments can retain `h^2` power scale but necessarily pay the `Theta(log log T)` Hilbert constant quantified in XF-012;
- an `L log L` adjacent-gap Orlicz statistic retains raw `h^2` scale **and** controls the `L^1` Cauchy driver with a uniform endpoint constant, by paying instead an entropy penalty on concentrated derivative profiles.

The most concrete next statistical question is therefore no longer only whether one can estimate moments with `p_T-1 \asymp 1/\log\log T`. It is whether unconditional, real-rootedness-safe zero information can control a mesoscopic adjacent-gap **entropy/Orlicz functional** at `Theta(log^{-2}T)` precision, or a configuration-level analogue that remains meaningful off the critical line. A no-go result for the statistical route must now block this endpoint Orlicz interface as well as the pure-power family.
