# XF-172 — Weighted subfundamental Laplace leakage controls finite-height zero resolution

**Evidence classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + ORDER-SHARP-SOURCE-BRIDGE`. Inside the normalized positive even common Xi scale-mixture gauge, a weighted amount of Laplace spectrum leaking below the first Xi atom `pi` dominates the central Mellin deficit of XF-171. Therefore an approximate single-square-lattice law gives an explicit finite-height zero-free criterion. No external theorem is load-bearing; the proof is a direct calculation in the XF-169 scale-mixture/Laplace dictionary followed by XF-171.

## Statement

XF-168 identified the exact source-side rigidity: support on the single square lattice `pi n^2` forces the Xi theta coefficients. XF-171 gave a soft zero-resolution modulus, but its defect

\[
d_\nu=1-M_\nu(1/2)=\int_{\mathbb R}(\cosh a-1)\,d\nu(a)
\tag{1}
\]

lives in the hidden scale-mixture parameter. The missing bridge was to control `d_nu` directly by an error visible in the positive Laplace spectrum of the theta prekernel.

Let `nu` be a finite positive even Borel measure satisfying

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\cosh a\,d\nu(a)=1,
\tag{2}
\]

and let the associated common-scale theta prekernel be the XF-169 mixture

\[
F_\nu(x)=\int_{\mathbb R} e^a F_\Xi(e^{4a}x)\,d\nu(a),
\qquad
F_\Xi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\pi n^2x}.
\tag{3}
\]

Its positive Laplace measure is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\nu
=\int_{\mathbb R}e^a
\sum_{n\ge1}\delta_{\pi n^2e^{4a}}\,d\nu(a),
}
\tag{4}
\]

in the sense that

\[
F_\nu(x)=\int_{(0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda x}\,d\mu_\nu(\lambda).
\tag{5}
\]

Define the **weighted subfundamental leakage**

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\nu
:=
\int_{(0,\pi)}
\left[
\left(\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\right)^{1/4}-1
\right]^2
\,d\mu_\nu(\lambda)
\in[0,\infty].
}
\tag{6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{d_\nu\le \mathcal L_\nu.}
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently the Mellin multiplier

\[
M_\nu(s)=\int e^{(1-2s)a}\,d\nu(a)
\tag{8}
\]

has no zero anywhere in

\[
0\le\operatorname{Re}s\le1,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Im}s|\le T
\tag{9}
\]

whenever

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\nu<\frac{1}{4T^2+2}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\nu=\delta_0,
}
\tag{11}
\]

so vanishing subfundamental leakage recovers exact Xi inside the entire normalized positive common-scale gauge.

The exponent in the finite-height criterion is optimal in order. For the XF-167 three-atom family, when `0<c<(1/2)log 2`, one has the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\nu_c}=d_c,}
\tag{12}
\]

and its first off-critical multiplier zero has height `T_c=pi/(2c)`, with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\nu_c}T_c^2\longrightarrow\frac{\pi^2}{16}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the natural source-resolution scale is genuinely `mathcal L T^2`, not merely an artifact of measuring the hidden mixing law by `d_nu`.

## 1. The first square-lattice atom already contains the Mellin deficit

For a fixed negative shift write `a=-b` with `b>0`. The `n=1` atom generated by `(4)` sits at

\[
\lambda_1(-b)=\pi e^{-4b}<\pi
\tag{14}
\]

and carries Laplace mass `e^{-b}dnu(-b)`. At that atom the weight in `(6)` is

\[
\left[
\left(\frac{\pi}{\pi e^{-4b}}\right)^{1/4}-1
\right]^2
=(e^b-1)^2.
\tag{15}
\]

Hence the contribution of the negative-shift `n=1` copy to `mathcal L_nu` is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{(-\infty,0)}
 e^a(e^{-a}-1)^2\,d\nu(a)
&=
\int_{(-\infty,0)}
(e^{-a}+e^a-2)\,d\nu(a)\\
&=
\int_{(-\infty,0)}2(\cosh a-1)\,d\nu(a).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Because `nu` is even and `cosh a-1` is even, the last expression equals

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(\cosh a-1)\,d\nu(a)=d_\nu.
\tag{17}
\]

Every other atom of `mu_nu` that happens to lie below `pi` contributes a nonnegative amount to `(6)`. Therefore `(16)`--`(17)` prove `(7)` immediately.

This is the source-side bridge that XF-171 did not yet have. The central Mellin deficit is not only an abstract coordinate of the hidden shift law: it is already paid, exactly once, by the weighted downward leakage of the **first** square-lattice atom. Higher lattice copies can only increase the observable defect.

The possibility `mathcal L_nu=+infinity` is harmless. Normalization `(2)` controls one exponential moment of `nu`, whereas the total contribution of very remote negative shifts can include many lattice copies below `pi`. The theorem only uses `(7)`; whenever a finite quantitative source approximation makes `mathcal L_nu` small, the desired Mellin conclusion follows.

## 2. Finite-height zero resolution follows from XF-171

XF-171 proved that every zero `s=sigma+iT` of `M_nu` in the critical strip must satisfy

\[
4T^2+(1-2\sigma)^2
\ge
\frac{1-d_\nu}{d_\nu},
\tag{18}
\]

and in particular that the whole rectangle `(9)` is zero-free if

\[
d_\nu<\frac1{4T^2+2}.
\tag{19}
\]

By `(7)`, condition `(10)` implies `(19)`, proving the finite-height statement.

At zero leakage, `(7)` gives `d_nu=0`. XF-171 then gives

\[
d_\nu=0
\iff
\nu=\delta_0,
\tag{20}
\]

under the positive even normalization `(2)`. Thus `(11)` is stronger than a zero-free statement: within this common-scale gauge, **no positive normalized deformation can keep all of its weighted Laplace spectrum at or above the first Xi atom unless the deformation is trivial**.

That conclusion is specifically about the common global scale-mixture gauge. It does not say that an arbitrary positive Laplace measure supported in `[pi,infinity)` must equal the Xi square lattice. XF-168 supplies the separate exact single-lattice coefficient rigidity once the full support law `pi n^2` is imposed. XF-172 instead shows that, for the scale-mixture contamination already classified by XF-169, the lowest spectral edge detects the entire hidden mixing defect quantitatively.

## 3. Stress test: the three-atom family saturates the bridge at small displacement

Take the normalized family from XF-167/XF-169,

\[
\nu_c
=
\frac12\delta_0
+
\frac{1}{4\cosh c}(\delta_c+\delta_{-c}),
\qquad c>0.
\tag{21}
\]

XF-171 computed

\[
d_c
=
\frac{1-\operatorname{sech}c}{2}
=
\frac{c^2}{4}+O(c^4).
\tag{22}
\]

For the negatively shifted copy, an `n`th lattice atom lies below `pi` exactly when

\[
\pi n^2e^{-4c}<\pi
\iff
c>\frac12\log n.
\tag{23}
\]

If

\[
0<c<\frac12\log2,
\tag{24}
\]

only `n=1` from the negative copy lies below the fundamental Xi atom. The central copy starts at `pi`, and the positive copy lies entirely above it. Therefore the nonnegative remainder discarded in proving `(7)` vanishes and

\[
\mathcal L_{\nu_c}=d_c,
\tag{25}
\]

which is `(12)`.

The same family has its first off-critical multiplier zeros at

\[
s=0\mp i\frac{\pi}{2c},
\qquad
s=1\mp i\frac{\pi}{2c}.
\tag{26}
\]

Putting `T_c=pi/(2c)` and using `(22)` gives

\[
\mathcal L_{\nu_c}T_c^2
=d_cT_c^2
\longrightarrow
\frac{\pi^2}{16},
\tag{27}
\]

which proves `(13)`. Hence no theorem based only on this spectral-edge leakage can replace the scale `T=O(mathcal L^{-1/2})` by a parametrically larger power. The source-side bridge inherits the same order-sharp obstruction as the Mellin defect itself.

## 4. What the theorem changes in the source-identification frontier

The previous frontier left two possibilities: derive `d(T)=o(T^{-2})` from approximate single-square-lattice information, or abandon the common-scale gauge. Equation `(7)` supplies a concrete version of the first route. It is enough to establish, from whatever approximate source law is available,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\nu=o(T^{-2}).
}
\tag{28}
\]

That condition is stated entirely in the positive Laplace spectrum of the theta prekernel and immediately excludes all added common-mixture off-critical zeros through height `T`.

This also clarifies what **not** to do next. Another qualitative classifier of positive common scale mixtures is unnecessary: the gauge now has a direct source-side defect, an exact comparison to the Mellin defect, a finite-height zero-free theorem, and an order-sharp stress family. The remaining useful common-gauge question is functional-analytic: which natural notion of approximate agreement with the single Xi square lattice forces `(28)`? For example, a reconstruction norm that quantitatively controls the weighted subfundamental spectral mass would close the added-zero problem at the corresponding height.

If no natural source approximation controls `mathcal L_nu`, the mathematically distinct next frontier is atomwise or mesoscopic deformation of the individual atoms `pi n^2`, where there is no single common shift measure and the argument above no longer applies.

The audit checked standard theta Mellin/Laplace-transform references and general positive Laplace-measure literature. The Mathia-specific content is the exact combination of the XF-169 normalized scale-mixture formula with the spectral-edge weight in `(6)`: the negatively shifted first theta atom contributes **exactly** `d_nu`, while every additional subfundamental atom contributes with the correct sign. XF-171 then turns that source-visible leakage into finite-height off-critical zero resolution. Since no external theorem is load-bearing, `SOURCES.md` requires no update.

## Falsification boundary

Positivity and evenness are load-bearing. Positivity makes the omitted higher-atom contribution nonnegative; evenness turns the one-sided negative-shift contribution in `(16)` into the full deficit `(17)`. Signed or asymmetric scale perturbations need not satisfy `(7)`.

The result is also gauge-specific. An atomwise perturbation can move some `pi n^2` while preserving the first spectral edge, so `mathcal L_nu=0` is not a universal metric on all possible Xi-source perturbations. The exact-rigidity conclusion `(11)` uses the **common scale-mixture representation** and must not be promoted beyond it.

Finally, the theorem controls only zeros supplied by the multiplier `M_nu`. At `nu=delta_0`, the multiplier is identically one and the inherited factor is exactly Xi; deciding its critical-line zeros or the de Bruijn--Newman transition is still the original problem. XF-172 is a finite-height source-resolution theorem for the classified contamination gauge, not a proof of RH or `Lambda=0`.
