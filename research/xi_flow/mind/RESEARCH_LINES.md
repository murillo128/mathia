## Prove a near-null coercivity theorem from finite-range shape force to geometric-ramp proximity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-collision-safe-coordinates-separate-singularity-from-source-information` and `MI-003-fixed-time-memory-is-mesoscopic-not-finite-jet`.

XF-032--XF-033 show that geometric gap ramps are exact bulk null modes not only for triples but for every finite-range translation-invariant scale-invariant local shape assembly. Fixed larger normalized discriminants therefore cannot see mean logarithmic contrast in their interior first variation.

XF-034 supplies the source half of the bridge: an exact or uniformly near-geometric ramp persisting across a super-mesoscopic Xi buffer must flatten, because the nested `M` and `2M` zero-counting spans force `r_T^M=1+o(1)`. The live theorem is quantitative stability: prove that small aggregate shape force or adverse bulk production makes a general source-valid profile close to this null family strongly enough for the counting rigidity to apply.

## Combine taper localization, collision coverage, and source-valid buffer constraints

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-collision-safe-coordinates-separate-singularity-from-source-information`, `MI-002-scale-free-gap-dynamics-still-need-an-xi-specific-law`, and `MI-003-fixed-time-memory-is-mesoscopic-not-finite-jet`.

The fixed-time core has `Theta(log^2 T)` gaps and the available source-valid buffers are parametrically larger. The nonlinear taper defect is already localized to first differences, while collision-positive overlap prevents absolute-value singularization. A positive theorem must use these together with the new flattening of coherent geometric null modes; a negative must exhibit source-compatible near-null profiles that evade both collision coverage and nested-span rigidity.
