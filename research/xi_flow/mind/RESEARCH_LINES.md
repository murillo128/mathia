## Convert exact square-lattice identification into complexity-adaptive quantitative coercivity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-004-endpoint-prime-free-fourier-sector-is-volterra-triangular`, `MI-008-gaussian-reference-localization-is-relatively-exact-but-not-global`, `MI-011-cross-scale-observability-can-compress-to-one-off-circle-heat-trace`, `MI-012-xi-tail-hazard-is-source-specific-but-not-yet-coercive`.

XF-154--XF-174 separate source identification, common-scale stability, and the topology actually seen at the spectral edge. Positive scale mixtures preserve much of the Jacobi structure while moving zeros, but the exact Xi square lattice is Hamburger-rigid. Subcritical edge observables give the right finite-height topology for the common-scale gauge; the quarter-power edge marks the onset of an extra tail tax.

XF-175 begins the atomwise regime and shows that fixed-height transform control is exponentially insensitive to remote bounded-factor atom motion. Direct local shell budgets therefore cannot convert approximate high-index placement into global zero control without additional cross-index structure.

XF-176 makes the exact side rigid again. For a positive sparse Gaussian comb whose atom locations approach the integer square-lattice asymptotics, exact Jacobi reciprocity forces full Fourier self-duality and, under the stated uniqueness hypotheses, the exact integer support. Thus exact modular coupling can identify the source even when pointwise asymptotics alone cannot.

XF-177--XF-178 then show that this exact identification is not stable in any fixed power-weighted real-axis Jacobi topology. A single remote moved atom already has a threshold at weight exponent `1/2`; more decisively, finite packets with enough vanishing moments can make **every fixed power weight and all fixed logarithmic derivatives arbitrarily small** while retaining a fixed support displacement. No fixed-complexity real-axis seminorm is coercive for uniform atom placement on the full finite-surgery class.

XF-179 resolves the information-theoretic part of that finite-packet obstruction. If exactly `m` positive unit-mass atom pairs are moved, the Jacobi residual's cusp jet through order `m` identifies the surgery exactly, and depth `m-1` is sharp. XF-180 then separates remote location from packet complexity in the quantitative inverse. The centered normalized observables `(partial_x+pi A)^ell J(0)/R^ell` are exactly the centered power moments of the squared atoms. For a consecutive Xi packet `((N+1)^2,...,(N+m)^2)` with `N>=m`, normalized separation is `>=1/(2(m-1))`, so the local Vandermonde inverse is bounded in terms of `m` alone, uniformly in the remote index `N`. However its norm is already at least `(m-1)^(m-1)/(m-1)! ~ e^(m-1)/sqrt(2 pi (m-1))` in these monomial coordinates.

The live source-side target is therefore no longer merely to invent an adaptive identifier. It is to make the adaptive hierarchy **quantitatively usable**: control an effective packet complexity small enough that the inverse remains compatible with zero sensitivity, find better-conditioned complexity-matched coordinates that still defeat the exact `m-1` cancellation, or estimate the centered cusp data from positive heat scales without reintroducing a remote-index cancellation cost. A stronger arithmetic/modular source law that forbids high-complexity balanced surgery remains an alternative. Exact Jacobi rigidity, cusp identifiability, finite-scale observability, and transition coercivity must remain separate gates.

## Treat exact modular identification, fixed-height spectral visibility, adaptive cusp recovery, and quantitative source stability as separate gates

The functional equation admits broad common-scale gauges; exact support plus reciprocity can nevertheless identify the Xi comb; fixed-height transform norms barely see remote atom motion; fixed-power Jacobi topologies can be defeated by moment-balanced packets; and exact cusp jets can recover finite packets only at a complexity-matched depth. XF-180 shows that absolute remote index is not intrinsically fatal once the cusp data are affinely normalized, but complexity growth is already exponentially costly in monomial moment coordinates. A useful transition theorem must specify how source information is transported into such adaptive data with an error budget compatible with the zero-sensitive region, rather than infer coercivity from exact uniqueness alone.
