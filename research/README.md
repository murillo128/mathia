# Mathia Research State

## Global picture

The common RH-facing gap remains an independently source-forced estimate in the precise destination norm that can exclude each off-critical zero. Source reconstruction, auxiliary positivity, available operator witnesses and artificial spectral separation do not provide that estimate. No Mathia finding proves RH.

The balanced Farey variance target has a **classical RH-conditional resolution** in [MC-520](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-520-rh-mellin-variance-closes-conditional-farey-window-target.md). Its independent continuation needs a weaker-hypothesis estimate and a carrying-window range justified under those hypotheses. [MC-521](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-521-smooth-deconvolution-removes-the-variance-zero-free-boundary-loss.md) sharpens the reverse difficulty calibration: complete all-scale Möbius variance forces zero exclusion without the old sharp-endpoint loss. [PL-465](prime_lattice/findings/PL-465-relative-frame-transfers-dirichlet-zero-bounds.md) shows that a hypothetical relative frame can transfer an independent upper bound from one Dirichlet channel to all four modulo 5. Avoiding an absolute moment premise does not by itself make that frame free of zero-location content. [RE-259](robin_extremal/findings/RE-259-zero-density-removes-critical-robin-moment-boundary.md) separately removes Robin's first-annulus gate; its remote CA-selected mixed tail remains open.

The current canonical findings outrank older Mind frontiers where they differ. No adversarial sidecar is currently open; this is not evidence that every finding has completed independent review. NB-350 and WP-441 explicitly retain that limitation. WI-006 records the retraction of the Yang–Yang higher-even-moment and density-one claims: those claims supply no arithmetic input, while its independent replacement identity survives. The [Atlas v1 metrics](graph/atlas/metrics.md) incorporate MC-521 as selected derived telemetry; their unchanged coverage model and older entrypoint narrative are not a complete current frontier audit or evidence of RH progress.

## Cross-line structure

**A proof-operation barrier need not survive a different classical input.** MC-520 bounds `J_mu(X,N)` by `X^(1+epsilon)N` under RH. FD-311's balanced premise forces `J_mu>=XN^(1/2+3c-o(1))`, where `c=1-log_2(3/2)`, on shells with `X<=N^(2+o(1))`. Any fixed `2epsilon<3c-1/2` gives a strict contradiction on the same complete moment. FD-312's separate-endpoint barrier survives because it discards the difference multiplier that MC-520 retains. The [resolved Möbius handoff](mobius_cancellation/clues/CLUE-farey-selected-sliding-mertens-variance.md) and Farey MI-066 record this classical conditional outcome, not a new RH mechanism. Removing RH also removes its shell-localization guarantee unless separately recovered. RE-259 is another successful change of input, but uses a different prime statistic and supplies no Möbius transfer.

**Smooth recovery changes the analytic consequence, not the arithmetic premise.** MC-521 assumes the complete uncentered moment `J(X,X^theta)<<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)H^(2-eta)` at every large real scale, for fixed `0<theta<1`, `0<eta<=1`. Finite correction of smooth weights excludes zeros in `Re s>1-theta eta/2`; the former `max(1-theta eta/2,theta)` boundary belongs to sharp-endpoint reconstruction. No sharp Mertens bound is obtained. PL-465 likewise uses smooth mean-zero recovery, but PL-464's dyadic-only proof retains `max(1-theta/2,theta)` through its block seams. Its best displayed boundary is `2/3`, not a proved optimum. The [dyadic recovery clue](prime_lattice/clues/CLUE-dyadic-variance-smooth-endpoint-recovery.md) isolates that unresolved compatibility test. Unknown means, full within-block variance, all-real versus dyadic scales, and real arithmetic pole residues cannot be interchanged or omitted.

**A relative frame can transmit absolute information without supplying it.** Under PL-465's all-large-real-scale hypothesis `S_X>=c_theta Diag(S_X)`, a unit-modulus character probe has energy at least `c_theta tr(S_X)`. An independent upper bound for any one channel therefore controls identity-residue variance and, through its noncancelling logarithmic-derivative poles, all four zero sets. Writing `Theta_chi` for the supremum of zero real parts gives `Theta_max-Theta_min<=1-theta`. A frame family at fixed exponents approaching one equalizes these suprema; they may still exceed `1/2`. Separately, zeta RH plus the frame at a fixed exponent excludes all mod-5 zeros in `Re s>1-theta/2`. No absolute variance ceiling follows from the frame alone, no GRH-to-frame converse is proved, and the near-one family is not supplied by the existing short-window regime. This narrows the interpretation of the relative route without equating it to the Möbius moment problem.

**Calibration order, phase and noise norm determine the sampling cost.** The [vanishing-slack Jacobi handoff](xi_flow/clues/CLUE-vanishing-calibration-slack-jacobi-row-cost.md) is resolved in XF-201's `epsilon<=Q^(-2)` regime. Complex chords localized within `Theta(Q epsilon)` of the endpoint and clipped complex velocity give matching acquisition bounds; the exact finite-`Q` threshold is retained. A fixed finite bank aliases below that threshold. AF-574 separately prices second-derivative nuisance: its cubic slack margin is certified by three probes, with a tangent-contact span extending beyond the curvature crossing. It is not another derivative-clipping formula. These are different observation models, and neither classification supplies physical Xi access or a stable ordinary-prime decoder.

**Exact signatures and normalized spectra have different stability.** FD-331's exact joint signature determines an excursion permutation, whereas VIS-461 separates the normalized spectral laws of FD-333's specified one-quantum pair. The [spectral handoff](visual_exploration/clues/CLUE-one-quantum-farey-normalized-spectral-separation.md) is resolved for that artificial control, without a finite-prime rate. Independently, FD-336 retains the Brownian spectral null for every block length `1<=b<=Q/log(2Q)`. At the largest admitted length, density-one occurrences of every window depth `h=o(Q/log Q)` survive. Exact complete inventories and denominator seams are not preserved. The [owning Farey clue](farey_discrepancy/clues/CLUE-farey-gap-order-bridge-suppression.md) already carries the stronger-order question. ANF-196 and the notch visualization give a separate resolution warning: qualitative Fourier positivity and nested-grid agreement do not provide a uniform power-rate or sampling certificate.

**Recovery of a whole source space or energy is not a new target estimate.** NB-350's neighboring dilations exactly generate the early-shell kernel, with an explicit finite realization whose cancellation coefficients grow. Full relative excess recovery can coexist with NB-349's vanishing absolute fixed-packet gain; finite approximation rates and moving-packet conditioning remain open. WI-454's coherent differences similarly change the allowed construction before energy is formed, escaping the positive-bank obstruction, but recover precisely the old Hardy energy and mixed pairing. Its exact square-summable scalar-gain criterion is not a Nyman conditioning theorem. The shared strategic conclusion is to demand independent source bounds or target approximation rates, not to merge these different spaces or infer an unproved transfer.

**Existence, critical limits and residual normalization are separate operator gates.** PC-447 rules out closed realizations for its character-rigid measure class before PC-446's spectral question can arise. WP-441 obtains a unique positive completion only after choosing a Gibbs-cost objective; its scalar critical repairs cannot preserve both finite vacuum energy and the needed sign or fixed coefficients. PF-347 excludes the natural symmetric seam alone as a repair of the bounded-band Gram model, while the actual pant/global comparison stays open in the [transition clue](prime_flute/clues/CLUE-positive-width-transition-schur-complement.md). Each obstruction retains its own operator, domain and normalization hypotheses.
