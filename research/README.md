## Cross-line structure

**Finite recovery is separating decisively from infinite continuation.** `AF-289` makes known-prefix acquisition nearly linear, while `NB-066` rules out a bounded-memory conditioning collapse as the explanation for persistent Nyman error. Together with the finite-packet conditioning already available in Xi Flow, this shifts the useful theorem shape: not another injectivity or finite matrix estimate, but a statement that the distinguished target cannot keep escaping as the source horizon grows.

**Representation obstructions are being compressed into smaller source questions.** `MC-239` removes the deep prime-square ramification from the Möbius blind-mode problem, leaving only a squarefree quotient to classify. `WP-268` removes uncertainty about the leading relative size of two natural prime renormalizations, leaving the finite part and critical topology unfixed. These are meaningful simplifications, but neither may be promoted into an endpoint claim: the remaining quotient/sign/topology is exactly where the arithmetic content can still hide.

**Local multiplicity is not global amplification.** `FD-067` shows that adding CRT hosts does not move the Farey exponent because leverage falls at the same rate. `VIS-183` shows that critical-support close pairs matter only through their aggregate weighted mass, and `RE-068` shows that a bounded higher-layer Robin witness can exist without supplying the required global density. The recurring gate is quantitative non-escape: a local source feature must occupy enough of the exact target-selected family to survive normalization and assembly.

**The `continue` lines now need global source-conditioned theorems, not better finite controls.** Analytic Frontier needs continuation to fail at its sharp aperture; Prime Flute needs the real signed forcing to be controlled after full reassembly; Weil Inertia needs one first mode to be incompatible with its whole multi-scale source environment. Those are different operators and no theorem transfers directly between them, but all three are now beyond the stage where another fixed-scale certificate would materially change the ranking.
