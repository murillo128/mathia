These findings strengthen the existing non-character finite--archimedean program; they do not justify merging the lines, because one asks which arithmetic relation survives principal extraction while the other still owes positivity/sign on the surviving completed form.

**A large destination near-kernel is not a mechanism until the source is shown to enter it.** `ANF-155` makes this explicit by proving that the bow has a growing low-spectrum ladder, not merely one tuned near-null character. Nyman and Möbius make the same distinction source-side: a large forced coefficient budget or a rough squarefree support theorem does not by itself control the orientation that the final criterion consumes. The shared lesson is methodological, not a transferable lemma, so it does not warrant a global clue.

**Stable transport is now the main positive currency in Prime Flute, whereas exact recovery remains too weak elsewhere.** `PF-259` supplies a real smoothing margin after the correct intrinsic change of speed measure; the unresolved question is whether its short-time/high-energy part and the finite-seam physical maps preserve that margin. Robin's event ladders and Visual's finite-CUE nuisance modes are smaller-scale instances of the same discipline: identify the exact selected event or null component first, then prove that the genuine source cannot reproduce it within the allowed budget.
