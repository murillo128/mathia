## Cross-line synthesis

The strongest new common boundary is **source breadth must scale in the same currency that the endgame consumes**. Farey now shows that every sublinear prime-prefix breadth can still be defeated by a horizon-dependent source. Prime Circle shows that arbitrarily many fresh-prime channels remain finite-dimensional if the base conductor is fixed. Nyman shows that a linearly large low-spectrum sector may still be almost target-orthogonal. Xi shows that arbitrarily rich finite-horizon observation remains insufficient when the source class allows analytic soft splicing. These are distinct no-go theorems, but together they rule out a broad procedural instinct: adding more coordinates, more auxiliary primes, more low modes, or more finite observation without increasing **source-faithful global information** is not evidence of progress toward RH.

A second convergence is that the three `continue` lines now have unusually small interfaces. Prime Flute needs one-sided graph locality of the actual pre-separation finite-seam conversion; the positive propagation theorem then supplies the needed smoothing. Analytic Frontier has already used genuine all-interval arithmetic cancellation to remove every fixed polylogarithmic endpoint scale and now needs a fixed-power extension. Weil Inertia has exhausted density-matched local screen capacity and now needs a theorem about nonlocal/spacing-surplus/source-side compensation. In all three cases the next result is falsifiable and sits immediately adjacent to the endgame; this, rather than the number of accumulated findings, is why they remain `continue`.

A third boundary concerns **scalarization and principal-mode leakage**. Prime Lattice and Weil Positivity continue to show that introducing rich operator structure after the zeta data have collapsed to a scalar normalizer is too late: programmability or divisor/sign loss reappears. Möbius Cancellation now adds a useful prior-art correction on the arithmetic side. Green--Griffiths control a full twisted Möbius mean by explicitly separating a principal-character contribution, but their theorem does not identify the centered quotient observable with the full twist. The live Möbius question is therefore exactly whether centering removes the principal obstruction cheaply or merely moves the hard principal-mode information into sparse higher correlations.

Visual Exploration supplies the stochastic version of the same discipline. The current terminal companion can be algebraically cleaned at low order, but after that cleaning its information lives in a signed low-frequency statistic whose covariance law has not yet been established. That statistic should be frozen and its null/finite-height transfer proved before fresh zeta data are inspected; otherwise cancellation engineering would only move the look-elsewhere problem into the observable definition.
