## Cross-line structure

### Finite acquisition is becoming cheap; stable target transport is not

`AF-284` materially changes the acquisition baseline. Stable recovery of a known two-prime prefix no longer requires a dense `Theta(N^2)` moment grid: an unweighted `O(N log N)` subset suffices existentially. Xi already has an `O(m)` local known-packet sample bank. Nyman supplies the complementary obstruction: `NB-061` proves that finite-window defect directions are abundant, yet the target question reduces to the finite visible projection `P_(G_R)J_RQe`. These results should not be merged into one theorem, but they jointly show why **recoverability of a finite source description is weaker than stable transport of the target-relevant direction**.

### Sign occurrence, signed amplitude, and source family are different gates

`MC-234` is a useful correction to the previous “parity barrier” language. In the hard Möbius progression family, both signs are already forced to occur by a Linnik-type theorem; what is missing is amplitude control close enough to balance the much larger squarefree support. `WI-251` also needs a signed source estimate, but on a completely different family selected by the first odd Weil zero mode. The shared lesson is only methodological: existence of both signs does not control the signed functional consumed by the target. No cross-line transfer is asserted, and the global source-budget rule forbids importing a cancellation theorem between these families without an explicit bridge.

### Canonical normalization can still fail at the topology layer

`WP-266` resolves one ambiguity and exposes a harder one. Centering by the Hamiltonian diagonal canonically identifies the finite part inside the natural regulator class, so choosing a favorable subtraction constant is no longer the live issue. But exact critical Mangoldt rescaling is nonclosable in that realization. This sharpens the program-level requirement: source identity and even canonical finite normalization must survive in the **same closable topology** in which positivity or inertia is read. It also explains why the proximity of Weil Positivity and Weil Inertia is not yet a valid clue transfer: their current operators and normalizations are not identified by a closable map.

### Fixed-complexity bulk observables keep classicalizing

`PC-270` and `VIS-178` extend the matched-control frontier in complementary ways. Prime Circle shows that even a fixed exact bounded resonance produces only one-torus periodic-Haar bulk IDS; Visual shows that fixed finite moment panels remain matched nulls well into polynomial support, with a precise parity-dependent wedge. Analytic Frontier already has an explicit growing-complexity aperture for its adversarial endpoint packet. The useful frontier across these lines is therefore moving toward **complexity that grows with scale, microscopic/subleading information, discrete or rare-event structure, or a source identity that a fixed representation cannot imitate**.

### Local source rigidity or amplification still has to survive global assembly

Farey, Robin, and Prime Flute now expose this boundary in different currencies. `FD-062` can conditionally move a growing packet below the old deterministic host scale, but the global denominator still blocks positive occupation. `RE-063` makes selected fringe hosts extremely sparse and structurally rigid, but sparse forced successors need not accumulate with the required sign. `PF-300` gives an exact scalar accumulation law, but the physical mixed `P/H` return may redistribute or cancel that scalar loss. More local structure is valuable only when it changes the global denominator, propagation law, or full response consumed by the endgame.
