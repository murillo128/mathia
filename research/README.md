# Mathia Research State

## Global picture

The common RH-facing gap remains an independently source-forced estimate in the precise destination norm that can exclude each off-critical zero. Source reconstruction, auxiliary positivity, available operator witnesses and artificial spectral separation do not provide that estimate. No Mathia finding proves RH.

[MC-522](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-522-centered-dyadic-variance-recovers-all-reciprocal-zeta-poles.md) recovers all reciprocal-zeta poles from complete centered variance at every large dyadic block. [MC-524](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-524-density-one-window-control-coexists-with-prescribed-sparse-poles.md) shows that meromorphic continuation and sparse poles do not by themselves repair density-one coverage: a signed square-free-supported control passes the retained complete window bounds while keeping two prescribed off-critical poles. Its moving profiles evade the fixed-profile natural boundary in [PL-468](prime_lattice/findings/PL-468-fixed-profile-sparse-overwrites-force-a-natural-boundary.md), but use a different, more frequently omitted mesh. Positive residue-source compatibility and the thinner Prime-Lattice mesh remain separate tests. The balanced Farey variance target remains **classically resolved under RH** by [MC-520](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-520-rh-mellin-variance-closes-conditional-farey-window-target.md); independent continuation needs weaker hypotheses and justified carrying windows. [RE-259](robin_extremal/findings/RE-259-zero-density-removes-critical-robin-moment-boundary.md) removes Robin's first annulus but leaves the CA-selected remote mixed tail open.

The current canonical findings outrank older Mind frontiers where they differ. No adversarial sidecar is currently open; this is not independent approval of the corpus. WI-002 has been withdrawn. [WI-456](weil_inertia/findings/WI-456-retracted-moment-transport-leaves-only-a-conditional-density-capstone.md) replaces its obsolete live-candidate assessment with a precise conditional moment-to-density theorem. The Yang–Yang arithmetic transport is retracted; neither model certificates nor the surviving conditional theorem restore it. Even a valid density-one theorem leaves individual exceptional zeros possible. The [Atlas v1 metrics](graph/atlas/metrics.md) include the strengthened AF-577/XF-205 calibration boundaries in their selective telemetry. Their unchanged coverage model and older entrypoint narrative do not cover the whole current source frontier; portfolio judgments below rest on canonical evidence.

## Cross-line structure

**A proof-operation barrier need not survive a different classical input.** MC-520 bounds `J_mu(X,N)` by `X^(1+epsilon)N` under RH. FD-311's balanced premise forces `J_mu>=XN^(1/2+3c-o(1))`, where `c=1-log_2(3/2)`, on shells with `X<=N^(2+o(1))`. Any fixed `2epsilon<3c-1/2` gives a strict contradiction on the same complete moment. FD-312's separate-endpoint barrier survives because it discards the difference multiplier that MC-520 retains. The [resolved Möbius handoff](mobius_cancellation/clues/CLUE-farey-selected-sliding-mertens-variance.md) and Farey MI-066 record this classical conditional outcome, not a new RH mechanism. Removing RH also removes its shell-localization guarantee unless separately recovered. RE-259 is another successful change of input, but uses a different prime statistic and supplies no Möbius transfer.

**Smooth recovery changes the analytic consequence, not the arithmetic premise.** MC-521 assumes the complete uncentered moment `J(X,X^theta)<<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)H^(2-eta)` at every large real scale, for fixed `0<theta<1`, `0<eta<=1`. Finite correction of smooth weights excludes zeros in `Re s>alpha=1-theta eta/2`. Its classical logarithm/reciprocal-zeta estimate followed by Perron now also gives `M(x)<<_epsilon x^(alpha+epsilon)`; the generic bounded-coefficient smooth argument alone does not give that sharp cutoff.

PL-466 resolves the [dyadic seam clue](prime_lattice/clues/CLUE-dyadic-variance-smooth-endpoint-recovery.md) for empirical identity-residue prime variance `V_j<<X_j H_j(log X_j)^A`, with `X_j=2^j`, `H_j=floor X_j^theta` and fixed finite `A>=0`. Seam-vanishing finite Fourier weights preserve the periodic unknown-mean factor. Sublinear arithmetic zero density excludes its pole progressions and then the polynomial residue sequences hidden by the filter, reaching `Re s>1-theta/2`. The positive dyadic density control still passes the variance bound and fails the pole-sparsity step. This is an arithmetic recovery theorem, not generic interpolation from sparse scales. The [Möbius handoff](mobius_cancellation/clues/CLUE-dyadic-centered-mobius-pole-recovery.md) is resolved by MC-522: under `V_j<<_epsilon X_j^(1+epsilon)H_j`, the finite recurrence acts on whole Laurent principal parts, so it detects multiple poles even when their residues vanish. It gives the same zero-free boundary and, using the classical reciprocal-zeta growth step, `M(x)<<_epsilon x^(1-theta/2+epsilon)`. Every sufficiently large dyadic block is required. Arbitrary selected subsequences and independent bounds for either source variance remain open. A centered bound on a Farey carrying shell would also need its mean contribution restored before the uncentered FD-311 destination can use it.

[MC-523](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-523-density-one-dyadic-window-control-permits-near-linear-prefixes.md) rules out replacing that coverage by mesh density alone. Its nonmultiplicative control has exact square-free support, all qualitative fixed-shift Chowla correlations and `J_a(X,H)<=16XH log X` for every integer `1<=H<=X` on retained dyadic blocks, with no exceptional starts. Only `O(log J)` indices up to `J` are omitted, with successive retained scales at most a factor eight apart, yet some prefixes have size `>>T/log T`. Arithmetic continuation and sparse poles are not supplied for this control. The [accepted density-one recovery clue](prime_lattice/clues/CLUE-density-one-dyadic-variance-arithmetic-recovery.md) retains this explicit thin mesh. PL-468 realizes a positive fixed-profile overwrite with the retained ceiling, but proves that its scalar lacunary Mellin term has a natural boundary, even for meromorphic continuation. This rejects that recipe, not the actual-prime implication.

[MC-524](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-524-density-one-window-control-coexists-with-prescribed-sparse-poles.md) instead uses `j_k=floor(k log k)`, omitting `O(J/log J)` indices up to `J`, still with no omission run longer than two. Growing-degree signed moment matching yields profiles of amplitude `x^(beta-1+o(1))` and an entire transform error. The fixed sequence has exact square-free support, every qualitative fixed-shift Chowla correlation, the retained complete window ceiling, and precisely the simple poles `beta +/- i gamma` on `Re s>1/2`. Thus continuation and sparse poles alone are insufficient for density-one recovery in this signed class. No Euler product, functional equation or reciprocal-zeta vertical-growth estimate is supplied. The [proposed positive-source transfer](prime_lattice/clues/CLUE-variable-profile-sparse-poles-on-moderate-mesh.md) tests residue positivity and negative real pole residues on this moderately sparse mesh. It does not resolve the accepted `3^k`-mesh clue. The common bottleneck is control of the omitted **variable-profile** Mellin remainder, not a scalar gap theorem inferred from index density.

**A relative frame can transmit absolute information without supplying it.** Under PL-465's all-large-real-scale hypothesis `S_X>=c_theta Diag(S_X)`, a unit-modulus character probe has energy at least `c_theta tr(S_X)`. An independent upper bound for any one channel therefore controls identity-residue variance and, through its noncancelling logarithmic-derivative poles, all four zero sets. Writing `Theta_chi` for the supremum of zero real parts gives `Theta_max-Theta_min<=1-theta`. A frame family at fixed exponents approaching one equalizes these suprema; they may still exceed `1/2`. Separately, zeta RH plus the frame at a fixed exponent excludes all mod-5 zeros in `Re s>1-theta/2`. No absolute variance ceiling follows from the frame alone, no GRH-to-frame converse is proved, and the near-one family is not supplied by the existing short-window regime. PL-467 makes that distinction concrete: one bounded positive integer-supported control has the `1/4` relative frame at all sufficiently large real scales for each fixed `1-beta<theta<1`, while every character has a finite symmetric pole set with common right frontier `beta>1/2`. Its centered variance is `asymp H^2 X^(2beta-1)`, exceeding the absolute ceiling when `beta>1-theta/2`. It lacks prime support, an Euler product and a completed functional equation. Pole sparsity repairs lost information after an absolute estimate; it does not turn a relative floor into that estimate.

Both testing and interpolation mechanisms have registered classical prior art. Separated vectors can have equal squared norms: genuine linear-band **energy-share** transfer remains open, and no failure at every positive linear cutoff is proved.
