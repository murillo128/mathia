# Mathia Research State

## Global picture

The common RH-facing gap remains an independently source-forced estimate in the precise norm used by the destination. Reconstruction, auxiliary positivity and existence of a zero-sensitive test do not supply that estimate. No current Mathia finding proves RH.

[MC-539](mobius_cancellation/findings/MC-539-two-random-starts-detect-mobius-poles-on-fourth-power-scales.md) and [PL-484](prime_lattice/findings/PL-484-two-random-starts-detect-prime-poles-on-fourth-power-scales.md) recover the respective RH and joint mod-5 GRH implications from two random starts per channel on every sufficiently late prescribed fourth-power scale. Their energy ceilings remain unproved. Enlarged square-free or positive prime-supported source classes still hide dispersion under faster sampling; those controls do not carry all canonical arithmetic constraints.

The strongest new distinction is between a representation's existence and the cost of accessing it. NB-371 forces divergent coefficients when finite dilation sums approximate the projected Nyman target, despite NB-370's adaptive component gain. PC-470 gives an attainable relative-entropy price for selecting rooted cotangent limits: bounded cost in units of `log log y` permits only finite supports almost surely. Neither result selects an arithmetic mechanism. PL-486 separately excludes universal subunit-order Mellin filtering as a way to preserve one fixed box range; specially chosen compatible tests remain open. WI-472 enlarges the sufficient fixed-test Euler sampling class by retaining kernel decay, while leaving shrinking thresholds and independent defect control unresolved.

WI-002 remains withdrawn, and [WI-456](weil_inertia/findings/WI-456-retracted-moment-transport-leaves-only-a-conditional-density-capstone.md) retains only a conditional moment-to-density theorem after retraction of the Yang–Yang arithmetic transport. No review sidecar is currently open; absence of a sidecar is not independent approval. The [Atlas v1 metrics](graph/atlas/metrics.md) and [frontier telemetry](graph/atlas/telemetry/frontier-summary.md) both distinguish NB-370's adaptive-selection extension from NB-371's coefficient barrier. Their retained episodes are selective and the Atlas entrypoint has an older source window; this is not a current whole-portfolio saturation assessment. No allocation conclusion here relies on those rates. Recommendations here use canonical findings, which also outrank older local Mind frontiers.
