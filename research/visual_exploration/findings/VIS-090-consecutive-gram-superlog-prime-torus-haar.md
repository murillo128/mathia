# VIS-090 — superlogarithmic consecutive Gram blocks are Haar on the prime torus

## Claim

Let `g_k` be the Gram points, defined for all sufficiently large `k` by

`theta(g_k)=pi k`,

up to the harmless conventional integer shift in the Gram indexing. Let

`Omega = product_(p prime) {z in C : |z|=1}`

with product Haar probability measure `m_H`, and define the consecutive-block empirical measure

`Q_(N,M) = (1/(M+1)) sum_(k=N)^(N+M) delta_((p^(-i g_k))_p)`.

Assume

`1 <= M=M(N) <= N-1`

and

`M/log N -> infinity`.

Then

`Q_(N,M) => m_H`

weakly on `Omega` as `N->infinity`.

More quantitatively, for every fixed nontrivial character

`chi_m(omega)=product_p omega(p)^(m_p)`,

where the integer vector `m=(m_p)` has finite support, one has

`| integral chi_m dQ_(N,M) | <<_m (log N)/(M+1)`.

Equivalently, every fixed finite prime-phase projection of a consecutive Gram block is Haar-equidistributed once the block length is superlogarithmic in its starting Gram index.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + CLASSICAL-FOURIER/EQUIDISTRIBUTION + NEGATIVE/CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This is a sampling-law control, not a new Gram-point equidistribution theorem. The short-interval Gram-point universality literature already contains the same Fourier estimate in a more general setting; the point here is to isolate its sharp visual consequence and combine it with `VIS-084`.

## 1. Every fixed prime-torus character has only logarithmic exponential-sum cost

Write `g(x)` for the continuous inverse of the Riemann-Siegel theta function on the eventual monotone branch,

`theta(g(x))=pi x`.

The standard theta asymptotics give

`theta'(t) = (1/2) log(t/(2 pi)) + O(t^(-2))`,

`theta''(t) = 1/(2t) + O(t^(-3))`.

Hence `theta'` is eventually positive and increasing, while

`g'(x)=pi/theta'(g(x))`

is eventually positive and decreasing.

Fix a nonzero character `m`. Its scalar frequency is

`a_m = sum_p m_p log p = log(product_p p^(m_p))`.

Unique factorization gives `a_m != 0`. The character evaluated at the Gram-phase point is

`chi_m((p^(-i g_k))_p) = exp(-i a_m g_k)`.

Set

`f_m(x) = -a_m g(x)/(2 pi)`.

Then

`f_m'(x) = -a_m/[2 theta'(g(x))]`.

For sufficiently large `N`, `f_m'` has fixed sign, is monotone, and has magnitude below `1/2`. On `N<=x<=N+M` with `M<=N-1`, the endpoint heights satisfy `log g(x) asymp log N`, so

`min_(N<=x<=N+M) |f_m'(x)| asymp_m 1/log N`.

The classical monotone first-derivative exponential-sum estimate therefore gives

`| sum_(k=N)^(N+M) exp(2 pi i f_m(k)) | <<_m log N`.

After division by `M+1`,

`| integral chi_m dQ_(N,M) | <<_m (log N)/(M+1)`.

Thus every fixed nontrivial Fourier coefficient vanishes whenever `M/log N -> infinity`.

## 2. Fourier convergence gives Haar on the whole infinite prime torus

The continuous characters of `Omega` are exactly the finite-support integer vectors above. The trivial character has Fourier coefficient one, while the preceding estimate sends every nontrivial fixed character to zero.

By the standard Fourier/Weyl criterion on compact abelian groups, these character limits determine weak convergence to Haar measure:

`Q_(N,M) => m_H`.

The infinite-torus formulation does **not** supply uniformity over characters whose support or coefficients change with `N`. It says that every fixed continuous cylinder observable, and by weak convergence every fixed continuous observable on the compact product torus, has the Haar population limit. It does not justify replacing an `N`-dependent growing-dimensional statistic by Haar without a separate uniform argument.

## 3. The raw consecutive-block population law is now classified across the Gram spacing scale

`VIS-084` gives the complementary local picture. If `H=o(log g_N)`, every fixed finite prime-phase block freezes to its starting point. At `H=Theta(log g_N)`, the first order motion is a deterministic one-parameter torus arc generated by the inverse Gram clock; this is the first order-one phase-motion scale, not a mixing theorem.

Since `log g_N ~ log N`, the present result supplies the third regime:

- sublogarithmic blocks: deterministic freezing;
- logarithmic blocks: deterministic local Gram-clock arc;
- superlogarithmic consecutive blocks with `M<=N-1`: Haar population law on every fixed prime torus, indeed weak Haar convergence on `Omega`.

Therefore simply lengthening a consecutive Gram block from the local regime toward a nearly linear block does not create a new raw fixed-prime population channel. The sampling geometry interpolates from a point to a deterministic arc and then to the same Haar torus already seen globally in `VIS-083`.

This does not say that the **ordered path** through the block is Haar-random, mixing, or independent. Empirical population equidistribution forgets temporal order. A statistic based on increments, long-range ordering, a growing prime support, or an additional analytic coordinate is a different object.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The global Gram-point prime-phase law is established prior art and is already recorded in `VIS-083`. Antanas Laurinčikas, **Joint Discrete Approximation of Analytic Functions by Shifts of the Riemann Zeta-Function Twisted by Gram Points**, *Mathematics* 11:3 (2023), 565, DOI `10.3390/math11030565`, works with the prime-phase torus behind Gram-point discrete universality.

More directly, Antanas Laurinčikas, **Joint Discrete Approximation of Analytic Functions by Shifts of the Riemann Zeta Function Twisted by Gram Points II**, *Axioms* 12:5 (2023), 426, DOI `10.3390/axioms12050426`, proves a short-interval weak-convergence input on the infinite prime torus under hypotheses strong enough for the accompanying zeta-universality argument. In the proof of its Lemma 6, the displayed Fourier estimate (19) has the form

`F_(N,M,alpha) << (1/M) N^(1-alpha_1) log N`.

For the single Gram exponent `alpha_1=1`, this is exactly the `O(log N/M)` character scale isolated above. The paper imposes a much larger lower bound on `M` because later analytic-function approximation requires additional mean-square control; the elementary prime-torus Fourier step itself already exposes the logarithmic threshold.

Accordingly **no novelty is claimed** for the equidistribution mechanism or bound. The durable Mathia contribution is the research-control synthesis: together with `VIS-084`, it closes the apparent raw fixed-prime consecutive-block escape route on both sides of the logarithmic Gram scale.

## Boundary and falsification

The quantitative bound is character-by-character. It is not uniform over prime support or Fourier complexity growing with `N`; such growth can invalidate a naive Haar replacement even though every fixed character converges.

The result concerns the empirical population of **consecutive** Gram indices and assumes `M<=N-1` only to keep the elementary uniform logarithmic comparison transparent. Nonconsecutive sampling, selected subsequences, genuinely path-sensitive observables, or a different long-range ordering question are not classified by this population theorem.

No statement is made about `Z(g_k)`, derivatives of zeta, zero occupancy, nearby-zero geometry, hybrid prime/zero data, or independence between those additional coordinates and the prime torus. No zeta approximation theorem or RH consequence follows.

Falsify the derivation by breaking the eventual theta derivative monotonicity/asymptotics, the first-derivative exponential-sum estimate, unique-factorization nonvanishing of `a_m`, or the compact-group Fourier criterion. A source-level challenge should also compare the claimed `O(log N/M)` specialization against the short-interval Fourier computation in Laurinčikas (2023).

## Visual consequence

No canonical PNG is retained. A finite Gram-torus scatter would be weaker than the quantitative Fourier bound and could misleadingly emphasize finite discrepancy. The useful visual statement is the exact scale classification: cluster, deterministic arc, then Haar population.

## Research consequence

The accepted `CLUE-zeta-prime-phase-recursive-geometry` should be narrowed again. Consecutive Gram blocks with fixed prime support do not gain a new raw population law by entering a superlogarithmic or nearly linear regime: once `M/log N -> infinity` they return to Haar.

A surviving Gram-based route must therefore use information not erased by this empirical-measure control — for example an ordered/path-sensitive statistic with its own sampling theorem, support that grows with height under a uniform analysis, a genuinely nonconsecutive sampling mechanism, or an independently informative Gram/zero coordinate — rather than treating longer consecutive prime-phase clouds as a new source law.
