# VIS-155 — critical fixed irregular Gram lags are Haar fibers over one deterministic clock curve

## Claim

Let `g_k` be the Gram points and

`z_k=(p^(-i g_k))_p in Omega`,

where `Omega` is the infinite prime torus. Fix `r>=2` and distinct positive integers

`1 <= b_1 < ... < b_r`.

For an integer step `h=h(N)` with `b_r h=o(N)`, define

`V_(k,h,b)=(Delta_(k,b_1 h),...,Delta_(k,b_r h)) in Omega^r`,

`Delta_(k,ell)=z_(k+ell) overline(z_k)`,

for `N<=k<=2N-b_r h`, and put `M_N=N-b_r h+1`, `L_N=log N`.

Assume the critical irregular-lag scaling

`h^r/[N^(r-1)L_N^2] -> lambda`,

with `0<lambda<infinity`.

Let `c=(c_1,...,c_r) in Z^r` be the primitive integer generator, oriented as below, of the one-dimensional moment-null lattice

`L_b={v in Z^r : sum_j v_j b_j^q=0 for q=1,...,r-1}`.

Set

`C_b=sum_j c_j b_j^r > 0`

and define the quotient homomorphism

`pi_c: Omega^r -> Omega`,

`pi_c(omega_1,...,omega_r)=product_j omega_j^(c_j)`.

Because `c` is primitive, `pi_c` is surjective. Let `H_c=ker(pi_c)` and let `m_(H_c)` be its Haar probability measure. Define

`kappa_(r,b)=2 pi (-1)^(r-1) C_b/[r(r-1)]`

and let `nu_(r,b,lambda)` be the pushforward of normalized Lebesgue measure on `[1,2]` by the deterministic prime-torus curve

`Gamma_(r,b,lambda)(u)`
` = (exp(-i kappa_(r,b) lambda log(p) u^(1-r)))_p`.

Then the empirical law

`mu_N=(1/M_N) sum_(k=N)^(2N-b_r h) delta_(V_(k,h,b))`

converges weakly to the unique probability measure `mu_(r,b,lambda)` on `Omega^r` whose quotient by `pi_c` is `nu_(r,b,lambda)` and whose conditional measure on every quotient fiber is Haar. Equivalently, if `s_c:Omega->Omega^r` is any integer-power section of `pi_c`,

`mu_(r,b,lambda)=m_(H_c) * (s_c)_* nu_(r,b,lambda)`.

Equivalently again, extend the primitive row `c` to any unimodular matrix `U in GL_r(Z)` and let `T_U` be the corresponding torus automorphism. Then

`(T_U)_* mu_N -> H_Omega^(tensor(r-1)) tensor nu_(r,b,lambda)`.

Thus the critical window left open by `VIS-154` is also completely generated by the deterministic inverse Gram clock. Fixed irregular commensurate lag geometry has the same three-way classification as the equal-step grid: a deepest clock character is coherent below the threshold, the critical law is Haar in all transverse directions over one deterministic clock curve, and the full lag population is Haar above the threshold.

For the equal-step nodes `b_j=j`, the primitive vector is

`c_j=(-1)^(r-j) binom(r,j)`,

`C_b=r!`,

so `kappa_(r,b)=2 pi (-1)^(r-1)(r-2)!` and this result reduces exactly to `VIS-153`.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-GRAM-ASYMPTOTIC + LATTICE/FOURIER CONTROL + NEGATIVE/CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## 1. The deepest irregular cancellation is a canonical primitive quotient

The integer moment matrix

`A_b=(b_j^q)_(1<=q<=r-1,1<=j<=r)`

has rank `r-1` over `Q` because the `b_j` are distinct and nonzero. Hence its rational kernel is one-dimensional, and the integer kernel `L_b=ker(A_b) cap Z^r` is generated by one primitive vector `c`, unique up to sign.

The Lagrange construction used in `VIS-154` makes this vector explicit. If

`P(t)=product_(ell=1)^r(t-b_ell)`

and

`w_j=1/[b_j P'(b_j)]`,

then

`sum_j w_j b_j^q=0`, `q=1,...,r-1`,

and

`sum_j w_j b_j^r=1`.

Clear denominators and divide by the gcd to obtain the primitive integer vector `c`; choose its sign so that

`C_b=sum_j c_j b_j^r>0`.

The corresponding product coordinate `pi_c` is canonical up to inversion. Primitivity is important: there exist integers `d_j` with

`sum_j c_j d_j=1`,

so

`s_c(omega)=(omega^(d_1),...,omega^(d_r))`

is a continuous group section of `pi_c`. Therefore `Omega^r` splits as a compact group into the Haar fiber `H_c` plus one quotient coordinate, although a basis built from all successive raw moment conditions need not itself be unimodular.

This isolates the only character direction capable of cancelling all moments below order `r` without introducing any finite-index ambiguity into the final probability law.

## 2. The quotient coordinate converges to one deterministic clock curve

At a fixed prime `p`,

`pi_c(V_(k,h,b))(p)`
` = exp(-i log(p) G_(h,b)(k))`,

where

`G_(h,b)(x)=sum_j c_j[g(x+b_j h)-g(x)]`

and `g(x)` is the smooth eventual inverse of `theta(g(x))=pi x`.

Taylor expansion is uniform on the dyadic sample block because the nodes are fixed and `h=o(N)`. The first `r-1` moments vanish, so

`G_(h,b)(x)`
` = (C_b/r!) h^r g^(r)(x) + O_b(h^(r+1) sup_(y~N)|g^(r+1)(y)|)`.

The remainder is `o(h^r g^(r)(x))` uniformly because `h/N->0`. The fixed-order inverse-Gram asymptotic used in `VIS-153`--`VIS-154` gives, for `r>=2`,

`g^(r)(Nu)`
` = [2 pi (-1)^(r-1)(r-2)!]/[N^(r-1)L_N^2] u^(1-r)(1+o(1))`

uniformly for `u in [1,2]`.

Under the critical normalization,

`G_(h,b)(Nu) -> kappa_(r,b) lambda u^(1-r)`

uniformly, since

`(C_b/r!) 2 pi (-1)^(r-1)(r-2)!`
` = 2 pi (-1)^(r-1) C_b/[r(r-1)]`.

Hence every fixed finite-prime continuous test of `pi_c(V_(k,h,b))` becomes a Riemann sum, and

`(pi_c)_* mu_N -> nu_(r,b,lambda)`.

The irregular node geometry changes only the integer `C_b`, and therefore the winding speed of the surviving deterministic clock curve.

## 3. Every transverse character is washed out at the critical scale

It remains to identify the full law rather than only its quotient.

Take a fixed character of `Omega^r`, described by finitely supported integers `m_(j,p)`. For each prime let

`m_p=(m_(1,p),...,m_(r,p)) in Z^r`.

Its phase along `V_(k,h,b)` can be written

`F_h(x)=sum_j a_j[g(x+b_j h)-g(x)]`,

where

`a_j=sum_p m_(j,p) log p`.

Define the moment coefficients

`D_(p,q)=sum_j m_(j,p)b_j^q in Z`,

`S_q=sum_j a_j b_j^q=sum_p D_(p,q) log p`.

Unique factorization implies rational linear independence of the prime logarithms, so `S_q=0` exactly when `D_(p,q)=0` for every prime in the character support.

If the character is not a pullback through `pi_c`, then some `m_p` is not an integer multiple of `c`. Since `c` generates the complete integer kernel of the first `r-1` moment rows, there is a first order

`q_0<=r-1`

with `S_(q_0)!=0`. The same fixed-character expansion as in `VIS-154` gives

`F_h'(x)`
` = [S_(q_0)/q_0!] h^(q_0) g^(q_0+1)(x)(1+o(1))`

uniformly, with eventual one-sign monotonicity. The total shear scale is

`h^(q_0)/[N^(q_0-1)L_N^2]`
` = lambda^(q_0/r) (N/L_N^2)^(1-q_0/r)(1+o(1))`,

which diverges because `q_0<r`. The first-derivative exponential-sum estimate therefore yields

`int Chi dmu_N -> 0`

for every character that is nontrivial on the Haar fiber `H_c`.

If instead the character is trivial on `H_c`, then for each prime

`m_p=n_p c`

with integers `n_p`, so the character is exactly a pullback of a character of the quotient `Omega`. Put

`B=sum_p n_p log p`.

Section 2 gives

`int Chi dmu_N`
` -> integral_1^2 exp(-i B kappa_(r,b) lambda u^(1-r)) du`,

which is precisely the corresponding Fourier coefficient of `nu_(r,b,lambda)`.

These two cases determine all Fourier coefficients on the compact abelian group `Omega^r`. Therefore

`mu_N -> m_(H_c) * (s_c)_*nu_(r,b,lambda)`.

This also proves the product formulation under any unimodular extension `U` whose last row is `c`: all characters involving one of the first `r-1` transformed coordinates vanish, while characters using only the last coordinate converge to the deterministic curve coefficients.

## 4. The apparent finite-index complication is a coordinate artifact

`VIS-154` left the critical irregular-node law open because a full basis made from successive moment-cancellation vectors can have nontrivial finite index in `Z^r`. That observation is correct for that particular adapted basis, but it is not an obstruction to classifying the probability law.

The critical law only needs the **primitive deepest cancellation direction**. Every primitive integer vector extends to a `GL_r(Z)` basis, even if the other basis rows no longer coincide with lower-order moment filters. Fourier cancellation then supplies the missing statement: every character transverse to the deepest direction has some first nonzero moment below order `r`, and is therefore Haar-averaged at the critical scale.

So the critical geometry has a canonical coordinate-free description: Haar measure on the codimension-one kernel of the deepest product character, fibered over a single inverse-clock curve. Equal spacing is merely the special case where the entire filtration can be represented simultaneously by the especially transparent Newton-difference automorphism.

For example, with nodes `(1,3)` the primitive deepest character is `c=(-3,1)`, `C_b=6`; at the critical scale `h^2/(N log^2 N)->lambda`, the quotient curve is

`p -> exp(6 pi i lambda log(p)/u)`.

The picture differs from the `(1,2)` Newton curve only by the node-dependent deterministic winding factor. It is not a new arithmetic source.

## Boundary and falsification

The result fixes `r`, the distinct positive integer node set `{b_1,...,b_r}`, and every tested torus character while `N->infinity`. It gives no quantitative uniformity when the node set, dimension, prime support, Fourier complexity, or character coefficients change with `N`.

The nodes are commensurate integer multiples of one step `h`. Genuinely noncommensurate real-time offsets are outside the construction. Selected or nonconsecutive Gram starting indices also change the sampling law and are not covered.

The theorem concerns the prime-phase lag torus only. It does not establish independence or dependence for `Z(g_k)`, zeta values, derivatives, zero occupancy, nearby-zero geometry, or any other analytic coordinate.

Falsify the claim by finding a character transverse to `L_b` whose first `r-1` integer moments all vanish, invalidating the uniform order-`r` Taylor/inverse-clock limit under the stated hypotheses, or producing a transverse fixed character whose normalized exponential sum does not vanish at the critical scale.
