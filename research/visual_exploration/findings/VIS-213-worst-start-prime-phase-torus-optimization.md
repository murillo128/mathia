# VIS-213 — pointwise worst starts are exactly a prime-phase torus optimization

## Claim

Keep the tapered quadratic statistic and notation of `VIS-192` and `VIS-193`. For fixed `y` and `H`, write

`F_(y,H)(T)=M_v(y;H,T)-1`

and

`c_(p,q)=Q_y^(-1) p^(-alpha) q^(-alpha) kappa_v(H log(q/p))`, `p<q<=y`.

Let `P_y` be the primes at most `y`, and define the Hermitian matrix `A_(y,H)` indexed by `P_y` by

`A_(p,p)=0`,
`A_(p,q)=c_(p,q)` for `p<q`,
`A_(q,p)=conjugate(c_(p,q))`.

For `z=(z_p)_(p in P_y)` on the prime torus `T^|P_y|`, put

`G_(y,H)(z)=z^* A_(y,H) z`.

Then `G_(y,H)` is real-valued and

`F_(y,H)(T)=G_(y,H)((exp(i T log p))_(p<=y))`.

Because the finite prime-log flow is dense in the full prime torus,

`sup_(T in R) |F_(y,H)(T)| = max_(|z_p|=1) |z^* A_(y,H) z|`.

The same supremum is obtained after discarding any bounded initial height interval: for every real `T_0`,

`sup_(T>=T_0) |F_(y,H)(T)| = max_(|z_p|=1) |z^* A_(y,H) z|`.

Thus the fixed-`(y,H)` pointwise worst-start problem is **exactly** a unit-modulus quadratic optimization problem on the prime-phase torus. Recurrence itself is not the missing ingredient: every torus phase configuration, including configurations arbitrarily close to a maximizer, is revisited at arbitrarily large starts. The genuinely quantitative questions are how the torus optimum scales as `y,H` grow and how long one must wait to approach its maximizing region.

Moreover the Haar mean square of the same torus quadratic form is exactly the long-start variance from `VIS-192`,

`int_(T^|P_y|) |G_(y,H)(z)|^2 dz = R_v(y,H)`.

Consequently

`sup_T |M_v(y;H,T)-1| >= sqrt(R_v(y,H))`.

In the strictly subcritical regime of `VIS-192`, where `H log y/y -> 0`, this gives the unconditional scale lower bound

`sup_T |M_v(y;H,T)-1| >>_(alpha,v) H^(-1/2)`.

This does **not** prove a macroscopic exceptional start when `H->infinity`; it only shows that no uniform pointwise bound can improve below the root-mean-square scale without additional structure.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL-KRONECKER REDUCTION + UNIMODULAR-QUADRATIC FORMULATION + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## 1. The start variable is a one-parameter orbit inside the prime torus

Set

`z_p(T)=exp(i T log p)`.

Then

`conjugate(z_p(T)) z_q(T)=exp(i T log(q/p))`.

Using the coefficient representation from `VIS-192`,

`F_(y,H)(T)`
` = 2 Re sum_(p<q<=y) c_(p,q) exp(i T log(q/p))`
` = z(T)^* A_(y,H) z(T)`.

The matrix is Hermitian by construction, so the last quantity is real. This is only a change of representation: no random-phase model or independence assumption has been introduced.

The geometry is useful because the pair-frequency family `log(q/p)` is generated by vertex phases `log p`. The apparent `Theta(pi(y)^2)` family of pair phases therefore lives on a torus of dimension only `pi(y)`, with all edge phases constrained to be ratios `z_q conjugate(z_p)`.

## 2. Kronecker density turns the orbit supremum into the full torus maximum

For a finite set of distinct primes, an integer relation

`sum_(p<=y) m_p log p = 0`

would imply

`prod_(p<=y) p^(m_p)=1`,

and unique factorization forces every `m_p=0`. Hence the prime logarithms are rationally independent.

`VIS-071` already records the corresponding Kronecker--Weyl conclusion: the continuous orbit

`T -> (exp(i T log p))_(p<=y)`

is equidistributed, and in particular dense, in the full finite prime torus.

Since `z -> |z^* A z|` is continuous on a compact torus, it attains a maximum `M`. Density gives orbit points arbitrarily close to a maximizer, so

`sup_T |F_(y,H)(T)|=M`.

The translated tail orbit starting at any `T_0` is again dense: equivalently, the Kronecker flow is a translation on the same compact group and `VIS-071`'s equidistribution is unchanged by a fixed initial phase. Therefore the same supremum is approachable at arbitrarily large starts.

This is stronger than merely saying that exceptional finite-support phase recurrences exist. It identifies **which** optimization controls their largest possible effect on the actual tapered statistic.

## 3. The torus objective is a phase-synchronization / unimodular quadratic problem

The deterministic optimization

`max_(|z_p|=1) |z^* A_(y,H) z|`

is a standard unit-modulus constrained quadratic form. In optimization language this lies in the family of unimodular quadratic programs; closely related Hermitian unit-circle objectives are also the basic nonconvex form appearing in phase synchronization.

This vocabulary does not supply a solution to the Mathia problem. It clarifies the correct finite-dimensional geometry: the edge coefficient `c_(p,q)` asks for a preferred relative phase between prime vertices `p` and `q`, while a single vertex phase assignment must satisfy all such preferences simultaneously. Any incompatible phase accumulation around cycles is therefore a genuine **frustration** of coherent alignment, whereas an edge-phase pattern close to a vertex coboundary can support a much larger torus optimum.

Thus the pointwise problem has two distinct components that should not be conflated:

- **amplitude/frustration:** how large is the unit-modulus quadratic optimum for the weighted prime graph?
- **access time:** how large a start `T` is required for the one-parameter prime-log orbit to enter a prescribed neighborhood of a near-maximizing torus phase assignment?

The first is a finite weighted-graph optimization problem. The second is a quantitative simultaneous Diophantine-approximation problem. Kronecker density settles existence at fixed dimension but gives no useful uniform growing-`y` access time by itself.

## 4. The long-start variance is the Haar `L^2` norm of the same objective

`VIS-071` identifies long vertical averaging of every fixed finite prime-phase observable with Haar averaging on the prime torus. Applying it to the continuous function `|G_(y,H)|^2`, or equivalently using the exact orthogonality calculation already persisted in `VIS-192`, gives

`int |G_(y,H)(z)|^2 dz = R_v(y,H)`.

On a probability space, `||G||_infinity >= ||G||_2`, hence

`max_(|z_p|=1) |z^* A z| >= sqrt(R_v(y,H))`.

Combined with `VIS-192`, this yields `sup_T |F(T)| >> H^(-1/2)` whenever `H log y/y ->0`. The conclusion is deliberately modest. It says the worst start cannot be uniformly smaller than the typical root-mean-square scale; it does not show that the torus optimization amplifies the statistic to order one.

Conversely, `VIS-193` proves that every start interval of length comparable to `y^2` has small **mean square** when `H->infinity`. There is no contradiction: a continuous quasiperiodic function can have rare near-maximizers while its average energy on every sufficiently long interval is small.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty boundary

The density/equidistribution step is classical Kronecker--Weyl theory and is already audited in `VIS-071` and `VIS-161`. No new recurrence or equidistribution theorem is claimed here.

The unit-modulus quadratic formulation is also a standard optimization object. Shankarachary Ragi, Edwin K. P. Chong, and Hans D. Mittelmann, **Polynomial-Time Methods to Solve Unimodular Quadratic Programs With Performance Guarantees**, *IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems* 55:5 (2019), 2118--2127, DOI `10.1109/TAES.2018.2881366`, uses the term unimodular quadratic program for maximizing a quadratic function of complex unit-modulus variables. Yiqiao Zhong and Nicolas Boumal, **Near-Optimal Bounds for Phase Synchronization**, *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 28:2 (2018), DOI `10.1137/17M1122025`, treats the closely related unit-modulus-constrained Hermitian quadratic objective in phase synchronization.

No novelty is claimed for UQP, phase synchronization, Kronecker recurrence, or the inequality `||G||_infinity>=||G||_2`. The Mathia-specific content is the exact identification of the `VIS-192`/`VIS-193` worst-start question with this torus objective, preserving the actual prime-ratio coefficients and separating phase-frustration size from Diophantine access time.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification

Everything here is fixed finite `y` and fixed `H` before the torus closure is taken. The theorem gives no rate at which the prime-log orbit approaches a maximizing phase assignment when the number of primes grows. A maximizer may require recurrence times far beyond any observation scale of interest.

The UQP formulation also does not imply that its optimum is close to the absolute coefficient envelope. Edge phases may be frustrated around cycles, taper weights may distribute mass over many incompatible edges, and the optimum may still tend to zero after normalization. Determining that asymptotic behavior requires new estimates.

The `H^(-1/2)` lower scale imports the matching `R_v(y,H) >> 1/H` regime from `VIS-192`; outside that strictly subcritical regime only the exact identity `sup_T |F(T)| = max_|z_p|=1 |z^*Az|` and the generic lower bound by `sqrt(R_v)` are asserted.

Falsify the finding by producing a finite prime set for which the prime-phase orbit is not dense, an error in the Hermitian quadratic representation of `F`, a torus phase point whose objective cannot be approximated along the start orbit, or a mismatch between the Haar second moment of `G` and the exact `VIS-192` variance.
