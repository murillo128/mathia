# VIS-061 — independent control replicates give a control-law L1 radius without within-replicate independence

## Claim

Fix one finite declared support `S` of size `K>=1` for the three-gap representation. Let

`X_1,...,X_B in Delta(S)`

be independent identically distributed random probability tables, where each `X_b` may be an arbitrarily complicated statistic of one complete random control replicate. In the intended finite-size CUE application, `X_b` may be the empirical three-gap table formed from **all** eligible overlapping triples inside the `b`-th independently generated CUE matrix; no independence among those within-matrix triples is assumed.

Define

`P_hat = (1/B) sum_(b=1)^B X_b`,
`P = E[X_1]`.

Then for every `0<rho<1`,

`Pr(||P_hat-P||_1 <= delta_B(rho)) >= 1-rho`,

with the explicit safe radius

`delta_B(rho) = min(2, [sqrt(K)+sqrt(2 log(1/rho))]/sqrt(B))`.

Consequently, under the fixed positive Fisher reference `H` and Markov-residual map `Delta(.)` of `VIS-057`, put

`h_min=min_c H(c)>0`,
`a_B = 6 delta_B(rho)/sqrt(h_min)`.

For any frozen zeta table `Z`, the control-only certificate of `VIS-060` applies with this explicit radius. In particular, writing

`R_Z=Delta(Z)`,
`R=Delta(P)`,
`R_hat=Delta(P_hat)`,
`d_hat=||R_Z-R_hat||_H`,

one has with probability at least `1-rho`

`| ||R_Z-R||_H - d_hat | <= a_B`.

Thus `d_hat>a_B` certifies separation of the frozen zeta residual from the population residual of the **whole-replicate control experiment** without pretending that the overlapping triples inside any control matrix are independent.

If a target Fisher residual radius `eta>0` is required before simulation, the sufficient matrix-replicate budget

`B >= [36/(h_min eta^2)] [sqrt(K)+sqrt(2 log(1/rho))]^2`

ensures the uncapped expression gives `a_B<=eta`.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + CONTROL-CONCENTRATION INTERFACE + REPRESENTATION CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The concentration theorem is classical bounded-differences machinery. The contribution here is only the specialization that closes the missing control-side `L^1` input in `VIS-060` while keeping arbitrary dependence inside each independently generated control replicate.

## 1. The mean L1 error is bounded at the replicate level

Let

`Y=P_hat-P`.

Because the `X_b` are independent and identically distributed,

`E||Y||_2^2`
` = sum_(c in S) Var(P_hat(c))`
` = (1/B) sum_(c in S) Var(X_1(c))`
` = (1/B) [E||X_1||_2^2-||P||_2^2]`
` <= 1/B`,

since every probability table satisfies `||X_1||_2<=||X_1||_1=1`.

Therefore Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen give

`E||Y||_1`
` <= sqrt(K) E||Y||_2`
` <= sqrt(K E||Y||_2^2)`
` <= sqrt(K/B)`.

No within-replicate observation count enters this argument. A replicate may contain a long sequence of heavily overlapping triples, deterministic constraints between cells, or any other internal dependence; all of it is absorbed into the random simplex vector `X_b`.

## 2. Bounded differences supplies the confidence tail

Consider

`f(X_1,...,X_B)=||P_hat-P||_1`.

If one replicate `X_b` is replaced by another possible replicate `X_b'`, then

`||P_hat-P_hat'||_1 = (1/B)||X_b-X_b'||_1 <= 2/B`,

because the `L^1` diameter of the probability simplex is `2`. The reverse triangle inequality gives the same `2/B` bound on `|f-f'|`.

McDiarmid's bounded-differences inequality therefore yields

`Pr(f-Ef >= t) <= exp(-B t^2/2)`.

Taking

`t=sqrt(2 log(1/rho)/B)`

and inserting the expectation bound proves

`||P_hat-P||_1 <= [sqrt(K)+sqrt(2 log(1/rho))]/sqrt(B)`

with probability at least `1-rho`. Since two probability laws are always at `L^1` distance at most `2`, taking the minimum with `2` gives the displayed radius.

The same argument works for independent non-identically distributed replicate tables if `P` is redefined as their average expectation. For the active experiment the i.i.d. formulation is cleaner: every control matrix should be generated by the same predeclared finite-size CUE/control procedure within one comparison panel.

## 3. Why this is different from treating triples as independent

A tempting calibration would concatenate every control triple and insert the total triple count into an i.i.d. multinomial radius. That is not justified merely because the matrices are random: consecutive spacing triples from one matrix overlap, and the eigenvalue process itself contains additional dependence.

The present certificate takes the opposite conservative route. Independence is invoked only at the level where the simulation genuinely supplies it: independently generated matrices or other complete control replicates. Each matrix contributes one simplex-valued table `X_b`; the theorem never decomposes that table into independent categorical draws.

This creates a useful separation from `VIS-058` and `VIS-059`. Those findings are needed when one wants a sharper uncertainty analysis for an empirical law formed from one dependent sequence and must expose overlap/long-memory terms. Here the complete within-matrix dependence is deliberately left unresolved and paid for through a replicate-level concentration radius.

The price is conservatism. The radius scales with `sqrt(K/B)` and ignores the large amount of information inside each matrix. A sharper theorem may exploit CUE structure or within-matrix covariance, but such a refinement must be justified rather than obtained by silently replacing the effective sample size by the raw number of overlapping triples.

## 4. Direct insertion into the fixed-window Fisher certificate

`VIS-057` says that a raw-law `L^1` radius `delta` gives the Fisher residual radius

`a=6 delta/sqrt(h_min)`.

`VIS-060` then shows that for a fixed target table `Z`, all probability can remain on the control estimator. Substituting `delta_B(rho)` gives `a_B` immediately.

Hence one can predeclare a finite partition/support/reference, freeze one zeta window, independently generate `B` matched control matrices, turn each complete matrix into its empirical triple table, average those tables, and obtain a mathematically valid control-population ball without any zeta-side model and without any within-CUE-triple independence assumption.

The displayed budget inequality is just `a_B<=eta` solved for `B` using the uncapped branch of `delta_B`. It is useful primarily as a feasibility diagnostic. Fine partitions increase `K`; a diffuse Fisher reference can make `h_min` small. Either effect can make this generic certificate too weak to be practical, which is itself important information before an expensive simulation campaign.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

The probability input is Colin McDiarmid, **On the method of bounded differences**, in *Surveys in Combinatorics, 1989*, London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 141, Cambridge University Press (1989), pp. 148–188, DOI `10.1017/CBO9781107359949.008`. The bounded-differences inequality is classical and no new concentration theorem is claimed.

The expectation estimate is elementary simplex geometry plus independence. The Mathia-specific step is only to recognize that a whole independently generated CUE matrix can be treated as one bounded simplex-valued replicate, making the control-side radius compatible with the nonlinear Markov/Fisher propagation already established in `VIS-057` and the fixed-target semantics of `VIS-060`.

This finding does not claim the radius is sharp, does not establish a finite-size CUE arithmetic correction, and does not show that zeta differs from CUE.

## 6. Falsification and boundaries

Falsify the displayed concentration bound by exhibiting independent replicate probability tables satisfying the hypotheses for which the stated `1-rho` radius fails. The proof depends only on the simplex diameter, the second-moment estimate, and the classical bounded-differences inequality.

For the intended application, the representation must be frozen across control replicates: same partition, declared support, unfolding/control construction, table-normalization rule, and Fisher reference. If the analysis chooses a partition, support edit, mode, matrix-size correction, or statistic after looking at the same control ensemble, the simple one-view coverage statement does not automatically survive that selection.

The target population `P=E[X_1]` is the mean table induced by the **entire declared control-replicate algorithm**. If the algorithm itself mixes matrix sizes, edge/bulk regions, or preprocessing conventions, the theorem faithfully concentrates around that mixture; it does not make the mixture a scientifically appropriate baseline.

When `delta_B=2` or `a_B` exceeds the residual scale of interest, the theorem is mathematically valid but uninformative. That failure should lead to a sharper justified control model, a coarser stable representation, or more independent replicates—not to an i.i.d.-triple shortcut.

## Research consequence

The accepted three-gap residual clue now has a concrete lowest-assumption control calibration. Fresh independent CUE matrices can each contribute their full overlapping-triple empirical table; replicate-level bounded differences supplies a safe population-law `L^1` radius, and `VIS-057`/`VIS-060` propagate it to the fixed-zeta Fisher comparison.

This does not execute the higher-window experiment and does not resolve the clue. It removes one methodological ambiguity from that experiment: **control-side uncertainty can be certified without either a zeta process model or an independence fiction for triples inside each CUE matrix**. The next empirical step can therefore budget independent control matrices against the explicit `a_B` scale before deciding whether a sharper CUE-specific concentration analysis is necessary.
