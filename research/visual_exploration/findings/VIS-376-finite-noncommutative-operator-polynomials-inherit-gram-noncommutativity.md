# VIS-376 — finite noncommutative operator polynomials inherit Gram noncommutativity

## Claim

Let `A_W` be a finite-dimensional Wang coefficient map with source-free Gram

`G=A_W^*A_W>=0`.

Let `H_1,...,H_r` be a fixed finite family of externally supplied Hermitian operators on the coefficient space. They need not commute with one another. For a word

`w=(i_1,...,i_d)`

write

`H_w=H_(i_1)...H_(i_d)`,

and let

`P(H_1,...,H_r)=sum_w a_w H_w`

be any finite noncommutative polynomial, with the empty word representing a scalar multiple of the identity.

Then the commutator with the Wang Gram has the exact wordwise expansion

`[G,H_w]`
` = sum_(k=1)^d H_(i_1)...H_(i_(k-1)) [G,H_(i_k)] H_(i_(k+1))...H_(i_d)`.

Consequently,

`||[G,P(H)]||_F`
` <= sum_w |a_w| sum_(k=1)^|w|`
`    (prod_(l<k) ||H_(i_l)||_op)`
`    ||[G,H_(i_k)]||_F`
`    (prod_(l>k) ||H_(i_l)||_op)`.

In particular, if

`R=max_j ||H_j||_op>0`,

`epsilon=max_j ||[G,H_j]||_F`,

then

`||[G,P(H)]||_F`
` <= epsilon sum_(w: |w|>=1) |a_w| |w| R^(|w|-1)`.

If every generator commutes with `G`, then every finite noncommutative polynomial in the generators commutes with `G` exactly, **even when the generators do not commute with one another**. Mutual source-operator noncommutativity therefore does not by itself create orientation relative to the Wang Gram.

If the evaluated polynomial is a positive Hermitian metric

`C=P(H_1,...,H_r)`,

with `mI<=C<=MI`, then the cluster control of `VIS-374` applies. For any Gram clustering of within-cluster radius `eta` and resolved inter-cluster separation `gamma>0`, there is a positive metric `C_*` commuting with `G`, with `mI<=C_*<=MI`, such that

`|s_k(A_W C^(1/2))^2-s_k(A_W C_*^(1/2))^2|`
` <= ||G||_op sqrt(M/m) D_P/gamma + 2M eta`,

where `D_P` is the explicit polynomial commutator budget on the right-hand side of the first inequality above.

Thus bounded finite polynomial assembly of finitely many external operators cannot manufacture resolved Gram orientation for free. Any such orientation is inherited from the generator commutators and can be amplified only by an explicit coefficient/degree/operator-norm complexity budget.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL INNER-DERIVATION LEIBNIZ IDENTITY + STRUCTURAL NEGATIVE CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This proves no estimate for the normalized Chebyshev-theta remainder, the prime-number-theorem remainder, zeta zeros, or RH. It does not rule out genuinely large generator-level Gram noncommutativity, growing/unbounded polynomial complexity, nonpolynomial singular functional calculus, unbounded operators, or source-dependent constructors.

## 1. Why this is the next external-geometry control

`VIS-372` showed that an external Hermitian operator commuting with `G` supplies only joint spectral filtering. `VIS-373` priced arbitrary near-commuting positive metrics by gap-normalized Gram commutator size. `VIS-374` showed that orientation confined to narrow nearly degenerate Gram clusters is spectrally cheap. `VIS-375` then treated scalar functional calculus of one external Hermitian operator and proved that `f(H)` can only rescale Gram off-diagonal blocks already exposed by `H`.

The natural next escape is to supply several physically meaningful external observables `H_1,...,H_r` and combine them noncommutatively. Even if no single observable is useful, one might hope that products such as `H_1 H_2 H_1`, symmetrized combinations, or more elaborate finite words create a new orientation because the external observables fail to commute with one another.

The exact derivation identity below shows the boundary. Noncommutative multiplication can redistribute and amplify generator-level Gram noncommutativity, but it cannot create it from zero. At fixed bounded algebraic complexity, small generator commutators remain small with an explicit budget.

## 2. The Gram commutator is an inner derivation

For arbitrary matrices `A,B`, direct expansion gives

`[G,AB]`
` = GAB-ABG`
` = (GA-AG)B + A(GB-BG)`
` = [G,A]B + A[G,B]`.

Thus the map

`delta_G(X)=[G,X]`

is a derivation on the matrix algebra. Iterating the product rule along a word

`H_w=H_(i_1)...H_(i_d)`

gives exactly

`delta_G(H_w)`
` = sum_(k=1)^d H_(i_1)...H_(i_(k-1)) delta_G(H_(i_k)) H_(i_(k+1))...H_(i_d)`.

Linearity then gives the corresponding expansion for `P(H)`.

This identity has a useful qualitative corollary before any norm estimate is made. If every `H_j` lies in the commutant of `G`, then the whole associative algebra generated by the `H_j` lies in the commutant of `G`. The generators may still have rich noncommutative relations among themselves; those relations do not define a new Gram orientation unless at least one generator already fails to commute with `G`.

## 3. Fixed polynomial complexity gives an explicit orientation budget

The Frobenius norm satisfies

`||A X B||_F <= ||A||_op ||X||_F ||B||_op`.

Apply this term by term to the exact word expansion. For one word,

`||[G,H_w]||_F`
` <= sum_(k=1)^d`
`    (prod_(l<k) ||H_(i_l)||_op)`
`    ||[G,H_(i_k)]||_F`
`    (prod_(l>k) ||H_(i_l)||_op)`.

Summing with coefficients proves the first bound in the claim.

When all generator norms are at most `R` and all generator Gram commutators are at most `epsilon`, a degree-`d` word contributes at most

`d R^(d-1) epsilon`.

Hence the complete polynomial is controlled by the coefficient-weighted algebraic derivative budget

`B_P(R)=sum_(w: |w|>=1) |a_w| |w| R^(|w|-1)`.

At fixed degree, fixed coefficient mass, and bounded generator norms, `B_P(R)` is finite and source-independent. Therefore an `o(1)` family of generator commutators remains `o(1)` after any uniformly bounded finite polynomial constructor. An order-one output commutator requires an order-one generator commutator or a diverging complexity budget.

The degenerate case `R=0` is trivial: every `H_j=0`, so every nonconstant word vanishes and the polynomial commutes with `G`.

## 4. Composition with the Wang spectral control

Suppose the polynomial has been chosen so that

`C=P(H_1,...,H_r)`

is Hermitian positive and satisfies `mI<=C<=MI`. The polynomial estimate gives

`||[G,C]||_F<=D_P`,

where `D_P` is the detailed generator-level budget in the claim.

Apply `VIS-374` to this `C`. With within-cluster Gram radius `eta` and resolved inter-cluster gap `gamma`, that finding constructs a positive exactly `G`-commuting metric `C_*` and gives

`|s_k(A_W C^(1/2))^2-s_k(A_W C_*^(1/2))^2|`
` <= ||G||_op sqrt(M/m) ||[G,C]||_F/gamma + 2M eta`.

Substitution yields the displayed composed bound.

This separates the resources cleanly. The external packet contributes generator-level orientation `||[G,H_j]||_F`; the noncommutative polynomial contributes only the explicit amplification budget `D_P`; the Wang Gram contributes the resolved-gap and cluster scales `gamma,eta`; and positivity contributes the metric bounds `m,M`.

A proposal must therefore expose which of these resources carries the claimed destination-scale gain rather than attributing it generically to “noncommutative external geometry.”

## 5. Prior-art audit

The algebraic identity is classical. For fixed `G`, the commutator map `delta_G(X)=[G,X]` is the standard example of an inner derivation, and the Leibniz rule for derivations immediately yields the word expansion above. Kadison and Ringrose, *Fundamentals of the Theory of Operator Algebras, Volume I: Elementary Theory*, Academic Press (1983; AMS reprint 1997), is a standard operator-algebra reference for this framework.

The Frobenius estimate uses only the elementary ideal inequality `||AXB||_F<=||A||_op||X||_F||B||_op`. The matrix spectral and unitarily invariant norm background is already audited in `VIS-374` and `VIS-375` through Bhatia's *Matrix Analysis* and the surrounding perturbation references.

No novelty is claimed for inner derivations, the Leibniz/product rule, commutator expansions of words or polynomials, or these norm inequalities. The Mathia-specific contribution is the control boundary after `VIS-372`--`VIS-375`: finite multi-operator noncommutative assembly does not evade the Wang orientation accounting merely because the external generators fail to commute with one another.

A repository duplication check found no existing Visual Exploration finding that already controls finite multi-generator noncommutative polynomial assembly. `VIS-375` is complementary rather than duplicative: it allows nonlinear scalar functional calculus of one Hermitian generator, while the present result allows several mutually noncommuting generators but only finite algebraic combination.

## Research consequence

A finite packet of external observables should not be credited with a new Wang mechanism merely because the observables are mutually noncommuting or because a finite polynomial in them has a complicated eigenbasis. The relevant first audit is generator-by-generator Gram orientation, followed by the explicit polynomial amplification budget.

If the generator commutators vanish, every finite polynomial constructor stays exactly in the Gram commutant. If they are small and polynomial complexity remains uniformly bounded, the resulting metric remains quantitatively controlled by `VIS-374`. A surviving positive-Hilbert route must therefore bring substantial resolved Gram noncommutativity already through at least one external generator, let algebraic complexity grow enough to repay the small commutators and charge that growth explicitly, use a genuinely nonpolynomial/singular operation, or leave the bounded finite-dimensional positive-Hilbert setting.
