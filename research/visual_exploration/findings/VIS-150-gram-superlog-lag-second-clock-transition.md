# VIS-150 — Gram lag increments have a second deterministic clock transition at `log^2 N`

## Claim

Let `g_k` be the Gram points, defined for all sufficiently large `k` by

`theta(g_k)=pi k`,

up to the usual harmless indexing shift, and let

`z_k=(p^(-i g_k))_p in Omega`,

where `Omega` is the infinite prime torus. For an integer lag `ell=ell(N)` define

`D_(k,ell)=g_(k+ell)-g_k`

and the lag increment

`Delta_(k,ell)=z_(k+ell) overline(z_k)`.

To remove the common lag rotation at the start of the block, define the dephased increment

`R_(N,ell)(k)(p)`
` = exp(-i log(p) [D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)])`.

Assume

`ell=o(sqrt(N) log N)`

and put

`L_N=log N`,

`A_N=ell/L_N^2`.

Then on the macroscopic consecutive block `N<=k<=2N-ell` the remaining ordered geometry is still generated by the Gram clock. More precisely, if `g(x)` is the continuous inverse of the eventual increasing branch of `theta`, define

`h_N(u)=-(L_N^2/(2 pi)) [g'(Nu)-g'(N)]`, `1<=u<=2`.

Uniformly on `1<=u<=2`,

`h_N(u)->log u`,

and uniformly for the integer lag block,

`D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)`
` = -2 pi A_N h_N(k/N)+o(1)`.

This gives a second clock hierarchy beyond `VIS-091`.

1. If `A_N->0`, then

`max_(N<=k<=2N-ell) |R_(N,ell)(k)(p)-1| ->0`

for every fixed prime `p`. Thus even genuinely superlogarithmic lags satisfying

`log N << ell << log^2 N`

remain a single moving clock point after quotienting the common lag rotation.

2. If `A_N->tau` with `0<tau<infinity`, then for `k/N->u in [1,2]`,

`R_(N,ell)(k)(p) -> exp(2 pi i tau log(p) log(u))`.

Hence the dephased empirical lag field converges, on every fixed prime projection, to the deterministic logarithmic torus curve

`u -> (exp(2 pi i tau log(p) log(u)))_p`, `1<=u<=2`,

with `u` distributed uniformly on `[1,2]` by the consecutive index sampling.

3. If `A_N->infinity`, still under `ell=o(sqrt(N) log N)`, then the empirical dephased increment measure converges weakly to Haar measure on `Omega`. The same is true for the raw lag-increment empirical measure, because it differs only by a common torus rotation.

The Haar limit in the third regime is therefore **not evidence of mixing or a new arithmetic channel**. It is already forced by accelerated deterministic shear of the inverse Gram clock across a macroscopic block.

**Evidence/status:** `CLASSICAL-GRAM-ASYMPTOTIC + DERIVED SECOND-CLOCK HIERARCHY + NEGATIVE/CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No growing-prime-support theorem, nonconsecutive sampling theorem, zeta-value/zero-coordinate result, stochastic mixing claim, or RH consequence is asserted.

## 1. The inverse Gram clock has a macroscopic `log^-2 N` shear

Write `g(x)` for the continuous inverse of the eventual monotone branch of the Riemann--Siegel theta function:

`theta(g(x))=pi x`.

The standard asymptotics used already in `VIS-084`, `VIS-090`, and `VIS-091` give

`theta'(t)=(1/2)log(t/(2 pi))+O(t^-2)`,

`theta''(t)=1/(2t)+O(t^-3)`,

and

`g(x)~2 pi x/log x`.

Implicit differentiation yields

`g'(x)=pi/theta'(g(x))`

and

`g''(x)=-pi^2 theta''(g(x))/theta'(g(x))^3`.

Therefore, uniformly for `x` in any fixed macroscopic interval such as `[N,3N]`,

`g''(x)=-(2 pi)/(x (log x)^2) (1+o(1))`.

For the rescaled shear

`h_N(u)=-(L_N^2/(2 pi))[g'(Nu)-g'(N)]`,

we have `h_N(1)=0` and

`h_N'(u)=-(N L_N^2/(2 pi)) g''(Nu) -> 1/u`

uniformly on `[1,2]`. Integrating gives

`h_N(u)->log u`

uniformly.

This identifies the next visual clock scale. Consecutive Gram spacing itself is `Theta(1/log N)` in index coordinates, so a lag of order `log N` produces order-one common phase motion as in `VIS-091`. But the *variation of that lag increment across a macroscopic block* is one derivative smaller: it accumulates at scale `ell/log^2 N`.

## 2. Finite lags reduce uniformly to the local inverse-clock derivative

For `N<=k<=2N-ell`, write the lag exactly as

`D_(k,ell)=integral_k^(k+ell) g'(x) dx`.

The preceding bound on `g''` gives

`D_(k,ell)=ell g'(k)+O(ell^2/(N L_N^2))`

uniformly on the block. The same estimate at `k=N` gives

`D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)`
` = ell[g'(k)-g'(N)] + O(ell^2/(N L_N^2))`.

The assumption `ell=o(sqrt(N) log N)` makes the remainder `o(1)`. By the definition of `h_N`,

`D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)`
` = -2 pi A_N h_N(k/N)+o(1)`

uniformly.

This formula is the useful representation: after removing one common torus rotation, every fixed-prime lag field is a deterministic reparameterization of the slowly varying Gram-spacing shear.

## 3. Below `log^2 N`, superlogarithmic order is still a point null

If `A_N=ell/L_N^2->0`, then `h_N` is uniformly bounded on `[1,2]`, so

`D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)->0`

uniformly. For each fixed prime `p`, the chord inequality gives

`|R_(N,ell)(k)(p)-1|`
` <= log(p) |D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)|`,

hence uniform collapse to one.

This closes the first naive interpretation of the superlogarithmic regime left open by `VIS-091`. Taking `ell/log N->infinity` is not enough to create nontrivial ordered geometry across a macroscopic consecutive block. All lags with `ell=o(log^2 N)` are still clock-coherent after one global dephasing.

The raw increment point may rotate with `N` and need not converge to a fixed torus element. That has no structural significance: the diameter of the whole lag cloud tends to zero.

## 4. At `log^2 N`, the cloud becomes a deterministic logarithmic torus curve

Suppose `A_N->tau in (0,infinity)`. Since `h_N(u)->log u` uniformly,

`D_(k,ell)-D_(N,ell)`
` -> -2 pi tau log u`

whenever `k/N->u`.

Therefore

`R_(N,ell)(k)(p)`
` -> exp(2 pi i tau log(p) log(u))`.

For any fixed finite set of primes and any continuous test function on its torus, the consecutive-index average is a Riemann sum. Hence the dephased empirical measure converges to the pushforward of normalized Lebesgue measure on `[1,2]` by the displayed deterministic curve.

This is the second visual transition of the Gram clock. It is not a random point cloud and not a mixing law. The natural picture is a one-parameter logarithmic shear orbit whose apparent winding complexity grows with the chosen prime projection.

## 5. Above `log^2 N`, deterministic clock shear alone produces Haar

Now assume

`A_N->infinity`

while still `ell=o(sqrt(N) log N)`. Fix a nontrivial torus character

`chi_m(omega)=product_p omega(p)^(m_p)`

with finite support and scalar frequency

`a_m=sum_p m_p log p !=0`.

Unique factorization gives the nonvanishing of `a_m`.

Using the uniform lag representation,

`chi_m(R_(N,ell)(k))`
` = exp(2 pi i a_m A_N h_N(k/N))+o(1)`

uniformly in `k`.

The functions `h_N` are eventually increasing with derivative bounded above and below by positive constants on `[1,2]`; one further differentiation of the standard theta asymptotics gives a uniform `C^2` bound on this compact rescaled interval. Therefore nonstationary phase gives

`integral_1^2 exp(2 pi i a_m A_N h_N(u)) du = O_m(A_N^-1)`.

The corresponding Riemann-sum error is `O_m(A_N/N)`, which tends to zero under the stated lag range. Thus

`(1/(N-ell+1)) sum_(k=N)^(2N-ell) chi_m(R_(N,ell)(k)) ->0`.

Every nontrivial fixed character vanishes, while the trivial character remains one. The compact-group Fourier criterion therefore gives weak Haar convergence on `Omega`.

Multiplying every sample by the common raw phase `Delta_(N,ell)` only rotates the empirical measure, so the raw lag-increment population has the same Haar limit.

The mechanism is important precisely because it is a null. A visually Haar-looking lag cloud at these superlogarithmic scales can be generated with **no source randomness beyond the deterministic Gram clock**.

## 6. Stress tests and boundaries

The theorem concerns consecutive Gram indices over a macroscopic block and fixed torus characters. It is not uniform over prime support, character coefficients, delay dimension, or Fourier complexity growing with `N`.

The range `ell=o(sqrt(N) log N)` is a clean sufficient condition ensuring that replacing a finite lag by `ell g'(k)` has uniform additive error `o(1)`. It is not claimed to be sharp. Larger lags require retaining more of the inverse-clock expansion rather than assuming the ordered route becomes arithmetic.

The critical `log^2 N` curve is stated only up to the common lag rotation removed by `R_(N,ell)`. That quotient is deliberate: a global torus rotation cannot supply cross-block shape information. A statistic explicitly sensitive to absolute phase must carry that common rotation as part of its null rather than treating it as an arithmetic residual.

The supercritical Haar statement is an empirical population result for the lag increments. It does not prove independence between different lags, path mixing, stochasticity, or independence from `Z(g_k)`, nearby zeros, derivatives, or other analytic coordinates.

Nonconsecutive/selected Gram sampling is outside the theorem. So are genuinely source-anchored observables that are not deterministic functions of the Gram index and fixed prime phases.

No untouched confirmation-zero data are used.

## 7. Prior-art and novelty audit

The analytic inputs are the classical Riemann--Siegel theta asymptotics and inverse-Gram-spacing calculus already anchored in `VIS-084`, `VIS-090`, and `VIS-091`. The Fourier/Haar step uses the same compact-torus character principle and elementary nonstationary-phase logic already used in the Gram sampling controls.

A targeted check against the Gram-point discrete/short-interval universality sources already cited by `VIS-090` and `VIS-091`, including Antanas Laurincikas, *Joint Discrete Approximation of Analytic Functions by Shifts of the Riemann Zeta-Function Twisted by Gram Points* (Mathematics 11 (2023), 565) and its short-interval sequel (Axioms 12 (2023), 426), did not provide a reason to claim this `log^2 N` lag-shear packaging as a new theorem. Those works control Gram-sampled prime-torus distributions in broader universality arguments; the present statement is an elementary asymptotic control specialized to the ordered lag visualization problem.

Accordingly **no novelty is claimed**. The durable value is the newly explicit second-clock null: the previously open phrase "genuinely superlogarithmic lag" contains a point regime, a deterministic-curve regime, and a clock-generated Haar regime before any new arithmetic source has been introduced.

## Research consequence

The accepted `CLUE-zeta-prime-phase-recursive-geometry` should be narrowed again. "Superlogarithmic" by itself is no longer a valid escape from the Gram-clock null:

- `log N << ell << log^2 N` still collapses to one dephased clock point;
- `ell~tau log^2 N` gives a deterministic logarithmic torus curve;
- `log^2 N << ell << sqrt(N) log N` gives Haar lag-increment population through deterministic clock shear, not through arithmetic mixing.

A surviving ordered prime-phase route must therefore remove or exceed this second-order clock geometry, use a sampling law not covered by consecutive Gram indices, grow support/complexity with a quantitative theorem, or introduce an independently informative analytic coordinate. A visual separator must not count either the critical logarithmic curve or the supercritical Haar-looking lag cloud as evidence until the corresponding Gram-clock null has been quotiented.
