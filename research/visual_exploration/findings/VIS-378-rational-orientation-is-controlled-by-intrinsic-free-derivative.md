# VIS-378 — rational orientation amplification is controlled by the intrinsic free derivative

## Claim

Let `G=G^*` be the source-free Wang Gram on a finite-dimensional coefficient space, let

`H=(H_1,...,H_r)`

be an `r`-tuple of external coefficient-space operators, and let `R` be a noncommutative rational function defined at `H`. Regard `R` as the function represented by its equivalent rational expressions, not as one chosen expression tree.

Noncommutative rational functions respect simultaneous similarity. Therefore, for every invertible `S` for which the evaluation is represented locally,

`R(SH_1S^(-1),...,SH_rS^(-1)) = S R(H) S^(-1)`.

Taking `S(t)=exp(tG)` and differentiating at `t=0` gives the exact identity

`D R_H([G,H_1],...,[G,H_r]) = [G,R(H)]`,

where `D R_H` is the Fréchet/directional derivative of the matrix evaluation of the rational function at `H`.

Equip perturbation tuples with

`||(K_1,...,K_r)||_(2,F) = (sum_j ||K_j||_F^2)^(1/2)`

and let

`L_R(H)=||D R_H||_( (2,F) -> F )`.

Then

`||[G,R(H)]||_F`
` <= L_R(H) (sum_j ||[G,H_j]||_F^2)^(1/2)`.

Thus the intrinsic local orientation-amplification resource of a fixed rational function is its derivative norm at the evaluated tuple, not the inverse norms of an arbitrary rational expression used to represent it.

For a family indexed by `T`, if

`epsilon_T=(sum_j ||[G_T,H_(j,T)]||_F^2)^(1/2) -> 0`

and

`sup_T L_(R_T)(H_T) < infinity`,

then

`||[G_T,R_T(H_T)]||_F -> 0`.

In particular, a fixed rational function evaluated along a family cannot create order-one Gram orientation from vanishing generator orientation unless its local derivative norm diverges. A chosen expression may contain very ill-conditioned intermediate inverses while the represented rational function has small or even zero derivative, so intermediate inverse conditioning is only an expression-level sufficient budget unless it survives at the function level.

If the output

`C=R(H)`

is a positive Hermitian metric with `mI<=C<=MI`, then the cluster control of `VIS-374` yields

`|s_k(A_W C^(1/2))^2-s_k(A_W C_*^(1/2))^2|`
` <= ||G||_op sqrt(M/m) L_R(H) epsilon/gamma + 2M eta`

for the exactly Gram-commuting cluster control `C_*`, with `eta` the within-cluster Gram width and `gamma>0` the resolved inter-cluster separation.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL FREE/NC DIFFERENTIAL CALCULUS + STRUCTURAL NEGATIVE CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No stronger estimate for the Chebyshev-theta remainder, the prime-number-theorem remainder, zeta zeros, or RH is claimed.

## 1. Why the expression-tree budget is not intrinsic

`VIS-377` proved a correct recursive commutator budget for any chosen finite `*`-rational expression. In particular an inverse node satisfies

`[G,X^(-1)] = -X^(-1)[G,X]X^(-1)`,

so that expression-level propagation can charge a factor `||X^(-1)||_op^2`.

That is a useful sufficient bound, but a rational function has many equivalent expressions. Internal near-singularities can cancel algebraically before they reach the output. Consequently the largest inverse norm appearing in one syntax tree is not, by itself, an invariant of the represented map.

The extreme example is

`E(X)=X^(-1)X`

on invertible `X`. The chosen tree contains `X^(-1)` and its recursive `VIS-377` budget may become arbitrarily large as `X` approaches singularity. But the represented rational function is the constant function

`R(X)=I`,

so

`D R_X=0`

and

`[G,R(X)]=0`

exactly. The apparent near-pole resource is removable representation conditioning, not output orientation.

This does not invalidate `VIS-377`; it identifies its correct role. `Q_G(E)` is an expression-dependent upper bound. To decide whether a rational construction genuinely amplifies Gram orientation, the conditioning must be quotiented by rational identities and measured at the evaluated function.

## 2. Similarity covariance gives the exact tangent identity

A noncommutative rational function evaluated on matrix tuples is similarity covariant wherever it is defined. Let

`S(t)=exp(tG)`.

Set

`H_j(t)=S(t)H_jS(t)^(-1)`.

Then

`d/dt H_j(t)|_(t=0) = [G,H_j]`.

Similarity covariance gives

`R(H_1(t),...,H_r(t)) = S(t) R(H) S(t)^(-1)`.

Differentiating at zero yields

`D R_H([G,H_1],...,[G,H_r]) = [G,R(H)]`.

No approximation or chosen rational syntax is used in this identity. The commutator is precisely the derivative of the rational function along the infinitesimal simultaneous-similarity orbit generated by `G`.

Taking Frobenius norms and the operator norm of the derivative immediately gives

`||[G,R(H)]||_F <= L_R(H) epsilon`,

with

`epsilon=(sum_j ||[G,H_j]||_F^2)^(1/2)`.

This is the function-level replacement for the expression-tree orientation ledger.

## 3. The derivative removes removable poles automatically

Equivalent rational expressions define the same matrix-valued function on the common local domain, hence the same derivative at a point where the rational function is defined. The derivative therefore ignores syntactic inverses that cancel in the represented function.

This is the key distinction from charging every intermediate inverse separately. An expression may be badly conditioned as a computational representation while the rational map is locally insensitive. Such conditioning cannot be credited as a mathematical orientation mechanism.

Conversely, if a pole or near-pole is genuine at the function level, it appears as large local sensitivity. The derivative norm therefore keeps exactly the conditioning that survives rational simplification.

The useful invariant is not “did the formula contain an inverse?” but “how strongly does the evaluated rational map respond to the Gram-generated similarity tangent carried by the external generators?”

## 4. Genuine resolvent poles survive the quotient

For the one-variable resolvent

`R_z(H)=(zI-H)^(-1)`,

the Fréchet derivative is

`D R_(z,H)[K] = (zI-H)^(-1) K (zI-H)^(-1)`.

Therefore

`L_(R_z)(H) <= ||(zI-H)^(-1)||_op^2`.

When `H` is Hermitian and `z` lies off its spectrum,

`||(zI-H)^(-1)||_op = dist(z,spec(H))^(-1)`,

so

`L_(R_z)(H) <= dist(z,spec(H))^(-2)`.

Thus the squared distance-to-spectrum tax identified in `VIS-377` is recovered when the near-pole is real. What changes is the interpretation: the denominator is a resource only insofar as it makes the rational function itself sensitive. A removable pole contributes no intrinsic amplification.

## 5. Uniform derivative control closes representation-dependent rational escape

Consider a parameter family `G_T,H_T,R_T`. Define

`epsilon_T=(sum_j ||[G_T,H_(j,T)]||_F^2)^(1/2)`.

The exact tangent identity gives

`||[G_T,R_T(H_T)]||_F <= L_(R_T)(H_T) epsilon_T`.

Hence if `epsilon_T->0` and the derivative norms stay bounded, rational processing remains asymptotically Gram-commuting regardless of how complicated or ill-conditioned one chosen expression for `R_T` looks internally.

If the output commutator remains order one while `epsilon_T->0`, then necessarily

`L_(R_T)(H_T) -> infinity`

along a subsequence. The surviving resource is therefore intrinsic rational sensitivity. It may arise from a genuine pole/singular boundary, growing function complexity, changing dimension, or another degeneration, but it cannot be supplied merely by writing the same rational function through a worse-conditioned expression.

This sharpens the finite-calculus frontier after `VIS-375`--`VIS-377`: scalar functional calculus, finite polynomial assembly, and finite rational assembly are all orientation-preserving unless the represented map itself develops enough sensitivity to amplify the inherited generator commutators.

## 6. Composition with the Wang destination control

Assume the rational output `C=R(H)` is positive with

`mI<=C<=MI`.

For a Wang-Gram clustering with within-cluster width `eta` and resolved inter-cluster gap `gamma`, `VIS-374` supplies an exactly Gram-commuting positive control `C_*` satisfying

`|s_k(A_W C^(1/2))^2-s_k(A_W C_*^(1/2))^2|`
` <= ||G||_op sqrt(M/m) ||[G,C]||_F/gamma + 2M eta`.

Substituting the intrinsic rational derivative bound gives

`|s_k(A_W C^(1/2))^2-s_k(A_W C_*^(1/2))^2|`
` <= ||G||_op sqrt(M/m) L_R(H) epsilon/gamma + 2M eta`.

The destination accounting is therefore expression-free. Generator orientation supplies the tangent input, the rational derivative supplies local amplification, Gram gaps and cluster widths determine how much resulting orientation is spectrally visible, and `m,M` charge positive-metric degeneration.

A rational formula with dramatic intermediate condition numbers but bounded `L_R(H)` receives no extra credit.

## 7. Prior-art audit

The function-theoretic ingredients are classical and no novelty is claimed for them.

D. S. Kaliuzhnyi-Verbovetskyi and V. Vinnikov, **Noncommutative rational functions, their difference-differential calculus and realizations**, *Multidimensional Systems and Signal Processing* 23 (2012), 49--77, DOI `10.1007/s11045-010-0122-3`, develops difference-differential calculus and directional derivatives for noncommutative rational functions.

I. Klep, V. Vinnikov, and J. Volčič, **Local theory of free noncommutative functions: germs, meromorphic functions and Hermite interpolation**, *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 373 (2020), 5587--5625, DOI `10.1090/tran/8076`, treats free/noncommutative functions as maps preserving direct sums and similarities and develops their local differential structure.

J. Volčič, **Matrix coefficient realization theory of noncommutative rational functions**, *Journal of Algebra* 499 (2018), 397--437, DOI `10.1016/j.jalgebra.2017.12.009`, develops matrix-point realization theory and emphasizes minimal realizations precisely because noncommutative rational functions do not have a canonical expression form.

These sources already provide the ambient similarity-covariant and differential calculus. The identity here is an elementary specialization obtained by differentiating similarity covariance along `exp(tG)`; it is not claimed as a new theorem of free analysis.

The Mathia-specific contribution is narrower: applying that classical differential identity to the Wang Gram-orientation audit shows that the intermediate inverse ledger of `VIS-377` should not be interpreted as an intrinsic resource. The invariant local budget is the derivative of the represented rational map, which removes algebraically cancellable near-poles while retaining genuine singular sensitivity and composes directly with the `VIS-374` destination bound.

A repository duplication audit found the expression-level rational closure in `VIS-377` and the earlier Green-resolvent source identity in `VIS-292`, but no existing Visual Exploration finding that replaces expression-tree inverse conditioning by the function-level similarity derivative.

## Research consequence

The rational-calculus frontier should now be stated in function-level rather than syntax-level terms. A proposed Wang rational metric or transfer operator must identify the external generators, their Gram commutators, the evaluated rational function, and an intrinsic local sensitivity bound such as `L_R(H)`. Intermediate inverse norms remain useful for proving such a bound, but they are not themselves evidence of a new mechanism when rational identities can cancel them.

A genuine finite-dimensional rational escape from vanishing generator orientation therefore requires diverging intrinsic derivative norm. Near-pole resolvents are one real way to obtain this, but then the pole distance is an explicit sensitivity cost. Growing rational families, growing dimension, controlled singular limits, unbounded/infinite-dimensional operators, indefinite geometry, and nonlinear source-dependent mechanisms remain outside the fixed bounded derivative closure and must expose their own topology, domain, normalization, and destination cost.
