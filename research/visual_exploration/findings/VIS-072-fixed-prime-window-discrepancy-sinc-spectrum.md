# VIS-072 — fixed-prime window discrepancy is a sinc-filtered torus spectrum

## Claim

Fix a finite prime set `P` and write

`omega = (log p)_(p in P)`.

Let `f : T^|P| -> C` be continuous and, for a fixed window length `L>0`, define the frequency-preserving window observable

`B_L(theta) = (1/L) integral_0^L f(theta - t omega) dt`.

The deterministic prime-phase window beginning at height `h` is `B_L(-h omega)`. If `Theta` is Haar-uniform on the same finite torus, then for every bounded continuous `Psi : C -> C`,

`lim_(H->infinity) (1/H) integral_0^H Psi(B_L(-h omega)) dh`
` = E Psi(B_L(Theta))`.

Thus **at fixed finite prime support and fixed window length, the long-height population law of every continuous sliding-window statistic is exactly the law of the frequency-preserving random-initial-phase control**. Randomizing the initial torus phase and evolving with the same `(log p)` frequencies is not merely a plausible matched null; it is the vertical limiting law of the actual fixed-prime windows themselves.

For a trigonometric-polynomial observable

`f(theta) = sum_(m in M) fhat(m) exp(i m dot theta)`,

put

`lambda_m = m dot omega = sum_p m_p log p`.

Then the finite-window discrepancy from Haar has the exact expansion

`B_L(theta) - fhat(0)`
` = sum_(m != 0) fhat(m) exp(i m dot theta) exp(-i L lambda_m/2) sinc(L lambda_m/2)`,

where `sinc(x)=sin(x)/x` with `sinc(0)=1`. Since unique factorization gives `lambda_m != 0` for every nonzero integer vector `m`,

`|B_L(theta)-fhat(0)|`
` <= sum_(m != 0) |fhat(m)| min(1, 2/(L |lambda_m|))`.

Under the frequency-preserving Haar control,

`E B_L(Theta) = fhat(0)`

and

`E |B_L(Theta)-fhat(0)|^2`
` = sum_(m != 0) |fhat(m)|^2 sinc^2(L lambda_m/2)`.

So finite-window deviation is an exact **sinc filter of the log-rational small-divisor spectrum**. Any persistent bands, beating, anisotropy, or slow filling generated by these modes belong to the matched prime-torus null unless an independently defined coordinate or a growing-support limit changes the problem.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL KRONECKER/FOURIER SPECIALIZATION + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No useful uniform growing-support rate, exceptional-window theorem, hybrid factor/residual independence result, or RH consequence is claimed.

## 1. A fixed window is itself a torus observable

`VIS-071` proves that the vertical orbit

`theta(h) = -h omega mod 2 pi`

is Haar-equidistributed on the finite prime torus. For fixed `L`, continuity of `f` implies continuity of

`B_L(theta) = (1/L) integral_0^L f(theta-t omega) dt`.

Therefore `Psi o B_L` is continuous whenever `Psi` is bounded and continuous. Applying `VIS-071` directly to this new torus observable gives

`(1/H) integral_0^H Psi(B_L(theta(h))) dh`
` -> integral Psi(B_L(theta)) dtheta`.

The right-hand side is exactly the population law obtained by drawing one Haar initial phase and evolving it through the same deterministic frequency vector for the whole window.

This closes a distinction left open by `VIS-071`. A finite window can differ strongly from the Haar mean because the orbit may fill the torus slowly, but **the distribution of such finite-window deviations across starting heights is itself the frequency-preserving random-phase null law**. A long-height population comparison of fixed finite windows therefore cannot produce a second arithmetic population.

The statement is not about independently resampling phases at every point inside a window. That would destroy the deterministic temporal dependence and is a different stochastic object.

## 2. Exact sinc kernel for every Fourier mode

For the Fourier character `exp(i m dot theta)`,

`(1/L) integral_0^L exp(i m dot (theta-t omega)) dt`
` = exp(i m dot theta) (1/L) integral_0^L exp(-i t lambda_m) dt`.

For `m != 0`, `VIS-071` gives `lambda_m != 0`, and direct integration yields

`(1/L) integral_0^L exp(-i t lambda_m) dt`
` = exp(-i L lambda_m/2) sinc(L lambda_m/2)`.

The zero mode is unchanged. Summing the finitely many modes proves the displayed expansion.

The elementary bound

`|sinc x| <= min(1,1/|x|)`

then gives

`|B_L(theta)-fhat(0)|`
` <= sum_(m != 0) |fhat(m)| min(1,2/(L|lambda_m|))`.

This is uniform in the initial phase. It separates two causes of finite-window structure cleanly: the Fourier weight `|fhat(m)|` supplied by the chosen observable, and the small divisor `|lambda_m|` supplied by the prime-log Kronecker flow.

## 3. The matched null has an exact RMS spectrum

Let `Theta` be Haar-uniform. Orthogonality of distinct torus characters gives

`E exp(i(m-n) dot Theta) = 0` for `m != n`.

Consequently the nonzero modes in the window discrepancy are orthogonal in `L^2(T^|P|)`, so

`E |B_L(Theta)-fhat(0)|^2`
` = sum_(m != 0) |fhat(m)|^2 sinc^2(L lambda_m/2)`.

The null mean is exactly zero after subtracting the Haar mean, and the RMS finite-window scale is therefore not an empirical nuisance parameter: for a frozen trigonometric-polynomial witness it is a deterministic function of its Fourier coefficients and the prime-log small divisors.

More generally, the whole matched finite-window null is the Haar pushforward of the finite trigonometric polynomial

`theta -> sum_(m != 0) fhat(m) exp(i m dot theta) exp(-iL lambda_m/2) sinc(L lambda_m/2)`.

The variance formula is only its simplest exact summary.

## 4. Prime-log small divisors are log-rational gaps

For nonzero `m`, split positive and negative exponents and define coprime positive integers

`a_m = product_(m_p>0) p^(m_p)`,

`b_m = product_(m_p<0) p^(-m_p)`.

Then

`lambda_m = log(a_m/b_m)`.

Unique factorization gives `a_m != b_m`. If `Q_m=max(a_m,b_m)`, the elementary inequality `log(1+x)>=x/(1+x)` gives

`|lambda_m| >= 1/Q_m`.

Indeed, if `a_m>b_m`, then `a_m>=b_m+1`, so

`log(a_m/b_m) >= log(1+1/b_m) >= 1/(b_m+1) >= 1/a_m`;

the other ordering is symmetric. Also

`Q_m <= product_(p in P) p^(|m_p|)`.

Hence one completely explicit but generally crude bound is

`|B_L(theta)-fhat(0)|`
` <= sum_(m != 0) |fhat(m)| min(1, (2/L) product_p p^(|m_p|))`.

This bound is intentionally not advertised as a useful growing-support estimate. Its rapid deterioration is the point: qualitative rational independence is enough for fixed-finite equidistribution, but uniform control over increasingly complicated modes is a genuine Diophantine problem. Near-resonant ratios of `P`-smooth integers can make a finite orbit look highly structured long before the eventual Haar law becomes visible.

## 5. Visual interpretation and control consequence

For a frozen finite observable, the natural diagnostic picture is not “arithmetic data versus random noise.” It is a **resonance spectrum** indexed by integer mode vectors `m`, with horizontal coordinate `lambda_m=log(a_m/b_m)` and window response `sinc(L lambda_m/2)` weighted by `fhat(m)`.

Changing `L` sweeps a deterministic low-frequency filter across this fixed log-rational spectrum. Long-lived ridges or beat patterns that track modes with small `|lambda_m|` are therefore expected null geometry. A visual statistic only escapes this obstruction if its information is not a fixed continuous functional of the same finite prime torus.

This also sharpens the admissible use of finite windows. A **specific externally anchored window** may still be tested against the exact frequency-preserving null if the anchor is independent of the prime-torus statistic and selection is controlled. But treating many ordinary starting heights as an arithmetic population distinct from randomized initial phases is closed by the first part of this finding.

## 6. Prior art and novelty boundary

The ingredients are classical. `VIS-071` already records the Kronecker--Weyl/Bohr vertical-limit background and proves the prime-log rational independence needed here. The present sinc factor is simply the exact integral of one Fourier character over a finite continuous-time window.

L. Kuipers and H. Niederreiter, **Uniform Distribution of Sequences**, Wiley (1974), and M. Drmota and R. F. Tichy, **Sequences, Discrepancies and Applications**, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1651, Springer (1997), are standard references for the Fourier/Weyl and quantitative-discrepancy framework in which small divisors control rates of equidistribution. No new general discrepancy theorem is claimed.

The Mathia-specific contribution is only the control diagnosis obtained by specializing that elementary Fourier calculation to the prime-log torus already isolated in `VIS-067`--`VIS-071`: the matched finite-window null is a sinc-filtered spectrum of log-rational resonances, and the actual sliding-window population converges to that same null.

## 7. Boundary and falsification

Everything remains fixed finite: prime support, Fourier support, window length, and observable. The exact spectral formulas above require a trigonometric polynomial; the sliding-window population-law statement only requires continuous `f` because it follows from the continuous torus map `B_L` and `VIS-071`.

The result does not provide a useful bound uniform in a prime cutoff `X=X(H)`, Fourier degree, coordinate dimension, adaptive witness, or moving window geometry. It also does not apply to an independently defined zero factor or hybrid residual merely because those objects are evaluated at the same heights.

Falsify the population-law claim by exhibiting fixed finite prime support, fixed `L`, continuous `f`, and bounded continuous `Psi` whose long-start-height average differs from the Haar-initial-phase expectation. Falsify the spectral formula by exhibiting a Fourier mode whose direct finite-time integral differs from the displayed sinc kernel.

## Research consequence

The fixed-finite **finite-window population-separation route is closed**, not merely the infinite-window limit. Finite-window deviations remain real and can be large, but across starting heights their population is exactly the frequency-preserving random-initial-phase control in the long-height limit, and for frozen trigonometric witnesses their scale is governed explicitly by the sinc-filtered log-rational small-divisor spectrum.

The surviving prime-phase routes must therefore cross a boundary excluded here: support/degree/dimension growing with height together with genuinely uniform quantitative control, an externally anchored window whose anchor contributes independent information and whose selection is audited, or an independently defined factor/residual coordinate not reconstructible from the finite prime torus.
