# VIS-398 — finite additive shared-gamma networks remain exactly calibratable qutrit nuisance families

## Claim

Continue in the generic rank-one qutrit setting of `VIS-388`--`VIS-397`, with positive commutator-Laplacian support

`0 < q_1 < q_2 < q_3`

and positive tangent sectors `H_1,H_2,H_3`.

Let the source-free sector energies be generated by three idiosyncratic Gamma blocks together with an arbitrary finite collection of additive shared Gamma blocks. For `i=1,2,3`, let

`X_i ~ Gamma(k_i, rate=lambda_i)`

independently, and for `b=1,...,B` let

`C_b ~ Gamma(h_b, rate=mu_b)`

independently of each other and of the `X_i`. Fix nonzero incidence/loading vectors

`a_b=(a_1b,a_2b,a_3b) in [0,infinity)^3`

and define

`Y_i = X_i + sum_b a_ib C_b`.

Normalize by

`W_i = Y_i/(Y_1+Y_2+Y_3)`.

Write

`K=sum_i k_i`, `H=sum_b h_b`, `M=K+H`.

For `w` in the open probability simplex, define

`x_i(w,r)=w_i-sum_b a_ib r_b`

and the latent-fraction domain

`R(w)={r in [0,infinity)^B : x_i(w,r)>0 for i=1,2,3}`.

Then `W=(W_1,W_2,W_3)` has the exact density

`f_W(w)`
` = Gamma(M)`
`   * prod_i [lambda_i^k_i/Gamma(k_i)]`
`   * prod_b [mu_b^h_b/Gamma(h_b)]`
`   * integral_(R(w))`
`       [prod_i x_i(w,r)^(k_i-1)] [prod_b r_b^(h_b-1)]`
`       / A(w,r)^M dr`,

where

`A(w,r)=sum_i lambda_i x_i(w,r)+sum_b mu_b r_b`.

Thus **every finite additive Gamma common-shock network with fixed nonnegative incidence vectors remains an exact finite-dimensional normalized-simplex nuisance family**. The single common block of `VIS-396`--`VIS-397` is only the `B=1`, `a_b=(1,1,1)` specialization.

For `i!=j`,

`Cov(Y_i,Y_j)=sum_b a_ib a_jb h_b/mu_b^2`.

Hence several shared blocks can generate a much richer positive-dependence pattern than one common shock. In particular, pair-specific blocks with incidence vectors `(1,1,0)`, `(1,0,1)`, `(0,1,1)`, optionally together with a fully common `(1,1,1)` block, supply independently interpretable cross-sector covariance channels while staying inside the exact formula above.

Fixing the qutrit first moment

`x=m_1=sum_i q_i w_i`

and using the affine residual coordinate `w(u)` from `VIS-389`, the conditional residual density is exactly

`p(u | x,q,parameters) proportional_to f_W(w(u))`.

The nuisance CDF is therefore obtained by finite-dimensional integration over the latent fractions followed by one-dimensional normalization in `u`. No closed form is required for exact specification, and no parameter may be fitted to the candidate residual being judged.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + SOURCE-FREE FINITE LATENT-GAMMA CONTROL + CLASSICAL VARIABLES-IN-COMMON/COMMON-SHOCK PRIOR ART + NEGATIVE CALIBRATION + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM FOR THE PROBABILITY CONSTRUCTION`.

No Wang anomaly, arithmetic source signal, destination gain, zeta-zero statement, or RH consequence is claimed.

## 1. Exact latent-fiber representation

For fixed positive `y=(y_1,y_2,y_3)`, write the latent shared vector as `c=(c_1,...,c_B)` and set

`x_i(y,c)=y_i-sum_b a_ib c_b`.

The joint density of `Y` is the finite latent convolution

`f_Y(y)=integral_(D(y)) prod_i f_Xi(x_i(y,c)) prod_b f_Cb(c_b) dc`,

where

`D(y)={c>=0 : x_i(y,c)>0 for all i}`.

Now use the normalized coordinates

`y_i=t w_i`, `sum_i w_i=1`, `t>0`,

and latent fractions

`c_b=t r_b`.

The ordinary simplex change of variables `y -> (w_1,w_2,t)` contributes `t^2`, while the `B` latent changes `c_b=t r_b` contribute `t^B`. The full Jacobian is therefore

`t^(B+2)`.

The Gamma power factors contribute

`t^[sum_i(k_i-1)+sum_b(h_b-1)] = t^(M-B-3)`.

Multiplying by the Jacobian leaves exactly `t^(M-1)`. The exponential factor is

`exp[-t A(w,r)]`,

with

`A(w,r)=sum_i lambda_i[w_i-sum_b a_ib r_b]+sum_b mu_b r_b`.

On `R(w)`, every idiosyncratic fraction is positive, all rates are positive, and hence `A(w,r)>0`. Integrating the total scale gives

`integral_0^infinity t^(M-1) exp[-A t] dt = Gamma(M)/A^M`,

which proves the stated density.

No asymptotic approximation or independence claim after normalization is used.

## 2. Single-common-block specialization

Take `B=1` and `a_1=(1,1,1)`. Then

`R(w)={0<=r<min_i w_i}`

and

`x_i(w,r)=w_i-r`.

The formula becomes

`f_W(w) proportional_to integral_0^(min_i w_i)`
`  r^(h_1-1) prod_i(w_i-r)^(k_i-1)`
`  / [mu_1 r+sum_i lambda_i(w_i-r)]^M dr`.

After the substitution `r_old=3r`, this is exactly the unequal-rate one-common-block formula in `VIS-397`. Thus the general finite-network expression genuinely extends the previous control rather than changing its normalization convention.

## 3. Pair-specific shocks already span richer positive dependence

For independent latent blocks, covariance is additive:

`Cov(Y_i,Y_j)=sum_b a_ib a_jb Var(C_b)`
`              =sum_b a_ib a_jb h_b/mu_b^2`.

With pair-specific unit-incidence blocks `C_12,C_13,C_23`,

`Y_1=X_1+C_12+C_13`,
`Y_2=X_2+C_12+C_23`,
`Y_3=X_3+C_13+C_23`,

one has

`Cov(Y_1,Y_2)=Var(C_12)`,
`Cov(Y_1,Y_3)=Var(C_13)`,
`Cov(Y_2,Y_3)=Var(C_23)`.

A further fully common block adds the same positive amount to all three off-diagonal covariances. Therefore unequal pairwise positive correlation, subset-specific common shocks, and several latent dependence scales are all source-free possibilities within an exactly specified family.

This does **not** imply that every positive-dependence law is representable by a small Gamma network. It establishes only that finite additive shared-Gamma architecture is not an analytically uncontrolled escape from the earlier nulls.

## 4. Conditioning on the one-dimensional qutrit residual

For fixed qutrit support and first moment, the simplex slice

`sum_i q_i w_i=x`

is the same affine segment used in `VIS-389`--`VIS-397`. Parametrize it by `0<u<1`, giving an affine `w(u)` with constant slice Jacobian.

Consequently

`p(u|x) proportional_to f_W(w(u))`.

For a fixed finite block architecture, evaluating this law requires only the `B`-dimensional integral over `R(w(u))` and the final one-dimensional normalization in `u`. This is an exact probability model even when no elementary antiderivative exists.

The distinction between **specified** and **fitted** nuisance structure is essential. Block incidences, shapes, rates, and any loading coefficients must come from the source-free architecture, independent calibration data, or pre-declared matched controls. Adding blocks until the candidate residual is reproduced would make the null post-hoc and destroy the intended falsification test.

## 5. Consequence for the Wang localization clue

`VIS-397` left several subset-specific shared blocks as the next obvious source-free dependence extension. The theorem above closes that entire finite additive-Gamma branch at once: increasing from one common shock to any fixed finite latent Gamma network increases integration dimension and nuisance flexibility, but it does not remove exact conditional calibratability.

The next useful source-free extension must therefore change mechanism, not merely add another finite Gamma common-shock block. Examples include genuinely non-Gamma packet-amplitude laws, signed or negative-dependence mechanisms not representable by nonnegative additive shocks, continuous/infinite latent mixtures, or a Wang admissibility constraint that derives a narrower coupled law from the construction itself.

Those are separate research questions and are not pursued here.

## Prior-art and novelty audit

Additive variables-in-common and multivariate-Gamma constructions are classical. A. M. Mathai and P. G. Moschopoulos, **On a multivariate gamma**, *Journal of Multivariate Analysis* 39:1 (1991), 135--153, DOI `10.1016/0047-259X(91)90010-Y`, study positively correlated multivariate Gamma constructions and density representations. Their **A form of multivariate gamma distribution**, *Annals of the Institute of Statistical Mathematics* 44:1 (1992), 97--106, DOI `10.1007/BF00048672`, studies multivariate Gamma laws built from sums of independent Gamma variables and exact density/conditional structure.

The broader variables-in-common/additive-model tradition is surveyed in N. Balakrishnan and C. D. Lai, **Continuous Bivariate Distributions**, 2nd ed., Springer (2009), especially the variables-in-common and bivariate-Gamma chapters. Modern common-shock literature likewise treats observations as linear combinations of idiosyncratic and shared shock components; for example Greg Taylor and Phuong Anh Vu, **Auto-balanced common shock claim models**, *Annals of Actuarial Science* 17:3 (2023), 580--605, DOI `10.1017/S1748499523000064`.

No novelty is claimed for latent additive Gamma/common-shock modelling. The Mathia-specific result is the explicit normalized-simplex fiber integral for an arbitrary fixed finite Gamma incidence network in the qutrit nuisance geometry and the resulting boundary: several shared latent Gamma blocks remain conditionally calibratable rather than constituting source evidence by themselves.

## Boundaries

- The number `B` of shared blocks is finite and fixed independently of the candidate residual.
- Shared contributions are additive and nonnegative, with fixed nonnegative incidence/loadings `a_ib`.
- All latent blocks are independent before they are mixed into the sector energies.
- Shapes and rates are fixed or independently calibrated; post-hoc architecture search is excluded.
- The formula allows arbitrary positive Gamma shapes even though integer shapes retain the direct replicated-Gaussian packet interpretation.
- The qutrit source support and conditioning value `m_1` are fixed.
- The result covers positive dependence generated by additive nonnegative shocks; it does not cover arbitrary copulas, negative dependence, signed latent factors, non-Gamma amplitudes, or infinite latent mixtures.
- Exact finite-dimensional integral representation means exact specification and arbitrarily accurate numerical calibration, not a claim of elementary closed form or negligible computational cost.
- No retained visualization is required because the useful result is an exact representation-independent probability decomposition once the qutrit reduction is fixed.
