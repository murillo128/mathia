# VIS-133 — bounded-tent CUE edge amplitudes retain an order-one covariance kernel

## Claim

Continue the bounded-source CUE construction of `VIS-130`--`VIS-132`. Let `U_N` be Haar distributed in `U(N)`, write its eigenangles as `theta_j in (-pi,pi]`, and unfold by

`y_j = N theta_j/(2 pi)`.

Assume

`N/L -> rho > 0`,  `M/N -> alpha in (0,1)`,

with a fixed non-wrapping source margin. Put

`h_M(y)=(1-2|y|/M)_+`,  `H_2=M/3`,

and, for a fixed scaled support-edge coordinate `v`, define the tapered edge amplitude

`Z_(N,v) = sum_j h_M(y_j) exp(2 pi i (1+v/L)y_j)`.

Let

`X_(N,v) = [Z_(N,v)-E Z_(N,v)] / sqrt(H_2)`.

Then for every fixed pair `v,w`,

`E[X_(N,v) overline{X_(N,w)}] -> R_(alpha,rho)(v-w)`,

where

`R_(alpha,rho)(d)`
` = (3/alpha) hat f_alpha(rho d)`
` = 6[pi alpha rho d - sin(pi alpha rho d)]`
`   / [pi^3 alpha^3 (rho d)^3]`

for `d != 0`, with the continuous value `R_(alpha,rho)(0)=1`, and

`f_alpha(theta)=(1-|theta|/(pi alpha))_+^2`.

In particular, every individual normalized edge amplitude has limiting variance one. For any finite set of distinct edge coordinates, the limiting covariance matrix is strictly positive definite. Thus the bounded source taper does **not** make nearby support-edge amplitudes asymptotically independent or self-average them as `N,L` grow at fixed ratios.

There is a second exact structural point relevant to the active Montgomery-weighted pair statistic. Since

`W_L(x)=1/(1+(pi x/L)^2)`
`      = integral_R L exp(-2L|r|) exp(2 pi i r x) dr`,

the realized source-weighted pair sum at `q=1+v/L` is an exponential average of squared amplitudes,

`sum_(j,k) h_M(y_j) h_M(y_k) W_L(y_j-y_k)`
`  * exp(2 pi i q(y_j-y_k))`
` = integral_R exp(-2|u|) |Z_(N,v+u)|^2 du`.

The kernel `exp(-2|u|)` has a fixed width in the scaled edge coordinate `v`. Therefore increasing `L` does not widen this averaging window into an increasing collection of decorrelated edge amplitudes.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + ASYMPTOTIC CUE TRACE-COVARIANCE CONTROL + NEGATIVE STOCHASTIC CONTROL + CLASSICAL FOURIER/POISSON INGREDIENTS + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This result does **not** determine the covariance of the quadratic off-diagonal pair statistic. In particular, order-one amplitude covariance does not by itself prove an order-one variance floor for the intensity field `|X|^2`; that requires fourth moments/cumulants under the exact source-window convention.

## 1. The tapered edge amplitude is a high-frequency CUE linear statistic

Set

`a_alpha(theta)=(1-|theta|/(pi alpha))_+`.

Because `y=N theta/(2 pi)` and `M/N->alpha`, the fixed-ratio version of the source taper is `a_alpha(theta)` on one non-wrapping chart. At the support edge,

`2 pi (1+v/L)y = (N + rho_L v) theta`,

where `rho_L=N/L`. Thus

`Z_(N,v)=sum_j a_(alpha_N)(theta_j) exp(i t_(N,v) theta_j)`,

with `alpha_N=M/N` and `t_(N,v)=N+rho_L v`.

For fixed `alpha`, define the continuous Fourier coefficient of the tent

`b_alpha(xi)`
` = (1/(2 pi)) integral_(-pi alpha)^(pi alpha)`
`     a_alpha(theta) exp(-i xi theta) dtheta`
` = (alpha/2) sinc_0^2(pi alpha xi/2)`,

where `sinc_0(z)=sin(z)/z` with `sinc_0(0)=1`.

Hence `b_alpha(xi)=O((1+|xi|)^-2)`. The mean is

`E Z_(N,v)=N b_alpha(N+rho_L v)=O(N^-1)`,

so it is negligible on the natural `sqrt(H_2)=Theta(sqrt(N))` fluctuation scale.

## 2. Exact CUE trace covariance leaves a terminal-frequency packet

For CUE linear statistics, the exact trace covariance used already in `VIS-132` gives

`Cov(Z_(N,v), overline{Z_(N,w)})`
` = sum_(k in Z, k!=0) min(N,|k|)`
`     b_alpha(k-t_(N,v)) b_alpha(k-t_(N,w))`

for the fixed-ratio tent, with the same formula up to the convergent parameter perturbation when `alpha_N->alpha`.

Write `k=N+n`. After division by `N`, each fixed `n` has

`min(N,|N+n|)/N -> 1`,

while the `O((1+|xi|)^-2)` decay supplies an absolutely summable dominating sequence. Therefore

`N^-1 Cov(Z_(N,v), overline{Z_(N,w)})`
` -> sum_(n in Z) b_alpha(n-rho v)b_alpha(n-rho w)`.

The important point is that the oscillation near the CUE terminal frequency `N` does not disappear under the tent. It selects a fixed packet of integer Fourier modes `k=N+n`, and each such mode has trace variance asymptotic to `N`.

## 3. The packet sum is exactly the normalized tent-square transform

The packet sum can be evaluated without approximation. Expanding the two factors `b_alpha` and summing over the integer translate gives Poisson summation in the form

`sum_(n in Z) b_alpha(n-a)b_alpha(n-b)`
` = (1/(2 pi)) integral a_alpha(theta)^2`
`     exp(i(a-b)theta) dtheta`.

The no-alias step uses `alpha<1`: if both tent factors are supported in `[-pi alpha,pi alpha]`, the constraint generated by the integer sum is `theta+phi=2 pi m`, and only `m=0` intersects the product support away from a measure-zero boundary.

With

`f_alpha(theta)=a_alpha(theta)^2`

this is `hat f_alpha(b-a)`. Direct integration, already used at integer frequencies in `VIS-132`, is valid for real `s` and gives

`hat f_alpha(s)`
` = 2[pi alpha s-sin(pi alpha s)]/(pi^3 alpha^2 s^3)`,

with `hat f_alpha(0)=alpha/3`.

Since `H_2=M/3 ~ alpha N/3`, division by `H_2` proves the covariance kernel in the claim.

The kernel is not merely nonzero on the diagonal. For distinct fixed coordinates `v_1,...,v_r` and coefficients `c_1,...,c_r`,

`sum_(i,j) c_i overline{c_j} R_(alpha,rho)(v_i-v_j)`
` = (3/(2 pi alpha)) integral_(-pi alpha)^(pi alpha)`
`     a_alpha(theta)^2`
`     |sum_i c_i exp(i rho v_i theta)|^2 dtheta`.

The right-hand side is strictly positive for every nonzero coefficient vector, because a nonzero finite exponential polynomial cannot vanish on an interval. Thus the limiting covariance matrix is strictly positive definite: there is no finite exact linear contrast of distinct edge amplitudes that removes this order-one CUE fluctuation.

## 4. Montgomery weighting averages a fixed correlated edge band

The identity

`W_L(x)=integral_R L exp(-2L|r|) exp(2 pi i r x) dr`

follows by the elementary Fourier transform of the Laplace density. Insert it into the realized double sum and interchange the finite sum with the integral. With

`Z_N(s)=sum_j h_M(y_j) exp(2 pi i s y_j)`

one obtains exactly

`sum_(j,k) h_M(y_j)h_M(y_k)W_L(y_j-y_k)`
`  * exp(2 pi i q(y_j-y_k))`
` = integral_R L exp(-2L|r|)|Z_N(q+r)|^2 dr`.

At `q=1+v/L`, put `u=Lr`; the right-hand side becomes

`integral_R exp(-2|u|)|Z_(N,v+u)|^2 du`.

The diagonal part `sum_j h_M(y_j)^2` is independent of `u` and the kernel has unit mass, recovering exactly the realized common mode isolated in `VIS-132`. Samplewise diagonal subtraction therefore converts the same identity into an exponential average of the off-diagonal intensity contribution.

This representation rules out one tempting but invalid self-averaging argument. The physical Montgomery width shrinks like `1/L` in the Fourier variable precisely so that, after support-edge rescaling, it becomes a **fixed** `u`-kernel. At the same time, the normalized amplitudes inside that kernel retain the fixed covariance `R_(alpha,rho)`. Nothing in the taper or Montgomery convolution alone creates an averaging population whose effective size diverges with `L`.

## Prior-art and novelty assessment

The exact CUE trace covariance and the Fourier representation of CUE linear eigenvalue statistics are classical. Persi Diaconis and Steven N. Evans, **Linear functionals of eigenvalues of random matrices**, *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 353:7 (2001), 2615–2633, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-01-02800-8`, provide the trace-moment/covariance machinery used here. `VIS-132` already uses the same source for the tapered diagonal linear statistic.

Poisson summation, the Fourier transform of a triangular window, and the Laplace/Fourier representation of `1/(1+x^2)` are classical harmonic analysis. `VIS-131` already uses Poisson summation to derive the bounded-tent mean edge profile.

There is also substantial prior art on fluctuations of CUE pair-dependent statistics. Aguirre--Soshnikov--Sumpter and subsequent work of Soshnikov establish Gaussian fluctuation results for smooth pair/local-correlation statistics in several global, mesoscopic, and local regimes. Those results reinforce that pair-statistic covariance is a genuine higher-order problem; they are not imported here because the present observable has an explicit bounded source taper, Montgomery difference weight, noninteger terminal-frequency phase, and the `VIS-130` source-window replacement convention.

The contribution recorded here is therefore only a control specialization for Mathia's frozen support-edge construction: the high-frequency tapered **amplitude** covariance has an explicit nonvanishing limit, and the Montgomery weight acts on a fixed correlated scaled band. No new random-matrix theorem or Gaussian limit is claimed.

## Boundary and falsification

The covariance limit assumes `N/L->rho>0` and `M/N->alpha in (0,1)` with fixed positive distance from the wrapping boundary. If the source fraction shrinks, approaches one, or the taper changes with `N`, the packet and alias analysis must be redone.

The result is second-order in the complex amplitudes. It does not supply the fourth-order quantity

`Cov(|Z_(N,v)|^2, |Z_(N,w)|^2)`

needed for the projected pair-statistic covariance. In particular, one must not replace the amplitude field by a Gaussian field merely because fixed smooth CUE linear statistics satisfy classical CLTs: the carrier frequency here grows with `N` and sits at the terminal `k~N` scale. Any Gaussian or Wick reduction must be proved for this triangular-array regime or replaced by an exact CUE fourth-moment/cumulant calculation.

Likewise, the strictly positive amplitude covariance does not prove that every quadratic contrast has an order-one variance floor; fourth-order connected terms can change the answer. Conversely, any claim that the quadratic statistic self-averages because the taper spans `Theta(N)` eigenvalues or because Montgomery weighting “smooths” frequencies is now insufficient: neither mechanism produces second-order amplitude decorrelation in the frozen scaling.

This is CUE-side only. It does not establish finite-height zeta-to-CUE transfer, independence of separated zeta height windows, an arithmetic lower-order term, Montgomery's pair-correlation conjecture, or any implication toward RH.

## Research consequence

The active support-edge clue can now narrow its covariance gate. The mean profile, effective-size sensitivity, realized diagonal common mode, and second-order edge-amplitude covariance are controlled. The next unresolved object is specifically the **connected fourth-order covariance of the samplewise diagonal-subtracted intensity field after the fixed Laplace average**, under the exact `VIS-130` finite-CUE/tent convention.

A successful next step must determine whether that quadratic covariance has an order-one floor, a decaying scale, or removable nuisance eigendirections. If it decays, the mechanism must come from genuine fourth-order cancellation rather than from an assumed growth of the smoothing bandwidth or independence of edge amplitudes. Only after that covariance and the finite-height transfer are fixed should untouched zeta confirmation be inspected.
