# VIS-422 — Gram-point prime phases converge to the fixed-shell Haar null

## Claim

Let `g_k` be the Gram points, indexed in any eventually consistent way by the equation

`theta(g_k) = k pi`

(up to an irrelevant fixed index shift), where `theta` is the Riemann--Siegel theta function. Fix distinct primes `p_1,...,p_d`. Then

`((p_1^(-i g_k),...,p_d^(-i g_k)))_(k>=1)`

is equidistributed on the `d`-torus with Haar measure.

Consequently, for any **fixed finite Wang shell** whose time dependence is a Laurent character polynomial in finitely many base-prime phases, the empirical distribution obtained by sampling that shell at Gram points is exactly the same asymptotic law as the fixed-shell prime-torus Haar self-null from `VIS-419`.

In particular, if `F` is one frozen shell, `M=||F||_infinity`, and

`A(t)=|F(t)|/M`,

then for every continuity point `a` of the Haar distribution function,

`(1/N) #{k<=N : A(g_k)<=a} -> P_Haar(A<=a)`.

The same conclusion holds for every fixed-radius continuous persistence statistic

`Q_rho(t)=sup_(|u|<=rho) |F(t+u)|/M`

with fixed `rho`.

Therefore **ordinary Gram points cannot supply an asymptotic fixed-shell low-tail excess relative to the exact Haar self-null**. They are a legitimate deterministic external selector, but in the fixed-shell regime their prime-phase law is already the null law.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + DECISIVE-NEGATIVE CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This finding makes no claim about a shell family whose support, coefficients, bandwidth, normalization, or persistence radius grow with `k`; that triangular-array regime still requires a uniform theorem.

## 1. Direct prior art already gives the prime-torus limit

Antanas Laurincikas, *Joint Discrete Approximation of Analytic Functions by Shifts of the Riemann Zeta-Function Twisted by Gram Points*, Mathematics 11 (2023), 565, DOI `10.3390/math11030565`, defines Gram points `t_k` by the Riemann--Siegel phase equation and studies the probability measures generated by

`(p^(-i t_k^alpha) : p prime)`.

Its Lemma 9 proves weak convergence to Haar measure on the infinite prime torus for fixed positive exponents `alpha<=1` (and jointly for distinct such exponents). Taking the one-coordinate case `alpha=1` gives exactly

`(p^(-i t_k) : p prime) => Haar`.

Projecting the infinite product torus onto any fixed finite set of primes preserves weak convergence. A fixed shift in Gram-point indexing does not change the limiting empirical measure.

Thus the prime-phase equidistribution needed here is **prior art**, not a new Mathia theorem. The Mathia consequence below is only its specialization to the shell representation already isolated in `VIS-419`.

## 2. Transfer to a fixed Wang shell

By `VIS-419`, a canonical finite Wang shell can be written

`F(t)=G(z_1(t),...,z_d(t))`,

where

`z_j(t)=p_j^(-it)`

for the finitely many base primes occurring in the shell and `G` is a finite Laurent character polynomial. Ratio-mode phases are not independent coordinates; they are characters of this same base-prime torus.

Because `G` is continuous on the compact torus, so is

`Phi_A(z)=|G(z)|/M`.

Weak convergence of the Gram-point phase vectors to Haar therefore implies weak convergence of the normalized shell amplitudes by the continuous-mapping theorem. Equivalently, empirical expectations of every bounded continuous test function of `A(g_k)` converge to the corresponding Haar expectation, and the distribution functions converge at their continuity points.

This is the exact asymptotic comparison required by the fixed-shell part of `CLUE-wang-phase-anchored-small-values`: no separately simulated ratio-phase null and no long translation window is needed.

## 3. Fixed-radius persistence is also a continuous torus observable

For fixed `rho`, define

`H(z,u)=G(z_1 p_1^(-iu),...,z_d p_d^(-iu))`.

The map `(z,u) -> H(z,u)` is continuous on the compact set `T^d x [-rho,rho]`. Hence

`Phi_Q(z)=sup_(|u|<=rho) |H(z,u)|/M`

is continuous in `z` (uniform continuity on the compact product suffices).

Therefore sampling `Q_rho` at Gram points also converges to the pushforward of Haar under exactly the same fixed-shell persistence observable. The conclusion is stronger than a statement about mean energy: the full asymptotic distribution of every fixed-radius continuous persistence statistic agrees with its Haar self-null.

## 4. What this kills and what it does not

This closes **Gram points as an asymptotically anomalous selector for any one frozen finite shell**. A finite panel can of course fluctuate, but a claimed persistent low-tail bias cannot survive merely by taking more ordinary Gram points while keeping the shell and statistic fixed.

The result does not close all deterministic arithmetic selectors. In particular it says nothing about a selector whose prime-phase image is not Haar, or about conditioning/subselecting Gram points by additional arithmetic information. Any such selector must be specified independently of the observed Wang-shell values and must be audited for the information it imports.

It also does not justify a physical growing-shell limit. If the shell `F_k`, its base-prime support, bandwidth, coefficients, normalization, or radius `rho_k` change with `g_k`, fixed-test-function weak convergence is insufficient. A positive claim there would need a quantitative uniform version strong enough for the changing observables, consistent with the small-divisor and transport warnings in `VIS-420` and `VIS-421`.

## Prior-art boundary

The load-bearing equidistribution theorem is Laurincikas (2023), Lemma 9; the paper proves the prime-torus Haar limit in a stronger infinite-product setting as part of a discrete universality argument. No novelty is claimed for Gram-point equidistribution, Weyl/Fourier criteria, or Haar convergence.

The only Mathia-specific step is the exact transfer through the already-persisted Wang base-prime character representation (`VIS-419`) and the resulting negative control for the accepted phase-anchoring clue.
