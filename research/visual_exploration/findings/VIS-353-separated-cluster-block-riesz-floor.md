# VIS-353 — bounded separated Wang clusters have a uniform block Riesz floor

## Claim

Fix a finite real interval `I` of positive length. Fix an integer `K>=1`, positive integers

`n_1,...,n_K`,

and constants `R>0`, `Delta>0`.

For each cluster `a=1,...,K`, let

`lambda_(a,0),...,lambda_(a,n_a-1)`

be a real ordered multiset, with repeated nodes allowed inside the same cluster, and define the cluster-local generalized divided-difference functions

`psi_(a,k)(x)=[lambda_(a,0),...,lambda_(a,k)](lambda -> exp(-i lambda x))`, `0<=k<n_a`.

Assume the complete spectrum has bounded diameter

`max_(a,j) lambda_(a,j) - min_(a,j) lambda_(a,j) <= R`,

and distinct clusters remain spectrally separated:

`|lambda_(a,j)-lambda_(b,l)| >= Delta`

whenever `a != b`.

Then there is a constant

`kappa=kappa(I,K,(n_a),R,Delta)>0`

such that for every admissible node family and every block coefficient vector `u=(u_(a,k))`,

`|| sum_(a=1)^K sum_(k=0)^(n_a-1) u_(a,k) psi_(a,k) ||_(L^2(I)) >= kappa ||u||_2`.

Consequently,

`sup_(x in I) |sum_(a,k) u_(a,k) psi_(a,k)(x)| >= kappa |I|^(-1/2) ||u||_2`.

Thus a fixed finite family of bounded collision clusters cannot obtain an additional smallness factor by cancellation **between** cluster subspaces when the clusters remain separated and the total phase span stays bounded. In stable local divided-difference coordinates, the union of the cluster blocks has a uniform lower frame bound.

Combined with `VIS-352`, suppose cluster `a` contains a fixed confluent Wang block with total multiplicity `N_a`, physical collision scale `delta_a`, nondegenerate normalized shape, raw polynomial coefficient vector `c_a`, and local stable coordinate vector `u_a`. Then

`||u_a||_2 >= C_a delta_a^(N_a-1) ||c_a||_2`,

so in the bounded separated-block regime,

`||F||_(L^2(I))`
` >= kappa (sum_a ||u_a||_2^2)^(1/2)`
` >= C (sum_a delta_a^(2(N_a-1)) ||c_a||_2^2)^(1/2)`

for a uniform `C>0` determined by the fixed geometric data.

There is therefore no second, independent polynomial gain available from summing already-priced separated clusters. Any further collapse must come from degeneration of the assumptions: an additional inter-cluster collision scale, growing cluster count/total multiplicity, unbounded spectral span or density, a shrinking reference window, or a genuinely small/singular source-to-polynomial-coefficient map.

**Evidence/status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED + CLASSICAL DIVIDED-DIFFERENCE/RIESZ CONDITIONING + STRUCTURAL NEGATIVE CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This is a finite-dimensional compactness statement. It does not provide a uniform lower frame bound when the number of clusters grows, when the total spectral diameter grows without control, when distinct cluster spectra approach each other, or when the reference interval changes with `X`.

## 1. Remove the irrelevant common translation

Let

`lambda_* = min_(a,j) lambda_(a,j)`.

Replacing every spectral node by

`tilde lambda_(a,j)=lambda_(a,j)-lambda_*`

multiplies every divided-difference function by the same unimodular factor `exp(-i lambda_* x)`. Therefore the `L^2(I)` norm of every linear combination is unchanged.

After this common translation all nodes lie in the compact interval `[0,R]`. The cross-cluster separation condition remains unchanged.

Hence the admissible node families form a closed subset of the finite product cube

`prod_a [0,R]^(n_a)`

cut out by the inequalities

`|lambda_(a,j)-lambda_(b,l)| >= Delta`, `a != b`.

This parameter set is compact. Collisions within a cluster remain allowed.

## 2. Each local divided-difference block is regular through collisions

For fixed `a`, generalized divided differences of

`lambda -> exp(-i lambda x)`

depend continuously on the node tuple, including repeated-node limits. At a node of multiplicity `m`, the limiting span is generated by

`exp(-i lambda x), x exp(-i lambda x), ..., x^(m-1) exp(-i lambda x)`

up to nonzero constants.

Thus the `n_a` local functions

`psi_(a,0),...,psi_(a,n_a-1)`

form a basis of the confluent exponential space associated with the spectral multiset of cluster `a`. This is exactly the stable coordinate mechanism already used in `VIS-351`.

## 3. Separated cluster blocks remain jointly independent

Because spectra from different clusters stay at distance at least `Delta`, no spectral node can belong to two distinct clusters. Suppose

`sum_(a,k) u_(a,k) psi_(a,k)(x)=0`

for almost every `x in I`.

The left side is an entire exponential polynomial in `x`, so vanishing on an interval forces it to vanish identically. Rewriting each local divided-difference block in its confluent derivative basis gives an identity of the form

`sum_r exp(-i nu_r x) P_r(x)=0`,

where the `nu_r` are distinct real frequencies and the `P_r` are polynomials whose degrees encode the corresponding multiplicities.

Distinct confluent exponentials are linearly independent. Therefore every `P_r` is zero, hence every local block coefficient is zero, and finally every `u_(a,k)=0` because each local divided-difference change of basis is invertible.

So the union of all cluster-local divided-difference bases is linearly independent at every admissible parameter point.

## 4. Compact Gram argument removes bounded inter-cluster cancellation

Let `G(Lambda)` be the Gram matrix of the complete ordered family

`{psi_(a,k) : 1<=a<=K, 0<=k<n_a}`

in `L^2(I)`.

Every Gram entry depends continuously on the node family `Lambda`, because generalized divided differences are continuous through within-cluster collisions. By the preceding section, `G(Lambda)` is positive definite at every admissible parameter point.

The smallest eigenvalue

`lambda_min(G(Lambda))`

is therefore a positive continuous function on the compact admissible parameter set. Hence

`lambda_0 = min_Lambda lambda_min(G(Lambda)) > 0`.

For the complete block coefficient vector `u`,

`||F||_2^2 = u^* G(Lambda) u >= lambda_0 ||u||_2^2`.

Taking `kappa=sqrt(lambda_0)` proves the claim.

The important point is the geometry of the parameter boundary. A uniform lower bound can fail only by leaving this compact separated regime: clusters collide with one another, the spectral span/count escapes, or another external scale degenerates. Cancellation between a fixed number of already-separated block subspaces is not itself an unpriced asymptotic resource.

## 5. Combination with the Hermite collision ledger

`VIS-352` prices one bounded cluster before clusters are combined. For cluster `a`, with total confluent multiplicity `N_a` and physical diameter `delta_a`, it gives

`||u_a||_2 >= C_a delta_a^(N_a-1) ||c_a||_2`

when the normalized internal shape remains nondegenerate.

Applying the present block lower bound to the union of clusters yields

`||F||_2 >= kappa (sum_a ||u_a||_2^2)^(1/2)`

and therefore

`||F||_2 >= C (sum_a delta_a^(2(N_a-1)) ||c_a||_2^2)^(1/2)`.

This is the correct two-level bookkeeping in the bounded regime:

- each intra-cluster collision is charged by its own Hermite power;
- the separated cluster union contributes only a fixed Riesz constant, not another power of the asymptotic parameter.

If two cluster centers start approaching so that the cross-cluster separation tends to zero, they should no longer be credited as two independent separated blocks. Merge them into a larger collision hierarchy and price the new scale by the same Hermite/divided-difference mechanism. If the number of blocks or their global span grows, that is a genuine new complexity parameter and lies outside the present compact argument.

## 6. Consequence for the Wang finite-bank loophole

For the confluent Wang tail

`E_(q,X)(xi)=sum_b exp(-i b xi) Q_(b,q,X)(xi)`,

suppose the active phases can be partitioned into a fixed number of collision clusters such that:

- each cluster has bounded total multiplicity;
- every internal collision scale is recorded as in `VIS-352`;
- distinct cluster spectra stay uniformly separated on the fixed reference interval;
- the total phase span stays bounded.

Then inter-cluster cancellation cannot supply an additional `X^-gamma` factor after the local collision powers have been charged. A claimed one-extra-order gain must already be visible in the locally priced cluster coefficients or in a hypothesis outside this regime.

Thus the finite frequency-independent bank has fewer remaining escape routes than the previous clue state suggested. The live bounded-dimensional question is primarily the map from Wang source/scale moments into the raw polynomial coefficient vectors. Global geometry becomes a distinct resource only when cluster count, total multiplicity, spectral span/density, or another scale grows with `X`.

## Prior-art audit

S. A. Avdonin and S. A. Ivanov, **Exponential Riesz bases of subspaces and divided differences**, *St. Petersburg Mathematical Journal* 13 (2001), 339--351; arXiv `math/0103160`, develops exponential divided differences precisely to obtain stable basis behavior for groups of close spectral points. Their framework is substantially stronger than the finite compactness argument used here and is direct prior art for treating separated close-point groups as Riesz-stable subspaces.

S. A. Avdonin and W. Moran, **Ingham-type inequalities and Riesz bases of divided differences**, *International Journal of Applied Mathematics and Computer Science* 11:4 (2001), 803--820, develops the corresponding Ingham/Riesz-basis inequalities for exponential divided differences under clustered spectra.

The present statement claims no new Riesz-basis theorem. It is the finite-dimensional Mathia accounting consequence of this classical machinery: after local phase collisions are represented in stable divided-difference coordinates, a bounded separated family of such blocks cannot manufacture an additional asymptotic gain by inter-block cancellation.

## Research consequence

Bounded inter-cluster cancellation is no longer an independent loophole in the Wang finite-bank ledger. Once each collision cluster is priced locally, any fixed number of clusters with bounded total span and uniform cross-separation has a uniform block Riesz floor. Further finite-bank work should therefore target either the source-to-polynomial-coefficient map itself or a genuinely growing/global regime: increasing multiplicity or cluster count, collapsing inter-cluster separations that create a new collision hierarchy, expanding spectral span/density, shrinking windows, or source/frequency-dependent structure.
