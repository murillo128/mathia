# VIS-202 — averaged four-form method has a sharp square-root-log cutoff threshold

## Claim

Keep the fixed-`A` strictly subcritical weighted-dephasing setting of `VIS-192`--`VIS-201`, with

`H=H(y) -> infinity`,

`H log y / y -> 0`,

and let `C_(y,H)(A)` be the normalized fixed-`A` near-collision energy of `VIS-195`.

After the averaged local-factor improvement of `VIS-201`, the generalized cutoff `X=(y/H)L` gives

`C_(y,H)(A)`
` <<_(A,alpha,v)`
`    (log y)^2/[log(H/L)]^4`
`    + (log y)^2/L^3`
`    + H/(y log y)`

whenever `L>=1` and `H/L -> infinity`.

Within this exact proof architecture, there exists a cutoff choice `L=L(y)` for which the displayed upper bound tends to zero **if and only if**

`log H / sqrt(log y) -> infinity`.

Thus `VIS-201` does more than improve the previous sufficient corridor: after its local-factor averaging, the remaining square-root-log separation is the sharp threshold of the current **dimension-four fiberwise sieve + taper-tail decomposition**. No scalar cutoff choice can make this bound prove `C_(y,H)(A)->0` when

`log H = O(sqrt(log y))`.

Conversely, whenever

`log H / sqrt(log y) -> infinity`

and the standing subcritical condition holds, the simple choice

`L=log y`

already makes all three terms vanish.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + METHOD-THRESHOLD + NEGATIVE/CONTROL FRONTIER + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This is a sharp threshold for the persisted upper-bound architecture, not for the true collision energy `C_(y,H)(A)`, the dephasing resource `D_v(y,H)`, or the optimal observation horizon.

## 1. Necessity for this bound

Suppose some admissible `L` makes the right-hand side tend to zero. The first term then forces

`(log y)^2/[log(H/L)]^4 -> 0`,

hence

`log(H/L)/sqrt(log y) -> infinity`.

Since `L>=1`,

`log(H/L) <= log H`.

Therefore necessarily

`log H / sqrt(log y) -> infinity`.

This implication uses only positivity of the three displayed terms. There is no cancellation between the sieve contribution and the taper tail inside the present majorant.

The tail term independently requires

`(log y)^2/L^3 -> 0`,

or equivalently

`L/(log y)^(2/3) -> infinity`.

That condition cannot rescue a horizon with `log H=O(sqrt(log y))`, because increasing `L` only decreases `log(H/L)` and therefore weakens the sieve term.

## 2. Sufficiency once `log H` dominates `sqrt(log y)`

Assume now

`log H / sqrt(log y) -> infinity`.

Take

`L=log y`.

Then

`(log y)^2/L^3 = 1/log y -> 0`.

Moreover

`log L = log log y = o(sqrt(log y)) = o(log H)`,

so

`log(H/L) = log H - log log y ~ log H`.

Hence

`(log y)^2/[log(H/L)]^4 -> 0`.

Also `H/L -> infinity`, because `log H >> sqrt(log y) >> log log y`.

Finally the standing strictly subcritical hypothesis gives

`H/(y log y)`
` = [H log y/y]/(log y)^2 -> 0`.

Therefore the full `VIS-201` upper bound tends to zero.

This proves the stated equivalence for existence of a successful cutoff within the current architecture.

## 3. What the threshold isolates

`VIS-200` showed that before averaging local factors, cutoff tuning could not remove the extra pointwise factor

`B(y)=(1+log log(3+y))^4`,

so the fiberwise sieve required

`log H >> (1+log log y) sqrt(log y)`.

`VIS-201` removed that pointwise local-factor loss by averaging the corrected discriminant factor across the second-gap fiber. `VIS-202` shows that after this improvement there is no further scalar-cutoff gain of the same kind: the remaining `sqrt(log y)` comes from the fourth power of the sieve logarithm against the `(log y)^2` coefficient scale.

Equivalently, once the local-factor maximum has been replaced by bounded mean, the present proof has only two independent adjustable costs:

- the dimension-four sieve term, which needs `log(H/L) >> sqrt(log y)`;
- the taper tail, which needs `L >> (log y)^(2/3)`.

The second requirement is logarithmically negligible compared with the first whenever the first is satisfiable. Hence the square-root-log threshold is generated by the dimension-four incidence architecture itself, not by the cutoff balance or the removed `log log y` local-factor loss.

## Prior-art audit

No new sieve theorem, polynomial-average theorem, or prime-tuple estimate is asserted here. The load-bearing analytic inputs are exactly the already-audited ingredients of `VIS-200` and `VIS-201`: the fixed-dimensional four-form upper-bound sieve and Henriot's discriminant-uniform Nair--Tenenbaum estimate with its non-monic erratum correction.

The present finding is an elementary optimization/necessity consequence of the persisted `VIS-201` inequality. No novelty is claimed for balancing positive error terms or for the classical analytic-number-theory inputs.

## Boundaries and falsification

The equivalence is only about whether the **displayed three-term majorant** can tend to zero for some scalar cutoff `L`. It does not prove that `C_(y,H)(A)` stays nonzero at `log H=O(sqrt(log y))`, nor that `D_v=o(y^2)` is impossible there.

The result inherits all hypotheses of `VIS-201`: fixed crowding multiplier `A`, the same nondegenerate four-prime fiber reduction, the averaged local-factor estimate, dimension-four fiberwise upper-bound sieve, taper exponent four, separate trivial tail treatment, and the strictly subcritical condition `H log y/y ->0`.

A coupled estimate across several fibers, cancellation before absolute values are taken, a stronger fourth-prime input, a lower effective sieve dimension, a sharper treatment of the large-gap tail, or a different dephasing resource can evade this method threshold without contradicting the finding.

Falsify the derivation by finding an admissible `L` for which all three terms in the persisted `VIS-201` upper bound vanish while `log H/sqrt(log y)` does not diverge, or by showing that `L=log y` fails one of the stated admissibility/vanishing checks under the sufficient hypotheses.

## Research consequence

Scalar cutoff engineering is now closed on both sides of the local-factor averaging step. Before `VIS-201`, the pointwise singular-series maximum imposed the extra `log log y` loss; after `VIS-201`, the exact remaining cutoff threshold is

`log H >> sqrt(log y)`.

Therefore any attempt to reach the boundary regime

`log H = Theta(sqrt(log y))`

or smaller while retaining the same dephasing objective must change a genuinely structural ingredient: couple dangerous determinant fibers before scalarization, exploit cancellation or packing across fibers, improve the effective four-prime incidence dimension, strengthen the prime input, or replace the sufficient resource `D_v`. Re-optimizing `L` cannot do it.
