# VIS-153 — critical fixed-dimensional Gram lag grids factor into Haar lower differences and a deterministic top-difference curve

`Delta_(k,ell)=z_(k+ell) overline(z_k)`

`h^r / [N^(r-1) L_N^2] -> lambda`,

`E_q(V_(k,h)^(r))(p)`

## 1. The Newton transform isolates successive clock-cancellation orders

Write the lag coordinates as `omega_j=Delta_(k,jh)`. At a fixed prime `p`,

`omega_j(p)=exp(-i log(p)[g(k+jh)-g(k)]).
`

Therefore

`E_q(V_(k,h)^(r))(p)`
` = exp(-i log(p) sum_(j=1)^q (-1)^(q-j) binom(q,j)[g(k+jh)-g(k)])`.

The omitted `j=0` binomial term is supplied exactly by the common `-g(k)` contribution because

`sum_(j=1)^q (-1)^(q-j) binom(q,j) = -(-1)^q`.

Hence the exponent is precisely `Delta_h^q g(k)`.

The map from ordinary lag coordinates to successive finite differences is triangular: `E_q` uses only `omega_1,...,omega_q`, and the exponent of `omega_q` is one. Its integer matrix therefore has determinant one and an integer inverse. No information is discarded. The transformation only rewrites the same lag vector in coordinates adapted to the deterministic Gram clock.

This is the natural coordinate system for the critical problem. `VIS-152` showed that a general fixed product character has a first nonzero lag moment of some order `q<=r`. The Newton transform makes those moment orders explicit coordinate directions.

## 2. The top finite difference has a uniform critical curve limit

The classical Riemann--Siegel theta asymptotic, together with implicit differentiation of its inverse, gives for each fixed `m>=2`, uniformly on a dyadic index block,

`g^(m)(x)`
` = 2 pi (-1)^(m-1) (m-2)! / [x^(m-1) (log x)^2] (1+o(1)).`

This refines the scale statement already used in `VIS-152`; for `m=2` it is the explicit

`g''(x)=-(2 pi)/(x (log x)^2)(1+o(1))`

from `VIS-150`.

Because `r` is fixed and `h=o(N)`, the integral representation of finite differences yields uniformly for `u` in `[1,2]`,

`Delta_h^r g(Nu) = h^r g^(r)(Nu)(1+o(1)).`

Under the critical normalization,

`h^r/[N^(r-1)L_N^2] -> lambda`,

so

`Delta_h^r g(Nu)`
` -> c_r lambda u^(1-r)`

uniformly on `[1,2]`, with

`c_r=2 pi (-1)^(r-1)(r-2)!`.

Consequently the top Newton coordinate satisfies, for every fixed prime `p`,

`E_r(V_(k,h)^(r))(p)`
` -> exp(-i c_r lambda log(p) (k/N)^(1-r))`

uniformly modulo the vanishing endpoint difference caused by `rh=o(N)`.

Any fixed finite-prime continuous test therefore turns the empirical average of the top coordinate into an ordinary Riemann sum. This proves convergence of that coordinate to `nu_(r,lambda)`.

## 3. Every lower Newton coordinate becomes Haar jointly

It remains to show that the first `r-1` Newton coordinates become jointly Haar and decouple in the weak limit from the deterministic top coordinate.

Take a fixed character of `Omega^r` after applying `T_r`. For each Newton coordinate `q`, aggregate its finitely supported prime exponents into

`b_q=sum_p m_(q,p) log p`.

Unique factorization gives `b_q=0` if and only if all prime exponents in that coordinate vanish. Let

`q_0=min{q: b_q!=0}`

for a nontrivial character. Its phase along the transformed lag vector is

`F_N(k)=sum_(q=q_0)^r b_q Delta_h^q g(k)`.

If `q_0<r`, differentiation and the fixed-order inverse-Gram asymptotics give uniformly on the sample block

`F_N'(x)=b_(q_0) h^(q_0) g^(q_0+1)(x)(1+o(1)).`

Higher-`q` terms are smaller by powers of `h/N`, so they cannot cancel the leading derivative. The derivative is eventually monotone with fixed sign, tends to zero pointwise, and its total shear across `Theta(N)` indices has size

`h^(q_0)/[N^(q_0-1)L_N^2]`.

At the order-`r` critical scale this equals

`lambda^(q_0/r) (N/L_N^2)^(1-q_0/r) (1+o(1))`,

which diverges for every `q_0<r`.

The same first-derivative exponential-sum estimate used in `VIS-151`--`VIS-152` therefore gives

`(1/M_N) sum_k exp(-i F_N(k)) ->0`

for every fixed character that uses at least one of the first `r-1` Newton coordinates.

If instead `q_0=r`, the character depends only on the top coordinate. The uniform curve limit from the previous section gives

`(1/M_N) sum_k exp(-i F_N(k))`
` -> integral_1^2 exp(-i b_r c_r lambda u^(1-r)) du.`

These are exactly the Fourier coefficients of

`H_Omega^(tensor (r-1)) tensor nu_(r,lambda)`.

The compact-group Fourier criterion proves the claimed joint weak convergence.

## 4. The surviving critical coordinate is not a new arithmetic source

The last factor `nu_(r,lambda)` is not full Haar measure on `Omega`. Its projection to any two prime coordinates is supported on the one-dimensional smooth image

`u -> (exp(-i c_r lambda log(p)u^(1-r)), exp(-i c_r lambda log(q)u^(1-r)))`,

inside the two-torus. That image has two-dimensional Haar measure zero. Thus the top coordinate retains a genuine deterministic curve even while all lower Newton coordinates have Haar limits.

This explains a visual regime that could otherwise be misread as partial stochastic mixing: most finite-difference directions can look fully dispersed while one high-order joint combination remains organized. The organization is not a hidden zeta source. It is the final order-`r` finite difference of the inverse Gram sampling clock.

The three regimes for fixed equal-step delay dimension are now complete away from the already classified `r=1` dephasing convention:

## Boundary and falsification

The result fixes `r>=2` and assumes equal-step consecutive Gram lags with `h=o(N)`. It is a weak-limit statement for fixed torus characters and is not uniform in delay dimension, prime support, Fourier complexity, or character coefficients.

The `r=1` critical regime requires removing the diverging common lag rotation and is already classified by `VIS-150`; it is not restated here.

Irregular lag sets need their own integer moment geometry. Growing `r=r(N)` requires uniform control of inverse-clock derivatives and the integer transformation, and selected or nonconsecutive Gram subsequences require a new sampling null. Adding `Z(g_k)`, zero occupancy, derivatives, or nearby-zero geometry also introduces information not covered by this prime-phase clock theorem.

Falsify the claim by finding a fixed character involving a lower Newton coordinate whose normalized average does not vanish under the stated scaling, by invalidating the uniform leading asymptotic for the critical top finite difference, or by showing that `T_r` fails to be an automorphism of the stated lag torus.
