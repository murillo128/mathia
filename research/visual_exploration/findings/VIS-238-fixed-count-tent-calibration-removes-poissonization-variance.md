# VIS-238 — fixed-count calibration removes a leading Poissonization variance channel from tent pair statistics

## Claim

Let `0<ell<=L/2` and define the tent kernel on `[0,L]`

`h_ell(x,y)=(1-|x-y|/ell)_+`.

For `m` independent uniform points `X_1,...,X_m` on `[0,L]`, set

`T_m=sum_(1<=i<j<=m) h_ell(X_i,X_j)`.

Write `u=ell/L`. Then the three kernel moments needed for the order-two U-statistic are exactly

`a=E[h_ell(X_1,X_2)]=u-u^2/3`,

`b=E[h_ell(X_1,X_2)^2]=2u/3-u^2/6`,

`c=E[h_ell(X_1,X_2)h_ell(X_1,X_3)]=u^2-17u^3/30`.

Hence

`E[T_m]=binom(m,2)a`

and

`Var(T_m)=binom(m,2)(b-a^2)+6 binom(m,3)(c-a^2)`.

Equivalently,

`b-a^2=2u/3-7u^2/6+2u^3/3-u^4/9`,

`c-a^2=u^3/10-u^4/9`.

For a homogeneous Poisson point process `eta` of intensity `rho` on `[0,L]`, define the same unordered-pair functional

`T_eta=sum_{ {x,y} subset eta } h_ell(x,y)`.

Then

`E[T_eta]=(rho^2/2)(L ell-ell^2/3)`

and

`Var(T_eta)`
` =rho^2(L ell/3-ell^2/12)`
`  +rho^3(L ell^2-17 ell^3/30)`.

The difference between fixed-count and Poisson calibration is leading-order in the critical finite-occupancy scaling. If `lambda=rho L` is the expected Poisson count and

`kappa=rho ell=lambda ell/L`

stays fixed while `lambda->infinity`, then

`E[T_eta]=lambda kappa/2-kappa^2/6`,

`Var(T_eta)=lambda(kappa/3+kappa^2)-kappa^2/12-17kappa^3/30`.

By contrast, if the fixed sample has `m` points and `kappa=m ell/L` stays fixed while `m->infinity`, then

`E[T_m]=m kappa/2+O(1)`,

`Var(T_m)=m kappa/3+O(1)`.

Thus unconditioned Poissonization contributes an additional leading variance channel `lambda kappa^2`. At critical occupancy `kappa=Theta(1)`, that channel is of the same extensive order as the intrinsic fixed-count pair noise; it is not a negligible finite-size correction.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL U-STATISTIC/POISSON-U-STATISTIC SPECIALIZATION + REPRESENTATION-MATCHED NULL CALIBRATION + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No prime-gap theorem, prime-specific anomaly, point-process limit theorem, new U-statistic theorem, or RH consequence is claimed.

## 1. Exact tent-kernel integrals

For a pair of independent uniform points, symmetry by the separation `r=|x-y|` gives

`int_0^L int_0^L h_ell(x,y) dx dy`
` =2 int_0^ell (L-r)(1-r/ell) dr`
` =L ell-ell^2/3`.

Dividing by `L^2` gives `a=u-u^2/3`.

Similarly,

`int_0^L int_0^L h_ell(x,y)^2 dx dy`
` =2 int_0^ell (L-r)(1-r/ell)^2 dr`
` =2L ell/3-ell^2/6`,

so `b=2u/3-u^2/6`.

For the shared-point moment, put

`A(x)=int_0^L h_ell(x,y)dy`.

When `L>=2ell`,

`A(x)=ell/2+x-x^2/(2ell)` for `0<=x<=ell`,

`A(x)=ell` for `ell<=x<=L-ell`,

with the reflected boundary formula near `L`. Therefore

`int_0^L A(x)^2 dx`
` =(L-2ell)ell^2+2 int_0^ell (ell/2+x-x^2/(2ell))^2 dx`
` =L ell^2-17ell^3/30`.

After division by `L^3`, this is `c=u^2-17u^3/30`.

## 2. Fixed-count variance is an order-two U-statistic identity

Write `h_ij=h_ell(X_i,X_j)`. In

`T_m=sum_(i<j) h_ij`,

disjoint edges are independent. The only nonzero covariance between distinct summands comes from two edges sharing one sample point. There are `binom(m,2)` individual edges and, for each unordered triple, three unordered pairs of incident edges; the variance expansion contributes the factor `2`, hence `6 binom(m,3)`.

Therefore

`Var(T_m)`
` =binom(m,2) Var(h_12)`
`  +6 binom(m,3) Cov(h_12,h_13)`
` =binom(m,2)(b-a^2)+6 binom(m,3)(c-a^2)`.

This is the elementary order-two specialization of classical U-statistic variance theory. Substituting `u=kappa/m` gives

`Var(T_m)`
` =m kappa/3`
`  +[-kappa/3-7kappa^2/12+kappa^3/10]`
`  +O(1/m)`.

The extensive coefficient is therefore `kappa/3`.

## 3. Poissonization adds a first-chaos/count channel

For the Poisson process, the same pair functional is a Poisson U-statistic. Direct factorial-moment expansion, equivalently the standard Poisson U-statistic variance formula, gives

`Var(T_eta)`
` =(rho^2/2) int int h_ell(x,y)^2 dx dy`
`  +rho^3 int [int h_ell(x,y)dy]^2 dx`.

Using the preceding exact integrals yields

`Var(T_eta)`
` =rho^2(L ell/3-ell^2/12)`
`  +rho^3(L ell^2-17ell^3/30)`.

There is also an exact conditioning interpretation. If `N=|eta|`, then conditional on `N=m` the Poisson points are independent uniform points, so the conditional law is exactly the fixed-count model above. Moreover

`E[T_eta|N]=binom(N,2)a`.

For `N~Poisson(lambda)`,

`Var[binom(N,2)]=lambda^3+lambda^2/2`.

Hence the law of total variance contains the explicit count-fluctuation contribution

`Var(E[T_eta|N])=a^2(lambda^3+lambda^2/2)`.

Under `u=kappa/lambda`, this term is

`lambda kappa^2+O(1)`.

That is exactly the additional leading channel visible in the Poisson asymptotic variance. It is generated by allowing the total number of points to fluctuate, not by a new feature of the short-gap geometry.

## 4. Critical prime-cell consequence

`VIS-237` reduced random-origin same-cell collisions to this tent-weighted pair functional. In the critical prime-cell regime inherited from `VIS-225`--`VIS-227`, the relevant window contains `m=Theta(y/log y)` primes while the cell width is `ell=Theta(log y)` inside a macroscopic interval `L=Theta(y)`. Therefore

`kappa=m ell/L=Theta(1)`.

At precisely this scaling, the distinction above is macroscopic:

- a fixed-count homogeneous baseline has variance `Theta(m)` with leading coefficient `kappa/3`;
- an unconditioned homogeneous Poisson baseline also has variance `Theta(m)`, but carries the additional leading coefficient `kappa^2` from total-count fluctuation.

For a fixed value of `y`, the prime count in the chosen interval is part of the observed deterministic configuration. If the scientific question is whether the **geometry of those points** has an anomalous tent-pair statistic, leaving the comparison count Poisson-random mixes a separate global-count noise channel into the null. Conditioning only on the total count removes that channel without fixing `T_m`; unlike the occupancy-histogram conditioning obstruction of `VIS-232`, total-count conditioning leaves substantial pair-geometry freedom.

This does not make independent uniform order statistics a faithful final model of primes. It only identifies the minimal homogeneous calibration appropriate to a fixed-count geometric comparison.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty boundary

Wassily Hoeffding, **A Class of Statistics with Asymptotically Normal Distribution**, *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 19:3 (1948), 293–325, DOI `10.1214/aoms/1177730196`, is the classical source for U-statistics and their variance decomposition in fixed samples.

Matthias Reitzner and Matthias Schulte, **Central limit theorems for U-statistics of Poisson point processes**, *Annals of Probability* 41:6 (2013), 3879–3909, DOI `10.1214/12-AOP817`, develops Poisson U-statistics, their finite Wiener–Itô chaos expansion, and the associated variance formula.

The present result claims no new general probability theorem. The contribution is the exact elementary specialization of those classical variance structures to the `VIS-237` tent kernel, together with the Mathia-specific calibration consequence that Poisson count randomness is a leading-order confound at the critical prime-cell occupancy scale.

## Boundaries and falsification

The displayed finite-window polynomial formulas assume `ell<=L/2`. For larger `ell/L`, the boundary integrals change piecewise; no claim is made that the same coefficients persist there.

The fixed-count baseline assumes independent uniform points. It does not model the slow variation of prime intensity, exclusion/repulsion effects, arithmetic congruence structure, or any higher-order prime-gap law. A stronger confirmation null must address whichever of those structures it intentionally admits.

Conditioning on total count is justified here only as a way to isolate point geometry at fixed `y`. It is not a general rule that every count fluctuation should be conditioned away in every research question.

Falsify the exact result by finding an error in the three kernel integrals, the incident-edge covariance count, the Poisson U-statistic variance expansion, or the Poisson conditioning identity. Falsify the calibration consequence by showing that the intended confirmation question genuinely includes total-count fluctuation rather than conditioning on the observed number of source points.

## Research consequence

The accepted critical-cell clue now has a sharper minimum null. **An unconditioned homogeneous Poisson process should not be the sole scalar confirmation baseline for the origin-averaged tent statistic when the observed total source count is fixed.** The fixed-count uniform order-statistic model removes a leading `Theta(m)` Poissonization channel while leaving the tested pair geometry nondegenerate.

The next independent question is whether the prime tent statistic has a stable residual after a stronger fixed-count ordered-point model also accounts for the relevant nonuniform intensity or short-gap law. That requires a new modeling choice and therefore lies beyond this finding's coherent thread.
