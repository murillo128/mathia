# VIS-352 — bounded Wang phase collisions pay a Hermite collision-scale budget

## Claim

Fix a finite real interval `I` of positive length, integers `B>=2` and `q>=1`, and put

`N=Bq`.

Fix normalized phase locations `nu_1,...,nu_B` in a bounded real set with pairwise separation at least `eta>0`. For a cluster center `b`, collision scale `0<delta<=1`, and

`mu_j=b+delta nu_j`,

consider a polynomial-exponential cluster

`F_delta(x)=sum_(j=1)^B exp(-i mu_j x) Q_j(x)`,

where every `Q_j` has degree `<q`. Let `c` be the vector of all polynomial coefficients, equivalently the coefficients in the derivative basis

`partial_mu^s exp(-i mu x)|_(mu=mu_j)=(-i x)^s exp(-i mu_j x)`, `0<=s<q`.

Then there is a constant

`C=C(I,B,q,eta,max_j |nu_j|)>0`

independent of `b` and `delta` such that

`||F_delta||_(L^2(I)) >= C delta^(N-1) ||c||_2`.

Hence also

`sup_(x in I)|F_delta(x)| >= C |I|^(-1/2) delta^(N-1) ||c||_2`.

The exponent `N-1` is a **uniform worst-case collision budget**: bounded total confluent multiplicity cannot make a polynomial-exponential cluster smaller than a power of its geometric phase diameter unless the raw coefficient/moment vector is itself small or the normalized cluster geometry degenerates and must be reclustered at a finer scale.

Applied to the Wang tail from `VIS-349`--`VIS-351`, let one collision cluster contain `B_X=B` active phase fibers and polynomial degree `<q`, so its total confluent multiplicity is `N=Bq`. Let `delta_X` be the cluster diameter after normalization by a uniformly separated shape, let `c_X` denote the corresponding Wang polynomial coefficient vector, and let `H_X` be the reference-interval sup norm. If the next-tail scale satisfies

`D_(q,X)/||c_X||_2 = X^(o(1))`

and one claims the one-extra-order condition

`H_X <= D_(q,X)/X`,

then necessarily

`delta_X <= X^(-1/(N-1)+o(1))`.

Thus a bounded-multiplicity phase collision can purchase the missing `X^-1` only by becoming polynomially microscopic. A collision with

`delta_X = X^(-gamma+o(1))`

can contribute at most the worst-case power

`X^(-gamma(N-1)+o(1))`

relative to a nondegenerate raw coefficient scale. This does **not** prove that the bound is attainable for the Wang moment map; it prices the largest gain that bounded confluent geometry can hide.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL HERMITE/DIVIDED-DIFFERENCE CONDITIONING + STRUCTURAL NEGATIVE CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The result assumes a fixed normalized cluster shape with distinct centers separated by `eta`. Hierarchical subcollisions, growing multiplicity, shrinking reference intervals, cancellation among distinct clusters, frequency/source-dependent coefficients, and a singular source-to-Wang-coefficient map remain outside the claim.

## 1. Replace polynomial weights by repeated spectral nodes

For

`f_x(lambda)=exp(-i lambda x)`,

one has

`partial_lambda^s f_x(lambda)=(-i x)^s exp(-i lambda x)`.

Therefore the space generated by

`exp(-i mu_j x) Q_j(x)`, `deg Q_j<q`,

is exactly the Hermite exponential space obtained by taking each spectral node `mu_j` with multiplicity `q`.

Form the ordered multiset

`Lambda_delta=(mu_1,...,mu_1,mu_2,...,mu_2,...,mu_B,...,mu_B)`,

with each `mu_j` repeated `q` times, and write its `N` entries as

`lambda_0,...,lambda_(N-1)`.

Let

`psi_k(x)=[lambda_0,...,lambda_k](lambda -> exp(-i lambda x))`, `0<=k<N`,

with repeated nodes interpreted as generalized divided differences. Every `F_delta` has a unique expansion

`F_delta=sum_(k=0)^(N-1) u_k psi_k`.

This is only a change of coordinates inside the same confluent exponential-polynomial space.

## 2. The divided-difference basis stays uniformly normed

All nodes lie in a bounded cluster around `b`. Translation gives

`psi_k(x)=exp(-i b x) psi_k^(0)(x)`,

so the absolute center does not affect `L^2(I)` norms.

Apply `VIS-351` with multiplicity index `r=N-1`. Generalized divided differences remain continuous through repeated-node collisions, and on a fixed interval the corresponding Gram matrix has a uniform positive lower eigenvalue on every bounded relative-node parameter set. Hence there is

`kappa=kappa(I,N,rho)>0`

such that

`||F_delta||_(L^2(I)) >= kappa ||u||_2`.

The only remaining issue is therefore how singular the map from the raw polynomial coefficients `c` to the stable divided-difference coordinates `u` can become as the phase diameter `delta` shrinks.

## 3. Hermite coordinate conversion loses at most `delta^(-(N-1))`

For distinct nodes an order-`k` divided difference is a linear combination of sampled exponentials with denominators that are products of `k` node gaps. Generalized Hermite divided differences are the continuous repeated-node limits of the same formulas, replacing coincident samples by derivatives.

Here every nonzero gap between different phase centers has the form

`mu_j-mu_l = delta (nu_j-nu_l)`.

The normalized gaps satisfy

`eta <= |nu_j-nu_l| <= 2 rho`,

with `rho=max_j |nu_j|` after a harmless enlargement. For fixed `B,q`, every coefficient appearing when `psi_k` is expanded in the raw derivative basis is therefore bounded by

`C_k delta^(-k)`.

Repeated nodes inside one phase contribute derivatives and factorial constants but no new scale smaller than the inter-phase `delta`; the worst possible power at level `k` is still `delta^-k`.

Let `A_delta` be the triangular Hermite coordinate matrix defined by

`c=A_delta u`.

Since `k<=N-1`, the preceding coefficient bound gives

`||A_delta|| <= C_1 delta^(-(N-1))`.

Therefore

`||c||_2 <= C_1 delta^(-(N-1)) ||u||_2`,

or equivalently

`||u||_2 >= C_1^(-1) delta^(N-1) ||c||_2`.

Combining this with the uniform divided-difference lower frame bound proves

`||F_delta||_2 >= kappa C_1^(-1) delta^(N-1) ||c||_2`.

No probabilistic or asymptotic input is used: this is a finite-dimensional Hermite interpolation estimate.

## 4. What the collision power means

The bound identifies the actual resource hidden by a bounded phase collision. Raw exponential/Vandermonde coefficients are the wrong accounting coordinates, as `VIS-351` already showed. But after switching to stable divided-difference coordinates, the map from the original polynomial coefficients can still become small. The present estimate says that for fixed total multiplicity `N` this loss is at worst polynomial in the physical cluster diameter, with exponent `N-1`.

The simplest case is `q=1`, where `N=B`. For two phases,

`F_delta=c_0 exp(-i b x)+c_1 exp(-i(b+delta nu)x)`.

The antisymmetric choice `c_0=-c_1` gives

`F_delta=O(delta)`

on a fixed interval, exactly the first finite-difference cancellation. For `B` fixed separated normalized nodes, higher finite-difference choices can cancel successive Taylor modes and push the scale to powers of `delta`; this is classical finite-difference/Hermite interpolation behavior, not a new arithmetic mechanism.

With polynomial weights (`q>1`), the same phenomenon becomes confluent Hermite cancellation: polynomial factors are spectral derivatives, so phase collision can combine ordinary finite differences with derivative data. What matters for Mathia is not that this classical mechanism exists, but that its possible gain is now denominated in an explicit geometric currency `delta^(N-1)`.

## 5. Consequence for the Wang one-extra-order gate

For the Wang tail,

`E_(q,X)(xi)=sum_b exp(-i b xi) Q_(b,q,X)(xi)`

from `VIS-349`, `VIS-350` shows that one-extra-order attenuation on the working window requires

`H_X <= D_(q,X)/X`

up to the fixed normalization already recorded there.

Suppose one candidate explains the small reference norm `H_X` by placing a bounded number `B` of phase fibers in one cluster of diameter `delta_X`, while the normalized phase shape remains uniformly separated and the polynomial depth `q` is fixed. Apply the present lower bound on the same fixed reference interval:

`H_X >= C delta_X^(Bq-1) ||c_X||_2`.

Hence

`delta_X^(Bq-1) <= C^-1 D_(q,X)/(X ||c_X||_2)`.

If the ratio on the right is `X^(-1+o(1))`, then

`delta_X <= X^(-1/(Bq-1)+o(1))`.

So bounded phase collision is not a free conditioning loophole. It must be paid for by a quantitatively microscopic translation scale. Increasing `Bq` relaxes the required diameter exponent, but that is exactly growing confluent complexity, already visible in the `VIS-350` total-multiplicity ledger.

This result is only a necessary condition. A microscopic cluster satisfying the diameter inequality may still fail because the Wang moment vector does not align with the required Hermite cancellation, because the next-tail normalization `D_(q,X)` grows, because different clusters interfere badly, or because Wang's final destination repays the gain through `Q'` or equivalent unsmoothed source information.

## 6. Hierarchical collisions must be reclustered, not credited twice

The normalized separation hypothesis is intentional. If two normalized centers `nu_j,nu_l` themselves approach one another, then the chosen `delta_X` is not the only geometric scale. Treating the whole configuration as one uniformly shaped cluster would hide a second collision parameter inside the constants.

The correct accounting is hierarchical: split the finer subcluster, apply the same divided-difference/Hermite conversion at its own diameter, then analyze the interaction between the resulting cluster subspaces. A multiscale hierarchy may be a real mechanism, but each shrinking geometric level must appear explicitly; it cannot be credited as unexplained ill-conditioning of one fixed cluster.

## Prior-art audit

Carl de Boor, **Divided Differences**, *Surveys in Approximation Theory* 1 (2005), 46--69, arXiv `math/0502036`, develops the basic generalized divided-difference and Hermite interpolation identities used in the coordinate conversion above. The present estimate is an elementary bounded-parameter consequence of that classical machinery.

Bengt Fornberg, **Generation of Finite Difference Formulas on Arbitrarily Spaced Grids**, *Mathematics of Computation* 51:184 (1988), 699--706, DOI `10.1090/S0025-5718-1988-0935077-0`, gives classical arbitrary-grid finite-difference weights and is prior art for interpreting collision-driven cancellation as finite-difference/Hermite cancellation rather than a new mechanism.

The exponential divided-difference stability references already used in `VIS-351` remain the direct functional-analytic prior art for the stable basis: S. A. Avdonin and S. A. Ivanov, **Exponential Riesz bases of subspaces and divided differences**, *St. Petersburg Mathematical Journal* 13 (2001), 339--351, arXiv `math/0103160`, and S. A. Avdonin and W. Moran, **Ingham-type inequalities and Riesz bases of divided differences**, *International Journal of Applied Mathematics and Computer Science* 11:4 (2001), 803--820.

No novelty is claimed for Hermite interpolation, confluent Vandermonde matrices, finite-difference cancellation, or divided-difference basis conditioning. The Mathia-specific contribution is the accounting consequence for the Wang obstruction chain: after raw collision coordinates are quotiented, a bounded confluent cluster can hide at most an explicit power of its shrinking phase diameter.

## Research consequence

The finite-bank collision branch is quantitatively narrower. For bounded `B` and fixed tail depth `q`, phase coalescence can explain the missing `X^-1` only if the translation cluster becomes polynomially microscopic at a rate visible in `delta_X^(Bq-1)`, unless the Wang coefficient vector or next-tail normalization itself supplies the gain.

Future work should therefore stop treating "near-colliding phases" as a qualitative conditioning possibility. It should either exhibit the required microscopic collision scale and the corresponding Hermite-aligned Wang moments, or move to the genuinely different remaining resources: hierarchical/growing cluster complexity, inter-cluster cancellation, frequency/source-dependent coefficients, infinite expansions, or direct signed arithmetic structure of `G`.
