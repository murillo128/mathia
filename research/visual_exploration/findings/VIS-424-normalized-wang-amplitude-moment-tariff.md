# VIS-424 — normalized Wang amplitude reduces to a scalar moment tariff

## Claim

Let

`F_N(z)=sum_(a in S_N) b_(N,a) z^a`

be a nonzero finite Laurent polynomial on a finite prime torus, and write

`lambda_a=sum_p a_p log p`,

`M_N=||F_N||_infinity`,

`W_N=(sum_a |b_(N,a)|)/M_N`,

`Omega_N=max_(a in S_N)|lambda_a|`.

Define the normalized squared amplitude

`X_N(z)=|F_N(z)|^2/M_N^2 in [0,1]`.

For each fixed integer `r>=1`, let

`delta_(N,r)=min{|nu|: nu!=0 is a character frequency occurring in X_N^r}`,

with `delta_(N,r)=+infinity` if `X_N^r` is constant. Then the dyadic Gram-block moment discrepancy obeys

`| (1/N) sum_(N<k<=2N) X_N(z(g_k))^r - int X_N(z)^r dm_H(z) |`

`<= C W_N^(2r) [ 1/N + 2r Omega_N/log N + (log N)/(N delta_(N,r)) ]`,

where `m_H` is Haar measure on the relevant prime torus and `C` is the absolute constant from `VIS-423`.

Consequently, if for every fixed `r>=1`

`W_N^(2r) [ 1/N + r Omega_N/log N + (log N)/(N delta_(N,r)) ] -> 0`,

then for every fixed continuous function `phi:[0,1]->C`,

`(1/N) sum_(N<k<=2N) phi(X_N(z(g_k))) - int phi(X_N(z)) dm_H(z) -> 0`.

Thus the nonlinear normalized-amplitude observable does **not** require a direct high-dimensional Fourier approximation on the prime torus. It is enough to control the scalar moments of `X_N`, because continuous functions of normalized amplitude are uniformly approximable by ordinary one-variable polynomials on `[0,1]`.

For a fixed threshold `t in (0,1)`, the same hypotheses imply convergence of the low-tail probabilities

`(1/N) #{N<k<=2N: X_N(z(g_k))<=t}`

to the corresponding Haar self-null probability provided the Haar pushforwards satisfy the uniform boundary-mass condition

`lim_(eta->0+) limsup_(N->infinity) m_H{|X_N-t|<=eta}=0`.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + QUANTITATIVE CONTROL + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

This is a sufficient reduction for the Wang/Gram normalized-amplitude branch. It is not a full growing-dimensional equidistribution theorem, does not control the persistence supremum observable, and gives no RH consequence.

## 1. The squared amplitude lives in the same Laurent algebra

The normalized squared amplitude is

`X_N=(F_N/M_N)(conj(F_N)/M_N)`.

For a finite Laurent polynomial `P=sum_m c_m z^m`, write

`||P||_A=sum_m |c_m|`.

The coefficient convolution inequality gives the elementary Banach-algebra bound

`||PQ||_A<=||P||_A ||Q||_A`.

Hence

`||X_N||_A<=W_N^2`

and, for every fixed `r`,

`||X_N^r||_A<=W_N^(2r)`.

Every frequency in `X_N^r` is a sum of `r` frequencies from `F_N` and `r` opposite frequencies from `conj(F_N)`. Therefore every such frequency has magnitude at most

`2r Omega_N`.

If `delta_(N,r)` denotes the smallest nonzero frequency actually surviving after coefficient combination, the three tariffs of `VIS-423` satisfy

`A_0(X_N^r)<=W_N^(2r)`,

`A_1(X_N^r)<=2r Omega_N W_N^(2r)`,

`A_(-1)(X_N^r)<=W_N^(2r)/delta_(N,r)`.

Substitution into `VIS-423` proves the displayed moment bound.

This exposes the real growing-shell costs. The modulus itself is not the obstacle; the costs are the normalized Wiener mass `W_N`, the outer character scale `Omega_N`, and the higher-order prime-log small divisors `delta_(N,r)` generated by fixed powers.

## 2. Scalar polynomial approximation closes continuous amplitude tests

Let `mu_N^G` be the empirical law of `X_N(z(g_k))` on `N<k<=2N`, and let `mu_N^H` be the Haar pushforward law of `X_N`.

For a fixed polynomial

`q(x)=sum_(r=0)^R q_r x^r`,

the moment estimate gives

`|int q dmu_N^G-int q dmu_N^H|`

`<=sum_(r=1)^R |q_r| Delta_(N,r)`,

where `Delta_(N,r)` is the right-hand side of the moment bound. Under the stated fixed-`r` hypotheses this tends to zero for every fixed polynomial `q`.

Now fix `phi in C([0,1])` and `epsilon>0`. By the classical Weierstrass approximation theorem there is one polynomial `q_epsilon`, independent of `N`, with

`||phi-q_epsilon||_infinity<epsilon`.

Therefore

`|int phi dmu_N^G-int phi dmu_N^H|`

`<=2 epsilon + |int q_epsilon dmu_N^G-int q_epsilon dmu_N^H|`.

Take `N->infinity` first and then `epsilon->0`. This proves continuous-test equivalence of the Gram empirical amplitude law and each shell's own Haar self-null.

The important point is dimensional: the approximation is performed in the **scalar variable** `x=X_N(z) in [0,1]`, not in the increasing prime-torus dimension. The high-dimensional cost enters only through the finitely many moment tariffs.

## 3. Low-tail frequencies need anti-concentration, not a new torus approximation

The clue of interest concerns unusually small normalized amplitude, hence a tail indicator rather than a continuous test. Fix `t in (0,1)`.

For any `eta>0`, choose continuous ramps `phi_-` and `phi_+` on `[0,1]` such that

`phi_- <= 1_[0,t] <= phi_+`,

and `phi_-`, `phi_+` differ only inside `[t-eta,t+eta]`.

The continuous-test result gives

`int phi_+-phi_- dmu_N^G`

asymptotically equal to the corresponding Haar quantity, while the latter is bounded by

`mu_N^H([t-eta,t+eta])`.

Hence

`limsup_N |mu_N^G([0,t])-mu_N^H([0,t])|`

is bounded by the limiting Haar mass in an arbitrarily small band around `t`. The displayed uniform boundary-mass condition therefore forces the tail-probability difference to zero.

For each individual nonconstant Laurent polynomial, `X_N` is real analytic on the torus and a fixed level set has Haar measure zero unless the level function is identically constant. That pointwise fact does **not** by itself give the uniform-in-`N` boundary estimate above. A growing family could concentrate more and more Haar mass near the chosen threshold. Uniform anti-concentration is therefore a genuine remaining control rather than a technicality that may be silently assumed.

## 4. Consequence for the live Wang/Gram clue

`VIS-423` left normalized amplitude among the nonlinear observables that might evade the changing-polynomial Gram tariff. The present reduction removes that ambiguity for continuous functions of amplitude and sharply isolates what a low-tail claim still needs.

For the pointwise normalized-amplitude branch, a proposed Gram anomaly must now defeat at least one explicit gate:

- fixed-order moment tariffs fail because `W_N` grows too quickly;
- character scale `Omega_N` is too large;
- some fixed-order prime-log divisor `delta_(N,r)` becomes too small;
- or the Haar self-null develops nonuniform concentration at the tested low-tail threshold.

If none occurs, every fixed continuous amplitude diagnostic is asymptotically identical to its Haar self-null, and fixed-threshold low-tail frequencies are also identical under the boundary-mass condition. Merely invoking the absolute value as a nonlinear observable is no longer a valid escape from `VIS-423`.

The persistence statistic

`sup_(|u|<=rho)|F_N(T+u)|/M_N`

is not a scalar function of `X_N(T)` and is not covered by this reduction. It retains a genuinely path-valued approximation/stability problem.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The only general tools used beyond `VIS-423` are classical. The coefficient estimate is the ordinary Cauchy-product/submultiplicativity property of absolutely convergent Fourier series, and uniform approximation of continuous functions on `[0,1]` by algebraic polynomials is the Weierstrass approximation theorem. Standard references include the Encyclopedia of Mathematics entries **Absolutely convergent series** and **Weierstrass theorem**.

The tail step is the usual continuous-sandwich argument for weak convergence at continuity boundaries. No novelty is claimed for Wiener-algebra norms, polynomial approximation, the method of moments, weak convergence, or anti-concentration arguments.

A targeted prior-art check did not identify a source formulating this exact Gram/Wang moment-tariff reduction. That absence is not a novelty claim. The durable Mathia content is the explicit reduction of the live normalized-amplitude escape route to the three measurable shell resources above plus Haar boundary mass.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification

The criterion is sufficient, not necessary. Failure of a tariff does not imply a Gram anomaly; it only means this elementary route no longer certifies Haar behavior.

The hypothesis is required for every **fixed** moment order used by a fixed scalar polynomial approximation. It does not require one Fourier polynomial on the full growing torus whose degree tends to infinity with `N`.

Uniform tail anti-concentration is separate from moment control. A family whose Haar amplitude law places increasing mass in a shrinking band around the chosen threshold can evade the fixed-threshold conclusion even while all fixed continuous tests are controlled in the sense above.

Falsify the derivation by exhibiting a failure of the Laurent `A`-norm product bound, a frequency in `X_N^r` outside the `2r Omega_N` envelope, an incorrect application of `VIS-423` to one of the moments, or a continuous `phi` for which fixed polynomial approximation on `[0,1]` fails. Any such failure would break an explicit step.
