# VIS-241 — random-cut variance separates endpoint phase from circular gap geometry

## Claim

Fix a deterministic configuration of `m>=2` distinct points on a circle of circumference `L>0`, and let `0<ell<=L/2`. Choose a cut point `Theta` uniformly on the circle, unwrap the circle at `Theta`, sort the resulting coordinates in `[0,L)`, and form the linear tent statistic

`T(Theta)=sum_(i<j) (1-D_Theta(i,j)/ell)_+`,

where `D_Theta(i,j)` is the ordinary linear separation after the cut.

For each unordered pair `p={i,j}`, let `delta_p` be its shorter circular distance. Pairs with `delta_p>=ell` never contribute. For every pair with `delta_p<ell`, define

`w_p=1-delta_p/ell`,

`a_p=delta_p/L`,

and let `A_p` be the minor circular arc joining the pair, of normalized length `a_p`. Then, outside the zero-weight antipodal boundary,

`T(Theta)=sum_p w_p 1_(Theta notin A_p)`.

Consequently the exact cut-averaged statistic is

`bar T := E_Theta[T(Theta)]`
` = sum_p w_p(1-a_p)`
` = sum_(i<j) (1-delta_ij/ell)_+ (1-delta_ij/L)`.

Moreover, if

`o_pq = |A_p intersect A_q|/L`,

then the exact conditional variance generated by the random cut is

`V_cut`
` = sum_p w_p^2 a_p(1-a_p)`
`   + 2 sum_(p<q) w_p w_q (o_pq-a_p a_q)`.

Now let the circular configuration itself be random, for example the symmetric-Dirichlet gap model of `VIS-240`, and denote its gap data by `G`. The law of total variance gives the exact decomposition

`Var_(G,Theta)(T)`
` = Var_G(bar T) + E_G[V_cut]`.

Thus the variance of the rotation-randomized/cut Dirichlet null contains two mathematically distinct channels: variation of the circular pair geometry itself and variation caused solely by where the finite interval is cut. The second channel is nonnegative in aggregate and disappears exactly when the statistic is replaced by its cut average `bar T`.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CONDITIONAL-VARIANCE DECOMPOSITION + REPRESENTATION-BOUNDARY CONTROL + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No closed form for `Var_G(bar T)` under Dirichlet gaps, prime-specific residual, fitted Dirichlet parameter, asymptotic point-process theorem, or RH consequence is claimed.

## 1. A contributing pair is switched off precisely when the cut crosses its minor arc

Take one unordered pair with shorter circular distance `delta<ell<=L/2`. If the cut lies outside its minor arc, unwrapping preserves the short separation and the pair contributes

`w=1-delta/ell`.

If the cut lies inside the minor arc, the two points land on opposite ends of the unwrapped interval and their linear separation becomes `L-delta`. Since

`L-delta > L-ell >= ell`

for positive weight, the tent kernel then vanishes. Therefore the pair contribution is exactly

`w 1_(Theta notin A)`.

Pairs with `delta>=ell` have zero tent weight for either placement of the cut and may be omitted. At the boundary `delta=ell`, and in particular at the possible antipodal case `delta=ell=L/2`, the weight is zero, so the nonuniqueness of a minor arc is irrelevant.

This converts the endpoint-phase problem into a finite weighted coverage problem on the circle.

## 2. The cut average is an intrinsic circular pair functional

Because `Theta` is uniform,

`P(Theta in A_p)=a_p=delta_p/L`.

Averaging the pair indicator gives

`E_Theta[w_p 1_(Theta notin A_p)] = w_p(1-a_p)`.

Summing over pairs proves

`bar T=sum_p w_p(1-a_p)`.

Unlike the linear statistic at a particular cut, `bar T` depends only on the circular pair distances. It therefore removes the arbitrary endpoint phase while preserving the random circular gap geometry.

This is not the same statistic as the grid-origin average in `VIS-237`. That earlier quotient averages the phase of an equal-width collision grid on a fixed interval and yields the triangular kernel `(1-d/ell)_+`. Here the averaged nuisance is the global circular cut itself, so an additional factor `1-delta/L` appears.

## 3. Pair-arc overlap gives the exact cut-phase variance

Write

`I_p(Theta)=1_(Theta in A_p)`.

Since `T(Theta)=sum_p w_p-sum_p w_p I_p(Theta)`, its variance equals the variance of the weighted arc-coverage count. For a uniform point on the circle,

`Var(I_p)=a_p(1-a_p)`

and

`Cov(I_p,I_q)`
` = P(Theta in A_p intersect A_q)-a_p a_q`
` = o_pq-a_p a_q`.

Expanding the variance of the weighted sum gives the displayed formula for `V_cut` exactly. Individual overlap covariances may be positive or negative, but their complete quadratic combination is a variance and hence nonnegative.

The formula is deterministic once the circular configuration is fixed. No Dirichlet assumption, independence approximation, simulation, or asymptotic limit is used.

## 4. Total variance isolates the remaining dependent-null problem

For the random circular configuration `G` of `VIS-240`, the independent random rotation followed by the fixed cut is equivalent to fixing `G` and choosing a uniform cut phase `Theta`. Hence

`E[T|G]=bar T`

and

`Var(T|G)=V_cut`.

The law of total variance therefore yields

`Var(T)=Var_G(bar T)+E_G[V_cut]`.

This separates two questions that were conflated by asking directly for the variance of the linear post-cut statistic. `Var_G(bar T)` is the variance of the underlying circular pair geometry under the dependent gap null. `E_G[V_cut]` is the additional finite-window endpoint-phase channel introduced by randomizing how that same circle is opened into an interval.

The exact mean derived in `VIS-240` is unaffected: by iterated expectation,

`E[T]=E_G[bar T]`.

The new decomposition therefore refines the variance target without changing the center already calibrated there.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

Uniform and circular spacings, including their Dirichlet representation, are classical; `VIS-240` records Ronald Pyke, **Spacings**, *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B* 27:3 (1965), 395–449, and standard Dirichlet-distribution references as the prior-art boundary for that null family.

The law of total variance and covariance expansion for indicators are elementary probability identities. No novelty is claimed for either ingredient, for random circular cuts, or for weighted interval-coverage variance in general.

The Mathia-specific role of the identity is narrower: it identifies which part of the still-missing variance calibration in `VIS-240` is caused by the representation boundary rather than by dependent gap geometry. It therefore supplies an exact nuisance quotient before a harder Dirichlet covariance calculation is attempted.

## Boundaries and falsification

Whether the cut phase is a nuisance is a modeling choice that must be fixed before confirmation. If the physical endpoints of the source interval are mathematically distinguished, the full post-cut statistic and its `E_G[V_cut]` channel belong to the null. If the circle is only an auxiliary stationary construction and the endpoint phase is arbitrary, `bar T` is the cleaner statistic because it removes that channel exactly.

This result does not show that `E_G[V_cut]` is negligible, dominant, or asymptotically of any particular order for symmetric Dirichlet gaps. It also does not compute `Var_G(bar T)`. The latter remains the genuine dependent-gap variance target.

Falsify the pair identity by giving a configuration, `ell<=L/2`, and cut for which a positive-weight pair contributes despite the cut lying in its minor arc or vanishes with the cut outside it. Falsify the variance identity by evaluating `T(Theta)` over the circle and obtaining a variance different from the stated weighted arc-overlap formula.

## Research consequence

The accepted critical-logarithmic-occupancy clue no longer needs the full variance of the random-cut Dirichlet statistic as one undifferentiated object. Before using that null, choose explicitly whether interval endpoints are signal or nuisance.

For an endpoint-phase-invariant test, the next coherent target is `Var_G(bar T)` under a predeclared symmetric-Dirichlet parameter `alpha`, where

`bar T=sum_(i<j) (1-delta_ij/ell)_+(1-delta_ij/L)`.

That calculation concerns only the dependent circular gap geometry. For an endpoint-sensitive test, calibrate the same gap term plus the exact additional expectation `E_G[V_cut]`. A prime residual should not be interpreted until this choice and its variance calibration are frozen independently of confirmation data.
