# VIS-148 — primitive cancellation converts growing-window source error into low-frequency powers

## Claim

Let `E` be an integrable source-transfer error supported on `[-M,M]`. Fix an integer `k>=1`. Suppose there is a `k`-fold primitive `G_k` with

`G_k^(k)=E`

in the weak sense and with vanishing boundary traces

`G_k^(j)(-M)=G_k^(j)(M)=0`,  `0<=j<k`.

For the centered-cosine response

`Delta_E(q)=integral_(-M)^M E(x)[1-cos(2*pi*q*x)]dx`,

repeated integration by parts gives the exact transfer identity

`Delta_E(q)=(-1)^k integral_(-M)^M G_k(x) d^k/dx^k[1-cos(2*pi*q*x)]dx`,

and therefore, for every `|q|<=b`,

`|Delta_E(q)| <= (2*pi*b)^k ||G_k||_1`.

Thus cumulative cancellation in the source error turns directly into powers of the shrinking companion bandwidth. It is a concrete stronger error structure of exactly the type left open by `VIS-146` and `VIS-147`; pointwise control of `E` is not the only possible route.

The same certificate controls the quartic spatial moment. For `1<=k<=4`,

`mu_4(E)=integral x^4 E(x)dx`
`       =(-1)^k [4!/(4-k)!] integral x^(4-k) G_k(x)dx`,

while for `k>=5`,

`mu_4(E)=0`.

More generally, for `1<=p<=infinity` and `1<=k<=4`, Holder's inequality gives

`|mu_4(E)| <= C_(k,p) M^(5-k-1/p) ||G_k||_p`,

with a constant depending only on `k,p`. On the `VIS-130` growing source window `M_L=Theta(L)`, every additional controlled primitive removes one power of the otherwise dangerous window growth.

If the post-calibration source is decomposed as

`H_L = H_L^main + E_L`

and `E_L` admits such a certificate with `||G_(k,L)||_1=eta_(k,L)`, then its low-frequency transfer error satisfies

`epsilon_L^(E) <= (2*pi*b_L)^k eta_(k,L)`.

Against the quartic carrier from `VIS-146`, whose intrinsic scale is `|A_4(L)|b_L^4`, this error component has the explicit normalized gate

`epsilon_L^(E)/(|A_4(L)|b_L^4)`
` <= (2*pi)^k [eta_(k,L)/|A_4(L)|] b_L^(k-4)`.

Hence a fourth-primitive certificate matches the quartic bandwidth order, and a fifth-or-higher primitive certificate gains a positive power of the shrinking band. A fifth-primitive certificate also annihilates the transfer error's quartic spatial moment exactly.

**Evidence/status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + CLASSICAL INTEGRATION-BY-PARTS/VANISHING-MOMENT CALCULUS + SOURCE-TRANSFER CERTIFICATE + REDESIGN GATE + NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

No claim is made that the actual finite-height zeta residual possesses even one of these primitive bounds, that `A_4(L)` is nonzero, that the required norms have favorable height dependence, or that the Rudnick--Sarnak support family becomes sufficient.

## 1. Repeated integration by parts produces a bandwidth gain

Put

`phi_q(x)=1-cos(2*pi*q*x)`.

The boundary assumptions remove every term generated by integrating `E=G_k^(k)` by parts `k` times, so

`Delta_E(q)=(-1)^k integral G_k(x) phi_q^(k)(x)dx`.

For every `k>=1`, the derivative is a sine or cosine times `(2*pi*q)^k`; hence

`||phi_q^(k)||_infinity <= (2*pi*|q|)^k`.

The `L^1-L^infinity` pairing gives the stated bound. Unlike the generic per-mode envelope in `VIS-146`, this estimate remembers oscillatory/cumulative structure of the source error. The gain is structural: multiplying packet weights cannot create it, while a source theorem establishing a controlled primitive can.

If a signed companion packet `nu_b` is subsequently applied, then

`|integral Delta_E(q)dnu_b(q)|`
` <= (2*pi*b)^k ||G_k||_1 ||nu_b||_TV`.

Combining this with `VIS-146` shows why `k=4` is the first primitive order naturally matched to a quartic-normalized packet and `k>4` is the first order that can gain from shrinking support without relying on scalar packet normalization.

## 2. The same hierarchy measures quartic-moment leakage

Apply the same integration-by-parts calculation to the polynomial `x^4`. For `k<=4`,

`d^k/dx^k x^4 = [4!/(4-k)!] x^(4-k)`,

which gives the exact formula for `mu_4(E)`. For `k>=5` the derivative vanishes, so the quartic moment of the error is exactly zero.

For conjugate exponents `p,p'`,

`|integral x^(4-k)G_k(x)dx|`
` <= ||G_k||_p ||x^(4-k)||_(p',[-M,M])`.

The polynomial norm scales as `M^(4-k+1/p')=M^(5-k-1/p)`, proving the displayed growing-window bound. In particular:

- direct `L^infinity` control of `E` has the `M^5` amplification already exposed in `VIS-147`;
- one controlled primitive replaces that by an `M^4`-type amplification at the same endpoint norm level;
- four controlled primitives reduce the signed quartic-moment loss to at most an `M` factor in `L^infinity`, or to no window-growth factor in `L^1`;
- five controlled primitives remove quartic-moment leakage altogether.

These are not assumptions about zeta. They are an exact accounting of how many cumulative cancellations a source-transfer theorem would have to buy.

## 3. Primitive certificates are cumulative moment cancellation in compact support

For compactly supported integrable `E`, the boundary certificate has a standard moment interpretation. If

`integral x^r E(x)dx=0`,  `0<=r<k`,

then the canonical repeated primitive

`G_k(x) = 1/(k-1)! integral_(-M)^x (x-t)^(k-1) E(t)dt`

has the required zero traces at both endpoints (with the usual lower-order derivatives obtained by differentiating the integral). Conversely, if a `G_k` with the stated endpoint traces exists, integrating `x^r E` by parts `k` times shows that all moments of degree `<k` vanish.

So the source-side escape is not a vague appeal to oscillation. It can be asked as a concrete finite hierarchy: after all frozen finite-CUE calibration directions are removed, which low algebraic moments of the transfer error vanish, and how large is the corresponding cumulative primitive in a useful norm?

This is especially natural in the `VIS-130` representation because the tent pair-overlap gives a genuinely bounded source-difference interval. The compact support makes the endpoint conditions and cumulative moments explicit rather than hiding them in a formal Fourier expansion.

## 4. What this does and does not repair in the current quartic route

The result sharpens one part of the `VIS-147` source problem. A fixed-spacing `O(L^-4)` pointwise remainder remains useless by itself on an `M_L=Theta(L)` window, but a source representation with exact moment cancellation and a controlled primitive can be useful even without an `O(L^-5)` or `O(L^-6)` uniform pointwise theorem.

It does not automatically control the sixth-order Taylor remainder of the *main* source carrier in `VIS-146`. If the quartic extraction of `H_L^main` is still justified by Taylor expansion, one still needs

`M_6(H_L^main)b_L^2 << |mu_4(H_L^main)|`

up to the fixed constants after the common packet factors are removed, or an exact oscillatory estimate that replaces that absolute-sixth-moment bound. Primitive cancellation of the transfer error should not be confused with absolute-moment control of the retained carrier.

Accordingly the deterministic problem separates into two certificates rather than one: identify a genuine post-calibration main quartic carrier with a controlled higher-order remainder, and control the source/theorem transfer error by a norm that preserves enough cumulative cancellation. The primitive hierarchy supplies one explicit candidate for the second certificate.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

Repeated integration by parts, compact-support moment cancellation, Fourier-transform zeros at the origin, and Holder bounds are classical analysis. No general theorem novelty is claimed here, and no external theorem is needed for the identities above.

The Mathia-specific contribution is the composition with the current shrinking-band support-edge architecture. `VIS-146` left open the possibility that sign, orthogonality, oscillation, covariance, or a stronger norm could beat a generic total-variation-linear error. `VIS-147` then showed that the standard fixed-spacing finite-height remainder does not provide the needed growing-window fourth moment. The present calculation turns one of those vague escape classes into an exact graded target: `k` controlled cumulative cancellations yield a `b_L^k` transfer factor, while the quartic carrier lives at `b_L^4`.

A literature source that already provides such primitive/moment bounds for the calibrated finite-height pair-correlation residual would therefore be directly relevant, but no such source is asserted here. Discovering or deriving that source-side estimate remains the live research question.

## Research consequence

Do not respond to `VIS-147` by demanding only a dramatically stronger uniform pointwise remainder. First test whether the full finite-height diagonal/off-diagonal source, after the frozen effective-CUE size and scale calibration, admits an exact decomposition whose transfer error has four or more vanishing low moments and a controlled compact-support primitive.

A `k=4` certificate is enough to put the source-transfer error on the same bandwidth order as the quartic signal, leaving the norm ratio `eta_(4,L)/|A_4(L)|` to decide the gate. A `k>=5` certificate is qualitatively stronger: it removes quartic coefficient contamination and can gain a positive power of `b_L` in the low-frequency transfer error.

If neither such primitive control nor an equivalent oscillatory/orthogonality estimate can be obtained from the actual finite-height source, the moment-cancelled companion remains blocked exactly where `VIS-147` placed it. Packet redesign should still wait.
