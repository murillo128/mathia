# Can energy-matched reflected and unreflected Farey controls distinguish parity from arithmetic order?

## Observation

The accepted general Farey null clue compares reflection-compatible and unreflected reassemblies; the accepted near-maximal clue studies FD-323's unreflected three-defect odd-mode limit. FD-324 adds an independent near-maximal stress control: reflection remains exact with four positive-near-one defects and every directed denominator edge preserved. Both explicit artificial families approach the **same family-specific** energy supremum \(3/\pi^2\), but the reflected limit uses even sine modes while the unreflected limit uses odd ones. FD-319's lower reflection-preserving energy was not a ceiling.

FD-323's normalized near-maximal weights occupy odd indices \(m\) with \(8/(\pi^2m^2)\). FD-324's occupy even \(m=2j\), \(j\) odd, with weights \(8/(\pi^2j^2)\). Their weighted profiles coincide after a parity-index reparameterization, but their **absolute-frequency bands differ**. The existing VIS-449 even-sector boundary layer in FD-323 and VIS-450's improved projected-energy stability tariff make it possible to test this distinction without treating a vanishing sector's denominator as harmless.

## Research question

Does any low-band allocation or parity-conditioned statistic distinguish these near-maximal, equally energetic artificial controls for an invariant source-order reason after the absolute-mode and parity-index conventions and each null's own energy/band normalizers are frozen? Is a genuine Farey spectral residual still discriminating against both controls? What non-tautological source-realizability or cumulative excursion-order law would forbid their simultaneous matching without merely defining the genuine Farey ordering by fiat?

## Why it may matter

The reflected near-maximal witness removes the supposed energy distinction based on reflection alone. It offers a specific matched-energy, opposite-parity falsification stratum, rather than reasking the broad previously accepted null-comparison question. The test can distinguish an authentic source-order observable from parity reindexing, conditional rare-sector amplification and artificial cumulative transport.

Reconstruct the two spectral distributions from the exact FD-323 and FD-324 fixed-parameter sine coefficients and check the claimed weight correspondence after \(m=2j\). Preregister and calculate both absolute-frequency cutoffs \(m\le q\) and reflection/parity-indexed cutoffs \(j\le q\) **separately**. Take each permitted prime \(p\to\infty\) with its control parameters fixed, then take the family limit; any simultaneous limit needs an explicit controlled slow diagonal. Apply VIS-447's \(E_p/T_p\) bound for full-mode transport and VIS-448's rank-variation tariff for growing bands. For FD-323's vanishing even sector, use **VIS-450's projected-energy bound** with the finite \(T_{\mathrm{even},p,k}\) denominator; in a joint diagonal the sufficient conditions include \(T_{\mathrm{even},p,k}\ge c/k\) and \(kE_p\to0\), together with the independently verified finite-transport-to-step convergence. Do not retain VIS-449's older stronger \(E_p=o(k^{-2})\) sufficient tariff as though it were necessary. Independently recompute each comparison null's expected total energy and each band fraction. A purported genuine-Farey anomaly that vanishes after consistent matched-energy and parity calibration is a falsifying control; a surviving claim requires a separately demonstrated source-natural transport/order condition.
