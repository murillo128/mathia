# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the prime-torus information class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has progressively removed representation and sampling effects that can look arithmetically structured without adding a new information source. `VIS-064`--`VIS-066` show that invertible hybrid factor/residual recombinations do not create information, while `VIS-083`--`VIS-091` and `VIS-150`--`VIS-156` show that the deterministic Gram clock can generate Haar populations, coherent lag fields, critical Haar fibers, irregular-delay geometry, and sparse affine samples from the same fixed-prime state.

`VIS-157`--`VIS-159` then classify increasingly rich fixed-complexity index selectors. A degree-`d` polynomial selector is Haar through `d` fixed samples and exposes a deterministic moment-null character at `d+1`; the full fixed power family `floor(n^alpha)` has the corresponding threshold `ceil(alpha)`. Fixed finite-order visual mixing can therefore be manufactured by deterministic selector complexity.

`VIS-160` closes the naive growing-complexity escape as well. It diagonally glues polynomial stages of increasing degree into one strictly increasing deterministic selector `K(n)` such that **every fixed finite sample-order/fixed-prime character test is eventually Haar**, all at once in the usual fixed-test asymptotic sense. Selector complexity growing with height is therefore not itself a new information source. A deterministic clock can pass every fixed finite-dimensional prime-phase Haar test if its complexity is allowed to grow adversarially.

## Research question

After quotienting the prime-torus population law, the fixed finite Gram-clock hierarchy, fixed affine thinning, fixed polynomial/power selectors, and the `VIS-160` diagonal growing-complexity control, does any zeta-facing visual statistic expose information not already determined by prime phases plus deterministic observation geometry?

A remaining sampling route must now contain quantitative structure that the diagonal construction cannot absorb: for example a **pre-specified natural selector** together with a test whose order, prime support, character complexity, or resolution grows with height at a controlled rate. The alternative is to add an independently anchored analytic coordinate such as Hardy `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or nearby-zero geometry and prove that its joint information is not measurable from the prime-phase/clock state.

## Why it may matter

The control class is now stronger than any collection of fixed-dimensional scatter plots or finite-order Haar tests. Even seeing correct Haar behavior at every fixed finite order, considered one order at a time, does not force stochasticity or a new arithmetic source; deterministic completely-uniform-distribution phenomena already warn against that inference, and `VIS-160` realizes the same obstruction inside the sampled Gram prime-phase setting.

A surviving positive statistic must therefore defeat a **quantitative** clock null rather than merely a qualitative finite-order one. That raises the evidentiary bar in a useful way: either the test complexity must grow fast enough to see through the diagonal construction, or the observable must carry analytic information absent from the prime-phase state.

## Decisive test

Use `VIS-150`--`VIS-156` for fixed lag geometry and affine thinning, `VIS-157`--`VIS-159` for fixed polynomial/power selectors, and `VIS-160` for arbitrary fixed finite-order tests under an adversarial deterministic selector whose local degree grows with height.

Do **not** treat any fixed collection of Haar, decorrelation, scatter, lag, or product-character tests as source evidence merely because all of them pass. `VIS-160` can absorb every such fixed test eventually.

A sampling-based escape must instead specify a selector independently of the observed success and prove a quantitative theorem for a jointly growing test family. Examples include sample order `r=r(N)`, prime support `P=P(N)`, Fourier/character complexity, or another resolution parameter increasing with height. The decisive comparison is whether the claimed zeta signal survives a deterministic clock control calibrated at the **same growing complexity**, with enough uniformity to prevent a stagewise diagonal selector from postponing each test until after its local degree has overtaken it.

For an added analytic coordinate, derive a statistic involving `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or zero geometry and test whether it is determined by the prime-phase/clock sigma-algebra admitted by the control. Kill the route if the coordinate reduces to an invertible/reconstructible transform of the same state or if a matched deterministic clock reproduces the claimed effect.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-150`--`VIS-159` are fixed-complexity asymptotic controls. `VIS-160` is an existential diagonal construction: it shows that one deterministic increasing selector can pass every **fixed** finite-dimensional character test, but it supplies no useful convergence rate and no uniform theorem when test complexity grows with height.

Accordingly, `VIS-160` does not rule out a natural explicit changing-complexity selector with strong quantitative uniformity, nor a jointly growing statistic that outruns the diagonal clock. It also says nothing about joint laws containing zeta values, Hardy `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or nearby-zero coordinates. None of these Gram-clock results is a zeta approximation, stochastic-mixing theorem, stronger zeta criterion, or RH consequence.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Fixed finite commensurate lag geometry, fixed affine thinning, all fixed polynomial/power-law selectors, and **generic growth of selector complexity by itself** are closed as positive information escapes on fixed prime support. Continue only through a genuinely quantitative changing-complexity law whose test complexity grows with height under pre-specified controls, a separately derived nonclock sampling mechanism that survives the same quantitative audit, or an independently informative analytic source.
