# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has closed a broad family of attractive visual signals as representation or sampling effects rather than source evidence. Fixed finite prime fields already have their shared-phase Haar population law (`VIS-071`); increasingly rich deterministic Gram selectors can reproduce every preassigned finite-order finite-prime Haar test (`VIS-160`); and ordinary omitted-prime controls already exhibit decoder failure, heavy collision tails, moment thresholds, and growing-support population barriers (`VIS-163`, `VIS-164`, `VIS-168`).

The weighted bulk is also representation-dependent. `VIS-172` and `VIS-173` separate the concentration scale `D_2` from the shape scale `D_4`; `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments from the deterministic continuous prime-log field to the matched Haar null. `VIS-175` closes the full-distribution loophole for square-summable weights, while `VIS-176` closes the complementary Lindeberg phase by giving the full Gaussian law for nonsquare-summable prime-power weights under its original polylogarithmic example.

`VIS-177` now sharpens the elementary nonresonant frequency gap in the fixed-moment transfer from `y^-k` to `y^-floor(k/2)`. In the Lindeberg prime-power phase `0<=alpha<=1/2`, this is enough to transfer every fixed moment — and hence the complete Gaussian weak law from `VIS-176` — for every cutoff `y(H)=H^o(1)`. Therefore merely leaving the polylogarithmic regime does not escape the representation-matched continuous null.

Thus the ordinary continuous additive prime-power family has a broad matched full-law control: Gaussian in the Lindeberg phase throughout subpolynomial support, and generally non-Gaussian infinite weighted Haar in the square-summable phase under the separate `VIS-175` gate. The visible weight-phase transition remains a representation effect rather than source evidence.

## Research question

After quotienting the finite prime-torus population law, the deterministic Gram-clock hierarchy, regular-decoder/collision geometry, support-volume effects, coordinate weighting, fixed-moment transfer, the two full-law weight phases, and the balanced-character sharpening, does any zeta-facing visual statistic exhibit a **predeclared source-sensitive separation from the representation-matched deterministic/Haar control**?

A surviving mechanism must leave at least one hypothesis of the closed control class in a mathematically essential way. Plausible boundaries now include a genuine hybrid prime/zero variable, Gram or other discrete sampling not covered by continuous vertical averaging, a rare/shrinking or unbounded functional requiring stronger than weak convergence, a genuinely polynomial-or-faster support regime outside the current all-fixed-moment transfer, or another source-dependent observable not reducible to the same normalized additive prime torus.

## Why it may matter

The useful target is now much narrower than “find an unusual prime-phase picture.” Stable non-Gaussianity in the square-summable phase, Gaussianization in the nonsquare-summable Lindeberg phase, and the persistence of that Gaussian null throughout arbitrary subpolynomial support are all forced by the representation itself under the stated continuous-window gates.

A positive result that survives the fully matched control would therefore isolate information not explained by ordinary prime-frequency geometry, coordinate weighting, or continuous vertical mixing. Failure is equally informative because it prevents increasingly elaborate remappings of the same additive torus from being mistaken for zeta-specific structure.

## Decisive test

Freeze before inspection the support/support-growth law, coordinate weights or prewhitening, sampling rule, normalization, zeta-facing observable, matched control, statistic, and decision rule.

For a fixed bounded continuous functional of an additive weighted prime-phase field under continuous vertical averaging, determine which representation phase applies. If the weights are square-summable, test the `VIS-175` gate `yW_y^2/H->0`. If the normalized largest weight vanishes, use the strengthened `VIS-177` fixed-moment transfer condition

`y^floor(k/2) D_2(y)^(k/2)/H -> 0`

for every fixed `k`, together with the `VIS-176` Lindeberg step. For prime-power weights `0<=alpha<=1/2`, every subpolynomial cutoff `y(H)=H^o(1)` is already controlled.

For collision or decoder tails, shrinking events, moving thresholds, or unbounded statistics, do not infer a separation from weak-law results; use the matched rare-event/moment controls from `VIS-164`--`VIS-168` or prove the stronger quantitative control actually required. For Gram/discrete sampling, use the Gram-clock controls rather than importing a continuous theorem. For a fixed positive-power support law `y=H^delta`, first establish whether a stronger discrepancy/characteristic-function argument controls the matched null before interpreting any residual as source-sensitive.

Accept a new direction only if it proves a predeclared separation from the relevant matched control in a regime where the control has nondegenerate mass and the proposed statistic is not already determined by the prime-torus representation. Kill it if the same effect is reproduced by the matched deterministic/Haar model, appears only after post-selected weights/windows/thresholds, or disappears once the representation and sampling geometry are matched.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-071` controls fixed finite continuous prime fields. `VIS-160` shows that arbitrary fixed finite-order Gram-phase tests can be reproduced by deterministic clock selectors. `VIS-163`--`VIS-168` control regular decoding, collision tails, moment growth, and growing-support event mass. `VIS-172`--`VIS-173` show that weighted bulk concentration and shape are separate representation effects. `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments to the deterministic continuous prime-log orbit. `VIS-175` gives the full weak law for additive square-summable weights, while `VIS-176` gives the complementary full Gaussian weak law in the Lindeberg/nonsquare-summable phase. `VIS-177` strengthens the elementary character separation and extends the latter control to arbitrary subpolynomial prime-power support.

None of these results proves a zeta-versus-control source separation. They do not control Gram-point/discrete sampling, hybrid Euler--Hadamard residuals, zeta-zero variables, moving rare-event tests, decoder singularities, unbounded observables, or genuinely polynomial-or-faster support in the Lindeberg phase. These remaining boundaries are research opportunities, not established mechanisms, and no result here implies RH.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Ordinary bounded-continuous distributional structure of the normalized additive prime-phase field under continuous averaging is closed as a standalone positive discriminator across the prime-power family in the regimes currently proved: the Lindeberg phase `0<=alpha<=1/2` is Gaussian throughout every subpolynomial support law, while the square-summable phase `alpha>1/2` has the generally non-Gaussian matched Haar law under the separate `VIS-175` growth gate.

Continue only through a predeclared zeta-facing separation that genuinely leaves these matched representation classes and also survives the appropriate sampling, rare-event, weighting, and support-growth controls. The strongest current candidates are source-bearing hybrid/zero observables, genuinely discrete sampling phenomena, stronger functional classes, or quantitatively new polynomial-support control questions — not another remapping of the same continuous weighted prime torus.
