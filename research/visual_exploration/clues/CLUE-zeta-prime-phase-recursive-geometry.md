# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the prime-torus information class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has progressively removed representation and sampling effects that can look arithmetically structured without adding a new information source. `VIS-064`--`VIS-066` show that hybrid factor/residual recombinations related by an invertible shear do not create information, while the later Gram sequence results show that an independently defined sampling clock can generate a surprisingly rich visual vocabulary on the same prime-phase data.

`VIS-083` gives full-population fixed-prime Haar behavior. `VIS-084`--`VIS-091` classify local freezing, deterministic inverse-clock arcs and finite-order corrections, growing-support collapse, and the separation between unordered long-block population and fixed local lag structure. `VIS-150`--`VIS-155` then close the complete fixed-dimensional commensurate lag hierarchy: fixed equal or irregular integer-node delays are subcritical coherent, critical Haar-on-fibers over one deterministic inverse-clock curve, or supercritical Haar according to the order of the deepest finite-difference/Vandermonde character.

`VIS-156` closes fixed affine starting-index thinning. Restricting Gram indices to `a+q n` preserves fixed-prime Haar population and the complete fixed-node lag classification; fixed congruence-class sparsity is only a deterministic clock reparameterization.

`VIS-157` now shows that even a genuinely nonlinear sparse selector can preserve this false-positive mechanism in a subtler form. For the quadratic index subsequence `g_(n^2)`, every fixed one-time and two-time prime-phase population is Haar, yet every three distinct fixed offsets admit a nontrivial product character tending to one. Quadratic sparse sampling is therefore pairwise Haar but not three-wise Haar: apparent pairwise mixing can be generated entirely by deterministic clock acceleration, with the hidden curvature reappearing at the first three-time finite difference.

## Research question

After quotienting the prime-torus population law, the complete fixed finite commensurate Gram-clock hierarchy, fixed affine index subsampling, and the quadratic selector's pairwise-Haar/triple-clock null, does any independently anchored or genuinely changing-complexity visual statistic expose information not already determined by the prime phases, the observation window, and deterministic sampling geometry?

The remaining routes must change a real hypothesis. Candidate escapes include polynomial selectors of higher degree only after deriving their full finite-sample-order null, genuinely non-polynomial or data-dependent subsequences, sampling laws whose complexity grows with height with quantitative uniformity, genuinely noncommensurate real-time offsets, or an analytic coordinate such as `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or nearby-zero geometry whose joint information is not determined by the prime-phase/clock state.

## Why it may matter

The visual false-positive space is now unusually constrained. The same fixed-prime data can appear as a cluster, deterministic arc, Haar population, coherent lag field, mixed Haar-times-curve critical law, irregular delay cloud, sparse residue-class sample, or even a nonlinear sparse sequence that is pairwise Haar while retaining a hidden three-time deterministic relation. None of those appearances alone establishes a new arithmetic source.

A surviving statistic would be materially more informative because it would have to leave this deterministic information class rather than merely make a plot look more irregular, sparse, mixed, or high dimensional.

## Decisive test

For consecutive fixed-prime lag populations, use `VIS-150`--`VIS-151`. For every fixed equal-step `r`-lag vector use `VIS-152`--`VIS-153`; below the order-`r` threshold a top finite-difference character remains coherent, at criticality the Newton coordinates are Haar in the transverse directions over one deterministic top-difference curve, and above threshold the fixed joint characters cancel.

For every fixed distinct integer lag-ratio set use `VIS-154`--`VIS-155`; changing a finite commensurate node geometry only changes the primitive Vandermonde character and deterministic winding constants. For fixed arithmetic-progression starting indices use `VIS-156`; fixed stride is only a sampled-clock reparameterization.

For the quadratic nonlinear selector `k=n^2`, use `VIS-157`: fixed one- and two-time prime-phase views are Haar, but any three distinct fixed offsets have a coherent second-difference product character. Do not treat pairwise scatter or pairwise decorrelation under quadratic sparse sampling as evidence of source independence.

A surviving nonlinear-sampling route must therefore derive its own higher-order null before interpreting visual randomness. The next clean control question is whether a fixed degree-`d` polynomial selector is Haar through `d` fixed samples but necessarily develops a coherent `(d+1)`-point finite-difference character. That statement is not yet established here. A non-polynomial or data-dependent selector must likewise be tested against the deterministic clock law it induces rather than being declared independent because its index set looks irregular.

Kill a route if its apparent signal is reproduced by the corresponding deterministic point/curve/Newton/Vandermonde/polynomial-clock law, disappears after an explicit invertible clock quotient, or depends only on a fixed finite transformation of the same prime-phase state.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-150`--`VIS-155` classify fixed-dimensional prime-phase lag populations over consecutive Gram indices across subcritical, critical, and supercritical scales. `VIS-156` extends that control to every fixed affine index subsequence `a+q n`. `VIS-157` treats only the specific nonlinear selector `n^2`: it proves fixed-prime population Haar, fixed-pair Haar, and a deterministic obstruction for every fixed triple of offsets.

These results do not provide uniformity when delay dimension, node geometry, sampling stride, prime support, Fourier complexity, or character coefficients grow with height. They do not establish the conjectural degree-`d` polynomial hierarchy, classify arbitrary sparse/nonlinear/data-dependent subsequences, or treat genuinely noncommensurate real-time offsets.

They also do not classify any joint law involving zeta values, Hardy `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or nearby-zero coordinates. The Gram-clock hierarchy is a sampling control, not a zeta approximation, stochastic-mixing theorem, stronger zeta criterion, or RH consequence.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Fixed finite commensurate lag geometry, fixed affine index thinning, and quadratic nonlinear index thinning are not positive information escapes. In the quadratic case even pairwise Haar behavior is insufficient: the deterministic clock survives as a three-time finite-difference character. Continue only through a genuinely hypothesis-changing route such as a separately derived higher-degree/non-polynomial sampling law, changing complexity with quantitative uniformity, noncommensurate sampling, or an independently informative analytic source.
