# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has closed a broad family of attractive visual signals as representation or sampling effects rather than source evidence. Fixed finite prime fields already have their shared-phase Haar population law (`VIS-071`); increasingly rich deterministic Gram selectors can reproduce every preassigned finite-order finite-prime Haar test (`VIS-160`); and ordinary omitted-prime controls already exhibit decoder failure, heavy collision tails, moment thresholds, and growing-support population barriers (`VIS-163`, `VIS-164`, `VIS-168`).

The weighted bulk is also representation-dependent. `VIS-172` and `VIS-173` separate concentration scale `D_2` from shape scale `D_4`; `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments from the deterministic continuous prime-log field to the matched Haar null. `VIS-175` closes the full-distribution loophole for square-summable weights under its sufficient growing-cutoff gate, while `VIS-176` closes the complementary Lindeberg phase in its controlled regime. `VIS-177` and `VIS-178` then sharpen finite-order transfer by using balanced character structure and the actual prime-power effective dimension.

At quadratic order the control is much stronger. `VIS-179` rewrites the normalized cosine field as a Dirichlet polynomial and applies Montgomery–Vaughan mean square to show

`| H^(-1) int_T^(T+H) S_y(t)^2 dt - 1/8 | = O(y/H)`

uniformly in the interval start, prime subset, and positive weights. Hence every strictly sublinear support law `y=o(H)` is already variance-matched.

`VIS-180` proves that this uniform arbitrary-subset theorem cannot simply be pushed to `y asymp H`: an infinite bounded-prime-gap subsequence with equal weights on two close primes leaves a constant beat term in the deterministic variance. The obstruction is not source-sensitive; it is slow mixing of two close prime-log frequencies.

`VIS-181` now separates that adversarial sparse obstruction from natural diffuse full-prime weights. Before the coarse `p<=y` substitution, the same Montgomery–Vaughan argument gives

`|time variance - 1/8| <= C [ L_y/(H Q_y) + D_2(y)/H ]`,

where `L_y=sum p w_p^2`. At `H asymp y`, full prime-power weights `w_p=p^(-alpha)` satisfy both leverage conditions for every fixed `alpha>=1/2`. In particular, at `alpha=1/2`,

`Q_y~log log y`, `L_y~y/log y`, `D_2(y)~4y/[(log y)^2 log log y]`,

so the normalized variance defect is only `O(1/[log y log log y])`. Thus the natural critical-line prime amplitude already washes out the bounded-gap linear-support crowding channel at global quadratic order.

The remaining prime-phase opportunity is therefore narrower than merely “go to linear support.” Any positive result must leave the relevant representation-matched class in a mathematically essential way.

## Research question

After quotienting the finite prime-torus population law, deterministic Gram-clock hierarchy, regular-decoder/collision geometry, support-volume effects, coordinate weighting, fixed-moment transfer, the two full-law weight phases, balanced-character sharpening, prime-density effective dimension, the sublinear Montgomery–Vaughan variance null, the sparse close-frequency linear-boundary obstruction, and the weighted linear-support leverage gate, does any zeta-facing visual statistic exhibit a **predeclared source-sensitive separation from its representation-matched control**?

For global continuous quadratic variance, full prime-power profiles with `alpha>=1/2` are now closed even at `y asymp H`. The unresolved linear-support quadratic region begins only with more diffuse profiles `alpha<1/2` or other coefficient families for which the weighted leverage criterion does not vanish; failure of that sufficient criterion is not itself a separation. Other live boundaries remain genuinely discrete sampling, higher or growing complexity, rare/shrinking or unbounded functionals, hybrid prime/zero variables, and source-bearing statistics outside the same additive torus.

## Why it may matter

A useful positive result should isolate information that cannot be recreated by ordinary prime-frequency geometry, coordinate weighting, prime density, classical Dirichlet-polynomial averaging, deterministic close-frequency beating, or the sampling clock. `VIS-181` matters because it removes a tempting false escape route: the critical-line coefficient profile does not rescue global variance merely by moving the prime cutoff to the linear window scale.

The coincidence of the leverage threshold with `alpha=1/2` is mathematically interesting but currently only a representation-level integrability transition. Treating it as evidence for RH would reverse the logic of the control: the theorem says the matched null becomes stronger there.

## Decisive test

Freeze before inspection the support law, coefficient profile or prewhitening, sampling rule, normalization, zeta-facing observable, matched control, statistic, windows/thresholds, and decision rule.

**Weighted linear-support quadratic gate.** For a continuous additive prime-log variance test define

`Q_y=sum w_p^2`, `L_y=sum p w_p^2`, `D_2=(sum w_p)^2/Q_y`.

If `H asymp y` and both `L_y/(yQ_y)->0` and `D_2/y->0`, kill the global quadratic statistic as a source discriminator by `VIS-181`. In particular, this kills the full-prime family `w_p=p^(-alpha)` for every fixed `alpha>=1/2`, including the critical-line profile `p^(-1/2)`.

**Sublinear quadratic gate.** If `y=o(H)`, kill global variance for arbitrary positive weights and arbitrary prime subsets by `VIS-179`.

**Linear-support crowding gate.** If the coefficient profile fails the weighted gate at `y asymp H`, do not interpret a variance residual relative to independent Haar phases as source-sensitive until the matched control preserves local prime-log crowding. `VIS-180` supplies an explicit two-prime counterexample showing why. A natural full-support statistic must either derive its actual off-diagonal asymptotic or compare against a deterministic crowding-preserving null.

For higher fixed-order information, use the `VIS-175`/`VIS-177`/`VIS-178` gates appropriate to the weight phase and moment order. For collision or decoder tails, shrinking events, moving thresholds, or unbounded statistics, use the stronger rare-event/moment controls from `VIS-164`--`VIS-168`; weak-law or variance matching is insufficient. For Gram/discrete sampling, use the Gram-clock controls rather than importing a continuous theorem.

Accept a new direction only if it proves a predeclared separation from the relevant matched control in a regime where the control has nondegenerate mass and the proposed statistic is not already determined by the prime-torus representation. Kill it if the same effect is reproduced by the matched deterministic/Haar model, by a frequency-crowding control at the same support/window scale, appears only after post-selected weights/windows/thresholds, or disappears once representation and sampling geometry are matched.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-071` controls fixed finite continuous prime fields. `VIS-160` controls arbitrary preassigned finite-order Gram-phase mixing tests through deterministic clock selectors. `VIS-163`--`VIS-168` control regular decoding, collision tails, moment growth, and growing-support event mass. `VIS-172`--`VIS-173` separate weighted concentration and shape. `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments. `VIS-175` and `VIS-176` give complementary full-law controls in their stated regimes. `VIS-177`--`VIS-178` sharpen finite-order growing-support transfer. `VIS-179` closes global variance throughout `y=o(H)`. `VIS-180` proves that arbitrary sparse weights can retain deterministic close-frequency variance at `y asymp H`. `VIS-181` proves that full prime-power weights with `alpha>=1/2` nevertheless remain variance-matched at that linear scale.

`VIS-181` is only a second-moment theorem. It does not prove a full weak law at linear support, does not control discrete sampling, does not settle `alpha<1/2`, and does not establish any zeta-versus-control source separation. The threshold `alpha=1/2` is not evidence for RH; it is where this coefficient-sensitive quadratic null becomes provably effective for the full-prime profile.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Ordinary bounded-continuous distributional structure and a large range of finite-order continuous prime-phase statistics are already representation-matched in the persisted regimes. Global quadratic variance is closed for every `y=o(H)` coefficient profile covered by `VIS-179`, and now also at `y asymp H` for the natural full-prime power family `alpha>=1/2` by `VIS-181`.

Continue only through a predeclared zeta-facing separation that genuinely leaves these matched representation classes and survives the appropriate sampling, rare-event, weighting, support-growth, frequency-crowding, and observable-complexity controls. At linear support, the unresolved quadratic case is restricted to more diffuse or otherwise high-leverage profiles and still requires an actual crowding-aware asymptotic; stronger candidates remain hybrid/zero observables, genuinely discrete sampling, higher or growing complexity, and stronger functional classes not reducible to another global variance plot.
