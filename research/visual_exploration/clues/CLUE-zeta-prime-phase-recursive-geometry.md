# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has closed a broad family of attractive visual signals as representation or sampling effects rather than source evidence. Fixed finite prime fields already have their shared-phase Haar population law (`VIS-071`); increasingly rich deterministic Gram selectors can reproduce every preassigned finite-order finite-prime Haar test (`VIS-160`); and ordinary omitted-prime controls already exhibit decoder failure, heavy collision tails, moment thresholds, and growing-support population barriers (`VIS-163`, `VIS-164`, `VIS-168`).

The weighted bulk is also representation-dependent. `VIS-172` and `VIS-173` separate the concentration scale `D_2` from the shape scale `D_4`: a natural prime-amplitude taper can concentrate while retaining a persistent non-Gaussian low-prime fingerprint. `VIS-174` then shows that, for slowly growing support, every preassigned fixed moment of the deterministic continuous prime-log field transfers to that same weighted Haar null.

`VIS-175` closes the full-distribution loophole for square-summable weights. If `sum_p w_p^2<infinity` and `yW_y^2/H->0`, the complete bounded-continuous empirical law converges to the infinite weighted Haar law; for `w_p=p^(-alpha)`, every `alpha>1/2` is covered at polylogarithmic support. `VIS-176` closes the complementary Lindeberg regime: if the largest normalized weight vanishes and every fixed moment satisfies the `VIS-174` transfer gate, the complete deterministic vertical law is `N(0,1/8)`. In particular every prime-power taper `0<=alpha<=1/2` is Gaussian under any fixed polylogarithmic cutoff.

Thus the whole prime-power family now has a matched full-law control under ordinary continuous vertical averaging: Gaussian for `alpha<=1/2`, generally non-Gaussian infinite weighted Haar for `alpha>1/2`. The visible change at `alpha=1/2` is a representation phase transition, not source evidence.

## Research question

After quotienting the finite prime-torus population law, the deterministic Gram-clock hierarchy, regular-decoder/collision geometry, support-volume effects, coordinate weighting, fixed-moment transfer, and both full-law weight phases, does any zeta-facing visual statistic exhibit a **predeclared source-sensitive separation from the representation-matched deterministic/Haar control**?

A surviving mechanism must leave at least one hypothesis of the closed control class in a mathematically essential way. Plausible boundaries now include a genuine hybrid prime/zero variable, Gram or other discrete sampling not covered by continuous vertical averaging, a rare/shrinking or unbounded functional requiring stronger than weak convergence, support growth substantially beyond the current sufficient transfer gates, or another source-dependent observable not reducible to the same normalized additive prime torus.

## Why it may matter

The useful target is now much narrower than “find an unusual prime-phase picture.” Stable non-Gaussianity in the square-summable phase and Gaussianization in the nonsquare-summable Lindeberg phase are both forced by the representation itself and inherited by the deterministic prime-log orbit under explicit support/window gates. A positive result that survives the fully matched control would therefore isolate information not explained by ordinary prime-frequency geometry, coordinate weighting, or continuous vertical mixing.

Failure is also informative: it continues converting visually compelling prime-phase phenomena into explicit null mechanisms and prevents downstream research from treating a weight-induced phase change as evidence for a zeta-specific source.

## Decisive test

Freeze before inspection the support/support-growth law, coordinate weights or prewhitening, sampling rule, normalization, zeta-facing observable, matched control, statistic, and decision rule.

For a fixed bounded continuous functional of an additive weighted prime-phase field under continuous vertical averaging, determine which representation phase applies. If the weights are square-summable, test the `VIS-175` gate `yW_y^2/H->0`; if the normalized largest weight vanishes, test the `VIS-176` fixed-moment transfer conditions. For prime-power weights at any `alpha>=0`, a fixed polylogarithmic cutoff is already covered by one of these two full-law controls.

For collision or decoder tails, shrinking events, moving thresholds, or unbounded statistics, do not infer a separation from weak-law results; use the matched rare-event/moment controls from `VIS-164`--`VIS-168` or prove the stronger quantitative control actually required. For Gram/discrete sampling, use the Gram-clock controls rather than importing a continuous theorem. A faster-growing support law is a legitimate boundary only after showing that the existing sufficient transfer gates genuinely fail and that the proposed statistic is not already forced by the matched Haar population.

Accept a new direction only if it proves a predeclared separation from the relevant matched control in a regime where the control has nondegenerate mass and the proposed statistic is not already determined by the prime-torus representation. Kill it if the same effect is reproduced by the matched deterministic/Haar model, appears only after post-selected weights/windows/thresholds, or disappears once the representation and sampling geometry are matched.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-071` controls fixed finite continuous prime fields. `VIS-160` shows that arbitrary fixed finite-order Gram-phase tests can be reproduced by deterministic clock selectors. `VIS-163`--`VIS-168` control regular decoding, collision tails, moment growth, and growing-support event mass. `VIS-172`--`VIS-173` show that weighted bulk concentration and shape are separate representation effects. `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments to the deterministic continuous prime-log orbit. `VIS-175` gives the full weak law for additive square-summable weights, while `VIS-176` gives the complementary full Gaussian weak law in the Lindeberg/nonsquare-summable phase under its stated moment-transfer conditions.

None of these results proves a zeta-versus-control source separation. The full-law results do not cover Gram-point/discrete sampling, hybrid Euler--Hadamard residuals, zeta-zero variables, moving rare-event tests, decoder singularities, unbounded observables, or support growth beyond their sufficient gates. These remaining boundaries are research opportunities, not established mechanisms, and no result here implies RH.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Ordinary bounded-continuous distributional structure of the normalized additive prime-phase field under polylogarithmic continuous support is closed as a standalone positive discriminator across the entire prime-power family `p^(-alpha)`, `alpha>=0`: the matched null is Gaussian for `alpha<=1/2` and generally non-Gaussian for `alpha>1/2`.

Continue only through a predeclared zeta-facing separation that genuinely leaves this matched representation class and also survives the appropriate sampling, rare-event, weighting, and support-growth controls. The strongest current candidates are source-bearing hybrid/zero observables, genuinely discrete sampling phenomena, or quantitatively stronger boundary statistics excluded by the existing weak-law theorems, not another remapping of the same continuous weighted prime torus.
