# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has progressively removed representation and sampling effects that can look arithmetically structured without adding a new usable mechanism. `VIS-064`--`VIS-066` show that invertible hybrid factor/residual recombinations do not create information, while `VIS-083`--`VIS-091` and `VIS-150`--`VIS-156` show that the deterministic Gram clock can generate Haar populations, coherent lag fields, critical Haar fibers, irregular-delay geometry, and sparse affine samples from the same fixed-prime state.

`VIS-157`--`VIS-160` then show that increasingly complicated deterministic selectors do not solve this problem. Every fixed polynomial/power-law selector has a finite-order clock threshold, and a diagonal selector of growing degree can pass every fixed finite-order finite-prime Haar test. Fixed-test success, even at arbitrarily high finite order, is therefore not source evidence.

`VIS-161`--`VIS-163` move from sampling to representation. Two exact prime phases already identify height set-theoretically, but only through a nowhere-continuous inverse; finite-prime Lipschitz decoding is exactly a phase-collision quotient; and a completely ordinary held-out prime already has no continuous or globally Lipschitz decoder from fixed retained support. Absolute decoder failure is therefore not source-sensitive.

`VIS-164` makes that omitted-source control quantitative on exact returns of one retained prime. With `d=|P|-1`, the held-out-prime collision score has a fixed-threshold Haar law with tail `c_d L^(-d)`. `VIS-165` crosses the first order-of-limits boundary: Matveev-type finite Diophantine control plus Erdős--Turán--Koksma discrepancy gives a polynomial finite-`Q` discrepancy rate for the prime-log rotation, and the collision exceedance law continues to track its shrinking Haar tail for thresholds `L(Q)=Q^gamma` with sufficiently small positive `gamma`. Thus even **positive-power growth of anchored collision scores is already present in the matched omitted-prime null**.

## Research question

After quotienting the prime-torus population law, the full finite-test Gram-clock hierarchy, pathological exact-height encoding, the Lipschitz collision criterion, the dimension-coded held-out-prime tail, and its first rigorous jointly growing threshold regime, does any zeta-facing statistic exhibit a **predeclared relative regularity/decoder separation from matched omitted-prime controls**?

The target is no longer absolute instability, raw collision growth, or successful fixed-dimensional visualization. A surviving route must distinguish the zeta-facing observable after matching support, anchor/recurrence geometry, normalization, decoder class, and finite-window complexity.

## Why it may matter

The control surface now reaches beyond qualitative equidistribution. `VIS-165` proves that the held-out-prime null itself has a nontrivial quantitative shrinking-target regime: some positive power of the anchor count is eventually exceeded while empirical exceedance frequencies still follow the Haar tail. Consequently a numerical or visual candidate with polynomially increasing collision scores can remain completely source-blind.

This sharpens the useful question to a relative one. A positive result would need an exponent, normalized tail, approximation error, or other complexity functional that differs from the matched omitted-prime baseline under a frozen representation budget. Such a separation would be materially stronger than the representation and clock effects already closed; failure would close another attractive visual route without confusing generic omitted-frequency instability with zeta-specific structure.

## Decisive test

Freeze before inspection:

- a retained finite prime support `P`, or an explicit support-growth law;
- the anchor/recurrence family and torus metric;
- the zeta-facing observable `A` and normalization;
- the base-height/window rule;
- one or more held-out-prime controls chosen by a predeclared matching rule;
- the decoder/approximation class and complexity budget;
- the collision or approximation statistic, threshold family, comparison functional, and decision rule.

For fixed support, use `VIS-164`--`VIS-165` as the mandatory baseline on the anchored recurrence family. The held-out-prime control already has fixed-threshold law `mu_d(L)`, tail `c_d L^(-d)`, polynomial star-discrepancy convergence, and a rigorous regime `L(Q)=Q^gamma` with positive admissible `gamma` in which the empirical shrinking-tail law remains valid.

A candidate is interesting only if it proves a predeclared separation from that matched law: for example a different asymptotic exponent, a normalized tail ratio bounded away from the control, a strictly different approximation-error law at matched decoder budget, or a stable distributional functional not forced by the same prime-log recurrence geometry. Mere divergence, positive-power growth, or a smaller raw collision count after adding retained primes fails the test.

If support grows with height, require uniformity strong enough to account simultaneously for the changing null dimension, the changing Diophantine constants, and the next omitted frequency. `VIS-165` is fixed-support and does not grant such uniformity. A positive growing-support theorem must therefore control these dependencies rather than silently moving the omitted-source difficulty outward.

Kill the route if the apparent zeta separation is reproduced by held-out-prime phases under the same frozen budget, disappears after normalization by the `VIS-164`/`VIS-165` control, or depends on choosing support, thresholds, windows, or controls after inspecting favorable residuals.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-150`--`VIS-160` are deterministic clock/sampling controls. `VIS-161`--`VIS-163` establish the representation and decoder boundaries. `VIS-164` gives the fixed-threshold anchored Haar law and support-dimension tail. `VIS-165` supplies a fixed-support polynomial discrepancy rate and a conservative but rigorous jointly growing threshold regime derived from classical linear-forms and discrepancy theory.

None of these results compares a zeta-facing observable with the held-out-prime baseline and proves a source-sensitive separation. `VIS-165` is not uniform in growing support, does not identify the true extreme-value scale, and gives no zeta or RH consequence. The current clue therefore remains an accepted research question rather than evidence for a new mechanism.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Fixed finite phase mixing, increasingly rich deterministic Gram selectors, raw-information arguments, absolute continuous/Lipschitz decoder failure, fixed-threshold omitted-prime collision tails, and **small positive-power anchored collision growth under fixed support** are all closed as standalone positive discriminators.

Continue only through a predeclared relative zeta-versus-held-out-prime separation after the `VIS-164`/`VIS-165` baseline is accounted for, or through a genuinely uniform growing-support theorem whose constants and changing null dimension are controlled rather than assumed away.
