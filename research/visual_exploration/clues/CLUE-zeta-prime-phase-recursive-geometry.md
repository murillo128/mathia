# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has closed a broad family of attractive visual signals as representation or sampling effects rather than source evidence. Fixed finite prime fields already have their shared-phase Haar population law (`VIS-071`); increasingly rich deterministic Gram selectors can reproduce every preassigned finite-order finite-prime Haar test (`VIS-160`); and ordinary omitted-prime controls already exhibit decoder failure, heavy collision tails, moment thresholds, and growing-support population barriers (`VIS-163`, `VIS-164`, `VIS-168`).

The weighted bulk is also representation-dependent. `VIS-172` and `VIS-173` separate the concentration scale `D_2` from the shape scale `D_4`; `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments from the deterministic continuous prime-log field to the matched Haar null. `VIS-175` closes the full-distribution loophole for square-summable weights, while `VIS-176` closes the complementary Lindeberg phase by giving the full Gaussian law for nonsquare-summable prime-power weights under its original polylogarithmic example.

`VIS-177` sharpens the elementary nonresonant frequency gap in the fixed-moment transfer from `y^-k` to `y^-floor(k/2)`. In the Lindeberg prime-power phase `0<=alpha<=1/2`, this is enough to transfer every fixed moment — and hence the complete Gaussian weak law from `VIS-176` — for every cutoff `y(H)=H^o(1)`. Therefore merely leaving the polylogarithmic regime does not escape the representation-matched continuous null.

`VIS-178` inserts the actual prime-power effective dimension into that generic fixed-character bound. For fixed `0<=alpha<1/2`, `D_2(y)~c_alpha y/log y`; at `alpha=1/2`, `D_2(y)~4y/[(log y)^2 log log y]`. If `e_k=floor(k/2)+k/2`, the `k`th deterministic/Haar moment transfers under a polynomial cutoff `y=H^delta` throughout the closed sufficient wedge `delta<=1/e_k`.

`VIS-179` now shows that the quadratic boundary is much larger than that generic wedge suggests. Rewriting the normalized cosine field as the real part of a Dirichlet polynomial and applying the classical Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem gives

`| H^(-1) int_T^(T+H) S_y(t)^2 dt - 1/8 | = O(y/H)`

uniformly in the interval start, prime subset, and positive weights. Thus **every sublinear support `y=o(H)` is already variance-matched**, including every polynomial law `y=H^delta` with `delta<1`. In particular, the entire region `1/2<delta<1` is not a new opportunity for a global quadratic energy/variance discriminator even though it lies beyond the `k=2` consequence of `VIS-178`.

Thus the ordinary continuous additive prime-power family has a broad matched control: Gaussian in the Lindeberg phase throughout subpolynomial support, generally non-Gaussian infinite weighted Haar in the square-summable phase under the separate `VIS-175` gate, finite-order agreement in an explicit polynomial-support wedge, and a substantially stronger weight-independent quadratic null throughout all strictly sublinear support.

## Research question

After quotienting the finite prime-torus population law, the deterministic Gram-clock hierarchy, regular-decoder/collision geometry, support-volume effects, coordinate weighting, fixed-moment transfer, the two full-law weight phases, the balanced-character sharpening, the prime-density effective-dimension correction, and the near-linear Montgomery–Vaughan quadratic null, does any zeta-facing visual statistic exhibit a **predeclared source-sensitive separation from the representation-matched deterministic/Haar control**?

A surviving mechanism must leave at least one hypothesis of the closed control class in a mathematically essential way. Plausible boundaries now include a genuine hybrid prime/zero variable, Gram or other discrete sampling not covered by continuous vertical averaging, a rare/shrinking or unbounded functional requiring stronger than weak convergence, a statistic of genuinely higher or growing complexity than total quadratic energy, support at or beyond the linear `y~H` transition with an independently justified observable, or another source-dependent quantity not reducible to the same normalized additive prime torus.

## Why it may matter

The useful target is now much narrower than “find an unusual prime-phase picture.” Stable non-Gaussianity in the square-summable phase, Gaussianization in the nonsquare-summable Lindeberg phase, persistence of that Gaussian null throughout arbitrary subpolynomial support, finite-order agreement in a positive-power support wedge, and variance matching throughout all `y=o(H)` are forced by the representation itself under the stated continuous-window gates.

A positive result that survives the fully matched control would therefore isolate information not explained by ordinary prime-frequency geometry, coordinate weighting, prime density, classical Dirichlet-polynomial mean-square cancellation, or continuous vertical mixing. Failure is equally informative because it prevents increasingly elaborate remappings or low-complexity summaries of the same additive torus from being mistaken for zeta-specific structure.

## Decisive test

Freeze before inspection the support/support-growth law, coordinate weights or prewhitening, sampling rule, normalization, zeta-facing observable, matched control, statistic, and decision rule.

**First apply the quadratic gate.** If the proposed continuous statistic is determined by the global second moment/variance of the additive prime-log cosine field and `y=o(H)`, kill it immediately as a source discriminator: `VIS-179` forces the deterministic variance to `1/8` for arbitrary positive weights and arbitrary prime subsets below `y`. Do not treat moving from `delta<=1/2` to `1/2<delta<1` as an escape from the matched null.

For higher fixed-order information, determine which representation phase applies. If the weights are square-summable, test the `VIS-175` gate `yW_y^2/H->0`. If the normalized largest weight vanishes, use the strengthened `VIS-177` fixed-moment transfer condition

`y^floor(k/2) D_2(y)^(k/2)/H -> 0`

for every fixed `k`, together with the `VIS-176` Lindeberg step. For prime-power weights `0<=alpha<=1/2`, every subpolynomial cutoff `y(H)=H^o(1)` is already controlled.

For a polynomial cutoff `y=H^delta` with prime-power weights and a genuinely higher-order fixed statistic, identify the effective moment/order complexity `K`. Put `e_K=floor(K/2)+K/2`. If the test is determined by a fixed panel through order `K` and `delta<=1/e_K`, kill it as a source discriminator by `VIS-178`. Beyond that sufficient wedge, establish whether a stronger collective mean-value, characteristic-function, or discrepancy theorem still controls the matched null before interpreting a residual as source-sensitive.

For collision or decoder tails, shrinking events, moving thresholds, or unbounded statistics, do not infer a separation from weak-law or variance results; use the matched rare-event/moment controls from `VIS-164`--`VIS-168` or prove the stronger quantitative control actually required. For Gram/discrete sampling, use the Gram-clock controls rather than importing a continuous theorem.

Accept a new direction only if it proves a predeclared separation from the relevant matched control in a regime where the control has nondegenerate mass and the proposed statistic is not already determined by the prime-torus representation. Kill it if the same effect is reproduced by the matched deterministic/Haar model, appears only after post-selected weights/windows/thresholds, or disappears once the representation and sampling geometry are matched.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-071` controls fixed finite continuous prime fields. `VIS-160` shows that arbitrary fixed finite-order Gram-phase tests can be reproduced by deterministic clock selectors. `VIS-163`--`VIS-168` control regular decoding, collision tails, moment growth, and growing-support event mass. `VIS-172`--`VIS-173` show that weighted bulk concentration and shape are separate representation effects. `VIS-174` transfers fixed weighted moments to the deterministic continuous prime-log orbit. `VIS-175` gives the full weak law for additive square-summable weights, while `VIS-176` gives the complementary full Gaussian weak law in the Lindeberg/nonsquare-summable phase. `VIS-177` strengthens the elementary character separation and extends the latter control to arbitrary subpolynomial prime-power support. `VIS-178` uses classical weighted-prime asymptotics to sharpen the generic finite-order polynomial-support wedge. `VIS-179` then uses the classical Montgomery–Vaughan Dirichlet-polynomial mean-value theorem to replace the generic `k=2` bound by the stronger weight-independent variance error `O(y/H)`.

The `VIS-178` polynomial-support statement remains only a sufficient fixed-order control for `k>=3` and for higher fixed panels. `VIS-179` is stronger only at quadratic order; it does **not** prove a Gaussian full law at any fixed positive `delta`, does not establish a sharp transition at `y~H`, and does not rule out source-sensitive observables using higher/growing order, rare events, unbounded functionals, discrete sampling, or hybrid prime/zero information.

None of these results proves a zeta-versus-control source separation. They do not control Gram-point/discrete sampling, hybrid Euler--Hadamard residuals, zeta-zero variables, moving rare-event tests, decoder singularities, unbounded observables, or the full law at genuinely polynomial-or-faster support in the Lindeberg phase. These remaining boundaries are research opportunities, not established mechanisms, and no result here implies RH.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Ordinary bounded-continuous distributional structure of the normalized additive prime-phase field under continuous averaging is closed as a standalone positive discriminator across the regimes currently proved. In addition, **global quadratic energy/variance is now closed throughout every `y=o(H)` support law**, for arbitrary positive prime weights, by the Montgomery–Vaughan mean-square control in `VIS-179`.

Continue only through a predeclared zeta-facing separation that genuinely leaves these matched representation classes and also survives the appropriate sampling, rare-event, weighting, support-growth, and observable-complexity controls. The strongest current candidates are source-bearing hybrid/zero observables, genuinely discrete sampling phenomena, stronger functional classes, higher or growing complexity not reducible to variance, or behavior at/above the linear support transition with a separately justified matched control — not another quadratic remapping of the same continuous weighted prime torus.
