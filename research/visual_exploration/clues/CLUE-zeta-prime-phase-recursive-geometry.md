# Can a zeta visual statistic leave the regular prime-torus representation class after the full Gram-clock quotient?

## Observation

The prime-phase program has progressively removed representation and sampling effects that can look arithmetically structured without adding a new usable mechanism. `VIS-064`--`VIS-066` show that invertible hybrid factor/residual recombinations do not create information, while `VIS-083`--`VIS-091` and `VIS-150`--`VIS-156` show that the deterministic Gram clock can generate Haar populations, coherent lag fields, critical Haar fibers, irregular-delay geometry, and sparse affine samples from the same fixed-prime state.

`VIS-157`--`VIS-159` classify increasingly rich fixed-complexity index selectors. A degree-`d` polynomial selector is Haar through `d` fixed samples and exposes a deterministic moment-null character at `d+1`; the full fixed power family `floor(n^alpha)` has the corresponding threshold `ceil(alpha)`. `VIS-160` then diagonally glues polynomial stages of increasing degree into one strictly increasing deterministic selector `K(n)` such that every fixed finite sample-order/fixed-prime character test is eventually Haar. Selector complexity growing with height is therefore not itself a new information source.

`VIS-161` adds a different representation boundary. For any two distinct primes `p,q`, the exact phase pair `(p^(-it),q^(-it))` is already injective in the real height `t`, even though its dense-orbit inverse is nowhere continuous. Consequently every same-height analytic coordinate is set-theoretically a function of two exact prime phases. Raw information or unrestricted measurability is therefore too permissive a discriminator: the unresolved content lies in **stable, regular, complexity-controlled recoverability** from the admitted phase/clock representation.

## Research question

After quotienting the prime-torus population law, the fixed finite Gram-clock hierarchy, fixed affine thinning, fixed polynomial/power selectors, the `VIS-160` diagonal growing-complexity control, and the pathological exact-height encoding exposed by `VIS-161`, does any zeta-facing visual statistic escape a predeclared **regular prime-phase/clock representation class**?

A remaining sampling route must contain quantitative structure that the diagonal construction cannot absorb: for example a pre-specified natural selector together with a test whose order, prime support, character complexity, or resolution grows with height at a controlled rate. The analytic-coordinate alternative must now be phrased as a regularity/complexity separation: Hardy `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, nearby-zero geometry, or another analytic coordinate should resist reconstruction or approximation by a frozen class such as bounded Fourier/character complexity, controlled modulus of continuity, Lipschitz maps, finite-resolution partitions, or another explicitly justified stable decoder family.

## Why it may matter

The control class is stronger than any collection of fixed-dimensional scatter plots or finite-order Haar tests. Even seeing correct Haar behavior at every fixed finite order, considered one order at a time, does not force stochasticity or a new arithmetic source; deterministic completely-uniform-distribution phenomena and `VIS-160` give exact controls of that inference.

At the same time, `VIS-161` shows that demanding an analytic coordinate be absent from the exact phase state is impossible in a raw information sense: infinite-precision phase data can encode height through a violently discontinuous inverse. This turns decoder regularity from an implementation detail into the load-bearing mathematical question. A useful positive result must defeat a **quantitative clock/representation null**, not merely exhibit a coordinate that looks different from an Euler-product picture.

## Decisive test

Use `VIS-150`--`VIS-156` for fixed lag geometry and affine thinning, `VIS-157`--`VIS-159` for fixed polynomial/power selectors, `VIS-160` for arbitrary fixed finite-order tests under an adversarial deterministic selector whose local degree grows with height, and `VIS-161` for the distinction between exact set-theoretic encoding and regular recoverability.

Do **not** treat any fixed collection of Haar, decorrelation, scatter, lag, or product-character tests as source evidence merely because all of them pass. `VIS-160` can absorb every such fixed test eventually. Likewise, do not claim that Hardy `Z`, zero occupancy, or another same-height coordinate is informationally independent merely because no simple formula from a few prime phases is known: `VIS-161` shows that exact phase pairs already identify height.

A sampling-based escape must specify a selector independently of the observed success and prove a quantitative theorem for a jointly growing test family. Examples include sample order `r=r(N)`, prime support `P=P(N)`, Fourier/character complexity, or another resolution parameter increasing with height. The decisive comparison is whether the claimed zeta signal survives a deterministic clock control calibrated at the same growing complexity, with enough uniformity to prevent a stagewise diagonal selector from postponing each test until after its local degree has overtaken it.

For an analytic-coordinate escape, freeze the decoder class **before** examining the positive residual. Then either prove a quantitative lower bound on reconstruction/approximation from the admitted prime-phase/clock state, or exhibit a representation invariant that the class cannot reproduce. Finite precision and stability must be part of the statement: a discontinuous exact inverse such as `VIS-161` is not an admissible reconstruction mechanism. Kill the route if the coordinate is reproduced within the same predeclared regularity/complexity budget or if the apparent separation vanishes once the matched deterministic clock is given the same resolution.

## Evidence boundary

`VIS-150`--`VIS-159` are fixed-complexity asymptotic controls. `VIS-160` is an existential diagonal construction: it shows that one deterministic increasing selector can pass every fixed finite-dimensional character test, but it supplies no useful convergence rate and no uniform theorem when test complexity grows with height.

`VIS-161` is also a boundary result, not an analytic reconstruction theorem. It proves exact injectivity of a two-prime phase coordinate and the nowhere continuity of its inverse; it does **not** provide a stable decoder for zeta values, Hardy `Z`, derivatives, zero occupancy, or zero geometry. Nor does it prove that any of those coordinates lies outside a particular regular decoder class. That separation must be established for the chosen class and approximation notion.

Accordingly, the current corpus does not rule out a natural explicit changing-complexity selector with strong quantitative uniformity, nor a jointly growing statistic that outruns the diagonal clock, nor a stable analytic coordinate that provably requires more regular complexity than the admitted phase representation. None of these Gram-clock or phase-injectivity results is a stronger zeta criterion or an RH consequence.

## Research disposition

Accepted in further narrowed form. Fixed finite commensurate lag geometry, fixed affine thinning, all fixed polynomial/power-law selectors, and generic growth of selector complexity by itself are closed as positive information escapes on fixed prime support. `VIS-161` also closes **raw information absence** as a useful analytic-coordinate criterion: two exact prime phases already encode height, but only through an unstable discontinuous inverse.

Continue only through a genuinely quantitative changing-complexity law whose test complexity grows with height under pre-specified controls, a separately derived nonclock sampling mechanism that survives the same quantitative audit, or an analytic coordinate separated from the prime-phase/clock state within an explicitly frozen regularity, stability, resolution, or computational-complexity class.
