# Visual Exploration

## Research mandate

### Primary object

This line studies mathematically faithful visual representations of arithmetic and analytic structures relevant to Mathia's Riemann research program. The primary objects are not pictures in isolation but explicit representation maps: coordinate embeddings, recursive subdivisions, residue and exponent geometries, complex or spectral portraits, arithmetic dynamics, mathematically induced graph layouts, multiscale projections, and related transformations whose rendered structure can be traced back to a precise mathematical construction.

The representation itself — including truncation, scale, normalization, ordering, projection, and parameters — is part of the object being studied whenever those choices can create or destroy apparent structure.

### Objective

Use visual compression and pattern recognition to discover mathematical questions that are difficult to notice directly in symbolic form: candidate invariants, symmetries, scaling laws, forbidden regions, recurrences, phase changes, obstructions, hidden equivalences, or cross-line correspondences.

The durable goal is not to establish claims by appearance. It is to turn robust visual structure into falsifiable mathematical questions and, when possible, into independently stated and justified mathematical results.

### Priority questions

Explore representations that may reveal:

- structure that persists across scale, truncation, projection, or reasonable reparameterization;
- arithmetic self-similarity or multiscale organization that is not forced by the chosen coordinates;
- geometric distinctions between prime/arithmetic objects and matched random, shuffled, generalized, or surrogate controls;
- hidden boundaries, gaps, clustering, anisotropy, recurrence, or spectral organization that can be translated into quantitative statements;
- visually natural dictionaries between constructions currently studied by different research lines;
- alternate representations that make a known obstruction, information-loss mechanism, or local/global distinction more transparent;
- mathematically meaningful structures suggested by fractal geometry, recursive tessellation, symbolic dynamics, arithmetic dynamics, profinite or p-adic organization, complex dynamics, or topological/spectral views;
- entirely new mathematically motivated visual directions whose usefulness is not yet owned by another research line.

These are opportunity classes rather than a fixed queue. The line is deliberately free to switch objects and representations when another visual experiment appears more informative.

### Scope and exclusions

The line may inspect any current research line as context and may initiate an exploration without an incoming clue. It may also explore a mathematically motivated direction that does not yet belong to any existing line when there is a plausible connection to the broader Riemann program.

Purely decorative images, arbitrary layouts with no mathematical semantics, parameter fishing for attractive patterns, and claims that depend only on subjective visual resemblance are outside the target. A visualization that merely redraws a known identity without exposing an additional discriminating question is not progress by itself.

This line does not absorb the scientific mandate of another Research Watch merely because it visualizes that line's objects. Its owner question is whether a representation exposes additional mathematical structure or a better falsifiable question.

### Line-specific falsification controls

For visually suggested structure, test the failure modes most likely to be induced by the representation itself:

- reasonable coordinate, ordering, projection, scale, normalization, and rendering perturbations;
- finite-window, finite-resolution, sampling, aliasing, and truncation effects;
- matched random or shuffled controls preserving the obvious local statistics;
- generalized-prime, surrogate, or de-arithmeticized controls when the claimed signal is supposed to be arithmetic-specific;
- construction-induced self-similarity, symmetry, clustering, or forbidden regions;
- apparent patterns that vanish when color or non-geometric encoding is removed;
- selection effects caused by choosing only visually favorable parameters or windows.

A candidate mechanism becomes more interesting when its mathematical content survives changes that should be irrelevant while failing on controls that remove the arithmetic structure it claims to expose.

### Prior-art domains

- experimental mathematics and mathematical visualization;
- fractal geometry, iterated function systems, and multiscale analysis;
- complex dynamics, Julia/Mandelbrot-type parameter spaces, and arithmetic dynamics;
- symbolic dynamics and automata-defined arithmetic sets;
- p-adic, profinite, and ultrametric geometry;
- residue-class, modular, prime-exponent, and multiplicative-coordinate representations;
- harmonic, spectral, phase-space, and complex-plane visualization of zeta and related functions;
- topological data analysis and persistent-structure methods when they are applied to mathematically intrinsic representations;
- visualization methods for prime distributions, sieves, zero statistics, and arithmetic point processes.

### Relationship to other lines

This is a transversal exploration line rather than a replacement for any existing mathematical attack. It may use objects, findings, obstructions, or open questions from any current line as input, and it may expose candidate connections or new directions that are better investigated elsewhere.

A result that becomes independently mathematical may remain a canonical finding of this line even when it concerns an object also studied elsewhere; genuine overlap should then be handled through the normal cross-line evidence and graph mechanisms. Visual similarities alone do not establish a bridge between lines.
