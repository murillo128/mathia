# MI-041 — Wang fragmentation renormalizes to lower-denominator mechanics before aggregation becomes necessary

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed synthesis from VIS-313--[VIS-325](../../findings/VIS-325-wang-center-gap-mechanical-renormalization.md), plus classical rational mechanical/Christoffel geometry and the dimension-two Selberg upper-bound sieve. No prime-pair asymptotic or RH consequence is claimed.

The cutoff-free Wang remainder has no useful global nearest-frequency spacing, but dyadic shells reduce the finite-window loss to prime-ratio occupancy with destination taper. VIS-317--VIS-323 identify the faithful coordinates: a unimodular Bézout chart preserves both prime exclusions, the thin domain has only a few mechanical branches, center-gap decimation converts them into fixed-gap diagonal runs, and the aggregate Hardy--Littlewood weight of those runs is neutral at the relevant scale.

VIS-324 prices the first-level run length. The standard translation-uniform two-linear-form Selberg sieve gives the required two-prime upper-bound scale whenever `k<=M^(1-delta)` at the natural Wang window. Every polynomially long first-level run is therefore already long enough; the apparent obstruction moves to `k=M^(1-o(1))`, especially `k>L`.

VIS-325 shows that `k>L` is not representation-final. In that regime the observed branch differs at only `O(1)` determinant indices from the rational mechanical path with slope `d/k`, where `k=|u-v|` and the original slope is its Farey neighbour at distance exactly `1/(km)`. The denominator-`k` word has period `k`, and one full period moves the lattice path by exactly `(sigma,sigma)`. The earlier diagonal decimation is therefore the period monodromy of a lower-denominator mechanical representation, not an accidental identity.

The reusable lesson is that fragmentation must be measured after the strongest faithful renormalization. A branch that looks singleton-sized after quotienting by one period can still carry a lower-denominator continued-fraction hierarchy. The next discriminator is whether that hierarchy exposes a shorter primitive block with enough repetitions for ordinary two-prime sieving, or whether the complementary continued-fraction regime supplies a deterministic discrepancy/energy estimate. Only after these possibilities are priced should one conclude that genuinely cross-fragment aggregation is unavoidable.

**Boundary.** VIS-325 is a representation theorem, not a prime-count estimate. It does not prove that continued-fraction iteration always yields a polynomially long affine run, nor that bounded-type paths satisfy the needed energy bound. VIS-324 remains only an upper-bound sieve result. Any positive continuation must preserve the two-prime local arithmetic and the Wang destination taper; mechanical renormalization alone is not cancellation.
