# MI-041 — Wang fragmentation renormalizes to small Christoffel-parent monodromies before aggregation becomes necessary

**Evidence level:** exact/literature-backed synthesis from VIS-313--[VIS-326](../../findings/VIS-326-wang-christoffel-parent-monodromy.md), plus classical rational mechanical/Christoffel geometry and the dimension-two Selberg upper-bound sieve. No prime-pair asymptotic or RH consequence is claimed.

The cutoff-free Wang remainder has no useful global nearest-frequency spacing, but dyadic shells reduce the finite-window loss to prime-ratio occupancy with destination taper. VIS-317--VIS-323 identify the faithful coordinates: a unimodular Bézout chart preserves both prime exclusions, the thin domain has only a few mechanical branches, center-gap decimation converts them into fixed-gap diagonal runs, and the aggregate Hardy--Littlewood weight of those runs is neutral at the relevant scale.

VIS-324 prices the first-level run length. The standard translation-uniform two-linear-form Selberg sieve gives the required two-prime upper-bound scale whenever `k<=M^(1-delta)` at the natural Wang window. Every polynomially long first-level run is therefore already long enough; the apparent obstruction moves to `k=M^(1-o(1))`, especially `k>L`.

VIS-325 shows that `k>L` is not representation-final. In that regime the observed branch differs at only `O(1)` determinant indices from the rational mechanical path with slope `d/k`, where `k=|u-v|` and the original slope is its Farey neighbour at distance exactly `1/(km)`. The denominator-`k` word has period `k`, and one full period moves the lattice path by exactly `(sigma,sigma)`.

VIS-326 resolves the first Christoffel split inside that lower-denominator word. Put `J=floor(m/k)` and `q=m mod k`. The larger-denominator Farey neighbour selects a parent of length `q`; the complementary parent has length `k-q`. Their Wang monodromies have consecutive integer coordinates and are primitive. For `v>u` they are `(1-J,-J)` and `(J,J+1)`; for `u>v` they are `(J,J-1)` and `(-J-1,-J)`. In the hard natural regime `k>L asymp M/log log M`, every coordinate is only `O(log log M)`.

This is a stronger representation gain than merely knowing a continued-fraction hierarchy exists. The first parent blocks already move corresponding prime-coordinate positions along small primitive affine directions. But the gain is conditional on **visible repetition**. `J=1` iff `m>2n`, and then the externally selected parent has axis monodromy `(0,-1)` or `(1,0)`, so copy-index sieving loses one arithmetic dimension; the complementary parent remains nondegenerate. For `J>=2`, both coordinates of both parent steps are nonzero and coprime, so the first remaining obstruction is repeat multiplicity and the offset-dependent resultant/local-factor cost, not step content.

The reusable lesson is that fragmentation must be measured after the strongest faithful renormalization and then after the **actual finite-window repetition pattern**. A branch that looks singleton-sized after quotienting by one period can contain a lower-denominator parent packet with very small primitive displacement, yet small displacement alone is not cancellation. The next discriminator is whether enough copies of a nondegenerate parent occur to support the two-prime sieve, whether the axis case can use the complementary parent or offset averaging, or whether the nonrepetitive regime instead admits a deterministic discrepancy/energy estimate.

**Boundary.** VIS-326 classifies only the first Christoffel/Farey split. It does not prove that a useful parent repeats many times in the observed subperiod, bound aggregate resultants/local factors, or establish prime pseudorandomness. The `J=1` axis case obstructs only the naive per-copy dimension-two sieve on that parent. Any positive continuation must preserve the two-prime local arithmetic and the Wang destination taper; mechanical renormalization remains structural simplification rather than arithmetic cancellation.
