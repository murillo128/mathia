# MI-001 — Visual residuals are meaningful only after exact quotients, matched nulls, and a predeclared common information geometry are explicit

**Evidence level:** supported through VIS-044 by exact reconstruction identities, finite-population controls, Hadamard factorization, Fisher-normal geometry, common-reference residual comparisons, and dual KL-pooling controls

## Core intuition

Visual complexity is not an independent arithmetic resource when it can be reconstructed from ordinary coordinates, lower-order statistics, symmetry, deterministic boundary geometry, or the complete zero divisor itself. The meaningful object is the residual after the strongest control preserving the information already known to generate the picture.

The three-gap results add a second information-loss gate. Scalar residual scores can preserve the **size** of irreducible dependence while discarding its direction. Direction can be compared only after processes are embedded in one common predeclared metric/gauge, and the construction of that gauge must itself be audited: changing pooling rule or KL direction can manufacture higher-order interaction from lower-order panel heterogeneity.

## Strongest justified principle

VIS-013--VIS-034 classify circular reconstruction, sampled statistics, Farey endpoint controls, same-gap/reflection nulls, and the complete-xi divisor quotient. VIS-035--VIS-040 then show that CA, Pearson, LRT/CMI and nearby finite-size expansions largely measure the same three-gap residual magnitude; locally CMI is one half of the squared Fisher norm of the perturbation normal to the Markov manifold and therefore discards orientation.

VIS-041 restores orientation after fixing one common positive Markov reference. VIS-042 extends this to a predeclared panel via logarithmic pooling of the panel Markov closures, preserving the Markov family and producing a positive-semidefinite residual-orientation Gram.

VIS-043 gives that gauge an exact but conditional variational meaning: for fixed panel, weights, support and KL direction, the logarithmic pool is the unique weighted `D_KL(gauge||closure)` barycenter. Its dispersion is entirely lower-order Markov disagreement, not own-process residual alignment.

VIS-044 shows why this representation contract matters. The reverse KL barycenter is the arithmetic pool; mixtures of Markov closures can leave the Markov family, with the induced three-way residual exactly equal to a weighted cross-panel covariance of the left/right conditional channels. The extra constrained-KL cost is exactly the mixture CMI. Thus an apparent higher-order residual can be a pooling artifact even when every panel member individually has zero such interaction.

## What remains possible

For Farey discrepancy, test pre-registered sublinear bands against the exact reflection-same-gap null and separate amplitude from redistribution before interpreting any residual.

For conditional residuals, decide first whether the claim concerns magnitude or direction. If direction matters, freeze one common external or panel-derived reference, including panel, weights, support, partition, pooling rule, and KL direction, before inspection. Report lower-order gauge dispersion separately from residual orientation, and challenge the conclusion under reasonable source-compatible gauge choices.

More broadly, useful visual independence should be organized by explicit information loss: finite prime versus zero truncations, localized windows, differently conditioned nulls, residual directions versus norms, and other maps that provably discard different data.

## Status / novelty

Farey geometry, correspondence analysis, Fisher information, Markov models, logarithmic/linear opinion pooling, KL barycenters, conditional mutual information, and Hadamard factorization are classical ingredients. The persisted synthesis is the visual gate: **first quotient deterministic/statistical redundancy; then state which residual magnitude or orientation survives; compare orientation only in one predeclared common gauge; and audit the gauge so that lower-order panel heterogeneity cannot be mistaken for higher-order source structure**.
