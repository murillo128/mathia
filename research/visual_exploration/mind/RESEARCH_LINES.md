## Separate concentration, fluctuation shape, and rare-collision sample complexity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-009-stable-decoding-is-a-collision-geometry-question`, `MI-010-weighted-null-needs-separate-concentration-and-shape-dimensions`.

VIS-161--VIS-168 establish the held-out-prime collision null and its growing-support rare-event barrier: large collision thresholds are high-dimensional small-ball events whose observable mass depends on `Q mu_d(L)`.

VIS-169--VIS-171 calibrate the ordinary equal-weight bulk separately. The matched Haar collision score is generically `d^(-1/2)`; after `sqrt(d)` normalization it has an explicit arcsine-type limit, its first relative fluctuation is Gaussian, and finite `d` admits explicit concentration/normal-approximation control.

VIS-172--VIS-173 show that weighted support needs a second effective dimension. `D_2=W^2/sum w_j^2` controls concentration and leading bulk scale, while `D_4=(sum w_j^2)^2/sum w_j^4` controls whether the first relative correction is Gaussian. These gates can separate: for prime tapers `p^(-alpha)` with `1/2<alpha<=1`, `D_2` diverges but `D_4` remains finite, so the bulk concentrates while standardized shape retains low-prime memory.

A source-sensitive growing-support experiment must therefore beat three distinct null budgets: leading concentration (`D_2`), first-correction shape (`D_4` or the exact weighted null), and rare-collision population mass. It must separately justify deterministic prime-log transfer when replacing Haar phases. Matching only nominal support size or variance is not enough.

## Keep source corrections, deterministic clock mixing, weighted bulk calibration, and rare-event complexity separate

Moment-null controls attack low-frequency source corrections; Gram selectors attack apparent phase mixing; weighted Haar controls calibrate both bulk concentration and fluctuation shape; held-out primes calibrate stable-decoder failure. A residual becomes arithmetic evidence only after it survives the control at the same weights, support growth, height range, and sample budget.
