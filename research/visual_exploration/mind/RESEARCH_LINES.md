## Precompute the exact shared-prime-phase null before interpreting second- or third-order geometry

VIS-067 shows that the covariance of a shared-Steinhaus prime-phase field is exactly the real Gram matrix of its deterministic prime-power coefficient vectors. Scale bands, covariance ridges, height oscillations, and anisotropy can therefore be forced by smoothing overlap and the retained prime frequencies even under the randomized null.

VIS-068 shows that moving to third order does not automatically escape this control. When powers of one prime share one random phase, the third cumulant is an exact same-prime additive-harmonic resonance tensor with `k+l=m`. Prime-power coherence alone can generate skewness and bispectral structure; the tensor vanishes only if the null is truncated to first harmonics.

A useful arithmetic second- or third-order witness must therefore be frozen **relative to these deterministic null tensors**. If the intended signal is covariance, subtract/whiten the coefficient Gram baseline. If it is third order, compare with the exact resonance tensor before attributing any residual to zeta-specific organization. Higher-order statistics need their own corresponding null audit rather than an assumed Gaussian baseline.
