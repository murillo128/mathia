# Prime-flute compact-reference residual achievement set

![Finite subsums of compact-reference coefficient residuals](prime-flute-compact-reference-achievement-set.png)

## Question

PF-164 proves that a finite-support change of the all-composite comparison surface changes the selected connected Ruelle cusp coefficient additively. After removing the obvious integer displacement of the reference labels, do the remaining nonlinear cotangent corrections form a Cantor-like dust, or do the branches overlap enough to fill intervals?

## Construction

Use the PF-164 endpoint coordinate

\[
V(x)=\pi\cot\frac{\pi}{x},\qquad f(x)=V(x)-x.
\]

For an odd prime `p` whose following prime gap is larger than `2`, the replacement

\[
q=p+1\longmapsto q'=p+3
\]

is an allowed ordered all-composite `+2` perturbation. Its contribution to the PF-164 cusp-coefficient shift is

\[
2\bigl(V(p+3)-V(p+1)\bigr)
  =4+a(p),
\]

where the nonlinear residual atom is

\[
a(p)=2\bigl(f(p+3)-f(p+1)\bigr)>0.
\]

For any finite set `F` of such sites, PF-164's finite-support law therefore gives exactly

\[
\Delta C(F)-4|F|=\sum_{p\in F}a(p).
\]

The plot starts at `p=103` and takes the first `18` eligible prime sites. Row `n` shows every one of the `2^n` exact finite subsums of the first `n` residual atoms; the horizontal scale is multiplied by `10^3`. The final row therefore contains `262144` subsums. Residual atoms were evaluated at high precision before rendering.

## Observation

The first few rows have the visually familiar binary branching of a Cantor construction, but the gaps rapidly overlap and the finite subsums thicken into an almost continuous band rather than continuing to split into separated dust.

That appearance suggested an exact translation to classical achievement-set theory. The associated canonical finding proves that these atoms are positive, decreasing and slowly convergent in Kakeya's sense on every sufficiently far tail. Consequently the infinite achievement set of a sufficiently far tail is a compact interval, and the finite subset sums are dense in it.

Thus the fractal-looking finite stages do not survive as a Cantor set in the limit for this perturbation family.

## Robustness

The effect is not a raster or binning artifact: every plotted point is a direct subset sum of the exact PF-164 residual formula, with no histogram threshold or interpolation.

As a conservative finite check, eligible sites were enumerated from `p=103` through `p<20000`. For every eligible site through `p=19973`, its residual atom was already smaller than the sum of the later atoms *before* adding the uncomputed tail beyond `20000`; only the final two sites of the truncated enumeration fail because their true tail was deliberately omitted.

The mechanism is also not prime-specific in the sense needed for an RH signal. The proof uses the prime sequence only to guarantee sufficiently many eligible sites and `p_n`-scale growth. Once the atom sequence has the resulting `1/(k^2 log^2 k)` scale, the interval-filling conclusion is a classical achievement-set phenomenon.
