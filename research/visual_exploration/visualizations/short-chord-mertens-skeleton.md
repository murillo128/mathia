# Short-chord Mertens skeleton of the gap-two remainder

![Short-chord Mertens skeleton](short-chord-mertens-skeleton.png)

## Question

`PC-144` gives an exact asymptotic constant for the average remainder energy seen from the gap-two matching space. How is that constant distributed over chord offsets? In particular, is the Mertens-scale average remainder already concentrated on bounded short chords, leaving any genuinely nonlocal structure to appear only after the scalar average has been removed?

## Construction

For an oriented gap-two primitive pair, `PC-144` derives the fixed-offset contribution

\[
a(h)=\mathbf 1_{\{h\equiv0,2\pmod6,\ h\ne0,2\}}\frac{S(h)}{h^2},
\qquad
S(h)=\prod_{\substack{p\ge5\\p\mid h(h-2)}}\frac{p-2}{p-3}.
\]

The exact limiting constant is the common positive factor `kappa/(4 pi^2)` times `sum_h a(h)`, so relative contributions can be studied without that factor. The upper panel plots `a(h)` for signed offsets `|h|<=200` on a logarithmic vertical scale. The lower panel plots

\[
F(H)=\frac{\sum_{|h|\le H}a(h)}{\sum_h a(h)}.
\]

For the displayed numerical fractions the absolutely convergent denominator was truncated symmetrically at `|h|<=200000`. The corresponding raw offset sum is approximately `0.2319929144`; increasing the cutoff from `100000` to `200000` changes that approximation by about `2e-5` relatively. The plot is only a visualization of the exact absolutely convergent series proved in `PC-144`.

## Observation

The Mertens constant has a pronounced short-chord skeleton. The symmetric windows `|h|<=6,20,50,100,200` capture approximately `50.9%`, `81.7%`, `92.3%`, `96.1%`, and `98.0%` of the offset sum. Arithmetic factors `S(h)` create visible spikes, but the inverse-square chord weight dominates the envelope strongly enough that bounded offsets carry most of the scalar average remainder.

This is visually consistent with the earlier `VIS-005` obstruction, where the fixed short offsets `4` and `6` already force a nonvanishing worst-principal-angle defect. The two facts together suggest a useful separation: both the Mertens-scale average energy and a certified worst-angle obstruction have short-chord witnesses, but it is not yet known whether the *full average spectral-projector rotation* is asymptotically generated by bounded chord windows.

## Robustness

The plotted weights come directly from the exact CRT/Mertens formula in `PC-144`, not from finite-conductor eigensolver output. The cumulative denominator is stable under doubling a large symmetric truncation, and `PC-144` independently proves absolute convergence, so the concentration is not a raster, ordering, or sampling artifact.

What does **not** follow is equally important. Concentration of `tr(P_x R_x P_x)` on short offsets does not imply concentration of the spectral projector itself: coherent long-range couplings can have little scalar trace mass while still rotating a subspace. The picture therefore does not establish locality of the eigenspace geometry.

## Research consequence

The visualization motivates the proposed Prime-Circle clue
[[research/prime_circle/clues/CLUE-short-chord-mertens-projector-locality.md]].

The decisive next test is to truncate the remainder operator by chord length and ask whether bounded windows approximate the top-band projector at the natural Mertens/Frobenius scale. A positive result would classicalize the average rotation as finite-local CRT structure; a negative result would isolate a genuinely nonlocal residual even though the exact scalar remainder is overwhelmingly short-chord dominated.
