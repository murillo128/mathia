# FD-028 — infinitely many sparse Möbius prime directions still allow GCD-duality saturation

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + COMMON-SOURCE-CONSTRUCTION + EXACT-SQUAREFREE-TERNARY-INCREMENTS + INFINITE-SPARSE-PRIME-MOBIUS-ANCESTRY + RH-SCALE-DIAGONAL-CONTROL + SPARSE-HORIZON-SATURATION + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`.

`FD-027` proves that any fixed finite family of exact prime-direction identities

\[
a_{pn}=-a_n\qquad(p\nmid n)
\]

can coexist with a synthetic squarefree-ternary source that asymptotically saturates the sharp Franel--Mertens GCD coordinate constant on sufficiently sparse horizons. That leaves a natural ambiguity: perhaps the obstruction disappears as soon as the source obeys infinitely many genuine Möbius prime relations, even if those relations are very sparse among the primes.

It does not. **Infinite cardinality of the enforced prime ancestry is still insufficient.** More strongly, any prescribed finite family of prime directions can be embedded in an infinite sparse family for which the `FD-026`/`FD-027` saturation construction survives all `1/2+epsilon` source envelopes.

Let

\[
K_H(d,e):=\frac{\gcd(d,e)^2}{de},
\qquad
m_d^{(H)}:=A\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Hd\right\rfloor\right),
\]

\[
E_H:=(m^{(H)})^TK_Hm^{(H)},
\qquad
R_H:=\frac{A(H)^2}{E_H},
\qquad
Z:=\zeta(2),
\tag{1}
\]

with `R_H:=0` when the horizon vector vanishes.

Fix **any finite set of primes** `P_0`. Then there exists an infinite set of primes

\[
P_0\subset S
\tag{2}
\]

and one infinite increment sequence `a=(a_n)` such that

\[
a_1=1,
\qquad
a_n=0\quad(n\text{ nonsquarefree}),
\qquad
a_n\in\{-1,+1\}\quad(n\text{ squarefree}),
\tag{3}
\]

and, simultaneously for every `p in S`,

\[
\boxed{
a_{pn}=-a_n
\qquad\text{whenever }p\nmid n.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Writing `A(x)=sum_(n<=x) a_n`, the same source can be chosen so that

\[
\boxed{
A(x)=O_\varepsilon\!\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
\quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0
}
\tag{5}
\]

and there is an unbounded sequence of recursively chosen horizons `H_j` for which

\[
\boxed{
R_{H_j}\longrightarrow Z.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the residual obstruction after `FD-027` is not merely "infinitely many prime relations". A source-side coercive theorem must use prime ancestry with **quantitative breadth at the active scale**, or a genuinely global multiplicative/divisor law that destroys the remaining core sign capacity. The construction below is deliberately sparse and does not approach full Möbius multiplicativity.

## 1. Choose an infinite prime family whose semigroup is subcritical

Let

\[
c_0:=\frac1Z\prod_{p\in P_0}\frac{p}{p+1}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

This is the density constant for squarefree integers coprime to the fixed product of the primes in `P_0`, as derived in `FD-027`.

Choose distinct primes `r_1<r_2<...`, all outside `P_0`, recursively so large that

\[
r_j>\exp\!\bigl(\exp(j^2+10)\bigr),
\qquad
\frac1{r_j}<\frac{c_0}{2^{j+3}}.
\tag{8}
\]

The infinitude of the primes is enough to make this choice. Put

\[
T:=\{r_1,r_2,\ldots\},
\qquad
S:=P_0\cup T.
\tag{9}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{p\in T}\frac1p<\frac{c_0}{4},
\qquad
\sum_{p\in T}\frac1{\sqrt p}<\infty,
\tag{10}
\]

and, for large `x`,

\[
\pi_T(x):=\#\{p\in T:p\le x\}
=O(\sqrt{\log\log x}).
\tag{11}
\]

Let `D_S` be the squarefree semigroup generated by `S`, and let `M_S` be the full multiplicative semigroup generated by `S` with arbitrary nonnegative prime exponents. If

\[
D_S(x):=\#\{q\in D_S:q\le x\},
\qquad
M_S(x):=\#\{s\in M_S:s\le x\},
\tag{12}
\]

then the finite set `P_0` contributes only powers of `log x`, while (11) gives

\[
D_S(x)\le \exp(O(\sqrt{\log\log x})),
\]

\[
M_S(x)
\le
\exp(O((\log\log x)^{3/2})).
\tag{13}
\]

In particular,

\[
D_S(x)M_S(x)=\exp(o(\sqrt{\log x})).
\tag{14}
\]

The reciprocal Euler products needed later also converge:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q\in D_S}\frac1{\sqrt q}
=
\prod_{p\in S}(1+p^{-1/2})<\infty,
}
\tag{15}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s\in M_S}\frac1s
=
\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-1})^{-1}<\infty.
}
\tag{16}
\]

These are the two quantitative meanings of "sparse" used by the construction: the ancestry semigroups have sub-`exp(sqrt(log x))` counting growth, while their reciprocal weights are summable.

## 2. Infinite prime ancestry factors through a free core

Define the core squarefree set

\[
\mathcal C_S
:=
\{m\ge1:m\text{ squarefree and }p\nmid m\text{ for every }p\in S\}.
\tag{17}
\]

Choose signs `b_m in {-1,+1}` on `mathcal C_S`, with `b_1=1`, and write

\[
B(x):=\sum_{\substack{m\le x\\m\in\mathcal C_S}}b_m,
\qquad B(0):=0.
\tag{18}
\]

Every squarefree integer has a unique factorization

\[
n=q\,m,
\qquad
q\in D_S,
\quad
m\in\mathcal C_S.
\tag{19}
\]

Set

\[
a_n=
\begin{cases}
\mu(q)b_m,&n=qm\text{ as in (19)},\\
0,&n\text{ nonsquarefree}.
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]

Then (3) holds. Multiplying a squarefree `n` by a prime `p in S` not already dividing it toggles exactly one prime in the `D_S` factor, so (4) holds **for every prime in the infinite set `S` and at every integer**, not merely at retained horizons.

Summing (20) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
A(x)
=
\sum_{\substack{q\in D_S\\q\le x}}
\mu(q)B\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{q}\right\rfloor\right).
}
\tag{21}
\]

At a horizon `H`, put

\[
q_d:=\left\lfloor\frac Hd\right\rfloor.
\tag{22}
\]

Nested floors give

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{q_d}{q}\right\rfloor
=
q_{qd},
\tag{23}
\]

where `q_k:=0` for `k>H`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
A(q_d)
=
\sum_{q\in D_S}
\mu(q)B(q_{qd}).
}
\tag{24}
\]

Only finitely many terms are nonzero for fixed `H,d`.

Equation (24) is the infinite-prime analogue of the finite triangular system in `FD-027`. The new issue is that it has an infinite ancestry tail. The sparsity in Section 1 makes that tail small enough to freeze below the active hyperbola prefix.

## 3. The free core still has uniform capacity on the planted hyperbola intervals

Let `C_(P_0)` denote the squarefree integers coprime to every prime of `P_0`. `FD-027` gives

\[
\#\{m\le x:m\in C_{P_0}\}
=c_0x+O_{P_0}(\sqrt x).
\tag{25}
\]

For a hyperbola interval

\[
I_d:=(q_{d+1},q_d]\cap\mathbb Z,
\qquad
L_d:=|I_d|,
\tag{26}
\]

this implies

\[
\#(C_{P_0}\cap I_d)
=c_0L_d+O_{P_0}(\sqrt{q_d}).
\tag{27}
\]

Passing from `C_(P_0)` to the final core `mathcal C_S` only removes integers divisible by at least one tail prime. By the union bound and (10)--(11),

\[
\#\{n\in I_d:\exists p\in T,\ p\mid n\}
\le
L_d\sum_{p\in T}\frac1p+\pi_T(q_d)
<
\frac{c_0}{4}L_d+\pi_T(q_d).
\tag{28}
\]

Therefore

\[
\#(\mathcal C_S\cap I_d)
\ge
\frac{3c_0}{4}L_d
-O_{P_0}(\sqrt{q_d})
-\pi_T(q_d).
\tag{29}
\]

Take

\[
R:=\lfloor H^{1/5}\rfloor.
\tag{30}
\]

Uniformly for `1<=d<=R`,

\[
L_d\asymp\frac{H}{d^2},
\qquad
\frac{\sqrt{q_d}}{L_d}
\ll\frac{d^{3/2}}{\sqrt H}
\le H^{-1/5},
\tag{31}
\]

while `pi_T(q_d)/L_d=o(1)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\#(\mathcal C_S\cap I_d)
\gg_{P_0}\frac{H}{d^2}
\qquad(1\le d\le R)
}
\tag{32}
\]

for sufficiently large `H`.

There is also the exact parity constraint

\[
B(x)
\equiv
\#\{m\le x:m\in\mathcal C_S\}
\pmod2.
\tag{33}
\]

As in `FD-026`/`FD-027`, if an interval contains `N` core points, every endpoint change `Delta` satisfying `|Delta|<=N` and `Delta congruent N (mod 2)` is realized exactly by assigning the appropriate numbers of `+1` and `-1` core signs; the ordering may be chosen with at most unit overshoot between the prescribed endpoints.

## 4. Freeze the infinite ancestry tail and invert the active prefix

Let

\[
\nu^{(R)}:=K_R^{-1}e_1,
\qquad
C_R:=\nu_1^{(R)}.
\tag{34}
\]

The canonical GCD-duality results give

\[
C_R\to Z,
\qquad
|\nu_d^{(R)}|\le\frac{Z^2}{d}.
\tag{35}
\]

Suppose core signs have already been chosen through `H_*`, and let

\[
B_*:=\max_{n\le H_*}|B(n)|.
\tag{36}
\]

At stage `j`, choose `H` so large that, with

\[
R=\lfloor H^{1/5}\rfloor,
\qquad
L(H):=\exp(\sqrt{\log H}),
\qquad
t:=\sqrt H\,L(H),
\tag{37}
\]

all of the following hold:

\[
q_{R+1}>H_*,
\tag{38}
\]

\[
\frac{(B_*+2)D_S(H)}{L(H)}\le\frac1j,
\tag{39}
\]

and

\[
(B_*+2)D_S(H)M_S(R)\le H^{1/4}.
\tag{40}
\]

Such an `H` exists because `B_*` is fixed during the stage and (14) is `exp(o(sqrt(log H)))`, whereas `L(H)=exp(sqrt(log H))` and every positive power of `H` grows still faster.

First assign the fresh core signs on `(H_*,q_(R+1)]` in balanced order. Then

\[
|B(x)|\le B_*+2
\qquad(x\le q_{R+1})
\tag{41}
\]

after enlarging the harmless initial constant once.

For `1<=d<=R`, split (24) into the active part and the already frozen lower tail:

\[
w_d
:=
\sum_{\substack{q\in D_S\\qd>R}}
\mu(q)B(q_{qd}).
\tag{42}
\]

Every sample in (42) is at most `q_(R+1)`, so

\[
|w_d|
\le
(B_*+2)D_S(H)
=:W_H.
\tag{43}
\]

Choose an integer `y_d` of the forced all-squarefree parity such that

\[
y_d\equiv \mathsf Q(q_d)\pmod2,
\qquad
|y_d-t\nu_d^{(R)}|\le1,
\tag{44}
\]

where `mathsf Q(x)` counts all squarefree integers up to `x`.

Now define the desired core endpoints `z_d:=B(q_d)` recursively for `d=R,R-1,...,1` by

\[
\boxed{
z_d
=
y_d-w_d
-
\sum_{\substack{q\in D_S\\q>1,\ qd\le R}}
\mu(q)z_{qd}.
}
\tag{45}
\]

Every index `qd` on the right is larger than `d`, so the recurrence is triangular. Combining (42) and (45) gives exactly

\[
\boxed{A(q_d)=y_d\qquad(1\le d\le R).}
\tag{46}
\]

The parity closes exactly as well. Unique factorization (19) gives

\[
\mathsf Q(x)
=
\sum_{q\in D_S}
\#\left\{m\le\left\lfloor\frac{x}{q}\right\rfloor:m\in\mathcal C_S\right\}.
\tag{47}
\]

Reducing (45) modulo `2`, using `mu(q) congruent 1 (mod 2)`, (33), and the already assigned tail in (42), yields inductively

\[
\boxed{
z_d
\equiv
\#\{m\le q_d:m\in\mathcal C_S\}
\pmod2.
}
\tag{48}
\]

Thus the triangular solution has exactly the parity of actual core sign assignments.

## 5. The semigroup inverse keeps the required endpoint jumps below capacity

On the finite index set `1<=d<=R`, let `(T_p z)_d=z_(pd)` when `pd<=R` and `0` otherwise. The active operator in (45) is

\[
\prod_{p\in S}(I-T_p),
\tag{49}
\]

where only finitely many factors act nontrivially on any coordinate. Its inverse is the finite truncation of

\[
\prod_{p\in S}(I-T_p)^{-1}
=
\sum_{s\in M_S}T_s.
\tag{50}
\]

Hence, with `epsilon_d:=y_d-t nu_d^(R)`, equations (43)--(45) imply

\[
|z_d|
\le
\frac{tZ^2}{d}
\sum_{s\in M_S}\frac1s
+
(1+W_H)M_S(R).
\tag{51}
\]

By (16) and (40),

\[
\boxed{
|z_d|
\ll_S\frac{t}{d}+H^{1/4}
\qquad(1\le d\le R).
}
\tag{52}
\]

For `d<R`, this gives

\[
|z_d-z_{d+1}|
\ll_S\frac{t}{d}+H^{1/4}.
\tag{53}
\]

For `d=R`, the lower endpoint `B(q_(R+1))` is bounded by `B_*+2<=H^(1/4)` by (40), so the same estimate holds with `z_(R+1)` replaced by the actual already assigned endpoint.

Compare (53) with the core capacity (32):

\[
\frac{t/d}{H/d^2}
=
L(H)\frac{d}{\sqrt H}
\le
L(H)H^{-3/10}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{54}
\]

and

\[
\frac{H^{1/4}}{H/d^2}
\le H^{-7/20}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{55}
\]

Therefore every prescribed endpoint jump is eventually smaller than the number of available core signs in its interval, with the correct parity from (48). Assigning those signs interval by interval realizes all `z_d` exactly and changes no previously fixed core sign. Repeating the stages defines one infinite source.

## 6. The GCD energy still approaches its equality ray

At the chosen horizon `H`, pad `nu^(R)` by zero coordinates through `H`. By (44) and (46),

\[
m^{(H)}=t\widetilde\nu+e,
\tag{56}
\]

with

\[
|e_d|\le1\qquad(d\le R).
\tag{57}
\]

The canonical estimate `1^T K_R 1 << R` gives

\[
\|e_{\le R}\|_{K_H}=O(\sqrt R).
\tag{58}
\]

For `d>R`, the sampled source point is at most `q_(R+1)`. From (21) and (41),

\[
|A(x)|
\le
(B_*+2)D_S(H)
=W_H
\qquad(x\le q_{R+1}).
\tag{59}
\]

Using the full-vector estimate `1^T K_H1 << H` already used in `FD-026`,

\[
\|e_{>R}\|_{K_H}
\ll W_H\sqrt H.
\tag{60}
\]

Since `t=sqrt(H)L(H)`, (39), (58), and (60) give

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|e\|_{K_H}}t
\ll
\frac{\sqrt R}{\sqrt H L(H)}
+
\frac{W_H}{L(H)}
=o(1)+O(1/j).
}
\tag{61}
\]

The top-left `R x R` block of `K_H` is `K_R`, so

\[
\|\widetilde\nu\|_{K_H}^2=C_R,
\qquad
A(H)=tC_R+O(1).
\tag{62}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz in the `K_H` norm now yields

\[
E_H=t^2(C_R+o(1)),
\qquad
R_H=C_R+o(1).
\tag{63}
\]

As `R=H^(1/5)->infinity`, `C_R->Z`, proving (6).

## 7. The same source obeys every `1/2+epsilon` envelope

The construction controls the auxiliary core cumulative function before transferring the bound to `A`.

On `I_d` with `d<=R`, equation (52) and the bounded-overshoot sign ordering give

\[
|B(n)|
\ll_S
\frac{\sqrt H L(H)}d+H^{1/4}+1.
\tag{64}
\]

There `n asymp H/d`, and `d<=H^(1/5)` implies `n gg H^(4/5)`. Hence

\[
\frac{\sqrt H}{d}\ll\sqrt n,
\qquad
L(H)=e^{\sqrt{\log H}}
\le e^{2\sqrt{\log n}}
\tag{65}
\]

for all sufficiently large stages, while `H^(1/4)<<sqrt n`. On the lower fresh interval `(H_*,q_(R+1)]`, balanced signs preserve the preceding maximum; the induction hypothesis then supplies the same monotone envelope. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|B(n)|
\ll_S
\sqrt n\,e^{2\sqrt{\log n}}.
}
\tag{66}
\]

Finally (21) and the convergent square-root Euler product (15) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
|A(n)|
&\le
\sum_{\substack{q\in D_S\\q\le n}}
\left|B\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac nq\right\rfloor\right)\right|\\
&\ll_S
\sqrt n\,e^{2\sqrt{\log n}}
\sum_{q\in D_S}q^{-1/2}\\
&\ll_S
\sqrt n\,e^{2\sqrt{\log n}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{67}
\]

Since `exp(2 sqrt(log n))=O_epsilon(n^epsilon)` for every `epsilon>0`, equation (5) follows.

## 8. Prior-art boundary and adversarial checks

The prime-set decomposition itself is classical territory. Terence Tao, *A remark on partial sums involving the Möbius function*, Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. **81** (2010), 343--349, DOI `10.1017/S0004972709000884`, studies Möbius sums on multiplicative semigroups generated by arbitrary finite or infinite sets of primes. Marco Aymone, *A note on multiplicative functions resembling the Möbius function*, J. Number Theory **212** (2020), 113--121, studies genuinely multiplicative squarefree-supported `+-1` functions with small partial sums; related work such as Aymone's 2022 Mathematika paper imposes complete multiplicativity on the sign factor. These are neighboring prior-art boundaries, not inputs to the proof above.

No novelty is claimed for decomposing a squarefree integer into its `S`-part and an `S`-free part, for the Euler products (15)--(16), or for partial multiplicativity itself. The Mathia-specific delta is the exact negative statement that an **infinite but sufficiently sparse** family of global Möbius prime-direction identities can still be absorbed by the Farey--Mertens GCD equality-ray interpolation while retaining a single source and all `1/2+epsilon` cumulative envelopes. The derivation uses only elementary counting plus the already-canonical GCD estimates, so no new load-bearing literature anchor is needed in `SOURCES.md`.

The main adversarial pressure points are explicit:

- The infinite sum in (21) is finite at every fixed argument. No convergence is assumed to define `A(x)`.
- The active recurrence (45) is genuinely finite and triangular; all terms with `qd>R` are frozen in `w_d` before any top-interval endpoint is chosen.
- The inverse (50) uses arbitrary powers of primes even though the forward operator uses squarefree `S`-products. Its reciprocal mass is finite by (16), while its finite counting growth is controlled by (13)--(14).
- Parity is not approximated: (47)--(48) is the exact partition of all squarefree integers into `S`-part times core, so every real-looking equality-ray endpoint is rounded to a realizable integer endpoint.
- The core does not disappear. The fixed set `P_0` first leaves density `c_0`; the added infinite tail removes at most `c_0/4` of an interval at the crude union-bound level, and the remaining endpoint error is negligible on all `d<=H^(1/5)` hyperbola intervals.
- The inherited GCD tail is included through (59)--(61). Its extra factor `D_S(H)` is precisely why the prime tail was chosen sparse enough that `D_S(H)=exp(o(sqrt(log H)))`.

A decisive falsification would therefore have to exhibit an error in the exact factorization/parity identities (19)--(24),(47)--(48), failure of the semigroup inverse bound (51), loss of core interval capacity in (29)--(32), or a non-negligible inherited `K_H` perturbation contradicting (61).

## 9. Consequence for the live Farey-discrepancy route

`FD-027` ruled out every **fixed finite** prime ancestry packet as the missing source rigidity. `FD-028` rules out a strictly larger class: even an infinite list of exact prime relations, containing any finite packet chosen in advance, can remain too thin to affect the sharp sparse-horizon GCD obstruction.

The surviving source-side question is therefore quantitative rather than cardinal:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How much prime ancestry must be visible at scale }H
\text{ before the equality-ray interpolation loses capacity?}
}
\tag{68}
\]

The present proof deliberately fails when the enforced prime family becomes dense enough that the free core is no longer locally abundant, when the reciprocal semigroup mass ceases to be summable, or when the ancestry-semigroup counting factor is no longer negligible against the `exp(sqrt(log H))` diagonal slack. Full Möbius multiplicativity lies far beyond those hypotheses and is not modeled by this construction.

No RH estimate follows. The result sharpens the negative control: **"infinitely many exact Möbius prime identities" is not itself a usable source invariant; any successful continuation must quantify their scale-dependent breadth or exploit a global compatibility law that cannot be hidden in a sparse prime semigroup.**
