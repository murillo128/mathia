# FD-166 — Unbounded exact Farey horizons rigidify a permanent source

- **Date:** 2026-09-17
- **Status:** proved
- **Task:** Farey discrepancy
- **Research line:** `farey_discrepancy`
- **Kind:** exact cross-horizon rigidity / permanent-source closure / information-boundary refinement
- **Depends on:** FD-117, FD-164, FD-165
- **Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + PERMANENT-SOURCE-RIGIDITY + REPRESENTATION-BOUNDARY`

## Claim

`FD-164` and `FD-165` show that at every finite cutoff one can build many non-Möbius squarefree-unit controls whose complete Farey fields agree exactly with the physical fields. Their controls, however, are allowed to depend on the outer cutoff. Once one asks for **one permanent source on the positive integers**, exact cross-horizon consistency is already rigid in the strongest possible sense.

Let

\[
a:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R,
\qquad
A(x):=\sum_{n\le x}a(n),
\tag{1}
\]

be any fixed source; no squarefree, sign, boundedness or multiplicativity assumption is required. Let `D_{H,A}` denote the synthetic complete Farey discrepancy field obtained from the `FD-117` source map at horizon `H`, and let `D_H` be the physical field generated by the Möbius staircase `M`.

Then equality at **one** horizon already forces an exact coefficient prefix:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_{H,A}=\mathcal D_H
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A(r)=M(r)\ \ (1\le r\le\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a(r)=\mu(r)\ \ (1\le r\le\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, if `mathcal H subseteq N` is any unbounded set of horizons and

\[
\mathcal D_{H,A}=\mathcal D_H
\qquad(H\in\mathcal H),
\tag{3}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{a(n)=\mu(n)\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.}
\tag{4}
\]

No density, nesting or growth condition on `mathcal H` is needed. The horizons may be arbitrarily sparse or superlacunary.

Equivalently, if `n_0` is the first coefficient at which a permanent source differs from Möbius,

\[
n_0:=\min\{n:a(n)\ne\mu(n)\},
\tag{5}
\]

then **every** exact complete-field match must satisfy

\[
\boxed{H<n_0^2.}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus a permanent non-Möbius source can agree with the physical complete Farey field at only a bounded set of horizons.

## 1. One complete field recovers a square-root source prefix

By `FD-117`, equality of the complete Farey fields at horizon `H` is equivalent to equality of the cumulative source on the floor-quotient staircase

\[
\mathcal Q_H
=
\left\{\left\lfloor\frac Hm\right\rfloor:1\le m\le H\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal D_{H,A}=\mathcal D_H
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A(r)=M(r)
\qquad(r\in\mathcal Q_H).
\tag{8}
\]

The elementary quotient-set decomposition already used in `FD-164` is

\[
\mathcal Q_H
=
\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor\}
\cup
\left\{\left\lfloor\frac Hj\right\rfloor:
1\le j\le\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Only the first inclusion matters here:

\[
\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor\}
\subseteq\mathcal Q_H.
\tag{10}
\]

Therefore (8) gives `A(r)=M(r)` at **every consecutive integer** through `floor(sqrt H)`. With `A(0)=M(0)=0`, differencing consecutive cumulative values yields

\[
a(r)
=A(r)-A(r-1)
=M(r)-M(r-1)
=\mu(r)
\tag{11}
\]

through the same prefix. This proves (2).

## 2. Any unbounded horizon hierarchy exhausts every finite coefficient

Assume (3) for an unbounded horizon set `mathcal H`. Fix an arbitrary integer `n`. Since `mathcal H` is unbounded, choose `H in mathcal H` with

\[
H\ge n^2.
\tag{12}
\]

Then `n<=floor(sqrt H)`, so the one-horizon prefix rigidity (2) gives

\[
a(n)=\mu(n).
\tag{13}
\]

As `n` was arbitrary, (4) follows. Notice that neither coherence between adjacent horizons nor a lower bound on the number of observed horizons enters the proof. One sufficiently large exact field settles each fixed finite coordinate, and an unbounded sequence eventually settles them all.

The detection-radius formulation (6) is the same argument read contrapositively. If `a(n_0) != mu(n_0)`, then any horizon `H>=n_0^2` exposes `n_0` inside the consecutive part of `mathcal Q_H`, so exact full-field equality is impossible.

## 3. Why this does not contradict the dense kernels of FD-164 and FD-165

The distinction is **finite-cutoff ambiguity versus permanent-source ambiguity**. `FD-164` constructs a source `a_T` separately for one horizon. `FD-165` constructs one source common to a whole finite or polylogarithmic bundle, but that common source may still depend on `T`. In fact its construction deliberately leaves a fixed macroscopic prefix unchanged and moves the altered orientations farther out as `T` grows.

Equation (2) shows that this escape to infinity is not incidental: it is necessary. If a sequence of exact matched controls `a_T` survives to arbitrarily large horizons, then for every fixed `n` all sufficiently large controls must satisfy

\[
a_T(n)=\mu(n).
\tag{14}
\]

So every pointwise-stable diagonal limit is Möbius even though each finite cutoff may contain a positive-density or nearly full-density set of altered high coefficients. The large finite-cutoff orientation kernel of `FD-164`--`FD-165` is therefore **non-compact in source coordinates**: its freedom drifts outward and cannot produce a distinct permanent exact source.

This closes the exact version of the cross-horizon consistency question left explicit in `FD-165`. There is no critical horizon density to discover for exact complete fields: every unbounded hierarchy is already enough.

## 4. Self-adversarial and representation audit

The theorem is intentionally exact. It says nothing quantitative if two complete fields are merely close in `L^2`, `L^infty`, Fourier norm or at finitely many sample points. `FD-117` is an algebraic inversion; the conditioning of that inversion under approximate data is a separate problem. Nor does the result address incomplete or coarsened Farey observations that fail to recover the whole quotient staircase.

No coefficient-class hypothesis is being smuggled into the argument. The proof works for an arbitrary real-valued source because cumulative equality at consecutive integers directly recovers the coefficients. Conversely, if only a nonconsecutive set of cumulative values were exposed, the balanced-block ambiguity of `FD-164` would return.

The rigidity is also not specifically Möbius after the representation has been reached. Abstractly, **any transform that exactly exposes cumulative samples on sets containing prefixes of length tending to infinity uniquely determines a permanent source across an unbounded hierarchy**. What is Farey-specific is the `FD-117` identification of the complete discrepancy field with the floor-quotient Mertens staircase; what is arithmetic is the elementary square-root prefix hidden inside every quotient set. No deeper source-native Möbius cancellation enters.

A hostile check therefore leaves the claim intact but narrows its significance: this is an exact information-closure theorem, not an RH estimate and not yet the sought source-native cancellation bridge.

## 5. Research consequence

The permanent-source branch of the exact cross-horizon frontier is now closed. The meaningful next questions are quantitative rather than combinatorial: determine how error in a complete Farey field propagates through the `FD-117` inversion to the growing prefix `1<=n<=sqrt H`, and determine whether substantially less than the complete field still forces enough stable coefficient information to interact with the Franel--Landau norm.

A second live route remains the source-native one already isolated by the Farey-gap and ordered-neighbor investigations: exploit monotone Farey geometry or multiplicative/divisor coherence in a way that cannot be represented merely as exact recovery of more cumulative source samples.
