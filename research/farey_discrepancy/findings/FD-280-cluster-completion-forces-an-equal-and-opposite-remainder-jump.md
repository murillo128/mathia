# FD-280 — Cluster completion forces an equal-and-opposite remainder jump

- **Date:** 2026-09-22
- **Status:** proved exact truncation-transfer identity and route correction
- **Task:** Farey discrepancy
- **Research line:** `farey_discrepancy`
- **Kind:** explicit-formula truncation / cluster completion / remainder transfer / divisor-replicated repair / route pruning
- **Depends on:** FD-267, FD-274, FD-279
- **Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + TRUNCATION-TRANSFER-IDENTITY + CLUSTER-BOUNDARY-REMAINDER-JUMP + REPAIR-KERNEL-TRANSFER + ROUTE-CORRECTION`

## Claim

FD-279 shows that a hard zero cutoff which splits one reciprocal-log cluster can be moved by only

\[
T^+-T=O\!\left(\frac1{\log\log x}\right)
\]

so that the cluster is complete, without changing the spectral-height exponent. Its remaining question was whether the nonzero explicit-formula remainder is stable under that tiny displacement.

For a **hard residue truncation**, small displacement by itself cannot give such a stability theorem. The reason is exact and independent of any zeta estimate: whenever an exact source is decomposed into a truncated residue packet plus a remainder, every residue added to the packet is removed from the remainder with exactly the opposite sign.

Let `A(y)` be any fixed target source, let `C(y)` collect terms independent of the spectral cutoff, and let

\[
P_T(y)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T} R_\gamma(y)
\]

be any hard finite residue packet. Define its exact complementary remainder by

\[
A(y)=C(y)+P_T(y)+E_T(y).
\tag{1}
\]

Then for every `T_0<T_1` avoiding zero ordinates at the endpoints,

\[
\boxed{
E_{T_1}(y)-E_{T_0}(y)
=-\bigl(P_{T_1}(y)-P_{T_0}(y)\bigr).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the remainder jump is **exactly the negative of the newly admitted zero packet**. There is no factor depending on `T_1-T_0`.

The same identity survives the consecutive-difference and divisor-replication operations used by the canonical Farey repair. Put

\[
\Delta_N E_T(q)
:=E_T(Nq)-E_T(N(q-1))
\]

and

\[
\mathcal R_{T,N}(d)
:=\sum_{q\mid d}\Delta_N E_T(q).
\tag{3}
\]

If

\[
P_{(T_0,T_1]}:=P_{T_1}-P_{T_0}
\]

and `G_{(T_0,T_1],N}` denotes the FD-267 divisor-replicated field generated by that added packet, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{T_1,N}(d)-\mathcal R_{T_0,N}(d)
=-G_{(T_0,T_1],N}(d)
}
\tag{4}
\]

for every depth coordinate `d`. Consequently, for every finite depth band `I`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d\in I}
\left|
\mathcal R_{T_1,N}(d)-\mathcal R_{T_0,N}(d)
\right|
=
\sum_{d\in I}|G_{(T_0,T_1],N}(d)|.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Equations (2)--(5) show that **hard-cutoff remainder continuity is equivalent to smallness of the boundary packet itself**. The geometric fact that the cutoff moves only `o(1)` in ordinate cannot make the remainder change small.

This is especially important for the FD-279 completion move. A partial close cluster is exactly the situation where the newly added residues can carry inverse-gap factors before the cluster is recombined. Hence the small completion distance and a small remainder jump are logically unrelated. The correct post-FD-279 target is not a Lipschitz-type estimate comparing `E_T` with `E_{T^+}`. It is a **direct estimate of the remainder at a cluster-complete cutoff** (or an equivalent contour formulation in which the packet and remainder are bounded together without separating an ill-conditioned partial cluster).

## Evidence

### 1. Truncation transfer is an algebraic identity

Writing (1) at two cutoffs gives

\[
A-C=P_{T_0}+E_{T_0}=P_{T_1}+E_{T_1}.
\]

Subtracting proves (2). No information about zero spacing, RH, simplicity, or the analytic construction of `E_T` is needed. Those hypotheses matter only when one tries to estimate the two pieces separately.

Apply the linear sampling difference

\[
(\Delta_N f)(q)=f(Nq)-f(N(q-1))
\]

to (2), then apply the divisor transform

\[
(\mathbf 1*h)(d)=\sum_{q\mid d}h(q).
\]

Linearity gives (4). Equation (5) is then equality of the `l^1` norms of two vectors that are negatives of one another coordinatewise.

This is exactly the same remainder channel isolated in FD-267. There, if `A_N=F_N+E_N`, the replicated source splits as the finite-packet field plus `\mathbf 1*\Delta E_N`. FD-280 adds the missing truncation relation: when `F_N` itself changes because the hard spectral cutoff changes, the corresponding `E_N` is not an independent slowly varying error term. It must absorb the opposite packet increment.

### 2. A close pair makes the failure of cutoff continuity explicit

Take the two-zero factorization used in FD-274 and FD-279,

\[
\zeta(s)=(s-\rho_-)(s-\rho_+)H(s),
\qquad
\delta:=\rho_+-\rho_-.
\tag{6}
\]

For any analytic amplitude `B`, the two individual residues are

\[
r_-
=
\frac{B(\rho_-)}
{(\rho_--\rho_+)H(\rho_-)},
\qquad
r_+
=
\frac{B(\rho_+)}
{(\rho_+-\rho_-)H(\rho_+)}.
\tag{7}
\]

If a hard cutoff lies between the two ordinates, moving it just beyond the second zero adds exactly `r_+`. Therefore (2) gives

\[
\boxed{
E_{T^+}-E_T=-r_+.
}
\tag{8}
\]

When `B/H` stays nonzero and regular while `|\delta|` is small, the newly added residue has the familiar scale

\[
|r_+|\asymp |\delta|^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

At the same time the **completed** pair is regular:

\[
r_-+r_+
=
[\rho_-,\rho_+]
\left(\frac BH\right),
\tag{10}
\]

which is the FD-274 divided-difference cancellation and has a finite coalescing limit when `B/H` is `C^1`.

So the paradox is only apparent. As the cutoff crosses the second zero, the packet can change by `O(1/|\delta|)` and the complementary remainder changes by the opposite `O(1/|\delta|)`, while their sum remains the same fixed source. Completing the cluster improves the conditioning of the **packet representation**, but there is no reason for the separately defined remainder to move continuously during that re-partition.

This stress test is structural. It does not assert that the Riemann zeta function supplies arbitrarily small gaps in the particular asymptotic family needed by the Farey argument. It proves that no bound tending to zero with `T^+-T` can follow from cutoff displacement alone.

### 3. The divisor-replicated repair inherits the same jump

For the packet amplitude used in FD-274,

\[
B_{N,d}(s)
=
N^{s-1/2}\frac{K_s(d)}s,
\qquad
K_s(d)=
\sum_{q\mid d}
\bigl(q^s-(q-1)^s\bigr),
\tag{11}
\]

the contribution of one simple critical zero is precisely the normalized repair coordinate before taking the real part. If `T` splits a close component and `T^+` completes it, (4) says that the replicated explicit-formula remainder acquires the negative of all newly added repair coordinates.

Hence a proposed estimate of the form

\[
\sum_{x<d\le2x}
|\mathcal R_{T^+,N}(d)-\mathcal R_{T,N}(d)|
\le
\varepsilon_x\,x^{2-\beta},
\qquad
\varepsilon_x\to0,
\tag{12}
\]

cannot be obtained merely from

\[
T^+-T=O(1/\log\log x).
\]

By (5), (12) is exactly the assertion that the added boundary packet is below threshold in the same replicated norm. If the hard-cut partial cluster is the ill-conditioned object one was trying to eliminate, using (12) to justify its elimination simply moves the same problem into the remainder.

This does **not** invalidate the complete-cluster bounds of FD-274--FD-279. Those estimates concern the packet after all members of the component have been summed, where the internal gap singularities cancel. The present identity says only that the bookkeeping remainder before and after completion need not be close.

## Stress tests

The first stress test is a completely regular two-pole toy model. Let

\[
H\equiv1,
\qquad
B\equiv1,
\qquad
\rho_+=\rho_-+\delta.
\]

Then

\[
r_-=-\delta^{-1},
\qquad
r_+=\delta^{-1},
\qquad
r_-+r_+=0.
\]

A cutoff between the poles has a packet of size `|\delta|^{-1}`. Moving the cutoff by only `O(|\delta|)` makes the completed packet exactly zero, but the exact complementary remainder jumps by `|\delta|^{-1}`. Thus smaller displacement can correspond to larger remainder change.

The second stress test replaces `B=1` by `B(s)=s`. Then the completed pair is exactly `1` while each separate residue still has inverse-gap size. The phenomenon is therefore not an artifact of a vanishing completed packet.

The third stress test is smoothing. A smoothly weighted zero sum need not have the discontinuous transfer law of a hard indicator `1_{\gamma\le T}`; differentiating the weight can spread the transfer over a spectral window. That is a genuinely different truncation architecture. FD-280 does not rule it out, but any such replacement must re-establish the cluster-complete packet bounds and the source/remainder bridge for the new weight rather than importing hard-cutoff conclusions unchanged.

The fourth stress test is the direct complete-cutoff strategy. Equations (2)--(5) place no lower bound on `E_{T^+}` itself. It may be small even when `E_{T^+}-E_T` is huge, because `E_T` may contain the compensating partial-cluster singularity. Therefore this finding does not obstruct a direct contour estimate at `T^+`; it identifies that estimate as the non-circular target.

## Prior-art check

The residue bookkeeping behind (2) is classical contour algebra and is not claimed as a new analytic-number-theory theorem. Lee--Leong's current Mertens treatment, already anchored in `SOURCES.md`, starts from truncated Perron, moves the contour, compares against a short sum over zeta zeros, and bounds the difference using reciprocal-zeta estimates. That literature supports the distinction used here: the error is controlled for a chosen contour/truncation, rather than by a general continuity principle in the truncation height.

A fresh search checked hard zero truncations, Mertens explicit formulae, moving Perron contours, and cutoff-dependent remainders. It found the standard contour/residue framework and discussions of convergence/ordering of the Mertens zero sum, but no result asserting a small hard-cutoff remainder change solely from a small change in spectral height. No external theorem is load-bearing for (2)--(5), so no new source anchor is required.

## Implications

FD-279 closes the **packet boundary geometry** loophole: in the relevant low-polynomial regime an analyst can move past the whole cut reciprocal-log component without paying a spectral power. FD-280 shows that this must not be reinterpreted as continuity of the complementary remainder. Cluster completion is a re-partition of the exact source, and the remainder takes the equal-and-opposite jump.

Therefore the next full-source theorem should be formulated at a cluster-complete cutoff from the start. Two non-circular forms are available:

1. choose a cluster-complete `T_*` and bound the explicit-formula remainder `E_{T_*}(Nq)` directly, strongly enough that FD-267's remainder ledger remains below the positive-capacity threshold after consecutive differencing and divisor replication; or
2. keep the contour contribution and the boundary zero cluster grouped as one analytic object and bound that grouped object before splitting it into packet and remainder pieces.

A theorem comparing `E_T` and `E_{T^+}` can still be useful if it also proves smallness of the newly added cluster field by independent means. But then the smallness comes from that additional mathematics, not from the `O(1/\log\log x)` cutoff displacement.

This narrows the spectral frontier without deciding it. The hard-boundary artifact itself remains removable by FD-279, but the final bridge to the physical Mertens source now requires **direct cluster-complete remainder control**, not remainder continuity.

## Limitations

The exact transfer identity assumes only that `E_T` is defined as the complementary term in an exact hard-truncated decomposition. A separately normalized or smoothed remainder may obey a different comparison law, but then it is a different decomposition and must be analyzed on its own terms.

The close-pair stress test demonstrates failure of a generic continuity inference; it is not an existence theorem for an asymptotic family of extremely close Riemann zeros. No new zero-spacing hypothesis is introduced.

Finally, FD-280 gives no quantitative upper bound for the remainder at a cluster-complete cutoff. It removes one misleading route to such a bound and identifies the direct quantity that still has to be controlled.
