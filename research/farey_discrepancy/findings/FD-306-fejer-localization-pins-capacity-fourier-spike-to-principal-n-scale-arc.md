## Why this is new relative to FD-303--FD-305

FD-303 proved that near-capacity aligned block energy thickens into a large complete sliding second moment, and then extracted many positive ordinary two-point correlations. FD-304 used RH to move a large family of those correlations to one common interval. FD-305 compressed that common-window family with the identity

\[
\int_0^1|F_X(\alpha)|^4d\alpha
=
\sum_h|R_h(X)|^2,
\tag{20}
\]

but the squaring in (20) discards where the correlations originated inside the short-interval geometry. Its resulting `alpha_*` is therefore unrestricted.

The present finding goes back one step and keeps the **positive sliding-square energy itself**. The triangular coefficients generated by a length-`N` sliding sum are exactly the Fourier coefficients of `|D_N|^2`; equivalently, the relevant weight is the Fejér kernel before normalization. Because `|D_N(alpha)|` decays away from the integer frequency at scale `1/N`, the capacity-forced energy can be localized to a principal `N`-scale arc. This is a stronger representation of the same source obstruction, not a new hypothesis about Möbius randomness.

It also pays a smaller depth threshold. The fixed-window transfer of FD-304 is not needed: after an RH endpoint audit, the original aligned blocks can be thickened inside the compact block `[X,2X)` directly. The pointwise spectral inflation therefore begins at (16), below the FD-304 common-window threshold `3/(10c)=0.7228262519...` and well below the FD-305 fourth-moment threshold.

## Process used here

The pass started from the current `farey_discrepancy` intelligence and the latest FD-303--FD-305 chain. The key question left by FD-305 was whether its source-selected large additive frequency could be localized rather than treated by a completely uniform exponential-sum estimate.

The first candidate was to infer localization directly from the positive FD-304 correlation subfamily. That is not valid: other shifts can contribute negatively to the Fejér-weighted correlation sum, so a subset of positive correlations alone does not lower-bound the signed triangular sum. The argument was therefore rebuilt from the nonnegative sliding-square quantity of FD-303, where cancellation cannot erase the energy. RH is used only to show that deleting the one aligned block whose forward thickening can cross the compact right endpoint loses `o(1)` of the shell energy.

A prior-art check covered Gallagher-type short-interval/Fourier lemmas, Fejér/Cesàro correlation kernels, classical Möbius exponential sums, and recent short-interval Möbius uniformity results. Those works confirm that the short-sum/Fejér duality is classical. No external result from that literature is load-bearing below; the durable content is the source-specific parameter transfer from the Farey near-capacity premise to the explicit shrinking arc and exponents (16)--(19).

## 1. RH makes the one dangerous endpoint block negligible

Write

\[
y_q:=N(q-1),
\qquad
u_q:=|U_N(q)|=\left|\sum_{r=1}^N\mu(y_q+r)\right|.
\tag{21}
\]

Equation (5) gives

\[
\sum_{Q<q\le2Q}\nu_q^2
\ge
QNx^{2c-o(1)}.
\tag{22}
\]

Under RH, Littlewood's Mertens criterion gives, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
M(y)=O_\epsilon(y^{1/2+\epsilon}).
\tag{23}
\]

Since `q~Q<=2x` in a fixed polynomial-depth regime,

\[
\nu_q^2
\le
NQx^{o(1)}.
\tag{24}
\]

Only the terminal index `q=2Q` can have its forward thickening touch or cross the right endpoint of the compact block `[X,2X)`. Removing it costs at most `NQx^{o(1)}`, whereas (22) has size at least `QNx^{2c-o(1)}`. Therefore

\[
\sum_{Q<q\le2Q-1}\nu_q^2
\ge
QNx^{2c-o(1)}.
\tag{25}
\]

The same argument as FD-303 allows the bounded-amplitude indices `nu_q<16` to be discarded as well, because their total square contribution is `O(Q)` while the right side of (25) grows polynomially. Thus all subsequent thickening may be restricted to indices for which `nu_q>=16` and `q<=2Q-1` without changing the power scale.

This endpoint audit is the only new place where RH is required beyond the inherited shell input. Without a pointwise envelope, one exceptional terminal block could in principle carry enough aligned energy that compactification would not be automatic.

## 2. The compact sliding energy is exactly Fejér-weighted Fourier energy

For every retained `q` and every integer

\[
0\le t\le\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_q}{8}\right\rfloor,
\tag{26}
\]

FD-303's `2`-Lipschitz sliding estimate gives

\[
\left|
\sum_{r=1}^N\mu(y_q+t+r)
\right|
\ge
\frac{3\nu_q}{4}.
\tag{27}
\]

Because `nu_q<=N`, these thickening intervals have length at most `N/8` and are disjoint for distinct aligned starts. More importantly, if `q<=2Q-1`, then

\[
y_q+t+N
\le
2X-\frac{7N}{8}
<2X,
\tag{28}
\]

while the smallest retained aligned start is at least `X`. Hence every term in every thickened short sum lies inside the fixed support of `f_X`.

Define the global compact convolution energy

\[
\mathcal E_X(N)
:=
\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}
\left|
\sum_{r=1}^N f_X(y+r)
\right|^2.
\tag{29}
\]

The disjoint thickened intervals from (26)--(27) imply

\[
\mathcal E_X(N)
\gg
\sum_{Q<q\le2Q-1}\nu_q^3.
\tag{30}
\]

Monotonicity of normalized `l^p` norms, together with (25), gives

\[
\sum_{Q<q\le2Q-1}\nu_q^3
\gg
Q\left(
\frac1Q\sum_{Q<q\le2Q-1}\nu_q^2
\right)^{3/2}
\ge
QN^{3/2}x^{3c-o(1)}.
\tag{31}
\]

On the Fourier side,

\[
F_X(\alpha)\overline{D_N(\alpha)}
=
\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}
\left(\sum_{r=1}^N f_X(y+r)\right)e(y\alpha).
\tag{32}
\]

Parseval therefore gives the exact identity

\[
\mathcal E_X(N)
=
\int_0^1|F_X(\alpha)|^2|D_N(\alpha)|^2d\alpha.
\tag{33}
\]

Combining (30)--(33) proves (8). No correlation sign estimate is used: the positivity is already built into the square on the physical side and into `|D_N|^2` on the Fourier side.

## 3. The Dirichlet-kernel tail forces the energy into the principal arc

The elementary kernel bound

\[
|D_N(\alpha)|
=
\left|
\frac{\sin(\pi N\alpha)}{\sin(\pi\alpha)}
\right|
\ll
\min\left\{N,\frac1{\|\alpha\|}\right\}
\tag{34}
\]

implies, outside `mathfrak A_W`,

\[
|D_N(\alpha)|^2
\ll
\frac{N^2}{W^2}.
\tag{35}
\]

Parseval for the compact Möbius block gives

\[
\int_0^1|F_X(\alpha)|^2d\alpha
=
\sum_{X\le n<2X}\mu(n)^2
\le X=NQ.
\tag{36}
\]

Hence

\[
\int_{\mathfrak A_W^c}
|F_X(\alpha)|^2|D_N(\alpha)|^2d\alpha
\ll
\frac{QN^3}{W^2}.
\tag{37}
\]

Relative to the lower bound in (8), this is at most

\[
\frac{N^{3/2}x^{-3c+o(1)}}{W^2}.
\tag{38}
\]

With the choice (9), (38) is `O(x^(-2eta+o(1)))`. Thus the entire off-arc contribution is negligible compared with the **forced lower-bound scale** in (8), which proves (11).

The wording matters here. Equation (11) does not claim that a `1-o(1)` fraction of the unknown total Fejér energy lies in the principal arc; the total could be larger than the lower bound. It says that the principal arc by itself already carries the full capacity-forced scale. That is the statement needed for the route reduction.

Since `|D_N|<=N`, (11) also yields

\[
\int_{\mathfrak A_W}|F_X(\alpha)|^2d\alpha
\ge
Q N^{-1/2}x^{3c-o(1)}.
\tag{39}
\]

The arc has measure `2W/N+o(W/N)`, so

\[
\max_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A_W}|F_X(\alpha)|^2
\ge
Q N^{1/2}x^{3c-o(1)}W^{-1},
\tag{40}
\]

which proves (13)--(14).

## 4. Depth thresholds and balanced calibration

Suppose first that

\[
\lambda<\frac1{2c}=1.2047104198\ldots.
\tag{41}
\]

Then

\[
N^{3/4}x^{-3c/2}=N^{3/4-(3c\lambda/2)+o(1)}
\tag{42}
\]

is a positive power of `N`, and (9) gives

\[
W=N^{3/4}x^{-3c/2+\eta}.
\tag{43}
\]

Substitution into (14) gives (15). In terms of `N`, the exponent of the square-root excess is

\[
-\frac58+\frac{9c\lambda}{4}-\frac{\eta\lambda}{2}+o(1).
\tag{44}
\]

After choosing `eta` arbitrarily small, this is positive exactly when

\[
\lambda>\frac{5}{18c}=0.6692835666\ldots,
\tag{45}
\]

proving (16).

If `lambda>=1/(2c)`, the balancing term in (9) is at most one and we may take `W=x^eta`. Then

\[
\frac{|F_X(\alpha_*)|}{X^{1/2}}
\ge
N^{-1/4}x^{3c/2-\eta/2-o(1)},
\tag{46}
\]

which is automatically a positive power for sufficiently small `eta`. Thus (45) is the global depth threshold for a power-above-square-root principal-arc coefficient within this argument.

At `lambda=1`,

\[
\frac34-\frac{3c}{2}
=0.1274437511\ldots,
\tag{47}
\]

so (17)--(18) follow. The excess exponent from (44) is

\[
\frac{9c}{4}-\frac58
=0.3088343734\ldots,
\tag{48}
\]

which proves (19). This is materially stronger than the `0.1176687467...` exponent of FD-305 because the fourth-moment route discarded the Fejér localization before taking a supremum.

## Proof status

Equations (8)--(19) are proved conditionally on RH and the near-capacity premise (3), with the same fixed polynomial-depth convention used throughout FD-299--FD-305. The Fourier identities, kernel tail bound, thickening argument, and exponent arithmetic are deterministic. RH supplies the pointwise Mertens envelope needed to compactify the thickened shell after removing the one dangerous terminal block.

No new theorem about Möbius exponential sums is assumed. In particular, (13) is a **necessary certificate** for the near-capacity Farey route, not an unconditional claim that such a coefficient actually exists for arbitrary source scales.

## Implications for the Farey route

FD-305 left open the possibility that its additive spike lived at an essentially arbitrary source-selected frequency, forcing any direct attack to use a uniform exponential-sum estimate. FD-306 removes that freedom. Above (16), a near-capacity source must exhibit a power-above-square-root coefficient in a shrinking neighborhood of the integer frequency whose width is explicitly controlled by (9).

This also exposes the next scale mismatch. Under RH, partial summation from `M(t)=O(t^(1/2+epsilon))` only gives schematically

\[
|F_X(\alpha)|
\ll
X^{1/2+\epsilon}(1+X\|\alpha\|).
\tag{49}
\]

On the arc (12), `X\|\alpha\|<=QW`, which can still be a large power. Thus existing RH control at frequency zero does not automatically rule out the localized spike. Classical Davenport-type uniform logarithmic savings are also far above the required square-root scale. The remaining problem is narrower than in FD-305 but still genuine: exploit the fact that the bad frequency lies in the **principal `1/N` neighborhood**, even though that neighborhood is much wider than the natural `1/X` scale of the full block.

A natural next test is therefore to obtain a low-frequency Möbius bound that interpolates between the Mertens point `alpha=0` and the width `W/N`, or to show that a coefficient of the size (13) across that shrinking principal arc would force an even more rigid short-interval Mertens anomaly.

## Adversarial review

The main failure mode considered first was incorrectly summing only the positive FD-304 correlations against triangular Fejér weights. That would be invalid because unselected shifts may be negative and can cancel the selected family. The proof avoids this completely by returning to FD-303's nonnegative sliding-square energy before any correlation extraction.

The compact-support boundary is also essential. FD-303's original sliding moment permits the top aligned block to thicken beyond `2X`, whereas the Fourier block `f_X` does not. Only `q=2Q` has this problem for the one-sided thickening used here. Under RH its square is `O(NQx^(o(1)))`, which is `x^(-2c+o(1))` of the shell-energy lower bound, so deleting it is legitimate. No analogous deletion of a positive proportion of the shell is hidden in (25).

The thickening intervals remain disjoint because their lengths are at most `N/8` while aligned starts are `N` apart. For `q<=2Q-1`, every thickened length-`N` sum lies strictly inside `[X,2X)`, so (32)--(33) are exact rather than an approximate Fourier replacement. The `l^3` amplification is applied only after the low-amplitude and terminal losses have been shown negligible.

The tail estimate uses only the upper bound `int |F_X|^2 <= X`; no unproved cancellation enters. The choice of `W` is deliberately enlarged by `x^eta`, so the off-arc contribution is `o` of the forced scale rather than merely comparable with it. The condition `W=o(N)` can always be met by choosing `eta>0` sufficiently small for fixed `lambda`; no uniformity in unbounded `lambda` is claimed.

Finally, the result does not claim that the whole Fourier energy, or even the whole Fejér energy, concentrates on `mathfrak A_W`; only that this arc carries at least the capacity-forced lower-bound scale. It does not identify a rational approximation beyond the principal arc, does not place `alpha_*` at scale `1/X`, and does not contradict known Möbius exponential-sum estimates. The implication is one-way and remains conditional on the near-capacity source premise.

## Bottom line

Under RH, the near-capacity Farey block field forces more than the unrestricted additive spectral anomaly of FD-305. The aligned short-interval geometry itself yields

\[
\int |F_X|^2|D_N|^2
\ge
QN^{3/2}x^{3c-o(1)},
\]

\[
\lambda>\frac{5}{18c}=0.6692835666\ldots,

The surviving frontier is therefore sharper than after FD-305: one no longer needs to exclude a large Möbius coefficient at an arbitrary additive frequency. It is enough to understand whether a coefficient of the required size can occur in this explicit principal `N`-scale neighborhood, or whether such an event necessarily forces an even stronger short-interval Mertens anomaly.
