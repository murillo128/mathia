## Claim

FD-295 shows that a polylogarithmically short complete zero packet is automatically capacity-subcritical below the current square-root Perron range when its desingularized reciprocal-log cluster backgrounds are tame. That local statement does not by itself control the absolute location of the complete hard remainder path.

There is, however, a lower spectral-height regime in which the **entire hard remainder path across one polynomial dyadic height window has subcritical diameter**. The new input is not another local-packet estimate: it is the exact FD-280 identity connecting hard-remainder differences to complete zero packets, combined with the aggregate population ledger of FD-278.

Assume RH and simple nontrivial zeros. Work at fixed polynomial divisor depth

\[
x=N^{\lambda+o(1)},\qquad \lambda>0,
\tag{1}
\]

and put

\[
\beta:=\log_2\frac32,
\qquad
c:=1-\beta=0.4150374992788438\ldots,

L:=\log(2Nx),
\qquad

\tag{2}

\[
J=[H,2H],
\qquad

\tag{3}

\operatorname{dist}(T,\Gamma_*)\ge \frac1L

Then for every two zero-safe cutoffs `T_0,T_1 in mathcal S_L(J)`, uniformly in their locations inside `J`,

\[
\boxed{

This converts the remaining occupation problem in a genuine low-height regime. Let

In this low-height regime, the FD-292/FD-293 near-linear good-gap population gate is therefore not an independent assumption once one buffered good return exists: it is automatically generated by hard-path rigidity.

The exponent itself is not new: it is the FD-278 aggregate tariff. The new conclusion is its interpretation as a **diameter threshold for the exact complete hard remainder path**, and hence as an occupation amplifier.

## 1. Zero-safe cutoffs make every intervening reciprocal-log cluster complete

Take `T in mathcal S_L(J)` and let `gamma_j<T<gamma_{j+1}` be the neighboring distinct zero ordinates. By definition,

\[
T-\gamma_j\ge\frac1L,
\qquad
\gamma_{j+1}-T\ge\frac1L.
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore

\[
\gamma_{j+1}-\gamma_j\ge\frac2L.
\tag{22}
\]

The FD-276 clustering rule joins consecutive simple critical zeros only across gaps `<2/L`. Hence no reciprocal-log component crosses a zero-safe cutoff. For any `T_0<T_1` in `mathcal S_L(J)`, the zeros with ordinates in `(T_0,T_1]` are therefore a disjoint union of **complete** reciprocal-log components.

This is the exact boundary property needed by the FD-278 aggregate ledger. It also avoids the incomplete-boundary singularity isolated in FD-279 and the false remainder-continuity inference rejected in FD-280: neither cutoff is moved after it is chosen.

## 2. Exact hard-complement telescoping turns FD-278 into a path-diameter bound

FD-280 gives, for arbitrary hard cutoffs `T_0<T_1`,

\[
\mathcal R_{T_1,N}-\mathcal R_{T_0,N}
=
-\mathcal G_{(T_0,T_1],N},
\tag{23}
\]

where the right side is the complete physical Farey image of all nontrivial-zero residue jumps whose ordinates lie in `(T_0,T_1]`.

For zero-safe endpoints, Section 1 partitions this packet into complete reciprocal-log components. Let `R` be their number and `m_*` their largest cardinality. Every component lies below `2H=x^{tau+o(1)}`. Riemann--von Mangoldt therefore gives

\[
R\le N_\zeta(2H)=x^{\tau+o(1)},
\tag{24}
\]

while the RH component-packing estimate of FD-278 gives

\[
m_*
\le
\left(\kappa(\tau,\lambda)+o(1)\right)
\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}.
\tag{25}
\]

FD-276 bounds every normalized complete component by

\[
\sum_{x<d\le2x}
|Q_{\mathcal C}(N,d)|
\ll
x(2L)^{m_{\mathcal C}-1}
\mathfrak B_{\mathcal C}.
\tag{26}
\]

Triangle inequality across all components, deliberately allowing perfect adverse alignment, gives

\[
\|\widetilde{\mathcal G}_{(T_0,T_1],N}\|_{1,x}
\ll
xR(2L)^{m_*-1}\mathfrak B_*.
\tag{27}
\]

Since

\[
\log(2L)=\log\log x+O(1),
\tag{28}
\]

equations (5), (24), and (25) yield

\[
\|\widetilde{\mathcal G}_{(T_0,T_1],N}\|_{1,x}
\le
x^{1+\tau+\kappa(\tau,\lambda)+b+o(1)}.
\tag{29}
\]

Under RH, restoring the common physical critical-line factor `N^(1/2)` gives (9) from (23). Comparing with (10) gives (12).

The argument is exactly an aggregate complete-packet upper bound. It uses no cancellation between components and no stochastic model for zero phases.

## 3. One buffered return fills almost the entire safe part of the window

Let `T_0` satisfy (14). For any `T in mathcal S_L(J)`, the triangle inequality and (12) give

\[
\|\mathcal R_{T,N}\|_{1,x}
\le
(1-\varepsilon)B_{N,x}
+
\mathcal C_{N,x}x^{-\Delta+o(1)}.
\tag{30}
\]

Because `ell(x)=x^(o(1))`,

\[
\frac{
\mathcal C_{N,x}x^{-\Delta+o(1)}
}{B_{N,x}}
=
\ell(x)x^{-\Delta+o(1)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{31}
\]

Thus the second term in (30) is eventually at most `varepsilon B_{N,x}`, proving that every zero-safe cutoff is `B_{N,x}`-good. Without the fixed buffer, the same calculation gives the `(1+o(1))B_{N,x}` statement.

It remains to measure the zero-safe set. Cover every distinct zero ordinate within distance `1/L` of `J` by an interval of length `2/L`. Counting with multiplicity only overestimates the union. Riemann--von Mangoldt gives

\[
N_\zeta(2H+L^{-1})-N_\zeta(H-L^{-1})
=
\left(\frac1{2\pi}+o(1)\right)H\log H.
\tag{32}
\]

Therefore the unsafe measure is at most

\[
\frac2L
\left(\frac1{2\pi}+o(1)\right)H\log H
=
\left(
\frac{\tau}{\pi(1+1/\lambda)}+o(1)
\right)H,
\tag{33}
\]

which proves (15).

In particular, under the buffered return hypothesis,

\[
\Omega_B(J)
\ge
\left(
1-
\frac{\tau}{\pi(1+1/\lambda)}
-o(1)
\right)H.
\tag{34}
\]

This dominates `H/L^P` for every fixed `P>0`. FD-292 then makes the phase-bad set negligible relative to (34), so phase-good, zero-safe, remainder-good cutoffs coexist automatically once the single buffered return is known.

## 4. Relation to FD-293--FD-295

FD-293 proves that an inverse-polylog safe occupation certificate requires `H^(1-o(1))` remainder-good gaps. FD-294 turns such a population into `H^(1-o(1))` short complete packet increments. FD-295 then shows that those short increments are automatically capacity-subcritical throughout the current `tau<1/2` range under tame backgrounds.

The present result runs in the opposite direction at lower height. Below `tau_rig`, the **aggregate packet across the whole dyadic height window** is already subcritical. Hence the hard remainder cannot move by a capacity-sized amount anywhere inside the zero-safe part of that window. If it enters a buffered target ball once, it remains inside the target ball at every zero-safe cutoff. The near-linear return population required by FD-293 is then a consequence rather than a separate input.

This does not contradict the much larger automatic-short-packet height ceiling in FD-295. A short packet has only `x^(o(1))` components and pays no population exponent. A full dyadic-window packet can contain `x^(tau+o(1))` components, so its ledger pays the additional `tau` in (11). The resulting global rigidity threshold is therefore the smaller FD-278 exponent near `0.27`, not the FD-295 local exponent above `0.65` for tame backgrounds.

## 5. Decisive falsification and limits

A counterexample to (12) under its hypotheses would require two zero-safe cutoffs in the same window whose hard remainders differ by capacity scale. By the exact identity (23), the intervening complete zero packet would then itself have capacity scale. But its number of components, largest cluster cardinality, and background envelope obey exactly the FD-278 ledger used in (24)--(29), contradicting the strict inequality (11). This is the decisive internal falsification test.

Several limits are essential:

- **No first good height is produced.** The theorem turns one buffered remainder-good safe cutoff into large occupation; it does not prove that such a cutoff exists.
- **The buffer matters for an unchanged budget.** From one point at exactly `B_{N,x}` the conclusion is only `(1+o(1))B_{N,x}` everywhere. A fixed interior margin, or an asymptotically equivalent enlarged budget, is needed for literal `B_{N,x}`-goodness.
- **The result is conditional.** RH and zero simplicity enter through the complete critical-line cluster formalism and the FD-278 cardinality bound. The background exponent `b` is a genuine hypothesis.
- **The threshold is a method boundary, not a claim of sharpness.** Above `tau_rig`, the aggregate upper bound ceases to be power-subcritical; this does not imply that the hard remainder actually has large diameter.
- **Zero-safe endpoints are load-bearing.** Incomplete packets can recover boundary inverse-gap singularities as in FD-279, so (12) is not asserted for arbitrary hard cutoffs.

The useful new frontier is consequently narrower. In the tame-background regime below approximately `x^0.27` spectral height, proving inverse-polylog remainder-good occupation no longer requires controlling a near-linear population of returns independently. It is enough to produce **one buffered zero-safe return** of the complete physical hard remainder. Above that regime the population/occupation problem remains genuinely aggregate.
