# FD-019 — integer-ratio GCD-duality saturation is classified by squarefree transition escape

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + VARIABLE-DILATION-DICHOTOMY + MIXED-TRANSITION-INTERPOLATION + SQUAREFREE-RECURRENCE-OBSTRUCTION + MULTIHORIZON-BOUNDARY + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`.

`FD-015`, `FD-017`, and `FD-018` leave a precise mixed-schedule question. A fixed squarefree dilation obstructs simultaneous saturation, a fixed nonsquarefree dilation permits full saturation, and transition ratios tending to infinity permit full saturation by fresh-prefix interpolation. For horizon chains whose successive ratios are integers but may vary, these mechanisms admit an exact classification in the unrestricted real-source relaxation: **the only obstruction to full asymptotic saturation is the recurrent presence of bounded squarefree transition ratios.**

Retain

\[
K_H(d,e)=\frac{\gcd(d,e)^2}{de},
\qquad
C_L=(K_L^{-1})_{11}
=\sum_{r\le L}\frac{\mu(r)^2}{J_2(r)},
\qquad
Z:=\zeta(2),
\tag{1}
\]

and, for a real sequence `A(1),A(2),...`, define

\[
m_d^{(H)}:=A\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Hd\right\rfloor\right),
\qquad
E_H:=(m^{(H)})^TK_Hm^{(H)},
\qquad
R_H:=\frac{A(H)^2}{E_H}
\tag{2}
\]

when the horizon vector is nonzero, with `R_H:=0` for the zero vector. The one-horizon duality of `FD-003` gives

\[
R_H\le C_H\le Z,
\qquad
C_L\longrightarrow Z.
\tag{3}
\]

Let

\[
H_0<H_1<H_2<\cdots,
\qquad
H_j=q_jH_{j-1},
\qquad
q_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\quad(j\ge1),
\tag{4}
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal S:=\{j\ge1:q_j\text{ is squarefree}\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{there exists one real source }A\text{ with }R_{H_j}\to Z\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\text{for every fixed }B<\infty,\text{ only finitely many }j\in\mathcal S
\text{ satisfy }q_j\le B.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, the squarefree transition ratios `q_j` tend to infinity **along the squarefree transitions themselves**; if there are only finitely many squarefree transitions, the condition is vacuous.

The constructive implication is quantitative in the same finite-prefix sense as `FD-017` and `FD-018`. Under the right-hand condition of (6), there are integers `L_j -> infinity` such that, for every positive sequence `delta_j -> 0`, one can construct one real source `A` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
R_{H_j}>C_{L_j}-\delta_j
}
\tag{7}
\]

on a tail of the chain. Equation (3) then squeezes `R_(H_j)` to `Z`.

This strictly extends both previously classified interpolation regimes. For example, the transition ratios may alternate between `4` and squarefree primes tending to infinity. The ratios then do **not** tend to infinity, so `FD-017` does not apply, while the chain is not generated by one fixed nonsquarefree dilation, so `FD-018` does not apply. Nevertheless (6) gives full saturation. Conversely, if any fixed squarefree integer occurs as a transition ratio infinitely often, full saturation is impossible.

As in `FD-016`--`FD-018`, this is a theorem only about the arbitrary-real-source relaxation used to diagnose common-source coherence. The interpolated sequence need not have bounded increments, Möbius signs, divisor identities, or any other property of the Mertens function. It is not a Farey discrepancy counterexample and gives no RH estimate.

## 1. The two local mechanisms needed for the classification

Write

\[
u^{(L)}:=K_L^{-1}e_1.
\tag{8}
\]

`FD-018` records the exact inverse-column formula

\[
u_d^{(L)}
=d\sum_{\substack{r\le L\\d\mid r}}
\frac{\mu(r/d)\mu(r)}{J_2(r)}.
\tag{9}
\]

Hence every nonsquarefree coordinate lies in the exact zero set of the equality ray:

\[
\boxed{
u_d^{(L)}=0
\qquad(d\le L\text{ nonsquarefree}).}
\tag{10}
\]

The other input is the squarefree repeated-prime obstruction of `FD-015`. For every fixed squarefree integer `q>=2`, choose a prime `p|q`. There is a fixed constant

\[
\kappa_{q,p}<Z
\tag{11}
\]

such that, for every sufficiently large `H`, every real source obeys

\[
\boxed{
\min\{R_H,R_{qH}\}<\kappa_{q,p}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus a bounded squarefree transition can be obstructive, whereas (10) makes a nonsquarefree transition special in the opposite direction: any exact divisor-index collision carrying the whole nonsquarefree transition factor lands on a zero equality-ray coordinate.

The classification consists of showing that these are not merely examples. For integer-ratio chains, every old/new prefix collision at a nonsquarefree transition has exactly the form needed by (10), while squarefree transitions can be made entirely fresh whenever their ratios escape to infinity.

## 2. An adapted prefix depth exists exactly when squarefree transitions escape

Assume the right-hand condition of (6). Discard finitely many initial horizons so that `H_j>8` and every squarefree transition on the retained tail has `q_j>4`. Reindex the tail from `j=0`; this changes neither condition (6) nor an asymptotic saturation conclusion.

For `j>=1`, define a provisional depth

\[
B_j:=
\begin{cases}
\left\lfloor H_j^{1/3}\right\rfloor,
& q_j\text{ nonsquarefree},\\[1mm]
\left\lfloor\min\{H_j^{1/3},\sqrt{q_j}\}\right\rfloor,
& q_j\text{ squarefree}.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]

At the first retained horizon take, for example,

\[
B_0:=\left\lfloor H_0^{1/3}\right\rfloor.
\tag{14}
\]

Because every `q_j>=2`, the horizons tend to infinity. On nonsquarefree transitions, therefore, `B_j -> infinity`. On squarefree transitions both `H_j -> infinity` and, by hypothesis, `q_j -> infinity` along that subsequence. Consequently

\[
\boxed{B_j\longrightarrow\infty.}
\tag{15}
\]

After discarding a further finite prefix if necessary, put

\[
L_j:=B_j\ge2.
\tag{16}
\]

Then `L_j -> infinity`, and the choice `L_j<=H_j^(1/3)` gives

\[
\boxed{H_j>2L_j^2.}
\tag{17}
\]

For a squarefree transition, also

\[
L_j\le\sqrt{q_j}<\frac{q_j}{2},
\tag{18}
\]

because the retained squarefree ratios exceed `4`. Hence

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{H_j}{L_j}\right\rfloor
=\left\lfloor\frac{q_jH_{j-1}}{L_j}\right\rfloor
\ge2H_{j-1}>H_{j-1}.
\tag{19}
\]

Every one of the first `L_j` floor-quotient source locations at such a squarefree step is therefore strictly above the entire range of every earlier horizon. The whole equality-ray prefix is fresh.

For nonsquarefree transitions freshness need not hold. The exact zero pattern (10) will instead make every inherited collision harmless at leading order.

## 3. Every inherited prefix collision at a nonsquarefree transition is square-killed

Fix a retained stage `j>=1` for which `q_j` is nonsquarefree, and define

\[
x_{j,d}:=\left\lfloor\frac{H_j}{d}\right\rfloor,
\qquad1\le d\le L_j.
\tag{20}
\]

Suppose one current prefix location was visible at some earlier retained horizon `H_i`, `i<j`. Then for some integer `e>=1`,

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{H_j}{d}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lfloor\frac{H_i}{e}\right\rfloor.
\tag{21}
\]

Since all transition ratios are integers,

\[
\lambda:=\frac{H_j}{H_i}
=\prod_{k=i+1}^{j}q_k
\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}.
\tag{22}
\]

Equality of the floors in (21) implies

\[
H_i\,|\lambda e-d|<de.
\tag{23}
\]

On the other hand, the common floor and `H_j>2d` give

\[
e
\le\frac{dH_i}{\lambda H_i-d}
<\frac{2d}{\lambda}.
\tag{24}
\]

Therefore, using (17),

\[
de
<\frac{2d^2}{\lambda}
\le\frac{2L_j^2}{\lambda}
<\frac{H_j}{\lambda}
=H_i.
\tag{25}
\]

If `lambda e-d` were nonzero, its absolute value would be at least `1`, and (23) would contradict (25). Thus

\[
\boxed{d=\lambda e.}
\tag{26}
\]

Now `lambda` contains the current factor `q_j`. Since `q_j` is nonsquarefree, `lambda` and hence `d` are nonsquarefree. Equation (10) yields

\[
\boxed{
u_d^{(L_j)}=0.}
\tag{27}
\]

This is stronger than a mere count of overlaps: **every current prefix coordinate whose source location can already have been fixed by an earlier retained horizon is forced into the exact zero set of the current GCD equality ray.** Nonzero equality-ray coordinates remain available for fresh assignment.

The same-scale prefix does not collide with its own tail. Indeed, for `d<=L_j`, (17) gives

\[
\frac{H_j}{d}-\frac{H_j}{d+1}
=\frac{H_j}{d(d+1)}>1,
\tag{28}
\]

so the first `L_j+1` floor quotients are strictly decreasing. No coordinate with index greater than `L_j` samples one of the current prefix locations.

## 4. Sequential interpolation across arbitrary mixed transitions

Construct `A` inductively over the retained horizons. At stage `j`, let

\[
u^{(j)}:=K_{L_j}^{-1}e_1.
\tag{29}
\]

For each current prefix location `x_(j,d)`, `1<=d<=L_j`, proceed as follows:

- if that source value has never been assigned, set
  \[
  A(x_{j,d})=t_j u_d^{(j)};
  \tag{30}
  \]
- if it was assigned at an earlier retained horizon, leave it unchanged.

At a squarefree transition every prefix location is fresh by (19). At a nonsquarefree transition, every inherited prefix location has `u_d^(j)=0` by (27). Thus in the latter case the old value is an additive inherited term, not a missing coefficient of the new leading equality ray.

After prescribing the prefix, set every other still-unassigned source location **visible at `H_j`** to zero. Do not assign source locations that are not sampled at the current horizon; they may be needed at a later stage.

By (28), no tail coordinate duplicates a newly assigned prefix location. Hence the complete horizon vector has the form

\[
\boxed{
m^{(H_j)}=t_j\widetilde u^{(j)}+b_j,}
\tag{31}
\]

where `widetilde u^(j)` is `u^(j)` embedded in the first `L_j` coordinates and `b_j` is fully determined before `t_j` is chosen. In particular, any inherited prefix values occur only at coordinates where `widetilde u^(j)` is zero.

The endpoint `H_j=x_(j,1)` is always fresh because it exceeds every earlier horizon. Therefore

\[
A(H_j)=t_j u_1^{(j)}=t_jC_{L_j}.
\tag{32}
\]

The top-left `L_j x L_j` principal block of `K_(H_j)` is exactly `K_(L_j)`, so

\[
(\widetilde u^{(j)})^TK_{H_j}\widetilde u^{(j)}=C_{L_j}.
\tag{33}
\]

Consequently

\[
E_{H_j}
=t_j^2C_{L_j}
+2t_j(\widetilde u^{(j)})^TK_{H_j}b_j
+b_j^TK_{H_j}b_j,
\tag{34}
\]

and, with all inherited data fixed,

\[
R_{H_j}
=\frac{t_j^2C_{L_j}^2}{E_{H_j}}
\longrightarrow C_{L_j}
\qquad(|t_j|\to\infty).
\tag{35}
\]

Choose a finite nonzero `t_j` large enough that (7) holds. Later stages never alter source values already assigned, so they do not change any earlier horizon vector. Induction produces one globally consistent real sequence on every source location ever sampled; assign all remaining untouched values arbitrarily, say zero.

Since `L_j -> infinity` and `delta_j -> 0`, equations (3), (7), and `C_(L_j)->Z` give

\[
Z\ge R_{H_j}>C_{L_j}-\delta_j\longrightarrow Z.
\tag{36}
\]

This proves the sufficiency direction of (6).

## 5. Bounded recurrent squarefree transitions are exactly the converse obstruction

Assume now that the right-hand condition of (6) fails. Then there is a finite `B` for which infinitely many squarefree transition ratios satisfy `q_j<=B`. Only finitely many squarefree integers lie in `[2,B]`, so one fixed squarefree integer `q` occurs as `q_j` for infinitely many `j`.

Choose a prime `p|q` and the corresponding fixed `kappa_(q,p)<Z` from (11)--(12). Along the infinite set of indices with `q_j=q`, the base horizons `H_(j-1)` tend to infinity. Thus, for all sufficiently late such indices,

\[
\min\{R_{H_{j-1}},R_{H_j}\}
<\kappa_{q,p}<Z.
\tag{37}
\]

If one source satisfied `R_(H_j)->Z` along the whole chain, then both members of every sufficiently late adjacent pair would exceed the fixed number `kappa_(q,p)`, contradicting (37). Therefore no such source exists.

This proves necessity and completes the equivalence (6).

## 6. What the classification changes in the multihorizon frontier

The result unifies and sharpens the present coherence controls.

First, the fixed-geometric dichotomy of `FD-018` is recovered immediately. If `q_j=q` is squarefree at every stage, the squarefree transitions stay bounded and saturation is impossible. If fixed `q` is nonsquarefree, there are no squarefree transitions and saturation is possible.

Second, the supermultiplicative interpolation theorem `FD-017` is a sufficient but not necessary regime. If every `q_j->infinity`, then certainly the squarefree transitions escape. But (6) allows bounded nonsquarefree transitions to recur arbitrarily often. For example, a chain with transition pattern

\[
4,p_1,4,p_2,4,p_3,\ldots,
\qquad p_k\to\infty\text{ prime},
\tag{38}
\]

admits full saturation even though its successive multiplicative gaps repeatedly equal `4`.

Third, overlap density by itself cannot be the missing coercive statistic. Repeated bounded nonsquarefree transitions create persistent cross-scale reuse, but the reused coordinates are square-killed on the equality ray and therefore do not obstruct leading saturation. What matters is overlap on **incompatible nonzero equality-ray coordinates**, exactly as suggested by `FD-018`.

The remaining integer-ratio coherence-only regime is correspondingly narrower. Any theorem that hopes to exclude full saturation in the unrestricted source class must exploit infinitely many bounded squarefree transitions; merely mixing in arbitrary numbers of bounded nonsquarefree transitions cannot suffice. This still does **not** show that the pairwise deficits from recurrent squarefree transitions accumulate at an RH-relevant rate. `FD-015` supplies a fixed local gap, while `FD-009` warns that a sufficiently dense exact hierarchy can simply reconstruct the source. The live question remains whether there is a nonreconstructive family with enough recurrent squarefree collision structure to produce a scale-sensitive cumulative deficit.

For the physical Mertens source, the classification is only a relaxation boundary. Source-specific Möbius constraints may rule out interpolants even on schedules allowed by (6). Any RH-facing gain must use those additional arithmetic restrictions or retain genuinely Farey-specific data rather than infer it from common-source coherence alone.

## 7. Prior art, novelty boundary, and falsification points

The square-GCD kernel, its Smith/Jordan factorization, inverse equality ray, and the limit `C_L -> zeta(2)` are classical ingredients already bounded by the prior-art audit in `FD-003`. The repeated-prime squarefree obstruction is the local theorem of `FD-015`; the variable fresh-prefix construction is `FD-017`; and the zero-coordinate interpolation mechanism for a fixed nonsquarefree dilation is `FD-018`.

Targeted literature searches again covered Jean-Paul Cardinal, *Symmetric matrices related to the Mertens function* (Linear Algebra Appl. **432** (2010), 161--172; arXiv:0811.3701), Jeffrey C. Lagarias and David H. Richman, *The floor quotient partial order* (Adv. Appl. Math. **153** (2024), 102615; arXiv:2212.11689), their later family of `a`-floor quotient partial orders (arXiv:2403.04342), and the neighboring Cardinal-matrix analysis of Lagarias--Montague (arXiv:1511.08154). These works supply neighboring Mertens-matrix and floor-quotient structure, not a theorem imported into the proof above. Searches by the variable integer-ratio, squarefree-transition, GCD-matrix, floor-quotient, and saturation formulations did not locate equivalence (6). That search absence is not a broad novelty claim.

The proof has four decisive failure points. First, at a nonsquarefree transition the exact floor collision argument must force `d=lambda e`; approximate proportionality would not suffice. Second, the current transition factor `q_j` must divide `lambda`, so its squared prime factor really forces the collided index `d` into the exact zero set (10). Third, at a squarefree transition the chosen depth must make the whole planted prefix fresh; this is where escape of the squarefree ratios is used. Fourth, inherited data in (31) must be independent of the newly chosen amplitude `t_j`; assigning unsampled source values prematurely would invalidate that step, which is why the construction assigns only currently visible locations.

A direct falsification of the sufficiency direction would be an integer-ratio schedule satisfying squarefree transition escape for which some required nonzero prefix coordinate is nevertheless forced by earlier visibility. A falsification of necessity would give full saturation while one fixed squarefree transition ratio recurs infinitely often, contradicting the established pair obstruction `FD-015`.

The durable conclusion is exact but deliberately restricted: **for integer-ratio horizon chains in the unrestricted common-source GCD-duality relaxation, full asymptotic saturation of the sharp one-horizon constant is possible if and only if the squarefree transition ratios escape every bounded set.** It classifies full saturation, not the quantitative size of the residual deficit when bounded squarefree transitions recur, and it does not by itself improve the Franel--Landau/RH exponent.
