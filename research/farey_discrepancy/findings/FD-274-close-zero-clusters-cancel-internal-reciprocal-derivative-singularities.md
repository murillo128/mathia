## Claim

FD-273 localizes any surviving threshold-sized finite critical-zero coherence, outside a polynomially sparse exceptional set, onto the close-gap complement. A tempting interpretation is that close gaps are useful because the individual residue weights

\[
\frac{1}{\zeta'(\rho)}
\]

can become large when nearby simple zeros make `|\zeta'(\rho)|` small. That interpretation is incomplete in the signed repair field. The singular factors contributed by the **internal gaps of a finite simple-zero cluster cancel exactly when the cluster residues are summed before absolute values are taken**.

Let

\[
\mathcal C=\{\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\}
\]

be distinct simple zeros of `\zeta`, and write locally

\[
P_{\mathcal C}(s)
:=\prod_{j=1}^m(s-\rho_j),
\qquad
\zeta(s)=P_{\mathcal C}(s)H_{\mathcal C}(s).
\]

For any function `A` analytic at the cluster nodes,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^m
\frac{A(\rho_j)}{\zeta'(\rho_j)}
=
[\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m]
\left(\frac{A}{H_{\mathcal C}}\right),
}
\tag{1}
\]

where the right side is the ordinary divided difference of order `m-1`. Thus all barycentric denominators generated by the mutual spacings inside `\mathcal C` are already the standard divided-difference denominators and do not survive as independent singular amplification.

For a pair `\mathcal C=\{\rho_-,\rho_+\}` this is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A(\rho_-)}{\zeta'(\rho_-)}
+
\frac{A(\rho_+)}{\zeta'(\rho_+)}
=
\frac{
(A/H_{\mathcal C})(\rho_+)
-(A/H_{\mathcal C})(\rho_-)
}{\rho_+-\rho_-}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, if the two nodes approach one another while `A/H_{\mathcal C}` remains `C^1` along the joining segment, the apparent `|\rho_+-\rho_-|^{-1}` growth of the two separate reciprocal derivatives is removable. More generally, for a consecutive critical-line cluster whose joining segment contains no further zeros after the cluster has been factored out, the Hermite--Genocchi representation gives

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{j=1}^m
\frac{A(\rho_j)}{\zeta'(\rho_j)}
\right|
\le
\frac{1}{(m-1)!}
\sup_{s\in I_{\mathcal C}}
\left|
\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)^{m-1}
\frac{A(s)}{H_{\mathcal C}(s)}
\right|,
}
\tag{3}
\]

where `I_{\mathcal C}` is the vertical segment spanning the cluster. Equation (3) is stated for critical-line nodes; parametrizing the segment by the ordinate changes the derivative only by unit powers of `i`.

The point is not that close clusters are harmless. It is more specific: **internal gap singularity is not by itself a usable source of large signed packet amplitude.** Any genuine close-cluster amplification must reappear through variation or poor conditioning of the desingularized background `H_{\mathcal C}^{-1}`, through the analytic packet amplitude, through interactions between different clusters, or through ingredients not covered by the simple finite-zero packet.

## Evidence

The identity is algebraic. At a simple cluster zero `\rho_j`,

\[
\zeta'(\rho_j)
=
P_{\mathcal C}'(\rho_j)H_{\mathcal C}(\rho_j),
\]

so

\[
\frac{A(\rho_j)}{\zeta'(\rho_j)}
=
\frac{(A/H_{\mathcal C})(\rho_j)}
{P_{\mathcal C}'(\rho_j)}.
\]

Since

\[
P_{\mathcal C}'(\rho_j)
=
\prod_{k\ne j}(\rho_j-\rho_k),
\]

summing over `j` is exactly the barycentric formula for the highest divided difference, proving (1). No zero-spacing estimate, moment conjecture, or asymptotic approximation enters this step.

For critical-line nodes write `\rho_j=1/2+i\gamma_j` and set

\[
f(t)
=
\left(\frac{A}{H_{\mathcal C}}\right)
\left(\frac12+it\right).
\]

The Hermite--Genocchi simplex formula for divided differences expresses the order `m-1` divided difference as an average of `f^{(m-1)}` over convex combinations of the ordinates. The simplex has volume `1/(m-1)!`; taking absolute values gives (3). This use of divided differences is classical interpolation algebra and is not claimed as novel mathematics.

## Transfer to the replicated zero packet

For the exact FD-270/FD-271 packet kernel define, for fixed integer horizon `N` and depth coordinate `d`,

\[
K_s(d)
=
\sum_{q\mid d}
\bigl(q^s-(q-1)^s\bigr),
\]

with the `q=1` term `(q-1)^s` interpreted as the identically zero analytic function on the right half-plane. Put

\[
A_{N,d}(s)
:=
N^{s-1/2}\frac{K_s(d)}{s}.
\tag{4}
\]

At a simple critical zero `\rho=1/2+i\gamma`,

\[
A_{N,d}(\rho)
=
N^{i\gamma}\frac{K_\rho(d)}{\rho},
\]

which is exactly the signed coefficient appearing before `1/\zeta'(\rho)` in the normalized packet coordinate `P_{T,d}(N)` of FD-271. Hence the contribution of a simple zero cluster to a **single physical repair coordinate** is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C}
N^{i\operatorname{Im}\rho}
\frac{K_\rho(d)}{\rho\zeta'(\rho)}
=
[\rho:\rho\in\mathcal C]
\left(
\frac{A_{N,d}}{H_{\mathcal C}}
\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

This cancellation occurs before the band functional

\[
S_{T,x}(N)
=
\sum_{x<d\le2x}
|\widetilde G_{T,N}(d)|
\]

takes absolute values. It therefore cannot be dismissed as cancellation in an auxiliary spectral norm: it is already present in each signed coefficient from which the physical `\ell^1` repair demand is formed.

The packet amplitude itself is smooth on the critical line. For `\Re s=1/2`,

\[
|K_s(d)|
\le
2\tau(d)d^{1/2},
\tag{6}
\]

and differentiation gives

\[
|K_s'(d)|
\le
2\tau(d)d^{1/2}\log(2d).
\tag{7}
\]

Therefore, away from `s=0`,

\[
|A_{N,d}(s)|
\le
\frac{2\tau(d)d^{1/2}}{|s|},
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
|A_{N,d}'(s)|
\ll
\frac{\tau(d)d^{1/2}}{|s|}
\left(
\log N+\log(2d)+\frac1{|s|}
\right).
\tag{9}
\]

For a close pair, inserting (8)--(9) into the derivative of `A/H` gives the explicit gap-free bound

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\frac{A_{N,d}(\rho_-)}{\zeta'(\rho_-)}
+
\frac{A_{N,d}(\rho_+)}{\zeta'(\rho_+)}
\right|
\le
\sup_{I_{\mathcal C}}
\left(
\frac{|A_{N,d}'|}{|H_{\mathcal C}|}
+
\frac{|A_{N,d}|\,|H_{\mathcal C}'|}
{|H_{\mathcal C}|^2}
\right).
}
\tag{10}
\]

No inverse power of the internal pair gap occurs in (10). On a dyadic zero-height block `U<\gamma\le2U` and a divisor band `d\asymp x`, the purely packet-side factors are at most

\[
|A_{N,d}|
\ll
\frac{x^{1/2+o(1)}}{U},
\qquad
|A_{N,d}'|
\ll
\frac{x^{1/2+o(1)}}{U}\log(Nx).
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the packet kernel itself contributes only logarithmic variation across a very small gap. Polynomial amplification, if present, must be carried by the background factor `H_{\mathcal C}^{-1}` or by a mechanism outside this simple-cluster reduction.

## Stress tests

The cancellation is visible in the smallest exact models. If

\[
\zeta_{a,b}(s)=(s-a)(s-b)
\]

and `A(s)=1`, the two individual residues have magnitude `|a-b|^{-1}` but their sum is exactly zero:

\[
\frac1{a-b}+rac1{b-a}=0.
\]

If instead `A(s)=s`, the same residue sum is exactly `1`, independent of the gap. These two models verify both the removable singularity and the fact that the cluster may retain a finite derivative signal after the cancellation.

The reduction also survives an adversarial background test. For

\[
\zeta_{a,b,H}(s)=(s-a)(s-b)H(s),
\]

nothing prevents `(A/H)'` from being large. Equation (2) then converges to that large derivative as the gap closes. Hence the result does **not** prove that close zeros cannot power the repair; it proves only that their mutual spacing is not an independent amplifier once the associated residues are recombined correctly.

For clusters of size `m>2`, the same distinction becomes higher order: coalescence replaces the barycentric singular denominators by an `(m-1)`st derivative of `A/H_{\mathcal C}`. Large high derivatives of the desingularized background remain a genuine escape route.

## Prior-art check

The barycentric formula for divided differences and its coalescing-node/Hermite--Genocchi interpretation are classical interpolation theory. No novelty is claimed for those identities. Likewise, the relation between unusually small `|\zeta'(\rho)|` and close zeta zeros is part of the established zero-spacing and zeta-derivative literature already surrounding FD-273.

A targeted search for close zeta zeros, reciprocal derivative residues, residue cancellation, and divided differences found the standard divided-difference literature and the established work relating zero spacing to zeta derivatives, but no theorem applying this cluster desingularization to the finite Mertens/Farey repair packet, consecutive differences, or the divisor-replication kernel of FD-267--FD-273. The new content here is the exact transport (5) and its consequence for how the close-gap escape in FD-273 must be interpreted.

No external theorem is load-bearing for the proof: (1) follows directly from factorization and the barycentric identity, while (5)--(11) are elementary consequences of the already persisted packet kernel. `SOURCES.md` therefore requires no new source dependency.

## Implications

FD-273 showed that, under RH, a threshold-coherent finite simple critical-zero packet on nonexceptional source-large horizons must be close-gap dominated. FD-274 sharpens what that statement can mean. **A large reciprocal-square moment concentrated on close zeros is not, by itself, evidence for a large signed repair field**, because the same small internal gaps that enlarge the separate values `1/\zeta'(\rho)` cancel exactly when the cluster residues are recombined.

The live spectral question therefore moves from raw close-gap counts or raw negative moments to a cluster-aware quantity: after factoring the close cluster itself out of `\zeta`, how large can the derivatives of

\[
H_{\mathcal C}^{-1}
\]

be on the cluster interval, and can those background-conditioned divided differences align coherently across the divisor band and the source-selected horizons? A useful next bound would control the mean square or tail distribution of the grouped coefficients in (5), rather than the ungrouped sum of `|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}`.

This also separates two mechanisms that raw negative moments conflate. Internal spacing produces large individual barycentric weights but is exactly desingularized by the cluster sum; smallness or rapid variation of the **external/background factor** survives and can still create genuine amplification. Any future close-zero argument must identify the latter resource explicitly.

## Limitations

Equation (1) assumes the zeros in the cluster are distinct and simple. Multiple zeros lead to higher-order poles in `1/\zeta` and require confluent residue/Hermite interpolation; they are not covered here.

The derivative bound (3) requires `A/H_{\mathcal C}` to be analytic along the convex segment spanning the nodes. Under RH this is ensured for a consecutive critical-line cluster after all zeros on that segment are included in `\mathcal C`; without RH, an off-line zero could obstruct that simple one-dimensional corridor. The exact nodal identity (1) itself does not require RH.

No useful unconditional upper bound on the derivatives of `H_{\mathcal C}^{-1}` is proved here. Those derivatives can in principle be large, particularly if other zeros lie just outside the chosen cluster or if a larger cluster is beginning to form. Therefore FD-274 is a reduction of the close-gap mechanism, not a proof that the spectral route fails.

The finding also concerns only the finite simple-zero packet. The explicit-formula remainder, zeros above the truncation height, multiple zeros, and any non-spectral switched-only repair remain outside the claim.

## Decisive audit

For any proposed close-gap lower bound derived from large individual reciprocal derivatives, first partition the relevant simple zeros into consecutive clusters and replace each cluster sum by (5). If the claimed lower bound disappears after this exact recombination, it was using a removable internal-spacing singularity. A surviving bound must exhibit one of the resources that (5) leaves intact: large derivatives of `H_{\mathcal C}^{-1}`, coherent variation of the analytic packet amplitude, inter-cluster phase alignment, or a component outside the simple finite packet.

Conversely, a counterexample to the present reduction would require distinct simple zeros and an analytic amplitude for which the residue sum is not the corresponding divided difference. That would contradict the exact barycentric identity, so the algebraic claim is directly auditable at finite cluster size.
