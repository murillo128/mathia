# FD-113 — fixed Stern–Brocot descendant geometry is a one-scalar Möbius lift

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + REPRESENTATION-CLASSIFICATION + STERN-BROCOT-ANCESTRY + ONE-SCALAR-MOBIUS-LIFT + NONZERO-PLUCKER-CAN-BE-EXTRINSIC + NEGATIVE/ROUTE-RESTRICTION`.

`FD-112` kills the minimal refinement carrier made from the two child gaps of one mediant: after parent-scale normalization and conservation control, the residual vector lies on a line. The immediate escape is to retain a deeper descendant fan. Three or more normalized descendant gaps live in a higher-dimensional simplex, and their centered exterior minors can indeed be nonzero.

That nonzero area is not yet a new information channel. For any fixed finite Stern--Brocot descendant template inside one Farey parent edge, the entire affine-normalized metric geometry is a rational function of the same single split scalar already present in `FD-112`. Equivalently, for the affine-normalized local carrier tested here, quotienting parent translation and scale leaves a one-parameter Möbius deformation of a fixed projective ancestry skeleton. Higher-dimensional Plücker minors may therefore measure the extrinsic curvature of a nonlinear embedding of one scalar rather than two independent residual coordinates.

The exact factorization is as follows. Let

\[
A=\frac ab < B=\frac cd,
\qquad
bc-ad=1,
\qquad
G:=B-A=\frac1{bd}.
\tag{1}
\]

Every Stern--Brocot descendant of the edge can be written

\[
x_{u,v}
=
\frac{ua+vc}{ub+vd}
\tag{2}
\]

for a primitive coefficient ray `(u,v)` with nonnegative integer coordinates. Fix any ordered finite template

\[
W=(w_0,\ldots,w_m),
\qquad
w_j=(u_j,v_j),
\tag{3}
\]

with `w_0=(1,0)`, `w_m=(0,1)`, and positive consecutive determinants

\[
\Delta_j:=u_jv_{j+1}-v_ju_{j+1}>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Put `r=b/d`. Then the affine-normalized descendant positions and selected gap lengths are exactly

\[
\boxed{
y_j
:=
\frac{x_{u_j,v_j}-A}{G}
=
\frac{v_j}{u_jr+v_j},
}
\tag{5}

and

\[
\boxed{
g_j
:=
\frac{x_{u_{j+1},v_{j+1}}-x_{u_j,v_j}}G
=
\frac{\Delta_j r}
{(u_jr+v_j)(u_{j+1}r+v_{j+1})}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus for fixed `W` the complete normalized geometry `(y_j)` or `(g_j)` factors through one positive scalar `r`. The first mediant split from `FD-112` has normalized coordinate

\[
\alpha=\frac d{b+d}=\frac1{1+r},
\qquad
s:=2\alpha-1=\frac{d-b}{d+b},
\tag{7}
\]

so

\[
r=\frac{1-s}{1+s}.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently there is a fixed rational map `Psi_W` such that

\[
\boxed{
(g_0,\ldots,g_{m-1})=\Psi_W(s).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Whenever the root split is retained, every fixed-template descendant gap is therefore reconstructible from the single `FD-112` imbalance scalar. Adding more local descendant gaps increases the ambient dimension but not the number of scalar shape parameters.

## 1. The full fixed descendant fan factors through the parent denominator ratio

The representation (2) follows directly from mediant recursion. The two parent endpoints have coefficient rays `(1,0)` and `(0,1)`, and taking a mediant adds numerator and denominator columns, so it also adds their coefficient rays. Induction therefore puts every descendant inside the positive integer cone generated by the two parents.

For a coefficient ray `(u,v)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{u,v}-A
&=
\frac{(ua+vc)b-a(ub+vd)}
     {b(ub+vd)}\\
&=
\frac{v(cb-ad)}
     {b(ub+vd)}
=
\frac{v}{b(ub+vd)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Dividing by `G=1/(bd)` gives (5). In projective ancestry coordinate `t=v/u`, whenever `u>0` this reads

\[
\boxed{
y=\frac{t}{r+t}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the normalized child geometry is literally the image of the fixed projective ancestry labels under a one-parameter Möbius map.

For two ordered rays `w=(u,v)` and `w'=(u',v')`,

\[
x_{w'}-x_w
=
\frac{(uv'-vu')(bc-ad)}
     {(ub+vd)(u'b+v'd)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Equation (6) follows after dividing by `G`. If the selected rays are consecutive in a full Stern--Brocot fan then `Delta_j=1`; allowing `Delta_j>1` merely permits a template that omits intermediate descendants. Hence the factorization does not depend on keeping every node of a finite subtree.

The conservation law is automatic:

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} g_j=1,
\tag{13}
\]

because the selected gaps telescope from `A` to `B`. But (5)--(9) say substantially more than conservation. Even after moving from two child gaps to an arbitrarily large fixed finite vector in the simplex, the normalized metric state still factors through one scalar shape parameter. Its numerators `a,c` have disappeared completely.

The same statement remains true for a predeclared family of templates `W_H` whose size grows with an external horizon: at each fixed `H`, once `W_H` is treated as known control data, the normalized metric vector still factors through `r`. What is not covered is an ancestry template whose **choice itself** depends on the local arithmetic state or on a global truncation rule; that state-dependent combinatorial selection is additional information and must be accounted for explicitly.

## 2. Nonzero higher-dimensional Plücker minors do not refute the one-scalar classification

Unlike the two-child vector of `FD-112`, a deeper gap vector need not lie on an affine line. The map `r -> Psi_W(r)` can be a curved one-dimensional subset of the simplex, so centered minors of different samples can be nonzero.

The smallest useful stress test takes

\[
W=((1,0),(2,1),(1,1),(0,1)),
\tag{14}
\]

corresponding to the parent left endpoint, its left grandchild, the root mediant, and the right endpoint. Equation (6) gives the three-gap vectors

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
r & (g_0,g_1,g_2)\\ \hline
1 & (1/3,\,1/6,\,1/2)\\
2 & (1/5,\,2/15,\,2/3)\\
3 & (1/7,\,3/28,\,3/4).
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]

All have total `1`. Center the last two samples at the `r=1` sample and keep their first two coordinates:

\[
u_2=\left(-\frac2{15},-\frac1{30}\right),
\qquad
u_3=\left(-\frac4{21},-\frac5{84}\right).
\tag{16}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\det(u_2,u_3)=\frac1{630}\ne0.
}
\tag{17}
\]

So the stronger statement "all deeper centered Plücker minors vanish" would be false. The correct representation conclusion is different: both vectors in (16), and therefore the determinant in (17), are deterministic functions of the scalar parent ratios `r=2,3` and the fixed template (14). The minor detects bending of the curve `Psi_W`; it does not certify a second local residual variable.

More generally, any predeclared deterministic feature map built only from the normalized positions, normalized descendant gaps, and fixed template has the form

\[
\mathcal F_W=\widetilde{\mathcal F}_W(s).
\tag{18}
\]

An exterior statistic comparing two events is therefore a two-point kernel `K_W(s_i,s_j)`. Such a kernel may retain the **ordered sequence** of split scalars and may be useful after a genuinely nonlocal aggregation, but the exterior operation itself has not manufactured information beyond that scalar sequence. In particular, a nonzero finite Plücker diagnostic is not by itself evidence that the clue's requirement of two independent controlled residual coordinates has been met.

This distinction is the main reason to record the finding. `FD-112` supplied a zero-minor kill test for the minimal carrier. `FD-113` shows what happens when that literal zero test stops working: the representation can still factor through one scalar even though its embedding produces nonzero areas.

## 3. Representation boundary for the ordered refinement clue

The relevant quotient is now explicit.

Before control, a parent edge carries its absolute location, its parent scale `G`, the coefficient template `W`, and its descendant metric data. The local metric representation classified here **chooses** to pass to affine-normalized descendants, thereby quotienting absolute parent position and scale for this particular carrier. This is a scope choice, not a claim that absolute position is irrelevant to Franel--Landau discrepancy. A future carrier that retains absolute parent position has an additional coordinate outside the present theorem.

After that local affine normalization, conditioning on the predeclared ancestry template leaves

\[
\boxed{\text{one continuous residual coordinate: }s
       =\frac{d-b}{d+b}.}
\tag{19}
\]

If the parent scale is retained, then `(G,s)` determines the denominator pair `(b,d)` over the positive reals because `G=1/(bd)` and `b/d=(1-s)/(1+s)`. That is genuine denominator information, but it already belongs to the parent Farey edge; deeper descendants do not create it. The present claim is only about the extra **shape** information supplied by fixed-template local refinement after the parent scale is controlled.

Likewise, if the template varies from edge to edge, its combinatorial ancestry label is an additional discrete carrier. The theorem does not erase that carrier. It says that, **conditional on the ancestry label**, the Euclidean descendant geometry supplies only `s`. A proposal that uses the varying ancestry itself must count it as a separate coordinate and test whether it reduces to the classical Farey-index/continued-fraction algebra already constrained by `FD-011`, rather than attributing the gain to a higher-dimensional gap vector.

A global Farey-order cutoff can also make the visible descendant template depend on `Q`, `b`, and `d`; such a state-dependent selection law lies outside the fixed-template statement. So does a coordinate carrying an independently defined Franel--Landau discrepancy residual, physical source tag, cross-horizon persistence variable, or other quantity not reconstructible from `(W,s,G)`.

The route restriction for `CLUE-ordered-plucker-refinement-transfer.md` can therefore be sharpened:

\[
\boxed{
\text{more fixed local descendants}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{more independent residual coordinates}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

To pass the representation gate, the next candidate must exhibit a second coordinate that remains non-reconstructible after conditioning on `s`, parent scale, and the predeclared ancestry control. A discrepancy/source residual attached to the refinement may qualify. Merely increasing descendant depth, retaining more Euclidean gaps, or nonlinearly embedding those gaps does not.

## 4. Prior-art boundary and surviving target

The matrix/projective description of the Stern--Brocot tree is classical. Modern treatments routinely formulate Stern--Brocot paths by `2 x 2` unimodular matrices and Möbius transformations; see, for example, J. L. Hughes and M. Niqui, *Admissible digit sets*, Theoretical Computer Science **351** (2006), 61--73, DOI `10.1016/j.tcs.2005.09.059`, and Christophe Reutenauer, *On the Stern--Brocot expansion of real numbers*, Journal de théorie des nombres de Bordeaux **31** (2019), 697--722, DOI `10.5802/jtnb.1104`. No novelty is claimed for mediant coefficient vectors, unimodularity, or the Möbius nature of Stern--Brocot ancestry.

Equations (5)--(9) are derived here directly and require no external theorem. The Mathia-specific durable content is the representation classification they impose on the current Farey-discrepancy program: **a deeper fixed local descendant gap vector can produce nonzero Plücker minors while still containing no more normalized metric information than the single root split scalar already isolated by `FD-112`.** The nonzero-minor example (17) is an explicit adversarial warning against treating extrinsic area as evidence of a second independent residual coordinate.

This does not show that the ordered scalar split sequence is useless, that varying ancestry is useless, or that a horizon-dependent refinement packet cannot be RH-relevant. It also does not touch a Plücker field whose second coordinate is an independently defined discrepancy/source residual. It closes the narrower but natural escape in which one tries to satisfy the two-coordinate gate merely by retaining a larger fixed Stern--Brocot descendant geometry.

The next decisive test should therefore define that second residual coordinate before inspecting data and prove an exact finite-order reduction. Its matched control should preserve as much of the normalized descendant geometry and declared nuisance data as is realizably compatible with Farey constraints while varying the claimed independent coordinate or Franel--Landau behavior; exact preservation of `(G,s,W)` is not required when those data rigidify the edge. If no independent residual can be exhibited under the strongest realizable control, the refinement branch has not yet left the one-scalar representation boundary.

No external theorem is load-bearing in the derivation, so `SOURCES.md` requires no change.
