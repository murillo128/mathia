## Claim

`FD-214` showed that exact squarefree support and exact unit amplitudes do not kill the all-integer switched Floquet blind mode of `FD-213`; its remaining mismatch with Möbius was the absence of multiplicative sign compatibility.

No **fixed finite set of prime Euler factors** repairs that mismatch.

Let `Pcal` be any fixed finite set of primes. For the same source-independent depth schedule

\[
q(n)=q_j,\qquad j=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor,\qquad
q_j=
\begin{cases}
0,&j\equiv0\pmod3,\\
3,&j\equiv1,2\pmod3,
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]

there exists a deterministic sequence `(xi_n)` such that

\[
\boxed{\xi_n\in\{-1,0,1\},\qquad \xi_n^2=\mu(n)^2\quad(n\ge1),}
\tag{2}
\]

and, after fixing `xi_1=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\xi_{pn}=-\xi_n
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P,\ p\nmid n).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every selected prime has exactly the Möbius Euler-factor sign and exact multiplicative propagation through every coprime integer. In particular `xi_p=-1` for every `p in Pcal`, and the source agrees with Möbius on the entire squarefree Euler cube generated by `Pcal`.

Nevertheless, with

\[
X(N):=\sum_{n\le N}\xi_n,
\qquad
\mathcal C_q^X(n):=(T-I)^q[X(2n)-X(n)]=(T-I)^{q+1}X(n),
\tag{4}
\]

one has simultaneously

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{q(n)}^X(n)=O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\sqrt{n\log n}\right)
\qquad(n\ge2),
}
\tag{5}
\]

while along the explicit points from `FD-213`,

\[
N_m=3\,2^{3m-1},
\tag{6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
|X(N_m)|\asymp_{\mathcal P} N_m^\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac43\log_2\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}
\approx0.9256558848>\frac12.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Therefore the selected all-integer switched root filter can satisfy the full RH-scale pointwise estimate while the summatory function violates square-root growth, even after imposing exact Möbius multiplicative coherence at **any prescribed finite collection of primes**.

This is still not a Möbius counterexample. The constants and the constructed source depend on the fixed set `Pcal`; the signs on the remaining squarefree core are not multiplicative. The result instead sharpens the representation boundary: neither squarefree support, unit amplitudes, nor finitely many exact Euler factors can be the missing ingredient. Any arithmetic rescue of the switched criterion must use genuinely global or scale-growing multiplicative compatibility, or an observation law that mixes the dyadic fibres by some other exact mechanism.

## 1. Start from the profile-lifted null mode

Use the exact `FD-213` profile

\[
A(x)=\varepsilon\,a_{j(x)}h(t(x)),
\qquad
j(x)=\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor,
\qquad
t(x)=\frac{x}{2^{j(x)}},
\tag{8}
\]

for `x>=1`, extended by `A(x)=0` for `0<=x<1`, with

\[
h(t)=(t-1)(2-t).
\tag{9}
\]

The scale amplitudes satisfy

\[
|a_j|\ll\rho^j,
\qquad
\rho=2^\alpha=\left(\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right)^{4/3}<2,
\tag{10}
\]

and the period-three recurrence from `FD-212` gives exactly

\[
\mathcal C_{q(n)}^A(n)=0
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{11}
\]

At the interior points (6),

\[
|A(N_m)|\asymp N_m^\alpha.
\tag{12}
\]

Because the profile vanishes at both dyadic endpoints, its real extension is continuous across band boundaries. On every multiplicative interval `[x,2x]`, its first derivative is piecewise smooth with only `O(1)` dyadic breakpoints, and

\[
|A(x)|\ll \varepsilon x^\alpha,
\qquad
|A'(x)|\ll \varepsilon x^{\alpha-1}
\tag{13}
\]

away from those breakpoints, with the same one-sided bound at each breakpoint. These elementary regularity estimates are the only properties of the explicit quadratic profile needed below.

## 2. Invert any fixed finite Euler cube before imposing support

For a prime `p`, let

\[
(S_pF)(x):=F(x/p).
\tag{14}
\]

If a source has the Möbius sign relation at every prime in `Pcal`, then its cumulative function is naturally acted on by the finite Euler operator

\[
D_{\mathcal P}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(I-S_p).
\tag{15}
\]

The key point is that this finite operator can be inverted on the sublinear profile (8) at finite cost. Let

\[
\langle\mathcal P\rangle
=
\left\{\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{k_p}:k_p\ge0\right\}
\tag{16}
\]

be the multiplicative semigroup generated by the selected primes, and define

\[
\boxed{
U(x):=\sum_{s\in\langle\mathcal P\rangle}A(x/s).
}
\tag{17}
\]

For each fixed `x` the sum is finite because `A(y)=0` below `1`. Equivalently, (17) is the product of the geometric inverses `(I-S_p)^(-1)`. The factors commute, so telescoping gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{D_{\mathcal P}U=A.}
\tag{18}
\]

Moreover, since `alpha>0`,

\[
\sum_{s\in\langle\mathcal P\rangle}s^{-\alpha}
=
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(1-p^{-\alpha})^{-1}<\infty.
\tag{19}
\]

Summing (13) termwise therefore gives, with constants depending on the fixed prime set,

\[
|U(x)|\ll_{\mathcal P}\varepsilon x^\alpha,
\qquad
|U(x+1)-U(x)|\ll_{\mathcal P}\varepsilon x^{\alpha-1}.
\tag{20}
\]

Write

\[
w_n:=U(n)-U(n-1).
\tag{21}
\]

On a dyadic block `X<=n<=2X`, each scaled profile `A(x/s)` crosses at most one of its dyadic breakpoints. Its contribution to the total variation of `(w_n)` is `O(epsilon X^(alpha-1)s^(-alpha))`. Summing (19) yields

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{X\le n\le2X}|w_n|
\ll_{\mathcal P}\varepsilon X^{\alpha-1},
\qquad
\sum_{X\le n<2X}|w_{n+1}-w_n|
\ll_{\mathcal P}\varepsilon X^{\alpha-1}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Since `alpha<1`, shrinking `epsilon` depending on `Pcal` makes all `|w_n|` as small as required without changing (11), (12), or the exponent `alpha`.

This is the step that makes finite odd-prime coherence harmless to the countermodel. An odd prime does mix dyadic mantissas, but a **fixed finite** collection of such relations can be absorbed into the finite Euler Green operator (17) before the source is discretized.

## 3. Project the primitive onto the squarefree core coprime to the selected primes

Let

\[
P_{\mathcal P}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p,
\qquad
g_{\mathcal P}(n):=\mu(n)^2\mathbf 1_{(n,P_{\mathcal P})=1}.
\tag{23}
\]

The fixed-prime squarefree counting function has the elementary estimate

\[
Q_{\mathcal P}(x):=\sum_{n\le x}g_{\mathcal P}(n)
=
\delta_{\mathcal P}x+O_{\mathcal P}(\sqrt x),
\tag{24}
\]

where

\[
\delta_{\mathcal P}
=
\frac1{\zeta(2)}
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac{p}{p+1}>0.
\tag{25}
\]

For completeness, (24) follows directly from

\[
\mu(n)^2=\sum_{d^2\mid n}\mu(d)
\tag{26}
\]

combined with the finite inclusion-exclusion for `(n,P_Pcal)=1`: the `O(1)` floor errors are summed over `d<=sqrt(x)` and finitely many divisors of `P_Pcal`, while the tail of the absolutely convergent `d^(-2)` main term is `O(sqrt(x))`.

Choose `epsilon` so small that `|w_n|<=delta_Pcal`, and put

\[
b_n:=\delta_{\mathcal P}^{-1}g_{\mathcal P}(n)w_n,
\qquad
B(N):=\sum_{n\le N}b_n.
\tag{27}
\]

Then `b_n=0` unless `n` is squarefree and coprime to every selected prime, and `|b_n|<=1`.

Let

\[
R_{\mathcal P}(x):=Q_{\mathcal P}(x)-\delta_{\mathcal P}x
=O_{\mathcal P}(\sqrt x).
\tag{28}
\]

Discrete summation by parts gives

\[
B(N)-U(N)
=
\delta_{\mathcal P}^{-1}
\left(
R_{\mathcal P}(N)w_N
+
\sum_{n<N}R_{\mathcal P}(n)(w_n-w_{n+1})
\right).
\tag{29}
\]

On a dyadic block of size `X`, (22) and (28) bound the variation term by

\[
O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(X^{1/2}X^{\alpha-1}\right)
=O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(X^{\alpha-1/2}\right).
\tag{30}
\]

The endpoint term has the same size. Since `alpha>1/2`, summing the dyadic blocks is dominated by the final one, hence

\[
\boxed{
B(N)=U(N)+O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(N^{\alpha-1/2}\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]

Thus imposing the exact squarefree zero pattern and removing every selected prime from the free sign core still preserves the desired primitive profile to an error smaller than square root.

## 4. Round one free sign per finite Euler cube

For every squarefree `m` coprime to `P_Pcal`, independently choose

\[
\eta_m=
\begin{cases}
+1,&\text{with probability }(1+b_m)/2,\\
-1,&\text{with probability }(1-b_m)/2,
\end{cases}
\tag{32}
\]

so that `E eta_m=b_m`. Fix `eta_1=1` deterministically; this alters the centered error by only a bounded amount.

Define

\[
Y(N):=
\sum_{\substack{m\le N\\g_{\mathcal P}(m)=1}}\eta_m.
\tag{33}
\]

For the centered error

\[
W(N):=Y(N)-B(N),
\tag{34}
\]

Hoeffding's elementary exponential-moment bound and Borel--Cantelli give, with probability one,

\[
W(N)=O\!\left(\sqrt{N\log N}\right)
\qquad(N\ge2).
\tag{35}
\]

Fix one realization satisfying (35).

Now extend each free sign through its entire selected Euler cube. For a squarefree integer `n`, write uniquely

\[
n=dm,
\qquad
d\mid P_{\mathcal P},
\qquad
g_{\mathcal P}(m)=1,
\tag{36}
\]

and set

\[
\boxed{
\xi_n:=\mu(d)\eta_m.
}
\tag{37}
\]

Put `xi_n=0` on nonsquarefree integers. Equation (2) is immediate. If `p in Pcal` and `p` does not divide `n`, multiplication by `p` simply toggles that prime inside `d`, so

\[
\xi_{pn}=-\xi_n.
\tag{38}
\]

This proves the exact finite-prime Möbius coherence (3), including `xi_p=-1` because `eta_1=1`.

The summatory function has the exact finite-Euler decomposition

\[
X(N)
=
\sum_{d\mid P_{\mathcal P}}
\mu(d)Y\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Nd\right\rfloor\right).
\tag{39}
\]

Using (31) and (35), and noting that the divisor set of `P_Pcal` is fixed,

\[
X(N)
=
\sum_{d\mid P_{\mathcal P}}
\mu(d)U\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Nd\right\rfloor\right)
+
O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(N^{\alpha-1/2}\right)
+
O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\sqrt{N\log N}\right).
\tag{40}
\]

The local Lipschitz bound (20) replaces each floor by `N/d` at cost `O_Pcal(N^(alpha-1))`. Equations (15) and (18) then give

\[
\sum_{d\mid P_{\mathcal P}}
\mu(d)U(N/d)
=
D_{\mathcal P}U(N)
=A(N).
\tag{41}
\]

Because `alpha<1`, `N^(alpha-1/2)=o(sqrt(N))`, so altogether

\[
\boxed{
X(N)=A(N)+O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\sqrt{N\log N}\right).
}
\tag{42}
\]

Evaluating at (6), where `|A(N_m)| asymp N_m^alpha` and `alpha>1/2`, proves (7).

## 5. The all-integer switched filter remains at RH scale

Write

\[
E(N):=X(N)-A(N).
\tag{43}
\]

Equation (42) gives

\[
E(N)=O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\sqrt{N\log N}\right).
\tag{44}
\]

The selected order is `r(n)=q(n)+1 in {1,4}`. Every `(T-I)^(r(n))` uses only the finitely many values `E(2^k n)` with `0<=k<=4`, and its binomial coefficient mass is uniformly bounded. Combining (11) and (44),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_{q(n)}^X(n)
&=(T-I)^{r(n)}X(n)\\
&=(T-I)^{r(n)}A(n)+(T-I)^{r(n)}E(n)\\
&=O_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\sqrt{n\log n}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]

which proves (5).

The random-cost audit from `FD-213--FD-214` is unchanged: the scheduler still uses only depths `q=0` and `q=3`, so the source-independent root-aligned Rademacher benchmark remains at exponent `H`. Adding finite Euler-factor coherence to the **countermodel class** does not alter that observation-family calculation.

## 6. What this closes, and the remaining arithmetic boundary

`FD-214` left open the possibility that even a small amount of genuine Möbius multiplicative coherence might couple the dyadic mantissas strongly enough to destroy the profile-lifted Floquet mode. Equations (17)--(45) show that no fixed finite amount can do so.

The conclusion is stronger than the prime `2` test. It includes any prescribed collection of odd primes, despite the fact that odd-prime dilation does move points between dyadic mantissas. The reason is that finitely many such constraints admit the convergent finite-prime Green operator (17), which preconditions the desired profile before squarefree projection and rounding.

The dependence on `Pcal` is essential. Neither the constants in (19), (20), (22), (24), nor the small scale `epsilon` are uniform as the selected prime set grows. The argument therefore does **not** construct one source satisfying (3) for all primes, and it does not show that full Möbius multiplicativity is compatible with the blind mode. Precisely that global limit remains outside the result.

Consequently the surviving arithmetic gate is now narrower: a proof specialized to Möbius must use multiplicative compatibility across a prime set whose complexity grows with scale, or an equivalent global relation that cannot be absorbed into a fixed finite Euler operator. A source-independent observation that genuinely mixes dyadic mantissas remains the other live escape.

This also differs from the earlier fixed-horizon squarefree-sign constructions `FD-007--FD-008` and finite-prime ancestry relaxations in the GCD-energy line. Here one coherent infinite ternary source satisfies an all-integer, scale-varying switched root-filter estimate while obeying any prescribed finite set of exact Möbius Euler factors and retaining super-square-root summatory growth.

## 7. Prior-art and falsification boundary

Rademacher random multiplicative functions are classically defined on squarefree integers by assigning independent signs at the primes and extending multiplicatively; they are standard probabilistic models for Möbius. That literature clarifies the remaining distinction here: the present free signs `eta_m` live on the `Pcal`-coprime squarefree **integers**, not on their prime factors, so multiplicativity outside `Pcal` is deliberately not imposed. No theorem about random multiplicative functions is used in the proof.

The proof uses only the explicit `FD-213` profile, the elementary squarefree counting estimate with finitely many excluded primes, finite Euler-product algebra, and independent bounded-variable concentration. No new external theorem is load-bearing, so no durable `SOURCES.md` anchor is required.

The construction has four exact audit points.

First, verify the finite Euler inversion (18): for every selected prime, `(I-S_p)` cancels one geometric summation index in (17), and the factors commute.

Second, verify the regularity transfer (22). The factor `s^(-alpha)` is essential; its finite-prime semigroup sum converges because `alpha>0`. Each scaled profile crosses at most one dyadic breakpoint on a factor-two interval, so no hidden accumulation of boundary jumps is permitted.

Third, verify the squarefree-core discrepancy (24) and the summation-by-parts error (31). The exponent `alpha-1/2` must remain below `1/2`; this uses the independent fact `alpha<1`.

Fourth, verify the exact Euler-cube lifting (36)--(39). Every squarefree integer has one and only one decomposition into a divisor of `P_Pcal` and a squarefree factor coprime to it. This uniqueness is what makes all relations (3) exact rather than asymptotic.

A numerical sanity check for `Pcal={2,3}` evaluated the finite-prime Green sum (17) at representative real arguments and recovered `D_Pcal U=A` to double-precision roundoff. This is only a check of the algebra; the proof of (18) is exact.

The result must not be quoted as evidence that Möbius itself admits the switched blind mode. Its precise content is the finite-versus-global boundary: **every fixed finite Euler-factor truncation of Möbius multiplicativity still admits the obstruction, while full multiplicative compatibility remains untested.**
