# FD-111 — finite local polynomial prime nonlinearities split into smooth even and invertible odd sources

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED + PHYSICAL-COEFFICIENT-LAW + FINITE-LOCAL-POLYNOMIAL-CLASSIFICATION + PARITY-EULER-FACTORIZATION + ODD-FLOOR-INVERTIBILITY + EVEN-SQUARE-ROOT-REMAINDER + NONLINEAR-ROUTE-RESTRICTION`.

`FD-109` and `FD-110` show that raw higher-order prime incidence, finite Boolean divisibility statistics, and least-prime-factor ordering remain inside the commuting dilation algebra generated by the physical source

\[
a(n):=\Delta\mathcal H(n)
=\frac{\mu(\operatorname{rad}n)\varphi(\operatorname{rad}n)}n.
\tag{1}
\]

They deliberately leave **nonlinear products before summation** as a possible escape. The first natural class of such nonlinearities can now also be classified exactly: take finitely many fixed integer shifts `d_1,...,d_m`, form any polynomial in the local values

\[
a(d_1n),\ldots,a(d_mn),
\tag{2}
\]

and then sum over `n<=X`. Every such observable is a finite linear combination of commuting divisibility transforms of the power sources

\[
\mathcal H_k(X):=\sum_{n\le X}a(n)^k,
\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

The power sources themselves have a sharp parity dichotomy. For every fixed odd `k>=1`, `mathcal H_k` and the original source `mathcal H=mathcal H_1` are exact mutually invertible floor-convolution transforms whose kernels are absolutely convergent on every half-plane `Re(s)>sigma>0`. In particular

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_k(X)=O(X^\sigma)
\iff
\mathcal H(X)=O(X^\sigma)
\qquad(\sigma>0,\ k\ \text{odd}),}
\tag{4}
\]

so every odd power source has the same zeta-zero power frontier `Theta` as `mathcal H`.

For every fixed even `k>=2`, there is an explicit positive constant `C_k` such that, unconditionally,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_k(X)=C_kX+O_{k,\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})
\qquad(\varepsilon>0).}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus after its deterministic linear background is removed, an even local power cannot carry a false-RH fluctuation of exponent `Theta>1/2`.

Consequently, **finite-degree local polynomial prime nonlinearities do not create an independent power-scale source channel**. Their odd-degree pieces are invertible re-encodings of the existing source; their even-degree pieces are a smooth linear background plus a square-root-scale remainder. Fixed divisibility filters do not change this conclusion because they act through the same commuting floor-dilation resolvents.

This closes the most direct finite-local interpretation of the “nonlinear products before summation” escape left by `FD-109`--`FD-110`. It does not close nonlinear functionals formed **after** cumulative summation, degree/support growing with the horizon, cross-horizon products, or refinement observables not generated by finitely many local prime shifts. Those remain genuinely different operations.

## 1. Every fixed multiplicative shift is one base coefficient times a Boolean prime factor

Let `d>=1` be fixed. For each prime power `p^e || d`, repeated use of the physical one-prime covariance gives

\[
\frac{a(p^en)}{a(n)}
=
\begin{cases}
-(1-p^{-1})p^{-(e-1)},&p\nmid n,\\[1mm]
p^{-e},&p\mid n.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]

The ratio therefore depends only on the Boolean bit `x_p(n)=1_(p|n)`, not on the full `p`-adic valuation of `n`. Hence for every fixed `d` there is an explicit function `F_d` on the finite Boolean cube supported on the primes dividing `d` such that

\[
\boxed{a(dn)=a(n)F_d((x_p(n))_{p\mid d}).}
\tag{7}
\]

Now expand any polynomial `P(a(d_1n),...,a(d_mn))` into monomials. A monomial of total degree `k` has the form

\[
a(n)^kF((x_p(n))_{p\in S})
\tag{8}
\]

for one finite prime set `S`. Every function on `{0,1}^S` has the unique multilinear expansion

\[
F(x)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}c_T\prod_{p\in T}x_p.
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore it is enough to classify, for each fixed `k` and finite `T`,

\[
D_{T,k}(X)
:=
\sum_{\substack{n\le X\\p\mid n\ \forall p\in T}}a(n)^k.
\tag{10}
\]

This reduction is exact. No asymptotic approximation, independence heuristic, or linearization of the polynomial has occurred.

## 2. Every power source has its own commuting prime resolvents

For a prime `p`, define

\[
\alpha_{p,k}:=(-1)^k(1-p^{-1})^k,
\qquad
\beta_{p,k}:=p^{-k},
\qquad
c_{p,k}:=\beta_{p,k}-\alpha_{p,k}.
\tag{11}
\]

Then

\[
a(pm)^k
=
\begin{cases}
\alpha_{p,k}a(m)^k,&p\nmid m,\\[1mm]
\beta_{p,k}a(m)^k,&p\mid m.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]

Let `T_pF(X)=F(floor(X/p))`. If `p notin T`, writing every integer counted by `D_(T union {p},k)(X)` as `pm` gives

\[
D_{T\cup\{p\},k}
=
\alpha_{p,k}T_pD_{T,k}
+c_{p,k}T_pD_{T\cup\{p\},k}.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus exactly

\[
\boxed{
D_{T\cup\{p\},k}
=
\alpha_{p,k}T_p(I-c_{p,k}T_p)^{-1}D_{T,k}.}
\tag{14}
\]

At a fixed horizon the inverse is only a finite geometric sum because repeated floor dilation eventually reaches zero. Since `T_pT_q=T_qT_p=T_(pq)`, all these generalized resolvents commute. Starting from `D_(emptyset,k)=mathcal H_k`,

\[
\boxed{
D_{T,k}
=
\prod_{p\in T}
\left[
\alpha_{p,k}T_p(I-c_{p,k}T_p)^{-1}
\right]\mathcal H_k.}
\tag{15}
\]

Equations (8)--(15) prove the finite-local polynomial classification: nonlinear multiplication before summation changes the scalar source from `mathcal H` to one of the finitely many `mathcal H_k`, but the prime-incidence geometry remains commuting and one-dimensional after that source choice.

For `k=1`, `c_(p,1)=1` and (14) is exactly the resolvent of `FD-109`. Thus the new algebra is a genuine extension of the linear classification rather than a different normalization.

## 3. Euler factors split the power sources by parity

The multiplicativity of `a` and

\[
a(p^e)=-\frac{p-1}{p^e}\qquad(e\ge1)
\tag{16}
\]

give, for `Re(s)>1`,

\[
A_k(s)
:=
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a(n)^k}{n^s}
=
\prod_pA_{k,p}(s),
\tag{17}
\]

with

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{k,p}(s)
&=1+(-1)^k\frac{(p-1)^k}{p^{s+k}-1}\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{1-c_{p,k}p^{-s}}
{1-p^{-(s+k)}}.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

The last identity is also a useful consistency check on the same coefficient `c_(p,k)` appearing in the finite-horizon resolvent (14).

If `k` is odd, then

\[
c_{p,k}
=p^{-k}+(1-p^{-1})^k
=1-\frac{k}{p}+O_k(p^{-2}),
\tag{19}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{A_k(s)=\frac{G_k(s)}{\zeta(s)}}
\qquad(k\ \text{odd}),
\tag{20}
\]

where

\[
G_{k,p}(s)
:=
\frac{A_{k,p}(s)}{1-p^{-s}}
\tag{21}
\]

has an absolutely convergent Euler product, as does `G_k(s)^{-1}`, on every half-plane `Re(s)>=sigma>0`. Indeed the local absolute deviation from `1` is `O_(k,sigma)(p^{-1-sigma})`; the denominators are nonzero because `0<c_(p,k)<=1` and `|p^{-s}|<1` there.

If `k` is even, then

\[
c_{p,k}
=p^{-k}-(1-p^{-1})^k
=-1+\frac{k}{p}+O_k(p^{-2}),
\tag{22}
\]

and instead

\[
\boxed{A_k(s)=\zeta(s)G_k(s)}
\qquad(k\ \text{even}),
\tag{23}
\]

where now

\[
G_{k,p}(s):=A_{k,p}(s)(1-p^{-s}).
\tag{24}
\]

Both `G_k` and `G_k^{-1}` have absolutely convergent Dirichlet series on every half-plane `Re(s)>1/2`; locally the deviation from `1` is

\[
O_{k,\sigma}(p^{-\sigma-1}+p^{-2\sigma}).
\tag{25}
\]

No analytic continuation theorem is being imported here. The factorizations follow termwise in the half-plane of absolute convergence, and the stated larger domains for `G_k` follow directly from the Euler factors.

## 4. Odd powers are exact invertible re-encodings of the original source

For odd `k`, compare (20) with the already established physical identity

\[
A_1(s)=\frac{\zeta(s+1)}{\zeta(s)}.
\tag{26}
\]

Their ratio is

\[
K_k(s):=\frac{A_k(s)}{A_1(s)}
=\frac{G_k(s)}{\zeta(s+1)}.
\tag{27}
\]

Both `K_k` and `K_k^{-1}` have absolutely convergent Dirichlet series on every `Re(s)>sigma>0`; write their coefficients as `kappa_k(n)` and `lambda_k(n)`. Dirichlet convolution then gives the exact coefficient identities

\[
a(n)^k=(\kappa_k*a)(n),
\qquad
a(n)=(\lambda_k*a^k)(n).
\tag{28}
\]

Summing to `X` and exchanging the divisor sums yields mutually inverse floor transforms

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_k(X)
=
\sum_{d\le X}\kappa_k(d)
\mathcal H\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Xd\right\rfloor\right),}
\tag{29}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(X)
=
\sum_{d\le X}\lambda_k(d)
\mathcal H_k\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Xd\right\rfloor\right).}
\tag{30}
\]

Fix any `sigma>0`. Absolute convergence gives

\[
\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{|\kappa_k(d)|+|\lambda_k(d)|}{d^\sigma}<\infty.
\tag{31}
\]

Equations (29)--(31) therefore prove (4). Combining with `FD-046`, every odd power source satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{X\to\infty}
\frac{\log(1+|\mathcal H_k(X)|)}{\log X}
=\Theta.}
\tag{32}
\]

This is stronger than saying that odd moments happen to contain a factor `1/zeta(s)`: the full summatory sequences `mathcal H_k` and `mathcal H` determine one another through explicit bounded floor-convolution operators on every positive polynomial-weight space.

## 5. Even powers are a linear mean plus a square-root-scale remainder

For even `k`, write

\[
G_k(s)=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{g_k(d)}{d^s}.
\tag{33}
\]

Equation (23) means

\[
a(n)^k=(1*g_k)(n).
\tag{34}
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal H_k(X)
=
\sum_{d\le X}g_k(d)
\left\lfloor\frac Xd\right\rfloor.
\tag{35}
\]

Put

\[
C_k:=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{g_k(d)}d=G_k(1).
\tag{36}
\]

Every local factor in (17) is positive at `s=1`, so `C_k>0`. For any fixed `sigma>1/2`, absolute convergence of `G_k` gives

\[
\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{|g_k(d)|}{d^\sigma}<\infty.
\tag{37}
\]

Using `|floor(X/d)-X/d|<=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal H_k(X)-C_kX|
&\le
\sum_{d\le X}|g_k(d)|
+X\sum_{d>X}\frac{|g_k(d)|}{d}\\
&\ll_{k,\sigma}X^\sigma.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]

Taking `sigma=1/2+epsilon` proves (5). This is unconditional and requires no zero-free region, PNT error term, or RH input.

The same conclusion survives every fixed divisibility filter from (15): applying finitely many fixed floor dilations and finite geometric resolvents to a linear function plus `O(X^(1/2+epsilon))` produces another explicit linear function plus an error of the same power. Thus the even branch cannot generate a new fluctuation of exponent `Theta>1/2` merely by attaching finitely many prime-incidence labels.

## 6. Stress tests and the exact boundary of the closure

The first algebraic stress test is `k=1`. Equations (18) and (20) reduce to

\[
A_1(s)=\frac{\zeta(s+1)}{\zeta(s)},
\qquad
c_{p,1}=1,
\tag{39}
\]

recovering both the known physical Dirichlet series and the exact one-prime resolvent of `FD-108`--`FD-109`.

The second is finite exact evaluation. Using rational arithmetic, direct sums (10) and the iterated operator formula (15) agree for `k=1,2,3,4`, prime sets `{2,3}`, `{3,5}`, `{2,3,5}`, and horizons `20,57,101`. This computation is not part of the proof; a mismatch would falsify the operator derivation, while (12)--(15) prove it symbolically.

The third is an adversarial scope check. The theorem does **not** say that every nonlinear terminal observable is reducible. It requires a fixed polynomial in finitely many fixed multiplicative shifts before the final linear summation over `n`. It does not cover:

- products such as `mathcal H(X/p)mathcal H(X/q)` formed after summation;
- a polynomial degree or prime support growing with `X`;
- nonpolynomial thresholding, maxima, signs, or adaptive selection based on cumulative packet values;
- correlations at two genuinely different horizons;
- ordered refinement vectors whose coordinates are not finite Boolean functions of local prime divisibility.

Those distinctions are structural, not technical. In particular, (29)--(30) show why merely replacing `a(n)` by an odd local power cannot create a second false-RH power source: it is losslessly recoverable from the first. The surviving nonlinear route must therefore exploit **where summation, horizon change, or growing complexity enters**, rather than only multiply finitely many local coefficient samples before summation.
