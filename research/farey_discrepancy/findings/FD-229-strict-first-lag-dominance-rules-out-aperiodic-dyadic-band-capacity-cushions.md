- **Depends on:** FD-228, FD-227, FD-226, FD-225, FD-212
- **Classification:** `EXACT-DERIVED + NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION + DYADIC-TOEPLITZ-KERNEL + STRICT-FIRST-LAG-DOMINANCE + SWITCHED-NORMALIZED-STABILITY + APERIODIC-BAND-CAPACITY-VETO`

## Claim

FD-228 shows that no **fixed-period** order-one oscillation among dyadic reservoir bands can hide the quadratic response generated by a capacity-scale divisor cushion. Periodicity is not the real obstruction. The full dyadic-band response is a causal convolution whose first lag strictly dominates the entire remaining tail, so the same veto holds for arbitrary bounded band amplitudes, including growing-period and aperiodic ones.

Let

\[
(\mathscr A b)(m)
=
\sum_{d\le m} b_d M\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac md\right\rfloor\right)
\tag{1}
\]

be the exact divisor-response operator from FD-225. Let `(a_j)` be any bounded complex sequence, set `a_j=0` for `j<0`, and suppose

\[
\boxed{
 b_d=d\,a_{\lfloor\log_2 d\rfloor}+e_d,
 \qquad e_d=o(d).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Write

\[
y_j=(\mathscr A b)(2^j),
\qquad
r_j=q_j+1=(1,4,4,1,4,4,\ldots),
\tag{3}
\]

with the switched schedule of FD-212. If

\[
\boxed{
(E-I)^{r_j}y_j=o(4^j)
}
\tag{4}
\]

for all sufficiently large `j`, where `(Ey)_j=y_{j+1}`, then

\[
\boxed{a_j\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus a dyadic-band cushion that stays an order-one fraction of its natural linear capacity envelope cannot be switched-null at quadratic scale. No periodicity assumption is needed. In particular, replacing the fixed period of FD-228 by a period `P=P(D)->infinity`, or by an arbitrary nonperiodic scalar amplitude on each whole dyadic band, does not evade the obstruction as long as those amplitudes remain uniformly comparable to their own common scale on a growing interior range.

The proof has two independent pieces. First, the divisor/Mertens response has an asymptotic causal Toeplitz kernel whose first coefficient is larger in modulus than the sum of all later coefficients. Second, after division by `4^j`, the `(1,4,4)` switched recurrence is strictly stable. Together they force every asymptotically invisible band amplitude to decay.

## 1. Arbitrary band amplitudes have a causal dyadic Toeplitz response

Partition the positive integers into dyadic bands

\[
B_k=[2^k,2^{k+1})\cap\mathbb N.
\tag{6}
\]

At `m=2^j`, the complete band `B_(j-ell)`, `ell>=1`, contributes

\[
H_{j,\ell}
:=
4^{-j}
\sum_{d\in B_{j-\ell}}
 d\,M\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2^j}{d}\right\rfloor\right).
\tag{7}
\]

For each fixed `ell`, the Riemann-sum limit is

\[
\boxed{
H_{j,\ell}\longrightarrow h_\ell
:=
4^{-\ell}
\int_1^2
 t\,M\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{t}\right\rfloor\right)dt.
}
\tag{8}
\]

The convergence uses only that the integrand is bounded and piecewise continuous. More importantly, the trivial estimate `|M(n)|<=n` gives, uniformly in `j` and `ell`,

\[
|H_{j,\ell}|\le2^{-\ell},
\qquad
|h_\ell|\le2^{-\ell}.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed each summand in (7) has modulus at most `2^j`, there are `2^(j-ell)` points in the band, and normalization by `4^j` gives `2^(-ell)`. The incomplete top band consists only of the endpoint `d=2^j`, whose normalized contribution is `O(2^(-j))`.

Therefore, for every bounded amplitude sequence `(a_j)`, dominated tail truncation followed by the fixed-`ell` limits yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(\mathscr A(d\,a_{\lfloor\log_2d\rfloor}))(2^j)}{4^j}
=
\sum_{\ell\ge1}h_\ell a_{j-\ell}+o(1).
}
\tag{10}
\]

The perturbation in (2) remains negligible. If `E(x)=sum_(d<=x)e_d`, then `E(x)=o(x^2)`. Expanding the Mertens kernel as in FD-228 gives

\[
\mathscr A e(m)
=
\sum_{a\le m}\mu(a)
E\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac ma\right\rfloor\right)
=o(m^2),
\tag{11}
\]

by splitting at `m/a>=Y` and using the summability of `sum a^(-2)`. Hence, with

\[
u_j:=4^{-j}y_j,
\qquad
v_j:=\sum_{\ell\ge1}h_\ell a_{j-\ell},
\tag{12}
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{u_j-v_j\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{13}
\]

This is the nonperiodic replacement for the finite Fourier multiplier calculation in FD-228.

## 2. The first lag strictly dominates the whole kernel tail

The first coefficient is exact. Except for the measure-zero endpoint `t=1`, `floor(2/t)=1`, so

\[
\boxed{h_1=\frac14\int_1^2 t\,dt=\frac38.}
\tag{14}
\]

The next coefficient is also exact. On `1<t<=4/3`, `floor(4/t)=3` and `M(3)=-1`; on `4/3<t<2`, `floor(4/t)=2` and `M(2)=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{h_2=-\frac7{288}.}
\tag{15}
\]

For `ell=3`, the relevant Mertens values lie between indices `4` and `8`, where `|M|<=2`, and hence

\[
|h_3|\le\frac3{64}.
\tag{16}
\]

For `ell=4`, the indices lie between `8` and `16`, where `|M|<=3`, giving

\[
|h_4|\le\frac9{512}.
\tag{17}
\]

For the remaining tail, (9) gives

\[
\sum_{\ell\ge5}|h_\ell|
\le
\sum_{\ell\ge5}2^{-\ell}
=\frac1{16}.
\tag{18}
\]

Combining (15)--(18),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\ell\ge2}|h_\ell|
\le
\frac7{288}+\frac3{64}+\frac9{512}+\frac1{16}
=
\frac{697}{4608}
<
\frac38
=|h_1|.
}
\tag{19}
\]

The strict margin is `1031/4608`. This is stronger than the unit-circle nonvanishing used in FD-228: it gives a direct causal inversion mechanism and makes no stationarity or periodicity assumption on `(a_j)`.

In particular, if `v_j->0` and `(a_j)` is bounded, then `a_j->0`. To see this, put

\[
L=\limsup_{j\to\infty}|a_j|.
\tag{20}
\]

Choose `n_k` with `|a_(n_k)|->L`. From

\[
h_1a_{n_k}
=
v_{n_k+1}
-
\sum_{\ell\ge2}h_\ell a_{n_k+1-\ell}
\tag{21}
\]

and boundedness of `(a_j)`, first truncate the absolutely summable tail and then let `k->infinity`. This gives

\[
|h_1|L
\le
\left(\sum_{\ell\ge2}|h_\ell|\right)L.
\tag{22}
\]

The strict inequality (19) forces `L=0`.

## 3. Switched subquadratic response forces the normalized response to zero

Condition (4) can be written directly in terms of `u_j`. Since `y_(j+s)=4^(j+s)u_(j+s)`,

\[
4^{-j}(E-I)^{r_j}y_j
=
(4E-I)^{r_j}u_j
=o(1).
\tag{23}
\]

The normalized switched system is strictly stable. FD-228 writes the exact three-step state matrix for the homogeneous `(1,4,4)` recurrence as

\[
\mathcal M=
\begin{pmatrix}
-10&15&-4\\
-2&4&-1\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{24}
\]

whose characteristic polynomial is

\[
(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2+7\lambda+1).
\tag{25}
\]

Advancing one schedule period advances every dyadic index by three, so normalization by `4^j` conjugates the period map to a matrix similar to `\mathcal M/64`. Its spectral radius is

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\rm norm}
=
\frac{7+3\sqrt5}{128}
\approx0.1071
<1.
}
\tag{26}
\]

The `o(1)` right side in (23) becomes an `o(1)` forcing of this finite-dimensional period recurrence. A stable linear recurrence with forcing tending to zero has state tending to zero, hence

\[
\boxed{u_j\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{27}
\]

for all three schedule phases. Combining (13) and (27) gives `v_j->0`; Section 2 then gives (5).

This is the step that removes the fixed-period hypothesis. FD-228 compared a finite Fourier mode of growth `4^j` against the switched Floquet radius. Here the normalized switched dynamics first kills **every bounded response profile**, and the strict causal dominance of the divisor kernel then transfers that decay back to **every bounded band-amplitude profile**.

## 4. Growing periods and aperiodic finite windows do not restore capacity-scale slack

The infinite statement has a uniform finite-window consequence. Fix constants `0<eta<=A<infinity`. Consider any sequence of increasingly deep divisor ranges and any sequence of scalar-per-band profiles whose amplitudes, after normalization by their own common cushion scale, satisfy

\[
\eta\le |a_k|\le A
\tag{28}
\]

through an interior block of dyadic bands whose distance from both ends tends to infinity. Suppose all switched residuals on that interior block were `o(4^j)` relative to the same cushion scale.

Center each block at an interior band and pass to a subsequence with a fixed scheduler phase modulo three. Boundedness gives a diagonal subsequential limit on every fixed relative band window. The kernel tail (9) makes bands receding to the left negligible, while the fixed row orders need only finitely many future samples. The limit is therefore a bounded bi-infinite band profile with `|a_j|>=eta` and exact switched-null normalized response on every index. Forward stability from Section 3 gives `u_j->0`, and the first-lag dominance argument of Section 2 gives `a_j->0`, a contradiction.

Consequently, allowing a period `P=P(D)` that grows with the block depth cannot by itself evade FD-228. Neither can a completely aperiodic choice of one scalar amplitude per dyadic band. Any such capacity-comparable family must leak at its own natural quadratic response scale somewhere in every sufficiently long interior block.

This compactness consequence is qualitative: it does not supply a sharp finite-`D` lower constant or identify the leaking row. That quantitative optimization remains separate.

## 5. Source-side meaning for the FD-226 reservoirs

FD-226 gives scale-adapted prime reservoirs `I_d^*` with exact quotient table

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{mN}{r}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lfloor\frac md\right\rfloor
\qquad
(r\in I_d^*),
\tag{29}
\]

and capacity

\[
|I_d^*\cap\mathbb P|
=\left(\frac14+o(1)\right)
B_Nd,
\qquad
B_N=\frac{N}{D\log N}.
\tag{30}
\]

Thus a source-side fill whose leading occupancy is one scalar fraction per dyadic `d`-band has the form

\[
k_{N,d}
=
B_N\lambda_N\,d\,
 a_{N,\lfloor\log_2d\rfloor}
+o(B_N\lambda_Nd),
\tag{31}
\]

within the available prime capacity. The exact quotient table means that, after the harmless common factor `2B_Nlambda_N` from prime sign flips is removed, its response is precisely the operator (1). The obstruction is therefore source-side realizable; it is not merely a transform-space statement.

Importantly, `lambda_N` may tend to zero. One first normalizes by that cushion scale. What is ruled out is a profile whose per-band amplitudes remain uniformly comparable after normalization. Hence shrinking the overall fraction of capacity does not by itself create a growing-period escape.

This closes the two explicit extensions left open by FD-228 -- `P=P(D)` and nonperiodic **bandwise scalar** oscillation -- in the capacity-comparable regime. It does **not** rule out structure inside individual dyadic bands, profiles whose relative slack itself degenerates on a growing set of bands, or a genuinely cellwise switched-null vector that cannot be approximated by one amplitude per band. Those are now the natural remaining escape routes from the FD-226 common-cushion bottleneck.

## 6. Prior-art boundary and verdict

Causal convolution equations and strict diagonal-dominance arguments are classical, as are Fourier/Wiener approaches to invertibility of convolution operators. No general novelty is claimed for those mechanisms. No external theorem is load-bearing here: the only inversion input is the explicit elementary estimate (19), and the switched stability matrix is already derived in FD-228.

The repository-specific content is the identification of the exact asymptotic dyadic Toeplitz kernel (8) for the FD-225 divisor/Mertens response, the strict first-lag margin (19), and its combination with the normalized `(1,4,4)` switched contraction to remove periodicity from the reservoir-capacity veto. No new durable source anchor is therefore required in `SOURCES.md`.

**Verdict.** The fixed-period qualification in FD-228 is not the real limitation. Any bounded scalar-per-dyadic-band cushion that retains a nonvanishing fraction of its own scale over growing interior ranges remains visible to the switched root rows. A successful capacity-adapted cushion must now exploit finer **within-band** structure or allow its relative slack to collapse substantially across the range; simply increasing the log-period or abandoning periodicity is insufficient.
