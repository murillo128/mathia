# Farey-discrepancy research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Farey-discrepancy intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Bridge positive anchor exposure to actual Möbius orientation across a genuinely informative horizon hierarchy

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-022-polylogarithmic-horizon-bundles-leave-dense-orientation-kernels`.

FD-145--FD-162 reduce finite-`L^q` binary ancestry recovery to anchored cut exposure and show that extra parents, finite auxiliary vocabularies, sparse holes, terminal-core migration and high-prime perturbations of an exhausting prime anchor do not remove the hidden-fibre cut after intrinsic divisor/principal closure. The first omitted anchor prime controls a growing squarefree closure volume.

FD-163 closes the remaining low-prime-hole geometric escape under bounded architecture resources. If anchored expansion stays uniformly positive, parent distortion and normalized leakage stay bounded, and a nontrivial auxiliary core persists, then only finitely many anchor primes can be relevant below the principal-closure cutoff. The corresponding prime-avoidance anchor therefore has a uniform positive density. A coercive bounded-resource ancestry architecture cannot make the anchor exposure vanish merely by keeping one fixed small prime outside the anchor.

FD-164 shows why that geometric closure is still not source justification. At a single Farey horizon `T`, the complete discrepancy field is exactly equivalent to the floor-quotient Mertens staircase. Within the exact squarefree-unit coefficient class one can flip paired Möbius signs inside finitely many macroscopic quotient shells, preserve every floor-quotient cumulative value and hence preserve the entire same-horizon Farey discrepancy function exactly, while changing an arbitrarily large fraction of any positive-density squarefree family.

FD-165 makes the finite-bundle cross-horizon boundary quantitative. For an arbitrary horizon family `H_T` of size `J_T`, one common squarefree-unit source `a_T` can match the physical Mertens values at every floor quotient contributed by every horizon in the family while changing a fraction

`1 - beta - O_beta(J_T M_beta(T)/T) - o_beta(1)`

of the squarefree orientations, where `M_beta(T)=max_(beta T <= x <= T)|M(x)|`. Thus the exact sufficient regime for dense finite-cutoff ambiguity is `J_T M_beta(T)=o(T)`. Davenport's unconditional log-power cancellation makes every polylogarithmically large arbitrary horizon bundle fall inside that regime.

FD-166 closes the exact **permanent-source** version of that question. Every quotient set `Q_H` contains all consecutive integers through `floor(sqrt(H))`; by FD-117, one exact complete Farey field therefore fixes `A(r)=M(r)` on that whole prefix and hence fixes the source coefficients `a(r)=mu(r)` there by differencing. If one fixed source matches complete fields on any unbounded set of horizons, however sparse or non-nested, those square-root prefixes exhaust every finite coordinate and the source is exactly Möbius. Equivalently, a permanent source whose first deviation is at `n_0` cannot match any complete physical field once `H>=n_0^2`.

The dense kernels of FD-164--FD-165 are therefore finite-cutoff but non-compact in source coordinates: their altered orientations must drift to infinity as the outer cutoff grows. There is no exact critical horizon density for one permanent source. The live bridge has moved to **stability and partial observation**: quantify how approximate complete-field agreement propagates through the FD-117 inversion to a growing coefficient prefix, determine how much of the field can be discarded before the prefix rigidity disappears, and test whether source-native multiplicative or Farey-order structure supplies useful orientation information without simply reconstructing the whole Möbius staircase.

The strongest immediate question is quantitative permanent-source stability. If a synthetic complete field at horizon `H` differs from the physical field by `epsilon_H` in a natural norm, what norm and scale of control on `A(r)-M(r)` or `a(r)-mu(r)` can be forced for `r<=sqrt(H)`? The answer must account for the conditioning of the exact FD-117 Fourier/sector inversion; exact algebraic recoverability alone does not imply a useful noisy estimate.

## Apply the information-budget gate only after orientation survives the horizon kernel

FD-144 remains a later lower bound: even after anchoring and quantitative cut exposure are solved, `o(r_T)` arbitrary source-independent normalized ancestry tests can still be fooled along a subsequence. FD-164--FD-166 sharpen the ordering of obligations. Before counting source bits, first quotient out the kernel of the **declared observation family** and distinguish finite-cutoff controls from one permanent source. Exact complete fields on an unbounded horizon set have no permanent-source kernel, whereas finite bundles retain a large high-coordinate kernel.

Audit future ancestry proposals in the order **anchor geometry and mass -> parent distortion/leakage -> intrinsic closure -> positive exposure -> observation-family orientation kernel -> permanent-source/arithmetic coherence -> information and precision budget -> Franel--Landau destination**.

## Keep coefficient coercivity and nonlocal destination coercivity separate

The current line has a sharp architecture boundary but not an RH proof. FD-163 gives a positive-density coefficient surface; FD-164--FD-165 prove that one horizon and even arbitrary polylogarithmic finite bundles do not orient that surface, while FD-166 shows that exact complete-field agreement across an unbounded hierarchy does orient every fixed coefficient. The remaining useful problem is no longer exact uniqueness but quantitative stability, economical partial observation, and genuinely source-native cancellation. Even a successful coefficient-recovery statement must still show that the recovered information controls the RH-equivalent discrepancy norm; neither step follows from the other.
