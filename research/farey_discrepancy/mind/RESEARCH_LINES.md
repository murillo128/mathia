## Separate source-side rigidity from the exact observation and recovery geometry

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-038-fixed-root-filter-families-have-an-annular-spectral-dichotomy`.

FD-187--FD-194 establish the source and observation gates. Prime-extension energy is a genuine Möbius relation currency, while dyadic signed-Fourier observation has a sharp geometric square-root threshold: below the critical band permanent quotient holes remain, while at and above it cumulative source recovery has an explicit uniformly stable inverse. The transition is one of geometry and injectivity, not a conditioning pathology of the raw coordinate map.

FD-195--FD-201 show why stable coordinate recovery does not automatically survive quadratic compression. Classical Franel/Sobolev energies price the same geometric witnesses differently from the raw inverse; on reciprocal-prime shells the RH-facing positive diagonal budget is substantially more coercive than the square-root Mertens target. Fixed-mode cross-horizon differences do contain new Möbius interval increments, but independently squaring the shell rows restores the `H^(3/2+o(1))` random-multiplicative tariff.

FD-202 gives the first exact escape. Taking adjacent-prime differences before a positive quadratic norm collapses overlapping long Mertens intervals to endpoint slabs, and the shell-normalized path energy has squarefree-Rademacher mean `Theta(H)`. FD-203 separates kernel removal from inverse conditioning, and FD-204 replaces the high-resistance path by a lowbit tree whose anchored point-evaluation resistance is only logarithmic while each physical source boundary is reused at most once per dyadic scale.

FD-205 shows that lowbit shell sensors recover global Mertens values through quotient-coordinate telescoping. FD-206 then identifies a sharper obstruction: on the source-independent horizons `H_n=n p_n`, where `p_n` is the largest prime at most `n`, the positive root anchor is exactly

`S_(2,H_n)(p_n)=M(2n)-M(n)`.

A square-root estimate for this scalar increment is already RH-equivalent by binary-prefix reconstruction. Thus favorable inverse geometry and a near-critical random benchmark do not make the proposed positive energy easier when one standalone nonnegative coordinate already contains the destination-complete problem.

FD-207--FD-209 test signed dyadic repair. Every fixed depth remains RH-complete. Growing a common depth `q=o(log X)` preserves the critical-power random benchmark, but the exact negative-binomial binary inverse still costs only a subpower factor on square-root scale windows. Common-depth growth therefore does not create an intermediate theorem surface.

FD-210--FD-211 close the fixed finite **general polynomial-filter** alternative as well. Let `Delta(n)=A(2n)-A(n)` for a bounded source and let `P_1(T),...,P_r(T)` be fixed root filters. If their gcd has a zero in the physical annulus

`sqrt(2)<|rho|<=2`,

the family has a bounded-alphabet super-square-root blind mode. If the gcd has no such zero, pointwise square-root-plus-epsilon control of the whole family forces the same bound for `A(N)` for every bounded source. For Möbius, an annulus-free fixed family is therefore RH-complete. The fixed collective question has an exact dichotomy: common annular root means genuine blindness; no common annular root means destination completeness.

The live signed-filter question is consequently no longer whether another **fixed** family can avoid both failures. It must change with scale, grow in degree, weaken the observation norm, lose pointwise coherence along binary ancestors, or use another non-fixed/non-polynomial structure. The scale-dependent diagonal depth `q=q(n)` remains a precise opening because FD-209 controls only one common depth on a whole scale window; a genuinely collective inequality is also open only when it cannot be reduced to one fixed finite polynomial family.

## Apply information budgets only after fixing source class, observation family and destination norm

The same Farey representation can be complete, target-rigid or permanently noninjective depending on the source class, observation family and destination norm. The current sequence separates quotient coverage, raw inverse conditioning, diagonal quadratic coercivity, cross-horizon source information, covariance-preserving signed projection, kernel dimension, graph resistance, source-overlap cost, quotient-coordinate recovery, destination-complete scalar anchors and now the spectral invertibility class of fixed signed filters.

FD-206--FD-211 add a required audit: a favorable random benchmark and improved inverse geometry do not imply analytic leverage if a positive coordinate, a common-depth dyadic transform or an annulus-free fixed filter family already admits a square-root-cost physical inverse. Conversely, a common annular characteristic root makes a fixed family genuinely blind even for bounded-alphabet sources. The right question is therefore not whether a transform looks signed or collective, but whether its exact source inverse lies outside the two stable spectral regimes and whether that failure persists under the destination norm actually being controlled.

## Keep source coercivity, coordinate inversion and the Franel--Landau destination separate

Stable source recovery above the square-root observation threshold still does not prove the RH-critical discrepancy estimate. The lowbit tree solves a genuine finite-dimensional conditioning/source-reuse problem, and the quotient-coordinate picture remains a useful exact recovery language.

But the present anchored positive norm, every fixed-depth pointwise binomial root repair, every common sublogarithmic binomial depth controlled on square-root scale windows, and every annulus-free fixed finite polynomial filter family remain destination-complete. Fixed families with a common annular root fail in the opposite direction by possessing explicit bounded-source supercritical blind modes. The high-value frontier is therefore scale variation or another observation class whose physical inverse cannot be summarized by one fixed gcd or one coherent depth across the binary tree.
