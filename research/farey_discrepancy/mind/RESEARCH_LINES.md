FD-116 then classifies fixed rational anchor histories as periodic Möbius filters.

FD-117 closes the broader moving-anchor escape. For every horizon, the **entire** cumulative discrepancy field is an explicit affine image of the quotient-Mertens staircase, and the first `N` Fourier modes invert that transform. The live question is no longer how to manufacture an independent source by enlarging the discrepancy representation. It is whether a reversible Farey/refinement/exterior representation can prove a quantitatively stronger cancellation or coercivity theorem for this same Mertens-equivalent source at the Franel--Landau/RH-critical scale. A nonzero minor, extra dimension or moving selector is not evidence by itself.

If no such quantitative gain can be isolated, the research line must leave cumulative Farey discrepancy as its source object and introduce genuinely additional arithmetic data rather than another transform of `D_N`.

## Locate the exact boundary of the high-mass transport mechanism

The adaptive source threshold remains intrinsic: the source-rich tag grows while `U_H/H` does. Near the linear floor, neither the current source core nor its error normalization supplies a growing tag. A continuation must either keep a false-RH chain a fixed power above that floor long enough, or introduce an observable whose compression error remains negligible at near-linear mass.

FD-107--FD-117 now rule out an increasingly broad family of substitutes for that missing information: generic coherence, first-order arithmetic covariance, fixed-order prime incidence, finite prime ordering, fixed local polynomial nonlinearity, finite local refinement geometry, canonical moving ancestry, centered discrepancy, fixed anchors and the full cumulative discrepancy field itself.

## Keep information equivalence, proof geometry and quantitative cancellation separate

FD-117 is an information-classification theorem, not a smallness theorem. A reversible transform can still expose a useful inequality even though it carries no new source bits. Future arguments must state whether they introduce genuinely new arithmetic information or merely reorganize the Mertens-equivalent field, and in the latter case identify the exact quantitative estimate that the new geometry makes accessible.
