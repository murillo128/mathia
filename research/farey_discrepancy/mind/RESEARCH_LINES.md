# Farey-discrepancy research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Farey-discrepancy intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Bridge exact permanent-source rigidity to restricted space-time observation and destination coupling

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-027-integer-geometric-ladders-have-polynomial-finite-certification-index`.

FD-145--FD-163 reduce finite-`L^q` binary ancestry recovery to anchored cut exposure and show that bounded-resource anchor geometry cannot make the exposed squarefree mass disappear merely by sparse holes, terminal-core migration or one omitted small prime. Under the stated expansion/distortion/leakage hypotheses a positive-density source surface remains visible.

FD-164 then separates visibility from orientation. At one horizon the complete Farey discrepancy field is exactly equivalent to the floor-quotient Mertens staircase, yet finitely many macroscopic quotient-shell sign flips can preserve that entire field while changing a dense fraction of squarefree orientations. FD-165 extends the finite-cutoff ambiguity to arbitrary horizon bundles of size `J_T` whenever `J_T M_beta(T)=o(T)`.

FD-166 closes the exact **permanent-source** version. Every quotient set `Q_H` contains the consecutive prefix `1,...,floor(sqrt(H))`. If one fixed source matches complete fields on any unbounded horizon set, those prefixes exhaust the source and force `a=mu` identically. A first source defect at `n_0` is exposed by every `H>=n_0^2`.

FD-167 gives the corresponding complete-field conditioning theorem in unnormalized `L^2`, and FD-168 solves the raw Fourier-coordinate partial-observation problem. For a target prefix `r<=R`, the union of the nonzero Möbius inverse-row supports is the coordinate-minimal raw sampler over the full formal source space and retains the same localized inverse modulus. FD-169 closes unrestricted mixed-linear acquisition: target-aware arbitrary mixing reduces the minimum count to target rank by moving the inverse into the sensor.

FD-170 adds a temporal axis. Fix only the midpoint but observe it over consecutive horizons. The first differences are `-(1/2)(u*a)(H)`, with `u` the odd-indicator, so odd-divisor Möbius inversion recovers every source coefficient. FD-171 proves exact zero temporal redundancy over unrestricted normalized formal sources: omitting one horizon leaves a permanent pulse-pair null direction.

FD-172 shows that this ambient rank theorem is source-class relative. For multiplicative squarefree-supported sources with `-1<=a(p)<=1`, the transformed coefficients become nonnegative products, so dyadic annuli cannot hide a prime defect. Exact midpoint agreement at `H=2^m` forces the Möbius target and small dyadic errors uniformly bound prime defects.

FD-173 closes the exact sparse-horizon classification in that restricted class. For an observed horizon set `mathcal H`, Möbius target-rigidity holds iff every odd prime has some power in `U(mathcal H)=union_(H in mathcal H)(H/2,H]`. Missing one entire prime-power orbit gives an explicit invisible permanent perturbation; hitting the orbit kills that prime defect. Every fixed integer geometric ladder `{B^k}` is therefore target-rigid, even for `B>2`, so bounded multiplicative-gap coverage is not necessary.

FD-174 makes the fixed-integer geometric case finite-scale. For `B>=3`, Dirichlet first reduces each prime-power hitting problem to a denominator `q<=Q_B=floor(log_2 B)+1`; integrality of the distinct numbers `p^q` and `B^m` then prevents an arbitrarily small near-return. Every prime `p<=X` is hit within a sample prefix

`K_B(X)=O_B(X^Q_B log X)`,

and uniform midpoint error on that prefix directly bounds all prime defects through `X`, hence the squarefree source prefix. This is a certification theorem, not an efficiency theorem: the largest observed horizon is exponentially large in the sample index and the bound is not claimed sharp.

The live observation problem has therefore moved again. For fixed integer geometric ladders, finite certification **exists explicitly**; the remaining quantitative question is the true worst-case first-hit/certification growth and whether it can be reduced dramatically. Separately, determine which parts of squarefree support, multiplicativity and primewise nonnegativity can be relaxed before FD-171-type signed null directions return. Genuine pairwise sparse inversion remains distinct from one-target rigidity, and coefficient coercivity still needs a destination coupling to the nonlocal Franel--Landau norm.

## Apply the information-budget gate only after the observation-family kernel and normalization are fixed

FD-144 remains a later lower bound: even after geometric exposure and orientation are solved, `o(r_T)` arbitrary source-independent normalized ancestry tests can be fooled along a subsequence. FD-168--FD-174 sharpen the order of obligations. First declare the observation family, including whether changing horizon counts as a new measurement, identify its kernel relative to both the target and admissible source class, and fix the norm/noise normalization; only then count measurements or price inverse stability.

For raw Fourier selectors, FD-168 gives a nontrivial coordinate-minimal support because the basis is fixed. For unrestricted linear acquisition, FD-169 shows target row space is the minimal measurement space, so count alone is tautological. For midpoint temporal acquisition, FD-170 shows one spatial coordinate can encode a whole source when the operator changes with horizon; FD-171 shows every row is essential over unrestricted sources; FD-172--FD-174 show nonlinear source restrictions can collapse that row requirement all the way to sparse orbit-hitting target tests with explicit finite certification. Any information-budget claim must therefore specify whether it concerns unrestricted recovery, target testing, pairwise recovery, or finite-scale certification inside a restricted arithmetic model.

## Keep coefficient coercivity and destination coercivity separate

Exact or uniformly subcritical complete-field errors on an unbounded hierarchy identify an integer-valued permanent source. FD-168 preserves coefficient coercivity under a sharply reduced raw Fourier skeleton, FD-170 gives a stable triangular temporal inverse from consecutive midpoint horizons, and FD-173--FD-174 classify and quantify sparse one-target midpoint rigidity inside the bounded squarefree multiplicative cone. None proves the RH-critical discrepancy estimate. A useful continuation must sharpen sparse orbit-hitting certification, weaken the source architecture without losing coercivity, develop pairwise/mixed observation coercivity under a precisely declared class, or connect source-native structure directly to the nonlocal Franel--Landau norm.
