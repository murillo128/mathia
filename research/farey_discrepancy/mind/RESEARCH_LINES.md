# Farey-discrepancy research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Farey-discrepancy evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Force one-sided repair variation past the positive-capacity boundary

FD-255--FD-264 separate the signed exact-omission threshold from the physical nonnegative-source boundary. The general positive switched-response cone has relative dyadic capacity exponent `beta=log_2(3/2)`, while response monotonicity strengthens this to inverse-linear depletion. The independent first-row obstruction remains at polynomial depth `lambda<=1-Theta`. For a same-amplitude deep repair to beat that ceiling in the general positive cone, the source must force depth amplification beyond `D^(2-beta)`.

Canonical full-grid inversion does not supply that amplifier. FD-262 bounds its total repair mass by `O(D log D)` after the explicit forcing terms are removed. FD-263 shows that deep forcing coordinates are copied only once into a dyadic coefficient band, so the absolute/one-sided ledger crosses the positive-capacity threshold only when its amplitude-density exponent is large enough. FD-264 then shows that signed band aggregation telescopes this population resource into a probability average of Mertens prefixes and gives zero interior weight to the deepest shell.

The live deterministic source question is therefore a theorem on **one-sided or `l^1` within-band repair variation** under the exact replication kernel, or a genuinely different switched-only repair whose intermediate motion changes the source demand. A negative signed mean or a dense population of deep blocks is not enough by itself.

## Decide whether close-gap zero structure can couple coherently to the physical horizons

FD-268--FD-272 sharply separate spectral magnitude from usable source-phase coherence. FD-268 caps every Mellin frequency at the physical divisor depth. FD-269 then shows that, on the critical line, the ordinary packet of zeros below depth `D` has enough absolute `l^1` budget to cross the `D^(1-beta)` complexity threshold under the natural first negative derivative-moment scale; the missing resource is no longer absolute spectral mass.

FD-270 replaces that absolute ledger by the phase-sensitive replicated field and obtains a Bohr mean-square bound controlled by the frequency-damped reciprocal-square derivative moment. FD-271 proves that the same power law survives sampling at the actual integer horizons. FD-272 then closes the cardinality-only source-correlation escape whenever the full reciprocal-square moment exponent is below `3-2 beta`: Mertens-large horizons have full polynomial counting exponent one and cannot all lie in the polynomially sparse coherent set.

FD-273 localizes the remaining finite critical-zero mechanism under RH without assuming a conjectural full `J_{-1}` bound. The published Bui--Florea--Milinovich negative-moment theorem gives the well-spaced zero packet RMS size `x^(5/4+o(1))`, so threshold coherence on that packet occupies at most an `x^(-kappa_*+o(1))` fraction of integer horizons, with `kappa_*=3/2-2 beta=0.3300749985...`. Outside that exceptional set, any full finite-packet mass at scale `x^(2-beta)` must contribute at least half that scale from the complementary close-gap zeros.

The finite low-zero route is therefore no longer merely a search for an unspecified rare coherent subsequence: **conditional on RH, it must be close-gap dominated** at the source-selected horizons, unless the missing mass is supplied by the explicit-formula remainder, higher zeros, multiple zeros or another spectral component. The live question is whether close-gap zeros have enough reciprocal-derivative weight and phase alignment under the exact divisor-replication kernel to meet the positive-capacity threshold on the relevant Mertens-large horizons. The stronger Bui--Florea--Milinovich separation result under the additional Farmer--Gonek--Hughes `S(t)` hypothesis would shrink the required exceptional family further, but that conjectural refinement is not part of the established frontier.
