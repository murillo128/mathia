## Separate source-side rigidity from the exact observation and recovery geometry

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-039-periodic-scale-switching-can-create-a-supercritical-floquet-blind-mode`.

FD-187--FD-194 establish the source and observation gates. Prime-extension energy is a genuine Möbius relation currency, while dyadic signed-Fourier observation has a sharp geometric square-root threshold: below the critical band permanent quotient holes remain, while at and above it cumulative source recovery has an explicit uniformly stable inverse. The transition is one of geometry and injectivity, not a conditioning pathology of the raw coordinate map.

FD-195--FD-201 show why stable coordinate recovery does not automatically survive quadratic compression. Classical Franel/Sobolev energies price the same geometric witnesses differently from the raw inverse; on reciprocal-prime shells the RH-facing positive diagonal budget is substantially more coercive than the square-root Mertens target. Fixed-mode cross-horizon differences do contain new Möbius interval increments, but independently squaring the shell rows restores the `H^(3/2+o(1))` random-multiplicative tariff.

FD-202 gives the first exact escape. Taking adjacent-prime differences before a positive quadratic norm collapses overlapping long Mertens intervals to endpoint slabs, and the shell-normalized path energy has squarefree-Rademacher mean `Theta(H)`. FD-203 separates kernel removal from inverse conditioning, and FD-204 replaces the high-resistance path by a lowbit tree whose anchored point-evaluation resistance is only logarithmic while each physical source boundary is reused at most once per dyadic scale.

FD-205 shows that lowbit shell sensors recover global Mertens values through quotient-coordinate telescoping. FD-206 then identifies a sharper obstruction: on the source-independent horizons `H_n=n p_n`, where `p_n` is the largest prime at most `n`, the positive root anchor is exactly

`S_(2,H_n)(p_n)=M(2n)-M(n)`.

A square-root estimate for this scalar increment is already RH-equivalent by binary-prefix reconstruction. Thus favorable inverse geometry and a near-critical random benchmark do not make the proposed positive energy easier when one standalone nonnegative coordinate already contains the destination-complete problem.

FD-207--FD-209 test signed dyadic repair. Every fixed depth remains RH-complete. Growing a common depth `q=o(log X)` preserves the critical-power random benchmark, but the exact negative-binomial binary inverse still costs only a subpower factor on square-root scale windows. Common-depth growth therefore does not create an intermediate theorem surface.

FD-210--FD-211 close the fixed finite **general polynomial-filter** alternative as well. Let `Delta(n)=A(2n)-A(n)` for a bounded source and let `P_1(T),...,P_r(T)` be fixed root filters. If their gcd has a zero in the physical annulus

`sqrt(2)<|rho|<=2`,

the family has a bounded-alphabet super-square-root blind mode. If the gcd has no such zero, pointwise square-root-plus-epsilon control of the whole family forces the same bound for `A(N)` for every bounded source. For Möbius, an annulus-free fixed family is therefore RH-complete.

FD-212 proves that the remaining diagonal opening is genuinely nonautonomous even at bounded depth. On the dyadic ray, the period-three schedule `q_j=(0,3,3)` has an exact Floquet multiplier `lambda=-(7+3sqrt(5))/2`, whose per-step modulus lies strictly between `sqrt(2)` and `2`. The resulting super-square-root mode can be embedded into an actual bounded source while the selected root filter vanishes at every dyadic observation point. Both instantaneous filters are individually annulus-free, their fixed family has gcd `1`, and the squarefree-Rademacher cost remains `Theta(H)`. The blindness is created by **ordered scale switching**, not by an annular root of any fixed filter or by growing depth.

The live signed-filter question has therefore narrowed to **cross-ray/all-integer coherence**. A proof that analyzes each dyadic ray independently cannot close the scale-dependent opening. One must determine whether requiring the diagonal estimate for every integer couples the binary rays strongly enough to kill all supercritical switched modes, or whether a bounded source can realize such a mode on a sufficiently rich all-integer/dense set. Only after that source-coherence question is settled does it make sense to ask whether Möbius-specific multiplicativity supplies extra rigidity.

## Apply information budgets only after fixing source class, observation family and destination norm

The same Farey representation can be complete, target-rigid or permanently noninjective depending on the source class, observation family and destination norm. The current sequence separates quotient coverage, raw inverse conditioning, diagonal quadratic coercivity, cross-horizon source information, covariance-preserving signed projection, kernel dimension, graph resistance, source-overlap cost, quotient-coordinate recovery, destination-complete scalar anchors, fixed-family spectral invertibility and now nonautonomous scale-switching coherence.

FD-206--FD-212 add a required audit: a favorable random benchmark and improved inverse geometry do not imply analytic leverage if a positive coordinate, a common-depth dyadic transform or an annulus-free fixed filter family already admits a square-root-cost physical inverse. Conversely, a common annular characteristic root makes a fixed family genuinely blind, while periodic scale switching can manufacture a supercritical blind multiplier even when every instantaneous filter is spectrally harmless. The right question is therefore not whether a transform looks signed or collective, but whether its **ordered source inverse** remains coherent across the actual set of scales and rays being controlled.

## Keep source coercivity, coordinate inversion and the Franel--Landau destination separate

Stable source recovery above the square-root observation threshold still does not prove the RH-critical discrepancy estimate. The lowbit tree solves a genuine finite-dimensional conditioning/source-reuse problem, and the quotient-coordinate picture remains a useful exact recovery language.

But the present anchored positive norm, every fixed-depth pointwise binomial root repair, every common sublogarithmic binomial depth controlled on square-root scale windows, and every annulus-free fixed finite polynomial filter family remain destination-complete. Fixed families with a common annular root fail in the opposite direction by possessing explicit bounded-source supercritical blind modes. FD-212 shows that bounded-depth diagonal switching occupies a third regime on sparse rays: no fixed annular root is present, yet the ordered product has a physical Floquet multiplier. The high-value frontier is whether full all-integer source coherence eliminates that nonautonomous regime or permits it to survive.
