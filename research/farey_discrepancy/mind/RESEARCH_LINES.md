# Farey-discrepancy research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Farey-discrepancy intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Bridge exact permanent-source rigidity to restricted space-time observation and destination coupling

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-028-sparse-midpoint-rigidity-comes-from-cross-coordinate-multiplicativity-not-coefficient-fidelity`.

FD-145--FD-163 reduce finite-`L^q` binary ancestry recovery to anchored cut exposure and show that bounded-resource anchor geometry cannot make the exposed squarefree mass disappear merely by sparse holes, terminal-core migration or one omitted small prime. Under the stated expansion/distortion/leakage hypotheses a positive-density source surface remains visible.

FD-164 then separates visibility from orientation. At one horizon the complete Farey discrepancy field is exactly equivalent to the floor-quotient Mertens staircase, yet finitely many macroscopic quotient-shell sign flips can preserve that entire field while changing a dense fraction of squarefree orientations. FD-165 extends the finite-cutoff ambiguity to arbitrary horizon bundles of size `J_T` whenever `J_T M_beta(T)=o(T)`.

FD-166 closes the exact **permanent-source** version. Every quotient set `Q_H` contains the consecutive prefix `1,...,floor(sqrt(H))`. If one fixed source matches complete fields on any unbounded horizon set, those prefixes exhaust the source and force `a=mu` identically. A first source defect at `n_0` is exposed by every `H>=n_0^2`.

FD-167 gives the corresponding complete-field conditioning theorem in unnormalized `L^2`, and FD-168 solves the raw Fourier-coordinate partial-observation problem. For a target prefix `r<=R`, the union of the nonzero Möbius inverse-row supports is the coordinate-minimal raw sampler over the full formal source space and retains the same localized inverse modulus. FD-169 closes unrestricted mixed-linear acquisition: target-aware arbitrary mixing reduces the minimum count to target rank by moving the inverse into the sensor.

FD-170 adds a temporal axis. Fix only the midpoint but observe it over consecutive horizons. The first differences are `-(1/2)(u*a)(H)`, with `u` the odd-indicator, so odd-divisor Möbius inversion recovers every source coefficient. FD-171 proves exact zero temporal redundancy over unrestricted normalized formal sources: omitting one horizon leaves a permanent pulse-pair null direction.

FD-172 shows that this ambient rank theorem is source-class relative. For multiplicative squarefree-supported sources with `-1<=a(p)<=1`, the transformed coefficients become nonnegative products, so dyadic annuli cannot hide a prime defect. Exact midpoint agreement at `H=2^m` forces the Möbius target and small dyadic errors uniformly bound prime defects.

FD-173 closes the exact sparse-horizon classification in that restricted class. For an observed horizon set `mathcal H`, Möbius target-rigidity holds iff every odd prime has some power in `U(mathcal H)=union_(H in mathcal H)(H/2,H]`. Missing one entire prime-power orbit gives an explicit invisible permanent perturbation; hitting the orbit kills that prime defect. Every fixed integer geometric ladder `{B^k}` is therefore target-rigid, even for `B>2`, so bounded multiplicative-gap coverage is not necessary.

FD-174 makes the fixed-integer geometric case finite-scale. For `B>=3`, Dirichlet first reduces each prime-power hitting problem to a denominator `q<=Q_B=floor(log_2 B)+1`; integrality of the distinct numbers `p^q` and `B^m` then prevents an arbitrarily small near-return. Every prime `p<=X` is hit within a sample prefix

`K_B(X)=O_B(X^Q_B log X)`,

and uniform midpoint error on that prefix directly bounds all prime defects through `X`, hence the squarefree source prefix. This is a certification theorem, not an efficiency theorem: the largest observed horizon is exponentially large in the sample index and the bound is not claimed sharp.

FD-175 identifies the structural boundary on the other side. For every fixed integer `B>=2` and every finite cutoff `X`, there is a nontrivial permanent source with `a(n) in {0,mu(n)}`, equal to Möbius through `X` and even at every prime, whose midpoint agrees with Möbius at **all** horizons `B^m`. The construction deletes only squarefree composites. Once a geometric sample at `h` has been balanced, the old-support response at `Bh` differs from `B` times the previous response by at most `floor(B/2)` per deleted coordinate; the fresh half-shell `(Bh/2,Bh]` contains linearly many squarefree coefficients of either Möbius sign and can repair that bounded rounding debt. For `B>=3` the deletion set may have density zero; for `B=2` its upper density can be made arbitrarily small. Any fixed low multiplicative-rank hierarchy can also be left untouched.

FD-176 strengthens the same non-rigidity to a growing multiplicative-rank window for `B>=3`. With `beta_B = 1-log_B(1+floor(B/2))`, every fixed `rho<beta_B` can be protected while geometric-horizon repair continues. This showed that a substantial growing low-rank cone is still not a substitute for the global product law, but its one-step recurrence left the dyadic growing-rank case unresolved.

FD-177 removes that apparent dyadic boundary and nearly exhausts coordinatewise rank fidelity as an escape. Grouping `k` consecutive dyadic samples into one repair block lowers the correction-count exponent to `alpha_k=log_2(k+1)/k -> 0`. Consequently, for **every** fixed `rho<1`, every finite cutoff and every `eta>0`, there is a permanent deletion-only source agreeing with Möbius on all sufficiently large squarefree coefficients with `omega(n)<=rho log n/loglog n`, agreeing at every dyadic midpoint horizon, and modifying only `O(x^eta)` coefficients up to `x`. Thus even correctness on any fixed subextremal fraction of the maximal multiplicative-rank scale, plus arbitrarily sparse deviations, does not restore sparse target-rigidity.

The live source-restriction problem is therefore much sharper. Squarefree support, ternary amplitudes, correct coefficient signs, exact prime data, fixed rank and **every fixed subextremal growing-rank cone** fail to replace full multiplicativity, even on the dyadic ladder. The remaining coordinatewise boundary is the extremal endpoint `(1-o(1))log n/loglog n` or all squarefree ranks; the more promising alternative is a genuinely cross-coordinate compatibility that couples low-rank data to the rare near-primorial repair coordinates. Inside the genuinely multiplicative cone, the quantitative first-hit/certification problem of FD-174 remains open and may still admit much sharper bounds. Genuine pairwise sparse inversion remains distinct from one-target rigidity, and coefficient coercivity still needs a destination coupling to the nonlocal Franel--Landau norm.

## Apply the information-budget gate only after the observation-family kernel and normalization are fixed

FD-144 remains a later lower bound: even after geometric exposure and orientation are solved, `o(r_T)` arbitrary source-independent normalized ancestry tests can be fooled along a subsequence. FD-168--FD-177 sharpen the order of obligations. First declare the observation family, including whether changing horizon counts as a new measurement, identify its kernel relative to both the target and admissible source class, and fix the norm/noise normalization; only then count measurements or price inverse stability.

For raw Fourier selectors, FD-168 gives a nontrivial coordinate-minimal support because the basis is fixed. For unrestricted linear acquisition, FD-169 shows target row space is the minimal measurement space, so count alone is tautological. For midpoint temporal acquisition, FD-170 shows one spatial coordinate can encode a whole source when the operator changes with horizon; FD-171 shows every row is essential over unrestricted sources; FD-172--FD-174 show global multiplicativity can collapse that row requirement to sparse orbit-hitting target tests with explicit finite certification; and FD-175--FD-177 show that deleting full multiplicativity restores a permanent sparse-time kernel even under nearly extremal coordinate fidelity. Any information-budget claim must therefore specify whether it concerns unrestricted recovery, target testing, pairwise recovery, finite-scale certification, or a source class with genuine cross-coordinate coupling.

## Keep coefficient coercivity and destination coercivity separate

Exact or uniformly subcritical complete-field errors on an unbounded hierarchy identify an integer-valued permanent source. FD-168 preserves coefficient coercivity under a sharply reduced raw Fourier skeleton, FD-170 gives a stable triangular temporal inverse from consecutive midpoint horizons, and FD-173--FD-174 classify and quantify sparse one-target midpoint rigidity inside the bounded squarefree multiplicative cone. FD-175--FD-177 prove that this sparse coercivity does not survive deletion-only squarefree perturbations that retain primes, every fixed subextremal rank cone and even arbitrarily sparse deviation support once the global product law is removed. None proves the RH-critical discrepancy estimate. A useful continuation must sharpen orbit-hitting certification inside the multiplicative cone, identify an endpoint-scale or genuinely cross-coordinate compatibility that rules out block shell repair, develop pairwise/mixed observation coercivity under a precisely declared class, or connect source-native structure directly to the nonlocal Franel--Landau norm.
