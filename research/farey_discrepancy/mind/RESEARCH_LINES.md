## Upgrade full-counting-exponent source abundance to a global occupation statement

**Linked intuition:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy`.

FD-072 supplies a source-selected positive mechanism: at a `sigma`-power record with `sigma>1/2`, the causal horizon `T=4X`, `D=3` confines the complete denominator to the record-controlled prefix and forces fixed positive nonsquarefree occupation and Schur defect. FD-073 turns each such episode into an amplitude-length protected block; FD-074 constrains the center; FD-075 forces a growing same-parity large-radical population inside the block.

FD-076 removes arbitrary power-scale sparsity of frontier records. FD-077 is stronger for abundance and no longer needs the points to be records. Pintz's average absolute order, transferred through the exact inverse floor-harmonic identity, gives logarithmic `L^1` source mass of exponent `Theta` in every fixed power annulus. Consequently, for every fixed `kappa>0`, the count of `n<=X` with `|H(n)|>=X^(Theta-kappa)` has logarithmic counting exponent tending to one.

Fixing any nonsquarefree Jordan row `q>D` converts the same abundant source samples into causal horizons with nonsquarefree occupation and normalized Schur projective defect at least `X^(-eta)` for any prescribed `eta>0`, again on a set of full polynomial counting exponent. Under false RH, these samples can simultaneously carry source size above the square-root scale.

The remaining theorem is therefore no longer merely “create more strict records.” Counting exponent one can still coexist with zero natural density and zero logarithmic density, and the occupation bound can still decay subpolynomially. The live problem is to upgrade this abundance to enough logarithmic/ordinary recurrence or a nonvanishing aggregate occupation statement, or to find an exact aggregation argument that uses the full-counting-exponent family without requiring strict-record block overlap.

## Keep record protection, source abundance, occupation strength and density separate

FD-071 explains why arbitrary-later persistence can be normalized away; FD-072--FD-075 give constant-strength protection around source-selected records; FD-076 makes such records power-dense. FD-077 shows that frontier-near source values themselves are far more abundant—full polynomial counting exponent—and that causal occupation on them loses at most an arbitrarily small fixed polynomial power. None of these implies positive natural/logarithmic density, a fixed positive occupation lower bound, or that the abundant points inherit the strict-record constant-occupation mechanism. Those are separate currencies.
