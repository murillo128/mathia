## Turn amplitude-length protected record blocks into a global recurrence theorem using the arithmetic of their record hosts

**Linked intuition:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy`.

FD-066 isolates the direct coherent-packet threshold, FD-068 shows that very low normalized nonsquarefree occupation cannot dominate logarithmic scale for q-subcovariant heads, and FD-069--FD-071 separate pointwise capture from later-horizon dilution.

FD-072 supplies a source-selected positive mechanism. If `X` is a `sigma`-power record of the physical source with `sigma>1/2`, the causal horizon `T=4X`, `D=3` confines the complete denominator to the record-controlled prefix and forces a fixed positive nonsquarefree occupation and Schur defect.

FD-073 shows that these protected episodes are not pointlike. The exact unit-increment law for the physical source propagates a record of amplitude `A=|H(X)|` to every `Y` with `|Y-X|<=eta A`; each recentered horizon `4Y` inherits a uniform positive occupation when `sigma>1/2`. Under a false-RH frontier, strict records therefore generate additive protected blocks of length `asymp A_j`, with `A_j/X_j^sigma -> infinity`.

FD-074 adds the first exact arithmetic restriction on the block centers. At every strict `sigma`-record,

`phi(rad X) > X|H(X)|[1-(1-1/X)^sigma] >= sigma |H(X)|`,

so `rad X > sigma|H(X)|`; once `|H(X)|>1`, the record sign is fixed by `mu(rad X)=(-1)^omega(X)`. Along any false-RH supercritical record sequence, `rad X_j/X_j^sigma -> infinity`, excluding fixed higher prime-power families and forcing the record centers into increasingly large-radical arithmetic hosts.

At the critical endpoint `sigma=1/2`, the causal mechanism still survives with the explicit loss `nu_(4Y,3) >>_eta 1/log Y`. This makes the endpoint degeneration quantitative but still does not produce a fixed critical-line gap.

The live problem is now a source-specific recurrence theorem rather than generic record spacing. One must determine whether the exact multiplicative increment law, together with the forced radical/parity constraints at strict record centers and the amplitude-length blocks around them, prevents supercritical records from being arbitrarily multiplicatively sparse or forces enough near-record quality to recur on logarithmic scale. Large radical alone is not a density theorem, so a negative result would need a control preserving substantially more of the physical coefficient law than one-Lipschitz regularity.
