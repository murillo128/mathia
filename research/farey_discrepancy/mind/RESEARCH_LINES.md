## Separate source-side rigidity from the exact observation and recovery geometry

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-001-cumulative-farey-discrepancy-is-mertens-energy` through `MI-037-growing-dyadic-root-differencing-has-a-sublogarithmic-critical-random-window`.

FD-187--FD-194 establish the source and observation gates. Prime-extension energy is a genuine Möbius relation currency, while dyadic signed-Fourier observation has a sharp geometric square-root threshold: below the critical band permanent quotient holes remain, while at and above it cumulative source recovery has an explicit uniformly stable inverse. The transition is one of geometry and injectivity, not a conditioning pathology of the raw coordinate map.

FD-195--FD-201 show why stable coordinate recovery does not automatically survive quadratic compression. Classical Franel/Sobolev energies price the same geometric witnesses differently from the raw inverse; on reciprocal-prime shells the RH-facing positive diagonal budget is substantially more coercive than the square-root Mertens target. Fixed-mode cross-horizon differences do contain new Möbius interval increments, but independently squaring the shell rows restores the `H^(3/2+o(1))` random-multiplicative tariff.

FD-202 gives the first exact escape. Taking adjacent-prime differences before a positive quadratic norm collapses overlapping long Mertens intervals to endpoint slabs, and the shell-normalized path energy has squarefree-Rademacher mean `Theta(H)`. FD-203 separates kernel removal from inverse conditioning, and FD-204 replaces the high-resistance path by a lowbit tree whose anchored point-evaluation resistance is only logarithmic while each physical source boundary is reused at most once per dyadic scale.

FD-205 shows that lowbit shell sensors recover global Mertens values through quotient-coordinate telescoping. FD-206 then identifies a sharper obstruction: on the source-independent horizons `H_n=n p_n`, where `p_n` is the largest prime at most `n`, the positive root anchor is exactly

`S_(2,H_n)(p_n)=M(2n)-M(n)`.

A square-root estimate for this scalar increment is already RH-equivalent by binary-prefix reconstruction. Thus favorable inverse geometry and a near-critical random benchmark do not make the proposed positive energy easier when one standalone nonnegative coordinate already contains the destination-complete problem.

FD-207 tests the most direct signed repair. For every fixed depth `q`, let

`C_q(n)=(T-I)^q[M(2n)-M(n)]`, with `(TF)(n)=F(2n)`.

Each `C_q` is an exact finite signed combination of root-aligned Farey sensors and still has the critical squarefree-random second-moment scale. Nevertheless, for every fixed `q`, square-root control of `C_q(n)` is again RH-equivalent. The bounded physical Möbius odd-step defect lets one invert the finite dyadic difference recursively along binary prefixes. Therefore a fixed number of signed root subtractions can remove the literal positive anchor before squaring without lowering the pointwise theorem surface.

FD-208 prices growing depth. The squarefree-random second moment is multiplied by the central Delannoy factor

`D_q=sum_(j=0)^q binom(q,j)^2 2^j`, with `log D_q=Theta(q)`.

On `H_n=n p_n`, the benchmark remains `H_n^(1+o(1))` exactly when `q=o(log n)`, and its surcharge is only polylogarithmic when `q=O(log log n)`. The largest dyadic horizon touched by `C_q` grows like `4^q`, so the same sublogarithmic condition keeps the observation span subpower.

FD-209 closes the inversion-constant side of that opening. For any source-independent common depth `q(X)=o(log X)`, square-root control of `C_(q(X))(n)` on the scale window `sqrt(X)/2 <= n <= X` is still RH-equivalent. The exact binary-prefix inverse has negative-binomial Green weights and amplifies square-root data by only `(1+sqrt(2))^(q+1)=X^(o(1))`; terminal and odd-step terms also have subpower combinatorial mass. Thus there is no sublogarithmic regime in which the comparator is still power-critical but the common-depth inverse has already become power-expensive.

FD-210 separates **random cost** from **spectral visibility** for general fixed signed root filters. If `P(z)=sum a_j z^j`, then `P(T)[M(2n)-M(n)]` is an exact finite signed combination of root-aligned Farey sensors and every nonzero fixed `P` still has squarefree-random shell cost `Theta(H)`. But if `P` has a characteristic root in the physical supercritical annulus `sqrt(2)<|rho|<=2`, there is a source with coefficients in `{-1,0,1}` whose summatory function grows like `N^alpha`, `alpha>1/2`, while the filtered observable is only `O_P(1)`. For a finite filter family, a common annular root gives the same joint blind direction. Thus a favorable random benchmark is not enough: fixed collective filters must also pass the necessary spectral test that their gcd has no root in that annulus.

The live growing-depth question is consequently sharper: can a **scale-dependent diagonal depth** `q=q(n)` exploit its lack of coherence along binary ancestors? The collective alternative is also narrower: can one control a signed family with no common physical supercritical root, or use specifically Möbius arithmetic to exclude the bounded-alphabet blind modes that generic fixed filters permit? Merely letting a common binomial depth tend to infinity inside `o(log X)`, or choosing a critical-cost fixed filter with an annular characteristic zero, no longer constitutes an unexplained escape.

## Apply information budgets only after fixing source class, observation family and destination norm

The same Farey representation can be complete, target-rigid or permanently noninjective depending on the source class, observation family and destination norm. The current sequence separates quotient coverage, raw inverse conditioning, diagonal quadratic coercivity, cross-horizon source information, covariance-preserving signed projection, kernel dimension, graph resistance, source-overlap cost, quotient-coordinate recovery and destination-complete scalar anchors.

FD-206--FD-210 add a required audit: a favorable random benchmark and improved inverse geometry do not imply analytic leverage if a positive coordinate, a signed dyadic transform with an effective physical inverse, or a spectrally blind collective filter already hides the destination issue. Growing the binomial signed depth has a measurable random cost, but throughout the full sublogarithmic critical-power window a common-depth inverse still costs only a subpower factor. General fixed signed filters introduce a different failure mode: a root between the RH dyadic radius `sqrt(2)` and the bounded-source radius `2` is invisible to the filter even though the random comparator remains critical.

## Keep source coercivity, coordinate inversion and the Franel--Landau destination separate

Stable source recovery above the square-root observation threshold still does not prove the RH-critical discrepancy estimate. The lowbit tree solves a genuine finite-dimensional conditioning/source-reuse problem, and the quotient-coordinate picture remains a useful exact recovery language.

But the present anchored positive norm, every fixed-depth pointwise binomial root repair, and every **common sublogarithmic binomial depth controlled on square-root scale windows** remain destination-complete, while arbitrary fixed signed filters can fail in the opposite direction by possessing bounded-source supercritical blind modes. The high-value frontier is therefore not simply “difference the root more times” or “pick another critical-cost signed polynomial.” It is to determine whether scale-varying depth incoherence survives physical inversion, or whether a collective family with no common annular root admits useful Farey control without collapsing back to an RH-equivalent pointwise row.
