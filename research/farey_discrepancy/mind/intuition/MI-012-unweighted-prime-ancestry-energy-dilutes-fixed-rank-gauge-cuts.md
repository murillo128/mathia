# MI-012 — Unweighted prime-ancestry energy dilutes fixed-rank gauge cuts

**Evidence level:** exact obstruction from [FD-130](../../findings/FD-130-vanishing-mean-prime-ancestry-defect-survives-every-fixed-low-rank-mobius-anchor.md)

On squarefree integers the gauge `b(n)=mu(n)a(n)` turns exact prime ancestry into the edge-constancy law `b(pn)=b(n)`. FD-130 chooses one fixed gauge with `b=1` through multiplicative rank `K` and `b=-1` above it. The source therefore agrees exactly with Möbius on every squarefree integer with `omega(n)<=K`, while every ancestry defect is concentrated on the single interface `K -> K+1`.

The defective-edge count is exactly `(K+1)N_(K+1)(H)`, hence at most linear in `H`. The complete prime-ancestry graph below `H`, however, has edge volume at least `H` times a divergent prime-harmonic factor. Consequently every fixed finite `L^r` ancestry defect normalized by **total edge count** tends to zero even though the source differs from Möbius on a positive proportion of coefficients.

The decisive currency is therefore not the number of prime directions available. Full prime breadth can coexist with vanishing global mean defect because the normalization makes a fixed-rank cut asymptotically invisible. Any coercive approximate-ancestry law must keep the relevant interfaces visible—for example through rank-sensitive weights, levelwise or primewise control, a local/supremum norm, or anchoring depth that grows with scale—or bypass coefficient recovery with a direct destination inequality.

This is a normalization obstruction, not a theorem against approximate ancestry in general. FD-130 does not exclude weighted energies that assign nonvanishing mass to low ranks, growing `K(H)`, uniform local defects, or a Fourier-skeleton estimate that never tries to reconstruct Möbius coefficients. It also does not show that the gauge witness can satisfy the separate Farey saturation requirements.
