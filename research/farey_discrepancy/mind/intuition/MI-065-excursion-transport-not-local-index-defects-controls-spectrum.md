# MI-065 — Excursion permutation spectra are controlled by cumulative length transport, not local Farey-index defects

**Evidence level:** exact permutation identity and uniform prime-order asymptotic from [FD-319](../../findings/FD-319-four-vanishing-farey-index-defects-carry-macroscopic-energy.md) and [FD-320](../../findings/FD-320-all-prime-farey-excursion-permutations-have-an-exact-transport-spectrum.md).

For prime-order Farey excursions `E_j` of geometric width `1/p` and gap count `ell_j`, set `w_j=ell_j/N_p-1/p`, `U_j=sum_(i<j)w_i`, and `U_t^sigma=sum_(s<t)w_(sigma(s))`. Reassemble any permutation `sigma` while retaining each directed denominator-edge gap. The full rank discrepancy vector satisfies the **exact** transport identity
`D^sigma=P_sigma D+v^sigma` with `||v^sigma||_2^2=T_p(sigma)=sum_t ell_(sigma(t))(U_(sigma(t))-U_t^sigma)^2`.
As the true Farey `||D||_2=o(1)`, uniformly in permutations,
`|sqrt(E_p^sigma)-sqrt(T_p(sigma))|<=sqrt(E_p)=o(1)`.
The discrete sine transform carries the same uniform `l^2` approximation over *all modes*.

The local Hall--Shiu defect count is instead the number of nonconsecutive boundaries in the excursion permutation. FD-319 exhibits a reflection-symmetric permutation with **four** defects, each distance `1/p` from integer index one, but `T_p->3/(4pi^2)`; FD-320 also builds reflection-symmetric permutations with diverging defect counts and `T_p->0`. Neither total index distance nor defect count controls the transported spectral energy in this class.

[FD-327](../../findings/FD-327-same-prime-three-defect-profiles-have-unbounded-energy-ratios.md) sharpens the value-only failure: two reassemblies of the *same* prime-order excursion list retain identical **ordered values** of three scaled local index defects and the entire directed-edge inventory, while their limiting transport energies can have an arbitrarily large ratio. Their defect **ranks** differ. In contrast, [FD-328](../../findings/FD-328-three-farey-index-defect-ranks-reconstruct-the-excursion-permutation.md) proves that the exact three defective rank positions determine the two swapped contiguous blocks: with original strictly increasing cumulative gap counts `S_j`, the three cut labels are `1+S^{-1}(r_1-1)`, `1+S^{-1}(r_1+r_3-r_2-1)`, and `1+S^{-1}(r_3-1)`. This is exact for a fixed prime, not a stability estimate.

[FD-329](../../findings/FD-329-four-defect-ranks-rigid-five-admit-transport-ambiguity.md) extends rank-only reconstruction to **four** defects (internal block order `CBA`) and breaks it at **five**. The original five-break pair `BDACR` versus `DBACR` has identical exact defective ranks and a nonzero transport difference of order `1/p`, without matching defective index values. [FD-330](../../findings/FD-330-five-identical-defect-ranks-permit-macroscopic-farey-energy-separation.md) strengthens this from a vanishing to a **macroscopic** discrepancy: for fixed integers `u>v>=3` and primes `p ≡ -1 (mod lcm(u,v))`, symmetric positive-proportion blocks give the same five ranks for the two words, while
`T_p(BDACR)-T_p(DBACR) -> (6/pi^2)(1/v-1/u)^2(1/u+1/v)>0`.
Their true reconstructed Franel-energy difference has the same limit, using the uniform `|sqrt(E_p)-sqrt(T_p)|=o(1)` transport approximation. Exactly equal **rank positions** therefore do not even imply asymptotically equal energy at five defects in this labeled-edge reassembly class; the **defect index values are not matched**, so rank-only ambiguity does not imply ambiguity of their joint signature.

[FD-331](../../findings/FD-331-joint-farey-defect-ranks-and-values-invert-all-excursion-permutations.md) closes exact identifiability for **any** number of defects, within the same fixed-prime whole-excursion reassembly class. The complete ordered signature of noninteger Hall--Shiu indices `(r_i,nu_i)` is a finite injective code for the permutation when `p` and the genuine positive excursion lengths `ell_j` are known. The first defect rank recovers the unchanged prefix via `S^{-1}(r_1-1)`; at each subsequent break `d_i=p(nu_i-1)=I_p(j+1)-I_p(k)` recovers the next run's initial label `k`, and the next rank gap fixes the terminal label by strict growth of cumulative gap counts `S`. Checking the recovered runs cover each label once gives a necessary-and-sufficient realization certificate. Exact signature equality therefore fixes `v^sigma`, `T_p` and all sine coefficients, even where rank-only energies differ macroscopically. Defect values lie in `1+p^{-1}Z`, giving exact-value stability **only** for errors strictly below `1/p` at identical ranks; no robustness for rank shifts, missing breaks or errors of order `1/p` follows.

[FD-340](../../findings/FD-340-one-erased-farey-defect-value-is-recoverable-two-need-not-be.md) shows that complete values are not all independently necessary: `sum_i d_i=0`, because both lists of inverse arguments at all seams permute `1,...,p-1`. With the entire exact rank list retained, any one erased value is recovered by this checksum. Two erasures can already hide distinct energies at `p=11`. This is recovery of known-position value erasures, not recovery of omitted ranks or correction of unknown-location errors.

[FD-344](../../findings/FD-344-reflection-makes-three-farey-defect-erasures-recoverable-four-need-not-be.md) strengthens that guarantee in the explicitly reflected class `sigma(n+1-t)=p-1-sigma(t)`, `n=p-2`. There is no defective central seam, and the complete signature has `m=2h`, `r_(m+1-i)=N_p-r_i`, **equal** mirror numerators `d_(m+1-i)=d_i`, and `sum_(i=1)^h d_i=0`. Copy each known value to its mirror; if exactly one whole mirror pair is missing, the half-checksum recovers it. Hence any three erased values are recoverable; more erasures are also recoverable whenever at most one whole pair is missing. This is a sufficient pattern condition, not a claim that every two-pair erasure is ambiguous.

The uniform three-erasure guarantee is sharp within actual reflected reassemblies: at `p=7`, the two permutations `(2,1,3,5,4)` and `(2,5,3,1,4)` have ranks `(1,4,8,10,14,17)` and numerator vectors `(-3,4,-1,-1,4,-3)` and `(-3,2,1,1,2,-3)`. Removing the two inner mirror pairs leaves identical records but Franel-energy difference `-11/2205`. At fixed ranks the realizable reflected value vectors have Hamming distance at least four, attained here; they are not asserted to form a linear code. The prime, genuine lengths, full ranks, and erasure locations remain exact inputs; no stability under rank noise follows.

FD-344 §4 also makes the four-erasure obstruction **macroscopic**, by specializing [FD-333](../../findings/FD-333-one-quantum-fixed-rank-farey-spectral-instability.md). For primes `p>=19`, `p=7 mod 12`, put `i=(p+1)/4`, `j=(p-1)/3` and split the original excursions into `A=E_1...E_(i-1)`, `B=E_i...E_(j-1)`, `C=E_j...E_(p-j-1)`, `H=E_(p-j)...E_(p-i-1)`, `R=E_(p-i)...E_(p-2)`, with gap counts `(a,b,h,b,a)`. The reflected orders `BACRH` and `HACRB` have the same six ranks `(1,1+b,1+a+b,N-1-a-b,N-1-b,N-1)`. Erasing the pairs `{1,N-1}` and `{1+b,N-1-b}` leaves the same two index values `7/p` at the remaining ranks. Nevertheless their **full** energies tend respectively to `19/(24pi^2)` and `211/(288pi^2)`. The difference tends to `17/(288pi^2)>0`; every scalar estimator of energy from the common erased record has `liminf` worst-pair error at least `17/(576pi^2)`. The PNT rank asymptotics and bounded transport, together with `E_p=o(1)`, justify these limits; finite computations alone do not. Exact energy as an extra datum distinguishes this family, so it cannot answer the energy-matched spectral question below.

[FD-345](../../findings/FD-345-exact-energy-does-not-repair-four-reflected-farey-defect-erasures.md) shows that supplying **exact positive energy as well** does not repair four erasures for normalized spectral recovery. Reverse an excursion of length `ell` in slot `t`, starting at rank `r`, by exchanging its label with its reflected partner at the mirrored slot. Its discrepancy changes by `D'_(r+a)=C-D_(r+ell-a)`, where `C=(2t+1)/p-(2r+ell)/N`. If `N(2t+1)=p(2r+ell)`, this is a signed reversal preserving full energy exactly. This balance is realizable at `p=617`, `N=115996`, `t=4`, `r=743`, `ell=206`; the source gives two reflected permutations with the same sixteen defective ranks and all values equal outside the mirror pairs `{743,115253}` and `{949,115047}`. It uses the genuine excursion words, not an arbitrary vector isometry.

Let `K` be the Dirichlet second-difference matrix, `lambda_m=2-2cos(pi m/N)`, and `pi_m(D)=|Dhat_m|^2/||D||^2`. Then `sum_m lambda_m^2 pi_m(D)=||KD||^2/||D||^2`. Reversing the excursion preserves its internal adjacent-gap differences but changes its boundary contribution by `2(b-a)(L-H)`, where `a,b` are its first/last gaps and `L,H` the exterior adjacent gaps. The mirrored slot doubles this change. FD-345's pair has equal positive `||D||^2` and equal first spectral moments (the latter depend only on the gap inventory), yet `||KD^sigma||^2-||KD^tau||^2=-11577/141348948592544`. Thus at least one normalized **even-mode intensity** differs; unequal vectors alone would not prove this. Three known-position value erasures remain uniformly recoverable, while four can hide spectral shape even after energy is supplied. This finite certificate gives no fixed-low-mode separation, asymptotic gap, or classification of all two-pair erasures.

The exact destination-side condition for asymptotically vanishing energy is `T_p(sigma)->0`, not approximate local index integrality. Nothing here turns an artificial reassembled grid into another canonical Farey sequence or changes the true Farey identity; the gap is whether natural source restrictions enforce small **weighted cumulative** excursion displacement.
