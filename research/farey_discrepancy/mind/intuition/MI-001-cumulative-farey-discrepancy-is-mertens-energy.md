# MI-001 — Farey transfer is governed by source coherence, causal reciprocal capture, and recurrence geometry of protected blocks

**Evidence level:** supported by the exact source/occupation reductions through FD-073 plus the cited short-interval Möbius input; no density theorem for protected blocks and no RH criterion are claimed.

FD-071 shows why persistence by itself does not improve normalization: an arbitrarily later source spike can dominate the same-horizon denominator and dilute every fixed earlier episode. FD-072 identifies the complementary positive mechanism. At a `sigma`-record `X`, `sigma>1/2`, choosing `T=4X`, `D=3` confines every reciprocal quotient to the record-controlled prefix while the nonsquarefree row `4` samples the record exactly, forcing `nu_(4X,3)>=kappa_sigma>0`.

FD-073 strengthens this from a sparse point theorem to an intrinsic block theorem. The physical source satisfies the exact one-Lipschitz law `|H(u)-H(v)|<=|u-v|`. If `A=|H(X)|` and `|Y-X|<=eta A`, then `Y` remains a quantitative near-record with a uniform factor depending only on `eta` and `sigma`. Re-centering the causal observation at `T=4Y` transfers the FD-072 denominator argument to the whole block.

Hence for every fixed `sigma>1/2`, each power record carries an additive neighborhood of radius comparable with its own amplitude on which the nonsquarefree occupation and Schur defect stay uniformly positive. Under a false-RH frontier and `1/2<sigma<Theta`, strict records satisfy `A_j/X_j^sigma -> infinity`, so the protected blocks themselves have polynomially growing length. The obstruction is no longer that protected episodes might be literally isolated points.

At `sigma=1/2`, the same finite-horizon argument gives an explicit harmonic loss `nu_(4Y,3) >>_eta 1/log Y`. The causal mechanism therefore does not disappear at the critical exponent; it weakens by a quantified logarithmic factor.

The reusable lesson is sharper: **causal normalization becomes controllable on a whole source-determined neighborhood when bounded source increments make record dominance stable on the amplitude scale**. What remains is recurrence geometry beyond that intrinsic scale: record blocks may still have zero ordinary or logarithmic density and arbitrarily large gaps.

**Boundary.** FD-073 gives amplitude-length block thickness, not a global spacing or density theorem. Each nearby point uses its own recentered horizon `4Y`; the result does not preserve one fixed observation horizon while moving through the block. The critical endpoint bound still decays, and none of these statements yields an RH criterion by itself.
