# MI-001 — Farey transfer is governed by source coherence plus a logarithmic multiplicative-recurrence constraint

**Evidence level:** supported by the exact source/occupation reductions through FD-068 plus the cited all-short-interval Möbius theorem; no pointwise obstruction at an adaptively selected zero-frontier spike is claimed.

The cumulative Farey obstruction is controlled by the physical shell source

\[
c(n)=\Delta\mathcal H(n)=\sum_{dm=n}\frac{\mu(m)}d.
\]

FD-054--FD-065 separate deterministic transfer, short-interval cancellation, host placement, packet multiplicity, horizon normalization, and Schur-head leverage. FD-066 isolates the invariant direct-transfer threshold. If a zero-frontier spike `A_X=X^{Theta+o(1)}` stays comparable on a backward window

\[
L_X=X^{\ell+o(1)},
\]

then reciprocal-floor geometry and the later normalization give

\[
\gamma_{\rm coh}-\gamma_{\rm term}
=
\frac{2-2\Theta-\ell}{1+\lambda}.
\]

Consequently the direct packet improves the terminal scalar obstruction exactly when

\[
\ell>2(1-\Theta).
\]

Host exponent and head exponent only redistribute this fixed source-coherence budget. Below `Theta=2/3`, the missing source theorem is therefore stronger than “cancellation on shorter intervals”: it needs source-relative persistence of the dangerous amplitude on a sufficiently long power window, or a different normalization mechanism.

FD-067 first showed that a moving head cannot erase the defect on every point of a fixed nonsquarefree dilation ray. FD-068 promotes that recurrence to the full horizon set for deterministic q-subcovariant head schedules. With

\[
\nu_H=\frac{U_{H,D_H}}{E_{H,D_H}},
\qquad
\left\lfloor D_H/q\right\rfloor
\le D_{\lfloor H/q\rfloor},
\]

one has

\[
\nu_H\ge
w(q)\frac{E_{\lfloor H/q\rfloor}}{E_H}.
\]

Integrating `log(1/nu_H)` against multiplicative measure makes all interior scales telescope. For `D_H=H^{delta+o(1)}` this yields the global logarithmic geometric-mean bound

\[
\liminf
\prod_{H<X}\nu_H^{\lambda_H/W_X}
\ge
w(q)q^{-\{\delta+2\Theta(1-\delta)\}}.
\]

Hence extremely small occupation cannot be typical in logarithmic scale. For a linear four-adic head the bound is `3/16` unconditionally; more generally a positive lower logarithmic density of horizons has scalar Schur loss `gg 1/D_H`.

The two statements answer different questions. The coherence inequality is a **pointwise source-to-target transfer threshold**. The dilation law is a **global recurrence restriction** on how often the defect can become tiny. FD-068 closes ordinary cross-ray hopping as a positive-log-density escape for covariant schedules, but it still permits a dangerous sequence concentrated in a logarithmically negligible exceptional set.

A genuine advance must therefore change a load-bearing currency: prove coherence beyond `2(1-Theta)`, force zero-frontier spikes to occupy nonnegligible logarithmic scale, couple spike size to occupation at the same horizon, control the sparse exceptional set, or bypass the reciprocal-floor/horizon ledger.

**Boundary.** FD-066 is a conditional classification inside the current direct transfer architecture. FD-068 is an all-horizon logarithmic-density theorem for q-subcovariant schedules, not a pointwise bound at an arbitrary adaptive spike and not a new zero-free region.
