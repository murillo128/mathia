# MI-001 — Farey transfer is governed by source coherence, reciprocal capture, and destination normalization

**Evidence level:** supported by the exact source/occupation reductions through FD-071 plus the cited short-interval Möbius input; no normalized RH obstruction at an adaptively selected zero-frontier spike is claimed.

The cumulative Farey obstruction is controlled by the physical shell source. Earlier results separate deterministic transfer, short-interval cancellation, host placement, packet multiplicity, horizon normalization, and Schur-head leverage. FD-066 identifies the direct coherent-packet threshold: a frontier spike coherent on a sufficiently long source window can beat the terminal mechanism, while shorter coherence cannot.

FD-067--FD-068 add a different multiplicative recurrence statement: very low normalized nonsquarefree occupation cannot dominate logarithmic scale, but a sparse adaptive spike sequence can still evade a density theorem. FD-069 removes host selection as the missing pointwise map. A source spike of height `A_X` is detected by a nonsquarefree reciprocal row on every sufficiently late admissible horizon once `T` reaches order `X^2/A_X`.

FD-070 promotes that row into a growing packet using the physical one-Lipschitz law. A spike of height `A_X` produces a source interval of width comparable with `A_X`, which becomes about `T A_X/X^2` reciprocal rows; its forced nonsquarefree energy is of order `T A_X^3/X^2` once the packet has genuinely grown.

FD-071 closes the hope that persistence by itself eventually improves the normalized ratio. The **entire** reciprocal image of one coherent source episode has the same cubic-linear scale `T A_X^3/X^2`, so for fixed episode it grows only linearly with the later horizon. Under a false-RH frontier `Theta>1/2`, arbitrarily later source spikes sampled on one fixed prime row contribute denominator energy of order `T^(2Theta-o(1))`. One can pair two frontier spikes so that the earlier packet has diverging absolute energy and growing cardinality while its fraction of the same-horizon denominator tends to zero.

Thus the useful quantifier is not “the packet persists at every later horizon.” A denominator theorem must be **episode-adapted**: capture near the onset of the episode before unrelated later spikes dominate, or control the interference of later quotient samples by a source-specific relation. Absolute persistence cannot be converted into an all-later-horizon relative lower bound.

The decisive currency is therefore captured source energy relative to the denominator generated on the **same, appropriately chosen horizon**. Coherence, recurrence, and reciprocal sampling already produce substantial numerator mass; what remains is temporal/source localization of the normalization or a different target currency that does not permit later-source dilution.

**Boundary.** FD-071 does not prove that onset-scale horizons have a favorable denominator, does not bound the total nonsquarefree ratio `U/E`, and uses `Theta>1/2` for the dilution construction. It rules out only the inference from arbitrary-horizon persistence of one episode to uniform relative control at all later horizons.
