# MI-001 — Farey transfer is governed by source coherence, causal reciprocal capture, and destination normalization

**Evidence level:** supported by the exact source/occupation reductions through FD-072 plus the cited short-interval Möbius input; no density theorem for protected record horizons and no RH criterion are claimed.

The cumulative Farey obstruction is controlled by the physical shell source. Earlier results separate deterministic transfer, short-interval cancellation, host placement, packet multiplicity, horizon normalization, and Schur-head leverage. FD-069 removes host selection as the missing pointwise map, and FD-070 uses the physical one-Lipschitz law to expand a spike into a growing reciprocal packet.

FD-071 shows why persistence by itself does not improve normalization. The complete reciprocal image of one coherent episode grows only linearly with a later horizon, while under a false-RH frontier an arbitrarily later source spike can be sampled by one squarefree row and dominate the denominator. A fixed earlier packet can therefore have diverging absolute energy and still carry a vanishing same-horizon fraction.

FD-072 identifies a complementary positive mechanism: **causal horizon selection at a source power record**. If `X` is a `sigma`-record of `|H(n)|/n^sigma`, `sigma>1/2`, choose `T=4X`, `D=3`. Every surviving reciprocal quotient `floor(4X/r)` is at most `X`, so the record envelope bounds every source value visible to the denominator. The row `r=4` is nonsquarefree and samples `H(X)` exactly. The resulting ratio obeys

`nu_(4X,3) >= kappa_sigma > 0`,

with the same fixed lower bound transferred to the normalized Schur defect and a positive scalar saturation loss. If `Theta>sigma>1/2`, strict `sigma`-records occur arbitrarily far out, so the false-RH frontier itself supplies infinitely many protected source-selected horizons.

The reusable lesson is sharper than “adapt the horizon to the episode.” **A normalization can become controllable when the observation geometry is chosen so that its entire denominator is causally confined to a prefix already dominated by the selected source episode.** This avoids later-source dilution without requiring short-interval coherence or arbitrary-later persistence.

What remains is recurrence geometry rather than existence of a protected subsequence. Power records can be arbitrarily sparse, FD-072 gives no density or gap bound, and its constant degenerates as `sigma downarrow 1/2`. A stronger theorem must propagate causal protection to enough nearby horizons, prove a useful density/spacing statement for record or near-record episodes, or replace the strict power-record envelope by a broader source-specific domination principle.

**Boundary.** FD-072 does not give a positive fraction at every sufficiently large horizon, does not control the spacing of records, and does not yield an RH criterion. The record theorem is tied to the same physical source used by the denominator, the horizon `4X`, the surviving row `4`, and `sigma>1/2`; moving arbitrarily far beyond the selected episode reopens the FD-071 dilution mechanism.
