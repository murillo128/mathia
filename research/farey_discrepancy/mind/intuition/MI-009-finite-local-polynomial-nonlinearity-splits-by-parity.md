# MI-009 — Fixed local polynomial nonlinearity splits into invertible odd signal and smooth even background

**Evidence level:** exact route classification from [FD-111](../../findings/FD-111-finite-local-polynomial-prime-nonlinearities-split-into-smooth-even-and-invertible-odd-sources.md). No theorem is claimed for growing-degree/support, post-summation nonlinearities or cross-horizon functionals.

The nonlinear escape left by FD-109--FD-110 has a sharp first boundary. For the physical coefficient law

`a(n)=mu(rad n) phi(rad n)/n`,

any fixed polynomial in finitely many fixed multiplicative shifts `a(d_1 n),...,a(d_m n)`, summed over `n<=X`, reduces exactly to a finite linear combination of commuting divisibility transforms of the power sources

`H_k(X)=sum_(n<=X) a(n)^k`.

So multiplication before summation does not by itself create a new incidence geometry. The same prime-direction information still factors through commuting floor-dilation resolvents; only the scalar source `H_k` changes.

The power sources then split by parity. For every fixed odd `k`, `H_k` and the original physical source `H=H_1` determine one another through mutually inverse floor-convolution transforms whose kernels are absolutely summable against every positive polynomial weight. Hence they have exactly the same power-growth frontier. Odd local polynomial pieces are not a new endpoint channel; they are invertible re-encodings of the old one.

For every fixed even `k>=2`,

`H_k(X)=C_k X + O_(k,epsilon)(X^(1/2+epsilon))`.

After subtracting the deterministic linear mean, the even source is already confined to square-root scale. Fixed divisibility filters preserve that scale. Thus even local polynomial pieces cannot carry a false-RH fluctuation exponent strictly above `1/2`.

The reusable distinction is **nonlinearity versus information creation**. A polynomial operation can be algebraically nonlinear while remaining information-neutral at the endpoint because its odd sector is invertible and its even sector smooths to a deterministic mean plus square-root error. Counting algebraic degree is therefore not enough; a viable nonlinear escape must change the source-access geometry or the scale at which information is coupled.

The surviving classes are now genuinely nonlocal in this sense: nonlinear functionals applied after cumulative summation, cross-horizon products/persistence, degree or shift support growing with the horizon, or refinement observables not generated by finitely many local multiplicative shifts. Those operations are not classified by FD-111 and may couple source scales that the fixed local polynomial algebra cannot.

**Boundary.** FD-111 is a fixed finite local theorem. It does not say that all nonlinear arithmetic statistics reduce to `H`, nor that growing-degree polynomial families remain harmless. The parity split is a route restriction for the specific physical coefficient algebra, not an RH consequence.
