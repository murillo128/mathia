# MI-061 — Threshold zero repair must pay spectral height, population or background

**Evidence level:** exact RH-conditional synthesis from FD-273, FD-276--FD-279, with simple zeros inherited from the reciprocal-log cluster decomposition.

FD-276 prices one complete reciprocal-log component by the additive exponent budget carried by cluster cardinality and the desingularized collar background. In the polynomial-depth regime `x=N^(lambda+o(1))`, with `beta=log_2(3/2)` and `c=1-beta`, a threshold-sized component with background exponent `b` needs cluster scale `kappa` satisfying `kappa+b>=c`. FD-277 then converts the required reciprocal-log population into a spectral-height tariff using Riemann--von Mangoldt and the RH bound for `S(t)`: tame background forces one threshold component polynomially high.

FD-278 shows that splitting the repair among many individually subthreshold complete components does not remove the tariff. If the number of components is `R=x^(r+o(1))`, the largest component has size `(kappa+o(1))log x/log log x`, and the maximal background envelope is `x^(b+o(1))`, then even granting perfect inter-component phase alignment a threshold aggregate requires

`r+kappa+b >= c`.

For all components below `T=x^(tau+o(1))`, RH gives `r<=tau` and `kappa<=tau/[2(1-tau/(pi(1+1/lambda)))]`. Hence tame aggregate repair has a genuine height floor: uniformly over fixed polynomial depths, `T>=x^(0.2683381468...-o(1))`, with exponent `0.2725714441...` at balanced depth `lambda=1`. At subpolynomial height both population and cluster cardinality are subpower, so the background must pay essentially the entire missing exponent `c`.

FD-279 removes the hard-cut boundary component as a separate power-scale escape throughout the low-height range `tau<pi(1+1/lambda)`. A reciprocal-log component cut by the spectral truncation has diameter only `O(1/log log x)` under the same RH/simple-zero hypotheses, so an analyst-controlled cutoff can be moved past the whole component without changing its spectral-height exponent. The grouped divided-difference cancellation is then restored. What remains outside this packet-level closure is the effect of that tiny cutoff displacement on the explicit-formula remainder, together with genuinely different escapes such as multiple or off-critical zeros and polynomially ill-conditioned backgrounds.

The durable lesson is that close-zero amplification cannot be credited separately from the resources that make the grouped packet available. Internal inverse gaps disappear after complete recombination; cluster size, component population and background conditioning share one exponent ledger; RH zero counting prices the first two by height; and a hard-cut partial cluster can be completed at negligible spectral-power cost. The next obstruction is therefore no longer an unpriced aggregate or boundary-cluster loophole, but the remainder/source-coupling problem at cluster-complete truncations and the possibility of genuinely bad desingularized backgrounds at the required polynomial heights.

**Boundary.** These tariffs are conditional on RH and simple zeros, apply to the complete reciprocal-log packet decomposition at fixed polynomial depth, and do not control multiple zeros, off-line zeros, explicit-formula remainders, or the actual existence of the source-selected phase alignment. They are obstruction accounting, not an RH implication.
