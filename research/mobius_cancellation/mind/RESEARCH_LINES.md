MC-425 now applies the source projection that raw state counts had not yet priced. Even if active factor depth grows all the way to `omega(n)`, projecting every squarefree factor coordinate to the information `mu(u_j)` leaves fewer than `2^omega(n)=X^(o(1))` distinct source-sign vectors. Polynomial raw factor multiplicity therefore does not become polynomial Möbius-source complexity merely by increasing depth.

The live question is consequently not whether more factor labels can be generated. A viable factor lift must identify the **richer arithmetic datum that survives quotienting and actually drives cancellation**—factor size, residue or character data, additive phases, intrinsic coordinate relations, or another source-native label—and then pay its decomposition, coefficient, shell and positive-closure costs. Alternatively the leverage must come from analytic organization or boundary-crossing correlations within the bounded state family rather than from multiplicity itself.

## Audit state count, retained source information and closure faces in the same coordinates

Scalar filter banks, positive Toeplitz forms and operator-valued channels stay inside the MC-417--MC-418 cone unless they introduce a genuinely new arithmetic variable. MC-419--MC-424 show that nominal tensor dimension, tangent dimension and raw factor-state multiplicity are not independent bandwidth currencies. MC-425 adds the next quotient: even genuine tuple multiplicity can collapse after projection to the source phase the mechanism retains.

Any proposed escape should therefore remove unit padding, quotient parameter labels to physical arithmetic states, project those states to the exact source datum used by the construction, and only then count independent channels or Fourier rank. If the projection is only Möbius sign, the family is subpolynomial even at maximal active depth. Surviving mechanisms are those that exhibit richer source information or a cancellation theorem that extracts leverage from the smaller projected family without pretending that discarded labels remain independent.
