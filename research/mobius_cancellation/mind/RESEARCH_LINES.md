MC-258 audits the normalization of that descent. Although `q=R^2` descends exactly to `R`, the source projector carries an exterior factor `1/R`, so direct support counting keeps the main scale `T/R^2` and does not improve the `R=X^(1/4+o(1))` support threshold.

MC-259 tests the strongest obvious individual-modulus analytic follow-up. The current smooth-modulus variance theorem applies to the exact shell radical and, after summing over the source coset, its rank-one pretentious main term either cancels or is carried by a **single character in the blind annihilator**. That surviving mode is the reciprocal-`L` obstruction already isolated by the line. But the theorem's residual is weaker than direct rough support, so generic all-residue dispersion cannot supply the missing fixed power.

MC-260 tests the complementary support-sensitive variance route. Harper's sparse BDH theorem has the desired diagonal scale `Q sum |a_n|^2`, but only for `Q>sqrt(2x)`. At the descended modulus `R=X^(c+o(1))` this would require `c>1/2`; at the original square modulus `R^2` it requires `c>1/4`, exactly where the elementary `T/R^2` support bound already reaches square-root size. Thus the currently available sparse diagonal regime begins only after the live shell problem has disappeared.

MC-261 makes the alternative generation exit exact. If `R=prod_(y<r<=2y) r` and `H=<p mod R:p<=y>`, then `H` is exactly the set of residue classes represented by `y`-smooth integers. Hence `H=(Z/RZ)^*` is equivalent to **every** reduced residue class having a `y`-smooth representative. But `log R=y+o(y)`, so the shell asks for generators of size only `(1+o(1)) log R`. Existing all-class smooth-number and bounded-prime-product theorems operate at fixed-power generator scale; the favorable smoothness of `R` does not bridge the exponential gap from `R^delta` to `log R`.

MC-262 closes the obvious growing-order black-box quadratic-large-sieve continuation. Liu's explicit 2026 dependence extends shell parity balance from fixed order to `t=o(log^(4)y)`, but MC-242 already forces every surviving quadratic blind support to size at least `(1/log 2-o(1))log log y`. More sharply, once `t/log^(4)y -> infinity`, every choice of the large-sieve `epsilon` loses at least a factor of order `y` before the sparse shell-product family is paid for. The standard all-squarefree-moduli embedding therefore cannot reach the live support scale. This is a method obstruction, not a theorem that shell balance itself fails there.

The surviving problem therefore has three precise exits that are really two categories. Prove a shell-specific all-class generation theorem at the logarithmic cutoff; or control the one possible blind pretender by a source-scale signed theorem, either directly or through a **product-family quadratic large sieve whose leading cost follows the number of shell products and remains uniform through `t~log log y`**. Moving-shell tame coupling remains another source-specific route. Recounting support, invoking smoothness of `R` without logarithmic generation, feeding current smooth-modulus bounds through Cauchy--Schwarz, importing large-modulus sparse BDH at the wrong scale, or applying the ambient quadratic large sieve with hidden/generic subpower loss are closed.
