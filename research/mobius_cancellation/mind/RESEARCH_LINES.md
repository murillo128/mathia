# Möbius-cancellation research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Möbius-cancellation evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Move fixed-rank source geometry to a genuinely retained arithmetic resource

MC-423--MC-445 separate source diversity, representation fidelity, Möbius top-interaction and natural-source measure. Fixed-rank normalized prime-gap geometry is dense in the ambient simplex and polynomially realizable, while the most direct bounded-height natural ensemble concentrates on a terminal simplex vertex. Continuous fixed-rank normalized observables therefore do not acquire robust prime specificity merely from support richness or from switching to the canonical counting measure.

The surviving resources are qualitatively different: growing rank, another justified source measure, absolute scale, discontinuous arithmetic data, cross-rank structure, or coordinates that retain enough factor identity to survive the Möbius projection. Any such carrier still has to produce an analytic cancellation estimate after the exact top-interaction quotient.

## Price the outer normalized-gcd classes before attacking residual dispersion

MC-446--MC-453 close generic fixed-lcm occupancy, bare frequency synchronization, final beta-sieve sign variation, raw truncated Möbius as an all-splits well-factorable sequence, and bounded component multiplicity after separate positive quadratic closure. A conductor-power gain must therefore enter through a genuine auxiliary factor representation before generic recombination, majorization or squaring destroys the retained variables.

MC-458--MC-463 locate that resource precisely enough to remove the apparent internal gcd-sector multiplicity. For a fixed outer pair, all compatible residual gcd sectors reduce to the same normalized anchor modulus `T=r_2/(h_0,r_2)` and must be aggregated into coefficients `\widetilde\beta(s_0)`. A fixed-anchor square-root mechanism can only become competitive after the support gate `1+S/T>p/L` (with the exact interval-length notation of MC-464), while the aligned second residual amplitude remains unexpanded.

MC-464 makes the outer gate arithmetic rather than heuristic. If `M=ceil(SL/(p-L))-1`, the favorable normalized shifts are exactly the gcd classes `T<=M`, and over one period their number is `sum_{T|r_2,T<=M} phi(T)`, at most `M(M+1)/2`. Thus `M=o(sqrt(r_2))` gives zero-density favorable shifts; away from the endpoint, a positive-density supply requires the necessary scale `SL \gtrsim p sqrt(r_2)`. A short shift range has the additional entrance gate `HM>=r_2`, equivalently `HSL \gtrsim p r_2` in the same regime.

MC-465 propagates the actual standard positive q-vdC/Fejér weights through those gcd classes. Once at least two compatible positive shifts are present, the favorable off-diagonal Fejér mass obeys

`F_Q(M;g,r_2)/D_Q(g) <= min(1, 2 M(M+1)/r_2)`.

Hence the triangular preference for short shifts does not rescue a sub-square-root family: `M=o(sqrt(r_2))` still gives vanishing relative nonzero-shift mass, uniformly in the common factor and bandwidth. The square-root normalized-quotient threshold is therefore a mass obstruction for the canonical Fejér outer average, not merely an unweighted counting artifact.

MC-466 returns to the original shift and closes the apparent large-gcd coefficient escape for that same Fejér architecture. For a fixed outer pair `(r,r')`, the normalized quotient is exactly `T=r'/(h,r')`, and with `E_Q=Q(Q-1)/2` the favorable positive-shift mass satisfies

`F_Q(r,r';M)/E_Q <= min(1, M(M+1)/r')`.

This envelope is independent of `g=(r,r')` and remains valid after multiplication by arbitrary nonnegative, shift-independent outer coefficient-pair weights. Concentrating such outer mass on large-gcd pairs therefore cannot by itself recover the loss: the larger favorable fraction among compatible shifts is offset by the thinner supply of compatible original shifts. This statement does not control signed or shift-dependent coupling, pair-dependent interval thresholds, the retained residual coefficient, or the aligned unexpanded amplitude.

MC-467 removes most of the remaining pair-dependent-threshold freedom in the bulk. In the exact MC-463 parametrization, increasing the source index by one changes the original integer by `[r,r']s_0`. On a dyadic shell `X<=s_0<2X`, one anchor contains at most `1+X/T` represented residual divisors while each residual divisor has source interval length at most `1+N/([r,r']X)`. In the only potentially gainful interior regime their product satisfies

`A_X H_X <= 4N/([r,r']T)`.

Thus support growth and interval length consume the same residual scale rather than providing independent resources. Away from the near-complete endpoint `L_X>(1-eta)p`, the shell threshold satisfies `M_X<2N/(eta p[r,r'])`; combining this with MC-466 forces any nonvanishing favorable Fejér fraction onto small outer scales, with the coarse necessary restriction `r' \lesssim_eta (N/p)^(2/3)`. This is a necessary scale restriction, not a proof that those cells have negligible coefficient mass.

The live q-vdC question has consequently compressed to three genuinely separate costs. First price the total well-factorable coefficient mass of the surviving small-`r'`/small-lcm cells. Treat near-complete source intervals as a separate endpoint regime rather than hiding them in a global threshold. Only if enough outer mass survives should one ask whether `\widetilde\beta(s_0)` has useful weighted participation and whether the aligned unexpanded amplitude yields additional cancellation before absolute majorization or the second residual expansion. A deliberately redesigned non-Fejér or nonpositive architecture remains logically separate; MC-416--MC-417 and MC-466--MC-467 mean that its favorable-set concentration, interval geometry and diagonal/positive-energy cost must be established together rather than treated as free reweighting.
