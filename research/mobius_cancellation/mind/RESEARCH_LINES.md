# Möbius-cancellation research lines

This file records the current mathematical questions that survive the Möbius-cancellation evidence. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Move fixed-rank source geometry to a genuinely retained arithmetic resource

MC-423--MC-445 separate source diversity, representation fidelity, Möbius top-interaction and natural-source measure. Fixed-rank normalized prime-gap geometry is dense in the ambient simplex and polynomially realizable, while the most direct bounded-height natural ensemble concentrates on a terminal simplex vertex. Continuous fixed-rank normalized observables therefore do not acquire robust prime specificity merely from support richness or from switching to the canonical counting measure.

The surviving resources are qualitatively different: growing rank, another justified source measure, absolute scale, discontinuous arithmetic data, cross-rank structure, or coordinates that retain enough factor identity to survive the Möbius projection. Any such carrier still has to produce an analytic cancellation estimate after the exact top-interaction quotient.

## Determine the physical anti-diagonal law of the first-exit source

MC-484--MC-502 isolate the endpoint-only geometry and close a large family of coefficient-blind escapes. The visible inverse shift is gauge, least-active-prime cells are disjoint, common transforms preserve the relevant orthogonality, native same-root repeated-residue coefficients make the surviving positive cross terms constructive, and complete lower-prime hard-mask averaging cannot supply a conductor-power gain. MC-497--MC-501 further price the endpoint branch by source location, source width, block population and effective start participation in the actual physical coordinate.

MC-503--MC-504 show that for a fixed incomplete profile, or for an exact profile class with its true source mass, the centered quadratic conductor phase is controlled by effective support in the start/profile variables. MC-505 removes the arbitrariness of any profile decomposition: for the actual nonnegative start/profile table `B=(B_(x,j))`, the kernel `F(x+j)` sees only the matched Fourier diagonal of `B`, and double centering removes the two marginals and grand mean without changing those visible modes. The nuclear interaction norm `||PBP||_*` is therefore a valid decomposition-free upper certificate, but it is not the minimal observable.

MC-506 identifies that minimal quotient exactly. Define the physical anti-diagonal pushforward

`c_t = sum_x B_(x,t-x)`,

with total mass `W=sum_t c_t` and normalized law `nu(t)=c_t/W`. Then

`C_F(B)=sum_t c_t F(t)=W E_nu F`.

For every centered kernel, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

`|C_F(B)|/W <= sigma_F sqrt(Delta(B))`,

where

`Delta(B)=p sum_t (nu(t)-1/p)^2 = p sum_t nu(t)^2-1`.

For the current centered quadratic phase `sigma_F<1`. Hence fixed normalized bias requires a fixed excess of weighted collisions in the **actual conductor argument** `t=x+j`; equivalently, the source pair count with `t_alpha=t_beta mod p` must exceed the uniform baseline `W^2/p` by a fixed relative amount. The matched Fourier diagonal of MC-505 is simply the Fourier transform of this one-dimensional pushforward. Structure in `B` that leaves `c_t` unchanged is invisible to the kernel.

MC-507 shows that this quotient cannot be controlled from broad marginals or unitary-invariant matrix complexity. Every probability law `nu` on `F_p` is compatible with the same perfectly uniform start and profile-offset marginals via `B_(x,j)=(W/p)nu(x+j)`. More sharply, affine permutation couplings can have identical row/column marginals, support size, singular values, centered Frobenius norm and centered nuclear norm while ranging from exactly uniform anti-diagonal law to a point mass. Full support does not repair the obstruction. Thus marginal equidistribution, profile count, broad support and the singular spectrum of the table are not surrogates for the conductor law.

MC-508 then rules out fixed finite local mask data as the missing coupling. At fixed conductor phase, any prescribed finite set of lower-prime roots `-h q^(-1) mod ell` can be realized by infinitely many primes once the realizing first-exit prime may escape the active source scale. Finite congruence coordinates therefore do not force source/profile rigidity; any useful dependence must use localization, a growing mask, the moving cutoff/order, archimedean row geometry or another source resource absent from that CRT control.

MC-509 shows that simply retaining more exact local roots does not create free repeated-profile averaging either. If a localized source interval has diameter `H`, a fixed conductor residue contributes modulus `p`, and roots are retained for lower primes up to `z`, equality of root profiles forces equality modulo `pM_z`, where `M_z` is the corresponding primorial product. Every exact-profile fibre has size at most `1+floor(H/(pM_z))`; once `pM_z>H`, the truncated root profile is injective on the localized source. Because `log M_z~z`, only logarithmic mask depth is needed in a dyadic block. Localization-induced rigidity is therefore a **source-addressing effect**, not automatically a cancellation or averaging resource.

MC-510 shows that quotienting the one-row affine gauge does not remove this addressing barrier; it turns it into rational reconstruction of an ordered source pair and now quantifies the entire approach to that threshold. For an oriented pair `(q,r)` the gauge-invariant lower-prime state is `lambda_ell(q,r)=q r^(-1) mod ell`. Equality of relative tuples forces

`pM_z | q r' - q' r`.

For source primes in an interval of diameter `H` inside `[Q,Q+H]`, the sharp product-interval uncertainty is

`B=H(2Q+H)`,

so `pM_z>B` makes the relative tuple pair-injective; in a dyadic block the sufficient modulus is `pM_z>3Q^2`. More importantly, before injectivity every exact relative-state fibre is already list-bounded by the unresolved determinant range: in the common regime `H<p min I`,

`|F_sigma| <= 1+2 floor(B/(pM_z))`.

Thus exact relative-state recurrence is depleted continuously as the CRT modulus grows. A longer exact mask is not a free averaging resource even below full reconstruction: its available population must be paid for by the remaining rational-reconstruction uncertainty.

MC-511 shows that exact-state recurrence can cease to be a viable **collision carrier** strictly before MC-510 reaches pair reconstruction. If `b_u(t)>=0` is the pair-resolved contribution to conductor coordinate `t`, `L` bounds the exact relative-state fibre size and

`R_* = W^2 / sum_(u,t) b_u(t)^2`

is the effective pair-phase participation, then the same-state share obeys

`p C_same/W^2 <= pL/R_*`.

With the MC-510 localized fibre tariff, in the common regime `H<p min I`,

`p C_same/W^2 <= p/R_* + 2B/(M_z R_*)`, with `B=H(2Q+H)`.

Hence `R_*>>p` and `M_z R_*>>B` force `C_same=o(W^2/p)` even while `M_z` can remain far below the pair-injectivity scale `B/p`. Any material excess collision must then be carried by **distinct exact states that collide only after the physical pushforward**. The conclusion is conditional on nonnegative pair-resolved provenance and on proving the required `R_*` from the actual source weights; a large raw pair count is not a substitute.

The live arithmetic question is therefore to estimate the physical effective participation and the **cross-state anti-diagonal pushforward** before spending more exact-state resolution. A viable theorem cannot rely on broad marginals, finite local congruence rigidity, many source labels sharing one exact growing profile, gauge quotienting alone, or an unspecified recurrent class at intermediate mask depth. First compare the source-proved `R_*` with the MC-510 fibre tariff. If `pL_z/R_*` is already negligible, the load-bearing object is the many-to-one map from distinct gauge-invariant states to the observed coordinate `x+j`; if it is not, a justified coarser state may still retain a collision budget. Endpoint/cutoff geometry before exact conditioning or a genuinely signed/complex pre-aggregation relation remain separate escapes. If the total excess collision statistic is `o(1)`, MC-506 closes the current quadratic hard-mask bias; if it has fixed excess, collisions are still only necessary and signed alignment with the centered quadratic phase must be tested.

A genuinely `q`-dependent signed or complex coefficient introduced **before** the nonnegative aggregation remains a distinct escape and requires its own normed-measure analysis; the probability/collision formulation above must not be applied to it without that extra work. The accepted localized-full-profile clue records this surviving branch, but MC-509--MC-510 remove exact absolute-profile recurrence and exact relative-profile recurrence from its possible mechanism.

## Which weaker hypothesis can control the complete short-window variance?

[MI-100](intuition/MI-100-mellin-differences-retain-the-rh-short-window-variance-gain.md) retains MC-520's conditional mechanism: RH bounds the reciprocal-zeta multiplier on each fixed line `sigma>1/2`, and keeping the difference before Mellin Plancherel gives `J_mu(X,H)<<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)H` uniformly for integers `X>=2`, `1<=H<=X`. This already settles the RH-conditional balanced Farey variance target. Chinis's large-prime-factor/block-extension limitation in MC-178 does not obstruct this different method.

Can an unconditional or explicitly weaker-hypothesis source estimate instead control `J_mu(X,H)` with a fixed power saving sufficient for a declared destination? For the balanced Farey threshold this means `J_mu(X,N)<=X N^(2-eta+o(1))` with `eta>3/2-3c`, `c=1-log_2(3/2)`, on the same carrying window. If RH is removed, its shell localization must also be replaced by justified evidence. A bound on the complete moment must include exceptional-start contributions; assuming the reciprocal-zeta estimate used by MC-520 would reimport the conditional input.

[MI-101](intuition/MI-101-smooth-deconvolution-separates-zero-exclusion-from-endpoint-recovery.md) prices an all-scale version more sharply. For fixed `0<theta<1`, `0<eta<=1`, a complete uncentered moment `int_X^(2X)|M(y+X^theta)-M(y)|^2dy <<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)X^(theta(2-eta))` at every sufficiently large real `X` forces `zeta(s)!=0` for `Re(s)>alpha=1-theta eta/2` and `M(x)<<_epsilon x^(alpha+epsilon)`. Finite correction of smooth test weights removes the endpoint term `X^theta` from the zero-exclusion step; MC-521's classical logarithm/reciprocal-zeta estimate and Perron argument then recover the sharp sum. The direct endpoint optimum limits neither consequence for Möbius, although the sharp recovery does not extend to arbitrary coefficients from holomorphy alone. Diagonal estimates at fixed exponents approaching `1`, each with its own constants and all-scale quantifier, are already equivalent to RH by MC-520/521.

[MI-102](intuition/MI-102-centered-dyadic-variance-recovers-full-principal-parts.md) shows that a complete centered moment can have the same zero-exclusion strength with less scale data: MC-522 needs only `X_j=2^j`, `H_j=floor X_j^theta` and `V_j<<_epsilon X_j^(1+epsilon)H_j` at every sufficiently large `j`. Seam weights retain the unknown means in a periodic meromorphic factor. Arithmetic `o(T)` pole counts eliminate its poles; a finite recurrence on full Laurent principal parts then undoes the filter even at multiple zeros with zero residue. The whole fixed-exponent family approaching `1` is again equivalent to RH. Neither unknown means nor dyadic sampling alone provide a weaker arithmetic route.

[MI-103](intuition/MI-103-bounded-jumps-price-deleted-starts-in-pole-tests.md) removes the complete-scale premise by a different analytic mechanism. MC-528 cancels every unwanted reciprocal pole with full multiplicity and bounds the cancelled quotient, producing one fixed complex test of size `X^beta (log X)^(m-1)` on every large block. Selected scales cannot evade it. MC-530 extends its bounded inverse tests through `H<=X/[log X (log log X)^r]`, each fixed `r>1`. MC-531 then allows arbitrary deletion of `B<X/2` integer starts and any one fitted constant, with the precise bill `sqrt(X V_E(c))+B^2 >= C H X^beta (log X)^(m-1)`. The jump bound `|S_H(j+1)-S_H(j)|<=2` bounds the complete linear test using retained energy, without reconstructing a full second moment.

Can the source control both retained energy and deleted count on the same carrying scales? At `H=floor X^theta`, `0<theta<1`, bounds `V_E(c)<<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)H` and `B<<_epsilon X^(delta+epsilon)` exclude zeros to the right of `max(1-theta/2,2delta-theta)`. At the near-linear length above, the `B<<_epsilon X^(3/4+epsilon)` budget with that energy ceiling on any unbounded integer scale set is equivalent to RH. MC-532 gives a second RH-equivalent criterion at `H=floor(X/20)`, `K=floor(sqrt(2)H)`: there exist one unbounded scale set and mask/center families, fixed before epsilon, on which each length satisfies its retained-energy and deletion bounds for every epsilon. The masks and empirical centers may differ between channels. Its complete-pairing floor is `(sqrt(X V_1)+B_1^2)/H+(sqrt(X V_2)+B_2^2)/K >= C X^beta (log X)^(m-1)`; MC-520 gives the RH converse with empty masks. [Global MI-040](../../mind/intuition/MI-040-paired-box-inversion-transports-trimmed-mobius-pole-tests.md) retains the exact cross-line inverse behind this change of observations. The `3/4` exponent is sufficient, not proved optimal for Möbius; bounded-coefficient tent controls only show sharpness of generic absolute-mass recovery. A Möbius-specific signed recovery improvement remains distinct from supplying these unproved arithmetic ceilings. A centered or trimmed estimate still needs its missing mean or mass restored before feeding Farey's complete uncentered moment, and the Farey-selected window range requires its own source justification.

MC-533 sharpens the observation question in MI-103: can the actual source bound the centered average energies `Q_i/q_X <<_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)` for both macroscopic lengths on the fixed grid of spacing `floor(sqrt X)`, on one unbounded integer scale set fixed before epsilon? This is already RH-equivalent, despite retaining only `q_X asymp sqrt X` starts. The recoverable cost is `(sqrt(X Vtilde_i)+D_i)/L_i`, with nearest-sample multiplicity weights in `Vtilde_i` and `D_i=sum_j dist(j,R_i)`. The grid pays `D_i=O(X^(3/2))`; arbitrary irregular samples cannot replace weighted energy by the unweighted one without paying the largest cell size. The generic bound `D>=(X^2/q-q)/4` limits absolute-mass recovery. MC-534 now closes the signed/common-source escape over the exact-magnitude class `|a(n)|=mu(n)^2`: any `q<=kappa_phi X` interval observations admit two sign assignments with identical values but fixed smooth-target separation `Omega_phi(X/(q+1))`. Their prefixes agree at all queried endpoints; nonnegative bridges between endpoints have total area `Omega_phi(X^2/(q+1))` on a fixed derivative-phase interval. Thus signed tests, square-free support and deterministic adaptive observations alone cannot save a sample-count power. Which consequence of actual multiplicativity or reciprocal-zeta structure excludes these bridges and controls the missing signed pairing? No such estimate follows from the control, which is not a Möbius lower bound; sparse sampled energy also does not itself recover Farey's full uncentered moment.

[MI-104](intuition/MI-104-small-random-sample-energy-controls-dispersion-but-subquarter-multiplicative-bridges-survive.md) distinguishes two observation contracts. MC-537 uses `2ceil(X^(1/4))` independent starts per channel and permits an arbitrary common unbounded scale subset chosen after sampling. MC-539 uses only two starts per channel but requires both energy ceilings along every sufficiently late prescribed scale `X_k=k^4`, with independent draws across scales. The complete pole test forces dispersion on every block; bounded jumps give pair-detection probability `gg X^(-(1-beta)/2)`, whose sum diverges on these scales for every hypothetical `beta>1/2`. Both criteria remain RH-equivalent almost surely, without supplying the ceiling.

For two starts, can Möbius-specific information extend detection to faster prescribed scales or dependent observation designs? For each `a>4`, MC-540 in MI-104 supplies one fixed square-free sign source defeating `X_k=ceil(k^a)`: choose `3/4<kappa<1-1/a`; both channels have dispersion `asymp X^(2kappa-1)` on every large integer block, but endpoint-hit probabilities `O(X_k^(kappa-1))` are summable and both sampled energies are eventually at most one. Common-source consistency is therefore insufficient. The remaining precise gap is multiplicativity together with this dense-scale dispersion, or a stronger consequence of the actual reciprocal-zeta pole certificate: MC-540 lacks both, while MC-538's multiplicative signed-target control is only lacunary. Neither establishes an optimal rate for Möbius.
