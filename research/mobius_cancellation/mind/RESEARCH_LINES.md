# Möbius-cancellation research lines

This file holds the current mathematical questions suggested by the durable Möbius-cancellation intuitions. It is not a roadmap, task queue, status page, or history.

## Convert forced source-incidence cost into endpoint coercivity

**Linked intuitions:** `MI-013-bost-connes-regularity-needs-an-intermediate-transverse-category` through `MI-037-source-incidence-cost-is-a-redundancy-scale-tradeoff`.

MC-293--MC-306 move from individual character cost through family energy, multiquadratic packaging, common radical and quotient occupancy, and show that the previous full-family scalar capacities can be simultaneously saturated by a matched disjoint-singleton control.

MC-307 forces complexity to survive every relevant target-coordinate projection. MC-308 dualizes this to source-prime cuts. MC-309 then uses Landau--Page to show that moderate-rank low-product quotients must have at least half occupancy apart from at most one exceptional frequency. MC-310 turns reciprocal equal-mass Page-cheap closure into the exact rank ceiling `t<=nu_2(R)` (or `nu_2(R)+1` with the Page exception), and MC-311 uses Bose--Burton to force at least `2^(R-nu_2(R))-O(1)` Page-expensive directions.

MC-312 resolves the first source-sharing question. Fix any common lower-prime representatives for the `R` endpoint generators, let `n` be the number of distinct source primes used and `Z` the largest. Generalized Hamming supports plus the MC-308 source-cut theorem give, for `t~2 log_2 R`,

`(n-R+O(log R)) log Z >= (1-o(1)) log y`.

Thus a common package cannot simultaneously have near-minimal coordinate count and uniformly small source primes. At the natural scale `Z<=y^((1+o(1))/R)` one needs `n>=2R-o(R)`; under the Page-scale cap one needs a positive linear redundancy in the reciprocal regime. Conversely, `n=R+o(R)` forces a much larger individual source scale.

The live theorem is therefore no longer to prove merely that source cost is nonshareable: one explicit nonshareable resource is already present. The remaining arithmetic step is to show that **either side of the redundancy/scale dichotomy is itself expensive for the Möbius endpoint**. One route could bound how much source-coordinate redundancy an admissible representation can carry; another could show that the forced large source prime destroys the low-cost/near-negative endpoint geometry. A matched construction realizing one escape would show which currency still fails to coerce.

## Keep target projection, source-cut quotienting, cheap-subspace closure, expensive-set density and representation support distinct

MC-307 asks what remains when endpoint coordinates are deleted. MC-308 asks what remains when source coordinates are deleted and their span is quotiented out. MC-309 gives a Fourier occupancy floor for low-cost source cuts. MC-310 imposes a separate linear-closure condition on Page-cheap frequencies. MC-311 converts the absence of large cheap flats into a lower bound for the **number** of expensive directions. MC-312 then asks how a whole subspace of endpoint representatives can share source coordinates and converts generalized Hamming support into a prime-product cost.

These resources are not interchangeable. Half occupancy of a quotient does not imply a large cheap subspace; many cheap points need not close linearly; a large expensive complement need not have additive source costs; and a large common source radical can still be assembled either from many moderate coordinates or a few large ones. Any closing argument must state which quotient/subspace is used, what representative package is fixed, how its source supports overlap, what largest-prime scale is allowed, and which endpoint consequence consumes the resulting tradeoff.

## Preserve matched controls, but require them to match the hereditary representation-code profile

The exchangeable controls of MC-295/MC-298 show that low-order combinatorial regularity can coexist with forbidden parity. MC-306 proves aggregate capacities noncoercive; MC-307--MC-310 add hereditary projection/quotient and linear-subspace audits; MC-311 adds expensive-set density. MC-312 shows that an invertible re-encoding on only `R` equal-scale source coordinates is no longer a valid matched escape: generalized Singleton exposes an `O(log R)`-dimensional subcode whose tiny union support would have to carry almost a shell-sized prime product.

A surviving matched control must therefore match not only expensive-direction cardinality but also the generalized support hierarchy of the common source representation. Recounting expensive modes or summing their individual Page thresholds is obsolete; the next control must explicitly realize the redundancy/large-source-scale escape if that escape is genuinely available.
