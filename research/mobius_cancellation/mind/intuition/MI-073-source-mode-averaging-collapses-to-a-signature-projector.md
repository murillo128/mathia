# MI-073 — Formal averaging and finite divisor incidence collapse to lcm/signature discrepancy; retained quotients still need independent signed structure

**Evidence level:** exact synthesis from [MC-397](../../findings/MC-397-source-mode-averaging-collapses-to-signature-projector.md)--[MC-405](../../findings/MC-405-mangoldt-quotient-self-coupling-needs-cross-scale-cancellation.md), interpreted against the source-rank/codimension framework of MC-385--MC-396.

MC-397 shows that averaging squared source modes Fourier-collapses to a signature-subgroup projector. MC-398 shows that the Selberg divisor pair collapses through its lcm fibre coefficient, MC-399 removes the surviving lcm character scalar at first additive correlation, and MC-400--MC-402 consume moving endpoints, the complete endpoint and generic truncation through exact inversion/factorization.

MC-403 identifies what remains when the whole source family is retained. If `B_omega(X)` is the signed lcm coefficient mass in source-signature class `omega`, Parseval gives

`sum_(I!=0) |A_I(X)|^2 = H sum_omega |B_omega(X)-B_bar(X)|^2`.

After the truncation inversion, the nonprincipal family energy is exactly the squared norm of a Mertens-weighted signed signature-discrepancy vector. Collective source averaging is therefore not a second cancellation resource beyond that vector.

MC-404 strengthens the lcm audit from a quadratic regrouping to an algebraic normal form. For any finite divisor set, the pointwise algebra generated by the incidence predicates `1_{d|n}` is exactly the finite lcm join-semilattice algebra because `e_d e_e=e_[d,e]`. Every finite Boolean function, polynomial, higher-degree Selberg weight or threshold built only from those incidences has a unique expansion over the lcm closure. No finite incidence-only enrichment can manufacture a retained tuple variable before the analytic estimate begins.

MC-405 then supplies a matched control outside the lcm fibres. The Möbius--Mangoldt identity

`sum_(n<=x) mu(n) log n = -sum_(b<=x) Lambda(b) M(x/b)`

retains a genuine prime-power quotient variable `b`, but the signed family aggregates exactly to

`M(x) log x - integral_1^x M(t) dt/t`.

Moreover, from a pointwise input `|M(y)|<=C y^theta`, `0<theta<1`, triangle inequality gives unsigned mass of order `Cx` because `sum_(b<=x) Lambda(b)b^(-theta) ~ x^(1-theta)/(1-theta)`, while the exact signed aggregate is only `O_theta(C x^theta log x)`. At the critical scale the missing resource is therefore explicit cross-scale cancellation among `M(x/b)`, not source-label retention by itself.

The reusable audit now has two distinct gates. First ask whether a proposed index/tuple/family survives exact Fourier, lcm, inversion and first-correlation reductions. If it does, then ask whether the retained family is independently informative after aggregation: compute any exact self-coupling, price its unsigned mass, and identify the signed/phase relation needed to beat triangle inequality. **A variable counts only when a source-specific relation both survives the quotient and yields control not already equivalent to the original Mertens quantity.**

For the current frame, the remaining possibilities are narrow: prove signed near-equidistribution of the actual lcm coefficient across source signatures; obtain a genuinely joint estimate for the Mertens-weighted discrepancy vector; or retain quotient/phase/congruence/translation data whose downstream family carries an independently controlled signed interaction rather than another exact self-coupling.

**Boundary.** MC-403 is an exact `L^2` identity and lower-bound obstruction, not an upper bound. MC-404 is an algebraic collapse theorem for finite incidence-only observables. MC-405 rules out only the naive use of the canonical Möbius--Mangoldt quotient family with pointwise bounds/absolute values; it is not an impossibility theorem for quotient methods, scale decompositions or signed cross-scale estimates. None of these results proves a bound for `M` or RH.
