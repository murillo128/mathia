# MI-070 — Strong terminal savings can be blocked by the admission range

**Evidence level:** literature-grounded exact synthesis from [MC-384](../../findings/MC-384-chang-composite-character-theorem-retains-loglog-source-horizon.md), interpreted against the source-horizon mechanisms isolated in MC-382--MC-383.

A character-sum theorem can be quantitatively much stronger than an endpoint argument needs after it applies and still provide no new source range because its **hypotheses** already impose the old horizon.

MC-384 tests this directly with Chang's composite-modulus theorem. In the squarefree primitive specialization relevant to the current exact-endpoint source package, one of the theorem's admission conditions has the scale

`log N > C log(2) log q / log log q`.

Writing the physical interval scale as `N=y^(1+o(1))` and the source modulus as `q=y^(a(y)+o(1))` turns that condition itself into

`a(y)=O(log log y)`.

Once admitted, the theorem supplies a stretched-exponential saving strong enough to dominate what the frame contradiction needs. The obstruction is therefore not a weak terminal exponent, and granting favorable local factor sizes does not remove it. The desired super-loglog moduli are rejected before the saving is available.

This is different from both nearby obstructions. MC-382 prices a theorem that is applicable but loses strength through an exponentially deep q-van der Corput cascade. MC-383 prices a collective estimate whose favorable family factor is absorbed by a fixed positive power of the complete modulus. MC-384 instead says that **applicability is itself a source currency**. A theorem can win the output inequality and still lose the input geometry.

The reusable design rule is to audit an analytic input in physical source coordinates in this order: first translate every hypothesis into the intended scaling regime; only if the target regime is admitted should the terminal saving be compared with the required destination error. Optimizing the conclusion of a theorem whose admission condition already excludes the desired scale cannot move the research frontier.

For the current Möbius endpoint, a genuinely new factorization-sensitive or multilinear estimate must therefore do more than exploit smooth/squarefree conductor structure. Its **admission range** must itself extend beyond `q=y^(O(log log y))`, or it must avoid a single-character/global-modulus formulation whose hypotheses reconstruct that barrier.

**Boundary.** MC-384 does not prove that every composite character-sum theorem has a log-log admission ceiling, nor that Möbius cancellation cannot use larger source moduli. The conclusion is method-specific to the audited theorem shape. A different theorem may have weaker local-factor assumptions, a factor-local domain of applicability, a rank-sensitive admission condition, or an entirely different source-to-destination interface.
