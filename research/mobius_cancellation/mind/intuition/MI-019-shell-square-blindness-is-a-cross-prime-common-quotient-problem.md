# MI-019 — Shell-square blindness is a high-support cross-prime common-quotient problem

**Evidence level:** proved for the shell-square subgroup geometry through MC-240; no fixed-power Möbius cancellation or Fourier-decay theorem follows.

MC-238 shows that smooth-dilation averaging is blind on the annihilator of the subgroup generated by primes up to `y`. MC-239 classifies this obstruction for

\[
Q_S=\prod_{r\in S}r^2,
\qquad
H_y(Q_S)=\langle p\bmod Q_S:p\le y\rangle.
\]

The whole principal-congruence kernel of reduction modulo the squarefree radical lies in `H_y(Q_S)`. Every blind character therefore factors through the shell-prime factors themselves. For sufficiently large `y`, the subgroup also projects surjectively onto every individual factor, so a proper global subgroup can survive only through a **subdirect relation among several primes**, not through a local lift defect.

MC-240 strengthens “several” quantitatively. Write a nonprincipal blind character as

\[
\chi=\prod_{r\in S}\chi_r,
\qquad
T(\chi)=\{r:\chi_r\ne1\}.
\]

Then, for all sufficiently large `y`,

\[
\boxed{|T(\chi)|\ge7.}
\]

Indeed the active character has conductor at most `(2y)^k` when `k=|T(chi)|`. Pollack's unconditional prime nonresidue theorem supplies a prime witness below conductor exponent `1/(4sqrt(e))+epsilon`. Since six times that exponent can be chosen below one, support `k<=6` would force a witness prime below `y`, contradicting blindness on every prime up to `y`.

The quadratic common-quotient sector inherits the same floor. For the Legendre matrix from MC-238, every nonzero kernel vector has Hamming weight at least seven, so

\[
\operatorname{spark}_{\mathbb F_2}(A)\ge7.
\]

The reusable lesson is sharper than local-versus-global generation: **complete local generation plus exclusion of all low-support global relations can still leave a genuinely high-dimensional blind quotient**. A code can have large minimum distance without having zero kernel. Eliminating pairs, triples, and other small relation motifs is therefore not the same as proving product generation.

The next algebraic object is the high-support annihilator: relations involving at least seven shell factors, their possible dimension, and their coupling to the source-selected progression vector. A useful theorem must either show this sector is trivial, show its weighted Fourier contribution has a fixed-power gap, or prove that the arithmetic source is orthogonal/insensitive to it for a structural reason.

**Boundary.** The support floor is asymptotic and algebraic. It does not prove `H_y(Q_S)` is the full product, does not estimate the weighted dilation measure on nonblind characters, and does not bound the source-selected Möbius progression sum. Large-support blind characters remain compatible with all proved statements.
