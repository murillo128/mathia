# MI-019 — Shell-square blindness, if it survives, is a quantitatively growing cross-prime common-quotient phenomenon

**Evidence level:** proved for the shell-square subgroup geometry through MC-242; no fixed-power Möbius cancellation, annihilator triviality, or growing-support Fourier-decay theorem follows.

MC-238 identifies smooth-dilation blindness with the annihilator of the subgroup generated by primes up to `y`. MC-239 shows that the whole prime-square congruence kernel is already generated and that every individual shell-prime factor is generated asymptotically. A proper global subgroup can therefore survive only through a subdirect relation among several shell primes.

MC-240 first excluded support at most six, and MC-241 upgraded this to qualitative divergence: every surviving blind sequence has `m(chi)->infinity`. MC-242 makes that collective cost quantitative by returning to the explicit iterated q-van der Corput inequalities rather than substituting a growing parameter into a fixed-depth theorem. For every surviving nonprincipal blind shell character,

`liminf m(chi)/log log y >= 1/log 2`,

or equivalently `2^m(chi) >= (log y)^(1-o(1))`.

The mechanism is source-specific. Blindness forces `chi(n)=1` on every `y`-smooth integer, hence a linear-sized character sum slightly above `y`. Iterated differencing peels off the shell-prime factors; if the support is below `(1/log 2-o(1)) log log y`, the explicit repeated-root and prime-complete-sum gains force that same normalized character sum to vanish, a contradiction.

The reusable lesson is sharper than “local generation is not global generation.” **Complete local generation plus elimination of all relations below a double-logarithmic support floor can still leave a genuinely collective blind quotient.** Support size alone does not imply zero kernel, and even a large-support surviving mode can matter unless its coupling to the actual Möbius progression vector is controlled.

The next algebraic object is therefore an annihilator whose support is at least `(1/log 2-o(1)) log log y`. A useful theorem must push the explicit differencing architecture beyond that floor, prove eventual annihilator triviality by another mechanism, or show source-specific weak coupling/Fourier decay for every surviving high-support character.

**Boundary.** The constant `1/log 2` is the threshold of the present iterative estimate, not an asserted arithmetic optimum. MC-242 controls support size only; it does not bound the number or distribution of blind characters, prove product generation, or estimate the signed Möbius amplitude carried by them.
