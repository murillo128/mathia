# MI-019 — Shell-square blindness, if it survives, is an unbounded-support cross-prime common-quotient phenomenon

**Evidence level:** proved for the shell-square subgroup geometry through MC-241; no fixed-power Möbius cancellation, annihilator triviality, or growing-support Fourier-decay theorem follows.

MC-238 identifies smooth-dilation blindness with the annihilator of the subgroup generated by primes up to `y`. MC-239 shows that the whole prime-square congruence kernel is already generated and that every individual shell-prime factor is generated asymptotically. A proper global subgroup can therefore survive only through a subdirect relation among several shell primes.

MC-240 initially made “several” quantitative by excluding support at most six. MC-241 upgrades this from a fixed numerical floor to a qualitative divergence theorem: for every fixed `K`, all sufficiently large shells contain no nonprincipal blind character supported on `K` or fewer shell primes. Along any surviving blind sequence,

`k(chi) -> infinity`.

The proof uses a feature special to the shell conductors. Blindness makes the character equal to one on every `y`-smooth integer, giving a linear-sized ordinary character sum on a slightly super-`y` interval. For fixed support, the conductor is a product of finitely many primes in `(y,2y]` and falls within the near-linear smoothness range of the 2026 Cochrane--Granville--Zheng character-sum theorem, which forces power cancellation on that same interval. These two statements are incompatible.

The reusable lesson is sharper than “local generation is not global generation.” **Complete local generation plus elimination of every bounded-size global relation can still leave a genuinely collective blind quotient.** Minimum support tending to infinity does not imply zero kernel, and it says nothing by itself about the Fourier coefficient of the actual source vector on a surviving high-support mode.

The next algebraic object is therefore an annihilator whose support grows with the shell. A useful theorem must either control the smooth-modulus character sum uniformly as the support grows, prove product generation by another mechanism, or show source-specific orthogonality/weak coupling to every surviving high-support character.

**Boundary.** MC-241 is qualitative in support size. It supplies no rate `k(y)`, does not prove eventual annihilator triviality, and does not estimate the source-selected Möbius progression sum. Large-support blind characters remain compatible with the proved statements.
