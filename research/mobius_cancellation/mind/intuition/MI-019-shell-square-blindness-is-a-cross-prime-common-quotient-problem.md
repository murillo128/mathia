# MI-019 — Shell-square blind modes are cross-prime common-quotient relations, not local lift failures

**Evidence level:** proved for the shell-square subgroup geometry in MC-239; no fixed-power Möbius cancellation or Fourier-decay theorem follows.

MC-238 shows that smooth-dilation averaging is blind on the annihilator of the subgroup generated by primes up to `y`. MC-239 classifies this obstruction much more tightly for

\[
Q_S=\prod_{r\in S}r^2,
\qquad
H_y(Q_S)=\langle p\bmod Q_S:p\le y\rangle.
\]

Let `R_S=rad(Q_S)` and reduce units modulo `R_S`. The whole principal-congruence kernel is already contained in `H_y(Q_S)`. Hence every annihilator character factors through the squarefree radical: the prime-square lifts create no additional blind sector.

For sufficiently large `y`, the projection of `H_y(Q_S)` onto each local factor `(Z/rZ)^x` is surjective, and therefore so is its projection onto `(Z/r^2Z)^x`. A proper global subgroup can survive only as a **subdirect relation among several shell-prime factors**. In particular a nonprincipal blind character must be nonprincipal at at least two shell primes.

The character-order support is correspondingly constrained. If a prime `ell` occurs in a blind character, then `ell` must divide `r-1` for at least two distinct shell primes. No odd `ell>y/2` can do so. The quadratic sector remains special because `2` divides every `r-1`; this explains why the Legendre-matrix rank test from MC-238 probes a genuine common quotient rather than a cosmetic first approximation.

The reusable lesson is that **local generation can be complete while global product generation still fails through shared quotient relations**. Eliminating local lift defects is not enough; the remaining obstruction is simultaneous compatibility across coordinates.

**Boundary.** This is an algebraic support theorem. It does not prove `H_y(Q_S)=G_S`, does not estimate the weighted dilation measure on nonblind characters, and does not bound the source-selected Möbius progression sum. Even if the blind quotient is trivial, the analytic route still needs fixed-power Fourier decay or another source-coupled signed mechanism.
