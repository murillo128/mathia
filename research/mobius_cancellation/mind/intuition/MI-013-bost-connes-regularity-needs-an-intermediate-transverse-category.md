# MI-013 — Bost--Connes regularity must leave every fixed-depth scalarized parity class

**Evidence level:** supported by exact canonical-representation classifications through MC-153

## Core intuition

The Bost--Connes weak-completion problem is not repaired by adding the system's obvious regularities, scalar prime weights, divisibility splits, or any fixed finite depth of mixed prime commutators. The surviving category must be transverse to canonical energy, quantitatively intermediate between profinite loss and target-complete weak closure, and **non-finite-depth in the prime-factor parity direction**.

The new boundary is stronger than “use cross-prime coupling.” MC-153 gives all distinct-prime mixed commutators through an arbitrary fixed depth and still constructs a support-matched sequence with linear mean that matches the complete norm hierarchy. A viable structure must therefore retain a relation across unbounded factor depth, states, or non-scalar channel interactions in a way that is not merely the full Möbius parity table under another name.

## Strongest justified principle

MC-147 identifies the target-completeness problem: the represented coefficient weak closure contains every bounded diagonal and the represented full weak closure is all bounded operators. MC-148 proves that the canonical Hamiltonian supplies no restriction there; every bounded diagonal is infinitely smooth and entire under the canonical flow. MC-149 tests the first transverse family: raw semigroup commutator boundedness admits every bounded diagonal, while compactness and finite Schatten decay exclude Möbius for every nontrivial dilation.

MC-150--MC-151 show that scalar weighting cannot create a hidden critical category. For arbitrary prime weights `w_p` and external `ell^q` aggregation, boundedness is exactly the condition `w in ell^q`, shared by Möbius, its squarefree-support control, and Liouville-type orientation controls. Dirichlet weights therefore place the threshold at `sigma=1/q`; the familiar half threshold at `q=2` is chosen by the aggregation space rather than by Möbius cancellation.

MC-152 retains divisibility sectors before scalarization and remains parity-blind. MC-153 keeps all mixed distinct-prime commutators through depth `R`. Their norms are the suprema of multiplicative Boolean differences, and Möbius saturates them at the anchor. Yet one parity-truncated squarefree sequence `b_R` matches every one of those norms, every scalar weighting, and every external `ell^q` aggregation through depth `R`, while `sum_{n<=x} b_R(n)` has nonzero linear density. Thus no fixed prime-factor depth can control the long additive excursion.

## Counterevidence / boundary

The classification is representation-specific and concerns the canonical Hamiltonian/semigroup sector followed by the stated scalar norm readouts. It does not classify genuinely unbounded-depth constructions, state-dependent or nonlinear relational constraints, non-scalar/twisted correspondences, alternative representations, infinite-prime coupled objects, or observables mixing the semigroup sector with other arithmetic data.

Those are category exits, not evidence. A successful escape must be source-forced, must exclude the explicit `b_R` family uniformly as `R` grows, and must transfer to additive Mertens excursion without simply reconstructing the full Möbius sign sequence.

## Epistemic status

**Exact no-go synthesis for canonical energy, raw semigroup regularity, scalar-weighted rows, divisibility splits, and every fixed mixed-commutator depth; the first plausible surviving category must be genuinely unbounded-depth or otherwise relational.**

## Falsification criterion

Exhibit a fixed-depth canonical mixed-commutator scalarization that distinguishes Möbius cancellation from the MC-153 parity-truncated controls, or invalidate the exact mixed-difference/norm identities. A positive escape should define an intrinsic unbounded-depth or genuinely non-scalar relation, prove that Möbius satisfies it non-tautologically, rule out the matched linear-bias controls, and derive quantitative additive excursion control below the RH-complete endpoint.
