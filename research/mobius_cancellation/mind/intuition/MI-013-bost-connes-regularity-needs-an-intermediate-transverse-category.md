# MI-013 — Bost--Connes cancellation needs a middle category between phase-blind size and target-equivalent sign

**Evidence level:** exact finite-algebra, modular-twist, and heat-orbit classifications through MC-171

## Core intuition

The tested Bost--Connes constructions repeatedly collapse into three endpoints: phase-blind operator size, a local phase selector, or a signed global quantity already as hard as the Möbius/RH target. None supplies a stable intermediate measure of accumulated cancellation.

## Strongest justified principle

MC-166 extends the finite two-sector grading through arbitrary finite coefficient insertions. MC-167 shows the canonical modular twist factors through the Hamiltonian commutator, so absolute regularity is phase-blind. MC-168 shows the associated Lipschitz condition detects local phase compatibility, not cancellation magnitude. MC-169 produces a genuine infinite signed heat orbit but its critical pointwise small-heat type is RH-equivalent. MC-170--MC-171 then show that stationary and sufficiently long moving positive mean-square statistics wash the phase out, with a sharp exponential horizon `tau=3/8` for the matched control.

## Program consequence

A useful continuation must define a source-forced observable whose modulus changes continuously with cancellation quality, survives beyond local phase consistency, and is not simply equivalent to `M(x)` or the RH heat bound. Sub-washout moving-time statistics are one remaining category to test, but only with an independent theorem relating them to cancellation.

## Counterevidence / boundary

The heat results do not classify all nonlinear or signed finite-window statistics below the washout horizon, and the finite two-sector theorem does not cover genuinely new infinite operations that couple sectors before compression.

## Epistemic status

**Supported category-boundary synthesis; no intermediate cancellation observable has yet been established.**

## Falsification criterion

Exhibit a finite coefficient/twist construction with a third independent phase sector, or a long-window positive heat statistic retaining prime orientation beyond the MC-171 horizon. A positive escape should instead prove a quantitatively stable intermediate signed statistic outside those classes.
