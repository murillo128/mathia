# MI-041 — Prime-shell family cancellation, not primitive provenance, closes the natural reciprocal endpoint

**Evidence level:** literature-assisted source-specific resolution from [MC-319](../../findings/MC-319-smooth-modulus-zero-free-dyadic-resolution-barrier.md), the exact matched observation-category obstruction [MC-320](../../findings/MC-320-smooth-modulus-interval-cancellation-prime-shell-conductor-inflation.md), and the decisive prime-family estimate in [MC-321](../../findings/MC-321-prime-halasz-montgomery-closes-natural-endpoint-family.md).

MC-319 shows that the natural endpoint source forces a smooth common modulus with strong collective zero-free control, but the known Rodosskii conversion operates on intervals much broader than the one-dyadic-shell signal consumed by the endpoint. MC-320 then proves that stronger **ordinary integer-interval cancellation** still targets the wrong observable: an imprimitive conductor-`4` character can have tiny interval sums while remaining maximally biased on the selected prime shell.

MC-321 identifies the missing theorem more directly than the primitive-conductor route suggested by that control. In the exact reciprocal endpoint there are `R` distinct characters, all viewed modulo one common `q<=4y^2`, and each has a fixed nonvanishing bias on the same dyadic prime shell. The prime Halász--Montgomery estimate of Puchta, in the form used by Klurman--Mangerel--Teräväinen, bounds the total squared correlation of a family of distinct characters against arbitrary coefficients supported on primes. Choosing those coefficients to be the exact endpoint shell turns the `R` simultaneous biases into a lower bound that contradicts the mean-square estimate.

The useful general statement is stronger than the endpoint specialization. For `R->infinity`, `R=o(log y)`, common modulus `q<=y^(8/3-delta)`, and a prime set of size comparable to `y/log y`, a family of `R` distinct characters cannot all carry a fixed-size bias on that same set for sufficiently large `y`. The endpoint lies comfortably inside this range.

So the missing resource was not generic “more cancellation” and not primitive conductor provenance by itself. It was **cancellation in the exact observation category, aggregated across the whole source-forced family**. Common-modulus smoothness and unweighted interval pseudorandomness can miss the endpoint, while a prime-supported family theorem sees it directly.

The reusable audit is: when a construction needs many source modes to be simultaneously exceptional on one thin population, do not test the modes only one at a time or on a broader ambient population. Search first for a family-energy inequality whose coefficients can localize to the exact destination set. Family multiplicity can be a stronger source constraint than individual provenance.

**Boundary.** MC-321 closes the exact natural-scale reciprocal one-defect endpoint under its common-modulus and growing-family hypotheses. It does not prove Möbius cancellation, does not rule out endpoint variants with too few strongly biased modes, larger common modulus, or materially different defect geometry, and does not make prime Halász--Montgomery a universal substitute for conductor information in other constructions.
