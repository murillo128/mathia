# MC-301 — Multiplicative large sieve gives a square-root source-energy capacity bound

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `EXACT-DERIVED` · `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT` · `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` · `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM` · `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` · `NON-RH`.

## Claim

Continue the localized Legendre-shell notation of `MC-291`–`MC-300`. Let

\[
\mathcal R_y
=\{r:y<r\le2y,\ r\text{ prime},\ r\equiv1\pmod4\},
\qquad N=|\mathcal R_y|,
\tag{1}
\]

let `H` be the binary span generated by the occupied lower-prime Legendre sign cells, and for `\lambda\in H^*` write

\[
x_\lambda
=\frac1N\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_y}(-1)^{\lambda(s(r))}.
\tag{2}
\]

Use the source cost from `MC-293`:

\[
\mathcal Q(\lambda)
:=
\min\left\{
\prod_{p\in V}p:
\prod_{p\in V}\left(\frac pr\right)
=(-1)^{\lambda(s(r))}
\ \forall r\in\mathcal R_y
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

For every real source cap `C>=2`, the complete low-source shell-bias energy obeys

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\lambda\in H^*\setminus\{0\}\\
                 \mathcal Q(\lambda)\le C}}
|x_\lambda|^2
\ll
\left(1+\frac{C^2}{y}\right)\log y.
}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, throughout the full square-root conductor range

\[
C\le \sqrt y,
\tag{5}
\]

one has the collective capacity bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\lambda\ne0\\\mathcal Q(\lambda)\le\sqrt y}}
|x_\lambda|^2
\ll \log y.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
d_\lambda=\frac{1+x_\lambda}{2}\le\tau<\frac12,
\tag{7}
\]

then `|x_lambda|>=1-2tau`, and hence

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\lambda\ne0:\mathcal Q(\lambda)\le C,\ d_\lambda\le\tau\}
\ll
\frac{(1+C^2/y)\log y}{(1-2\tau)^2}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Thus below source cost `sqrt(y)` there can be only `O_tau(log y)` strongly near-negative shell modes. Unlike `MC-293`, `MC-299`, and `MC-300`, this conclusion does not isolate a single Page-exceptional character and does not use a zero-free region or Deuring–Heilbronn repulsion. It is a purely family-energy statement coming from the classical multiplicative large sieve.

The bound becomes especially informative on the reciprocal endpoint-partition extremizer exposed in `MC-295`–`MC-296`. Suppose

\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_R
\tag{9}
\]

are linearly independent, their defect sets partition the shell, and

\[
d_{\lambda_i}=\frac1R
\qquad(1\le i\le R).
\tag{10}
\]

For `I subseteq [R]` write `lambda_I=sum_{i in I}lambda_i`. Then `MC-296` gives exactly

\[
x_{\lambda_I}
=(-1)^{|I|}\left(1-\frac{2|I|}{R}\right).
\tag{11}
\]

Summing over the complete selected Fourier subspace yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{I\subseteq[R]}|x_{\lambda_I}|^2
=\frac{2^R}{R}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Combining `(6)` and `(12)` therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq[R]\\
                 \mathcal Q(\lambda_I)>\sqrt y}}
|x_{\lambda_I}|^2
\ge
\frac{2^R}{R}-1-O(\log y).
}
\tag{13}
\]

and, since every squared bias is at most one,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq[R]:\mathcal Q(\lambda_I)>\sqrt y\}
\ge
\frac{2^R}{R}-O(\log y).
}
\tag{14}
\]

Whenever

\[
\frac{2^R}{R\log y}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{15}
\]

asymptotically all of the **Fourier bias energy** in this reciprocal defect-partition subspace is forced above square-root source cost. This is a substantially larger source threshold than the pointwise Page/Deuring–Heilbronn bands in `MC-293` and `MC-299`–`MC-300`.

However, `(15)` also exposes an exact near miss. The blind-support floor from `MC-242` is

\[
R\ge\left(\frac1{\log2}-o(1)\right)\log\log y,
\tag{16}
\]

for which `2^R=(\log y)^{1+o(1)}` and therefore

\[
\frac{2^R}{R}
=\frac{(\log y)^{1+o(1)}}{R}.
\tag{17}
\]

At that critical floor the complete reciprocal-partition Fourier energy is still smaller by a factor of order `R` than the generic `O(log y)` large-sieve capacity. To force `(13)` to be nontrivial by this argument one needs, asymptotically, about one additional `log_2 R` layer of independent endpoint directions:

\[
R
\ge
\log_2\log y+\log_2 R+\omega(1).
\tag{18}
\]

Thus the classical family-energy mechanism reaches dramatically farther in source conductor than the Page pointwise theory, but it still misses the exact blind-support frontier by a small and explicit additive dimension gap. No bound for `M(x)` follows.

## 1. Every low-source shell functional is a distinct primitive real character

Fix a nonzero `lambda` and choose a set `V` attaining `(3)`. Put

\[
m_\lambda=\prod_{p\in V}p=\mathcal Q(\lambda),
\qquad
\chi_\lambda(n)=\prod_{p\in V}\left(\frac np\right).
\tag{19}
\]

As in `MC-293`, the moduli in `V` are distinct odd primes, so `chi_lambda` is a primitive real Dirichlet character of squarefree conductor `m_lambda`. Because every shell prime satisfies `r equiv 1 mod 4`, quadratic reciprocity gives

\[
\chi_\lambda(r)
=
\prod_{p\in V}\left(\frac rp\right)
=
\prod_{p\in V}\left(\frac pr\right)
=(-1)^{\lambda(s(r))}.
\tag{20}
\]

Hence

\[
N x_\lambda
=
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_y}\chi_\lambda(r).
\tag{21}
\]

The selected characters are distinct. Indeed, if two chosen primitive characters agreed as Dirichlet characters, they would agree on every shell prime and hence define the same values on every occupied sign cell. Since those cells generate `H`, the corresponding functionals in `H^*` would be equal. Possible nonuniqueness of a minimizing ambient representative causes no problem: choose one representative for each functional; the resulting selected primitive characters remain injectively indexed by the functionals.

This injectivity is the only structural input needed to insert the entire low-source family into one classical large-sieve sum.

## 2. The multiplicative large sieve gives the energy bound

Let

\[
a_n
=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\in\mathcal R_y,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\tag{22}
\]

viewed as a coefficient sequence on the interval `(y,2y]`, of length `y`. The classical multiplicative large sieve for primitive Dirichlet characters states, in this shifted-interval form,

\[
\sum_{q\le C}\frac q{\varphi(q)}
\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}^{*}
\left|\sum_n a_n\chi(n)\right|^2
\ll
(y+C^2)\sum_n|a_n|^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Here the star denotes primitive characters. This is standard large-sieve theory; `MC-068` already uses the same multiplicative form, citing Iwaniec–Kowalski, *Analytic Number Theory*, Chapter 7, Theorem 7.13, and Montgomery–Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), 119–134, DOI `10.1112/S0025579300004708`. Montgomery–Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory II* (Cambridge University Press, 2026), Chapter 19, also presents the character-sum mean-square form as a standard consequence of the large sieve.

Since `q/phi(q)>=1`, restricting the left side of `(23)` to the distinct primitive real characters `(19)` with `m_lambda<=C` and using `(21)` gives

\[
N^2
\sum_{\substack{\lambda\ne0\\\mathcal Q(\lambda)\le C}}
|x_\lambda|^2
\le
(y+C^2)N.
\tag{24}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{\substack{\lambda\ne0\\\mathcal Q(\lambda)\le C}}
|x_\lambda|^2
\le
\frac{y+C^2}{N}.
\tag{25}
\]

The prime number theorem in the fixed progression `1 mod 4` gives

\[
N\sim\frac{y}{2\log y},
\tag{26}
\]

and `(4)`–`(6)` follow immediately. Markov's inequality in the finite family gives `(8)`.

The `C^2` term is load-bearing. The same black-box inequality ceases to give an `O(log y)` energy budget once the source cap moves substantially beyond `sqrt(y)`.

## 3. Exact Fourier energy of the reciprocal endpoint partition

Under `(9)`–`(10)`, linear independence makes the `2^R` subset sums `lambda_I` distinct, and `MC-296` gives `(11)`. Therefore

\[
\sum_{I\subseteq[R]}|x_{\lambda_I}|^2
=
\sum_{j=0}^{R}\binom Rj
\left(1-\frac{2j}{R}\right)^2.
\tag{27}
\]

If `K` is binomial with parameters `(R,1/2)`, then

\[
\mathbb E\left(1-\frac{2K}{R}\right)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}\left(1-\frac{2K}{R}\right)=\frac1R.
\tag{28}
\]

Multiplying `(28)` by `2^R` proves `(12)`. The empty subset contributes exactly one unit of energy. The low-source nonzero subset sums contribute at most the global budget `(6)`, so subtracting both contributions proves `(13)`. Equation `(14)` follows from `|x_lambda|<=1`.

The exact support-floor comparison is now transparent. At

\[
R=\log_2\log y+o(\log\log y),
\tag{29}
\]

one has `2^R=(log y)^{1+o(1)}`, so `(12)` has size only `(log y)^{1+o(1)}/R`. To dominate the `O(log y)` capacity in `(6)`, one needs

\[
2^R/(R\log y)\to\infty,
\tag{30}
\]

which is exactly `(18)` up to the displayed `omega(1)` margin.

This is not merely a constants issue: at the exact `MC-242` floor, a generic multiplicative-large-sieve energy estimate has enough budget to accommodate the entire reciprocal-partition spectrum.

## 4. Relation to the Page / Deuring–Heilbronn frontier

The new bound and `MC-293`/`MC-299` answer different questions.

Page and Deuring–Heilbronn are **pointwise**: below their source band, every nonexceptional character is individually balanced, and at most one real exceptional character can survive. Their strength is near-complete control of each mode; their weakness is the quasi-subpower source ceiling quantified sharply in `MC-300`.

The multiplicative large sieve is **collective**: it reaches all primitive source characters up to square-root conductor but controls only the total squared shell bias. It therefore cannot eliminate one exceptional mode, nor even `O(log y)` strongly biased modes. What it supplies is exactly the orthogonal axis requested by the current frontier: a conductor-wide capacity law independent of exceptional-zero repulsion.

The two mechanisms can be combined only by respecting this distinction. Page theory may remove all but one mode in its smaller band, while `(6)` limits the total energy in the much larger band. Neither statement by itself controls the source spectrum above `sqrt(y)`, and neither proves that the actual Möbius/Legendre geometry realizes enough endpoint-partition energy to contradict `(6)` at the blind-support floor.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

No new large-sieve theorem is claimed. The analytic input `(23)` is classical. The same multiplicative large-sieve inequality already appears inside this research line in `MC-068`, where it bounds the number of prime quadratic conductors that can sustain an extreme terminal prime bias. The present finding transposes that classical mechanism to the exact source-cost family of `MC-293`: arbitrary squarefree lower-prime products are treated simultaneously, and the output is a total Fourier-energy capacity rather than the earlier prime-conductor feedback count.

A fresh literature check also found recent work on thin character families, including Darbar, Kerr, Munsch and Shparlinski, *Large values of L(sigma, chi) for subgroups of characters*, arXiv `2604.02960` (submitted 3 April 2026). That work develops mean-value and zero-density estimates for thin subgroups or small-doubling sets of characters at a common modulus, with its main subgroup theorem formulated for prime modulus. It is structurally adjacent to the desired collective-family direction, but it does not directly subsume `(4)`: the source characters here have varying squarefree conductors `Q(lambda)`, and no common-prime-modulus subgroup is being assumed.

There is also a broad literature on large sieves for sparse or special modulus sets and on quadratic large sieves. Those refinements do not make `(4)` novel, and `MC-262`–`MC-263` already show why the opposite shell-product-modulus orientation has different quantitative barriers. The durable contribution here is only the line-specific synthesis of the classical multiplicative large sieve with the source-cost map and the exact endpoint-partition Fourier profile.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification tests

The conclusion is intentionally narrow.

- The character family in `(23)` is larger than the selected source family, so `(4)` is an upper-bound capacity statement, not a sharp asymptotic for the actual Legendre shell.
- The source cost must be realized by primitive real characters as in `MC-293`. This is true for the squarefree lower-prime product representatives used here; changing the source definition would require rechecking the conductor map.
- The argument controls squared biases. It cannot distinguish one Page/Siegel mode from many weak modes with the same total energy.
- The square-root source threshold is a feature of the `y+C^2` large-sieve envelope. It is not asserted to be an intrinsic arithmetic phase transition.
- The reciprocal endpoint partition is an exact finite-geometric extremizer from `MC-295`–`MC-296`; the present argument does not prove that the actual Möbius shell realizes such a partition.
- At the blind-support floor `(16)`, the method does **not** contradict the extremizer. The additive `log_2 R` gap in `(18)` is a real residual capacity gap for this black-box energy route.
- No zero-free region, GRH, RH, random-walk model, or continuation of `1/zeta(s)` enters the proof. No estimate for `M(x)` follows.

A future improvement must therefore change at least one quantitative ingredient: replace the generic `O(log y)` square-root-conductor energy capacity by a smaller source-specific bound, prove that the actual endpoint obstruction carries more Fourier energy than the reciprocal extremizer at the critical rank, or obtain an independent arithmetic constraint coupling high-source modes so that their large-sieve energy cannot be spent freely.

## Consequence for the active frontier

`MC-300` showed that pointwise Deuring–Heilbronn technology cannot enlarge the nonexceptional source band past its Lambert-W envelope without stronger exceptional-zero information. The present result supplies a distinct collective theorem beyond that ceiling: **all source characters up to conductor `sqrt(y)` have only `O(log y)` total shell-bias energy**.

That advance does not close the endpoint escape. Instead it quantifies it. In the reciprocal endpoint-partition model, source cost above `sqrt(y)` is forced once the selected dimension exceeds the blind-support scale by roughly `log_2 loglog y`; exactly at the known `MC-242` support floor, the classical energy budget remains larger than the entire obstruction spectrum by a factor of order `loglog y`.

The next useful target is therefore sharper than “control more conductors.” It is to recover that missing logarithmic factor through source-specific family structure, or to prove an arithmetic lower bound on obstruction energy/rank that pushes the actual shell beyond the generic large-sieve capacity.
