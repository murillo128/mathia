## Claim

Continue the localized prescribed-Legendre setting of `MC-252` and `MC-283`. Let

\[
\mathcal R_y=\{r:y<r\le2y,\ r\text{ prime},\ r\equiv1\pmod4\},
\]

let `U` be a finite set of odd primes at most `y`, put

\[
Q(U):=4\prod_{p\in U}p,
\qquad d:=|U|,
\tag{1}
\]

and for a sign vector `\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_p)_{p\in U}\in\{\pm1\}^U` define

\[
R_y(\varepsilon;U)
:=
\#\left\{r\in\mathcal R_y:
\left(\frac pr\right)=\varepsilon_p\ \text{for every }p\in U
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

There exist absolute constants `b,B>0` such that, uniformly for every `U` and every sign vector `\varepsilon` satisfying

\[
Q(U)\le \exp\!\bigl(b\sqrt{\log y}\bigr),
\tag{3}
\]

one has, for all sufficiently large `y`,

\[
\boxed{
R_y(\varepsilon;U)
\gg
 y\exp\!\bigl(-B\sqrt{\log y}\bigr).
}
\tag{4}
\]

The implicit lower-bound constant is ineffective because the exceptional-zero case uses Siegel's lower bound. If none of the quadratic characters generated by `U` and the parity character modulo `4` is Page-exceptional at scale `y`, the stronger expected-density asymptotic holds:

\[
R_y(\varepsilon;U)
=
(1+o(1))
\frac{\operatorname{Li}(2y)-\operatorname{Li}(y)}{2^{d+1}}.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the polylogarithmic conductor restriction in `MC-283` is not the true boundary of localized Legendre-pattern realization. The Page/Landau exceptional-zero dichotomy leaves at most one biased Fourier mode, and even the worst depleting exceptional mode cannot empty a prescribed cell while `(3)` holds with `b` sufficiently small.

Consequently, if

\[
1\le t=o\!\left(y e^{-B\sqrt{\log y}}\right),
\tag{6}
\]

then the projected fixed-weight shell-product image on `U` is the full Boolean cube: for every `\eta\in\{\pm1\}^U` there is a `t`-element subset `T\subset\mathcal R_y` such that

\[
\prod_{r\in T}\left(\frac pr\right)=\eta_p
\qquad(p\in U).
\tag{7}
\]

In particular this applies at the live blind-support scale `t\asymp\log\log y`.

The empirical Christoffel escape of `MC-281` therefore acquires a substantially stronger necessary condition. Let `p_a` be an endpoint-normalized degree-`m` polynomial on the shell feature matrix, let `U(a)` be the union of lower-prime coordinates actually appearing in monomials with nonzero coefficient, and put

\[
Q(a):=4\prod_{p\in U(a)}p.
\tag{8}
\]

At any `t` satisfying `(6)`, if `Q(a)` obeys `(3)`, then every `t`-subset of the all-positive shell cell evaluates at the virtual endpoint on all coordinates used by `p_a`, hence

\[
\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_t}|p_a(x(T))|^2
\ge
\binom{R_y(+1;U(a))}{t}
\ge1
\tag{9}
\]

for all sufficiently large `y`. Therefore a Christoffel certificate with empirical energy strictly below one must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
Q(a)>
\exp\!\bigl(b\sqrt{\log y}\bigr)
}
\tag{10}
\]

eventually. This strictly strengthens the `Q(a)>(\log y)^A` for every fixed `A` conclusion of `MC-283`.

No blind row is excluded here, no bound for `M(X)` is improved, and `(10)` remains only a necessary complexity condition on this exact low-depth Christoffel route.

## 1. Character expansion converts simultaneous sign prescription into one principal mode plus real quadratic modes

For shell primes `r>y` with `r\equiv1\pmod4`, quadratic reciprocity gives

\[
\left(\frac pr\right)=\left(\frac rp\right)
\qquad(p\in U).
\tag{11}
\]

Let `\chi_p(n)=(n/p)` and let `\chi_4` be the nonprincipal character modulo `4`. On primes `r` coprime to `Q(U)`, the indicator of the prescribed cell is exactly

\[
\mathbf1_{r\equiv1\ (4)}
\prod_{p\in U}
\mathbf1_{\chi_p(r)=\varepsilon_p}
=
\frac1{2^{d+1}}
(1+\chi_4(r))
\prod_{p\in U}(1+\varepsilon_p\chi_p(r)).
\tag{12}
\]

Expanding `(12)` gives all products

\[
\chi_{S,\delta}
:=
\chi_4^\delta\prod_{p\in S}\chi_p,
\qquad
S\subseteq U,
\quad \delta\in\{0,1\},
\tag{13}
\]

with coefficient

\[
c_{S,\delta}(\varepsilon)
=
\prod_{p\in S}\varepsilon_p
\in\{\pm1\}.
\tag{14}
\]

Each nonprincipal character in `(13)` is induced by a primitive real quadratic character whose conductor divides `Q(U)`. Distinct local choices give distinct characters. Thus the entire simultaneous pattern problem is a normalized sum of real primitive-character prime sums with conductor at most `Q(U)`.

Define the logarithmically weighted cell mass

\[
\Theta_y(\varepsilon;U)
:=
\sum_{\substack{y<r\le2y\\r\text{ prime}}}
\log r\,
\mathbf1_{r\equiv1\ (4)}
\prod_{p\in U}
\mathbf1_{(p/r)=\varepsilon_p}.
\tag{15}
\]

Equation `(12)` gives

\[
\Theta_y(\varepsilon;U)
=
\frac1{2^{d+1}}
\sum_{S,\delta}
 c_{S,\delta}(\varepsilon)
\bigl(\vartheta(2y,\chi_{S,\delta})-
      \vartheta(y,\chi_{S,\delta})\bigr),
\tag{16}
\]

where each imprimitive presentation is interpreted through its primitive inducing character. Since every prime factor of `Q(U)` is at most `y`, the shell itself contains no conductor prime and this replacement changes no shell-prime value.

## 2. Page leaves at most one dangerous Fourier mode

The Page/Landau theorem and prime-number theorem for characters used already in `MC-067` give absolute `c_0>0` such that, after shrinking the conductor constant if necessary, every nonexceptional primitive real character of conductor

\[
q\le \exp\!\bigl(2c_0\sqrt{\log y}\bigr)
\tag{17}
\]

satisfies

\[
\vartheta(x,\chi)
=O\!\left(xe^{-c_0\sqrt{\log x}}\right)
\tag{18}
\]

at `x=y,2y`, while among all primitive real characters in this conductor range there is at most one exceptional character `\chi_*`. If present, with exceptional zero `\beta=1-\delta`, its shell sum has the form

\[
\vartheta(2y,\chi_*)-
\vartheta(y,\chi_*)
=
-\frac{(2y)^\beta-y^\beta}{\beta}
+O\!\left(ye^{-c_0\sqrt{\log y}}\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Prime-power removal from the corresponding `\psi` formulas is negligible on this scale, as in `MC-067`.

The normalization in `(16)` is important. There are `2^{d+1}` modes, but every error term is multiplied by `2^{-(d+1)}`. Hence summing all nonexceptional errors still costs only

\[
O\!\left(ye^{-c_0\sqrt{\log y}}\right),
\tag{20}
\]

not a factor `2^d` more. The only possible structured deviation from equal cell mass is the single Page-exceptional mode.

If no character from `(13)` is exceptional, the principal mode and `(20)` give

\[
\Theta_y(\varepsilon;U)
=
\frac{y}{2^{d+1}}
+O\!\left(ye^{-c_0\sqrt{\log y}}\right).
\tag{21}
\]

Because every `p\in U` is at least `3`,

\[
2^d
\le
\left(\prod_{p\in U}p\right)^{\kappa}
\le Q(U)^\kappa,
\qquad
\kappa:=\frac{\log2}{\log3}.
\tag{22}
\]

Under `(3)`, the main term in `(21)` is therefore at least

\[
\gg y\exp(-\kappa b\sqrt{\log y}).
\tag{23}
\]

Choosing `b` with `\kappa b<c_0` makes `(20)` negligible and proves `(5)`.

## 3. A Landau–Siegel zero can deplete one cell, but not empty it in the small Page range

Assume now that one mode in `(13)` is the exceptional character. Let

\[
\sigma_*(\varepsilon)\in\{\pm1\}
\tag{24}
\]

be its coefficient in `(16).` If `\sigma_*=-1`, the negative exceptional prime sum in `(19)` increases the prescribed cell mass, so the principal contribution alone already gives a stronger lower bound than `(4)`.

The only dangerous case is `\sigma_*=+1`. Then the principal and exceptional main terms combine as

\[
y-rac{(2y)^\beta-y^\beta}{\beta}
=
\int_y^{2y}\bigl(1-t^{-\delta}\bigr)\,dt.
\tag{25}
\]

For `t\in[y,2y]`,

\[
1-t^{-\delta}
=1-e^{-\delta\log t}
\gg
\min\{1,\delta\log y\},
\tag{26}
\]

and therefore

\[
y-rac{(2y)^\beta-y^\beta}{\beta}
\gg
 y\min\{1,\delta\log y\}.
\tag{27}
\]

The classical Siegel lower bound used in `MC-067`, with fixed exponent `1/2`, gives an ineffective absolute constant `C>0` such that

\[
\delta\ge C q_*^{-1/2}.
\tag{28}
\]

Since `q_*\le Q(U)` and `(3)` holds,

\[
\delta\log y
\gg
(\log y)\exp\!\left(-\frac b2\sqrt{\log y}\right).
\tag{29}
\]

For sufficiently large `y` the right side is below one, so `(27)`--`(29)` give the lower main mass

\[
\gg
 y(\log y)
 \exp\!\left(-\frac b2\sqrt{\log y}\right).
\tag{30}
\]

After the cell normalization and `(22)`, the dangerous exceptional cell still has

\[
\Theta_y(\varepsilon;U)
\gg
 y(\log y)
 \exp\!\left(-b\left(\kappa+\frac12\right)
                    \sqrt{\log y}\right)
+
O\!\left(ye^{-c_0\sqrt{\log y}}\right).
\tag{31}
\]

Choose `b>0` so small that

\[
b\left(\kappa+\frac12\right)<c_0.
\tag{32}
\]

Then the positive term in `(31)` dominates the uniform error `(20)`. Dividing by `\log(2y)` converts weighted prime mass to prime count and proves `(4)` with, for example,

\[
B=b\left(\kappa+\frac12\right)
\tag{33}
\]

after harmless adjustment of constants.

This is exactly the issue left open in `MC-283`: simultaneous pattern counting outside Siegel–Walfisz encounters severe exceptional-character bias, but Page says there is only one such Fourier mode and Siegel says its depletion cannot be complete at sufficiently small stretched-exponential conductor.

## 4. Fixed-weight products inherit full projected cube coverage

Let

\[
v_U(r)=\left(\left(\frac pr\right)\right)_{p\in U}
\in\{\pm1\}^U.
\tag{34}
\]

By `(4)`, every vector in `\{\pm1\}^U` is attained by at least

\[
N_0(y)\gg y e^{-B\sqrt{\log y}}
\tag{35}
\]

distinct shell primes. If `t=o(N_0(y))`, then for a desired product vector `\eta` choose one prime with vector `\eta` and `t-1` distinct primes with the all-positive vector; when `\eta=+1`, choose all `t` primes from the all-positive cell. This proves `(7)`.

For the Christoffel application, only the all-positive cell is needed. If `p_a(1)=1` and all coordinates used by `p_a` lie in `U(a)`, every `t`-subset drawn from that cell satisfies

\[
p_a(x(T))=1.
\tag{36}
\]

Its contribution to counting-measure empirical energy is therefore exactly the binomial term in `(9)`. At the live choice `t\asymp\log\log y`, `(35)` is astronomically larger than `t`, so `(10)` follows.

The conclusion is directional. It does not say that the full ambient degree-`m` feature family has conductor below the Page range; a sparse candidate separator may deliberately import one or more large lower-prime coordinates. It says that **no endpoint-normalized certificate supported entirely on a stretched-exponential effective coordinate conductor can have empirical energy below one**.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

Every analytic ingredient is classical. The prescribed-sign character expansion is finite Fourier analysis on the elementary quadratic character group, equivalent to the CRT/quadratic-reciprocity mechanism already audited in `MC-252`. `MC-283` used its residue-class form together with Siegel–Walfisz to obtain expected-density shell realization for fixed-power polylogarithmic `Q(U)`.

The exceptional-zero input is the classical Page/Landau uniqueness theorem, the character prime-number theorem with one possible exceptional main term, and Siegel's lower bound for the exceptional zero. These were already source-audited against Montgomery–Vaughan Chapters 4 and 11 (`MC-S15`) in `MC-067`. An independent literature check through 14 September 2026 also recovers the standard Page statement in modern work on primes in arithmetic progressions: there is at most one primitive real exceptional character in the relevant conductor/height box. Effective Chebotarev results treat related multiquadratic splitting questions but do not supply a different theorem needed for the present derivation.

No novelty is claimed for Page's theorem, Siegel's theorem, quadratic reciprocity, character orthogonality, or prescribed Legendre symbols. The durable Mathia delta is their composition with the specific localized feature-image obstruction of `MC-281`--`MC-283`: normalized Fourier expansion prevents the number of prescribed coordinates from multiplying the Page error, and the one exceptional mode still leaves enough shell primes to force high Christoffel energy. The result closes the explicit caveat in `MC-283` and replaces its super-polylogarithmic necessary condition by the stretched-exponential lower bound `(10)`.

A targeted search found no direct theorem stated as the empirical-Christoffel consequence `(10)` for this shell matrix. That search boundary is not a novelty claim.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Small absolute Page constant.** The theorem asserts existence of some absolute `b>0`; it does not optimize `b`. It must be chosen below the constants in the character prime-number theorem and additionally satisfy `(32)`.
- **Ineffectivity.** Uniform positivity in the exceptional case uses Siegel's lower bound `(28)`, so the threshold implicit in "sufficiently large" is ineffective. The nonexceptional asymptotic `(5)` is effective once the underlying Page constants are fixed.
- **Real quadratic family.** The argument works because every Fourier mode in `(12)` is real quadratic and Page permits at most one exceptional real character. It is not a black-box statement for arbitrary high-order residue-symbol families.
- **Shell width.** The proof uses the macroscopic interval `(y,2y]`. It does not claim the same result in genuinely short intervals, where the exceptional and nonexceptional prime-sum errors have different barriers.
- **Conductor versus formal degree.** Equation `(10)` concerns the union product of coordinates actually used by the separator, not its formal polynomial degree alone.
- **No RH-scale import.** Only classical near-`1` zero-free/exceptional-zero theory for Dirichlet `L`-functions is used. No continuation or zero-free hypothesis for `1/\zeta(s)` near `\Re s=1/2`, and no RH-equivalent Mertens estimate, enters the proof.
- **Exceptional sign.** The worst case is exactly coefficient `+1` for the exceptional mode. Coefficient `-1` increases the cell mass; omitting this sign check would overstate the obstruction.
- **Error normalization.** The `2^{d+1}` character errors do not accumulate after `(16)` because of the prefactor `2^{-(d+1)}`. Conversely the principal/exceptional main contribution does pay the cell factor `2^{-d}`, which is why `(22)` and the smallness requirement `(32)` are load-bearing.
- **Higher conductor remains live.** The theorem says nothing once `Q(a)` exceeds `\exp(b\sqrt{\log y})`. That high-conductor directional regime is now the surviving Christoffel route, not a solved problem.

## Updated frontier

`MC-283` showed that ordinary Siegel–Walfisz already prevents any low-energy empirical separator from remaining inside a fixed polylogarithmic effective coordinate conductor. Its explicit caveat was that simultaneous pattern universality could fail beyond that regime because an exceptional character may strongly bias the cells.

The Page–Siegel audit resolves that caveat throughout a fixed stretched-exponential window: **there can be only one exceptional Fourier direction, and even its maximally depleting sign leaves exponentially many shell primes after the cell normalization when the conductor constant is small enough.** The live localization theorem is therefore sharper than the current mind snapshot records: a successful degree-`m` directional separator must import effective lower-prime conductor beyond `\exp(b\sqrt{\log y})`, or abandon this endpoint-normalized scalar Christoffel architecture for a genuinely different source-native inverse.
