# MC-208 — Square-defect transfer has a level-doubling barrier and a polynomial-parity tail

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

`MC-206` reduces the first source-aligned sign/support shift to Möbius sums in square-modulus progressions, but leaves several possible reasons that this might still be technically tractable: the shifted residue can be non-reduced for general `h`, the logarithmic primorial pre-sieve might be the main complication, and average progression technology reaches ordinary moduli close to the square-root level.

For the primitive shift `h=1`, the first two complications disappear exactly, and the third exposes a sharper obstruction.

Let

\[
y=c\log X,
\qquad
0<c<\frac12,
\qquad
q=q_y=\prod_{p\le y}p,
\qquad
T=X-q,
\]

with `X` large enough that `q<X/2`, and define the signed shifted square-defect transform

\[
\Delta(X;y)
:=
\sum_{\substack{n\le T\\(n,q)=1}}
\mu(n)\bigl(1-\mu^2(n+q)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

The square-divisor expansion from `MC-206` specializes to

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(X;y)
=-
\sum_{\substack{d>y\\(d,q)=1\\d^2\le X}}
\mu(d)
A_d(T),
}
\tag{2}
\]

where

\[
A_d(Y)
:=
\sum_{\substack{n\le Y\\(n,q)=1\\n\equiv-q\pmod{d^2}}}
\mu(n).
\tag{3}
\]

Because `(d,q)=1`, the residue `-q (mod d^2)` is reduced for **every** term in `(2)`. Thus non-coprime residue classes are not an intrinsic obstruction to the branch: the primitive shift removes them completely.

The primorial pre-sieve can also be removed algebraically. Let

\[
\mathcal S_q
=
\{r\ge1: p\mid r\Rightarrow p\mid q\}
\]

be the multiplicative semigroup generated by the primes dividing `q`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu(n)\mathbf 1_{(n,q)=1}
=(\mu * \mathbf 1_{\mathcal S_q})(n).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, since every `r in S_q` is coprime to `d`, one has the exact reduced-progression decomposition

\[
\boxed{
A_d(Y)
=
\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal S_q\\r\le Y}}
\ \,
\sum_{\substack{k\le Y/r\\
k\equiv-q\bar r\pmod{d^2}}}
\mu(k),
}
\tag{5}
\]

where `r bar r = 1 (mod d^2)`. Every inner residue in `(5)` is coprime to `d^2`.

At the logarithmic sieve level `y=c log X`, the number of possible `r<=X` in `S_q` is `X^{o(1)}`. Hence the pre-sieve creates only subpower combinatorial multiplicity. It does not by itself explain the missing fixed-power gain, although any application of a progression theorem must still track that its length has shortened from `Y` to `Y/r`.

The decisive obstruction is instead a **level-doubling barrier** forced by the square modulus. Split `(2)` at `D`, with `y<=D<=sqrt(X)`. The absolute tail satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\substack{d>D\\(d,q)=1\\d^2\le X}}
\mu(d)A_d(T)
\right|
\ll
\frac{X}{D}+\sqrt X.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore, if the part with `d<=D` were controlled by progression information for all moduli up to

\[
Q=D^2=X^\alpha,
\]

then even **perfect cancellation on that entire controlled range**, followed only by absolute treatment of the remaining square divisors, leaves the barrier

\[
\boxed{
X^{1-\alpha/2}+\sqrt X.
}
\tag{7}
\]

To make `(7)` as small as `X^{1/2+epsilon}`, one needs

\[
\alpha\ge1-2\epsilon,
\tag{8}
\]

that is, progression control almost to modulus `X`, not merely to the classical square-root level. In particular a Bombieri--Vinogradov/Shao-type modulus range `Q<=X^{1/2-sigma}` corresponds to

\[
D<=X^{1/4-\sigma/2}
\]

and leaves an absolute square-defect tail of size at best

\[
\boxed{
X^{3/4+\sigma/2}+\sqrt X.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This is a structural exponent loss, not a defect in the reduced-residue formulation.

The large-`d` tail has an exact dual form showing what extra information would be needed to beat `(6)`. For `D>=y`, substitute `n=d^2m-q` in `(2)`. Since `(d,q)=1`,

\[
(n,q)=1\iff(m,q)=1.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{>D}
=-
\sum_{\substack{m\le X/D^2\\(m,q)=1}}
\sum_{\substack{
\max(D,\sqrt{q/m})<d\le\sqrt{X/m}\\
(d,q)=1}}
\mu(d)\mu(md^2-q).
}
\tag{10}
\]

Moreover

\[
\gcd(d,md^2-q)=\gcd(d,q)=1,
\]

so each inner product may equivalently be written

\[
\mu(d)\mu(md^2-q)
=
\mu\bigl(d(md^2-q)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]

For fixed `m`, the large-divisor tail is therefore a Möbius sum on the cubic polynomial

\[
P_{m,q}(d)=d(md^2-q).
\tag{12}
\]

At the natural transition `D=X^{1/4}`, square moduli `d^2` reach `X^{1/2}` while the dual parameter satisfies `m<=X^{1/2}`. The branch therefore has a precise hyperbola-type dichotomy:

- below `d≈X^{1/4}`, it is naturally an arithmetic-progression problem to square moduli;
- above `d≈X^{1/4}`, any gain beyond the `X^{3/4}` absolute budget requires cancellation across `d`, equivalently a family of nonlinear polynomial Möbius correlations of the form `(10)`--`(12)`.

Thus the square-defect route is not blocked by the primorial pre-sieve or by non-reduced residues in its simplest shift. It is blocked by a more substantive transfer problem: **ordinary level-of-distribution information must be supplemented by cancellation across the square-divisor variable, and that missing cancellation enters polynomial-Chowla/parity territory.**

No improved bound for `Delta`, `M(X)`, or the zeta zero set is claimed.

## 1. Exact removal of the pre-sieve

The convolution identity `(4)` is local at the primes. If `p|q`, then the Euler factor of `mu` is `1-z` while the factor of `1_{S_q}` is `(1-z)^{-1}`, so their product is `1`: every positive power of `p` receives coefficient zero. If `p` does not divide `q`, the second factor is `1` and the local coefficients remain those of `mu`. This is exactly `mu(n) 1_{(n,q)=1}`.

Expanding `(4)` in `(3)` gives

\[
A_d(Y)
=
\sum_{r\in S_q}
\sum_{\substack{k\le Y/r\\rk\equiv-q\pmod{d^2}}}
\mu(k).
\]

Because all prime factors of `r` divide `q` and `(d,q)=1`, `r` is invertible modulo `d^2`; this proves `(5)` and shows that every resulting progression has a reduced residue.

For completeness, the subpower count of `S_q` follows already from Rankin's trick. Put `sigma=1/log y`. Then

\[
\#\{r\le X:r\in S_q\}
\le
X^\sigma
\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-\sigma})^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]

Each Euler factor is `O(log y)`, while Chebyshev's estimate gives `pi(y)=O(y/log y)`. With `y=c log X`, the logarithm of the right side of `(13)` is

\[
O\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}
+\frac{\log X\,\log\log\log X}{\log\log X}\right)
=o(\log X),
\]

which proves the asserted `X^{o(1)}` count. This observation concerns algebraic multiplicity only; it does not license applying an arithmetic-progression theorem outside its uniform length/modulus range.

## 2. Why a square-root level of distribution becomes a three-quarter barrier

For each `d`, the progression in `(3)` has at most `X/d^2+1` points. Hence

\[
\sum_{D<d\le\sqrt X}|A_d(T)|
\le
X\sum_{d>D}\frac1{d^2}+O(\sqrt X)
\ll\frac XD+\sqrt X,
\]

which is `(6)`.

This estimate deliberately grants the small-`d` range for free. It therefore proves a limited but useful no-go: **no argument whose only nontrivial input is cancellation for the individual square-modulus progressions up to `d^2<=X^alpha`, and whose complement is discarded absolutely, can beat the exponent `1-alpha/2`.**

The statement does not say that a level beyond `1/2` is impossible, nor that progression technology cannot exploit the sparse family of square moduli. It says that even dramatic improvement on the controlled half cannot reach the RH scale unless the complementary divisor tail also cancels.

This explains why the natural `d≈X^{1/4}` boundary is not an arbitrary technical cutoff. It is where the square modulus itself reaches the classical `X^{1/2}` distribution scale, while the untreated divisor tail still has the `X^{3/4}` absolute size in `(9)`.

## 3. The tail is a nonlinear Möbius-correlation problem

Starting directly from the large-`d` part of `(2)`, write

\[
n=d^2m-q.
\]

The upper condition `n<=X-q` becomes `d^2m<=X`; positivity of `n` gives `d^2m>q`. Since `d` is invertible modulo `q`,

\[
(d^2m-q,q)=(m,q),
\]

which proves `(10)` after interchanging `d` and `m`.

The polynomial identity `(11)` is also exact because `gcd(d,md^2-q)=1`. Thus the extra cancellation needed beyond `(6)` is not hidden in another residue-class normalization: it is cancellation in a family of nonlinear multiplicative-sign sums.

This is a useful frontier diagnostic. If a proposed proof of a fixed-power bound for `Delta` splits at square-root moduli and then estimates the complementary `d`-sum without introducing genuinely new signed information, the missing `X^{1/4}` exponent has merely been moved into the polynomial tail.

## 4. Prior-art audit

Xuancheng Shao, *Gowers norms of multiplicative functions in progressions on average*, Algebra & Number Theory 11 (2017), 961--982, arXiv `1607.01814`, proves that the Möbius function has small Gowers norm on average over progressions `n=a_q (mod q)` for arbitrary reduced residues and moduli `q<=X^{1/2-sigma}`; for `U^1` this is the Bombieri--Vinogradov-type Möbius regime. This is the closest direct language for the small-square-modulus side, but the exponent calculation `(7)`--`(9)` shows that a square-root modulus range alone cannot dispose of the complementary square-divisor tail.

There is specialized literature on sparse and powerful moduli, and `MC-206` already records the nearby square/powerful-modulus work. Such results may improve the small-`d` treatment and should not be conflated with the no-go `(7)`: the present obstruction survives even if the controlled range is estimated perfectly, until the reachable modulus exponent approaches one or the tail itself is given signed cancellation.

For the dual side, polynomial parity cancellation is established conjectural territory. Peter Borwein, Stephen K. K. Choi and Himadri Ganguli, *Sign Changes of the Liouville Function on Quadratics*, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 56 (2013), 251--257, DOI `10.4153/CMB-2011-166-9`, records Chowla's conjecture `sum_{n<=x} lambda(f(n))=o(x)` for nonsquare polynomial `f` and emphasizes the difficulty already for degree greater than one. More recent work obtains polynomial Chowla statements after averaging over the polynomial: Timothy Browning, Efthymios Sofos and Joni Teräväinen, *Bateman--Horn, polynomial Chowla and the Hasse principle with probability 1*, arXiv `2212.10373`, published online in Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik in 2026, proves such results for almost all polynomials ordered by height. These do not furnish the uniform, growing-parameter family `(12)`.

The exact polynomial in `(12)` concerns Möbius rather than Liouville and also varies with `m` and the growing primorial `q`. The cited polynomial-Chowla literature is therefore a **difficulty boundary**, not a theorem transferred to `(10)`. No literature-absence claim is made. The durable content here is the exact line-specific reduction showing where the square-defect branch changes mathematical language.

## 5. What this changes on the frontier

`MC-206` left two apparently plausible repairs: use reduced residues to exploit progression technology, or change the shifted support observable because squarefree defects looked too sparse. The first repair is now classified more sharply.

For the primitive shift, reduced residues are already automatic and the primorial pre-sieve is an `X^{o(1)}` convolutional wrapper. The real bill is that square divisors double the modulus exponent. Square-root-level distribution sees only `d` through roughly `X^{1/4}`, while the untreated divisor region is still an `X^{3/4+o(1)}` object.

A viable continuation of the **same square-defect branch** must therefore do at least one of the following:

1. obtain distribution for the relevant Möbius progressions to square moduli at an effective modulus exponent approaching one, with all shortened-length and pre-sieve losses restored;
2. prove cancellation across the `d`-variable in `(10)`, i.e. a structured polynomial-parity estimate unavailable from ordinary progression control alone;
3. derive a coupled identity in which the small- and large-divisor pieces cancel before absolute values are taken.

Merely importing a stronger Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem at level `1/2`, removing non-coprime residues, or expanding the primorial condition cannot cross the present exponent barrier.

Alternatively, the line can follow `MC-207` and replace squarefree support by a source relation whose nonprincipal component does not factor through a square-divisor expansion at all. That is a genuinely different escape rather than another normalization of `(2)`.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- The level-doubling statement is a no-go only for **absolute treatment of the complementary `d>D` tail**. Signed cancellation between large divisors can beat `(6)`; equation `(10)` states exactly the new theorem such a proof must supply.
- The `X^{o(1)}` smooth-number count in `(13)` does not prove that existing progression estimates survive the convolution with no loss. Each inner sum has length `Y/r`, so its theorem-specific modulus range must be rechecked.
- The primitive shift `h=1` is enough to show that non-reduced residues are not an intrinsic obstruction. General `h` can have additional gcd structure and is not classified here.
- The polynomial-Chowla references establish only an adjacent parity barrier. They do not prove that `(10)` is equivalent to a standard polynomial Chowla conjecture, nor that no special cancellation is available from the coupled `(m,q)` family.
- No inference from `(7)` to RH is made. The calculation identifies the quantitative information missing from this candidate route; it does not establish that such information is unattainable.
