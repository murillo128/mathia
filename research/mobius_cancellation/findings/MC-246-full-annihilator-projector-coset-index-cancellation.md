## Claim

`MC-238` identified the full smooth-dilation blind family for the shell-square modulus

\[
q=Q_S=\prod_{r\in S}r^2,
\qquad
G=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times,
\]

as the annihilator of the subgroup generated by the small primes,

\[
H=H_y(q):=\langle p\bmod q:p\le y\text{ prime}\rangle\le G,
\qquad
A:=H^\perp\le \widehat G.
\tag{1}
\]

`MC-244`--`MC-245` then inverted only the **quadratic** part of this blind family. The same inversion works exactly for the whole annihilator, and it changes the frontier in one important respect: eliminating all blind quadratic characters is not the same as eliminating all blind characters.

For any arithmetic function `f`, put

\[
S_f(N,\chi)=\sum_{m\le N}f(m)\chi(m).
\]

For a reduced residue `a mod q`, the contribution of all characters in `A` to the ordinary character expansion is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_A(f;N,q,a)
:=
\frac1{\varphi(q)}
\sum_{\chi\in A}\overline{\chi(a)}S_f(N,\chi)
=
\frac1{|H|}
\sum_{\substack{m\le N\\a^{-1}m\bmod q\in H}}f(m).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, finite-group orthogonality gives

\[
\sum_{\chi\in A}\chi(b)
=|A|\,\mathbf 1_{b\in H},
\qquad
|A|=[G:H]=\frac{\varphi(q)}{|H|}.
\tag{3}
\]

The factor `|A|` therefore cancels exactly against the density `1/|A|` of the selected multiplicative coset. In particular, for `|f|\le1`,

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal P_A(f;N,q,a)|\le \frac Nq+O(1),
}
\tag{4}
\]

independently of every positive value of the full blind index `[G:H]`. Only `H=G` removes the nonprincipal full blind family altogether.

For the exact smooth-inverse source of `MC-233`, let

\[
y=c\log X,
\qquad
P=\prod_{p\le y}p,
\qquad
T=X-P,
\]

and recall

\[
B_S(X)
=
\sum_{\substack{d\le T\\P^+(d)\le y}}
M_\mu\!\left(\frac Td;q,-Pd^{-1}\bmod q\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Every `y`-smooth `d` and `P` lie in `H`. Hence every `\chi\in A` satisfies `\chi(d)=\chi(P)=1`. Using the exact smooth inverse from `MC-181`,

\[
s_y(d)=\mathbf 1_{P^+(d)\le y},
\qquad
g_y=\mu*s_y
=\mu(n)\mathbf 1_{P^-(n)>y},
\tag{6}
\]

the entire full-blind source contribution collapses to

\[
\boxed{
B_S^{(A)}(X)
=
\frac1{|H|}
\sum_{\substack{n\le T\\-n\bmod q\in H}}
g_y(n).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the source does not naturally leave one quadratic Legendre cell only. At full character resolution it leaves one **multiplicative coset** `-H`, carrying a logarithmically rough Möbius sum.

The absolute support bound is again independent of the blind index. The coset `-H` contains exactly `|H|` reduced residue classes modulo `q`, so

\[
A_H(U):=
\#\{m\le U:-m\bmod q\in H\}
=
\frac{|H|}{q}U+O(|H|).
\tag{8}
\]

Because every divisor of `P` lies in `H`, multiplication by such a divisor preserves the coset. Inclusion-exclusion therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_H(T)
&:=\#\{n\le T:-n\bmod q\in H,(n,P)=1\}\\
&=\sum_{d\mid P}\mu(d)A_H(T/d)\\
&=
\frac{|H|T}{q}\frac{\varphi(P)}P
+O\!\left(|H|2^{\pi(y)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Since the support of `g_y` is contained in this rough set, `(7)` and `(9)` imply

\[
\boxed{
|B_S^{(A)}(X)|
\le
\frac Tq\frac{\varphi(P)}P
+O\!\left(2^{\pi(y)}\right).
}
\tag{10}
\]

At `y=c\log X` and `q=X^{2\alpha+o(1)}` this is

\[
|B_S^{(A)}(X)|
\ll
\frac{X^{1-2\alpha+o(1)}}{\log\log X}
+X^{o(1)}.
\tag{11}
\]

So enlarging the quadratic projector to the complete blind family does not change the support exponent. As in `MC-245`, support alone reaches `X^{1/2+o(1)}` only at `\alpha\ge1/4`.

The new structural point is the relation between this full coset and the quadratic cell. Let

\[
\lambda:G\to\mathbb F_2^S
\]

be the shell Legendre-sign map of `MC-238`, and let

\[
W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{\lambda(p):p\le y\text{ prime}\}.
\]

For the odd prime-square modulus `q`, the kernel of `\lambda` is the square subgroup `G^2`. Since `\lambda(H)=W`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda^{-1}(W)=HG^2.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently the quadratic affine cell in `MC-245` is the larger set `-HG^2`, while the full source coset in `(7)` is `-H`:

\[
-H\subseteq -HG^2.
\tag{13}
\]

If `\kappa` denotes the quadratic blind dimension of `MC-244`, then

\[
2^\kappa=[G:HG^2]
=|(G/H)/(G/H)^2|.
\tag{14}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\kappa=0
\iff HG^2=G
\iff [G:H]\text{ is odd}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

The last equivalence uses the fact that squaring is surjective on a finite abelian group exactly when that group has odd order. Thus `\kappa=0` eliminates every quadratic blind character but **does not imply `H=G`**. A nontrivial odd-order quotient `G/H` leaves higher-order blind characters even though the Legendre matrix has full column rank.

The full source obstruction is therefore sharper than the quadratic dichotomy left by `MC-244`--`MC-245`: one must either prove the actual small-prime generation statement `H=G`, or obtain signed cancellation for the single rough Möbius coset `(7)` (equivalently, cancellation across the complete annihilator family). Proving only `\kappa=0` can still leave an odd-order blind quotient untouched.

## 1. Full subgroup orthogonality gives the exact projector

For `b\in G`, the characters in `A=H^\perp` are precisely the dual group of `G/H`. Standard finite-abelian orthogonality yields `(3)`. Substituting it into the character expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_A(f;N,q,a)
&=
\frac1{\varphi(q)}
\sum_{m\le N}f(m)
\sum_{\chi\in A}\chi(a^{-1}m)\\
&=
\frac{|A|}{\varphi(q)}
\sum_{\substack{m\le N\\a^{-1}m\in H}}f(m),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is `(2)` because `|A|/\varphi(q)=1/|H|`.

There are `|H|` eligible reduced residue classes, each contributing `N/q+O(1)` integers through height `N`. Taking absolute values only after the exact projector gives `(4)`. The conclusion is the full-character analogue of the dimension cancellation in `MC-244`: the number of blind modes and the density of their inverse-transform support are reciprocal quantities.

## 2. The smooth source collapses to one rough coset

For `\chi\in A`, every prime `p\le y` satisfies `\chi(p)=1`; hence `\chi(d)=1` for every `y`-smooth `d`, and also `\chi(P)=1`. Isolating `A` in `(5)` and interchanging finite sums gives

\[
B_S^{(A)}(X)
=
\frac1{\varphi(q)}
\sum_{\chi\in A}\chi(-1)
\sum_{n\le T}(\mu*s_y)(n)\chi(n).
\tag{16}
\]

Using `(6)` and then `(3)` on `-n` proves `(7)`. This is coefficientwise: no contour shift, zero-density estimate, or analytic continuation enters the collapse.

The same subgroup invariance makes the rough support count exact up to the elementary residue-class endpoint error. For `d\mid P`, one has `d\in H`, so

\[
-dm\in H\iff -m\in H.
\]

Inserting `\mathbf1_{(n,P)=1}=\sum_{d\mid(n,P)}\mu(d)` into `(8)` therefore yields `(9)`, and hence `(10)`.

## 3. Quadratic full rank detects only the elementary 2-quotient

For each shell prime `r`, the Legendre character on `(\mathbb Z/r^2\mathbb Z)^\times` has the square subgroup as its kernel. By the Chinese remainder theorem,

\[
\ker\lambda=G^2.
\tag{17}
\]

Because `H` is generated by the small primes and `W` by their `\lambda`-images,

\[
\lambda(H)=W.
\tag{18}
\]

For any homomorphism, the inverse image of the image of a subgroup is that subgroup times the kernel; `(17)`--`(18)` therefore prove `(12)`.

Now let `Q=G/H`. The quotient `G/HG^2` is exactly `Q/Q^2`, so it is the maximal elementary `2`-quotient of `Q`. Its dual is the quadratic blind family. This proves `(14)` and shows precisely what the Legendre-rank test can and cannot certify.

A simple finite model demonstrates the distinction: if `Q` is cyclic of order `3`, then `Q/Q^2` is trivial, so there are no nonprincipal quadratic characters, while `Q` itself still has two nonprincipal characters of order `3`. Nothing in `\kappa=0` excludes this configuration. The shell-square arithmetic may of course force more structure, but such a theorem would have to go beyond the quadratic rank calculation.

## 4. The analytic obstruction also lifts to the full annihilator

For every `\chi\in A` and `\Re(s)>1`, the rough Möbius twist satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{y,\chi}(s)
&:=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{g_y(n)\chi(n)}{n^s}\\
&=\prod_{p>y}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^s}\right)\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac1{L(s,\chi)}
\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus a mode-by-mode proof for the complete blind family faces reciprocal Dirichlet `L`-functions, not just their quadratic subfamily. On the other hand, the source asks for only one inverse-transformed coset. Its Dirichlet series is

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\varphi(q)}
\left(\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\right)
\sum_{\chi\in A}\frac{\chi(-1)}{L(s,\chi)}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore cross-character cancellation in the reciprocal-`L` aggregate is logically distinct from controlling every blind `L(s,\chi)^{-1}` separately. Equation `(20)` does not supply such cancellation; it identifies exactly where it would have to occur.

## 5. Prior-art and novelty audit

The algebra in this finding is classical. Annihilator orthogonality, the duality between `G/H` and `H^\perp`, the identification of quadratic characters with the dual of `Q/Q^2`, and inclusion-exclusion on the small-prime set are standard finite-group and multiplicative-number-theory mechanisms. `MC-238` already identified `H`, its full annihilator, and the distinction between full generation and the quadratic Legendre-rank diagnostic. `MC-244`--`MC-245` specialized the inverse transform to the quadratic family.

The external literature also makes character-family/coset analysis standard. Shōta Inoue, *Some explicit formulas for partial sums of Möbius functions* (J. Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 33 (2021), 273--315, DOI `10.5802/jtnb.1162`), treats Möbius partial sums in arithmetic progressions through Dirichlet characters and finite Euler products. Ian Petrow and Matthew P. Young, *The fourth moment of Dirichlet L-functions along a coset and the Weyl bound* (Duke Math. J. 172 (2023), DOI `10.1215/00127094-2022-0069`, arXiv `1908.10346`), and Bradford Garcia and Matthew P. Young, *Asymptotic second moment of Dirichlet L-functions along a thin coset* (Forum Math. Sigma 13 (2025), e83, DOI `10.1017/fms.2025.44`), study moments of Dirichlet `L`-functions over character cosets/subgroups. These works do not provide the reciprocal-`L` cancellation required by `(20)` for this moving smooth-prime annihilator.

No novelty is claimed for any of those mechanisms. The durable mathematical delta is an **internal frontier correction**: the exact source inversion and index cancellation of `MC-244`--`MC-245` extend to the complete annihilator, and the resulting group quotient shows that quadratic full rank can leave an odd-order blind sector. This prevents `\kappa=0` from being mistaken for the full small-prime generation gate.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification tests

- No proof of `H=G` is given. The least-character-witness/small-generator problem identified in `MC-238` remains open for the shell-square family.
- No signed power cancellation is proved for the rough coset `(7)`. The support estimate `(11)` remains insufficient throughout `\alpha<1/4`.
- `\kappa=0` is neither claimed nor assumed. The point is conditional and structural: even if it were proved, odd-order blind characters could remain unless `H=G` follows from additional arithmetic input.
- The identity `\ker\lambda=G^2` uses the present product of odd prime squares. A different modulus family requires its own quadratic quotient calculation.
- Equation `(19)` is initially an absolutely convergent Euler-product identity in `\Re(s)>1`. Moving it left cannot be used to smuggle in the zero-free region one is trying to prove.
- A bound for the aggregate `(7)` does not imply comparable bounds for every `\chi\in A`; conversely, bounding every mode separately is stronger than the source requires.
- The cited `L`-moment papers establish adjacent character-coset machinery, not a theorem about the reciprocal-`L` sum `(20)` or the present moving subgroup `H_y(q)`.

## Research consequence

The current blind-sector fork should be stated at the level of the full quotient `G/H`, not only its quadratic shadow. There are now two exact exits:

1. prove **full small-prime generation** `H_y(q)=G`, which kills every nonprincipal blind character; or
2. prove fixed-power signed cancellation for the **single source-selected rough Möbius coset** `-H` in `(7)` (or an equivalent cancellation theorem for the full reciprocal-`L` aggregate `(20)`).

Quadratic full rank is valuable evidence about the `2`-primary quotient, but by itself it is not either exit.
