# MC-404 — Finite divisor-incidence algebra collapses exactly to the lcm semilattice

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` · `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` · `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT` · `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM` · `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` · `NON-RH`.

## Claim

`MC-398` showed that the two divisor labels in the standard squared Selberg weight collapse exactly to their least common multiple. The same obstruction is not specific to degree two.

Let `D` be any finite set of positive integers and put

\[
e_d(n):=\mathbf 1_{d\mid n}\qquad(d\in D).
\]

Let `L(D)` be the finite lcm-closure of `D`, including the empty lcm `1`:

\[
L(D):=\{\operatorname{lcm}(S):S\subseteq D\},
\qquad \operatorname{lcm}(\varnothing)=1.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose a weight `F:\mathbf N\to\mathbf C` depends on `n` **only through the finite divisor-incidence vector**

\[
v_D(n):=(e_d(n))_{d\in D}\in\{0,1\}^{D}.
\tag{2}
\]

Then there are unique coefficients `C_F(\ell)` for `\ell\in L(D)` such that

\[
\boxed{
F(n)=\sum_{\ell\in L(D)} C_F(\ell)\,\mathbf 1_{\ell\mid n}
}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, the pointwise algebra generated by the divisibility indicators is exactly the finite semilattice algebra generated by lcm:

\[
\boxed{
e_d e_e=e_{[d,e]}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, for every arithmetic observable `T(n)` and every `N`, one has the exact regrouping

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}F(n)T(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{\ell\in L(D)\\\ell\le N}}
C_F(\ell)
\sum_{m\le N/\ell}T(\ell m).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus **no finite-degree nonlinear modification of divisor incidence, and in fact no arbitrary finite Boolean function of the divisor-incidence pattern, can manufacture a retained tuple variable**. Products, higher powers, higher moments, threshold functions, complements, or mixed polynomial expressions in the predicates `d\mid n` may change the scalar lcm coefficient and enlarge the lcm-closure, but all tuple labels still factor through one join variable `\ell` before any analytic estimate is applied.

For the current source/Selberg branch this gives a broader no-go than `MC-398`: replacing the quadratic Selberg majorant by a cubic, quartic, fixed higher-degree, or other finite incidence-only reweighting cannot by itself create the Stadlmann-type arithmetic variable sought by the accepted retained-variable clue. A surviving pre-lcm mechanism must leave the divisor-incidence algebra: some divisor/source label must affect a quotient, phase, congruence, translation, interval, or other downstream arithmetic object in a way that is **not constant on lcm fibers**.

No estimate for `M(x)` follows.

## 1. Divisibility indicators form an lcm semilattice algebra

For positive integers `d,e`, simultaneous divisibility is equivalent to divisibility by the least common multiple:

\[
\mathbf 1_{d\mid n}\mathbf 1_{e\mid n}
=
\mathbf 1_{[d,e]\mid n}.
\tag{6}
\]

Hence `(4)` holds pointwise. By associativity,

\[
\prod_{d\in S} e_d(n)
=
 e_{\operatorname{lcm}(S)}(n)
\tag{7}
\]

for every finite `S\subseteq D`, with the empty product equal to `e_1=1`.

The divisibility order on positive integers has join equal to lcm, so `(6)` is exactly the multiplication law of the join-semilattice generated by `D`. This is the abstract reason the pair collapse in `MC-398` was unavoidable: it is an algebra identity, not a peculiarity of the particular Selberg coefficients.

## 2. Arbitrary incidence-pattern weights have the same normal form

Write `Y=v_D(\mathbf N)` for the finite set of incidence patterns actually realized by integers. If `F` depends only on `v_D(n)`, there is a function `h:Y\to\mathbf C` with

\[
F(n)=h(v_D(n)).
\]

Extend `h` arbitrarily to a function on the full Boolean cube `\{0,1\}^D`. Every function on a finite Boolean cube has a multilinear expansion

\[
h(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq D} a_S\prod_{d\in S}x_d.
\tag{8}
\]

Substitute `x_d=e_d(n)` and apply `(7)`:

\[
F(n)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq D} a_S e_{\operatorname{lcm}(S)}(n)
=
\sum_{\ell\in L(D)}
\left(
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq D\\\operatorname{lcm}(S)=\ell}}a_S
\right)e_\ell(n).
\tag{9}
\]

This proves existence in `(3)`. It also shows explicitly what happens to every apparent high-dimensional tuple or subset label: all coefficients in one lcm fiber are added before the arithmetic variable `n` is seen.

The coefficients in `(3)` are unique as functions on all positive integers. Indeed, the finite family of distinct divisibility indicators `e_\ell`, `\ell\in L(D)`, is linearly independent. If

\[
\sum_{\ell\in L(D)}c_\ell e_\ell(n)=0
\quad\text{for all }n,
\]

choose a divisibility-minimal `\ell` with `c_\ell\ne0` and evaluate at `n=\ell`. Every proper divisor from the family has zero coefficient by minimality, while no non-divisor contributes, leaving `c_\ell=0`, a contradiction.

Therefore `(3)` is an intrinsic normal form, not merely one possible polynomial presentation.

## 3. Weighted sums lose the tuple labels before Cauchy or differencing

Insert `(3)` into an arbitrary weighted sum and exchange finite sums:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\le N}F(n)T(n)
&=\sum_{\ell\in L(D)}C_F(\ell)
  \sum_{\substack{n\le N\\\ell\mid n}}T(n)\\
&=\sum_{\substack{\ell\in L(D)\\\ell\le N}}C_F(\ell)
  \sum_{m\le N/\ell}T(\ell m),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is `(5)`.

This supplies a representation-invariance test stronger than the pairwise test in `MC-398`. Suppose an argument expands a nonlinear divisor weight into labels `(d_1,\ldots,d_k)` and obtains a saving by counting those labels as separate observations after Cauchy, Hölder, or `q`-van der Corput. If the downstream arithmetic object depends on the labels only through the predicates `d_j\mid n`, then `(3)`--`(5)` regroup the whole expression before the estimate into lcm coefficients. A saving that disappears under that exact regrouping is coefficient multiplicity, not arithmetic sample multiplicity.

In particular this applies to weights built from one or several finite divisor sums

\[
S_j(n)=\sum_{d\in D_j} g_j(d)\mathbf 1_{d\mid n}
\]

through any fixed polynomial `Q(S_1(n),\ldots,S_r(n))`. It also applies to non-polynomial rules such as clipping or thresholding **provided their value is determined solely by the same finite Boolean incidence vector**: on a finite Boolean cube such a rule still has the multilinear representation `(8)`.

## 4. Consequence for the source-signature frontier

Take for `T(n)` the current source-signature weight from `MC-397`, for example

\[
T_a(n)=\mathbf 1_{(n,P)=1}\,|\widehat a(\Phi(n))|^2.
\]

Equation `(5)` becomes

\[
\sum_{\ell}C_F(\ell)
\sum_{m\le N/\ell}
\mathbf 1_{(\ell m,P)=1}
|\widehat a(\Phi(\ell m))|^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This has exactly the same structural type as the lcm-normal form isolated in `MC-398`: the nonlinear incidence rule changes `C_F` and the set of possible `\ell`, but supplies no second arithmetic label inside the inner sum.

Therefore a proposal to escape `MC-397`--`MC-403` by “keeping more Selberg divisor variables”, “using a higher moment”, or “using a more nonlinear divisor majorant” must first answer a sharper question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What downstream arithmetic quantity varies inside a fixed lcm fiber?}
}
\tag{11}
\]

If the answer is “none”, the proposal remains inside the algebra `(3)` and the extra formal variables are compressible bookkeeping. If the answer is a quotient such as `n/d`, a residue condition involving a particular divisor label, a phase depending separately on `d`, a shifted endpoint depending on `d`, or another tuple-sensitive object, then `(3)` no longer applies automatically and the branch is genuinely outside this obstruction. Such a candidate must still show that the surviving variable changes the post-Cauchy diagonal geometry rather than merely surviving notation.

This sharpens the phrase “pre-quadratic variable” in the accepted clue: **pre-quadratic is not enough if the construction still uses only simultaneous divisor incidence. The variable must be pre-lcm in the stronger sense of changing arithmetic within an lcm fiber.**

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

The underlying algebra is classical. In the divisibility lattice of positive integers, gcd is meet and lcm is join; finite joins form a join-semilattice. Standard lattice terminology and the associativity, commutativity, and idempotence of join are covered, for example, in B. A. Davey and H. A. Priestley, *Introduction to Lattices and Order*, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press (2002), Chapter 2, DOI `10.1017/CBO9780511809088.004`.

The degree-two number-theoretic specialization is likewise standard Selberg-sieve algebra. Zeev Rudnick's 2015 course notes *Selberg's Sieve — An Introduction* explicitly pass from simultaneous conditions `d_1\mid n`, `d_2\mid n` to `[d_1,d_2]\mid n` and the classical quadratic form with lcm denominator: https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/courses/sieves2015/selbergsieveintro.pdf.

A targeted mechanism-level search on 20 September 2026 found no basis for a novelty claim for `(3)` as abstract algebra: it is an elementary finite-Boolean/semilattice consequence of `(6)`. No novelty is claimed. The durable contribution is the **frontier-level no-go** obtained by applying that classical algebra to the live Mathia escape route: `MC-398` is not just a quadratic accident, and changing the degree or nonlinear presentation of a pure divisor-incidence weight cannot restore the missing retained arithmetic dimension.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Finite incidence family.** The statement is pointwise for every finite `D`; `D` may grow with the external scale. No uniform analytic bound in `|D|` or `|L(D)|` is asserted.
- **The lcm coefficient can be very complicated.** Equation `(3)` is a representation theorem, not a useful bound for `C_F(\ell)`.
- **Tuple-sensitive arithmetic is outside the theorem.** If a divisor label enters a quotient, phase, congruence, translation, endpoint, or another downstream object separately, it cannot be discarded merely because the same label also appears in a divisibility predicate.
- **Higher moments are not ruled out in general.** They are ruled out only as a source of independent tuple multiplicity when all retained label dependence is incidence-only.
- **No positivity is created.** Even when `F(n)\ge0`, the normal-form coefficients `C_F(\ell)` may be signed.
- **No source-signature estimate is supplied.** Equation `(10)` still requires actual arithmetic control of the lcm/source structure identified in `MC-403`.
- **No Mertens cancellation is assumed or proved.** The argument is finite algebra and does not use `1/\zeta`, analytic continuation, or a zero-free region.
- **No RH conclusion.** The result removes a representation class from the retained-variable search; it does not improve a bound for `M(x)`.

## Consequence for the research line

The lcm compression of `MC-398` extends to the entire finite algebra of pure divisor-incidence observables. Therefore the live retained-variable branch should not spend effort on increasing the degree or nonlinear complexity of a Selberg/divisor weight while leaving every divisor label coupled to `n` only through `d\mid n`.

The surviving alternatives are now cleaner. On the existing lcm side, one must exploit the actual coefficient/source-signature arithmetic identified in `MC-403`. On the alternative side, one must construct an object whose arithmetic content varies **within** an lcm fiber before the relevant Cauchy/differencing step and then verify that this variation produces a genuinely smaller diagonal or a new off-diagonal estimate. Merely carrying more divisor labels through notation cannot do so.
