# MC-383 — Small-doubling family mean values retain a global-modulus horizon

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `EXACT-DERIVED` · `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` · `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT` · `SOURCE-COST` · `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` · `NON-RH`.

## Claim

The collective escape route left open by `MC-382` is narrower than “average over many related characters.” A recent theorem of Darbar--Kerr--Munsch--Shparlinski gives exactly such an estimate for sets of characters with small multiplicative doubling, but its conductor dependence shows why this particular form of family averaging cannot recover the rank-linear source tariff sought beyond the current `log log y` horizon.

Their Theorem 2.7 states that for a sufficiently large **prime** modulus `q`, a set `A` of characters modulo `q` with

\[
|\mathcal A\mathcal A|\le K|\mathcal A|,
\qquad A:=|\mathcal A|,
\]

and coefficients `|alpha_n|<=1`, `N<=q`,

\[
\frac1A\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal A}
\left|\sum_{n\le N}\alpha_n\chi(n)\right|
\ll
K^{1/2}
\left(
N^{1/2}+A^{-1/2}N+A^{-3/8}N^{1/2}q^{1/4}
\right)q^{o(1)}.
\tag{1}
\]

This theorem does **not** directly apply to the reciprocal endpoint source package: its common source radical is squarefree composite and the canonical endpoint characters have varying primitive conductors. That mismatch is already enough to prevent a direct use of `(1)`.

More importantly, even granting the endpoint a strictly stronger hypothetical version of `(1)` -- valid for its composite common radical `P` and with the entire `P^{o(1)}` loss deleted -- the direct prime-shell application would only force

\[
\boxed{
P\gg
\frac{y^2 2^{3d/2}}{(\log y)^4}
}
\tag{2}
\]

from a rank-`d` constant-bias endpoint subgroup. Consequently the logarithmic source cost obtained from this family-size gain is only

\[
\boxed{
\log P
\ge
2\log y+\frac32d\log2-4\log\log y-O(1),
}
\tag{3}
\]

not the `d log y` scale required for a source exponent linear in rank.

Thus a collective theorem whose decisive error term has the schematic form

\[
A^{-\beta}N^\gamma q^\theta
\qquad(\beta>0,\ \theta>0\text{ fixed})
\tag{4}
\]

cannot obtain a `q>=y^{cR}` source tariff merely from a character family of size `A<=2^{O(R)}` and an interval length `N=y^{O(1)}`. The family cardinality contributes only `O(R)` to `log q`, whereas `q>=y^{cR}` requires `Omega(R log y)`.

This does **not** rule out collective character methods. It rules out one important scaling pattern. To beat the `MC-382` horizon, a useful collective estimate must avoid paying a fixed positive power of one global common modulus, replace that power by a parameter that decays with the available rank/scale, exploit factor-local conductor structure, or gain substantially more than an exponential-in-rank family cardinality can supply.

No estimate for `M(x)` follows.

## 1. A constant-bias endpoint subgroup has small multiplicative doubling

Use the exact endpoint notation of `MC-321` and `MC-329`. The `R` independent endpoint generators give characters indexed by

\[
a\in\mathbf F_2^R,
\]

and on the distinguished dyadic prime shell their normalized bias is

\[
b(a)=(-1)^{|a|}\left(1-\frac{2|a|}{R}\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Fix any constant `eta` with `0<eta<1/2`, and let

\[
d:=\lfloor\eta R\rfloor.
\]

Take the subgroup `H_d` generated by any `d` coordinate endpoint characters. Then

\[
|\mathcal H_d|=2^d,
\qquad
|\mathcal H_d\mathcal H_d|=|\mathcal H_d|,
\tag{6}
\]

so the small-doubling parameter is exactly `K=1`. Every `a` in this subgroup has `|a|<=d`, hence

\[
|b(a)|\ge1-2\eta=:c_\eta>0.
\tag{7}
\]

If `N_y` is the number of primes in the endpoint shell, `N_y\asymp y/\log y`, then choosing coefficients that select exactly that shell gives

\[
\frac1{|\mathcal H_d|}
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal H_d}
\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal R_y}\chi(p)\right|
\ge c_\eta N_y
\asymp_\eta\frac y{\log y}.
\tag{8}
\]

The point of using a low-dimensional coordinate subgroup rather than the whole cube is that `(8)` has fixed bias amplitude: no `R^{-1/2}` average-bias loss is needed.

## 2. Granting an ideal composite-modulus extension still gives only `(2)`

The actual theorem `(1)` assumes prime `q`, so the following is deliberately an **optimistic stress test**, not an application of the literature theorem.

Grant a version of `(1)` for the common squarefree endpoint radical `P`, delete its `P^{o(1)}` factor entirely, and put

\[
A=2^d,
\qquad N\asymp y,
\qquad K=1.
\]

Equations `(1)` and `(8)` would then give

\[
\frac y{\log y}
\ll_\eta
 y^{1/2}
 +2^{-d/2}y
 +2^{-3d/8}y^{1/2}P^{1/4}.
\tag{9}
\]

The first term is negligible. If, for example,

\[
d\ge(2+\varepsilon)\log_2\log y
\tag{10}
\]

for fixed `epsilon>0`, then the second term is also `o(y/log y)`. Therefore the third term must carry the lower bound, which rearranges to `(2)` up to a constant depending on `eta`.

Taking logarithms gives `(3)`. For `d\asymp R`, the rank contribution is only `O(R)` in `log P`. In contrast, a rank-linear source exponent means

\[
\frac{\log P}{\log y}\gg R,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\log P\gg R\log y.
\tag{11}
\]

The mismatch is a factor of `log y` in the rank-dependent part of the source bill.

The restriction `(10)` is not an inconvenience that hides a gain. If `d` is smaller, the `A^{-1/2}N` term in `(1)` is itself too large to contradict the constant shell bias. Thus this theorem shape becomes useful only after the family already has enough entropy to beat the diagonal term, and once it does, its fixed `P^{1/4}` tariff converts the remaining exponential family gain into only an additive `O(d)` contribution to `log P`.

## 3. The fixed positive global-modulus exponent is the structural bottleneck

The preceding calculation is not special to the numerical exponents `3/8` and `1/4`.

Suppose a direct shell-family estimate contains a term

\[
A^{-\beta}N^\gamma q^\theta
\tag{12}
\]

with fixed `beta,theta>0`, while

\[
A\le e^{CR},
\qquad
N=y^{O(1)}.
\tag{13}
\]

Balancing `(12)` against any shell lower bound of size `y^{1-o(1)}` gives, after taking logarithms,

\[
\theta\log q
\lesssim
O(\log y)+\beta\log A
\le
O(\log y+R).
\tag{14}
\]

Hence

\[
\log q=O(\log y+R)
\tag{15}
\]

at the transition where the estimate can first contradict the shell bias. No optimization of the subset dimension can turn `(15)` into `Omega(R log y)` while `theta` stays fixed and the available family has only exponentially many modes in `R`.

This is the collective analogue of the distinction emphasized in `MC-381`--`MC-382`: **recovering more modes is not enough if the analytic engine charges the wrong resource scale**. In q-vdC the loss is repeated square-rooting with factor depth; here it is a fixed positive power of the global modulus.

## 4. Relation to the existing common-modulus barriers

`MC-329` already shows that the prime Halász--Montgomery bias-energy estimate has a quartic fixed-common-modulus horizon. The present calculation does not improve that exponent and should not be read as an alternative proof of `MC-329`.

Its role is different. `MC-382` explicitly left **collective/multilinear estimates seeing several source factors at once** as a possible way around the q-vdC depth cascade. The Darbar--Kerr--Munsch--Shparlinski theorem is a recent, genuine collective estimate for thin character sets with exactly the small-doubling structure an endpoint subgroup possesses. Auditing its scaling shows that “collective” by itself is insufficient: the conductor tariff in the theorem must also be structurally compatible with the `R log y` source bill.

The endpoint therefore needs more than a stronger large-sieve constant or a larger mode set. A promising replacement would have to exhibit at least one qualitatively different feature:

- factor-local dependence on the source radical rather than `P^theta` for fixed `theta>0`;
- a conductor exponent that decreases with rank or another growing family parameter;
- a multilinear gain whose logarithm is itself of order `R log y`;
- or a source-to-destination invariant that does not reduce to a single common-modulus character-sum norm.

These are requirements on the **shape** of a useful future estimate, not assertions that such an estimate exists.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

The analytic theorem is primary literature, not a Mathia result:

Pranendu Darbar, Bryce Kerr, Marc Munsch and Igor E. Shparlinski, *Large values of L(sigma, chi) for subgroups of characters*, arXiv:2604.02960v1, submitted 3 April 2026; manuscript dated 24 August 2026. Theorem 2.7 is the small-multiplicative-doubling bound `(1)`. Lemma 3.1 records the underlying Heath-Brown-type double-sum estimate for distinct characters of the same conductor in the prime-modulus setting. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02960

The prime-modulus restriction is material. The endpoint common radical is squarefree composite, and this finding does not claim that the theorem extends to that setting. The paper's Remark 2.8 notes an alternative reduction for subgroups to counting congruences `m = gamma n (mod q)` using a dual multiplicative subgroup and points to earlier product-set/subgroup literature; it does not supply the composite endpoint theorem needed here.

No novelty is claimed for Theorem 2.7, small-doubling character families, or the elementary rearrangement of its exponents. The line-specific durable result is the **resource audit** `(2)`--`(15)`: even after granting away the theorem's two immediate endpoint mismatches (prime modulus and the subpower loss), this family-averaging shape cannot supply the rank-linear source exponent that remains live after `MC-382`.

## Boundaries

- This is a no-go for a theorem **shape and direct shell-family use**, not for all collective character methods.
- The actual theorem is not applied to the composite endpoint package.
- The artificial removal of `P^{o(1)}` only strengthens the hypothetical estimate; failure after that strengthening makes the scaling obstruction more, not less, conservative.
- A different choice of coefficients could exploit structure absent from the raw shell bias. This finding does not rule that out.
- A composite-modulus theorem with factor-local arithmetic may behave differently from a naive replacement `q -> P` in `(1)`.
- Nothing here proves the source-cost lower bound in `(11)` beyond the ranges already established by earlier findings.
- No statement about RH or the true order of `M(x)` is made.
