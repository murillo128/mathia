# MC-141 — Primitive Bost–Connes character quadratics collapse exactly to coprimality support

**Status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

Let `q>=3`, let `chi` be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo `q`, and use the degree-zero cyclotomic mode from `MC-140`

\[
A_{\chi,q}
=
\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}
\overline{\chi(u)}\,e(u/q)
\in C^*(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z).
\tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
E_n=\mu_n\mu_n^*
\]

be the canonical Bost–Connes divisibility projection. Then the most obvious symmetry-neutral quadratic survivor left open by `MC-140` has the exact algebraic form

\[
\boxed{
A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\chi,q}
=
q\prod_{p\mid q}(1-E_p).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the quadratic character-balanced observable is **independent of the primitive character `chi`**. After dividing by `q`, it is simply the projection onto residue frequencies coprime to `q`; it remembers which primes divide the frequency and forgets the cyclotomic orientation carried by `chi`.

Consequently, for every Bost–Connes `KMS_beta` state and every `beta>0`, the diagonal product law from `MC-138` gives

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta(A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\chi,q})
=
q\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-\beta}).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, for the normalized mode `B_{chi,q}=q^{-1/2}A_{chi,q}`,

\[
\boxed{
\|B_{\chi,q}\|_{2,\phi_\beta}^2
=
\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-\beta}).
}
\tag{4}
\]

There is also an exact high-temperature Gram statement. If `chi` and `psi` are primitive characters modulo the same `q`, then for `0<beta<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta(A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\psi,q})
=
\mathbf 1_{\chi=\psi}\,
q\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-\beta}).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, the diagonal term is `(3)`, while for `chi!=psi` the product transforms under the nontrivial cyclotomic character `\overline\chi\psi` and is therefore annihilated by the unique symmetry-invariant high-temperature KMS state, exactly as in `MC-140`.

This closes the first nonlinear escape explicitly left open there. Balancing a primitive cyclotomic character against its conjugate makes the observable symmetry-neutral, but at quadratic order that balancing does not retain a hidden oriented signal: it collapses **exactly** into the divisibility diagonal already classified by `MC-138`. In particular, replacing the vanishing linear character moment by its KMS norm, same-modulus Gram matrix, or any statistic determined by `A_{chi,q}^*A_{chi,q}` cannot produce a new critical-half Möbius channel.

## 1. The finite Fourier transform diagonalizes the cyclotomic mode

The finite group algebra generated by the elements `e(a/q)` is canonically the group C*-algebra of `Z/qZ`. Its characters are indexed by `m mod q`; evaluation at the character `m` sends

\[
e(a/q)\longmapsto e^{2\pi i ma/q}.
\]

Therefore `(1)` has Fourier value

\[
\widehat A_{\chi,q}(m)
=
\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}
\overline{\chi(u)}e^{2\pi i mu/q}.
\tag{6}
\]

For a primitive character, the classical separable Gauss-sum identity applied to `\overline\chi` gives

\[
\widehat A_{\chi,q}(m)
=
G(m,\overline\chi)
=
\chi(m)G(1,\overline\chi),
\tag{7}
\]

where the standard extension of a Dirichlet character makes `chi(m)=0` when `(m,q)>1`. Primitivity also gives

\[
|G(1,\overline\chi)|^2=q.
\tag{8}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat A_{\chi,q}(m)|^2
=
q\,\mathbf 1_{(m,q)=1}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

The decisive point is already visible here: squaring the primitive character Fourier mode eliminates the character completely. Only coprimality to its modulus remains.

The Gauss-sum input is classical. NIST DLMF §27.10, equations 27.10.9–27.10.12, records the finite Fourier/Gauss transform of Dirichlet characters, separability for primitive characters, and `|G(1,chi)|^2=q`. No novelty is claimed for `(7)` or `(8)`.

## 2. Coprimality is exactly a product of Bost–Connes divisibility projections

The standard Bost–Connes relation

\[
\mu_n e(r)\mu_n^*
=
\frac1n\sum_{ns=r}e(s)
\tag{10}
\]

at `r=0` yields

\[
E_n
=
\mu_n\mu_n^*
=
\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e(j/n).
\tag{11}
\]

For a prime `p|q`, evaluation at frequency `m mod q` gives

\[
E_p(m)
=
\frac1p\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}e^{2\pi i mj/p}
=
\mathbf 1_{p\mid m}.
\tag{12}
\]

Therefore

\[
P_q^\times
:=
\prod_{p\mid q}(1-E_p)
\tag{13}
\]

has Fourier values

\[
P_q^\times(m)=\mathbf 1_{(m,q)=1}.
\tag{14}
\]

Combining `(9)` and `(14)`, and using that the finite Fourier transform separates elements of `C^*(Z/qZ)`, proves the algebra identity `(2)`.

This identity is stronger than a coincidence of KMS expectations. The quadratic character mode has already collapsed to the coprimality projection **before a state, temperature, limit, or analytic continuation is chosen**.

## 3. KMS evaluation returns the diagonal product law

`MC-138` proves for every `KMS_beta` state that

\[
\phi_\beta(E_p)=p^{-\beta}
\]

and that the diagonal algebras attached to distinct primes factor exactly. Applying that result to `(13)` gives

\[
\phi_\beta(P_q^\times)
=
\prod_{p\mid q}\phi_\beta(1-E_p)
=
\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-\beta}),
\tag{15}
\]

which proves `(3)` and `(4)`.

This has no special critical-half signature. For example, if the moduli run through primorials `q_y=\prod_{p\le y}p`, then

\[
\|B_{\chi,q_y}\|_{2,\phi_\beta}^2
=
\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-\beta}).
\tag{16}
\]

The Euler-product boundary of this quantity is `beta=1`: the product tends to a positive limit for `beta>1` and to zero for `0<beta<=1`. In particular, `beta=1/2` is not singled out. This is consistent with the local support-versus-orientation diagnosis of `MC-138`: once the character is paired with its conjugate, the surviving quadratic data are ordinary divisibility/coprimality data, not an oriented Möbius fluctuation.

The primorial observation is only a diagnostic. Varying `q` also varies the primitive character family, and no Mertens implication follows from `(16)`.

## 4. The high-temperature primitive-character Gram matrix is scalar

The cyclotomic symmetry acts on the modes by

\[
\alpha_g(A_{\chi,q})=\chi(g)A_{\chi,q}.
\tag{17}
\]

Hence

\[
\alpha_g(A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\psi,q})
=
\overline{\chi(g)}\psi(g)
A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\psi,q}.
\tag{18}
\]

If `chi!=psi`, the quotient character `\overline\chi\psi` is nontrivial. `MC-140` proves that for `0<beta<=1` the unique KMS state is cyclotomic-symmetry invariant and annihilates every nontrivial character subspace. Therefore

\[
\phi_\beta(A_{\chi,q}^*A_{\psi,q})=0
\qquad(\chi\ne\psi),
\tag{19}
\]

while `(3)` supplies the common diagonal value. This proves `(5)`.

So the obvious attempt to preserve phase information by keeping a whole finite family of primitive character modes does not help at the level of the KMS quadratic form: in the unique phase their Gram matrix is a scalar multiple of the identity, and that scalar depends only on the prime divisors of `q` after normalization.

## 5. Why this does not control Mertens excursion

The right-hand side of `(2)` is a projection determined entirely by divisibility of a residue frequency by primes dividing `q`. It contains no anchored order of the integers `1,2,...,x`, and it does not retain which primitive character generated it. Thus the quadratic observable loses exactly the orientation that motivated the character route.

Equation `(2)` also shows why a formal meromorphic continuation would be irrelevant here. There is no hidden `L(s,chi)/zeta(s)` quotient left to continue: the balanced quadratic has become a finite algebraic coprimality projector. Any transfer from `(3)` or `(4)` to

\[
M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)
\]

would need additional information not contained in the quadratic mode itself.

Accordingly this finding gives no improved bound for `M(x)` and no RH implication. It is a representation-level obstruction: one natural nonlinear attempt to evade the linear high-temperature projection in `MC-140` lands exactly back in the phase-blind diagonal sector already known to be insufficient.

That absence is **not** treated as a novelty claim. Equation `(2)` is an immediate synthesis of standard Bost–Connes and primitive-character Fourier identities, and the finding is classified accordingly as `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED` and `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

The durable line-specific contribution is the frontier classification: the first symmetry-neutral nonlinear carrier explicitly left open by `MC-140` is not merely numerically phase-insensitive under a KMS state; its loss of character orientation is an exact algebra identity.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Primitivity is essential to the clean statement.** For imprimitive characters modulo `q`, the finite Fourier transform is controlled by the conductor and can have additional vanishing/support structure. Equation `(2)` is not claimed for an arbitrary imprimitive character at its nonminimal modulus.
- **The Gram statement `(5)` is high-temperature and same-modulus.** For `beta>1`, extremal KMS states need not be cyclotomic-symmetry invariant, so mixed character expectations can survive. Characters at different moduli also require a separate common-level analysis.
- **Only quadratic balanced observables are classified.** Higher symmetry-neutral products can contain Jacobi-sum or other multi-character arithmetic and need not reduce to `(2)`. This finding does not rule them out.
- **Non-KMS and non-equilibrium objects are outside the claim.** Time correlations, chosen noninvariant states, or other dynamical constructions may retain information discarded by an equilibrium quadratic form.
- **The identity is algebraic, not asymptotic.** A counterexample to `(2)` would require failure of the primitive Gauss-sum formula, the Bost–Connes projection identity `(11)`, or the finite Fourier separation argument; all are exact and independently checkable.
- **No Mertens transfer is hidden in the norm.** Even if a family of moduli makes `(4)` small, that controls coprimality projections in the Bost–Connes representation, not first-absolute or endpoint excursion of the ordinary Möbius summatory function.

## Consequence for the line

The equilibrium Bost–Connes route has now passed three successive filters. `MC-138` removes diagonal cross-prime covariance, `MC-139` removes nonzero time-evolution degree, and `MC-140` removes linear nontrivial cyclotomic character moments in the unique phase. The present result shows that the most immediate symmetry-neutral repair — pair a primitive character mode with its conjugate and use the quadratic KMS form — **collapses exactly to a coprimality projection in the diagonal algebra**.

Future work should therefore not revisit KMS norms or same-modulus quadratic Gram matrices of the primitive modes as candidate critical-half carriers. A surviving Bost–Connes mechanism would have to use genuinely higher-order symmetry-neutral structure, a non-equilibrium/non-KMS observable, or a different arithmetic carrier, and it would still need an independently cheaper theorem connecting that information to anchored Mertens excursion.
