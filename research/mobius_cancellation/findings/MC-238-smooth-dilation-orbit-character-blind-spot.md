# MC-238 — Smooth-dilation averaging has an exact small-prime character blind spot

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT`, `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

`MC-233` reduces the diagonalized first-shell route to a subpower family of ordinary Möbius progression sums

\[
B_S(X)
=
\sum_{\substack{d\le T\\P^+(d)\le y}}
M_\mu\!\left(\frac Td;Q_S,a_{S,d}\right),
\qquad
 a_{S,d}\equiv-Pd^{-1}\pmod{Q_S},
\tag{1}
\]

where

\[
y=c\log X,
\qquad
P=\prod_{p\le y}p,
\qquad
Q_S=\prod_{r\in S}r^2,
\qquad r\in(y,2y].
\tag{2}
\]

`MC-237` shows that estimating the terms in `(1)` separately by currently available smooth-modulus variance loses a fixed power throughout the strict hard region `2alpha+beta<1/2`. The remaining possibility explicitly left open there is to exploit cancellation **between the smooth-dilation components before applying the triangle inequality**.

There is an exact group-theoretic obstruction to the most immediate version of that escape.

Let

\[
G_S=(\mathbb Z/Q_S\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

and let

\[
H_y(Q_S)
:=
\left\langle p\bmod Q_S:\ p\le y,\ p\text{ prime}\right\rangle
\le G_S
\tag{3}
\]

be the subgroup generated by the small primes. Every `y`-smooth dilation `d` is a unit modulo `Q_S` and lies in `H_y(Q_S)`. Since `P` also lies in that subgroup,

\[
\boxed{
a_{S,d}\in -H_y(Q_S)
\quad\text{for every }P^+(d)\le y.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the source-selected residues in `(1)` do **not** range over arbitrary reduced residue classes. They remain inside one multiplicative coset determined entirely by the small-prime-generated subgroup.

This has a sharp Fourier consequence. For any finite set `\mathcal D` of `y`-smooth dilations and arbitrary complex weights `w_d`, define the residue measure

\[
\nu_w(a)
=
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}w_d\,
\mathbf 1_{a\equiv-Pd^{-1}\, (\mathrm{mod}\,Q_S)}.
\tag{5}
\]

For a Dirichlet character `chi (mod Q_S)`, with Fourier convention

\[
\widehat\nu_w(\chi)
=
\sum_{a\in G_S}\nu_w(a)\overline{\chi(a)},
\]

one has exactly

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_w(\chi)
=
\overline{\chi(-P)}
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}w_d\chi(d).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Let

\[
H_y(Q_S)^\perp
:=
\{\chi\in\widehat G_S:\chi(h)=1\ \text{for every }h\in H_y(Q_S)\}
\tag{7}
\]

be the annihilator. Equivalently, `chi` lies in `(7)` exactly when

\[
\chi(p)=1
\qquad\text{for every prime }p\le y.
\tag{8}
\]

For every such character, `chi(P)=chi(d)=1`, and `(6)` collapses to

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_w(\chi)
=
\overline{\chi(-1)}
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}w_d.
}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular, for nonnegative weights with positive total mass, the normalized Fourier coefficient has modulus exactly one on every character in `H_y(Q_S)^\perp`. **Smooth-dilation averaging produces zero Fourier cancellation on these modes, regardless of how many dilations are combined or how their sizes are distributed.**

Therefore a proof that hopes to rescue `(1)` merely by viewing the residues `a_{S,d}` as a well-spread sample of reduced classes has an additional necessary gate:

\[
\boxed{
H_y(Q_S)=G_S
}
\tag{10}
\]

or, more generally, it must separately control every nonprincipal character in the annihilator `(7)`. By finite-abelian duality, `(10)` is equivalent to

\[
H_y(Q_S)^\perp=\{1\}.
\tag{11}
\]

This is a genuine **least-character-witness / small-generator problem** at the logarithmic cutoff `y=c log X`; it is not supplied by the variance theorem used in `MC-237`.

The square-shell structure gives a concrete finite diagnostic for the real-character part of the obstruction. For each shell prime `r\in S`, let `(\frac{\cdot}{r})` be the quadratic character modulo `r`, inflated to a character modulo `Q_S`. Define the `pi(y) x |S|` matrix over `F_2`

\[
A_{p,r}
:=
\frac{1-(\frac p r)}2,
\qquad p\le y\text{ prime},\ r\in S.
\tag{12}
\]

A vector `v=(v_r)_{r\in S}\in\mathbb F_2^S` defines the quadratic character

\[
\chi_v(n)
=
\prod_{r\in S}
\left(\frac nr\right)^{v_r}.
\tag{13}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\chi_v\in H_y(Q_S)^\perp
\iff
Av=0.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently the dimension of the **quadratic blind sector** is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\bigl(H_y(Q_S)^\perp[2]\bigr)
=
|S|-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}A.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Full column rank of this lower-prime / shell-prime Legendre matrix is therefore a necessary and sufficient condition for eliminating blind quadratic characters. It is not sufficient for `(10)`, because higher-order blind characters may still remain.

At the active incidence scaling

\[
|S|=
(\alpha+o(1))\frac{\log X}{\log\log X},
\qquad
Q_S=X^{2\alpha+o(1)},
\tag{16}
\]

with fixed `alpha>0`, the cutoff satisfies

\[
y
=
\left(\frac{c}{2\alpha}+o(1)\right)\log Q_S.
\tag{17}
\]

Thus `(10)` asks for generation by primes only **linear in `log Q_S`**. Classical generic generator theorems do not provide this. Under the extended/generalized Riemann hypothesis, Bach's explicit subgroup-witness theorem gives generation by elements below `2(log Q_S)^2` for sufficiently large modulus; factoring those elements shows that primes below the same quadratic-logarithmic scale generate. This is still one logarithmic factor longer than `(17)`.

Moreover, a linear-logarithmic generator cutoff is false for general moduli: Graham and Ringrose proved that infinitely many prime moduli `ell` have least quadratic nonresidue

\[
n(\ell)\gg \log\ell\,\log\log\log\ell.
\tag{18}
\]

For such primes, every prime below any fixed multiple of `log ell` is eventually a quadratic residue, so the subgroup generated by those primes lies inside the proper quadratic-residue subgroup. This is only a matched prior-art control: it does **not** prove that the special shell-square moduli `Q_S` have a nontrivial annihilator. It does prove that logarithmic small-prime generation cannot be inserted as a generic fact.

The new frontier is therefore sharper than "average the smooth dilations before taking absolute values." Any such averaging argument must answer at least one of the following source-specific questions:

1. prove that the primes `p<=y` generate the relevant `G_S`, or at minimum eliminate/estimate the annihilator modes actually seen by the Möbius progression vector;
2. prove quantitative Fourier decay of the bounded smooth-dilation measure on every surviving character, not merely all-residue variance of the Möbius values;
3. exploit additional arithmetic coupling between the dilation and the Möbius sum which is not captured by the residue orbit `(4)`.

No estimate for `B_S(X)`, no bound for an individual progression in `(1)`, no assertion that `H_y(Q_S)` is proper for the Mathia moduli, and no consequence for `M(X)` or the zeta zero set is proved here.

## 1. Exact coset confinement

Every prime dividing `Q_S` lies in `(y,2y]`, whereas every prime dividing a `y`-smooth `d` or `P` is at most `y`. Hence

\[
(dP,Q_S)=1.
\]

By definition of `H_y(Q_S)`, both `d mod Q_S` and `P mod Q_S` lie in `H_y(Q_S)`. Since a subgroup is closed under inversion,

\[
Pd^{-1}\in H_y(Q_S),
\]

which proves `(4)`.

The point is not merely that there are `X^{o(1)}` dilations, as already quantified in `MC-233`. Even an arbitrarily large collection of `y`-smooth dilations would remain in the same subgroup. The obstruction is algebraic support in residue space, not cardinality.

## 2. Blind characters are preserved exactly by dilation averaging

Starting from `(5)`, substitute the source residue:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\nu_w(\chi)
&=
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}
 w_d\overline{\chi(-Pd^{-1})}\\
&=
\overline{\chi(-P)}
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}w_d\chi(d),
\end{aligned}
\]

because Dirichlet characters are unitary on `G_S` and therefore

\[
\overline{\chi(d^{-1})}=\chi(d).
\]

This proves `(6)`. If `chi` is trivial on `H_y(Q_S)`, then every small prime, `P`, and every smooth `d` has character value `1`, proving `(9)`.

This is stronger than saying that a character sum over the dilations might be difficult. On an annihilator character there is **no oscillatory character sum at all**: the dilation variable has been erased exactly.

Finite-abelian duality gives `(10)`--`(11)`: a subgroup of a finite abelian group is proper exactly when a nontrivial character of the quotient `G_S/H_y(Q_S)` exists. Inflating that quotient character gives a nonprincipal Dirichlet character trivial on `H_y(Q_S)`.

## 3. Quadratic blind modes reduce to an exact Legendre-matrix rank test

Because every shell prime `r` is odd, the unit group modulo `r^2` has one nontrivial quadratic character, induced from the Legendre symbol modulo `r`. Chinese remaindering across the distinct shell primes gives an `|S|`-dimensional elementary `2`-subgroup of the character group. Its elements are exactly `(13)`.

For a small prime `p<=y`,

\[
\chi_v(p)
=
\prod_{r\in S}(-1)^{v_rA_{p,r}}
=
(-1)^{(Av)_p}.
\]

Hence `chi_v(p)=1` for every small prime if and only if `Av=0`, proving `(14)` and `(15)`.

This representation produces a concrete falsification test for future smooth-dilation arguments. If one can exhibit a nonzero kernel vector in `(12)`, then the corresponding quadratic mode is provably untouched by every residue-only smooth-dilation average. If instead the matrix has full column rank, that removes the quadratic blind sector but says nothing yet about characters of odd or higher `2`-power order, nor about quantitative equidistribution of the bounded smooth-dilation set.

The shell cardinality does not force either outcome. Since

\[
\pi(y)\sim\frac{y}{\log y}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\pi(2y)-\pi(y)\sim\frac{y}{\log y},
\]

the number of lower-prime constraints and the maximum number of shell-prime columns live at the same first-order scale. There is therefore no automatic dimension-count nullspace in the full first shell, and no automatic full-rank theorem either. The rank is genuine arithmetic information.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The subgroup-annihilator statements, character orthogonality, and the Legendre-matrix encoding are standard finite-abelian harmonic analysis and elementary quadratic-character theory. No novelty is claimed for them as abstract mathematics.

Eric Bach, *Explicit bounds for primality testing and related problems*, Mathematics of Computation 55 (1990), 355--380, DOI `10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8`, gives under the extended Riemann hypothesis explicit small witnesses outside proper multiplicative subgroups; in particular the standard consequence is generation of `(Z/qZ)^*` by elements below `2(log q)^2` for sufficiently large `q`. This is the relevant classical comparison for `(10)`.

S. W. Graham and C. J. Ringrose, *Lower Bounds for Least Quadratic Non-Residues*, in *Analytic Number Theory*, Progress in Mathematics 85 (1990), 269--309, DOI `10.1007/978-1-4612-3464-7_18`, prove the lower-bound phenomenon `(18)`. It supplies a direct classical warning that `O(log q)` small-prime generation is not valid uniformly over moduli.

The recent Matomäki--Teräväinen preprint *Linnik's problem for multiplicative functions*, arXiv:`2605.27833` (2026), independently emphasizes a real-character obstruction in obtaining sign changes in every reduced residue class. That theorem concerns sign abundance at much larger multiplicative scales and does not imply the generator or amplitude statements here; the connection is structural only.

The durable Mathia delta is the specialization of these classical ideas to the exact `MC-233` source orbit: smooth-dilation recombination has a precise annihilator determined by small-prime generation modulo the shell-square modulus, and its quadratic part is computably equivalent to the rank of `(12)`. This turns an informal "maybe the d-sum cancels" escape into a sharper arithmetic gate without claiming a new theorem of analytic number theory.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification tests

- The finding does **not** assert that `H_y(Q_S)` is proper. For the special Mathia family it may equal the full unit group. Proving either behavior asymptotically is separate arithmetic work.
- Bach's conditional `O((log q)^2)` generation scale is used only as a generic comparison. Its failure to reach `O(log q)` does not prove that the special shell-square family fails to be generated at the shorter cutoff.
- Graham--Ringrose concerns specially chosen prime moduli, not `Q_S`; it is a matched control showing that logarithmic generation cannot be assumed universally.
- Full rank of the quadratic matrix `(12)` eliminates only order-two blind characters. Higher-order characters can remain in the annihilator.
- Even `H_y(Q_S)=G_S` does not imply that the **bounded** set of smooth dilations appearing in `(1)` is equidistributed or has small Fourier coefficients. Generation is only the first algebraic gate.
- Conversely, a nontrivial annihilator does not by itself prove that `B_S(X)` is large. The Möbius progression coefficients may cancel for reasons unrelated to residue sampling. The no-go applies only to cancellation attributed to variation of the smooth-dilation residues alone.
- The weights in the actual sum `(1)` are not a fixed common-length residue measure: the progression length is `T/d`. Equation `(9)` therefore does not estimate `(1)` directly. It identifies modes for which the residue-location part of the `d`-variation contributes no oscillation; any gain on those modes must come from the changing Möbius sums or another source-coupled identity.

## Consequence for the live frontier

The aggregate smooth-dilation escape from `MC-237` remains open, but its first algebraic requirement is now explicit. The residues `-Pd^{-1}` explore only the coset `-H_y(Q_S)`, and every character trivial on the small-prime subgroup is completely blind to that exploration. Future uses of cancellation across `d` should therefore begin by auditing the annihilator—at least the exact quadratic rank test `(12)`--`(15)`—rather than treating the source-selected residues as generic samples of `(Z/Q_SZ)^*`.

If the blind sector is nontrivial, a successful argument must control those character modes by a separate signed-amplitude mechanism. If it is trivial, the next question is quantitative expansion/Fourier decay of the bounded smooth-dilation orbit, not merely group generation. Either outcome is a strictly sharper target than applying another all-residue variance theorem to the individual progression components.
