# MC-142 — Finite Bost–Connes primitive-character polynomials reduce to cyclotomic imbalance or diagonal coprimality

**Status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

For a primitive Dirichlet character `chi` of conductor `q>=3`, retain the Bost–Connes coefficient-algebra mode from `MC-140`–`MC-141`

\[
A_{\chi,q}
=
\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}
\overline{\chi(u)}\,e(u/q)
\in C^*(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z).
\tag{1}
\]

Consider any nonempty finite `*`-monomial

\[
W=\prod_{j=1}^r A_{\chi_j,q_j}^{\varepsilon_j},
\qquad
\varepsilon_j\in\{+1,-1\},
\tag{2}
\]

where exponent `+1` means `A_{chi_j,q_j}` and exponent `-1` means its adjoint. Let

\[
Q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,\dots,q_r),
\]

inflate each character to `(Z/QZ)^times`, and define the net cyclotomic character

\[
\Psi=\prod_{j=1}^r \chi_j^{\varepsilon_j}.
\tag{3}
\]

Also write

\[
\tau_j=G(1,\overline{\chi_j}),
\qquad
C(W)=
\prod_{\varepsilon_j=+1}\tau_j
\prod_{\varepsilon_j=-1}\overline{\tau_j}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then there is an exact dichotomy.

If the net character is trivial,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W=C(W)\,P_Q^\times,
\qquad
P_Q^\times:=\prod_{p\mid Q}(1-E_p).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus **every finite symmetry-balanced monomial of primitive cyclotomic modes, even across different conductors, is only a scalar Gauss-factor times the divisibility/coprimality diagonal**.

If the net character is nontrivial, then `W` transforms under the nontrivial global Bost–Connes cyclotomic character `Psi`. Hence the unique high-temperature equilibrium from `MC-140` annihilates it:

\[
\boxed{
\Psi\ne1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\phi_\beta(W)=0
\qquad(0<\beta\le1).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Combining `(5)` with the diagonal product law of `MC-138` gives, for every `0<beta<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta(W)
=
\begin{cases}
C(W)\displaystyle\prod_{p\mid Q}(1-p^{-\beta}),&\Psi=1,\\[1ex]
0,&\Psi\ne1.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{7}
\]

The balanced identity `(5)` is algebraic and in fact holds before choosing any state or temperature. Therefore every finite `*`-polynomial in the primitive character modes has, under high-temperature KMS expectation, only two possible ingredients term by term:

1. a nontrivial cyclotomic character, which symmetry projects to zero; or
2. a symmetry-balanced term whose operator content has already collapsed to a finite coprimality projection, with any residual character dependence confined to the scalar product of Gauss-sum phases.

This strictly strengthens `MC-141`. That finding classified the quadratic same-modulus survivor `A_{chi,q}^*A_{chi,q}` and left higher products and different moduli open. The common-level Fourier calculation below shows that **all finite primitive-character polynomial moments of this coefficient-algebra type obey the same collapse mechanism**. Jacobi/Gauss arithmetic can survive in the scalar `C(W)`, but it does not supply a new frequency-dependent or cross-prime operator carrier.

## 1. Common-level finite Fourier transform

All factors in `(2)` lie in the finite subgroup algebra generated by denominator `Q`, canonically

\[
C^*\!\left(\tfrac1Q\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\right)
\cong C^*(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z).
\]

Its characters are indexed by `m mod Q`. Evaluation at `m` sends

\[
e(a/q_j)\longmapsto e^{2\pi i ma/q_j}.
\]

For primitive `chi_j`, the classical separable Gauss-sum identity gives

\[
\widehat A_{\chi_j,q_j}(m)
=
G(m,\overline{\chi_j})
=
\tau_j\chi_j(m),
\tag{8}
\]

with the standard extension `chi_j(m)=0` when `(m,q_j)>1`. Taking adjoints yields

\[
\widehat {A_{\chi_j,q_j}^*}(m)
=
\overline{\tau_j}\,\overline{\chi_j(m)}.
\tag{9}
\]

Because finite Fourier transform converts multiplication in this commutative group `C^*`-algebra to pointwise multiplication, `(8)`–`(9)` imply

\[
\widehat W(m)
=
C(W)\,\Psi(m)
\prod_{j=1}^r \mathbf 1_{(m,q_j)=1}.
\tag{10}
\]

The support product is exactly

\[
\prod_j\mathbf 1_{(m,q_j)=1}
=
\mathbf 1_{(m,Q)=1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\widehat W(m)=C(W)\,\Psi(m)\mathbf 1_{(m,Q)=1}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This formula is the entire classification. In particular, if `Psi=1` on `(Z/QZ)^times`, then the Fourier transform of `W` is the constant `C(W)` on unit frequencies and zero elsewhere.

`MC-141` already identified the coprimality projector

\[
P_Q^\times=\prod_{p\mid Q}(1-E_p)
\tag{13}
\]

by its finite Fourier values

\[
\widehat P_Q^\times(m)=\mathbf 1_{(m,Q)=1}.
\tag{14}
\]

Finite Fourier separation now gives `(5)` immediately.

The same argument handles arbitrary powers without extra work: repeat the relevant factor in `(2)`. For example, if `chi` has exact order `d`, then

\[
A_{\chi,q}^d=\tau(\overline\chi)^d P_q^\times.
\tag{15}
\]

Although the coefficient-side expansion of such a power may be expressible through Jacobi sums or other finite character convolutions, all of that arithmetic is compressed by Fourier diagonalization to the single scalar in `(15)` plus the same coprimality support projector.

## 2. Cyclotomic symmetry supplies the complementary high-temperature projection

The global Bost–Connes symmetry group

\[
G=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}
\]

acts on each primitive mode by

\[
\alpha_g(A_{\chi_j,q_j})=\chi_j(g)A_{\chi_j,q_j},
\tag{16}
\]

and therefore on `(2)` by

\[
\alpha_g(W)=\Psi(g)W.
\tag{17}
\]

For `0<beta<=1`, the KMS state is unique and hence `G`-invariant, as recorded in `MC-140`. If `Psi` is nontrivial, choose `g` with `Psi(g)!=1`; invariance then forces

\[
\phi_\beta(W)=\Psi(g)\phi_\beta(W),
\]

which proves `(6)`.

If `Psi=1`, no symmetry argument is needed. Equation `(5)` reduces the monomial to the diagonal projection. `MC-138` gives exact primewise factorization and

\[
\phi_\beta(E_p)=p^{-\beta},
\]

so

\[
\phi_\beta(P_Q^\times)
=
\prod_{p\mid Q}(1-p^{-\beta}),
\]

proving `(7)`.

Thus the high-temperature `*`-moment problem for any finite family of these primitive cyclotomic modes is exhausted by finite Fourier/Gauss constants, a net-character selection rule, and finite Euler coprimality factors. No higher polynomial order creates an additional equilibrium carrier inside this class.

## 3. What survives, and what does not

The result does **not** say that all arithmetic information in a balanced higher moment disappears. The scalar

\[
C(W)
\]

can retain finite-conductor Gauss phases and can be rewritten in terms of classical Gauss/Jacobi-sum arithmetic in special cases. For normalized modes `B_{chi,q}=q^{-1/2}A_{chi,q}`, primitivity gives `|tau(chi)|=sqrt(q)`, so the corresponding balanced scalar has unit modulus.

What disappears is the hoped-for **new operator-valued equilibrium structure**. Once the net cyclotomic character is neutralized, every finite product has the same frequency support shape `1_(m,Q)=1`; all dependence on `beta` in its KMS expectation is the finite diagonal factor

\[
\prod_{p\mid Q}(1-p^{-\beta}).
\tag{18}
\]

That factor has the same divisibility interpretation already classified in `MC-138` and `MC-141`. It does not encode the anchored additive order of `1,2,...,x`, and finite products do not single out `beta=1/2` by a new cancellation mechanism.

This is stronger than saying that one chosen norm or Gram matrix is uninformative. It classifies the entire **finite polynomial equilibrium envelope** generated by primitive coefficient-character modes. Within that envelope, raising polynomial degree or mixing finitely many conductors cannot restore a phase-sensitive Möbius carrier that was lost at linear and quadratic order.

## 4. Prior art and novelty assessment

Every mathematical ingredient is classical.

- NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §27.10, especially equations 27.10.10–27.10.12, records separability of primitive Dirichlet Gauss sums, the vanishing away from unit frequencies, and `|G(1,chi)|^2=q`; it cites Apostol, *Introduction to Analytic Number Theory* (1976), Chapter 8.
- Finite Fourier transform on cyclic group algebras, multiplication of one-dimensional characters, and the common-level embedding through the least common multiple of the conductors are standard finite harmonic analysis.
- The Bost–Connes cyclotomic symmetry action, high-temperature KMS uniqueness, divisibility projections, and diagonal factorization used here were already audited in `MC-138`–`MC-141` against the classical Bost–Connes/Neshveyev theory.

A targeted literature search located the standard primitive-character Fourier/Gauss identities and Bost–Connes symmetry ingredients but did not identify this all-orders finite-polynomial classification as a separately named theorem. That absence is **not** a novelty claim: `(12)` makes the result an immediate finite-Fourier consequence of established identities. The durable Mathia delta is the exact frontier closure, not a new theorem about Dirichlet characters or the Bost–Connes system.

In particular, the reference in `MC-141` to possible higher Jacobi-sum structure should be read more narrowly after this finding. Such arithmetic may remain in the scalar coefficient `C(W)`, but for finite products of the primitive modes `(1)` it does not produce an additional symmetry-balanced operator beyond `P_Q^times`.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Primitive input is essential to `(8)` in this clean form.** Imprimitive characters at a nonminimal modulus have conductor-dependent Fourier support and are not classified here merely by relabelling them as primitive.
- **The theorem classifies finite `*`-polynomials in coefficient-algebra modes `(1)`.** Infinite series, limits over growing conductor families, completions requiring nontrivial convergence, and observables involving semigroup isometries are different mathematical objects and require separate audits.
- **Balanced Gauss phases survive as scalars.** The theorem does not claim those phases are arithmetically trivial; it says they do not create a new operator-valued KMS carrier or new `beta`-dependence.
- **High-temperature annihilation of unbalanced terms uses `0<beta<=1`.** In symmetry-broken extremal KMS states for `beta>1`, nontrivial character moments may survive, as in `MC-140`.
- **Non-equilibrium/time-correlation constructions are outside the claim.** The result concerns coefficient-algebra products evaluated as equilibrium KMS moments.
- **No Mertens estimate follows.** Even complete knowledge of `(7)` for all finite words does not itself control `M(x)`; any such transfer would need additional information carrying ordinary integer-prefix order.
- A counterexample to `(5)` under the stated hypotheses would have to violate either primitive Gauss separability `(8)`, the support identity `(11)`, or finite Fourier separation. These are exact finite statements and provide a decisive audit test.

## Consequence for the line

The Bost–Connes equilibrium residual is now substantially smaller than after `MC-141`. It is no longer productive to escalate from linear character expectations to finite higher polynomial moments of the same primitive cyclotomic coefficient modes, nor to mix finitely many primitive conductors and hope that symmetry balancing creates a new critical-half channel. Every such monomial is either projected out by nontrivial cyclotomic symmetry or collapses algebraically to a scalar multiple of the already-classified coprimality diagonal.

A genuinely new Bost–Connes continuation must therefore leave this finite polynomial coefficient envelope in a substantive way: for example through convergent infinite/conductor-growing constructions with a proved information budget, degree-zero observables involving semigroup structure not reducible to coefficient products, or non-equilibrium dynamics. Any survivor still has to solve the independent source-to-amplitude problem: produce a Möbius-specific quantity whose control transfers to anchored Mertens excursion more cheaply than an RH-equivalent estimate.
