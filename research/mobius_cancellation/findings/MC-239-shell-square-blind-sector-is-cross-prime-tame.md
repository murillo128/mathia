# MC-239 — The shell-square blind sector is tame and necessarily cross-prime

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT`, `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM`, `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

Continue the smooth-dilation character gate isolated in `MC-238`. Let

\[
y=c\log X,
\qquad
S\subset\{r\text{ prime}:y<r\le 2y\},
\]

and write

\[
Q_S=\prod_{r\in S}r^2,
\qquad
R_S=\operatorname{rad}(Q_S)=\prod_{r\in S}r,
\]

\[
G_S=(\mathbb Z/Q_S\mathbb Z)^\times,
\qquad
H_y(Q_S)=\langle p\bmod Q_S:p\le y\text{ prime}\rangle.
\]

`MC-238` shows that every source-selected smooth-dilation residue lies in one coset of `H_y(Q_S)` and that characters in `H_y(Q_S)^\perp` are completely blind to residue-location averaging across those dilations.

For the special shell-square modulus, the blind quotient is substantially smaller than an arbitrary character group modulo `Q_S`.

Let

\[
\rho:G_S\longrightarrow (\mathbb Z/R_S\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

be reduction modulo the squarefree radical and let

\[
K_S:=\ker\rho.
\]

Then, **without any unproved hypothesis**, one has

\[
\boxed{K_S\subset H_y(Q_S).}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
G_S/H_y(Q_S)
\cong
(\mathbb Z/R_S\mathbb Z)^\times/\rho(H_y(Q_S)),
}
\tag{2}
\]

and every blind character `\chi\in H_y(Q_S)^\perp` factors through the squarefree radical `R_S`. The square lifts `r^2` contribute **no extra wild/principal-congruence blind characters**.

There is a second, asymptotic sharpening. For all sufficiently large `y`, the projection of `H_y(Q_S)` onto each individual local factor

\[
(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times,
\qquad r\in S,
\]

is surjective. Together with `(1)`, its projection onto every

\[
(\mathbb Z/r^2\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

is therefore also surjective. Thus any proper subgroup

\[
H_y(Q_S)<G_S
\]

that survives for large `y` is a **genuinely cross-shell subdirect relation**: it cannot be explained by failure to generate one shell-prime factor locally.

Equivalently, any nonprincipal blind character must have nonprincipal components at at least two shell primes. More precisely, if a prime `\ell` divides the order of a blind character, then `\ell` divides `r-1` for at least two distinct `r\in S`. Hence no odd prime

\[
\ell>\frac y2
\]

can divide the blind quotient: for such `\ell`, at most one shell prime can satisfy `\ell\mid r-1`.

The remaining obstruction in `MC-238` is therefore not a local least-primitive-root or square-lift problem. It is a **simultaneous compatibility problem across shell primes sharing character-order factors**. The quadratic Legendre-matrix sector from `MC-238` is the first natural surviving layer because `2\mid r-1` for every odd shell prime.

No estimate for `B_S(X)`, no proof that `H_y(Q_S)=G_S`, no bound for `M(X)`, and no consequence for the zeta zero set is proved here.

## 1. The entire square-lift kernel is generated by small primes

By the Chinese remainder theorem,

\[
K_S
\cong
\prod_{r\in S}K_r,
\qquad
K_r:=\ker\!\left((\mathbb Z/r^2\mathbb Z)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times\right),
\tag{3}
\]

and each `K_r` is cyclic of prime order `r`.

Fix `r\in S` and set

\[
a_r:=r-1.
\]

Every prime factor of `a_r` is at most `y`. Indeed, if a prime `p>y` divided `r-1`, then because `r-1` is composite and even,

\[
r-1\ge 2p>2y,
\]

contradicting `r\le2y`. Thus `a_r` is `y`-smooth and its residue modulo `Q_S` belongs to `H_y(Q_S)`.

It is also a unit modulo `Q_S`. If a shell prime `s\in S` divided `r-1`, then

\[
0<r-1<2y<2s
\]

when `s\ge r`, while if `s<r` the same divisibility together with `s>y` forces `r-1=s`; either way the only possible shell-sized divisor would give two odd primes differing by one, which is impossible. More directly, since every shell prime exceeds `y` and `r-1<2y`, a shell prime divisor would have to equal `r-1`, but `r-1` is even and greater than `2`.

Let

\[
L_S:=\operatorname{lcm}_{s\in S}(s-1).
\tag{4}
\]

For every `s\in S`, Fermat's theorem gives

\[
a_r^{L_S}\equiv1\pmod s,
\]

so

\[
a_r^{L_S}\in K_S\cap H_y(Q_S).
\tag{5}
\]

At the distinguished coordinate `r`, the binomial expansion gives the exact first-order lift

\[
(r-1)^{r-1}=(-1+r)^{r-1}
\equiv 1+r\pmod{r^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore

\[
a_r^{L_S}
\equiv
1+r\frac{L_S}{r-1}
\pmod{r^2}.
\tag{7}
\]

The coefficient `L_S/(r-1)` is not divisible by `r`. For every `s\in S`, one has `s-1<2y<2r` whenever `r` is the smaller relevant shell prime; in general `r\mid s-1` would force `s-1=r` because `s-1<2y<2r` from `r>y`, hence `s=r+1`, impossible for odd primes above `2`. Thus `r\nmid(s-1)` for every `s\in S`, so `r\nmid L_S`.

Hence the projection of `(5)` to `K_r` is nontrivial. Since `K_r` has prime order `r`, the projection

\[
K_S\cap H_y(Q_S)\longrightarrow K_r
\]

is surjective for every `r\in S`.

Now `|K_S|=\prod_{r\in S}r` is squarefree. Surjectivity onto `K_r` implies `r\mid|K_S\cap H_y(Q_S)|` for every shell prime `r`; therefore

\[
\prod_{r\in S}r
\mid
|K_S\cap H_y(Q_S)|.
\]

The reverse inequality is automatic because the intersection is a subgroup of `K_S`. Thus

\[
K_S\cap H_y(Q_S)=K_S,
\]

proving `(1)`.

Equation `(2)` now follows from the first isomorphism theorem. A character trivial on `H_y(Q_S)` is in particular trivial on `K_S`, so it descends uniquely through `G_S/K_S\cong(\mathbb Z/R_S\mathbb Z)^\times`.

## 2. Every individual shell factor is generated locally for large `y`

Fix `r\in S`. There are exactly

\[
\varphi(r-1)
\tag{8}
\]

primitive-root residue classes modulo `r`, represented by integers in `[1,r-1]`.

An integer `n<r<2y` which is not `y`-smooth must itself be a prime in `(y,r)`. If a composite `n` had a prime divisor `p>y`, then

\[
n\ge2p>2y>n,
\]

which is impossible. Therefore the number of non-`y`-smooth representatives in `[1,r-1]` is at most

\[
\pi(2y)-\pi(y)=O\!\left(\frac y{\log y}\right).
\tag{9}
\]

On the other hand, the classical lower bound for Euler's totient gives

\[
\varphi(r-1)
\gg
\frac{r-1}{\log\log(r-1)}
\gg
\frac y{\log\log y}.
\tag{10}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{y}{\log\log y}
\gg
\frac{y}{\log y},
\]

for sufficiently large `y` not all primitive-root representatives can lie among the exceptional primes `(y,r)`. Hence there exists a primitive root `g_r<r` whose prime factors are all at most `y`.

Such a `g_r` is a product of the generators defining `H_y(Q_S)`, so its reduction modulo `r` lies in the local projection of `H_y(Q_S)` and generates `(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{proj}_r\rho(H_y(Q_S))
=(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times
}
\tag{11}
\]

for every shell prime `r`, once `y` is sufficiently large.

Because `(1)` already supplies the full principal-congruence kernel at `r`, the projection of `H_y(Q_S)` onto `(\mathbb Z/r^2\mathbb Z)^\times` is also surjective.

The estimates `(9)`--`(10)` are classical. `MC-S6` records Rosser--Schoenfeld's explicit prime-function estimates; the same paper contains the standard explicit control of `n/\varphi(n)` giving `(10)`. No new primitive-root theorem is being claimed: the argument is only a counting specialization to the unusually dense smooth set available in the shell `r<2y`.

## 3. What a remaining blind character must look like

By `(1)`, every blind character factors through

\[
\overline G_S
:=
(\mathbb Z/R_S\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
\prod_{r\in S}(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times.
\tag{12}
\]

Let

\[
\overline H_S:=\rho(H_y(Q_S)).
\]

Equation `(11)` says that `\overline H_S` is a **subdirect subgroup** of the product `(12)`: every coordinate projection is onto. A proper subdirect subgroup can still exist through relations between common quotients of different coordinate groups, so local surjectivity does not imply `\overline H_S=\overline G_S`.

This gives an immediate support restriction. Write a blind character as

\[
\chi=\prod_{r\in S}\chi_r.
\tag{13}
\]

If exactly one local component `\chi_r` were nonprincipal, surjectivity of `\overline H_S\to(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times` would let that coordinate range over the full local group while `\chi` stayed trivial on `\overline H_S`, forcing `\chi_r=1`. Therefore every nonprincipal blind character has at least two nonprincipal local components.

Apply the same argument to any nontrivial `\ell`-primary component of `\chi`. Such a component can be nonprincipal at `r` only when

\[
\ell\mid r-1.
\]

Hence a necessary condition for `\ell` to divide the order of the blind quotient is

\[
\boxed{
\#\{r\in S:\ell\mid r-1\}\ge2.
}
\tag{14}
\]

For an odd prime `\ell>y/2`, a shell prime `r\in(y,2y]` satisfying `\ell\mid r-1` must have

\[
r-1=k\ell,
\qquad k<4.
\]

Since `r-1` is even and `\ell` is odd, `k` must be even; hence `k=2` and `r=2\ell+1`. There is at most one such shell prime. Thus `(14)` cannot hold, proving

\[
\boxed{
\ell>y/2,\ \ell\text{ odd}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell\nmid |G_S/H_y(Q_S)|.
}
\tag{15}
\]

The prime `2` is fundamentally different because every odd shell prime has `2\mid r-1`. This explains why the quadratic Legendre matrix of `MC-238` is not merely a convenient finite diagnostic: it probes the first common quotient shared by every local shell factor. Higher-order blind relations can remain, but only on character-order primes shared by at least two values `r-1`.

## 4. Consequence for the live smooth-dilation route

`MC-238` left two superficially different concerns under the heading of small-prime generation modulo `Q_S`: failure to generate local prime-square unit groups, and correlations tying several local factors together. The present reduction separates them.

The prime-square lift is closed exactly by `(1)`, and local tame generation is closed asymptotically by `(11)`. Thus future work on the aggregate smooth-dilation escape should not spend effort proving a generic least primitive root bound for each individual shell prime or controlling new conductor-`r^2` characters. Those are not the surviving obstruction for this family.

The unresolved algebraic gate is instead

\[
\boxed{
\overline H_S
\stackrel{?}{=}
\prod_{r\in S}(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times,
}
\tag{16}
\]

where `\overline H_S` is already known to project onto every factor. Any failure of `(16)` must come from a simultaneous character relation

\[
\prod_{r\in S}\chi_r(p)=1
\qquad
\text{for every prime }p\le y,
\tag{17}
\]

with at least two nonprincipal components and with their orders supported on common divisors of the numbers `r-1`.

This is a sharper target than the generic small-generator question in `MC-238`. In particular, it isolates the quadratic rank problem there as a genuine common-quotient obstruction while removing the entire square-lift kernel and all one-coordinate blind modes from consideration.

Even if `(16)` is eventually proved, `MC-238`'s next quantitative gate remains: the bounded smooth-dilation measure must have enough Fourier decay to provide a fixed-power gain. Group generation alone does not estimate `B_S(X)`.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The ingredients are classical:

- the Chinese remainder decomposition and the principal-congruence kernel modulo `r^2`;
- Fermat's theorem and the binomial lift `(6)`;
- the fact that `(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times` has `\varphi(r-1)` primitive roots;
- classical bounds `\pi(2y)-\pi(y)=O(y/\log y)` and `\varphi(n)\gg n/\log\log n`, with explicit versions in Rosser--Schoenfeld (`MC-S6`);
- the standard finite-group fact that a subgroup can project surjectively to every factor while remaining a proper subdirect product through common quotient relations.

No novelty is claimed for any of those statements. The literature audit did not supply a reason to treat the exact shell-square specialization as a new theorem of analytic number theory. The durable contribution here is the source-specific reduction forced by the `MC-233`/`MC-238` geometry of the progression family: the annihilator cannot hide in the square lifts or in a single shell factor, so the surviving blind sector is exactly a tame cross-prime compatibility problem.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification tests

- `(1)` is exact for every finite shell set `S`; the local-surjectivity statement `(11)` is asymptotic in `y`.
- Local surjectivity does **not** imply global generation. Diagonal/subdirect subgroups are the explicit matched group-theoretic control against that false inference.
- The finding does not prove that the quadratic Legendre matrix in `MC-238` has full rank. On the contrary, quadratic relations remain the most universal common-factor possibility because every `r-1` is even.
- The finding does not exclude higher-order cross-prime characters when the corresponding order divides at least two numbers `r-1`.
- It does not establish Fourier decay of the bounded set of actual smooth dilations, even when the generated subgroup is full.
- It does not use an unproved non-Wieferich-prime hypothesis. The exact elements `(5)`--`(7)` generate the square-lift kernel without needing a primitive root modulo `r^2`.
- It does not estimate any Möbius progression sum. All analytic signed-amplitude obligations from `MC-233`--`MC-238` remain.

## Consequence for the live frontier

The `MC-238` annihilator gate can now be reduced from characters modulo the full shell-square modulus to **cross-shell characters modulo its squarefree radical**. Every local factor is already generated for large `y`, and the entire principal-congruence kernel is generated for all `y` in the regime. The remaining algebraic question is whether the small-prime images satisfy a nontrivial simultaneous relation across at least two shell primes, beginning with the quadratic Legendre-matrix sector.

This removes a large class of false escape obstructions while making the surviving one more arithmetic: progress now requires controlling common-quotient correlations across the shell, or bypassing the residue orbit with a genuinely source-coupled signed mechanism.
