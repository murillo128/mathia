# MC-204 — Full permutation-invariant ternary source data collapse exactly to signed sum and support count

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED`, `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT`, `DECISIVE-NEGATIVE`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

`MC-203` proved that every within-class statistic with one signed factor and an arbitrary permutation-symmetric function of the remaining support bits reduces to a support-weighted class sum. It left additional signed factors as one possible escape. That escape is genuine only if the added signs retain relational information such as positions, shifts, order, divisor labels, or another marking.

For a fixed source class with

\[
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in\{-1,0,1\}^N,
\tag{1}
\]

put

\[
A(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N x_i,
\qquad
B(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Let `S_N` act by permuting the coordinates. Then:

\[
\boxed{
 x\text{ and }y\text{ lie in the same }S_N\text{-orbit}
 \iff
 (A(x),B(x))=(A(y),B(y)).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently **every** coordinate-permutation-invariant statistic

\[
H:\{-1,0,1\}^N\to Y
\tag{4}
\]

with arbitrary codomain `Y` factors uniquely through the feasible pair `(A,B)`:

\[
\boxed{H(x)=h(A(x),B(x)).}
\tag{5}
\]

This is not restricted to polynomials, fixed degree, one signed factor, or support-only kernels. It classifies the full information quotient produced by forgetting coordinate labels inside a ternary source class.

For the signed polynomial hierarchy this becomes explicit. Define the ordered distinct-site `r`-sign sum

\[
D_r(x):=
\sum_{\substack{i_1,\ldots,i_r\\\text{all distinct}}}
 x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r},
\qquad D_0=1.
\tag{6}
\]

Then

\[
D_r(x)=r!\,e_r(x_1,\ldots,x_N),
\tag{7}
\]

and the whole hierarchy has generating function

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^N \frac{D_r(x)}{r!}t^r
=
(1+t)^{(B+A)/2}(1-t)^{(B-A)/2}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Thus adding two, three, or arbitrarily many signed factors while preserving full coordinate-permutation symmetry **does not create any source invariant beyond `A` and `B`**. The exact escape left by `MC-203` is narrower: one must break this symmetry by retaining a relation between sites, or introduce information not determined by the ternary class itself.

Applied classwise to the logarithmic-primorial source decomposition of `MC-200`--`MC-203`, each residue class `a` has the pair

\[
A_a=\sum_i x_{a,i},
\qquad
B_a=\sum_i x_{a,i}^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Any statistic invariant under arbitrary permutations of the source sites **within each class** therefore factors through the profile

\[
\bigl((A_a,B_a)\bigr)_{a\bmod q}.
\tag{10}
\]

No improved bound for `M(X)`, the rough Möbius sum, or the zeta zero set is claimed. In particular, `(5)` is an informational classification, not a theorem saying that nonlinear functions of `(A_a,B_a)` can never be analytically useful.

## 1. Orbit classification is exact

Write

\[
n_+(x)=\#\{i:x_i=1\},\qquad
n_-(x)=\#\{i:x_i=-1\},\qquad
n_0(x)=\#\{i:x_i=0\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Then

\[
A=n_+-n_-,
\qquad
B=n_++n_-,
\qquad
N=n_++n_-+n_0,
\tag{12}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
n_+=\frac{B+A}{2},\qquad
n_-=\frac{B-A}{2},\qquad
n_0=N-B.
}
\tag{13}
\]

A coordinate permutation preserves the three symbol counts, hence preserves `(A,B)`. Conversely, if two ternary vectors have the same `(A,B)`, equation `(13)` gives the same numbers of `+1`, `-1`, and `0` coordinates, so a permutation carries one vector to the other. This proves `(3)`.

An invariant map `H` is constant on every orbit. Since `(A,B)` labels the orbits bijectively, define `h(A,B)` to be the common value of `H` on that orbit. This proves `(5)` and its uniqueness on the feasible state set

\[
\Omega_N=
\{(A,B):0\le B\le N,\ |A|\le B,\ A\equiv B\pmod2\}.
\tag{14}
\]

There are exactly

\[
\boxed{|\Omega_N|=\sum_{B=0}^N(B+1)=\frac{(N+1)(N+2)}2}
\tag{15}
\]

permutation orbits, compared with `3^N` labelled ternary configurations. The quotient is therefore an exact collapse from exponentially many labelled states to a quadratic-size orbit space. The relevance is structural rather than entropic: every positional relation between equal symbol counts has been deleted.

## 2. All symmetric signed interactions are functions of `(A,B)`

The elementary symmetric polynomial identity gives

\[
\prod_{i=1}^N(1+t x_i)
=
\sum_{r=0}^N e_r(x)t^r.
\tag{16}
\]

For a ternary vector the left side can be grouped by symbol count:

\[
\prod_i(1+t x_i)
=(1+t)^{n_+}(1-t)^{n_-},
\tag{17}
\]

and substituting `(13)` proves `(8)`.

In particular,

\[
D_1=A,
\qquad
D_2=A^2-B,
\tag{18}
\]

and every higher `D_r` is likewise an explicit polynomial in `(A,B)` for fixed `N`. More generally, the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials says that every symmetric polynomial in the labelled coordinates is generated by the elementary symmetric polynomials; here `(8)` shows that the entire elementary-symmetric generator family has already collapsed to `(A,B)`.

But the orbit proof is stronger than the polynomial statement: **arbitrary** permutation-invariant functions on the finite ternary state space factor through `(A,B)`, including discontinuous, thresholded, optimized, or otherwise nonlinear statistics.

Because `x_i^2` is itself determined by the ternary symbol `x_i`, allowing arbitrary mixtures of signed factors and support factors does not alter this conclusion as long as the final observable is invariant under the full coordinate permutation group.

## 3. What this closes from `MC-203`

`MC-203` classified one signed factor with arbitrary symmetric processing of all remaining support sites. Its exact reduction retained the pair `(A_a,B_a)` and identified additional signed factors as one possible way out.

The present orbit theorem shows that **additional signed factors alone are not an escape** if they are aggregated in a fully symmetric way. For example, replacing the one-sign/support hierarchy by all-pairs, all-triples, or all-`r`-tuples of signs merely reads another coefficient of `(8)` and therefore remains a function of the same two counts.

This is an information statement, not a complexity statement. A high-degree function `h(A,B)` can still be difficult to estimate, and an independently proved estimate for such a function could in principle imply new information about `A`. What cannot happen is that increasing symmetric signed degree recovers source structure that `(A,B)` did not retain.

For the classwise primorial representation, the honest remaining routes must therefore keep information beyond the `S_N` orbit. Examples include fixed-shift products `x_i x_{i+h}`, ordered or cyclic correlations, Fourier modes in the site coordinate, divisor-labelled interactions, adjacency data, or another marked relation between sites. These observables are not determined by the three symbol counts and are not covered by `(3)`.

## 4. Why shifted Möbius correlations survive the no-go

The distinction matters for the line's Chowla/correlation frontier. A fixed-shift statistic such as

\[
\sum_i x_i x_{i+h}
\tag{19}
\]

is not invariant under arbitrary permutations of the site labels: it uses the relation `j=i+h`. Two ternary vectors can have identical `(A,B)` and very different shifted correlations because their signs and zeros can be arranged differently along the labelled source coordinate.

This is exactly the kind of relational information that logarithmically averaged Chowla/Elliott results study. `MC-S28` treats products of multiplicative functions evaluated at fixed shifted arguments and therefore lies outside the fully permutation-invariant quotient classified here. The present result does not weaken or subsume that analytic theory; it explains why **positional correlation data are qualitatively different from adding more fully symmetric sign moments**.

The implication for representation design is sharp: if a proposed source decoder first forgets the labels of all source sites and retains only a permutation-invariant summary, no later nonlinear processing can reconstruct fixed-shift, ordering, adjacency, or phase relations from that summary. To exploit such information, it must be retained before quotienting.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The mathematical mechanism is classical. The orbit of a finite-alphabet word under the full symmetric group is determined by its symbol multiplicities, and the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials says that symmetric polynomials are generated by the elementary symmetric polynomials. The Encyclopedia of Mathematics entry *Elementary symmetric polynomial* records both the standard product identity and the completeness of elementary symmetric polynomials as invariants for `S_N`: https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_polynomial. No novelty is claimed for these facts, for multinomial type classes, or for equation `(16)` as general mathematics.

The arithmetic boundary is also established prior art. Tao and Teräväinen's `MC-S28`, *The structure of logarithmically averaged correlations of multiplicative functions, with applications to the Chowla and Elliott conjectures* (Duke Math. J. 168 (2019), 1977--2027, DOI `10.1215/00127094-2019-0002`), studies fixed-shift logarithmically averaged correlations and obtains odd-order logarithmically averaged Chowla cases. Those correlations explicitly preserve a shift relation and therefore are not permutation-invariant symbol-count statistics.

A targeted external audit of finite-alphabet permutation orbits, symmetric-polynomial invariants, and higher shifted Möbius/Liouville correlation language found the component mechanisms above as standard and no basis for a novelty claim about the abstract orbit theorem. The durable contribution is the line-specific no-go: it closes the fully symmetric multi-sign branch left open by `MC-203` and identifies **relational site information**, rather than higher symmetric degree, as the missing representation coordinate.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- The theorem `(3)` assumes a fixed class capacity `N` and the ternary alphabet `{-1,0,1}`. The same count-vector principle extends trivially to other finite alphabets, but that generalization is classical and is not claimed as a Mathia result.
- The theorem applies only after quotienting by the **full** coordinate permutation group inside a class. Invariance under a smaller group, such as cyclic shifts, can preserve substantially more information and is not classified by `(A,B)`.
- The result does not say that `(A,B)` is useless. `A` is the signed class sum itself, so any bound on a suitable function of the class sums may still contain real arithmetic content.
- The result does not say that higher signed correlations are useless. It says that fully symmetric all-site aggregations add no invariant beyond symbol counts. Shifted, ordered, Fourier, divisor-labelled, or otherwise relational correlations escape.
- The result is independent of Möbius cancellation, analytic continuation, zero-free regions, the choice of primorial scale, and the square-free support estimate used in `MC-201`. It is therefore a matched-control no-go valid for every ternary source array.
- Equation `(15)` counts feasible `(A,B)` states for fixed `N`; it is not an entropy estimate for the actual Möbius distribution.
- No analytic claim is made that every nonlinear `h(A,B)` is reducible to a linear decoder or inherits the target-scale error estimate of `MC-203`. The present conclusion is only about retained information.

The orbit classification is finitely falsifiable: a counterexample is any pair of ternary vectors with equal `(A,B)` but different symbol-count triples, or with equal symbol-count triples that cannot be related by a coordinate permutation. Equation `(8)` is falsified by any finite ternary vector whose directly enumerated distinct-site signed sums disagree with its coefficients.

## Consequence for the active frontier

The symmetric mixed-source hierarchy is now closed at the representation level more strongly than `MC-203` alone implied. Once a source class is made permutation-invariant, its complete ternary orbit is exactly the pair `(A_a,B_a)`; adding arbitrarily many symmetric signed or support interactions cannot recover anything else.

Therefore the next genuinely different source mechanism must **retain a relation between labelled sites before symmetrization**, or obtain a new analytic theorem directly on the existing `(A_a,B_a)` profile. The most principled representation escape is the former: fixed-shift, ordered, Fourier, divisor, or other marked relational observables preserve information that the orbit quotient provably destroys. Whether any such retained relation can be controlled strongly enough to improve global Möbius cancellation remains open.
