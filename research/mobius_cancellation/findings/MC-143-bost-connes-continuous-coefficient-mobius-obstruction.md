# MC-143 — Möbius orientation has no continuous realization on the Bost–Connes coefficient spectrum

**Status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

The infinite/norm-completed coefficient-algebra escape left open by `MC-142` cannot recover the Möbius sign by taking a norm-convergent limit of finite cyclotomic coefficient observables.

The Bost–Connes coefficient algebra is the commutative group `C^*`-algebra

\[
C^*(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\cong C(\widehat{\mathbb Z}),
\tag{1}
\]

because the Pontryagin dual of the discrete group `\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z` is the profinite completion `\widehat{\mathbb Z}`. The ordinary integers embed densely in `\widehat{\mathbb Z}`.

There is **no** continuous function

\[
f\in C(\widehat{\mathbb Z})
\]

such that

\[
f(n)=\mu(n)
\tag{2}
\]

for every square-free positive integer `n`. In fact it is already impossible to satisfy `(2)` only on the union of primes and products of two distinct primes.

More quantitatively, if

\[
\mathcal S=\{n\ge1:\mu(n)\ne0\},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{f\in C(\widehat{\mathbb Z})}
\sup_{n\in\mathcal S}|f(n)-\mu(n)|=1.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The upper bound is attained by `f=0`; the lower bound follows from an exact profinite collision between prime and semiprime sequences of opposite Möbius sign.

Consequently a `C^*`-norm-convergent infinite series or growing-conductor sequence of Bost–Connes **coefficient-algebra** observables cannot converge to an exact Möbius-orientation observable, nor even approximate the square-free Möbius sign uniformly with error strictly below `1`. This closes one precise infinite-completion loophole in `MC-142`: increasing the number of finite cyclotomic modes and then taking a norm limit does not restore the lost sign if the limit remains inside `C^*(Q/Z)`.

This does **not** rule out nonuniform finite-prefix approximations, non-norm-convergent limits, measurable/unbounded multipliers, non-equilibrium distributions, or constructions involving the semigroup/crossed-product part of the Bost–Connes algebra. It is a topological obstruction to continuous coefficient encoding, not a no-go theorem for the full Bost–Connes system.

## 1. Opposite Möbius signs converge to the same profinite point

For each integer `K>=2`, put

\[
N_K=K!.
\]

By Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, there are infinitely many primes congruent to `1 mod N_K`. Choose three distinct such primes

\[
p_K,q_K,r_K\equiv1\pmod{N_K}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then both

\[
p_K\equiv1\pmod{N_K}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q_Kr_K\equiv1\pmod{N_K}.
\tag{5}
\]

For every fixed modulus `m`, once `K>=m` we have `m|K!`, so `(5)` implies

\[
p_K\to1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q_Kr_K\to1
\tag{6}
\]

in the profinite topology of `\widehat{\mathbb Z}`.

But the chosen integers are square-free and

\[
\mu(p_K)=-1,
\qquad
\mu(q_Kr_K)=+1.
\tag{7}
\]

If a continuous `f` satisfied `(2)`, continuity at the common limit `1` would force both sequences of values to converge to `f(1)`, while `(7)` makes them identically `-1` and `+1`. This is impossible.

The argument uses no zeta continuation, zero-free region, probabilistic model, KMS state, or asymptotic cancellation estimate. It is an exact incompatibility between Möbius parity and the profinite topology carried by the coefficient spectrum.

## 2. The uniform distance is exactly one

Suppose for contradiction that some `f in C(\widehat Z)` satisfies

\[
\sup_{n\in\mathcal S}|f(n)-\mu(n)|<1.
\tag{8}
\]

Applying `(8)` to the two sequences above gives

\[
|f(p_K)+1|<1,
\qquad
|f(q_Kr_K)-1|<1.
\tag{9}
\]

By `(6)` and continuity, both `f(p_K)` and `f(q_Kr_K)` converge to the same complex number `f(1)`. Passing to the limit in `(9)` would place `f(1)` simultaneously in the open unit discs centered at `-1` and `+1`. Those discs are disjoint. Hence `(8)` is impossible, proving the lower bound in `(3)`.

The zero function gives

\[
\sup_{n\in\mathcal S}|0-\mu(n)|=1,
\]

so equality holds.

Thus the obstruction is not merely failure of exact interpolation. The restriction of the continuous coefficient algebra to the square-free integers stays a full unit away, in uniform norm, from the Möbius sign.

## 3. Why norm completion does not evade `MC-142`

The finite coefficient polynomials generated by the characters `e(r)`, `r in Q/Z`, are dense in the group `C^*`-algebra `C^*(Q/Z)`. Under `(1)` their norm completion is exactly `C(\widehat Z)`.

Therefore let `F_j` be any sequence of finite Bost–Connes coefficient polynomials converging in `C^*` norm to `F`. The limit corresponds to a continuous function on `\widehat Z`. Equation `(3)` implies

\[
\sup_{n\in\mathcal S}|F(n)-\mu(n)|\ge1.
\tag{10}
\]

This statement is independent of how the finite polynomials are organized. Their conductors may grow without bound, the number of cyclotomic modes may diverge, and the coefficients need not come from the primitive-character family classified in `MC-142`. If the sequence converges in the coefficient-algebra norm, its limit is continuous and the profinite collision applies.

This is a different obstruction from the finite Fourier collapse in `MC-142`. There, finite primitive-character monomials either carry a nontrivial symmetry character or reduce to a coprimality projector. Here no primitive-character assumption is needed: **the entire continuous norm completion is too coarse topologically to encode Möbius parity pointwise across scales.**

## 4. What the obstruction says about the source-to-Mertens problem

The coefficient spectrum organizes integers by congruence at every fixed finite modulus. Profinite closeness means eventual agreement modulo each such modulus. Möbius orientation is not continuous for that notion of closeness: prime and semiprime integers can be made arbitrarily indistinguishable by all prescribed finite congruence tests while retaining opposite sign.

This gives a concrete form of the representation-loss problem already visible in `MC-138`–`MC-142`. Finite divisibility/cyclotomic information can be enlarged to all continuous profinite information without producing a globally stable sign observable. The missing carrier is not simply “more conductors”. Any exact pointwise Möbius encoding must retain structure that is discontinuous in the profinite coefficient topology or must live outside that coefficient algebra.

The result still does not control

\[
M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n).
\]

A successful Bost–Connes mechanism might avoid reconstructing `mu(n)` pointwise and instead control an aggregate statistic that transfers to `M(x)`. This finding rules out only the direct continuous-coefficient realization of the sign, not every possible aggregate route.

## 5. Prior art and novelty audit

The structural ingredients are standard.

- The Bost–Connes coefficient algebra `C^*(Q/Z)` and its cyclotomic generators are classical and were already audited in `MC-139`–`MC-142` against Jean-Benoît Bost and Alain Connes, *Hecke algebras, type III factors and phase transitions with spontaneous symmetry breaking in number theory*, Selecta Mathematica (N.S.) **1** (1995), 411–457, DOI `10.1007/BF01589495`.
- The identification `C^*(Q/Z) ~= C(\widehat Z)` is the standard Gelfand/Pontryagin-duality identification for a discrete abelian group; the Pontryagin dual of `Q/Z` is the profinite integers.
- Existence of arbitrarily large primes in the class `1 mod N_K` is a special case of Dirichlet's theorem, and the line already has the stronger prime-number-theorem-in-arithmetic-progressions anchor `MC-S15`.
- A targeted search also found literature on “polyadicly continuous” arithmetic sequences, where continuity means precisely uniform stability under sufficiently fine congruences. No source located in the audit stated the Möbius prime/semiprime collision above as a separate named theorem. That absence is not treated as evidence of novelty: the proof is an immediate synthesis of classical profinite topology and Dirichlet's theorem.

Accordingly no novelty is claimed for the topological or arithmetic ingredients. The durable Mathia result is the route classification: the `C^*`-norm-completed coefficient escape explicitly left open by `MC-142` cannot contain a uniformly informative pointwise Möbius-orientation observable.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Only coefficient-algebra continuity is ruled out.** The full Bost–Connes crossed product contains semigroup isometries and noncommutative observables not represented by a single function in `C(\widehat Z)`.
- **The obstruction is pointwise/uniform.** An observable may correlate with Möbius, recover a smoothed statistic, or encode an averaged quantity without agreeing with `mu(n)` on square-free integers. Such a route requires a separate source-to-`M(x)` theorem.
- **Finite-range interpolation is not ruled out.** For each fixed finite set of integers one can choose continuous cylinder functions separating the relevant residue classes. What fails is a single norm-convergent global coefficient observable retaining the sign at all scales.
- **Non-norm convergence is different.** Weak, pointwise-on-selected-states, distributional, measurable, or unbounded limits can leave `C(\widehat Z)` and are outside `(3)`–`(10)`.
- **The same profinite point is essential.** A proposed counterexample must confront both sequences in `(6)`; changing to a topology that separates them changes the mathematical carrier and therefore requires independent justification inside the Bost–Connes construction.
- **No RH consequence is asserted.** The finding proves information loss in one representation class and no bound for `M(x)`.

## Consequence for the line

The “infinite/conductor-growing coefficient completion” residual in `MC-142` can now be narrowed sharply. Merely taking a `C^*`-norm limit of increasingly rich cyclotomic coefficient observables cannot restore Möbius orientation: all such limits are continuous on `\widehat Z`, while prime and semiprime sequences of opposite Möbius sign collide at the same profinite point.

A genuinely new Bost–Connes continuation therefore has to do at least one of the following: leave the continuous coefficient algebra through a mathematically controlled weaker completion, use nontrivial semigroup/crossed-product structure, pass to non-equilibrium dynamics, or identify an aggregate observable whose transfer to anchored Mertens excursion does not require pointwise reconstruction of `mu`. Each option has a distinct first-kill test; simply increasing conductor or polynomial complexity inside `C^*(Q/Z)` is now closed.
