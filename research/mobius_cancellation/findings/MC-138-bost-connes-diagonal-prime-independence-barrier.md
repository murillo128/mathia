# MC-138 — Bost–Connes diagonal prime independence blocks critical-half phase covariance

**Status:** `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

Let `E_n=S_nS_n^*` be the canonical divisibility range projections in the Bost–Connes system, and let `D` be the commutative C*-subalgebra generated by the prime-power projections `E_{p^k}`. For any `KMS_beta` state `phi_beta`, `beta>0`, the restriction of `phi_beta` to disjoint prime-coordinate subalgebras is an exact product state.

More precisely, if `p_1,...,p_m` are distinct primes and `A_{p_j}` is any bounded element of the local diagonal algebra generated by `E_{p_j^k}`, then

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^m A_{p_j}\right)
=\prod_{j=1}^m\phi_\beta(A_{p_j}).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, the induced valuation variables are independent geometric coordinates:

\[
\Pr_\beta(v_p=k)=(1-p^{-\beta})p^{-k\beta},
\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots.
\tag{2}
\]

Hence every connected covariance between centered observables on distinct prime coordinates vanishes exactly. In particular, for the phase-deformed Möbius local observable from `PL-211`,

\[
Q_{p,\omega}
=1+(\omega-1)E_p-\omega E_{p^2},
\qquad |\omega|=1,
\tag{3}
\]

which takes the values `1, omega, 0` on `v_p=0,1,>=2`, let

\[
r=p^{-\beta},\qquad
m_{p,\omega}=\phi_\beta(Q_{p,\omega})=(1-r)(1+\omega r),
\]

and `X_{p,omega}=Q_{p,omega}-m_{p,omega}`. Then for `p!=q`,

\[
\boxed{
\phi_\beta(X_{p,\omega}^*X_{q,\eta})=0
}
\tag{4}
\]

for arbitrary unimodular phases `omega,eta`.

The local centered variance is

\[
\boxed{
\|X_{p,\omega}\|_{2,\phi_\beta}^2
=r(1-r)\left[2(1-\cos\theta)+(2\cos\theta-1)r+r^2\right],
\qquad \omega=e^{i\theta}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Therefore the actual Möbius orientation `omega=-1` has

\[
\|X_{p,-1}\|_2^2
=r(1-r)(4-3r+r^2)
\sim 4p^{-\beta},
\tag{6}
\]

whereas the phase-blind square-free-support choice `omega=1` has

\[
\|X_{p,1}\|_2^2
=r^2(1-r^2)
\sim p^{-2\beta}.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently the unweighted centered prime sum

\[
C_F=\sum_{p\in F}X_{p,-1}
\]

obeys, by exact orthogonality,

\[
\|C_F\|_2^2=\sum_{p\in F}\|X_{p,-1}\|_2^2,
\tag{8}
\]

so it has an `L^2` limit as `F` exhausts the primes only for `beta>1`. The corresponding support statistic `sum_p X_{p,1}` has the weaker threshold `beta>1/2`.

Thus the canonical Bost–Connes **diagonal divisibility algebra contains no phase-sensitive cross-prime connected covariance at the critical-half boundary**. Prime orientation enters at first order in `p^{-beta}`, whose natural unweighted Hilbert threshold is `beta=1`; the `beta=1/2` threshold appears only after the first-order phase information cancels and the surviving fluctuation is of order `p^{-2 beta}`.

This resolves the diagonal version of `CLUE-bost-connes-phase-sensitive-mobius-transfer`: the obvious attempt to recover Möbius parity through connected KMS covariances cannot work inside the product divisibility diagonal. A surviving Bost–Connes route must leave this exact product-statistic class — for example by using genuinely non-diagonal crossed-product data — and must still prove an independently cheaper transfer to anchored Mertens excursion.

## 1. Product factorization is forced by the divisibility projections

`PL-211` establishes for every KMS state that

\[
\phi_\beta(E_n)=n^{-\beta}.
\tag{9}
\]

For distinct primes and nonnegative exponents,

\[
\prod_{j=1}^m E_{p_j^{k_j}}
=E_{\prod_j p_j^{k_j}}.
\]

Applying `(9)` gives

\[
\phi_\beta\!\left(\prod_jE_{p_j^{k_j}}\right)
=\prod_jp_j^{-k_j\beta}
=\prod_j\phi_\beta(E_{p_j^{k_j}}).
\tag{10}
\]

Finite linear combinations of the nested projections `E_{p^k}` form a dense *-subalgebra of each prime-coordinate diagonal algebra. Multilinearity and continuity extend `(10)` to `(1)`.

The same calculation identifies the valuation law. Since `E_{p^k}` is the event `v_p>=k`,

\[
\Pr_\beta(v_p=k)
=\phi_\beta(E_{p^k}-E_{p^{k+1}})
=p^{-k\beta}-p^{-(k+1)\beta},
\]

which is `(2)`. Joint tail probabilities factor by `(10)`, so the prime valuations are independent under the diagonal KMS restriction.

This product structure is not a Mathia invention. Neshveyev's adelic realization of the Bost–Connes KMS states explicitly writes the canonical measure as a product `mu_beta=otimes_p mu_{beta,p}` and constructs the finite-prime `L^2` basis from the corresponding tensor factors. The derivation above isolates the exact consequence needed here and also shows that it holds on the divisibility projection algebra for every KMS state, including the symmetry-broken low-temperature family, because the KMS relation fixes all `E_n` moments.

## 2. Connected prime covariances vanish before any asymptotics

Take local observables `A_p` and `B_q` with `p!=q`, and center them:

\[
A_p^\circ=A_p-\phi_\beta(A_p),
\qquad
B_q^\circ=B_q-\phi_\beta(B_q).
\]

Equation `(1)` immediately gives

\[
\phi_\beta((A_p^\circ)^*B_q^\circ)
=\phi_\beta((A_p^\circ)^*)\phi_\beta(B_q^\circ)=0.
\tag{11}
\]

The same factorization holds for arbitrary disjoint finite prime sets, so every mixed centered moment built from disjoint diagonal coordinate blocks factors. There is therefore no hidden cross-prime covariance to amplify near `beta=1/2`: within `D`, the equilibrium state has already separated the prime coordinates exactly.

This is stronger than observing that `M_F^*M_F` is phase-blind. `PL-211` showed that one particular second moment loses the Möbius sign. Equation `(11)` shows that replacing that norm by a connected covariance between distinct diagonal prime coordinates does not restore the missing orientation: the covariance is identically zero for structural reasons.

## 3. Phase sensitivity itself restores the beta=1 cost

For `(3)`, the geometric law `(2)` gives

\[
\phi_\beta(Q_{p,\omega})
=(1-r)+\omega(1-r)r
=(1-r)(1+\omega r),
\tag{12}
\]

and, because `|Q_{p,omega}|` is `1` on `v_p=0,1` and `0` afterwards,

\[
\phi_\beta(Q_{p,\omega}^*Q_{p,\omega})=1-r^2.
\tag{13}
\]

Subtracting the squared mean yields `(5)` exactly.

The two relevant specializations expose the obstruction. For the square-free-support orientation `omega=1`, the first-order `r` term cancels and the variance begins at `r^2`; summing those variances therefore sees the critical exponent `2 beta=1`. For the Möbius orientation `omega=-1`, the variance begins at `4r`; summing over primes requires convergence of `sum_p p^{-beta}`, hence `beta>1`.

More generally, if a phase family satisfies `1-Re(omega_p)>=c>0` on a set of primes whose `sum p^{-beta}` diverges, the same first-order lower bound forces divergence of the unweighted centered variance there. A phase choice can move the threshold downward only by approaching the support phase `omega_p=1` sufficiently rapidly, which externally damps the very orientation contrast that the statistic was meant to retain.

This gives an exact version of the support-versus-orientation split:

\[
\text{phase-blind first-order cancellation}
\Rightarrow p^{-2\beta}\text{ scale and }\beta=1/2,
\]

while

\[
\text{undamped Möbius orientation}
\Rightarrow p^{-\beta}\text{ scale and }\beta=1.
\]

## 4. Why this does not transfer to Mertens excursion

The product KMS law is a distribution on prime-adic valuation coordinates. It does not retain the anchored additive order of the integers `1,2,...,x` that defines

\[
M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n).
\]

Equation `(11)` therefore does not merely fail to prove a Mertens estimate; it identifies the structural reason this diagonal covariance route is unsuitable. The state deliberately treats disjoint prime coordinates independently, whereas Mertens excursion is an ordered prefix statistic in the ordinary integer coordinate.

Introducing a decaying external weight `a_p` can of course make

\[
\sum_p a_pX_{p,-1}
\]

square summable at smaller `beta`, but then the new threshold is determined by the chosen weight budget `sum |a_p|^2p^{-beta}`. Such a weighting is not a source-forced transfer to `M(x)` unless an independent theorem connects it to first-absolute or endpoint Mertens control.

Likewise, this finding does not rule out the full noncommutative Bost–Connes algebra. Off-diagonal semigroup elements, arithmetic-unit characters, or other crossed-product observables are outside the product diagonal theorem. But they now carry a sharper proof obligation: they must create genuine cross-prime or cross-scale orientation information not reducible to `(1)`, and then connect that information to the additive prefix order without reconstructing `M(x)` at comparable cost.

Primary source for the product structure and the high-temperature phase transition:

Sergey Neshveyev, *Ergodicity of the action of the positive rationals on the group of finite adeles and the Bost-Connes phase transition theorem*, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society **130** (2002), no. 10, 2999–3003, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9939-02-06449-3`, arXiv `math/0002141`.

Neshveyev explicitly writes the canonical adelic measure as `mu_beta=otimes_p mu_{beta,p}` and uses finite-prime tensor-product `L^2` bases. For `0<beta<=1` he proves ergodicity of the positive-rational action and uniqueness of the KMS state. His proof also shows that nontrivial finite-prime unit characters are projected away in the invariant subspace; this is consistent with, but stronger in a different direction than, the diagonal covariance calculation above.

A targeted search located the classical product-measure/KMS framework but did not locate the exact centered variance formula `(5)` or the specific `beta=1/2` versus `beta=1` covariance diagnosis stated here. These are immediate consequences of established Bost–Connes structure and `PL-211`, so the finding deliberately records them as `LITERATURE+EXACT-DERIVED` with **no novelty claim**.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- The exact independence theorem applies to the **commutative divisibility diagonal** generated by the `E_{p^k}`. It does not claim independence for arbitrary noncommuting Bost–Connes observables.
- The covariance obstruction concerns disjoint prime-coordinate blocks. Same-prime moments are nontrivial and are exactly what produces `(5)`.
- The `beta=1` threshold in `(8)` is for the natural unweighted centered first-prime chaos. Arbitrary coefficient weights can change summability, but then their information budget must be justified independently.
- A phase family that approaches `1` on most primes can suppress the first-order term. That is a real mathematical escape from the variance lower bound, but it simultaneously suppresses the Möbius `-1` orientation and therefore is not a matched representation of the actual Möbius phase.
- The result does not prove that every phase-sensitive statistic in the full Bost–Connes system has threshold `beta=1`; it proves that the diagonal connected-covariance route has no cross-prime signal at all and that its natural unweighted phase fluctuation pays the `beta=1` prime-harmonic cost.
- No Mertens bound or RH implication is obtained. The result is a representation-level obstruction and a narrowing of the Bost–Connes handoff.

## Consequence for the line

The `beta=1/2` threshold supplied by the canonical Bost–Connes Möbius observable is not merely phase-blind after taking a norm. The whole diagonal KMS state is prime-product, so connected diagonal cross-prime covariances vanish exactly. Restoring the actual Möbius phase at first order moves the unweighted fluctuation cost back to the ordinary prime-harmonic boundary `beta=1`.

Accordingly, future Bost–Connes work in this line should not search for a critical-half covariance inside the divisibility diagonal. Any surviving route must identify a genuinely non-diagonal arithmetic observable and, crucially, a non-tautological theorem transferring it to anchored Mertens excursion.
