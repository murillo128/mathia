# MC-303 — Endpoint source inflation forces a multiquadratic discriminant barrier for black-box Chebotarev

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` · `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT` · `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION` · `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM` · `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` · `NON-RH`.

## Claim

Continue the localized Legendre-shell setup of `MC-293`–`MC-302`. Let

\[
\mathcal R_y
=\{r:y<r\le2y,\ r\text{ prime},\ r\equiv1\pmod4\},
\]

let `H` be the binary span generated by the occupied lower-prime Legendre sign cells, and let

\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_R\in H^*
\tag{1}
\]

be linearly independent shell functionals whose defect sets form an exact endpoint partition as in `MC-296`. For each `i`, choose a source-cost-minimizing lower-prime product representative from `MC-293` and write

\[
\chi_i(n)=\prod_{p\in V_i}\left(\frac np\right),
\qquad
\prod_{p\in V_i}p=\mathcal Q(\lambda_i).
\tag{2}
\]

On every shell prime, quadratic reciprocity gives

\[
\chi_i(r)=(-1)^{\lambda_i(s(r))}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then the global quadratic characters `chi_1,...,chi_R` are linearly independent over `F_2`. Hence

\[
X:=\langle\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_R\rangle
\cong(\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^R
\tag{4}
\]

is the full character group of a multiquadratic abelian extension `K/Q` with

\[
\boxed{[K:\mathbf Q]=2^R.}
\tag{5}
\]

For `I subseteq [R]`, put

\[
\chi_I=\prod_{i\in I}\chi_i,
\qquad
\lambda_I=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i,
\]

and let `f_I` be the primitive conductor of `chi_I`. The conductor itself is an admissible lower-prime product representative of the shell functional `lambda_I`, so

\[
\boxed{f_I\ge \mathcal Q(\lambda_I).}
\tag{6}
\]

The classical conductor–discriminant formula for abelian extensions now converts the entire source-cost spectrum into one field invariant:

\[
\boxed{
|D_K|=\prod_{I\subseteq[R]}f_I,
}
\tag{7}
\]

with `f_emptyset=1`.

Let

\[
D_y=\exp\!\left(\kappa\frac{\log y}{\log\log y}\right)
\tag{8}
\]

be the Page source threshold from `MC-293`, and assume the minimum endpoint defect satisfies the quantitative hypothesis of `MC-296`, namely `d_min>epsilon_y`. That finding gives

\[
H_R
:=
2^R-\binom{R}{\lfloor R/2\rfloor}-2
\tag{9}
\]

nonzero subset modes with

\[
\mathcal Q(\lambda_I)>D_y.
\tag{10}
\]

Combining `(6)`–`(10)` yields the collective discriminant lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\log|D_K|
>
H_R\log D_y.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{H_R}{2^R}
=1-O(R^{-1/2}),
\tag{12}
\]

the root discriminant already satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log|D_K|}{[K:\mathbf Q]}
\ge
\bigl(1-O(R^{-1/2})\bigr)\log D_y.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the endpoint partition does not merely force many individually expensive quadratic modes: after they are packaged into their natural multiquadratic field, almost every character conductor contributes multiplicatively to a field discriminant of exponential-in-rank size.

At the blind-support floor of `MC-242`,

\[
R\ge\left(\frac1{\log2}-o(1)\right)\log\log y,
\qquad
2^R\ge(\log y)^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{14}
\]

Equations `(8)`, `(11)`, and `(14)` imply

\[
\boxed{
\log|D_K|
\ge
\frac{(\log y)^{2-o(1)}}{\log\log y}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular,

\[
\frac{\log|D_K|}{\log y}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{16}
\]

This closes one natural next move after `MC-301`–`MC-302`: instead of treating the source characters independently with another large sieve, package them into the single multiquadratic extension whose Frobenius elements encode the complete sign vector. The packaging is exact, but generic effective Chebotarev does **not** become a cheap source-specific mixing theorem. The same endpoint structure forces the field discriminant so high that the standard unconditional Chebotarev uniformity ranges lie far beyond the shell scale `x asymp y`.

More precisely, write

\[
n_K=[K:\mathbf Q]=2^R.
\]

Thorner–Zaman recall the classical effective asymptotic range

\[
\log x
\gg
\frac{(\log D_K)^2}{n_K}
+n_K(\log n_K)^2
+(1-\beta_1)^{-1},
\tag{17}
\]

with the final term omitted when no exceptional zero exists. Even after dropping that nonnegative exceptional-zero term, `(11)` gives

\[
\frac{(\log D_K)^2}{n_K}
\ge
\frac{H_R^2}{2^R}(\log D_y)^2
=
(1-O(R^{-1/2}))\,2^R(\log D_y)^2.
\tag{18}
\]

At `(14)`, the right side has size at least

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(\log y)^{3-o(1)}}{(\log\log y)^2},
}
\tag{19}
\]

which dominates `log y` by an unbounded factor. Their improved Theorem 1.1 also assumes

\[
x\ge(D_K n_K^{n_K})^{c_2}
\tag{20}
\]

for an absolute `c_2>0`; `(16)` alone already shows that `x asymp y` cannot satisfy `(20)` for large `y`.

Therefore the natural black-box Chebotarev strategy has a precise obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{endpoint Frobenius concentration}
\Longrightarrow
\text{large source spectrum}
\Longrightarrow
\text{huge }D_K
\Longrightarrow
\text{generic Chebotarev loses shell-scale uniformity}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

This is a theorem-range obstruction, not evidence that the actual Frobenius classes fail to mix at scale `y`. A future route could still exploit the very special ramification/character geometry of this particular multiquadratic family, a family-specific zero-density estimate, a source-coupled large sieve, or some other input that avoids paying for the full discriminant as a black-box complexity parameter. No estimate for `M(x)` follows.

## 1. Shell independence lifts to global character independence

For each `i`, the minimizing representative `(2)` is a primitive real Dirichlet character supported on lower primes, as in `MC-293`. Suppose that for some nonempty `I subseteq[R]` one had the global relation

\[
\chi_I=1.
\tag{22}
\]

Restricting `(22)` to every `r in R_y` and using `(3)` would give

\[
(-1)^{\lambda_I(s(r))}=1
\qquad\text{for every }r\in\mathcal R_y.
\tag{23}
\]

The occupied shell cells generate `H`, so `(23)` says that `lambda_I` is the zero element of `H^*`. This contradicts the linear independence of `(1)`. Hence no nonempty product of the `chi_i` is principal, proving `(4)`.

Equivalently, the compositum of the quadratic fields associated with the `chi_i` has maximal possible degree `2^R`. This is exactly the standard Kummer/multiquadratic independence criterion, but here the independence is certified by the already-defined shell functionals rather than imposed separately.

## 2. Source cost lower-bounds every Artin/Dirichlet conductor in the character group

Each `chi_i` is a product of Legendre characters at distinct lower primes. In a subset product `chi_I`, repeated prime factors cancel because every factor has order two. Thus its primitive conductor `f_I` is the squarefree product of the lower primes occurring with odd multiplicity.

For a shell prime `r equiv 1 (mod 4)`, quadratic reciprocity again gives

\[
\chi_I(r)
=
(-1)^{\lambda_I(s(r))}.
\tag{24}
\]

Consequently the prime set defining `f_I` is one of the ambient lower-prime product representatives admitted in the minimization defining `Q(lambda_I)`. This proves `(6)`.

Notice the direction: `Q(lambda_I)` may be strictly smaller because two globally different characters can agree on the finite shell. That only strengthens the present argument. A Page-forced lower bound on the **minimum** shell source cost automatically lower-bounds the conductor of every global representative, including `chi_I`.

## 3. Conductor–discriminant turns the subset spectrum into one collective cost

For a finite abelian extension of `Q` with Dirichlet character group `X`, the conductor–discriminant formula states

\[
|D_K|=\prod_{\chi\in X}f_\chi.
\tag{25}
\]

De la Bretèche, Kurlberg, and Shparlinski explicitly recall this formula in their treatment of multiquadratic fields and apply it to a character group `X` with Galois group `(Z/2Z)^R`; see Section 5.2 of *On the Number of Products Which Form Perfect Powers and Discriminants of Multiquadratic Extensions*, International Mathematics Research Notices 2021, no. 22, 17140–17169, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnz316`, arXiv `1901.10694`.

Apply `(25)` to `(4)`. `MC-296` shows that under the endpoint-partition and `d_min>epsilon_y` hypotheses, at least `H_R` of the nontrivial `lambda_I` satisfy `(10)`, including the explicit allowance for the possible Page-exceptional mode. Equation `(6)` makes the same `H_R` conductors exceed `D_y`. Discarding every other conductor from the product gives `(11)`.

The central-binomial estimate

\[
\binom{R}{\lfloor R/2\rfloor}
=2^R\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi R}}(1+o(1))
\]

then gives `(12)`–`(13)`. The field discriminant is therefore the first natural source-specific invariant in this endpoint branch that couples essentially the **whole** subset spectrum rather than paying for characters one at a time.

## 4. The endpoint Frobenius support is tiny, but the field needed to see it is huge

Every shell prime `r>y` is unramified in `K`, because all finite character conductors are products of lower primes. Since the characters in `X` separate `Gal(K/Q)`, the sign vector

\[
(\chi_1(r),\ldots,\chi_R(r))
\tag{26}
\]

identifies the Frobenius element of `r` in the multiquadratic group.

In the reciprocal endpoint model `d_i=1/R`, every shell prime has exactly one defect. Hence its Frobenius sign vector belongs to only `R` one-defect patterns out of the `2^R` possible classes. This makes Chebotarev an apparently natural source-coupled replacement for the generic large sieves of `MC-301`–`MC-302`: sufficiently uniform shell-scale Frobenius equidistribution would immediately forbid such concentration.

But `(11)` shows that the field complexity is not independent of the concentration one is trying to rule out. At the live rank floor the same Page argument that forces almost all subset modes to have large source conductor also forces `D_K` to be superpolynomial in `y`, in the precise logarithmic sense `(15)`–`(16)`.

Thus the multiquadratic reorganization reveals useful structure but does not obtain a free equidistribution theorem. Any successful Chebotarev-style continuation must exploit more than the generic pair `(n_K,D_K)`.

## 5. Effective Chebotarev range audit

Jesse Thorner and Asif Zaman, *A unified and improved Chebotarev density theorem*, Algebra & Number Theory **13** (2019), no. 5, 1039–1068, DOI `10.2140/ant.2019.13.1039`, arXiv `1803.02823`, recall that the classical unconditional asymptotic requires `(17)` in order for the main term to dominate. Their Theorem 1.1 gives an improved uniform asymptotic in the range `(20)`.

Substitution of the endpoint discriminant lower bound is enough to kill both black-box ranges at `x asymp y`. For the classical range, `(18)`–`(19)` fail even if one assumes away the exceptional-zero term entirely. For the improved theorem, `(16)` implies `log D_K/log y -> infinity`, while `(20)` requires `log x` to dominate a fixed positive multiple of `log D_K+n_K log n_K`.

This comparison is deliberately one-sided. It does **not** prove a lower bound for the least prime in a missing Frobenius class, and it does not show that a sharper theorem specialized to these fields is impossible. It shows exactly that replacing the current source-spectrum problem by an off-the-shelf effective Chebotarev theorem does not close the endpoint escape.

## 6. Prior art and novelty boundary

Nothing here is a new conductor–discriminant or Chebotarev theorem. The two analytic/algebraic inputs are classical or established literature: the conductor–discriminant formula for abelian extensions and effective Chebotarev with explicit dependence on field degree/discriminant.

A targeted literature audit for multiquadratic discriminants, Legendre-symbol Frobenius patterns, and effective Chebotarev found the standard ingredients separately, including the explicit multiquadratic conductor–discriminant treatment above, but did not identify a source asserting the Mathia-specific composition `(11)` from endpoint Page source inflation. **No standalone novelty claim is made.** The durable delta is a frontier refinement: `MC-296`'s almost-total source-cost inflation has a canonical collective realization as discriminant growth, and that growth quantitatively falsifies generic Chebotarev as an immediate cure for the factor-`R` family-energy gap left by `MC-301`–`MC-302`.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **Endpoint partition is load-bearing.** The `H_R` count comes from `MC-296`. Without exact partition, or another theorem producing comparably many high-source subset modes, `(11)` must be weakened accordingly.
- **The minimum-defect condition is inherited honestly.** The general bound uses `d_min>epsilon_y`; the reciprocal endpoint `d_i=1/R` satisfies it in the live `R asymp log log y` regime because `epsilon_y=O((log y)^-2)`.
- **Global character independence is proved, not assumed.** A global relation would restrict to a shell relation, so shell independence excludes it. The converse is not needed.
- **`f_I>=Q(lambda_I)` is the needed direction.** Source cost is a minimum over shell representatives; the conductor of the subset product supplies one such representative. No equality is asserted.
- **Possible 2-adic discriminant factors cause no loophole.** Formula `(25)` uses the actual primitive conductors. Any additional conductor contribution only increases `|D_K|`; the lower bound uses only Page-forced conductors.
- **Chebotarev failure here means failure of a black-box theorem range.** It is not a claim that Frobenius equidistribution is false at scale `y`, nor a no-go against a theorem exploiting special multiquadratic/source geometry.
- **No generalized RH is imported.** A GRH-level Chebotarev estimate would add exactly the kind of generalized zero-location input this line is not allowed to assume as an explanation of Möbius cancellation.
- **No estimate for `M(x)` follows.** The result narrows the source-specific frontier by identifying both a canonical collective complexity measure and the quantitative price paid by the most obvious generic equidistribution theorem.

## Consequence for the research line

`MC-301` and `MC-302` showed that generic character-family energy reaches much farther in conductor than Page pointwise theory but misses the blind-support endpoint by an explicit factor of order `R`. The multiquadratic package is the first natural way to couple all subset characters without summing them as unrelated modes. It succeeds algebraically: the full source spectrum becomes one degree-`2^R` field and one discriminant.

The same calculation also explains why that reorganization does not yet improve the cancellation frontier. **The endpoint obstruction makes the field hard at exactly the rate needed to defeat generic discriminant-uniform Chebotarev.** The next productive target is therefore narrower: a theorem exploiting the special shared lower-prime ramification and Boolean character group while avoiding full-discriminant uniformity, or a proof that even such source-coupled refinements retain an endpoint-capacity gap.
