# MC-304 — The selected quadratic family has one fixed ramification radical and obeys a sharper sparse-character capacity dichotomy

**Status:** `EXACT-DERIVED` · `LITERATURE+DERIVED` · `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT` · `STRUCTURAL-REDUCTION` · `CLASSICAL-MECHANISM` · `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED` · `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM` · `NON-RH`.

## Claim

Continue the localized Legendre-shell and selected-character setup of `MC-293`–`MC-303`. Let

\[
\mathcal R_y
=\{r:y<r\le 2y,\ r\text{ prime},\ r\equiv1\pmod4\},
\qquad
N=|\mathcal R_y|,
\]

and let

\[
\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_R
\]

be the linearly independent primitive quadratic source characters attached in `MC-303` to independent shell functionals. Write

\[
\chi_i(n)=\prod_{p\in V_i}\left(\frac np\right),
\]

where every `V_i` is a finite set of odd primes at most `y`. Put

\[
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^R V_i,
\qquad
P=\prod_{p\in U}p,
\qquad
X=\langle\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_R\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

Then `|X|=2^R`, every character in `X` has primitive conductor dividing the single squarefree radical `P`, and the conductor–discriminant product from `MC-303` collapses exactly to

\[
\boxed{
|D_K|=P^{2^{R-1}},
\qquad
|D_K|^{1/2^R}=P^{1/2}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the huge multiquadratic discriminant in `MC-303` is not generated by `2^R` unrelated conductor parameters. It is the exponential-in-rank replication of one finite ramification radical.

This fixed-radical structure also gives a source-specific family-energy estimate that is sharper than inserting the selected characters into the full quadratic family of `MC-302`. View every `chi in X` as the character modulo `P` induced by its primitive character. Since every shell prime exceeds every prime divisor of `P`, these induced characters have exactly the same values on `R_y`. Define

\[
x_\chi=\frac1N\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_y}\chi(r),
\qquad
E_X=\sum_{\chi\in X}|x_\chi|^2.
\tag{3}
\]

The fixed-modulus large sieve and its sparse Halász–Montgomery variant imply

\[
\boxed{
E_X
\ll
\log y\left(
\frac{\varphi(P)}{P}
+
\frac1y
\min\left\{\varphi(P),\ 2^R\sqrt P\log(2P)\right\}
\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

The first term is the reduced-residue density of the common radical. The second is a finite-modulus collision/capacity term, with the sparse-character estimate replacing the full `phi(P)` character family by the actual family size `2^R` when that is cheaper.

For the exact reciprocal endpoint partition used in `MC-301`–`MC-303`, one has

\[
E_X=\frac{2^R}{R}.
\tag{5}
\]

Combining `(4)` and `(5)`, and retaining the sparse branch, yields the necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\frac1R
\ll
\frac{\log y}{2^R}\frac{\varphi(P)}{P}
+
\frac{\sqrt P\log(2P)\log y}{y}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, every reciprocal endpoint partition must pay at least one of the following two source costs:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\varphi(P)}{P}
\gg
\frac{2^R}{R\log y}
}
\tag{7}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt P\log(2P)
\gg
\frac{y}{R\log y}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

At the blind-support floor where

\[
R=\log_2\log y+o(\log\log y),
\qquad
2^R=(\log y)^{1+o(1)},
\tag{9}
\]

this becomes a sharp structural dichotomy. The first branch says, up to the subpower slack in `(9)`, that the common ramification radical must remain **totient-rough** at scale `1/R`; the second forces

\[
\boxed{P\ge y^{2-o(1)}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Indeed, if `P>y^2` there is nothing to prove, while for `P\le y^2`, `(8)` and `log(2P)\ll log y` give

\[
P
\gg
\frac{y^2}{R^2(\log y)^4}
=y^{2-o(1)}.
\]

This is the first current endpoint estimate that uses the actual family as a subgroup of characters with one common ramification radical rather than as an arbitrary collection of quadratic conductors. It does **not** close the live factor-`R` gap: present source-cost information does not rule out either a radical with `phi(P)/P` of order at least `1/R` or a radical of near-quadratic or larger size. But it replaces the open-ended request for a “special family theorem” after `MC-303` by two explicit escape currencies.

The Page-induced discriminant lower bound from `MC-303` can also be rewritten exactly through `(2)`. If

\[
D_y=\exp\!\left(\kappa\frac{\log y}{\log\log y}\right)
\]

and `H_R=2^R-O(2^R/\sqrt R)` subset modes have source cost above `D_y`, then

\[
2^{R-1}\log P
=\log|D_K|
>H_R\log D_y,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
P>D_y^{\,2(1-O(R^{-1/2}))}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

At the blind-support floor this only forces `P=y^{o(1)}`. Hence the superpolynomial field discriminant obstruction of `MC-303` and the near-quadratic-radical branch `(10)` are genuinely different scales: the former can arise solely from degree replication, while the latter would be a new statement about the finite ramification support itself.

No estimate for `M(x)` follows.

## 1. Every ramified prime occurs in exactly half of the selected characters

Represent each `chi in X` by its squarefree support vector in `F_2^U`: multiplication of characters is symmetric difference of supports. Because `U` is the union of the generator supports, for every `p in U` the coordinate map

\[
e_p:X\longrightarrow\mathbf F_2
\]

is nonzero. A nonzero linear functional on an `R`-dimensional binary vector space has a kernel of size `2^{R-1}`. Therefore exactly `2^{R-1}` characters in `X` contain `p` in their primitive conductor.

Each local factor `(n/p)` is primitive of odd conductor `p`, and products over distinct primes have coprime primitive conductors. Thus for `chi in X`,

\[
f_\chi=\prod_{p\in\operatorname{supp}(\chi)}p.
\tag{12}
\]

Multiplying `(12)` over all characters and exchanging the order of multiplication gives

\[
\prod_{\chi\in X}f_\chi
=\prod_{p\in U}p^{2^{R-1}}
=P^{2^{R-1}}.
\tag{13}
\]

The conductor–discriminant formula for the abelian multiquadratic extension `K/Q` attached to `X`, already audited in `MC-303`, says

\[
|D_K|=\prod_{\chi\in X}f_\chi.
\]

This proves `(2)`. In this exact odd-conductor realization there is no hidden 2-adic factor: every selected character was built from primitive Legendre characters of odd prime conductor.

## 2. The selected family embeds into one fixed modulus

For each primitive `chi in X`, induce it to a Dirichlet character `tilde chi` modulo `P`. Distinct elements of `X` remain distinct modulo `P`, because their support vectors in `F_2^U` are distinct.

For every shell prime `r in R_y`, one has `(r,P)=1`: all prime divisors of `P` are at most `y`, whereas `r>y`. Hence

\[
\widetilde\chi(r)=\chi(r)
\qquad(\chi\in X,\ r\in\mathcal R_y).
\tag{14}
\]

So all shell sums may be treated as a sparse subset of the ordinary character group modulo the single modulus `P` without altering any value relevant to the endpoint construction.

This is a strictly finer organization than the quadratic-large-sieve embedding in `MC-302`, which forgets the fact that all selected conductors are divisors of one common squarefree radical.

## 3. Fixed-modulus and sparse-character large sieves give two capacities

Use coefficients

\[
a_n=\mathbf1_{\{n\in\mathcal R_y\}}
\]

on the interval `(y,2y]`. Then

\[
\sum_n|a_n|^2=N.
\tag{15}
\]

Klurman, Mangerel and Teräväinen, *Multiplicative functions in short arithmetic progressions*, Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society **127** (2023), 366–446, DOI `10.1112/plms.12546`, record in Lemma 6.1 the standard fixed-modulus character large sieve

\[
\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}
\left|\sum a_n\chi(n)\right|^2
\ll
\left(\varphi(q)+\frac{\varphi(q)}qY\right)
\sum_{(n,q)=1}|a_n|^2
\tag{16}
\]

for an interval of length `Y`. With `q=P`, `Y=y`, and restriction to the nonnegative subfamily `X`, `(14)`–`(16)` give

\[
E_X
\ll
\log y\left(
\frac{\varphi(P)}P+rac{\varphi(P)}y
\right),
\tag{17}
\]

using the prime number theorem in the fixed progression `1 mod 4`, namely `N\asymp y/log y`.

The same paper's Lemma 6.4 gives the Halász–Montgomery sparse-family estimate. Specializing its archimedean parameter to the single value `t=0` and taking the selected set `X` gives

\[
\sum_{\chi\in X}
\left|\sum a_n\chi(n)\right|^2
\ll
\left(
\frac{\varphi(P)}P y
+2^R\sqrt P\log(2P)
\right)N.
\tag{18}
\]

After division by `N^2`,

\[
E_X
\ll
\log y\left(
\frac{\varphi(P)}P
+
\frac{2^R\sqrt P\log(2P)}y
\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Taking the better of `(17)` and `(19)` proves `(4)`.

The common first term is important. Sparse-family counting alone cannot force a contradiction if the ramification radical has reduced-residue density large enough to absorb the endpoint energy. Conversely, if `phi(P)/P` is small, the second term asks whether `P` is already so large that a length-`y` interval cannot exploit fixed-modulus orthogonality cheaply.

## 4. Reciprocal endpoint energy converts the capacity estimate into a radical dichotomy

For the exact reciprocal endpoint partition, `MC-301` proves

\[
\sum_{\chi\in X}|x_\chi|^2=\frac{2^R}{R}.
\]

Insert this into `(19)` and divide by `2^R`. This gives `(6)`. If the first summand on the right of `(6)` supplies at least a fixed fraction of the left side, `(7)` follows. Otherwise the second summand does so and yields `(8)`.

Thus the missing factor `R` identified in `MC-301`–`MC-302` can no longer be treated as one undifferentiated large-sieve deficit. In the fixed-radical representation it splits into two concrete possibilities:

1. enough reduced-residue density remains in the ramification radical to carry the endpoint energy; or
2. the common radical is itself so large that the sparse-character collision term can carry it.

This is a necessary-condition theorem for the reciprocal endpoint model, not a proof that the actual shell realizes that model and not a theorem ruling out either branch.

## 5. Prior art and novelty boundary

The ingredients are established. The conductor–discriminant formula and its use for multiquadratic extensions are classical; `MC-303` already audits de la Bretèche, Kurlberg and Shparlinski, *On the Number of Products Which Form Perfect Powers and Discriminants of Multiquadratic Extensions*, IMRN 2021, no. 22, 17140–17169, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnz316`. The fixed-modulus and sparse-character estimates `(16)` and `(18)` are Lemmas 6.1 and 6.4 of the Klurman–Mangerel–Teräväinen paper already retained as `MC-S45`.

A targeted search for multiquadratic discriminants, fixed-radical quadratic-character families, characters with conductors dividing one modulus, and sparse character large-sieve bounds found these standard components and broader quadratic-large-sieve literature, but no source was identified asserting the endpoint-specific composition `(4)`–`(8)`. **No standalone novelty claim is made.** The durable result is the exact specialization to the current selected family and the resulting refinement of the live source-capacity bottleneck.

The ordinary all-quadratic-family bounds of `MC-302` remain valid and may be stronger outside the parameter ranges where the fixed-radical terms are small. `MC-304` does not replace them; it isolates additional structure that those bounds deliberately average away.

## Boundaries and falsification tests

- **The common radical is exact, not an extra hypothesis.** It is the union of the lower-prime supports of the selected generator characters already present in `MC-303`.
- **The odd-conductor realization is load-bearing for `(2)`.** The generators are products of primitive Legendre characters at distinct odd primes. With different local character conventions, 2-adic conductor factors would have to be restored explicitly.
- **Induction to modulus `P` changes values only on integers sharing a factor with `P`.** Shell primes are all larger than every prime divisor of `P`, so `(14)` is exact on the only sample used here.
- **The large sieve is an upper bound, not equidistribution.** Neither `(4)` nor `(6)` states that the selected shell values are random or independent.
- **The reciprocal partition remains a model hypothesis.** Equation `(5)` is the exact endpoint extremizer from the existing branch. A general defect pattern needs its own lower bound for `E_X` before `(6)` can be transferred.
- **Large discriminant and large radical are different currencies.** Equation `(11)` forces only `P=y^{o(1)}` at the critical rank, whereas the second capacity branch forces `P=y^{2-o(1)}`. One cannot substitute the former for the latter.
- **Totient roughness is not currently excluded.** Existing source-cost lower bounds do not imply `phi(P)/P=o(1/R)`. Proving such a statement, or replacing it by another source-specific restriction, is a genuinely new obligation.
- **No circular zero-free input enters `(2)`–`(10)`.** The only Page information is used in the separate comparison `(11)` inherited from `MC-303`.
- **No estimate for the Mertens function follows.** The result narrows one endpoint escape mechanism and identifies the source parameters a future theorem must control.
