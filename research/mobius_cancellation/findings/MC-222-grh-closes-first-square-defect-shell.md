# MC-222 — GRH closes the first square-defect shell, isolating a Dirichlet-family barrier

**Status:** `LITERATURE+DERIVED`, `FRONTIER-REFINEMENT`, `CONDITIONAL`, `NEGATIVE/OBSTRUCTION`, `PRIOR-ART-AUDITED`, `NO-NOVELTY-CLAIM`.

## Claim

`MC-221` reduces the first square-defect shell to the diagonal-scale estimate

\[
W_y(X)
:=
\sum_{y<d\le 2y\atop d\ {\rm prime}}
 d\,|A_d(T)|^2
\le X^{1+o(1)},
\tag{1}
\]

where

\[
y=c\log X,
\qquad
q=\prod_{p\le y}p,
\qquad
T=X-q,
\qquad
A_d(T)=
\sum_{\substack{n\le T\\(n,q)=1\\n\equiv -q\pmod{d^2}}}\mu(n).
\tag{2}
\]

The moving-degree parity problem in `MC-219`--`MC-221` is therefore an unconditional problem. Conditionally, however, the first shell is already within classical Dirichlet-`L` technology:

\[
\boxed{
\text{GRH for Dirichlet }L\text{-functions}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_d(T)=X^{1/2+o(1)}
}
\tag{3}
\]

uniformly for every prime `y<d\le 2y`, and consequently

\[
\boxed{
W_y(X)\le X^{1+o(1)}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The point is not to import GRH as an input to an RH program. The point is to identify exactly what the present shell is asking from classical analytic number theory. The primorial pre-sieve can be removed at only subpower cost, after which each summand becomes an ordinary Möbius sum in a reduced residue modulo `d^2`. Halupczok--Suger's GRH theorem supplies square-root-scale cancellation uniformly for moduli far larger than the polylogarithmic `d^2` occurring here.

Thus the live first-shell target is **not** obstructed by the primorial roughness itself. If one attacks each progression separately by reciprocal Dirichlet `L`-functions, the natural sufficient input is a GRH-strength family statement for the nonprincipal character sectors as well as the principal sector. Since that is stronger than RH for `zeta`, a successful unconditional/RH-directed proof must exploit something that the characterwise route discards: averaging across the shell, source-lift structure `n+q=d^2m`, principal/transverse interference, or another coupling that avoids paying for every Dirichlet `L(s,chi)` separately.

No unconditional estimate `(3)` or `(4)`, no new zero-free region, and no implication from RH alone is proved here.

## 1. Exact removal of the primorial pre-sieve

Let

\[
\mathcal S_q
:=
\{r\ge1: p\mid r\Rightarrow p\mid q\}
\tag{5}
\]

be the semigroup generated by the primes at most `y`. The exact convolution identity used already in `MC-210` specializes to

\[
\boxed{
\mu(n)\mathbf 1_{(n,q)=1}
=
(\mu*\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S_q})(n).
}
\tag{6}
\]

For a shell prime `d>y`, every `r\in\mathcal S_q` is coprime to `d`. Put

\[
a_d\equiv-q\pmod{d^2}
\]

and define the ordinary arithmetic-progression Mertens sum

\[
M(U;Q,b)
:=
\sum_{m\le U\atop m\equiv b\pmod Q}\mu(m).
\tag{7}
\]

Inserting `(6)` into `(2)` and writing `n=rm` gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
A_d(T)
=
\sum_{\substack{r\le T\\r\in\mathcal S_q}}
M\!\left(\frac{T}{r};d^2,a_d\overline r\right),
}
\tag{8}
\]

where `\overline r` is the inverse of `r modulo d^2`. Every residue `a_d\overline r` is reduced modulo `d^2`.

Equation `(8)` is the useful structural reduction: the rough selected-residue sum is a sparse superposition of completely standard Möbius progression sums. There is no loss from inclusion-exclusion over the `2^{pi(y)}` divisors of the primorial; the smooth-semigroup representation is better adapted to square-root estimates because its relevant weighted mass is subpower.

## 2. The GRH progression theorem applies throughout the main range

Karin Halupczok and Benjamin Suger, *Partial sums of the Möbius function in arithmetic progressions assuming GRH* (arXiv:1111.3305, to appear in Functiones et Approximatio), prove under GRH that, uniformly for reduced `b modulo Q`,

\[
M(U;Q,b)
\ll_\varepsilon
\sqrt U\,
\exp\!\left(
(\log U)^{3/5}(\log\log U)^{16/5+\varepsilon}
\right)
\tag{9}
\]

for

\[
Q
\le
\exp\!\left(
\frac{\log 2}{2}
\left\lfloor
(\log U)^{3/5}(\log\log U)^{11/5}
\right\rfloor
\right).
\tag{10}
\]

The source derives `(9)` by expanding the progression in Dirichlet characters and controlling the reciprocal factors `1/L(s,chi)` under GRH. See https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3305, Theorem 1 and the character expansion in its introduction.

For the present shell,

\[
d^2\le4c^2(\log X)^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Choose, only to separate a harmless tiny-quotient tail,

\[
U_0
:=
\exp\!\big((\log\log X)^4\big)
=X^{o(1)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Uniformly for `U_0\le U\le X`, the right side of `(10)` eventually dominates every fixed power of `log X`, so `(11)` lies inside the theorem's modulus range. Moreover the exponential factor in `(9)` is `X^{o(1)}` uniformly for `U\le X`. Hence every term of `(8)` with `T/r\ge U_0` obeys

\[
M(T/r;d^2,a_d\overline r)
\ll
\sqrt{T/r}\,X^{o(1)}.
\tag{13}
\]

## 3. The rough convolution costs only a subpower factor

Summing `(13)` over the main range gives

\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal S_q}
\sqrt{T/r}\,X^{o(1)}
\le
X^{1/2+o(1)}
\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}.
\tag{14}
\]

The Euler product in `(14)` is itself subpower. Indeed

\[
\log\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}
\ll
1+\sum_{p\le y}p^{-1/2}
\ll
\sqrt y
=O(\!\sqrt{\log X}\!),
\tag{15}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}
=X^{o(1)}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

For the tail `T/r<U_0`, use the trivial progression bound

\[
|M(T/r;d^2,b)|
\le
\frac{T}{rd^2}+1.
\tag{17}
\]

At the logarithmic smoothness threshold `y=c log X`, the classical smooth-number estimate already used in `MC-210` gives

\[
|\mathcal S_q\cap[1,X]|=X^{o(1)}.
\tag{18}
\]

Since every tail element satisfies `r>T/U_0`, `(17)` and `(18)` give a total tail contribution `X^{o(1)}`. Combining this with `(14)`--`(16)` proves the uniform conditional bound `(3)`.

The argument uses only a subpower amount of information to undo the rough cutoff. Therefore the decisive conditional input is the square-root progression estimate, not the primorial sieve.

## 4. Square-root progression cancellation is exactly enough for the shell energy

From `(3)`,

\[
W_y(X)
\le
X^{1+o(1)}
\sum_{y<d\le2y\atop d\ {\rm prime}}d.
\tag{19}
\]

The last sum is at most `O(y^2)`, and `y=c log X=X^{o(1)}`. Hence `(4)` follows immediately.

By `MC-221`, `(4)` is exactly the required weighted RMS square-root parity balance on the first shell. Thus GRH proves that total moving-degree parity balance without needing to resolve the individual Hamming layers of `MC-219`--`MC-220`.

This is useful diagnostically. The fixed-degree cascade is a valid warning against finite-degree truncations, but it is not evidence that the full parity balance requires a new combinatorial law: classical analytic cancellation already supplies it conditionally once the relevant Dirichlet zero divisors are placed on the critical line.

## 5. Characterwise interpretation and relation to the existing frontier

`MC-210` and `MC-213` give the exact character expansion

\[
A_d(T)
=
\frac1{\varphi(d^2)}
\sum_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ d^2)}
\overline{\chi(a_d)}
S_d(\chi),
\qquad
S_d(\chi)
:=
\sum_{\substack{n\le T\\(n,q)=1}}
\mu(n)\chi(n).
\tag{20}
\]

For `Re(s)>1`, the corresponding rough twisted Dirichlet series is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(n,q)=1}
\frac{\mu(n)\chi(n)}{n^s}
=
\frac1{L(s,\chi)}
\prod_{p\le y}
(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.
}
\tag{21}
\]

Thus the same boundary seen in the progression theorem is intrinsic to the parity endpoint: a character-by-character contour argument naturally sees the zero divisor of every `L(s,chi)` occurring in `(20)`. The principal character recovers the zeta zero-mode obstruction already isolated in `MC-185`--`MC-187`; the nonprincipal characters introduce the wider Dirichlet family.

This also sharpens the role of `MC-213` and `MC-215`. The source-selected residue can still exploit interference among character sectors, and the explicit phases themselves are gauge by `MC-215`. Therefore `(21)` does **not** prove that GRH is necessary for `(4)`. It proves only that the naive strategy of controlling every sector separately is naturally a GRH-strength strategy. The remaining Mathia opportunity is precisely to avoid that overpayment by proving cancellation in the aggregate selected coordinate or across the shell.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty boundary

The conditional progression bound `(9)`--`(10)` is Halupczok--Suger's theorem, not a Mathia result. Character orthogonality, the reciprocal `L`-series in `(21)`, and the convolution identity `(6)` are classical; `(6)` was already recorded in `MC-210`. `MC-186` already audits Alamoudi's unconditional rough zero-mode estimate and its subpower barrier.

The repository contribution here is the **frontier specialization to the exact first square-defect shell**: composing the classical GRH progression theorem with the logarithmic-primorial smooth-semigroup transform shows that the whole `MC-221` parity-energy target is conditionally closed, while identifying the extra Dirichlet-family zero information that a sectorwise proof would import.

No novelty is claimed for GRH consequences for Möbius sums in arithmetic progressions.

## 7. Boundaries and falsification tests

- This is a conditional result. It cannot be used as an input to prove RH without circularly assuming the stronger Dirichlet-family GRH.
- The result concerns only the first shell `d\asymp log X`. The Halupczok--Suger modulus range is large but not arbitrary; no claim is made that the same argument closes every square-defect shell.
- The conclusion controls the complete signed sum `A_d`, not the distribution of `omega(n)` degree by degree. It therefore does not supply the moving-degree Sathe--Selberg theorem contemplated after `MC-220`.
- No converse is claimed. The averaged selected-residue energy `(4)` may hold even if some nonprincipal Dirichlet `L`-function violates GRH, because character sectors can interfere and the shell estimate observes only one aggregate coordinate per modulus.
- The tiny-quotient split in `(12)` is technical. Any subpower `U_0` growing fast enough that `(11)` lies in the modulus range of `(9)` gives the same fixed-power conclusion.
- A proposed unconditional proof that first expands `(20)` and bounds every `S_d(chi)` separately at square-root scale should be classified as having replaced the RH target by a Dirichlet-family problem unless it supplies an unconditional family theorem. A proof using aggregate cancellation is not covered by this warning.

## Consequence for the live frontier

The first-shell question can be narrowed from "find moving-degree parity cancellation" to a more discriminating alternative:

1. derive the parity balance by a genuinely arithmetic moving-degree theorem that does not hide reciprocal-`L` zero information; or
2. exploit the source-selected character aggregate/cross-modulus structure strongly enough to obtain `(4)` without proving square-root cancellation for each Dirichlet character separately.

The second route is now especially well motivated: the conditional calculation shows that the target scale itself is exactly the scale supplied by GRH, while `MC-213`--`MC-215` show where aggregate interference remains available to beat the characterwise cost.
